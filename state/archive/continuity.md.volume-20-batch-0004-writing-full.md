## WHAT THE REVIEW-FIX PASS ON DAYS 971 TO 980 SETTLED, AND IT IS THREE THINGS

1. **THOSE TWO BOARDS ARE NOT ONE BOARD AND THE CLOTH IS NOT THE STALLHOLDER'S.** The man of about thirty-four's board is
   **face in against the wood of his own trestle with the bare back turned out into the row behind his own shoulder**; the
   nineteen rates and the empty two-finger space are **the tradesman's**, two stalls along; and **the cloth is on the
   tradesman's shoulder.** 0978 and 0980 both broke this and both are fixed. A chapter that reads either man's board across,
   or puts the cloth on the wrong man, is wrong.
2. **THAT STAIR IS NOT COUNTED END TO END AND ITS BOTTOM STEP IS NAMED.** No chapter of days 971 to 980 counts it from the top
   step down to the flags and no person in them stands on a step that does not exist. **977 does name the bottom step as the
   eleventh and the last, which is the settled geometry set out at the foot of this file and printed on day 959, and that
   naming stands.** A state file or a record may not assert that the stair was never counted while a chapter of the same batch
   names its last step.
3. **THE CREASE ALONG THE TOP OF THAT FLAP IS A STATE AND IT CHANGED ON 973.** By the end of that day the flap lay against the
   body of that satchel with a finger's width of air between them at the edge nearest his own side and nothing holding it
   open. **The flap was not opened on any day of days 971 to 980 and nothing says why he opens nothing he carries.**

## THE TWO STATES THAT CHANGED ON DAYS 971 TO 980, AND WHAT EACH OF THEM COSTS A LATER WRITER

**A state changing is not a thing being answered, and neither of these two is closed by any later chapter.**

**1. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO IS AT THE FAR END OF THAT BENCH AND NOT AGAINST HER OWN FAR WALL.** On 971 at about twenty
minutes past the seventh hour she came off that wall, without a word to anybody, and went the length of that room and stood
at the far end of that bench, past the second book, with her own shoulder to the wood behind the far end of it and the window
at her back and her own hands at the sides of her dress. She said nothing about why. **Her stool has not moved and still
stands a foot further out from that wall than it did before she carried it there, and the clear floor between the stool and
the wood of the wall is about the width of a hand the whole length of it, with the grit in the joints of the flags inside
that room along that strip standing up where nothing has walked on it at any point of that flood.** She is at the far end of
that bench on 972, 973, 974, 975, 976, 979 and 980. **A chapter that stages that wall after 971 stages it with her at the
bench, and a chapter that puts her back on the wall is wrong.** On 977 she comes down that stair at about the ninth hour and
stands on the bottom step about as long as it takes a man to read a thing twice, and goes on down and out under the awning.
On 978 she is in the market row and not in that room.

**2. THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN HAS NOW BEEN ASKED ONE THING ABOUT HIS OWN TROUGH AND HAS ANSWERED IT.** On 975 the man of
about thirty-nine who trades on a board asked him, at the top of that step with his own two hands on the rim of the trough,
whether that rim would take a hoop of the width they get at the wharf, and he told him it would take two of them and not
one, and neither of those two men went up that step and looked at the rim of it. **The trough got no heat on that day and no
heat on any of days 971 to 980, and its ice was not broken on any of them, and the asking was about a hoop and not about a
heat.** That exchange is on no surface anywhere and it is not in either of those two books.

**AND THE FOUR STATES THAT MOVED ON DAYS 961 TO 970 ARE STILL WHERE THOSE TWO DAYS PUT THEM.** The stool a foot further out
with a clean square on the flags under where its near leg was. The gap at the head of the keeper's leaf closed and the dry
cloth with nowhere to go. The empty second hole in the outside wall soft about the width of a nail all round its lip, and now
soft a whole finger's width from the hole all the way round after the rain of the night of 974 stood in it and went down over
an hour of afternoon sun. A clean arc about the width of a hand on that gate's hinge, with the gate standing open.

## WHERE THE STANDING OBJECTS STAND AT DAY 980

1. **THE BENCH IN THAT ROOM. Moved on 953, about a foot further along toward the wall, and not put back.** Under the place it
   came off there are four squares of clean flags about the size of a hand and the grit in the joints inside them is pressed
   flat and has not come back up. **On 975 the dry cloth was carried off the head of the keeper's leaf and laid on the bare
   wood of that bench for about half the morning and put back, and the wood under the place where it lay stands a shade
   paler than the wood either side of it along the whole length of the cloth and about a finger's width wide.** The two books
   went with the bench and are still open side by side on it.
2. **THE WEAR IN THE MIDDLE OF THE NINTH STEP FROM THE TOP OF THAT STAIR. Opened on 959.** The stone shows about the size of a
   hand and a shade paler than the stone either side of it, and the flakes are in a heap at the foot of that step about a
   foot to one side of the middle of it against the riser, and no boot goes near that heap. Nobody fetched a heat for it,
   nobody broke anything at it and nobody measured it.
3. **THE TROUGH AT THE TOP OF THAT MARKET STEP, ITS CRACK AND ITS ICE.** The thread of water out of the crack in its near
   end still crosses the flags. **The ice is still whole, about the depth of a thumb, and no heat was fetched on any day of

## THE TWO RULES OF THIS VOLUME THAT THE FIRST BATCH BROKE AND THAT HOLD, AND WHICH A WRITER WHO READS ONLY THE PLAN WILL NOT SEE

**§17.5 IS BINDING AND THE MEASURED COUNT BEHIND IT IS ZERO.** A card may not make an attribution restate a person's whole
handle every time that person speaks. The second handles this volume has and may use are **the keeper, the tradesman, the
stallholder, the carrier, and the man of about thirty-eight**, and each is beside the age and the trade on first appearance
in a chapter and never instead of it. **Full-descriptor restatements across days 971 to 980: 0, and exactly one person speaks
twice in one of those chapters and that is the keeper on 975.**

**A SCENE'S STATE IS PAST TENSE AND A GENERAL TRUTH IS NOT, AND THE LINE IS PUBLISHED SO IT CAN BE HELD.** A verb describing
the state of a specific located object on that morning goes to the past. A verb describing how a material behaves stays in the
present. **Days 971 to 980 were written in that tense and converted nothing after the fact, and the gnomic present was not
touched once.**

## THE GEOMETRY OF THAT STAIR, WHICH IS SETTLED GEOMETRY AND NOT A FIGURE ANYBODY MAY RECOUNT

**THAT STAIR HAS ELEVEN STEPS IN IT. The ninth step down from the top is neither the bottom step nor the top step, and there
are two steps under it before the flags.** It follows from three things already on disk: the plan puts **the ninth step down
from the top of that stair** as the place Adrian Vale's hands are on on day 959 at §4.1 and as a card in two separate days;
**the top step of that stair, which is not the bottom step**, where the bar lies out of its sockets, is §5; and 0953 had a
man at the ninth step get up and **come the last two steps**. **Measured across days 971 to 980, `nine steps` stands at 0 and
no person in those ten chapters stands on a step that does not exist.** The **ninth step from the top** carries the single
worn place in the middle of it. The **bottom step**, the eleventh and the last, is where the strip of ground begins and is
where the woman of about fifty-two stood on 957 and 959 and on 977. The **top step**, where the bar lies and the trough
stands, is nine risers above the ninth and is not near it.
