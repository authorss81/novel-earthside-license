**THE FIRST ONE PUT TO ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM IN THE FIFTY DAYS IS ON 957.** The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a
public register names her own cost out loud first, in her own mouth, before anything else that morning, and then puts
one thing to the man of about thirty-four about his own stall and his own board, in front of the room, with the date
in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair behind her. **He answers her. Nobody in that room was
ever going to have it, and no leaf in either of those two books carries any part of either the putting or the answer.
Nobody thanked anybody and nothing whatever was done about it before the light went or after it.** The chapter does not
say that it is the first, does not say how long the room has gone without, does not say what was asked last, does not
print the words of the putting and does not print the answer.

**THE MORNING AFTER IT, ON 958, THE VOLUME'S FINDING ARRIVES AS A FACT.** The man of about thirty-nine who trades on
a board says out loud, with both hands on the face of his own board and not moving, that somebody put a thing to him
on the seven hundred and forty-eighth day of this flood and that he did not answer it. **He can say where he was
standing and what the light was doing and cannot say what the thing was, and nobody in that room can say it either.
No chapter prints any part of it or any guess at it. Nobody established which of the two women of twenty-nine it was,
nobody went down the nine steps to ask the other one, and nothing said that morning settled whether those two are one
woman or two.**
