## What these ten days were, in one paragraph, and the story comes first

A plank went off a second row of flats in the night and nobody in this city has ever been shown who took it, and at the seventh hour of the next morning a woman with two buckets went over the gap where it had been and got her leg to about the knee and came out of it on her own, and the man who had laid the plank was two rows off with his back turned and did not see and was not told. A man of about sixty stood at the near end of a shut road with a handcart and a hazel mallet and sixteen willow posts and eleven withies that he put up himself and were not sent, and he said out loud that nobody was going to ask him about it, and nobody asked him, and a man with a load went round the long way because nobody would say how long the long way was, and a woman with a bundle on her head went back the way she had come. The woman who keeps that city's only public book took the register off its shelf and opened it at a page, and a man of about thirty-four looked at the one place on a sheet lying on the table and then at the column on the right of that page and said out loud in about nine words that they were the same shape, and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it and nobody in that room settled whether they were the same thing. The man of about thirty-nine counted his own board from the end he does not read it from and said out loud that the two ends of it do not agree and that he was not going to say what either end comes to, and a woman who wanted one line off it went and read it at the other end and took her morning elsewhere. Four hundred yards of flags stood doubled back on themselves in four days of weather and were no drier at the eighth hour than at the fourth, and the water at a near gate went down twice with nobody in this city touching it, and the trough that was carried down that row put a foot of water into a doorway before the light was good. A man of about thirty-four stood at a corner of that shut road where no cord has ever been taken and told a man with an empty barrow and a woman with a hand-basket that he was not going to be asked what was past it, and the two hours he gave that corner cost the stall next along a morning of trade and nobody told her where he was. About nine people who were already in a room counted the sheets that have come up that road in this flood and got two numbers that do not agree, and the woman who said both of them said out loud that the person who will have to put one of them on something is standing at the head of that bench, and nobody thanked her. The man who carries things for a living said out loud one sentence about the difference between this flood's two sheets with words on them and said it was not what is in the place but what the place is for, and that the two of them went up that road on different days and nobody in that room will ever be able to tell afterwards which of the two was written first, and nobody thanked him. A man of about fifty-nine stood a hurdle in a walk at the back of a room, put a bar in it longer than the one that came out, said one thing nobody had asked him for, and went away without one word about the three who went down that road. And a room worked out out loud what a sheet on a table is for, and the woman who keeps that city's only public book said it before anybody asked, and the four days that would have been the cooling are not in this city, and nothing was decided and nothing was put anywhere.
