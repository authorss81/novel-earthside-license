## THE STAGING OF 965, THE DAY ADRIAN VALE IS IN

**ADRIAN VALE, Stage 2, in the room on 965 and obtaining nothing.** Both hands flat on the wood of the near end of that
bench, palms down and fingers out about the width of a hand apart, from the fourth hour to the light's going, with nothing in
either of them. **He is the cause of one thing that happens to somebody else: the near end of that bench is where the
tradesman keeps his own board and it is taken, so the tradesman puts his board at the far end instead, the keeper moves her
register a hand along the wood, and the second book comes a hand nearer to her than it has stood all week.** What he wanted
was to hear the woman of about fifty-two come to that end of that bench and say, in her own words and to him and not to the
room, what she had just refused. **She did not come to that end of that bench. Nobody in that room put anything to him at any
point of the day, nobody said his name, nobody was waiting for him to be useful to anybody, and he was not useful to any of
them.**

**THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO.** At her own far wall from the fifth hour, hands at the sides of her dress. **She says her
refusal first, alone, in daylight, with the date on the outside of the door behind her own shoulder, and does not say it a
second time before the light goes or after.** She puts one thing to the tradesman across about nine feet of that room and it
is said once; **the chapter does not give her words.** She then says what it costs her to have said it in front of about
nine people. **Nobody thanked her, nobody told her she was right and nobody told her she was wrong.** By the light's going
her own two hands are flat on the bare wood of that bench at the far end of it, well past the second book and not on it.
**On 969, four days later, she says she was asked something on day 598 and has been carrying the answer to it and did not
know until that morning that she had it, and does not say what the answer is.** She is the person whose descriptor the plan
puts in a mouth on 994.

**THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE WHO TRADES ON A BOARD, the tradesman.** Comes up that stair at the fourth hour to put his own
board on the near end of that bench as he does every morning, and finds that end taken. **He stands in the door of that room
for a moment, then puts his own board down at the far end past the keeper.** He is about nine feet off the woman of about
fifty-two for the rest of that day. **He answers her once and nobody in that room is told what the answer was.** He says one
further sentence to her about it and stops. **On 967 he is alone in that room with the stool for the better part of three
quarters of an hour, does not put his own hand on it, does not move it, and thinks about a room where a stool is brought out
of another room and put against a wall and never sat on, and gets as far as no conclusion and says nothing out loud.** He
comes up that stair and goes down it on 962, 966, 968 and 969 as well. **On 958 he says out loud that he was asked
something on the seven hundred and forty-eighth day of this flood and did not answer it, and that he cannot say what it
was.** His nineteen rates and the two-finger space at the foot of the middle column were not written on and nobody in that
room said one word about the space.

**THE WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO KEEPS A PUBLIC REGISTER, the keeper.** **On 957 she names the cost of the putting-to
out loud in her own mouth before she does anything else, puts one thing to the man of about thirty-four about his own stall
and his own board and nothing else, and does not print what his answer was, because the chapter does not print it and the
room does not write it down.** She is thanked by nobody and told by nobody that she was right, and she goes on with her own
page. **Her second speech that morning is attributed to the keeper and not to her descriptor.** On 965 she moves her own
register along the wood about the width of a hand and says not a word about it. **On 968 she works at the gap at the head
of that leaf for the better part of half an hour with the end of the cloth and her own fingers behind it and it will not go
in twice, says one word under her own breath at it that nobody in that room heard, squares the register against the fold
and goes on with the column that holds figures.** **On 969 the record says she was not there on day 598 for either of the
two people remembering, and she said nothing about it and went on with her own page.**

## THE TWO MANNER RULES THIS VOLUME'S FIRST BATCH BROKE AND THAT NOW HOLD, SO NOBODY HAS TO LEARN THEM AGAIN

**1. §17.5 — AN ATTRIBUTION DOES NOT RESTATE A PERSON'S WHOLE HANDLE EVERY TIME THAT PERSON SPEAKS.** Measured across the
ten files of days 951 to 960 the count of full-descriptor restatements was **2 and is now 0**, and across days 961 to 970 it
was **0 on the first draft and is 0 now**. The second handles are **the keeper, the tradesman, the stallholder, the carrier
and the man of about thirty-eight**, and She and He where a person has been established in the chapter. **A descriptor
belongs beside the age and the trade on first appearance in a chapter and never instead of them.** Used on days 961 to 970:
the stallholder on 962 and 968, the tradesman on 967, the carrier nowhere twice in a row, and She and The man of about
thirty-eight on 969.

**2. PAST TENSE FOR A SCENE'S STATE, PRESENT TENSE FOR A GENERAL TRUTH.** A verb describing the state of a specific located
object on that morning is in the past — the sockets are empty, the board was not turned over, the bar lay where it has lain.
A verb describing how a material behaves stays in the present — it is not the round hole a nail leaves, and the lid is a
little proud of the rim on one side of it and not on the other. **22 conversions were made on days 951 to 960 and the gnomic
present was not touched once; days 961 to 970 converted nothing after the fact because they were written in that tense.**

**AND THE THIRD MANNER RULE THE FIX PASS ADDED, WHICH IS ABOUT THE BOLD SPEECH MARKER AND NOT ABOUT A PERSON.** Every speech
in this volume is printed as a bold speaker marker because §17.7 makes that marker the thing both speech instruments read.
**The comma belongs inside the closing quote and the tag belongs outside it**, so the form is `**"…," **said the man of
about thirty-nine` and not `**"…."** said`. **Volume 20 was written with 39 instances of the wrong form and every one of
them is now corrected.** Volumes 17 and 18 already print it correctly and are the model.

## WHAT THE FIRST REPAIR PASS RESTAGED, AND NEITHER FACT OF EITHER DAY CHANGED

**957.** The putting-to, the answer, and the room are as at `state/continuity.md`. What moved is that **the tradesman
comes away off the near end of the bench in the middle of the morning and does not come back, the near end stands with
nothing against it from about the eleventh hour with people standing in that room behind it, and that had not happened

**959.** Adrian Vale has both hands flat on the stone of the ninth step down from the top of that stair, on the worn place in
the middle of it, a quarter of an hour. **That step is the ninth one down and there are two more under it before the flags,
so the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living is on the step below him and the woman of about fifty-two is
on the bottom step, which is the eleventh and the last.** He takes the packed grit out a piece at a time with his thumbnail
and it falls in flakes to the flags at the foot of that step. **He wants somebody else to put again the thing he asked once
and mean it the second time, and nobody in that room put anything to him at any point of the day, nobody said his name, he
asked nothing, and nobody came up that stair to stop on it and put anything to him.** He goes down about the eleventh hour,
goes along the row past the store's end and out to where the flags give out into the side of a yard, is not on that row on
anybody's account, and nobody asks him where he has gone. The tin is not in that room and he does not have it. He goes past
the sill with the two undated shapes in its dust and does not look at it.
