## What these ten days were, in one paragraph, and the story comes first

A man wheeled an empty barrow the length of a yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats and set it down across the open end of it, and then came back and put his thumb on one corner of a line of grey grit where it had lifted over a figure of this city's own, and pressed that corner flat, and the rest of the line came up off the board again by itself within the hour and the corner did not. Two men carrying a board between them got as far as the near post and stopped, and set what they were carrying down on the wet flags, and one of them got down and lifted a shaft, and the board came up with a wet edge along the bottom of it, and the man who had said one thing about that wet edge said it to the other man and not to the man who had moved the barrow, and the man at the foot of that stack would not move a barrow that was not his. A room over a market asked one question in ordinary conversation for about an hour, and about four of the nine people in it said out loud that they did not know and about four said nothing at all, and the man who trades nineteen rates on a board said in about four seconds that if one of them had been told, one of them would have said so in that room by now, and nobody in that room said he was right, and a woman asked to be sure of it twice and got the same answer twice, and he said out loud that he had never asked the woman who keeps that city's only public book one thing about that column and would take it back tomorrow if somebody in this city did ask her, and a boy went down that stair afterwards with nine people asking each other what a column was in their heads, and a man at the end of the row below took his cloth in and has not put it out since. On the Monday a room of about nine people found out what it was being asked, four of them at the ninth hour and four of them at the eleventh, and neither of the two groups told the other anything, and a man said out loud in about nine words that nobody had told that room what that word was for, and a man with a length of cord and a folded paper under his arm stood at the foot of the stair for about as long as it takes a man to decide whether a room is a room to come into, and went back down. On the Tuesday a woman alone with a register and two slates drew a bench out from the wall, opened the book, looked at the column on the right of that page and did not write in it, refused a man who wanted any line at all put in that book and told him why twice, got down on the floor and looked along the underside of a shelf without touching anything on it, and went out and stood on the step and read a date in chalk with nothing under it. **On the Wednesday, the nearer of this volume's two middle days, a man of about thirty-four said out loud what it was going to cost before he decided anything, and then decided that the word going into the column on the right of that page is his and that it goes in before the word going into the one place on that sheet and that neither of the two of them is to be read out in a room, and there was no vote and no cooling and no notice, and a man who wanted it put to the room was told out loud what that would cost and went down that stair at the tenth hour and did not come back up it that day.** On the Thursday, the other of the two middle days, a man who carries things for a living went up and down the stair at the back of that room four times with an empty satchel, showing two people two ways of carrying a sheet up a road, and a woman with two buckets waited on the bottom step and went to the channel instead of to the store, and he said the one thing nobody in that room had asked him for, which is that he has an opinion about the order of the two words and is not going to give it to anybody. On the Friday a hand went into an inside breast pocket and came out again with nothing in it, a woman went down into the heap of the store's own chalk with the flat of her hand and put about as much of it as would cover a coin back into a box on a step and the lid of that box did not shut, and a woman who came to the front of a stall at the seventh hour was not served and went to the stall next along and did not come back. **On the Saturday a woman asked one man one question about him and not about the book, and he said one thing out loud once, and the room did not ask either of them a second question, and a woman at the one window waited from the tenth hour until the half hour after the eleventh for a figure of this city's own written out for her and took her own business back down that stair.** On the Sunday a man who washes that board took a trough and a bucket out to that yard before the light and would not move that trough six feet for a man with a barrow and would not move it at all for a second man with a barrow, and two men carried a board the length of that yard and set it down at the foot of that stack, and the top of that stack ended the morning two boards higher. And on the Monday a room read out loud an order it is going to keep and wrote neither word down, and about four people in that room had guessed which of the two words goes first and about four had not, and the keeper of the book turned the heading of the sheet the wrong way up for anybody who came in after that and left it that way.
