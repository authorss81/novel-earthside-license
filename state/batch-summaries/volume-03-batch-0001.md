# Volume 03, Batch 0001 — Chapters 101–110: The Names That Went Down a River

Ten days. Days 101 to 110, the eleventh to the twentieth day of the month after this one, and the first ten days of Volume 03. **This batch works out that there is no column in the world for an answer, tries five times in nine minutes to find out how a page ended a document, finds out that a city which has spent a hundred and six days not writing names down cannot answer a question that asks for names, walks nine miles to look at seven cuts in a wall that nobody had ever looked at, and comes back with a number instead of a meaning and puts it on a page in a stone.** Nobody was convicted of anything. The undertaking is still void, on a nail, on a river, and no office has been told. A field is not paid. A woman of forty-eight is still owed one question and was not asked. A date on a page came and went and a door did not open. **The gains are a count of four hands, a count of five rooms that failed, a law about institutions being tired people, a sentence about what cannot be asked, and twenty-nine lines on a page in a stone. None of them is a power and all of them are heavier than a power.**

**This batch has not been reviewed independently. The reviewer agent is configured as a subagent and the review prompt executes under the writer agent, which has now happened in five batches running, and no claim of an independent review is made anywhere in the state files for this batch.** The checks that were run against the ten chapters as they stood at the checkpoint, and the twenty-nine defects they found and fixed, are in §10.

## 1. The batch's spine

1. **Day 101, on a wharf.** There is no column in the world for an answer, this city has been protected by that for a hundred and forty years, and the protection is that nothing comes back. The voidness of the undertaking is in a book in this city; the question in the eighth column is in a case on the same river; no vehicle in this world carries one to the other; **and the next time this city can say anything to that office is about five weeks after the hearing.**
2. **Day 101, on the same wharf.** **Both names are not repeated and neither is explained.** A woman of about thirty-four refuses to say them and gives the reason. A man in a very good coat says again, in front of nine people, that he is not going to explain either of them, and asks for it to be entered a second time. **He is not convicted of anything and is not made useless in any scene.**
3. **Day 102, in a converted salt store.** Five things tried in nine minutes, four of them empty, the fourth holding, and the fifth — why — running out. **The count of rooms is entered as five, with the four named, because a city that has failed at a thing four times and has never written the four down will fail a fifth time in the same place.** The one sentence that came closest was not written down.
4. **Day 103, two hundred steps down a condemned rail.** **The cost of a gate is the answer and the cost of an answer is a person**, in sixteen words. A gate and a page are compared and **the comparison is refused in about four seconds by the man who has done it and the refusal is entered.** *A single witness* is said aloud in the local language by a person who was given it. Nothing happened for nineteen minutes on purpose.
5. **Day 104, ten days of failing finished.** **Four lines describe a room and every line of them is about the eleven people who have been in it.** The boards were not lifted. Nobody answered *who is it about*.
6. **Day 105, four hours in a low room by the salt wharf.** **Every institution in this city is a person and every one of them is tired.** A person is the only shape that can be found, so agreeing to carry a cost writes down where that cost is — **and a cost that nobody holds is a cost that has got nobody.** Both errors refused in one room. **The twenty-eighth thing the wall has said is accepted in four seconds by a bone-setter and moves nothing.**
7. **Day 106, the middle of the batch, two pages side by side.** **The not-writing was the protection and is now the reason nobody can answer.** Two ways to refuse the question, both refused on purpose with the date on them. **Then we go and look at the seven** — four words, and it is not a plan, it is a date on a slate.
8. **Day 107, in a room over a market with the only bound book in this city in it.** **The answer is not a person and that is the whole of what we know.** A clerk is told he can correct a number alone, says yes in four seconds, and does not do it, in twenty-three words of his own. The difference between his two books was nine before the first registered refusal and is ten now.
9. **Day 108, four days out, nine miles in the wrong weather.** **A woman of about sixty-one hands nine years of paper to a girl of seventeen on the second mile and asks for the date and not herself.** The oldest year has two lines struck out of it and **the number has not gone down.**
10. **Days 109 and 110, the road and the way down.** **Seven cuts looked at for the first time in nine years and nobody can say what any of them means.** Nobody on that road asked a Hearthguard of forty-four one question. The count goes onto a page in a stone in six lines. **A date on a page is a door and not a promise, and the door did not open.** A hearing is in forty-four days and there is one hearing and one clock.

## 4. The batch's turn, and it is not Adrian's

**It belongs to a Hearthguard of forty-four who has been right for nine years and has just found out what his rightness cost, and to a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, and to a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and is on nothing.** None of the three asked for anything and none of the three is thanked, and the room's correct response to all three was to let them stand.

Hettis Roke, in eighteen words and then the rest of it, said:

> **I asked for a question instead of a name, and I have got a question I cannot answer.**

And then, on a road, four hours after nine people stood next to him and not one of them asked him anything:

> **It is a man standing next to his grandfather's hand. And I have not been asked in nine years and I have not refused, and a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused, and I said that to a room in this city on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of the flood, and I have not said one word about my own eight until this morning and I am not going to say it on a road in the wrong weather to nine people who have not asked me.**

And when a man of thirty-two asked him whether he would say it in a room:

> **I do not know.**

Three words, and it is entered that it is the first honest sentence about himself he has said in nine years, and that nobody checked the count.

**He was also asked to read his grandfather's name aloud and said no — to a person, and not to a document — and it is entered that a document would have got a yes out of him, and that this was the first time anybody had found that out.** Nobody may ask him again until he is asked properly, in a room, on a different day.

## 5. The rules established in this batch, binding on Batch 0002 and later

1. **There is no column in the world for an answer, and this city has been protected by that for a hundred and forty years, and the protection is that nothing comes back.** The reason nobody can be found is the same fact as the reason nothing can be audited, and **the second is the first with a river in the middle of it.** A quarter is about thirteen weeks; the hearing is about eight; **the next time this city can put anything in front of that office is about five weeks after the hearing.**
2. **The Void stays spent and the failure to explain it is the engine.** Five rooms, five identical failures, the count entered with the date on it. **No chapter may show the page working, reproduce it, or produce a mechanism for it. About four months of failing is now on the clock.**
3. **The one sentence that came closest was deliberately not written down, because a sentence about a page that works is a mechanism and a diagram of the most dangerous object in this book is a worse thing to have than nine minutes of nothing.** Adrian Vale has entered this reason four times in ten days. The repetition is the method.
4. **A gate and a page are the same kind of thing and are not, and the comparison was refused in four seconds and entered.** A gate takes a person and does not ask; a page ended a document and did not ask the man at the top of it. **That is a resemblance.** **The reason nobody is going to find out is that the finding out is the using** — four reasons entered with the date on them: we do not ask, we do not go and look, we do not put a second copy of anything anywhere, and we do not write a resemblance down as a cause.
5. **A page in this world can only be aimed at a condition, and a person cannot be found by a condition.** The standing test now applies to a room as well as to a document: **if the page is about the people who have looked at it, it is also a description.**
6. **A question that asks for names is unanswerable in a district that has spent a hundred and six days not writing names down, and the not-writing was the protection.** Two ways to refuse the eighth column's question exist and **both were refused on purpose with the date on the entry**, and the reason is not that the names are lost.
7. **A person is the only shape that can be named, so the only shape that can be found, so agreeing to carry a cost writes down where that cost is — and a cost that nobody holds is a cost that has got nobody.** Both errors refused in one room. **Every institution in this city is a person and every one of them is tired, and that is not a complaint, it is a list.**
8. **The answer to most of what this city cannot settle is not a person and therefore cannot be asked anything.** A number cannot be asked whether it will have it; a page cannot; a person can. **A number is safe and a page is not, and the difference is that a number cannot end anything and a page can, and there is one of the second kind in this city and it is spent.**
9. **The difference between a clerk's two books was nine before the first registered refusal and is ten now, and he could close it alone tonight and did not, and entered that he could.** **A number that nobody has to agree with is the only thing in four hundred miles this city has not tried to turn into a person, and it is the reason nobody can be found, and it is going to come back from Aurel as a correct number about a district with eight people not in it.**
10. **Both names are in ordinary use and neither is explained.** A name that has been given may be repeated by a person who was in the room when it was given, in the local language, and may not be guessed aloud by anybody who was not. **A thing said in a language is a thing that has left your mouth.**
11. **A finding about a failure is the only kind of finding this city has ever written down.** Six lines of count went on a page and not one line of meaning, and **nobody has written anything on the wall, in nine years or in a hundred and ten days, because a wall is the cheapest thing in this city and there is already one.**
12. **A date on a page is a door and not a promise, and the door did not open on the day.** There is no instrument in four hundred miles that can make a household keep a door open either, and this city built that on the same afternoon. **The two institutions in this city that will still be here in four months are the one where nobody asked and the one nobody can carry.**
13. **The twelve names on the second page in the stone are twelve people and not twelve debts, and the page is the only roll in this city that does not go to Aurel, and the copy in a girl's book is headed *it is a copy and the stone does not travel* because a book with twelve names in it is a door into a list.** A list that enters a person twice is a list of a person and not of a debt.
14. **The four who read the join-line are still four and only four and have now disagreed five times since the first of March, and all five entries stand, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken.** A fifth disagreement is not forbidden and no fifth reading is. A dressed join does not narrow; it gets dressed.
15. **The interface has made twenty-nine offers across twelve batches, has registered one refusal, and both of its offers in this batch were accepted in about four seconds by persons who are not Adrian Vale** — a bone-setter and a clerk of the Registry. **The count of refusals in the second book kept at home is still twenty-three and did not move. A field answered by a person goes in one of two books and nobody in this city knows which, and a clerk has now been unable to say which one six times in a hundred and five days.**
16. **Nineteen silences in a row, nineteen for nineteen**, counted silences and not days, and **a day the wall speaks on is not a silence however hard the day was**; it spoke on the fifteenth and the seventeenth.
17. **A person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused, and this is now the second time it has decided a scene in four days** — once for the Hearthguard on the road, and once for a man of twenty who has been fed every day for a hundred and nine days by people who did not ask him whether he wanted the food.
