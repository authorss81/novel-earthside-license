# Volume 02, Batch 0004 — Chapters 81–90: The False Oath

Ten days. Days 81 to 90, which are the fifty-sixth to sixty-fifth day of the new month, and which contain **the last day of the new month and the first of March** and **the last day of the volume's clock.** This batch does one thing and refuses to do it cleanly: **a document in this city's own words arrives, is offered at no cost to anybody, is understood by about nine people and found faultless by about two hundred and thirty, and goes down a river unsigned and unrefused.** Nobody is convicted. The Concord does not lose the lock, the roll, the notary or the gauge. Adrian gains nothing at all.

---

## 1. The batch's spine

1. **A case goes out with the right things in it and the roll goes with a hole in it**, and the emptiest document in the case is the safest one, and a carrier of twenty-four says the reason before she has taken her coat off.
2. **A woman of thirty-eight who is on no roll at all is asked one thing by a person who is not Adrian Vale, and says a daughter's name and its spelling out loud in a room, in two languages, to a person who wrote it down, and says the second half of the cost herself.**
3. **Perry Lask is asked, once, on the named day, and answers in nine words, and the ninth of the nine words is the word for a person.** The three refusals were standing before the question was asked and the answer and the refusals are on the same line.
4. **Elsa Skell says the thing nobody had said: nobody in eighty-three days has asked her whether she wants the field, and she does not know, and she said a number out loud in daylight on the first of March anyway.**
5. **A document arrives in this city's own words, and every clause in it is a clause somebody in the room wrote in the last eighty-four days, and the reason it is not refused is that it is the most durable answer anybody in ninety days has been offered.**
6. **The first of March. The season is settled in at ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty, forty unpaid men turn up, a sixty-four-day-old sentence is said in a room, and the join stops being worked.**
7. **A girl of seventeen finds the batch's finding in nine minutes: a document that costs nobody anything has no people in it, does not have to say what they are called, and has therefore already answered the eleventh question.**
8. **A man of sixty-one who has kept this city's words in a book for nineteen years copied the document out and was never asked to be the holder of anything, and said nine words about not having a man of his own to lose.**
9. **Three people, in three rooms, independently establish that the same thing is true: an undertaking is not in force until a person stands at the top of it, and this one has no person at the top of it.**
10. **Adrian Vale proposes nineteen words that are the old form of the oath with a person in it, a man of forty-two says on the page that he will do it, a list of debts goes inside a stone, and a page goes down a river in a case.**

---

## 2. What was paid

- **A woman described her own work for the first time in the history of this world**, in nine words, in her own words, in daylight, in a room, and the ninth of them is the word for a person.
- **A daughter is named aloud, in two languages, to a person who wrote it down, by her mother, who chose the room.**
- **The roll of strangers went, with a hole in it, and the hole is entered as a place where a number used to be, and the notary who made the gap on purpose has entered that she is not going to be the reason eleven people get a name put on them out of it.**
- **A field was settled in and not paid, and the join stopped being worked, and the day's work is entered as not done, and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody.**
- **Three debts were written down and owed to named people, and none of them was asked for:** the work in a woman's own words, a figure on the end of a cost column, and one question to a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house at Nine Elms.
- **Nineteen words went on a slate on a nail, and a man of forty-two said on the page that the man who proposed them will do it.**

---

## 3. What the batch gained, and it is all paper, stone and nine words

A case on a river. A roll with a hole in it. Four lines in a document that says the work is not written down anywhere. Nineteen words on a slate. Nine words from a woman who will not be asked anything again. Ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty. A person's name in a column that had a figure missing out of the middle of it. A list of debts in a stone, with a price under it, and three people who stepped over it and a boy of nine who did not. And a document that is live.

**None of these is a power and all of them are heavier than a power.**

---

## 4. The batch's turn, and it is not Adrian's

The batch's sharpest reversal belongs to **a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and whose name is not on anything**, including the four names on the levy of the first Wayhouse, because she sells no goods on the road and pays the levy on what the others pull. She stood up in about forty people and said nine sentences, and the third of them is this:

> **That is not because it is true. It is because every sentence in it is a sentence somebody in this city said out loud, and a sentence somebody said out loud cannot be a lie, and that is the entire way it was built.**

And the seventh and the eighth are the finding: **you cannot check it, because checking a document means finding a sentence that is not true, and there is not one; and you cannot refuse it, because refusing a document means being angry at something, and there is nothing in it to be angry at. Those two things are usually the same thing and they are not the same thing here.**

**A page made out of true sentences is harder to argue with than a page that is not, and this city has spent ninety days learning that and about nine of its people learned it too late.** She has no standing and nothing at stake and she said so herself first, before she said anything else, and she has not been thanked and has said she is not going to be one of the people who says she tried.

**Nothing in this batch softens that and nothing in this batch makes it anybody's fault.** The man who could have refused the document in four seconds said out loud in a yard that he wanted to call it a false oath and that he did not know whether it was one, and a keeper of about seventy told him not to use the word, and he said the other thing instead, and the other thing is the batch's real argument: **a sentence somebody has heard is not a promise to that person any more.**

## 5. The rules established in this batch, binding on Batch 0005

1. **A case is a case a carrier is paid for, and a case with nothing in it is a case nobody can charge to carry, and the safest thing in a case is the emptiest thing in it.** The roll went with a hole in it and **a later writer may not present the roll as complete and may not fill the hole.**
2. **A person is not owed the choosing of the room she says a thing in, and nobody is thanked for it.** A name on a page is a thing that can be found, and the woman who said the name said the second half of the cost herself.
3. **The four things a holder of a workway may be asked were put in an order on purpose — three refusals first, the question last — and the answer and the refusals are on the same line because they are the same decision.**
4. **A woman may describe her own work once, in her own words, in daylight, in a room; the description is hers and may be taken back on a date; and nobody may re-ask her.**
5. **A year of certainty is a year of promises, and the promise was that somebody would be standing in a ditch in March.** The level of the ditch is the promise.
6. **The undertaking of the Lantern Concord, seven clauses and a date, in full.** **It is neither refused nor accepted, it is live, and it is voided in Chapter 0092, inside Batch 0005 (Chapters 91–100), on the ground that its own condition was not met.**
7. **A sentence somebody has heard is not a promise to that person any more**, and a page built out of sentences a city has already said to itself hands the whole of them to somebody who did not hear them said.
8. **A guild that does not go home does not get old, does not want a different winter, cannot be refused by the people it costs, and cannot be asked separately about anything.** **That is a lock that a guild can close.**
9. **An undertaking is not in force until a person stands at the top of it and answers for it alone, and this document has no person at the top of it, and that is either the safety or the hole.**
10. **A guild that can be asked has a standing, and a standing cannot be refused, and I know it because we have refused three** — twenty-four words of a woman of seventy, and the batch's finding about the shape.
11. **A document that costs nobody anything has no people in it, does not have to say what they are called, and has therefore already answered the eleventh question.** The answer it gave by accident is the same four words a docker gave eight days earlier.
12. **A page on a nail beside a form is two instruments in one arch; an arch is a place; a place is a room.**
13. **The three descriptions of the shape, said about one document, all at once, by a Hearthguard of forty-four — and the correct ones are the problem, said out loud by the man the shape was first said about, and said again worse, and both readings entered with neither above the other.**
14. **There is nothing here to resign from and a man with nothing to resign is not a man being let go** — now spoken by the man it is about, about a document, and no word of the word *resign* was said by anybody in that yard.
15. **A document with no person in it can be argued about only by people who have time**, entered as what a person said and not as a finding.
16. **Nineteen words, a proposal and not a refusal: put a person at the top of it, and make that person able to say no, out loud, here.** **A man of forty-two said the proposal on the table is the old form of the oath with a person in it, and that the man who proposed it will do it, and the argument about it is on Tuesday.**
17. **A form about a person may not be used on a document that has no person in it.** Entered as an unfinished thing, and unfinished is allowed in this city and is the only word the notary had.
18. **A room which has heard a sentence has not answered it, and a sentence said twice is not a finding, it is a standing, and a standing cannot be refused.** Applied for the first time to a notary rather than to a form.
19. **A document is the four lines for the nine people at the front of the yard.**
20. **About nine people in this city have built the same thing four times in ninety days and none of them has a name for it**, and it is the other half of a docker's sentence that a record is not a fence.
21. **The second page in the shrine asks and names at the same time, and a page which asks and names is a page a person can be angry at, and that is the first time in ninety days this city has written one.** A stone on a boundary is nine inches of protection and the person holding it is nobody's servant.
22. **The cost column is a sentence with a number missing out of the middle of it, and this city has been arguing with the wrong noun for eighty-five days.**
23. **A cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody, on any day, for any reason** — and the day it was written for is the day it was used.
24. **The oldest sentence in this city is that you stand under a gate because somebody owes you and you have not been paid, and it is on a page for the first time at a docker's own asking.**
25. **The refusal count in Renner Sallow's second book ends this batch at twenty-two, unchanged, because both of this batch's fields were answered by nobody at all.**

## 12. One rule worth carrying, written after the review

**An elapsed count inside a chapter is that chapter's own day number subtracted from the day the thing happened, and the day a thing happened has to be looked up rather than remembered.** Three of the hard errors in this pass were the same mistake in three costumes: *seventy-eight* for Odile Ferrand's fifty-six, *sixty-four* for Renna Ord's thirty-nine, and *nine days* for the ten days a page had been in a stone. Two of the three came from subtracting a **new-month ordinal** from a **flood day**, or the other way round, and the third from carrying a figure forward from a note. **A writer who states a day of the new month anywhere near an elapsed count should state the flood day in the same sentence**, which is the habit that would have caught all three.
