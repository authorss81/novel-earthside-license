# Volume 07, Batch 0005 — Chapters 0341–0350, days 341 to 350

**Ten chapters, days 341 to 350, one chapter to one day, the first and the last of them both Mondays, and the Bare Month days twenty-six to thirty-five. This batch writes the volume's climax, its consequence, its resolution and its last image. A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall takes a word off the register-keeper's second slate in chalk with a cloth, in daylight, in a room with a date on the door, in front of about nine people, and after that the word is on no page in the world and never will be. A man of about fifty-two who digs walks eleven miles, puts a barrow against the end of a cut at a stone about nine hundred paces off a shut gate, looks along a mark in the ground from the end of it, does not touch it, and says one sentence that is not an answer. A line is entered in a register with a day on it and nothing under the day and no word on it, recorded as one that may not be dug. Nine boards come off a wall at a shut gate. A man of about sixty with a handcart and a length of rope puts a fence four feet off a line nobody has measured at the top of a road nobody has opened, and nobody sent him and nobody stops him. Nobody got stronger. Nothing was measured, dug, counted, probed or resolved, and no character in any of the ten goes and looks at the line except the digger on day 344, and he looks at it from the end of it and does not touch it.**

## 1. The batch goal, and whether it was met

**The goal was: hold the word for four chapters and then spend it, hold the day-154 sentence out of it, take the word off the page in chalk with a cloth by the man who said it and not by the protagonist, make everything after it worse, send the digger and let him learn nothing, put the only recording in four hundred miles that an office nine hundred miles off cannot ask to have produced on a page, take the boards off the wall, put a fence four feet off a line nobody measured, and end the volume on a Monday — without asking anybody whether anything would go, without naming anybody, without putting a name at the top of anything, without sending a sixth notice, and without measuring anything.**

Eleven things were required and all eleven are on a page.

1. **The shut gate, a lane nobody goes up, and a man with a barrow who is not asked anything.** Chapter 0341. Day 341, a Saturday, the twenty-sixth. The woman of about fifty chalks the twenty-sixth on the post and rubs nothing out. The man of about thirty-eight comes up the lane at about the ninth hour, goes past the gate, puts the barrow down about nine hundred paces off it, stands with his forearms on the handles, looks at one side of the road, does not go in, and comes back down. **In a room over a shop on the low ground in the afternoon he is not asked anything and says the whole of what he did because nobody is going to ask him for it, and he does not say what he stood there for.**
2. **THE CLIMAX. Four sentences and a cloth.** Chapter 0342. Day 342, a Sunday, the twenty-seventh. 3. **Everything after the climax is worse, and the chapter says so in a yard and in a room.** Chapter 0343. Day 343, a Monday, the twenty-eighth. **The naming bought that field one afternoon and it cost that field thirty-two days, and the man of about fifty-seven says the arithmetic out loud in a yard and nobody tells him it is a sad one.**
4. **The digger goes, and comes back with nothing.** Chapter 0344. Day 344, a Tuesday, the twenty-ninth. A barrow against the end of a cut, a look along it from the end, hands kept where they were, and one sentence. **The man of about sixty with a handcart and a length of rope is in the chapter and nobody sent him and he does not put a fence in it.**
5. **A second bar, and a line nobody can administer, and a sixth refusal nobody asked for.** Chapter 0345. Day 345, a Wednesday, the thirtieth. Four streets come together at a gate. 6. **THE RESOLUTION. A line entered, and a man told what he has, and a question nobody answers.** Chapter 0346. Day 346, a Thursday, the thirty-first. **The thirty-first day of that month at the top, underneath it that a straight mark in a field may not be dug, no name, no word for the mark, and the third column left empty on purpose.**

8. **A fence four feet off a line nobody has measured, and it is the most ordinary thing in the world and the most permanent.** Chapter 0348. Day 348, a Saturday, the thirty-third. **The man of about sixty sets out from a row of trees and stops at a stone and does not measure anything, and a fence is a political act, and about four people on that road have worked out that it is the last political act anybody in this flood will perform.**
9. **A room with nothing on the table, a fold, and a person who is not going to be told.** Chapter 0349. Day 349, a Sunday, the thirty-fourth. Tamsin Quill and no other of the ten, the consent fracture visible and not mended, three of the four in the room and the fourth not coming, and the nine words still in a coat.
10. **THE LAST IMAGE, and it is a fence, and the day is a Monday.** Chapter 0350. Day 350, a Monday, the thirty-fifth. Thirteen posts in the ground and three in the cart and eleven withies, a handcart turning at the end of the road, and a man of about fifty-seven who put his hand flat on the second post the way a man does to a gate post.

**The gains are a cloth, four sentences, thirty-two days off a slate, a length of new rope nobody has used, a barrow against the end of a cut, one sentence that is not an answer, a second bar on no roll, a sixth refusal, an entry with an empty third column, nine boards and a bare wall, thirteen posts and a fence. None of them is a power. One of them is the price of a power. All of them are heavier than a power.**
