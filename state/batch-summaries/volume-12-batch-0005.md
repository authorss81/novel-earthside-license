1. **Day 591.** A date was wiped off the outside of a door in a room over a market and the day of that morning was chalked on again under where it had been, and a man who trades nineteen rates named the third bill out loud in about eleven words in his own mouth — that nobody four hundred miles off is going to know a person made that figure, and that the person who made it on foot is not on the paper and is not going to be — and not one of the nine people in that room is the person that bill is about, and nobody thanked him for naming it, and a stool was carried from a wall to the end of a bench by a man nobody in that room has ever asked about anything.
2. **Day 592.** A cord was drawn across the face of a tally-board lying face up on a stack of boards at a salt wharf to mark off a length, and a line of grey grit came off the boards onto the figure on it, and the number on that board is one line harder to read than it was at the seventh hour and about four people a day walk past that yard and not one of them will know it ever was not, and a man held the near edge of it with his own hand the whole time and wanted that figure to be one somebody in this city could check and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that could check it.
3. **Day 593.** A man kept two fingers on the near edge of another man's own board on a table under a window for most of a Saturday morning so that it would not slide, and then asked where the space at the foot of the column between the other two goes, and got an answer that it came from nowhere he was going to say, and nobody in that room asked him about the space again, and nobody in that room asked the man whose two fingers those were about his own board or about the piece of chalk in his pocket, and this was the last morning on which either question could have been put.
4. **Day 594.** A register was carried off a shelf and opened on a table and read down, five figures with a day written beside each, and the six documents in this flood were named one after another in the order they were made in and not one of them was laid beside another or put in one hand or compared out loud, and nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-eight where his own figure came from, he did not say, and nothing says why.
5. **Day 595.** A sheet with a figure of this city's own in the one place under a heading and a line of words under that figure went off a table in a room over a market into a satchel with a flap and down the stair on the round, and nobody in that room opened it and nobody is going to, it is the first thing this flood has sent out of this city with words on it, the man whose figure it is was in the room and is not on it, and the wood of that table is a shade paler where it lay.
6. **Day 596.** A board at one end of a market was washed once at the fourth hour instead of twice, because a man nobody in that row has ever spoken to did one pass with a rag and then did not do the second one, the man who washes that board every morning stood and looked at it and asked nothing, the grit stayed in the grooves along its lower edge, and two stalls along a man put his own hand on that edge to lean past it and carried a mark away with him that he has not washed off and does not know how it got there.
7. **Day 597.** A man carried a satchel with a flap out of this city on the round, through standing water at a near gate and up a stretch of wet road, and did not open it, and a man of about sixty with a handcart stood at that gate and looked at the water and at him and took the cart back the way he had come and said nothing, and about four people came down that road after him and about four of them put a foot in one of his own prints in the wet.
8. **Day 598.** The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, in a room of about nine people, what a person is called in a column on a page when the only thing anybody will ever use the name for is to be able to say where the person was; the room did not hear it, the next line says so, he has not answered it at any hour since, and the three who went down the four hundred miles of that other road on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood out of a party of four are in the same chapter and are not joined to it by anybody.
9. **Day 599.** A man said, in about nine words and for the first time in fifty chapters, that a column like that is where a word goes, and the room did not do it, and the reason nobody wrote in it is in narration and not in a mouth: nobody in that room has ever been told what that column is for, and a person who does not know what a column is for should not write in it, and the book went back on its shelf with that column as empty as it was.
10. **Day 600.** A board was washed in two passes at the fourth hour and the stone above the top edge of it was not washed, a book on a shelf had five figures in one column and a column beside it that nobody has ever been told the purpose of, a slate stood on that shelf with a day across the head of it and nothing under it, a length of new rope lay over the back of a chair and was not lifted, a tally-board lay face up at a salt wharf with a figure of this city's own on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and three barrows went down a four hundred mile road out of a party of four on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road, and no elapsed figure for that road is printed anywhere in this chapter because the plan holds two for it and neither is corrected.
