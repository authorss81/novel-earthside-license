## What these ten days were, in one paragraph, and the story comes first

A woman at the near end of a bench stood up in front of about nine people and said out loud, in her own mouth, that every one of them had been looking at her instead of at the book for the whole of this flood, and that she was the only person in that room who could have said what that column was for, and that nobody had asked her; a man at the far end of that bench disagreed with her for a reason of his own and was not argued out of it, and nobody thanked her, and a woman with a hand-basket stood at the one window from the eighth hour until the light went off it and went back down that stair with what she came up it for still inside the basket. **A man at a salt wharf took a length of cord up off the boards, walked it out along the top of a stack and back, laid it on the top face, and grit came off the boards above and came down onto the cord and stayed on the cord and did not come off that board; a man with a trough on his own shoulder got his hand on the cord and lifted it and the same thing happened again, and he said out loud that he was not going to wipe a board that had been lying face up in the weather since the water came, and nobody thanked him, and the man who owns that board had been asked nothing by anybody in this city and said out loud that he was not going to start this morning.** A man of about thirty-four walked out past the last house with a name on it, stood at a fence of sixteen willow posts and eleven withies with his own hand on the top of the near post, said one thing to the post, and came back without measuring it and without moving a post, and this was the last morning on which anybody in this volume could have asked him about his board or his pocket and nobody asked and the chapter does not say why. A room asked a question all morning and the only person in it who could have answered was not asked, and the man of about thirty-nine said out loud for the third time that he had never asked her, and nobody thanked him for the second time either. **A woman folded a sheet shut along the two folds it came up that road in, pressed the bone down the whole length of each with the heel of her own hand, and gave it to a man with a satchel with a flap, and it went four hundred miles, and nobody in that room opened it, and nobody ever will.** A board was washed once at the fourth hour and went back up on its two nails and was not washed again, and a man two stalls along leaned past it and went away with a mark on his own hand that he has not washed off. A man went down a road with a closed flap to a yard that is not the yard he went to and came back up it with the flap still down. **A man asked one woman what a room is called, in a column, on a page, when the only thing anybody will use the name for is to be able to find the door, and she did not answer it, and the room did not hear it.** A woman took a book off a shelf and looked down the column beside five figures and put it back where it was. **And on the last morning a board was washed once and went back up, a man looked at a fence and came back, a board at a wharf lay face up under a line of grey grit nobody wiped, a book stood on a shelf with five figures in one column and one word near the head of the column beside it, three barrows stood on a four-hundred-mile road, a length of new rope lay over the back of a chair unused, and a man of about thirty-four said one thing to a market row at the ninth hour and nobody thanked him and the volume ends with him walking the length of that row with his right hand in his pocket.**
