# Volume 04, Batch 0001 — Chapters 0151–0170, days 155 to 174

**This is the batch record for the first batch of Volume 04, *The White Ledger*. It is not a close record. The volume's close record is `state/volume-04-close.md` and it is written by the close phase, and it does not exist yet.**

**The batch wrote twenty chapters on twenty consecutive days, day 155 to day 174 of the flood, one chapter to one day, the last day a Sunday. It was checkpointed mid-flight and repaired in a second run; §14 is the record of what that repair did and it is binding on the phase that reads this next.**

---

## 1. The batch goal, and whether it was met

**The goal, as the cards set it: put a working settlement on the page as a fact nobody in this city can argue with, name the place and the gate it is going to take, and make the cost of Adrian's silence visible and dated before the thing it costs has happened.**

**All three halves are on the page and the third is the one that matters.**

- **A settlement that works is on the page as a fact.** Jonah Pike walked nine miles in the rain on day 155 and handed over a printed wage sheet before he said a word, and the sheet said out loud that the ground was taken without asking and that the paper money is worth nothing. He answered Adrian's ninth sentence with nine names of people in a store who have work and are paid this week, **and eight of the nine sentences were the best arguments anybody had made in this city in a hundred and fifty-five days and the ninth one was not an argument at all.** The competence is the argument and the volume did not make him a villain with a schedule.
- **The place and the gate are named, in rooms, in daylight, with dates on them.** Redroot on day 157. The Nine Elms gate on day 158, on a board in chalk, with a keeper's name on it. The Redroot gate board on day 161. The word *commons* written on a board three times on day 169. **Redroot Commons named on day 170 in a room with a door by a woman who did not want to be asked, with the date in chalk on a door, and Adrian not in the room.**
- **The cost of the silence is visible and dated before it happens.** On day 164, in a gateway, in daylight, a man with no mark and on no roster said out loud that the quarter runs out on **day 189**, that the hearing was on day 154, that the interval is thirty-five days, and that **he is going to do nothing about it for twenty-five days on purpose**, and the delay is the length of the quarter exactly. He entered it in his own hand. It is a page and it is not a refusal, and it goes nowhere.

## 2. The four handoff facts as they were used

The batch prompt carried four handoff facts from the Volume 03 close. All four are on the page and none was softened.

1. **The hearing was held on day 154 and it is over, and the next time this city can put anything in front of that office is about five weeks later.** Used as a clock and only as a clock. **Day 189, a Monday, thirty-five days after day 154, is the only thing in the batch running towards something.** It is stated aloud in Chapter 0160 and paid for in Chapter 0170, where the twenty-fourth of the Long Month is the fifteenth day of the twenty-five.
2. **Adrian holds nothing: no mark, no roster, no seat, no wage, no office, and he is the most findable person in this world and has said so himself.** Used, not asserted. **He was offered a share with no post, no roll, no title, no conditions, no date and no way to revoke it, and he did not refuse it in four seconds, and he took four minutes, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said out loud that four minutes is a new thing.** He also asked a question for the first time in a hundred and sixty-six days, and the woman he asked it to answered it in one word that is not a noun.
3. **Redroot had never been named in prose and is not on the Chapter 0010 list of the hamlets on the shared gates.** **Chapter 0153 is the chapter that says the name out loud once, at a gate, to the safest person in four hundred miles, and says why before she says it, and Chapter 0154 is the chapter that finds the hole in the list: Redroot is not on it and the water-count has known that since the tenth day of this flood and has never once asked why.**
4. **There is no water gate on a page, and the volume's decision is that what a household has of a gate is the days on it.** **Chapter 0154 puts it in a mouth, in the local language, at a junction, in daylight: a gate is not owned and cannot be bought; what a household has is the days, and a board, and a keeping of it, and a name in a speaker's record; and the only way a household loses a gate is by agreeing, and if it gives the days it still has the gate.** That sentence is the reason the volume's climax is recoverable and it was said once and never improved on.

## 3. The date-form amendment, and where it was established

**The calendar now has four forms, and the fourth was established on the page in Chapter 0152, day 156, in a room over a market, in daylight, with a date on the door.**

1. *The Nth day of this flood* — days 1 to 40.
2. *The Nth day of the new month* — days 26 to 90, so the Nth is day 25 + N.
3. *The Nth day of the month after this one* — days 91 to 155, so the Nth is day 90 + N. **Chapter 0151 is the only chapter in this batch that uses the third form, and it is the sixty-fifth and last day of it.**
4. *The Nth day of the Long Month* — **the Nth is day 155 + N, so the first day of the Long Month is day 156, a Wednesday, and its sixty-fifth day is day 220.** Established in Chapter 0152 and registered at `bible/power-system.md` §24.

**Two things about the fourth form are on the page and are the reason it was worth inventing.** A notary of fifty gave nine sentences on why a name invented by a notary is a name an office can use, and she was right, and the room still did it anyway and she entered that she understood the room when she said no. And **the date in the new form was tried three times on one door in one day — by the room, by a market underneath, and by a woman of about fifty who counts water — and nobody washed it off, and the door carried two dates for a whole day while about two hundred and forty people came up a stair to look at it.**

## 4. The pressure rotation as it actually came out

The cards predicted five chapters of physical work, one of recovery, three of relationship that are not about a form, four of discovery, and eight of political or procedural pressure, and said the eight was more than a third and should be said rather than disguised. **What came out matches the count and the texture claim did not hold.**

- **Physical work: 0155, 0159, 0162, 0165, 0170** — five, as planned. Two hours in a queue in the wet and nine sacks; a line cut into a channel with a barrow going over; one day of a wage paid out of a store to nine of thirty-nine men; nine days of racks, a channel cleared, a queue fed; and a Sunday on a flat with a bell on a post.
- **Recovery: 0161** — one, as planned, and it is the only chapter in the batch where nothing happens for about nine hours and the chapter does not apologise for it.
- **Relationship, not a form: 0157, 0160, 0168** — three, as planned. A kitchen at Redroot and a bowl of hot water. A gateway and a season of a field paid by strangers. Six hours copying a better record and giving it away.
- **Discovery: 0153, 0154, 0156, 0158** — four, as planned.
- **Political or procedural: 0151, 0152, 0163, 0164, 0166, 0167, 0169** — seven, and the eighth chapter the cards counted as political is Chapter 0170, which is a cost and a decision and is in the physical column because the day is a day of racks.

**The texture claim is the finding: five of the eight do not turn on a form being read in a room, they turn on a person saying a number out loud in a market or at a gate, and the two chapters where procedure is the subject (0152 and 0167) are the two where it is the right subject. **What the cards did not predict, and what the batch found, is that the batch's real change of texture is not in the rotation at all. It is that five chapters end on an object rather than on a date and a list: a printed sheet in a coat, a slate nobody wrote on, a figure on the end of a line, a bell on a post, a date in chalk that the rain took.**

## 6. What the twenty chapters put on the page that a later writer must hold

1. **A water gate is not owned.** What a household has is **the days on it** — which days of which week that household's share may be let through — **and a board, and a keeping of it, and a name in a speaker's record.** The gate itself stays where it is. **The only way a household loses a gate is by agreeing to the days, and if it gives the days it still has the gate.** This is the volume's engine and it was said once, in Chapter 0154.
2. **The Reach's record of who may use a water gate is a list of persons, and it has kept one for nine years, and every name on it is alive, and nobody in this city has ever been angry about it.** Chapter 0154 holds this without flinching and without resolving it.
3. **The keeper of a gate is not on that list, because nobody ever asked her, and the reason she said no is that the day her name goes on that list the gate stops being hers and becomes the Reach's.** Chapter 0154. **She has kept a gate for nine years and has a thing nobody in four hundred miles can take off her, and she has never been able to say whether that is a good answer.**
4. **The White Ledger's wage page carries the payer's name and not the paid man's.** The settlement arrived at the shape on its own, in nine weeks, and **nobody in this city told them.** Chapter 0160 says so in a mouth.
5. **A wage paid out of somebody's own pocket is a favour and a favour is a thing that stops. A wage paid out of a store by people who owe it is not a favour, and stopping is a different act.** Chapter 0160, and the settlement's man had learned the difference from a man of about thirty-four in a market in this city and neither of them thought of it as a lesson.
6. **A workway read out with its full cost before the accepting, in daylight, with a date on it, is lawful, is probably not a breach, and is the reason the keeper of the Redroot gate said yes.** **A Warden is a groundkeeper: it reinforces a claimed place, organises defence, resists involuntary displacement, it must defend the place and its residents, and abandoning a claimed ground is a matter of the holder's convenience.** Chapter 0158.
7. **A duty to a place is the only wage anybody in this world has ever been able to offer anybody.** Chapter 0158, said by the keeper, nine sentences, and the ninth one asked the person taking the workway to say the cost out loud again with the board in the room.
8. **Redroot Commons exists as of day 170**, in about nine words and a date in chalk on a door, in a room with a door, in daylight, in about nine people. **It has no roll, no officer, no seat and no standing, and nobody's workway is a term of it, and no party to it may be asked to give one up.**
9. **A name said out loud that is a place a person can be found in.** Jonah Pike gave a man of thirty-two the name **the witness** in nine sentences on day 173, in daylight, in a room over a market, and **it is the first time in this book that the two halves of that rule have come apart, and the finding of the chapter is that being findable is not a penalty for being real. It is the price of it.**
10. **The word *order* has been arrived at nine times in this book by a man from another world and by a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read, and neither of them has ever heard of the other, and the first time the two have been in the same room is Chapter 0169 and no character in it knows.**
