# Volume 01 Batch 0005 Summary — Choice at the Lock (Chapters 41–47)

## What happened

Seven days, one burial, one rule taken off a board in daylight, one ordinary tide, one more attractive offer, one question nobody had asked, and a man saying *yes* in a room to a thing he says will fail.

The batch opens on the Sunday that Owen Park is buried and the keeper's board comes off, and it ends on the Saturday at about the eleventh hour of the night with a man in the lower gallery of a lock who has committed to a working that is on the board for Monday and has not been done. **The choice is closed in this batch and the working is not performed in this batch**, and that is the point of the split: the closing distributed working is Chapters 48–50 and nothing in these seven chapters pretends to be it.

**Chapter 41 is a procedure and a collision.** The burial is in the ordinary ground behind the ordinary wall, which is Hettis Roke's grandfather's ground, which now holds twelve graves of which eight have no name stone. The name is cut twice, in two languages, in two hands, and **the second line is upside down because Roke is not a mason and nobody in that ground was going to come and say so to him.** Hesper Saye reads the four pages in about nineteen minutes in full and does not cry, and about eleven people in that ground do not know what to do with their hands because a reading is not a ceremony. **A man is a scar in a person** is in the account, it is Owen's own sentence, and Roke says the other half of it at the grave and Vask supplies the mason's reason. At about the seventh hour, in daylight, in front of about a hundred and forty people, **Nyla Ferrow washes her own three lines off her own board with a cloth and a bucket and reads out what comes off before she takes it off — and does not wash off the sentence a man wrote about himself, because washing a man's own writing off a board is a decision and not a keeping** — and **Adrian washes his own line off himself with the same cloth** and adds to the book that he wrote it because a man asked for the true reason and he did not have his. Six hours later, in a converted salt store, about forty people review the post: **nine for keeping, nine for dropping**, and Adrian says out loud that **two posts are the same shape of rule, only one of them is still standing, and he is the one who has to say so**, and that the post is not a mechanism and is a liability with a man in it — **and Sera Vail stops him in the middle of it and makes him say it as what he said, because the sentence is a finding and findings may not be entered.** The seat is **readmitted for a final term ending on the forty-ninth day** and the post is **changed** from *the ground does not begin without him* to *the ground may not begin without his being told of it and asked for, and he is not the answer if he cannot be got*. He asks in the room to be removed from the terms of the post and **is not**, and the notary enters that she cannot enter the request, and why. He signs nine pages **having read them, in eleven minutes, cost column included** — the first entry he has signed in this city having read it, and the whole counterweight to the eleventh page of the thirty-sixth. And in a doorway, **Sabra Vint asks him about his eight stones and he gives her a true answer that is not the answer**, and she says: *I have never once been answered with a sentence about somebody else's nine years.*

**Chapter 42 is a city given one night and it wants the wrong thing in eleven minutes.** Kerrow reads three instruments. The room wants the Crown's single-author containment — ninety hands, and **eleven people put a hand up for it before it had finished being read** — and it wants it because there is a stipend in it and a lawyer named in it, and about nine people in the room know that is not a reason and both of those are the reason. **Tamsin Quill reads the fortieth's entry aloud twice and says one word: *standing*** — a thing whose answer cannot be answered, in the third line of **the old form of the maintenance oath, which binds the gate and the standing of the man in it, was taken out of the oath in Saltwake sixty years ago after a man in it died**, and which is in the Registry's notice, and in the Crown's third instrument, and in a hundred and thirty words a man in a good coat said in a yard. **It is not in one office and it is not in one office's coat.** The old form's death is spoken about in full for the first time in this city, in a hall, and four people who can still say it were in the room and nobody asked them. A pale field arrives and is refused in under a second: **the first of the sixteen to put nothing at all in the place where a cost goes.** The first question anybody asks Adrian when the city has a night is **is anybody near the scar**, and the answer is a tarpaulin and two new boards and a date chalked on the underside — and a boy of nineteen who stands on that tarpaulin every morning because the rule says whoever is nearest.

**Chapter 43 is an ordinary tide and a number, and the man with the chalk is a boy.** Hesk, who has never once said the four words under the near gate, does not go under it. The day's work is a staging and a prop, and **a mason of thirty-four years enters his own decision in his own hand at the seventh hour, before the number is read, and Vask enters it and Adrian does not ask her to**, and he gives two true sentences about whether he has thought about the pale edge. The **twenty-third reading is eight and seven-eighths**, a quarter of an inch in one step, and **Wenna Cray arrives with a slate and asks in front of nineteen men whether he is going to enter it as a fact or as a fault, because a fault can be given to somebody and a fact can only be carried** — and about nine people in that gallery had already decided the number was a boy's, and Adrian asks the first of them to say so out loud, and nobody does, and the boy asks for the first time he asked for the rule to be left in the book. **Jonah Pike is on the wall with a hauling line and no standing in the building and says one sentence and holds the line for two hours:** *the man who cannot be replaced is not a holder, he is a bottleneck with a name on it, and the only reason nobody has broken it yet is that nobody has wanted to.* **Ovin Sarr, nineteen, writes *works* in the last column of the tide table with a stick of chalk and asks whether it is the right word, and is told it is the only one anybody has ever written in that column, and says it is not the only word.** And he is given the bottom field as a day's work and refuses it in nine seconds in front of about forty people: *a field is not a day's work; a field is a thing a man is asked for again next season.* The debt is entered for the sixth time and the notary says the half she has never said: **a column re-entered six times in a month is a column this city can afford, and the reason is that about four hundred people have never met the man the column is in.**

**Chapter 44 is the turn, and the offer has the Crown taken out of it.** For about two hours, with no document, in a room in a house with a burnt lintel that Sabra Vint has let him come up for the first time in nine years, **Ivenn Marrow offers the third option in a form that costs this city nothing at all**: the containment held by Adrian, no workway, no mark, no stipend, no advocate, no seal, no office, **no Crown**, entered in this city's own book, with his own hand under it that the Crown has no part in it, and Adrian's answer not answerable by any other person in Saltwake. **He does not ask for obedience and he does not ask for a mark and he asks for a seat** — and a seat is a place to be angry at and has to be legible to a person who does not like you, and that is the one argument in this city Adrian could not get round and did not say so for two hours. Adrian refuses in a yard in about two minutes with two witnesses and says out loud the sentence he has been carrying since the thirty-eighth: **a man in a good coat asked me for the true reason and I wrote mine, and a man on a table told me the difference between an argument and a record, and it took him four minutes and it took me nineteen days, and you have been in this city for eight weeks and you did not have to wait for a man to die to work it out.** Marrow's face does not do anything, which Roke enters as the first time in nineteen years he has seen a face do nothing on a true thing. He answers in four seconds: **"I did not have a man to die. I have nineteen years of other people's records, and a great many of them are about men who did not die."** And Adrian writes that **a man who has kept the record for nineteen years and is asking to be the only hand that answers is not asking for a seat**, and that he does not have a name for it and is not going to invent one in a room, because a quarter of a thing is not a thing. **Sabra Vint is asked what she thinks because she is the only person in that yard who can be asked for nothing, and says the true thing about her own paper: she writes the shapes and has never once written what she thinks the shapes mean, and somebody has to be the one who writes that down, and she is sixty-one and would like to stop.** Peth Lomas thanks him for the other one in public and says he is sorry about it. And at the ninth hour, two hundred steps above him, **she says the number nineteen to a man on a road for the first time in nine years, and he says goodnight, and she writes in the margin: said it. Nobody answered. Go home.**

**Chapter 45 is the discovery, and it is that the old lock can be held by a city, and the price is a crew, and the crew is the wall.** Tamsin Quill gives her reason for keeping the word *standing* out of the book — she said it in a room of two hundred and forty before she had read it in a book, and it costs about nine months, and she is not going to be the fifth person to find that out — and **she asked for the non-entry herself and entered that she asked and not that she was asked.** On the walk-round, at the cap of an old landing, Adrian looks at the socket for four minutes and can read nothing, and Renna Ord enters that she had been waiting nine days for somebody to try to use him for it. **Tamsin proposes that the ordinary work of the Lock of Salt *is* the working that closes the join, and that the hand in it is the crew and not a man, which has no instance in this world.** Vask gives the batch's spine in two sentences: **a floor that has come open once is not a pattern, it is a hole**, and **the forty words under the near gate are the toll and they are the pattern, and a city cannot spend a thing and be its own foundation at the same time** — and the cost of them is not a season but the **year of certainty** gone out of a hundred and forty acres, and the four villages whose gates cannot be promised are four miles away and are not in the group. Renna Ord names the price: forty men twice a day indefinitely, **four of the forty hurt before March, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, and no instrument can stop it**, four people in the wrong weather with a gauge before every tide, and **the day the bottom field is not paid in March, this stops, and the join stops being worked, and the join will be where it is** — and **you do not get a door that is not somebody's field.** And then the condition that makes it plural: **nobody can open it; forty men turn up or it does not happen; nine named persons can stop it and one of the nine named herself; a door with no handle in it.** Adrian says **I cannot promise you that it will close** before anybody asks him, because Renna Ord asked him to. On the Friday, **the two documents are put side by side in a yard and Adrian reads the notary's entry of the fortieth aloud first and says she was right**, and there are three instruments of that shape in this city in five weeks and only two of them are the Crown's. **About ninety people refuse the Crown's third instrument in public in nine minutes and the refusal cannot be entered as a refusal of the city**, because ninety of the ninety are owed money and a refusal a person cannot afford is not a refusal — it goes in as ninety statements of error, and Adrian reads out the fifth clause of the Registry's notice and says he did not write it and that a woman of this city read it to a dying man. **Jonah Pike argues for the Crown's form in nine sentences and is right in eight of them**, and Adrian says the tenth, which is that a Crown advocate is a man who is expensive and he is not on this side of the water — and then says to Jonah that he is not wrong to want a structure and that this city does not have to want it. **And then a flood-creel woman of about forty asks, in four words, whether the crew knows, and nobody in that yard had asked, and three people who had known for a month all say the same eleven words: we had not been asked and we were not going to say.**

**Chapter 46 is the midpoint and it is the crew, and the form of the answer is Renna Ord's and not Adrian's.** **Hesk says the four words under the near gate for the first time in his life at the sixth hour of the morning, before anybody has asked him for anything**, and Renna Ord notices and says nothing and enters why: a woman's noticing a man's first time saying a thing is the oldest trick there is. The ask is made in a chapel in front of about two hundred and forty people **and the crew of forty is not in the room**, and about four of the people putting the ask together knew it. **At the sixth hour of the evening, under the near gate, with forty-one in the room, the crew says no in about nine minutes**, and the reason is eleven words old and not written down: **a thing nobody has asked for cannot be owed, and a thing that cannot be owed cannot be paid, and a thing that cannot be paid is a tax.** Hesk says nobody in this city has ever asked him anything and asks to be asked. Renna Ord then says what she has been waiting five weeks to say: **the owing is not the fairness, the owing is the mechanism**; a shift is a thing the city owes, on a roll with forty names and **no name at the top of it**; a man who has not been paid does not come and there is no entry and no fault and nobody goes to anybody's door; and the city's credit is a field four miles away. **At the eighth hour they say yes: thirty-seven for, three against, one abstention, with all three against entered by name** — Hesk, who said the words that morning and voted no fourteen hours later anyway, Ovin Sarr, and Ferris, whose reason is the shortest in the book. The six conditions go in that night: no single person may open or suspend it; four inspectors and a gauge before every tide; a flood-season review with a date on the page before the day; a right to be asked before a day's work is added; a roll with forty names and no name at the top, owed by the city; and **nobody whose standing comes from the holder of the pull may open, inspect, sign or suspend it, and the holder of the pull is not on the roll, at his own request, in his own hand, with the date on it.** That condition takes **two hours and ten minutes** to be accepted because nine people think he is building a martyr; he cannot say the true reason and has entered that he has been the fair reason for five weeks; Tamsin Quill declines twice, saying a sentence in a room is not a wall; and **Priya Shah gets it accepted in forty seconds with a document sentence, and asks that it be entered that he did not think of it in two hours and that he does not think of things that are about his own death, and that this is a fault and not a virtue.** **About a minute after the sixth condition is entered, a pale field appears: a person struck out of a roster may be entered again by a person who remains on it, and the person who enters him is answerable for him** — the Crown's *a position may be filled by a person who can hold it* reformulated, and the first of the sixteen that names a process instead of a person. Adrian refuses it in under a second: **answerable is not the opposite of possible.** Renner Sallow says *it is now the eighth.* And at about the second hour of the morning, the loft wall's **fourth column is on the ninth day of eleven** and **about nine people in that room are reading it as the group finishing, as the dead man's work going to the lock crews, and as somebody about to be asked to be in charge of it, and nobody in that room pointed at the column or said the word column**, and Adrian writes that the wall does not care what he meant.

**Chapter 47 is the batch climax, and the sentence is not *no*, and it has never been *no*.** **About forty people ask him to promise** and he opens his mouth about eleven times in nineteen minutes and does not say the word, and **Priya Shah takes it off him in English in front of about two hundred people: do not promise, promises have no date and no price and nobody can check them in four months, sign the paper** — and the reason is a promise binds one person to another person and there is no page, and the only man she has watched make a promise in this city is under a board with a lease he never met. **Wenna Cray enters the third statement of error in the history of the book, against a person, naming him, four sentences, all of them correct**, and is answered by **I stand by it and I can say no more** — with the admission, said aloud, that on the fortieth night he refused a pale field and called it a version lock with better manners and **was right about the field**, and that he is using the clause today, and that both are true and he does not know which matters more. **Marrow is in the room for nine hours and is the most useful man in it and says nothing wrong**, and Adrian writes nine lines about it and can tell nothing at all. Then Adrian says **I do not know** first, unprompted; **the crew is the wall and a wall cannot be a price for very long** second; and then the sentence — **yes, and here is what it will cost, and I am the one who will have to say so every time** — and stops for four seconds, which Vask entered as the only four seconds in the history of this book she has wanted in a book, and says out loud that he believes it will fail, and begins a sentence about a man in a good coat and **stops and does not finish it**. He mispronounces *charter* in a yard and **Tamsin corrects him in front of about two hundred and forty people** — the fourth time, and she says it is not going to stop being fair and it is going to keep costing him a room. **Ottoline Vask names the instrument: it is the same shape as the five words in that yard, it is a widening and not a rota, and if a name goes at the top of it I will read it out in a yard.** He signs nine pages having read all nine in about nineteen minutes, reading the part about the forty men twice. The **ninth page is entered and sealed** with the work **on the board for the forty-ninth day and not done**, the main breach open, the ground under the chapel a black mark in a place and not repaired, and a man in the ordinary ground under a name he chose himself — and **the Registry's demand is answered a second time**, in nine lines, not in the form required, with the notary asking a clerk in Aurel to notice which of the two answers has a price at the end of it. At about the tenth hour of the night he stands on the chapel stone and **it says nothing**, and he thinks, and does not say, that the twelve words it said on the twenty-fifth day describe the Lock of Salt, and has told a notary once and no one else. At the bottom of the two hundred steps he stops at a form on a stair wall with a refusal clause in it, four feet from a wall with an empty column in it, and there is not one instrument in this world that connects them. In the lower gallery the number is unmoved, the box against the forty-ninth day is empty, and a man who has said *yes* in a room does not go up the two hundred steps.

## Deviations from the batch cards (deliberate, recorded)

1. **Seven chapters, not ten, and the split is the point.** The volume outline splits the remainder in two and the next batch is three chapters and then a close. **Nothing in these seven chapters performs the working.** The working is on the board for the forty-ninth day of the flood and Chapter 47 ends with an empty box and a man who has not gone up the steps.
2. **Chapter 41 carries both halves of its card, and the order is wash then review then graveside.** The burial is set in the morning and the reading is at the second hour of the afternoon; the board comes off at about the seventh hour and the review is taken at about the tenth; and the card's ending image — the board, clean, with the keeper's four words and her fifth back on it and nothing else — is at about the eighth hour of the evening. The consequence inside the card, **a document Adrian signs having read it**, is the counterweight to the thirty-sixth and it is the review itself.
3. **The keeper's three lines and Adrian's line both come off the board, in the same hour, and only one of them was asked for.** The card only required the rule about a person to come off. **Adrian takes his own sentence off himself with the same cloth**, after reading it aloud, and the addition *I have written it here because a man asked me for the true reason and I did not have his* goes into the book. This is deliberate and it is the chapter's argument made physical: two rules about people came off one board in daylight, and the one nobody asked for is the one that has to be read aloud before it is taken down.
4. **The post is not simply kept or dropped.** It is **readmitted for a final term ending on the forty-ninth with the terms changed**, and the change is the batch's finding: notice replaces presence, and *he is not the answer if he cannot be got*. The card allowed either and said whichever it is had to be earned; this one is earned by nine people arguing each way in a room of forty and by the collision with a board washed four hours earlier.
5. **The ninth person who argued for keeping the post is not in the card and the count is nine and nine.** Ilda Marsh, Delya Crane, Marek Osley, Aldis Kerrow, Hesk, Sesh Brack, Renna Ord, Nell Bray, Nyla Ferrow, Peth Lomas against, with Wenna Cray, Osten Fenn, Vask, Sabra Vint and Saye. **Marek Osley supplies the sentence that turns the argument — a liability can be got rid of in a room and only a liability can be got rid of in a room** — and that sentence is the reason the post is a different problem from a mechanism.
6. **The word Tamsin says in Chapter 42 is *standing*, and it resolves into the old form of the maintenance oath.** The card required a person who reads Crown Law for a living to read a document a second time and say one word that is neither Marrow's nor the Choir's. The word is on the page in the third line of **the oath this whole book has been quoting for forty chapters**, it is why that form was stopped sixty years ago, and it is in both Crown documents and in the yard sentence. **This is the batch's central technical discovery and it is not new lore: it is a thing a mason and a keeper have been standing next to for four hundred years.**
7. **Marrow's offer in Chapter 44 is a local instrument, not a Crown one, and that is the point.** It is cheaper, better, cheaper still, and it asks for nothing of Aurel, and the only thing wrong with it is the word. **A room cannot argue with it**, because every objection in this city has been about who pays and this costs nobody anything, and the batch's answer is that the objection is not about who pays.
8. **Chapter 45 covers Thursday and Friday, Chapter 46 covers Friday night and the small hours of Saturday, and Chapter 47 is the Saturday.** No event has two dates. Chapter 46's loft is at about the second hour of the morning of the twenty-second day of the new month and Chapter 47 opens at the first light of that same day.
9. **The two pale fields are the fifteenth and sixteenth offers, and Renner Sallow is in the room for the second one.** The card required a field in Chapter 42 and allowed others. **There are two and no more**, and the count of what a clerk in Aurel has seen is now entered on the page and is eight, and he has asked for it to be kept.
10. **Chapter 47 is the longest chapter in the batch at about 6,990 words and nothing in it is ornament.** It carries the read-back, the forty promises, Priya's rule against a promise, Wenna Cray's third statement of error, the answer by standing-by-it, Marrow's nine useful hours, the sentence, the four seconds, the unfinished sentence about Marrow, the *charter* correction, Vask's naming of the instrument, the nine pages read and signed, the ninth page, the second answer to the Registry, the silent stone, the form on the stair wall, and the gallery.
11. **Chapter 41 runs slightly over the ordinary range at about 6,700 words**, which is where Batch 0004's Chapter 0039 sat, and it was trimmed by paragraph and by re-wording rather than by cutting a beat. Chapter 42 is about 6,140; Chapter 43 about 5,135; Chapter 44 about 5,870; Chapter 45 about 6,460; Chapter 46 about 5,350.

## What the next batch inherits
