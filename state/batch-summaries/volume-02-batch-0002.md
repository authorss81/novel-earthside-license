# Volume 02, Batch 0002 — Chapters 61–70: The Silver and the Beds

Ten days. Days 61 to 70, which are the thirty-sixth to the forty-fifth day of the new month, and which end **five days before the flood-season review.** This batch does one thing and does it badly, in public, with the questions on the page: **a city writes down the questions it is going to be reviewed against, and finds out while writing them what it has already agreed to.**

---

## 1. The batch's spine

1. **Tamsin Quill's twenty-four words are acted on.** Four kitchens, a salt factor, a woman of thirty-four with a bar, and **nineteen lines that are a widening and not a rota** — what a person in this city may be asked to turn up for, at what notice, at the ordinary rate, with no name at the top of anything, and with a line that says a person may refuse and the refusal does not stop the line being offered again on a different day. The room's word is not *work*; it is **turning up.** About nine people understood. About two hundred and thirty thought it was a job.
2. **The second list has not gone into a wooden case since the third week of the new month, and the reason is a box, not a conspiracy.** The roll is written by hand, the hand is the notary's, and she did not fill in a column, and eleven days later a licensed carrier came to a counter and waited an hour and went away. **A roll is the only thing in this world that makes a person findable, and being on no roll is at the same time the protection and the disappearance. A person cannot have one without the other.**
3. **There is no silver in this city that a city can use.** About forty ounces in eleven private places, about eleven people who know the total, and the reason none of them has said it out loud is that saying the number is making a list. **The credit of this city is not a chest of silver. It is a list of about eleven people who each believe they are the reason something happened.**
4. **The first Wayhouse exists, on a page, with a date**, and it was nearly lost to a board in chalk in a yard. **A board anybody can read is a thing anybody can copy.**
5. **The first mixed trade happened on a floor, badly, in the wrong weather.** Four transactions, three mistakes and one habit, and a woman of fifty-eight was paid in salt and said nine words and the ninth of them is the name of the metal the fee is in.
6. **The batch's turn.** The Crown accepts salt for a silver fee, at a generous rate, on the same evening, in the form required. **A Crown office that will take salt for silver is a Crown office that wants salt, and the district that can pay a fee in the only thing it has is a district whose good faith now has a price on it and a buyer.** The salt was not sent. The finding stands anyway.
7. **The questions got written.** Four wrong drafts, eleven questions, a second page headed *what we are not asking and why* that is longer and is part of the instrument, and an eleventh question on the back of a slate, four words long, written by a woman who cannot read, **which is the only question on the page that could lose the licence on its own.**

---

## 2. What was paid

- **A bed that nobody slept in.** The two beds in Silt Row are the test case and they fail on the first morning, because the clause that protects a household from being asked again is the same clause that makes a person who did not turn up unfindable. **The most uncomfortable page written in sixty days.**
- **A woman of twenty-nine of the second list worked a thing out in an hour and told a woman of twenty-four and not the man**, because she did not know whether she was complaining or asking. She knew on the forty-fifth and said it to him, and nobody offered her a line for it except a line four lines high on a slate.
- **A man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set was put on the nineteenth line of a page about what a person may be asked to turn up for**, and Adrian Vale watched it happen and wrote down that this is what he does instead of asking. The shoulder does not go and no scene made it whole.
- **Nine Elms refused for the third time and the refusal was affordable**, because a Crown factor used the Crown's own fifth clause *correctly* to advance the quarter against the district's own reeds. **The village is right and cannot afford to be right.** The village's winter is worse than it was on the eleventh day of the new month.
- **A housekeeper's board came down in daylight and four householders' names went into a book with a date.** About two hundred and forty people watched a thing be made legal and nobody said the word.

---

## 3. What the batch gained, and it is all paper

**Nineteen lines** of what a person in this city may be asked to turn up for. **A form with a date on it** asking whether a person on no roll at all wishes to be described, signed by about nine and not signed by about nine. **The first Wayhouse** — a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, paid by a levy on the road's trade at a rate the traders agreed, kept by a named household with a date, open to a list of nobody, closable by its own household, not a Crown instrument, and not a page anybody may carry to Aurel. **Eleven questions and a second page of nineteen reasons for not asking**, both going to Aurel. **A slate on a nail.** **Four transactions entered as three mistakes and one habit.** **A roll of strangers with a hole in it.** **A line in a notary's hand: that the office has been told what this district has, and this district has been told what the office will take.**

**None of these is a power and all of them are heavier than a power.**

---

## 4. The batch's turn, and it is the Crown's

> **An office that will take your goods for its fee is telling you what it thinks you are, and it is also telling you what it thinks you will be.**

Thirty words, said in nine seconds by the second clerk of the Registry at Aurel, in a room, and entered in full in both languages, and **it is the first time he has ever written a sentence like it and he entered that he had not.** Adrian Vale said, in nine words, that he had wanted that sentence for four months and that he wanted it written in a clerk's hand and not in his own.

**And the twenty-first field is the best of the twenty-one and it puts nothing at all where a cost goes.** It is not a workway. It is an offer to **describe a place**: the district may be published as a district that pays in kind and will not be charged a fee again. It contains no lie. It is a mechanism, and it was refused in under a second in three parts, and the third part is the one that matters.

## 5. The rules established in this batch, binding on Batch 0003

1. **A review's questions are not a list of subjects. A review's questions are a decision about what the answer is allowed to be, and the person who writes them has done something to the door that no amount of daylight undoes.** Chapter 0068.
2. **The difference between a question a city asks when it wants to keep a door and a question a city asks when it wants to be able to close one is not in the tense and is not in the mood. It is in who is able to answer.** Sera Vail, nine words, twenty-six years of Crown procedure.
3. **A second page headed *what we are not asking and why* is part of the instrument and not an annexe**, and if the instrument is reviewed without it, the review is reviewed without it too. Nineteen reasons, in nineteen hands.
4. **A page about what a city is not asking, sent by itself, is a thing an office can answer in one line; sent beside eleven questions it is a thing an office has to answer in a paragraph.** Vail chose the longer answer, for them, against herself, in nine seconds, and did not enjoy it.
5. **A Wayhouse is a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, paid by a levy on the road's trade, kept by a named household, open to a list of nobody, closable by its own household, not a Crown instrument, and six things of which the sixth — nobody may be turned away for asking what it is — is the only reason it will still be there in four months.** Local law in this book means somebody in the room had to say no to something, and four of the six are refusals.
6. **A board anybody can read is a thing anybody can copy**, and moving a thing off a wall onto a page with a date is the only legality mechanism this city has found. Nobody said the word *legal* and about nine people had it in their heads.
7. **A description is a removal with a column for where the person is**, and the column for where a *place* is is very wide and blank at both ends, and **a thing an office knows it can make a district do is a mechanism and not a favour and not a courtesy.**
8. **A Crown fee is a statement about what an office thinks a district is, and an office that will take payment in kind has learned what the district has and is pricing its good faith.** The rate was generous and the generosity is the finding.
9. **A form written by a city that has decided to be unhelpful and a form written by a city that has nothing look the same on the page, and the page is what an office reads.** The honest useless form put a Crown officer in a village in four days, and Priya Shah said in nine sentences that she is proud of it and afraid of it, in that order, and has now said both twice.
10. **A refusal which costs other people money is a decision about other people, and the Crown's own fifth clause can be used to help a village, and a correct instrument used correctly can do damage, and no instrument in this world can make a correct thing expensive.**
11. **Being useful is not safe and is not unsafe. It is a description.** Nadia Brandt, twenty-nine, to two men in a room, and both of them were arguing about the wrong thing.
12. **The bar on Adrian's page is a bar and not a wall, and it did not reach the twentieth field either — and a page read out loud by nine people with no authority stopped it in nine seconds. A page is not a rule. A page does not stand up on its own. The next time there may not be a room.** This is the pre-echo of Chapter 0075.
13. **Adrian is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, and has gained nothing in seventy days.** He performed no working in this batch. He is on no roster. He has put his own name in one Crown column headed *person to be asked* and has been offered the one thing he has never offered anybody — to be asked — and has been refused it in four seconds, correctly.

## 6. What must not be re-litigated in Batch 0003

- **Owen Park is dead, buried, and his burial term was formally closed on the twenty-fifth day of the new month of Volume 01.** Nothing in this batch touched it, softened it, summarised it or referred to it, and nothing in Batch 0003 may. **Nobody may write *he died because*.** A form may not shorten a record because a form is small. His scalp is about sixty days unclosed and the shoulder is in a grave.
- **Dante Kuo's right shoulder went on the thirty-first day of the flood, was reduced twice, cannot be set, and did not go in this batch. No scene may make him die and no scene may make him whole.** The man of the flood crew who complained about a shoulder in the fifth week is a different man and is in a different book, and the two may never be put in one sentence.
- **The measurement column is not continuous.** Twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's own hand before the number was read, **one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken and was not taken on the fortieth or the forty-first or the forty-fifth day of the new month.** A dressed join does not narrow; it gets dressed. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent, and has never said a word.
- **The chapel stone is silent and unrepaired and did not speak in this batch.** The ground under the chapel is **SCARRED**, is not repaired, and **the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood.** The form on the nail has **four items, with the price of the salt written under them, and a fifth item added on the forty-ninth day of this flood, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room.** The numbering is Volume 01's and may not be changed: see `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0048.md` item Four and `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0049.md`. **The price of the salt is not a fifth of the four.**
- **The knocks.** Three on the twenty-ninth day of the new month and four on the thirty-fifth, in a dressed join, **both entered and neither deleted**, on the standing rule. No chapter may use the knocks as an instrument, a signal, a proof, a rescue or a reason to open anything. A number going up is not a pattern and is also not nothing. **In this batch the knocks appeared in the second page of the review as one of the nineteen things the city is not asking, in Renna Ord's own words, and that is all they did.**
- **The five lock sites stay apart, and nothing happened at three of them.** The main breach; the **Narrow Mark**, the only licensed route, **opened twice in sixty days and not a third time in this batch**; the near gate, where forty men stand twice a day and where the rail on the two hundred steps is **condemned** and the four go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge; the chapel-floor dressed joint, re-boarded on the fortieth and not opened; the chapel stone, silent.
- **The two marks in the world.** Eli Mercer's return mark belongs to him and to nobody else. Owen Park's is in a grave and does not transfer. **Adrian has none of his own and got none in this batch.**
- **Four Crown instruments and never collapse them:** the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood; the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month, a single-author containment held by a Concord clerk answerable only to Aurel, refused in a yard and entered as ninety statements of error; the second answer of the twenty-second, not in the form required; and the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first, a table and not a version of the other three, with a fee of eleven ounces of silver. **The answer of the forty-second day of the new month accepting salt is the same instrument as the schedule of crossings and not a fifth, and Sera Vail said so out loud in nine seconds, which is the first time in five weeks she has said that something from that office is the same document and not a new one.** The **Quiet Choir's design is a fifth shape and is not the Crown's**, and it is the one Adrian is frightened of, and it has three descriptions on the page and no name: a rule that only covers the thing you can see, a standing that cannot be refused, and a guild that does not go home.
- **The name *Single Witness* and the name *First Grammar* are still not used in prose and must not be used before Chapter 0091, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it.**
- **Mina Okoye's sentence is hers and hers alone:** *a hold is a working without a witness, and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.* No character may claim to have coined it. **It is not used in this batch and nobody in this batch claimed it.**
- **A rescue is a working. A pattern is a toll. A cost is a promise.** A floor that has come open once is a hole and not a pattern; a description is not a place; a quarter of a thing is not a thing; a rule about a thing is not a rule about the world.
- **A statement of error's third clause is a real procedure and using it is correct**, and it is not a version lock, and a local instrument with a notary, an hour, a person named and a term is not a pale field with no office and no hour.
- **Marrow stays publicly useful and unaccused.** No scene may convict him and none may make him useless. **He has not mentioned the letter to Hattie Park and it is now forty-four days since the fortieth.** On the forty-fourth day of the new month he asked Adrian Vale for the first time in two volumes *what do you want me to be useful for*, and said *I have been useful for a month, and it got me nothing, and I did not notice*, and this city has not convicted him of anything and is not going to.
- **A stipend is a wage, a wage is owed to a person, and a person is described on a roll** — and from the thirty-fourth day of the new month, **a guild is not a person, a guild can be refused, and the fear of the word *list* in this city is a fear of persons and has never once been about guilds.** The four transactions of Chapter 0065 were costed against both sentences, in that order: **nobody in this batch was paid a wage out of a second page except a woman of forty-one who was removed from it in the same entry, and nobody was paid a stipend because a stipend is a roll.**
- **The calendar is load-bearing.** Day 1 is the Tuesday Adrian came out of the wall. Day 50 is the Tuesday. Day 60 is the Friday. The new month began on day 26, so the Nth day of the new month is day 25 + N.
- **Two date forms, and only two.** *The Nth day of this flood* up to the fortieth, *the Nth day of the new month* after it. **Never *of this month*, *of the month*, *of next month*, *of this new month*, and never a spelled-out ordinal that cannot exist such as *the thirtieth-first day*.** A date after the new month has run out is **the Nth day of the month after this one**, and that is the only forward-looking date form in the series, and it appears once in this batch, in Chapter 0065, for the workway's review date. ***March* is a month in this world's calendar** and is the month the bottom field is settled in, and it is fifteen days away at the end of this batch.
- **The word-count device is exact.** If a chapter says a sentence is *N* words, *N* is the count of the sentence as it stands on the page. **Renna Ord's eleven words are Volume 01 canon and are not re-counted.** The corrected counts for this batch are listed in `state/continuity.md` under *Volume 02, Batch 0002*.
- **The age of the trial licence is that day minus fifty.** Signed and sealed on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which is day 50. It is **eleven days old on the sixty-first, fifteen on the sixty-fifth, sixteen on the sixty-sixth, seventeen on the sixty-seventh, eighteen on the sixty-eighth, nineteen on the sixty-ninth, twenty on the seventieth, and twenty-five days old at the review on day 75.**

## 7. Two places where a chapter card and the exact-count rule had to be reconciled, and how it was done

1. **Chapter 0068's card called Adrian Vale's question five words long.** The question on the page is **What is a person with no work for?** which is **eight**. **The count on the page is eight**, because the exact-count rule outranks a card, and Chapter 0070 uses the same question with the same figure.
2. **Chapter 0065's card said the twentieth field was refused in nine seconds.** It was: the refusal took the room **nine seconds**, because there is a page and nine people read it out loud. **The holder also said yes, in four seconds**, and the acceptance is entered in her own words with a date, and **the refusal is not the interesting half**, because the interesting half is that a woman who has said yes to two of these in her life said yes to a third and it was a page and not a rule that stopped her.
