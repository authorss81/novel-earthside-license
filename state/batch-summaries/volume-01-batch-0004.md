# Volume 01 Batch 0004 Summary — The False Rescue (Chapters 31–40)

## What happened

Nine days, two false rescues, a true reason on a keeper's door, a man sent into a floor, and the volume's only death.

The batch opens on a Friday that is the seventh day of a new month, because a man in a good coat had told a boy of nine on a step twelve days before and nobody else. It ends on the following Saturday at ten past midnight, in a gallery under a lock, in front of an empty box on a tide table that somebody else will fill in on Tuesday with a stick of chalk.

**Chapters 31–33 are consequence and politics.** Marrow's date is honoured literally and turns out not to be a trap but a request: he wants the **true** reason for refusing a workway written in Adrian's own hand on a keeper's board, and the reason is that Adrian refused it **because it worked** — because he liked saying no, in a room, in front of people, in the rain, with witnesses, and because a reason that works is a rule, and a rule is a thing that stands there after the man who made it has gone. It is a worse sentence than the one in the hall and it goes on a wall in a city of eleven hundred people with a mason's own name under it. On the same day the account of the other side is read at Verrow before a clerk in Aurel knows it exists, and the first thing the meeting-ground does with it is ask a question about a **person**: *is there a person on the other side of it*, asked out loud, twice, by a woman of thirty-one who does not speak for the assembly. Adrian does not answer it and says so on the page. On the Sunday, Jonah Pike proposes a settlement in the yard — a dry building, shared equipment, work rights at the lock, for exclusive access — and **Adrian cannot say the sentence that ends it**, to a man who has been his ally for three weeks, and a silence entered in a yard is not a refusal and is not a consent and is a third thing for which this city has no word. By the Monday the Brass Measure's fourth term is on the table and Renna Ord, who cannot read, has it read aloud in a yard of a hundred and forty people and says eleven words: **that is a lock that a guild can close.** On the Tuesday, Nine Elms exercises the fourth line of the Reed Reach's own term sheet — Ilda Marsh's line, forced through on the twenty-third — and the whole of the communal charge returns to the private debtor, which is Adrian, and the Reach is off the hook, and the door stays shut, which is the correct outcome and a catastrophe for him.

**Chapter 34 is the midpoint.** Adrian is four miles away in a village, and the thing he was watching gets watched. A reed cutter of forty-four is sent politely to the bottom of a reed channel over a barrow — the fourth man Owen has spent in a month, and **Renna Ord is not there to catch it and Adrian is**. The man sees a thumb-shaped light. Adrian countersigns a page of the public book **without reading it**, which is the first thing he has signed in thirty-six days without reading it and the first thing he has signed without noticing himself not reading it. A woman of forty-one enters the second statement of error in the history of the book, against him, about the eleven page, and it is correct. And nobody notices that **a man with a shoulder is a man who is not available**, and availability is a thing this city has been quietly redistributing for a month.

**Chapters 35–38 are the second false rescue and the death.** A gate-hand goes missing from the reed at Long Water on the night of the thirty-sixth. A man in a good coat has been to the house twice and has told the family, in a kitchen, that there is a place on the other side of that floor — and has also told a boy of nineteen, in the same room, that it is a room eleven people stood in and there was nobody else in it. A rescue is proposed in a yard and voted by a count, one hundred and forty-one for, and **a temporary return mark is established for Owen Park** — the second ever, expensive, witnessed, entered for the person and not for the route. Terms are written and read. A mason of thirty-four years refuses to read them publicly and says the true thing: *a rescue is a working and a working needs a pattern, and a floor that has come open once under three hundred and ten people is not a pattern, it is a hole.* She is outvoted by a family and a boy of nineteen, and her refusal is entered in her words, and **that refusal is the only reason the record of the thirty-eighth survives it.** Adrian writes the site as *a dressed joint, described on the twenty-fourth day and not named*, which is true of two places in this city, and one of them is a scar in a place, and he did not see it, and a description is not a place. At half past one on the Thursday, four people who were not sent find the missing man alive, eleven miles the wrong way, on foot, because he was where the water was not. The finding and the terms are entered on facing pages four hours apart with neither above the other, and the holder of the pull is in the room when the finding is read and does not say the word, and the notary enters that fact herself, because she is barred from letting a book record what a person said and not what a person did not say. At four in the morning a man goes through the floor of a yard and the floor takes him, because a scar is not a pattern and a scar is where a rule broke and went, and there were two men on that floor and one of them was in the terms. **Owen Park comes back up out of it alive and badly hurt at twenty past the sixth hour, and dies at about half past four in the afternoon**, of a head wound that opened on the first day of this flood in another world, on a shoulder that had been out of its socket since the twenty-fifth and was never set, in a body that took a working's residue on a flagstone in front of three hundred and ten people.

**Chapters 39–40 are the record and the refusal to let a death become an instruction.** The longest entry in the history of the book is made on the Friday morning by a notary and not by the holder, on a woman's use of a standing clause she did not write: *a record is not a finding; it obliges somebody to answer it and it obliges nobody to believe it, and it is not a finding and it cannot be used as one.* Therefore nobody may write **he died because**, and the entry is four pages long, and it contains three sentences that are in no other entry in that book. On the Saturday the market has already begun, a number nobody put there is on a board, a woman of sixty-one walks down two hundred wet steps with nine years of paper and it is read aloud in a yard, and a Hearthguard of nineteen years says four words in front of about two hundred and ten people about a man they all watched die, and is told by about forty of them that it was the worst possible use of the sentence and that they are keeping it, and a docker in a felt hat says the sentence that is the volume's whole argument: **a record is not a fence.**

## Results

- **Power: nothing gained anywhere in the batch.** Adrian is **Stage 1, Witness** at the end exactly as at the start. He cannot perform a working. On the thirty-eighth day he stood four feet back from a floor with a rule and a stub of chalk and **marked nothing**, because there was no join, there was a hole, and he has been reading a join for thirty-eight days. He is **Unbound**, refused three times, and the last refusal reason he gave in public is on a keeper's board in chalk in his own hand and it is worse than the one in the book.
- **The volume's only death, and it is permanent.** **Owen Park, thirty-eight days in this world, from Dunedin, died at about half past four in the afternoon of the thirty-eighth day.** No respawn, no return, no inheritance, and **the mark does not transfer**. There are **two marks in the world and one of them is a scar**: Eli Mercer's is not one, and about a third of it went. His three causes are all on the page and all three are entered: Adrian's underestimated crossing cost, the Chapter 25 shoulder that was never set, and Owen's own refusal to follow instructions. He was not disobedient. He was asked, in a yard, in front of two hundred and forty people, to be sent, and he asked, and nobody said the word, and he went.
- **A rescue was costed, witnessed and consented to, and it was not a working.** One named person per opening, a mark set by a separate expensive act, a cost entered for the fifth time, a fresh inspection, a list read in the yard nine minutes beforehand because the clause as it stands said *before*, four witnesses, and Adrian four feet back with a rule. Every term was satisfied. **The site was wrong, and the site was wrong because Adrian wrote a true sentence about a place instead of the place, and a description is not a place, and a quarter of a thing is not a thing was his own rule from the twenty-fourth day and it came back through a floor.**
- **Three System messages, in Chapters 33, 36 and 40**, and none in 31, 32, 34, 35, 37, 38 or 39. All three are true, all three place the cost in the grammatical position where a cost goes, and all three are the same construction seen for a fourteenth time: *a private debtor may be replaced by a person who will hold the debt, and the debtor's name is not struck out*; *the terms of a crossing may be written by the person who will be carried through it, and a person who is carried through a working is not answerable for the working*; *an entry that is wrong may be entered against any entry, and the person named must answer it, or withdraw it, or stand by it in public and say that they can say no more*. The first is the same shape as the Chapter 11 replacement offer and the Chapter 29 debt closure. The second is the best one in the book: it does not move a cost out of the way, it takes a thing away and calls it free, and Adrian refuses it in under a second and can say why. The third arrives in a yard in a language built out of his own, twenty-two seconds after the city has built a procedure for being wrong in public, and he refuses it in public and names it a version lock with better manners, and a man in a good coat says *that is the best answer anybody has given in five weeks* and then says **I did not put it there** and is not believed and is not convicted and has not been.
- **The five lock sites stayed apart and one of them is now a scar in a person.** The main breach is measured, never spoken from, and the twenty-second reading is **eight and five-eighths** and has not moved. The Narrow Mark is the seam in the outer wall of the outer channel and was **touched once on the thirty-eventh, in a test that did nothing for four minutes and a half**. The near gate is the dockers' inner working gate and forty of this city's own men stood under it twice on the thirty-third at their ordinary volume. The chapel-floor hairline seam is the top of the dressed joint and was **closed and re-boarded** on the fortieth. The chapel stone is the voice's only channel and **did not speak in this batch**, and is not repaired.
- **The debt grew and the cost column became a notation.** A season of the bottom field has now been entered **five times** in a month, and the notary has entered, in her own words and read out twice, that a cost entered five times is not a cost any more.
- **The Reed Reach's concession was used against the Reach, in nine days, by the woman who wrote the line.** The fourth line works. Adrian is the creditor of his own concession. He asked them not to and they refused to be protected from his arithmetic, and the truth of it is that the Reach is now off the hook and the door is shut and the cost is his and he cannot pay it.
- **Marrow stays publicly useful, privately dangerous, and unconvicted.** He asked for a true reason and got a better one than the one he wanted. He went to a grieving kitchen twice and said the true thing twice. He said *I did not put it there* in front of two hundred people and was not believed and was not accused. He offered to carry a dead man's letter to Dunedin through a Crown channel out of Aurel, in about thirty seconds, entirely reasonably, and Adrian did not refuse him.
- **The Single Witness problem arrives early, in a reasonable accent, in a yard.** A man in a good coat says the only way to close the join in the gate is to give it to one person, and says it as a fact about how a working works. A notary enters underneath it that it is **not** the notice of the Registrar of Crossings and Tides, that it is not the same instrument, and that the two documents that say the same thing **do not agree with each other**. Adrian does not know yet what the sentence is. **This is not the Chapter 30 Registry demand and the two must never be collapsed**; the Registry is a bureaucracy and the demand is a form, and this is a mason and a pattern and a name for the thing a mason has been looking at for thirty-four years.
- **The premise surfaces once, and it surfaces in a reaction.** A Veyran woman in a yard at Nine Elms says the two words she has heard a chandler say in a market, Tamsin translates them because Tamsin translates everything, and Tamsin says in the local language: *that is a word she has heard in a market, it is not a word she has a use for, do not give it to her and do not take it from her, and Adrian, sit down, you are doing the face you did on the twenty-fifth.* A Veyran reacting to them is not a Veyran believing it, and Adrian finds the difference unbearable and is right to. **The word *quest* is not used. No Veyran is called anything but a person. Adrian's own vocabulary slips once and is corrected in public: he says *chair* about a seat in a hall on the thirty-fifth and Tamsin Quill corrects him in the middle of a room, and Nell Bray's sentence from the second week is quoted back at him.**

## Deviations from the batch cards (deliberate, recorded)

1. **Chapter 31 also carries the thirty-third day.** The tide of the thirty-third is at the small hours of the thirty-second, so the measurement and the ordinary word in the tide table are at the end of Chapter 31 and Chapter 32 opens on the Sunday morning. The day map's `33 | Saturday | 31/32` is honoured.
2. **Chapters 32 and 33 share the Monday, and Chapters 33 and 34 share the Tuesday.** Chapter 32 ends at the seventh hour of the evening of the Monday with Jonah's unanswered question; Chapter 33 opens at the eleventh hour of that night with the plan for Nine Elms and runs all Tuesday; Chapter 34 runs from the second hour of the afternoon of the Tuesday. The day map is honoured and no event is given two dates.
3. **The first descent's death is a man who was found.** Ceri Vaughan is found alive at half past one on the thirty-eighth, four hours before the crossing, and the finding is entered on facing pages. **The false rescue is a rescue of nobody**, which is the title of the batch and the thing the whole batch is about, and it is not prevented from being a rescue of nobody by anything but the finding, which arrives in time and is not acted on.
4. **The rescue is proposed by a man in a good coat through a grieving family over two days, in a room, and cannot be traced.** The connection between a man lost in a reed at Long Water and a room on the other side of a floor in Saltwake is made **twice, gently, helpfully, to a woman with a boy of nine, by a Crown Law scholar**, and it is made to a boy of nineteen as well and to him it is the opposite. It is not convictable, it is not proved, and Adrian cannot show it to anybody, and he has written that down.
5. **The mark is set for Owen on the night of the thirty-seventh and the test is on the walk-round at two in the morning of the thirty-eighth**, so the crossing at the fourth hour is four hours after the last thing that worked. The terms say the first use of a mark is a test and not a passage, and Adrian agrees to it in eleven seconds, and a docker says a test is cheaper than a rescue, and the test is a failure and the difference is not visible until the morning.
6. **Three chapters run slightly over the 3,300–6,500 range** — 31 at about 6,600 words, 37 at about 6,570, 39 at about 6,670 — and two are permitted to be longer: **38 at about 7,700 and 40 at about 7,800**. Chapter 38 is the volume's only death and the card requires it at full length, and it is the longest chapter in the volume. The three near-limit chapters are close to the boundary rather than over it in substance; a reviewer should check them against the cards rather than against the count, and every trim pass on them cut flab rather than content.
7. **The line about the trench in Chapter 22 was not repeated.** The four clauses of the term sheet are a document and are read on a raft, and the fourth line is exercised in a yard at Nine Elms, and no System field appears anywhere near it.

## What the next batch inherits

Everything is dated, everything is armed, and there is a man in a good coat who has said a sentence in a yard that a notary has underlined, and a notary has put a difference on a page while she is the only person who has read all three documents.

**Day 41 is the Sunday** and Owen Park is buried under his real name in the ordinary ground with his sister's name read out and a four-page account he dictated himself read in full by a woman of fifty-eight who has never read anything she wrote about a living man to a dead one. The keeper's board comes off on the Sunday in daylight and a rule that made a ground answerable to a man is washed away with a cloth and a bucket, and about a hundred and forty people read it and eleven can still repeat it.

**Day 42 is the Monday** and the post goes into its review. **Day 43 is a Tuesday and it is the next tide**, and the last column of the tide table has an empty box against it, and a man with a stick of chalk is going to fill it in, and it is not going to be Adrian, and it is not going to be the woman with tweezers, and it is not going to be the man in the good coat.

**The Registry's clock is running** and the next quarterly sitting is in about a hundred and ten days. **The main breach is open and stepping and the only question left in Saltwake is whether the join in that gate is going to be closed.** **A docker has said in a yard that a record is not a fence, and the fence is the thing the next ten chapters have to build.** **A woman of thirty-one from Nine Elms has asked twice whether there is a person on the other side of a floor and has not been answered, and Adrian's fourth reading is that there is somebody in there and we know it and a room with a man in it is a room somebody has been in twice.** **A boy of nineteen is hurt in the right hand, holds the only working mark in the world, has refused to join the six and to speak for anybody, and has asked for a standing before he needed one.** **A woman of sixty-one's nine years of paper has been read aloud in a yard and nineteen of the shapes are the same and eight are not, and a Hearthguard has eight stones of his own in a ground behind a wall and has not mentioned them.** **A man in Dunedin and a woman in Camden are owed a letter each and there is no route in this world that a letter can travel, and a man in a good coat has offered a Crown channel and been neither refused nor accepted.** **Page forty-one is in a notary's keeping and Adrian has four readings of it and has not asked for it back.** **And the fourth column of a wall in a room above a market is empty, on purpose, and stays empty for eleven days.**

## Repair pass note

No blocking defects were carried into this batch. One thing a reviewer should check with particular care, because it is the batch's central mechanism and it is easy to get wrong: **the missing gate-hand is found alive at half past one on the thirty-eighth, the terms were read and the rescue voted on the evening of the thirty-seventh, and the crossing is at the fourth hour of the morning of the thirty-eighth.** The finding must not be moved later than the crossing and must not be moved earlier than the vote, and the book must not be read as though the city did not know. It knew. A notary entered the knowledge and the holder's silence on facing pages four hours apart, and every person in this book who has read those pages knows exactly what happened and what did not happen inside them.
