# Volume 01 Batch 0006 Summary — The Trial Licence (Chapters 48–50)

**This is the closing batch of Volume 01 and the last batch of the volume.** Three chapters, three days, the volume's climax and its resolution. The volume's close record is `state/volume-01-close.md`.

## What happened

**The main breach closes, the licence is signed, the field is not saved.** The batch opens on the Sunday before the working and closes on the Tuesday of the licence, and between them a city closes a join in its own gate by doing ordinary work on a named tide with a cost entered before the tide turned, a scar under a chapel floor is given a form and not a promise, a dead man's four pages are confirmed and cannot be shortened, seventeen people from another world are named with the longer list being what they do not have, and a boy of nine asks whether the next ones are going to be players.

**Chapter 48 is the day before, and the thing that is not a working is the scar.** The four named people go down two hundred steps in the wrong weather at a falling tide to look at **a gauge with one mark cut on it on the twenty-seventh day of this flood** — the only number in this city that can stop a tide — and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read has been arguing for nine days that **a river does not keep one number**, and is entered as not answered, because a mark is not a rule, not a term and not a promise and can therefore only be argued about, in markets and not in books. The six conditions are read for the last time with a seventh item: **the cost is entered before the tide turns and not after**, because a cost entered before is a price and a price is a thing a person can refuse. **Nine names are filled in over most of the day**, and the room's finding is the finding of the batch: **the crew cannot stop the crew, the crew is the hand, and so the nine have to be people the working costs.** Adrian is refused a place on the list by Wenna Cray and not by himself, and Marek Osley is refused a place after asking for one, and says the true reason: a man who is owed a season cannot also be the man who stops the thing that spends it, because then the only way to stop it is to have already lost. **A form of watching the scar is written by Nyla Ferrow on a slate on a nail** — a line of salt, four chalk depths, four people twice a day, a price — and the keeper enters what it **cannot** do: it cannot close anything, and it cannot say where tomorrow's residue will go, and it is a form and not a promise, and a form with no price on it is a favour. **The fourth page of the instrument is written and it is the one nobody wanted**: what happens on the day the crew does not turn up, that the join is where it is, that the instrument is not suspended by anybody, that the record says the crew did not turn up and does not say a fault and does not name a man and has no entry of anybody going to anybody's door, and **that a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason**. Vail says she has never entered a page whose purpose is to be read by somebody who is angry, and a man from another world says four words — *then that is the point* — and about four people in that yard understand. **Renna Ord gets one line changed**, wants *refused*, gets **not owing**, and says it is worse than hers and says good. **A boy's name is found at the top of the second page of the roll** for the true reason that a page with no date on it can be moved to a different month, and Vask reads it out in a yard and the name comes off and **the date stays**. **The twenty-fourth reading is prepared and not taken**, in a gallery with nineteen men, because a reading on the ebb of a working tide is a number and a number taken on the wrong water is a lie, and **Nell Bray says it is the first true thing anybody in that gallery has said about a number in forty days.** Vask's two sentences go in front of the room a second time and are not answered a second time, and Renna Ord answers in nine words — **the toll is the crew, the date is March** — the room does not find it satisfactory and the crew does not care. **No System field.** The chapter ends at the eleventh hour with an empty box and a boy of nineteen at the bottom of two hundred steps asking for a rule for Monday, and there is none, and the difference between an answer and a rule is that **the page is what is left when the person is asleep or hurt**.

**Chapter 49 is the working, and it is a Tuesday's job.** The four go down at the fourth hour with a gauge, the water is four inches below the twenty-eighth mark, the tide is worked, **and the four say four different things about the same piece of oak and do not agree**, which the notary enters. **The post ends at noon because a term is a term and a date is a date** — the seat of holder of the pull is vacant, a vacant seat is not a fault, the terms go with it, and Wenna Cray says *then somebody had better put him in the form* and the form gains a fifth item: that Adrian is told and asked, may say no, that the refusal is entered, and that **it is a question and not a duty and the day it becomes a duty somebody is to take it off the wall in daylight and say so.** The crew goes under the near gate twice at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and **Adrian stands four feet back on the sill of the third bay and a docker makes him say out loud, in the local language and to about twenty men, that he is not in it and is barred from opening, inspecting, signing and stopping it.** The join **steps** at the seventh hour and the step is entered **in the unnamed mason's own hand before the number is read**, the second time in nine days, and he writes that he has thought about the pale edge every day since the twenty-fifth and ordered it anyway. **It is closed in about eleven minutes of decided work** — a bar, a line, a woman calling a number every eleven minutes from a step, forty men on a Tuesday's job — and Ottoline Vask gives the mason's sentence of the volume: **a closed join does not get narrower, it gets dressed**, and the docker's *nine* and the boy's number on the wall of the second bay **stay where they are for the rest of this world's life**. Nobody died and nobody was torn, and **one man did not come back for the second time under the near gate** — Ferris, who voted no on the twenty-first and asked to be asked again, and who has not been paid, because the city's credit is a field four miles away — and **there is no entry and no fault and nobody went to anybody's door**, and Renna Ord is seen at the top of two hundred steps not going down. Renna Ord's prediction is **entered as a prediction with her date and the right date beside it and neither above the other**, and she says she will be proved wrong and hopes to be. **The twenty-fourth reading is eight and seven-eighths**, because the join is closed, and one ruled line is left in the column, and Vask says a decision can be looked up and a number can only be taken. **The cost is entered for the seventh time before the tide turns and not after**, and Vail says her sentence about a column this city can afford and then **says the other half**: a column re-entered at its new size seven times in one month is a column being kept honest, and it is the first thing in five weeks she has been able to enter that she is not ashamed of, and she has not slept. **The residue goes under the chapel floor** — a man with a barrow who was nearest because the rule says whoever is nearest felt a finger's breadth and warm boards, and nothing came up and nothing came out — **and Renna Ord says in nine words that it was luck and not skill: that is not a thing we did, that is a thing that did not happen to us**, and Hettis Roke enters that nothing in six conditions, nine pages, a cost column or a set of terms chose it. **The main breach is closed and said nothing.** Tamsin Quill says she did not feel a working. **No System field.** The row for the forty-ninth day stays empty, because **a boy of nineteen who wrote that word once puts the chalk down on a ledge and says he is not going to be the man who writes it every time**, and a woman writes in the fourth volume until the lamp goes out.

**Chapter 50 is the licence, and it says nobody.** **The four who signed the inspection of the fourth day of this flood — Tamsin Quill, Nell Bray, Ovin Sarr, Hettis Roke — re-inspect the Narrow Mark with Ottoline Vask's hands flat on the stone**, and Vask says *it has not moved, not one sixteenth, and I have been wrong about that for a month, and being wrong about that is the best thing anybody has said to me in a month.* **The count required per opening goes from four to two; the quorum does not change and cannot.** Nell Bray gives the argument the volume was built for: *four was a quorum, two is an opening, and if you make an opening cost a quorum then nobody will ever open it, and if you make the quorum cheap then you have a man who opens it on his own* — **a working can be made cheaper without being made private** — and the two may not both be of the crew, because the crew is the hand and the hand may not hold the door. The standing order is entered whole. **The fourth column of the loft wall is filled in for the first time in fifty days**, and the finding is that nobody in that room, including the man who put the wall up, had ever written down what the work was, because the fourth column is what the work is **for** and not what the person is for. **The work is the nineteen stones**, because Sabra Vint asked on the nineteenth day of the new month for somebody to write down what she thinks they mean, and the cell is filled in two hands — a woman's from another world and a Hearthguard's — with the nineteen, the eight that are different, the meaning nobody has written, and the single word **Eight** — and it is not a post and anybody may pick it up. Roke says in nine seconds that he has not been asked about his own eight in nine years and is not being asked now, and **a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused**, and he does not look at Sabra Vint when she comes down the steps for the third time in nine years. **Owen Park's burial term is settled in four pages** that stand, are not shortened, are not summarised in any instrument, return, quarterly description or letter, and are entered as what a person said with the attribution in the sentence — containing the name cut twice in two languages in two hands with the second upside down, **the shoulder**, his own reason for his own name, the year of certainty in a mason's words, and Mina Okoye's sentence in her own words and hers alone — and **nobody may write *he died because*. Aldis Kerrow tries**, with a pen and a Crown column four lines high, and **Priya Shah takes it out of his hand and gives him four lines that fit the column instead**, and Kerrow says he had known the rule and has been doing it for nineteen years. **The seventeen are named**, six on one page and eleven on another, and **the longer list is what they do not have**: no land rights, no class, no standing, **no workway of any kind**, entered as a term and not a favour, and Adrian's page says **Unbound** in his own words with two dates on it and that he is party to the licence and is given nothing by it. About ninety people want a stipend and **Renna Ord refuses it in the language of the room**: *a stipend is a wage, a wage is owed to a person, a person is described on a roll, and the roll of everybody in this city who is not from it goes to a building in Aurel four times a year in a wooden case.* **The licence has to say who answers for the next test, the room reaches for the only man in it everybody can be angry at, he is offered a small seat for the third time in five weeks, and the instrument says nobody** — and he says the sentence himself, and **Marrow does not put his hand up** and enters that he was going to and does not know why, and Adrian cannot tell whether it was a choice or a technique. **The seventeenth field arrives on the page that lists what the seventeen do not have**: the roll may be closed, the person named may be returned on the next tide once, and the person named does not owe the standing charge. **Three true things and a price, and the price is a field and nine households who have already refused twice.** He refuses it in under a second and gives the reason himself in three parts, and the smallest one is the real one: **if the roll is closed then the woman in Camden is never told anything, ever, by anybody.** Renner Sallow says it is now the ninth. **Adrian says *class* in a room of two hundred and forty people and is stopped by a procedure and not by a woman** — the bar from the twenty-sixth day reads the document back as an error — and says *people* instead, and about two hundred and forty people hear the difference. The licence is **signed, sealed, dated on every page**, with a right of review, a right of refusal, a right to be asked, a page that says what it costs and the fourth page attached; **he reads it in twenty-nine minutes and signs last**, and a man from another world is not a seat. In the evening **Ferris writes *works* in the box against the forty-ninth day**, and fifty days after she wrote the word against the thirty-third, **Renna Ord is asked what she decided**, and gives the smallest answer in the book: *I decided to write it because I did not want to be the only person in the room who had not.* **The lock opens for one named living person** — two witnesses not of the crew, a fresh inspection signed that morning, the four who authorised the route in the yard and not at the opening — and **Eli Mercer, who refused eleven times, strikes his own name and then unstrucks it**: *I have said no eleven times to people who wanted something. I am going to say yes once to a page.* He goes and comes back in about eleven minutes and says **four words — *same room, taller*** — and nobody asks him a second question. **The final image:** Tamsin Quill writes the next inspection date in the public ledger on a slate on a nail in the yard while the entry of the fourth day of this flood, the one that names Adrian Vale as the route's first witness, lies on a sill two hundred steps below under a piece of ordinary river stone; **Sabra Vint says the number nineteen to a boy of nine who does not know what it is**; and **Tam Crane asks whether the next ones are going to be players**, and Adrian Vale looks at the dark water and has not yet found an answer that does not make the child smaller, and says instead that nobody who comes through that wall is a special kind of person, and when the boy says *but you were the first one*, he says **I was not; I was the first one anybody could see.** **Nothing gained. He is Unbound to the last line.**
