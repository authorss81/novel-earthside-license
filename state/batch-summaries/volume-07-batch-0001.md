# Volume 07, Batch 0001 — Chapters 0301–0310, days 301 to 310

**Ten chapters, days 301 to 310, one chapter to one day, and the last of them is a Wednesday. These are the first ten days of Volume 07 and they are the first ten days in this manuscript to be written on a plan that decided its own subject, because the Volume 07 outline phase was the second phase in this repository permitted to decide and it took four decisions, all four of which are on the page in `outline/series.md` under *DECISIONS OF RECORD, VOLUME 07* in that phase's own words.**

**The subject of the ten days is a boundary. The first pressure is a barrow of boards against the wall of a shut gate and a Thursday three days off. The middle is a kitchen, a tin, and a question in a woman's own hand with four days of notice behind it. The last is a man of about thirty-four who says one word out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door and does not say what it means. A road has been shut since the thirty-ninth of that month and a month has six days left in it and no form for the day after. Nobody got stronger. Nothing was beaten, outwitted or destroyed. It is outlasted, refused, and then a word is put on a page, which is the only thing this batch did that cannot be taken back.**

## 1. The batch goal, and whether it was met

**The goal was: put a boundary nobody here can say where it is on a page, put a hundred-and-forty-year-old copy in a clerk's hand on a page with a town four hundred miles inland at the end of it, find out in a room that this world's calendar has six days left in it and nothing after them, and say one word out loud about a line in the ground — without a monster, without a power, without a search, and without a chapter in which two people in a yard talk about a day.**

Eight things were required and all eight are on a page.

1. **The volume's central pressure, and a notice in a hand with no name at the top of it.** Chapter 0301. Day 301, a Monday, the forty-sixth of the Short Month. **A woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register walks up a lane nobody sent her up, writes a question in her own hand for a room four days off on the fiftieth, and leaves it on the wall of a shut gate under a stone off a doorstep without calling at a door.** The first thing on the slate in the room over a market that evening is four lines saying the ground on that lane has to be settled before the season turns, and **nobody in that room can say where the line is.** The woman of about fifty at the gate is not asked anything and says four things to a post. **Change: the first pressure of Volume 07 is on a page in about nine people's hands, and it is a thing nobody in the room can do.**
2. **What a boundary is in this world, and the word nobody will define.** Chapter 0302. Day 302, a Tuesday, the forty-seventh. Three things on a slate, two of them possible. **A boundary is settled by two parties agreeing where it runs or by nobody walking it, and there is no third way and there has never been one.** The register-keeper is asked the word in the third place of the Crown's page and says before the question is finished that she is not going to define it, and stops there. A man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living says the room has been standing on the wrong side of a thing since before the thirty-ninth of that month and will not say what the thing is until the deed is read. **Change: no paper anybody in this city holds can settle a boundary, and the word that would let a page do it is refused out loud four times in four rooms.**

Day 310, a Wednesday, the fifty-fifth. **A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and wrote the ninth line says *scar*, in one word, in the local language, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, and does not say what it means, and the mark is not a pattern, and the word goes on the register-keeper's second slate in chalk with no name on the line.** **Change: there is now a word in this city for a thing about nine hundred paces off a shut gate, and a reader has been told what it is called and no page in this manuscript will be able to say so for long.**

**The gains are a question in a hand with no name at the top of it, a stone off a doorstep, a barrow of boards that does not go, a tin with a folded sheet thirty years old in it, a spade and a willow line and a bucket and nine feet of silt, a deed and a clerk's copy with a pin through the corner of it, nineteen stones and eight that are different, a length of new rope nobody has used, a tally with two figures in it and one of them not printed, a sentence about a schedule being filled in from the returns, six days left in a month and nothing after them, and one word in chalk on a slate.
