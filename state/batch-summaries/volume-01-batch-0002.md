# Volume 01 Batch 0002 Summary — The Second Sitting (Chapters 11–20)

## What happened

The batch turns a public defeat into ordinary work. Nothing opens in ten chapters and everything gets harder, which is the point.

Chapter 11 is a hearing that does not end: a notary who will not construe her own terms, a salt factor who wants a date in a box, a complaint entered on page one, a docker who defines what a seat is, and four minutes of writing that make the instrument real. The chapter's discovery is that Adrian's name in a book has turned him into a **counter** — a queue of people who want something from him — and the batch spends the next nine chapters teaching him what that is.

Chapters 11–13 are political and procedural. The Crown's first letter is courteous, and the most dangerous document in the city turns out to be a form with one field and no exit. Adrian refuses it and the refusal costs the group the only protection on offer, including money that would have gone to the eleven villages. The chapter's other discovery is that the name spoken at the back of a salt store was never a leak: it was read aloud, in a market, by the only person in Saltwake who can translate — and it sounded like a roll because an old customs clerk really did write down six things that came out of a hole, three hundred and ten years ago, and Ivenn Marrow told a room it was a fraud.

Chapters 14–16 are character, work and temptation. The six take local jobs and each is bad at the first version. The batch's best question is asked by Noor Haddad, who is not a mystic and has no stake in the wall: *who does the job?* — because a workway is a duty with a shape and a duty with a shape displaces a duty, and nobody has asked the man it lands on. The batch's best refusal is Adrian's second one, in a room with witnesses, on **obligation** rather than risk, and the batch's designed danger is Marrow reading the omitted cost out loud unasked and then arguing that the cost is worth paying. Five of the six say something Adrian would not have chosen and Mina argues **for** taking it.

Chapters 17–18 are dissent and cost. A salt factor who was reasonable in Chapter 11 becomes the first dissent in Chapter 17, and he is right that he is carrying a hazard of unknown size, and he is the direct result of Adrian's own cost column. Nine Elms makes its refusal a standing commitment at the price of the instrument. Adrian says out loud, on the record, that he does not know what the mark is for, and loses something he cannot get back. Chapter 18 turns the lost season into a scene: nothing is dead, the field will be replanted, and what is gone is a village's ability to **promise** — which is the batch's thesis in one agricultural fact, and which turns the debt from a fine into a relationship.

Chapters 19–20 escalate into the descent. The batch climax is Chapter 20: a real dig, on a real rota, in real weather, with a hundred small failures before one large discovery — a worked, dressed, nine-foot joint four feet under the chapel floor that was opened and closed two hundred years ago, is **dry**, and runs under the river bank where nobody has ever dug. The dig stops. Nothing is identified. Adrian is given a post rather than a payment.

## Results

- **Power: nothing gained anywhere in the batch.** Adrian is **Stage 1, Witness** at the end exactly as at the start. He cannot perform a working. In Chapter 15 he goes *down* the hole on Vask's ladder and is pulled out after four minutes; in Chapter 16 he refuses Vask's request for a safe line in front of the room, because a man who cannot say no is a man who will not be able to stop; in Chapter 20 Vask refuses **him**, in front of thirty people, and the keeper puts him four feet back from a hole. He is **Unbound**, having refused the Wayfinder profile twice in rooms with witnesses, the second time on obligation grounds, and the refusal is entered in the roll of strangers as a public Crown-readable sentence.
- **No crossing, no return mark, no travel.** The route is inspected, formally declared unrecognised, and empty. There is still no mark on any living person in this world that would take one home.
- **Nobody dies.** Owen Park's scalp is diagnosed as **unclosed** — clean skin over it and nothing happening underneath. **His shoulder is untouched and remains reserved for Chapter 25.** Dante Kuo's right forearm is reduced three days late. Nobody else is hurt. No respawn, no return, no inheritance of a mark.
- **Two marks, still separate.** The pale fragment-mark is in the maintenance seam and the palm keeps a thin trace. The **thin black line** opened in Chapter 8 is three weeks old, has not moved, and is still not a return mark. Vask's reason is unchanged: nobody steps over a hand.
- **The five lock sites stay apart.** The main breach is in the gate and is **measured, never spoken from**. The Narrow Mark is the maintenance seam in the outer channel wall. The near gate is the dockers' inner working gate, and Chapter 19 establishes that it and the breach are nine feet apart **in the same course of stone**, which is the mechanism, not a site confusion. The chapel-floor hairline seam is shown in Chapter 20 to be the top of a nine-foot dressed joint. The chapel stone is the raised threshold slab and is the voice's only channel.
- **The voice has one home.** It speaks only out of the **black line in the chapel stone**, and in Chapter 15 it repeats the keeper's fifth word a half-second after Nyla Ferrow says it, which means it is standing inside the binding. The main breach does not open in Chapter 19 and the gate does not speak. The working behind it is still a man in the lock join below.
- **Marrow's complicity stays plausible and unproven.** He is the only man in Saltwake with reason and access, and he is the only one who told a room the old customs document was a fraud when it is genuine. **No public scene convicts him.** He remains publicly useful, he has just made his own offer a shape with a date on it, and he ends the batch telling Adrian the true and dangerous reason he wants the workway taken: tethering, not control.
- **The premise half is still deferred.** No Veyran calls anything a game and no Veyran is an NPC. Adrian's own vocabulary slips — *onboarding question* in Chapter 12, *chair* in Chapters 11, 16 and 19, and *quest*, which is **Jonah Pike's word in Chapter 19**, said out loud in a room above the market for a thing in a wall. In the middle of that sentence Adrian understands that he has been waiting all day for a pale field to tell him what to do next, and that he is the only one in the room who knows what the word is supposed to cost. He says *do not use that word in this room again* and writes the date down. Chapter 20 looks back on that night. The narration does not assert the belief.

## Deviations from the batch cards (deliberate, recorded)

1. **The leak mechanism was the one Batch 0001 already fixed**, and it is now on the page with its real shape: not a document, not a spy, but **an interpreter**. Tamsin read the sheet aloud in a market because the rules do not work if you only know them in your own language, and the group is one interpreter wide. The old customs report is genuine, contains **no name**, and its danger is that a real roll makes new gossip sound like a roll.
2. **Marrow is not convicted.** The card required his complicity to stay plausible and unproven, so the customs report is handled entirely through Ketch, who says *he let the room believe the whole of it* and stops there. Adrian never learns that Marrow found it in the first week. He does learn that Marrow said the document was a fraud, and files it without consequence.
3. **Chapter 15's descent was moved after the keeper's price and the entered terms**, and given a nosebleed that stops it. The card's Chapter 20 would otherwise have had nothing left to open.
4. **Chapter 16's five "unusual" answers were re-cast so that Mina argues for the workway and Noor asks the labour question.** That makes the batch's designed danger actually do work, because the room is split rather than politely unanimous.
5. **Chapter 17's instrument survives on the term rather than on a bare technicality**, and the technicality is named: the standing requires a fresh inspection before any use, one was performed and recorded, and no holder was present to open anything. The instrument is alive because it did nothing, and Vail says so in front of ninety people.
6. **The main breach measurement was rebuilt** so that the series is exactly twenty readings with exactly four jumps totalling three inches, and the gauge marks are in the gate, not on the walk-round. The batch's central discovery only works if the arithmetic is honest.
7. **Chapter 19's crowd is ninety, not two hundred**, and the four step-dates are four distinct days, in line with Chapter 17.
8. **Chapter 14's carded beat — Adrian's borrowed route-reading used by another person for the first time — did not happen, and nothing in Batch 0002 or later may assume it did.** Chapter 14 turned out to be entirely local work, and the one time somebody is tempted to lean on him is Vask asking for a safe line in Chapter 16, which he refuses. If a later chapter wants that beat, it has to be built, not inherited.
9. **Chapter 19's carded ending — the Morrowglass document and the question of who else on that side of the wall was reading his files — was deliberately refused in favour of the other reading.** Adrian names the building and the file aloud, so the Morrowglass thread is on the page, but the chapter ends with him saying *it is in the room. It is the room*, which is consistent with `bible/world.md`: no human conspiracy is standing behind Morrowglass. The second reading is that the thing has been inside him since Tuesday. Both remain open; nobody has proved either.
10. **The moment Adrian stops expecting System output is dramatised in Chapter 19, not recalled in a notebook.** The word *quest* is now on the page, in Jonah Pike's mouth, in the Salt Weavers' chapel two floors above the market, on the Friday night — and the recognition, the refusal of the word, and the written date all happen in the room.
11. **The batch is nineteen days, not three weeks, and the calendar is now explicit.** `state/continuity.md` carries an authoritative day table: day 1 is the Tuesday Adrian came out of the wall and day 19 is the Saturday of Chapter 20. Both dated stamps on the page — the market board of Chapter 12 and the notebook of Chapter 20 — are corrected to that table, and every specific day count in Chapters 14–20 has been made to agree with it.

## What the next batch inherits

The calendar itself: **day 19** is where Chapter 20 ends, and **day 20 is Chapter 21**. The hearing is **dated**: three days after the next tide, and the next tide is **day 28**, nine days out from the end of Chapter 20, which puts the hearing on **day 31**. The field review is **day 25**, six days out, and is the day a woman in Corrow decides how much of her life to put in the ground. Marrow's written offer expires on that same day. Also running: Dante's arm clock, which stops being careful on day 21 and goes about day 31. The ground under the chapel is boarded and continues under the river bank. The main breach steps when the lock is worked, and the only known remedy is a flood. The interface has no memory of a refusal, is current to the hour, and its costs are edited. A sentence Adrian **deleted** came out of a stone in another world, and the file it was in no longer exists, so either something has been inside him since Tuesday or something read a document that was not there. Owen's light is still unverified and still seen by one man. Nineteen stones and eight missing name stones are on the same list and belong to Roke. And the man in the seat owes a field, owes a village, owes a room he cannot leave, and is further from home than he was ten chapters ago and considerably more use.
