# Character State

Compact live state for the next batch. Updated after Batch 0003 (Chapters 21–30). Private want, current pressure, physical state, and what the last ten chapters cost them. The full character bible is `bible/characters.md`; the long arc is `bible/premise.md`.

## Adrian Vale — 32, protagonist

- **Stage 1, Witness.** Perceives the nearest join-line and understands one direct Veyran promise at a time. Cannot create a working, open a threshold, or hold a place. **Unbound — no workway, refused twice in rooms with witnesses and re-refused out loud in a hall of three hundred people on day 30**, with the reason given in full and entered.
- **Mark:** the pale fragment-mark is in the maintenance seam; the palm keeps a thin trace. Under it, separate, is a **thin black line two inches long** opened on the second night, now **thirty days old, and unmoved by one sixteenth of an inch through the whole batch, including while a chapel floor came apart.** He wrote on day 25 that it is not warning him; it is keeping score.
- **Seat:** named holder of the pull, **readmitted on day 30 for a further term** with a different cost column: the yard's standing labour twice a day with him as the nearest, a new ledger page for the second group that he may not move and may not neglect, and a review on a named date.
- **Debt:** a season of the bottom field, entered day 3 at its smallest, **continued and enlarged on day 30 with no final figure**, on the grounds that a debt that can be closed is a debt that can be traded out. He is deeper in the book than he was ten days ago and said so himself.
- **Post:** for as long as the ground is open, the ground does not open without him. He was present at every beginning in the batch. He has noticed that the post is now the reason the instrument can be operated at all and he hates it.
- **Public record:** page one against his name; the roll of strangers in Aurel says **Unbound**; on day 30 he said in a hall that he does not know whether a first license may exist in Saltwake, for the second time, and asked that the next person to ask him be somebody not in the hall; on day 25 he said *there is no* in front of two hundred people when asked by a boy of nineteen whether this is a game, and Roke entered all of it unsoftened; on day 31 he said *it is a place where one person at a time can be named, out loud, by other people, before they go somewhere*, and that the price of a door in this city is the crew.
- **What he has invented:** the form for a claim that cannot be tested, with Saye's sixth clause, on the loft wall; the twelve-day answerable list, which he cannot execute; and — with a notary — the Concord's first **statement of error**, in which he is barred from entering one against his own seat.
- **Body:** cut hand from the Chapter 2 spike, healing. Left ear still ringing. A headache behind the left eye whenever the reading runs. **A nosebleed is no longer the signal; the signal is that the reading now costs him about four minutes.** He has not slept properly since the flood began and he is thirty-one days in.
- **What he wants now:** to know which of his four readings of page forty-one is true; a road home that he no longer believes is coming; and to be asked the question by somebody who is not in the room. He has stopped expecting the interface and has not written down the date he stopped, because he did it in a yard without noticing.
- **What he has learned about himself this batch:** that a rule about people which he made about *them* rather than with them was the worst rule on the wall, and that the repair is a **refusal clause**; that being at a counter means being the man who finds out somebody has not turned up; that the price of a door in a city is the crew, and the crew is nineteen men; that the honest answer to a debt is sometimes to refuse to be forgiven; and that the fourth reading of his own mystery requires no conspiracy and only him.
- **Voice:** flatter, shorter, more willing to say *I do not know* in a room and mean it. Slips: *cohort* (stopped in public by Priya), *onboarding* (stopped in public by himself, in a hall, in front of four hundred people), *a machine somewhere* (Chapter 25, refused on purpose in the same sentence). Never says *quest*.

## Tamsin Quill — 29, anchorwright

- **State:** working partner, not lover. She checked him in public four times in ten days and he let her every time, and in Chapter 26 she told him to use English in a hall and in Chapter 29 she told him in front of two hundred people that he was wrong about the field. He asked her, in Chapter 28, to be the person who keeps page forty-one.
- **What she did this batch:** made him learn his own vocabulary in public; refused to translate a working for a missing child and said the sentence that ended it — *a door is not a search party*; read the eleven-line boundary table out in a yard for the second time in her life and got the answer; was the only person in the city who was useful in the first hour at the meeting-ground; and in Chapter 26 said, to a nineteen-year-old who was being brave for the wrong reason, *ask, and tell me what it costs to say no.*
- **Her exposed flank:** line seven of the boundary table, and about forty people in the Reach now know her name is on it, and in Chapter 22 she said it herself on a road rather than waiting for it to be found out.
- **New:** she now holds Adrian's page forty-one. She has read it. She has said nothing about it.
- **Voice:** short sentences, level voice that gets worse when she is least angry. Says a thing once and does not repeat it. In Chapter 30 she named the Concord's first procedure **say it out loud again**, and it is in the ledger.

## Mina Okoye — 29, first-cohort medic

- **Injuries:** none new. Working four days a week in Saye's room.
- **What changed:** she was the hardest voice in the Chapter 27 argument and she was right — the first opening in this country will be remembered as the one spent on the day a child was missing. She held a flat flagstone for five hours and refused a bench twice. She named the true thing about Owen's arm: **a hold is a working without a witness and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.** She knows Saye's arm rule beat the arm by a week.
- **Pressure:** her rule now runs in three parts — numbers as *what we said*; names as *what the person said about themselves*; and the harm clause, which she extracted from Adrian's form and which is now a clause against a city. She put Wren Halloway on a step and kept her there.
- **Want:** to get back to her family and to be proven right about something other than a river. **Fear:** that a hold she did not call in time is a working nobody agreed to.

## Dante Kuo — 35, Hearthguard captain in everything but name

- **Injuries:** right forearm reduced day 10, fine. **Right shoulder dislocated day 25, reduced twice, put in at two minutes past two, and not settable because there is nothing out of place.** He will not be able to put both hands above his head; he will not notice for a year. He does not die in this volume.
- **What changed:** he stopped being careful on day 21, deliberately, in front of people, and nobody stopped him. He corrected Jonah Pike in nine words — *you'd be sending them because they're cheap; that's what cheap means.* He got to the bottom of the field on the twenty-seventh and could not go up the walk-round with the others, and did not go, and did not say anything about it. **He was the one person in the batch who was made safer by Ivenn Marrow without being told**, because Marrow put him on the yard work and the ledger and never once let him lift anything.
- **Pressure:** the rescue argument with Adrian is still owed. He is now physically a man who can be hurt by a day's work rather than by an event, which is exactly the thing he was told on day 10 and did not believe.
- **Voice:** short, unfair, usually right, and he has learned to say the nine words and stop.

## Priya Shah — 24, first public record

- **Injuries:** forearm, healed.
- **What changed:** she stopped a word in public. *Cohort* is a word for a thing that was selected, the first six came out of a hole in a numbered order too, and she had been ashamed of saying it for twenty-five days. She then wrote down that **a document which is internally consistent and which can only be checked by one person is not a record, it is a story with a seal on it**, and a notary entered the idea into the Concord's book. She was the first person in the room to see the number eleven arrive on a market board and the first to say it could not be got off.
- **Standing:** her log now carries a refused gatekeeper offer, a refused Brass Measure deal, a refused workway she wanted, Owen's refused offer, Adrian's second refusal, the public statement she did not write, and **three withdrawals by Jonah Pike, all in public, all at her insistence.**
- **Fear:** that the whole thing was a bit and she made people laugh. Now also: that the second group will be called her people, and that she will enjoy it.

## Jonah Pike — 42, the colonial mirror

- **Injuries:** none. He arrived dry and has stayed dry.
- **What changed:** three arguments and three withdrawals, all in a room, all entered, all at somebody else's insistence — the account of the other side in a market, an immediate structure for the second group, and sending somebody with nothing here. He has begun to say the withdrawals himself before they are forced out of him, which is the first honest sign in the book that he is capable of the ending.
- **Want:** a safe, recognisable settlement where the seven can live without depending on an indifferent Earth. He has not got it and he has begun to suspect the settlement is the thing that arrives instead of the home. **The White Ledger is four volumes away and this is where it starts.**
- **His ninety-per-cent rule:** he is right about nine parts in ten of almost everything, and the tenth part is always the same part, and the tenth part is always a local woman's word for a social fact. *Renna Ord's word is* order. *Dante Kuo's is* cheap.

## Owen Park — 36, security contractor

- **Injuries:** scalp from the wall, **unclosed on the twenty-ninth day and not resolved.** **Right shoulder dislocated day 25, not settable, and he will not know he is overloading it.** He does not die in this batch.
- **Private light:** he alone perceives a relic-shaped light. He has been to the bottom of the field twice. On the twenty-seventh he stood on a stake for a hundred and ten minutes at four in the morning in the rain and told Adrian exactly where it was, and Adrian saw nothing, and neither of them mentioned it for two days. **His statement is on the wall in Adrian's form with no witness chain on it, because he had not told anybody and the form does not allow a witness who has not been asked.**
- **What changed this batch, four times:**
  - He had stopped asking to go anywhere and had started making other people go — a mason over lime, a boy of nineteen with a flask of tea at half past three in the morning — and **Renna Ord named it as spending three men, and Adrian admitted in public that he watched it for four days and did not stop it.**
  - He asked for the only thing he has ever asked anybody for, and asked it properly, and it was not the wall: *come and stand in that field with me at four in the morning and tell me afterwards whether you can see anything.* Adrian went. He saw nothing. He spent a hundred and ten minutes failing to notice that Owen was in pain, and said so in a hall, unprompted, to a woman who had not asked.
  - He said the true thing about his own arm, in a room, in the record: that the road and the person to send are the same thing, that the fact that he cannot go is true of him for the rest of his life, and that if Eli Mercer does not come back he will say so in a book with his name on it on the day.
  - He took a child off a standing flagstone with his right shoulder in front of three hundred and ten people and lost the shoulder of that arm for the rest of his life.
- **Planned death, Chapter 38, permanent.** It must carry three causes: Adrian's underestimated crossing cost, the Chapter 25 shoulder no healer could set, and Owen's own refusal to follow instructions. He is alive at the end of this batch with a shoulder he cannot raise, a scalp that has not closed in twenty-nine days, a lease he has not signed and a landlord's condition he has still not met, a light only he can see, and a nine-year-old who follows him at six feet.

## Ivenn Marrow — 41, Quiet Choir's First Reader

- **Public face:** Crown Law scholar from Aurel, in the city eight weeks, the man who arrived before the Crown's clerks. Charming, patient, useful, and the only person in Saltwake not frightened by the floor.
- **What he actually did this batch, four things, all of them in public:**
  - He was the **most useful man in the yard on day 25** — tarpaulins, a stretcher, eleven frightened people sat down in a ring with his back to the weather, and a bonesetter he had gone and found *before* the tear, on the Thursday, because he has a list of every person in this city who could be useful in a room. He put Dante Kuo on paper work for five days and never told him, which is the single most useful thing he has done and which protected a shoulder from a cause nobody had seen coming.
  - He said aloud, in a yard, the true thing about the tear: a good working's residue arriving at a place it was not sent. **Adrian underlined it twice and it is the reason the volume's midpoint is not a villain.**
  - He **translated a frightened group's account of the other side for two hours, word for word, kept the four disagreements in, and added one sentence saying he had not removed them** — and then said, unprompted, that a man in a good coat doing the thing he is best at, in a room of frightened people, is genuinely useful, **and that this is the dangerous part.** That is the most frightening version of him yet: he is reading their account as a professional and not as a recruiter, and he is therefore genuinely useful to the people he wants to use.
  - He **took his own expired chalk off the keeper's board in daylight, in front of thirty people, and withdrew the offer in his own hand** — refusing to let it stand as a refusal, because the refusals are in the roll and the withdraw is his. He wrote that the holder's answer is better than the one he would have given, asked for the true reason to be written under it in Adrian's own hand, and added that Adrian will do it.
- **What nobody suspects:** that the prompts are his. He is the only man in Saltwake with both reason and access. **His complicity must stay plausible and unproven to the end of the volume and he must stay publicly useful. No public scene may convict him.** He has also just written, in chalk, that he expects to be argued with, and that he has nowhere else to be — which is a man building a dependency in public, in a good hand, in daylight.
