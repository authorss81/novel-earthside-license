## THE PROTAGONIST

**ADRIAN VALE. NOT IMPROVED, NOT THANKED, NOT TOLD HE WAS RIGHT, NOT ASKED ANYTHING, NOT USEFUL TO
ANYBODY.** In Volume 15 he is on eleven days and in no others: 0703, 0706, 0709, 0712, 0714, 0718,
0719, 0731, 0737, 0739 and 0743. **He is in none of the volume's seven heavy days — 725, 728, 740, 745,
748 and 750 — and no page is read out, no decision is taken, a hand goes into no place on a page, the
wall says nothing, no name is spoken, no question is asked and the volume's last image does not stand
on any day he is in.**

**THE ELEVEN COME OUT FOUR OBTAINED AND SEVEN NOT, AGAINST FIVE AND SIX IN THE VOLUME BEHIND THIS ONE.
NEITHER SET IS A REWARD AND NEITHER IS A LESSON, AND NO REVIEWER MAY READ THE FOUR AS THE VOLUME GETTING
WORSE OR THE SEVEN AS THE VOLUME GETTING BETTER.**

**"Nobody asks him anything" is the pressure of this volume and it is not the reward.** In every one of
the eleven days he must be the cause of at least one thing that happens to somebody else. The
consequence may not be a decision, may not be a word, may not be a document, may not be a notice and
may not be the panel. **On 0743 his hands are on a bar in the sockets on the far side of that door, and
what he wanted the thing for is for that door to come off its hinges so that a board could be carried
past it into that room. IT DOES NOT, HE DOES NOT TAKE IT OFF ITS HINGES, NOBODY IN THIS CITY ASKED HIM
TO AND NOBODY THANKED HIM FOR NOT DOING IT. NOT OBTAINED.**

**HIS SPENDING: the personal return mark, spent at Volume 16 Stage 5 in the series plan, which is the
only time he loses one. From here on he has none of his own and every crossing in either direction
needs other witnesses to carry him. The finale gives up the authority, not the door.**

## THE VOLUME'S PEOPLE, AND THE SECOND HANDLE EACH ONE CARRIES BESIDE THE AGE AND THE TRADE AND NEVER INSTEAD OF IT

**IT IS THE SAME HANDLE IN EVERY CHAPTER THAT PERSON IS IN, AND A CHAPTER MAY NOT DROP IT AND MAY NOT
GIVE IT A NEW ONE.**

**The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board.** A board under his arm, and nineteen rates, and
one name that is not on it. He has traded those rates in four markets for about nine years. **On day
748 he is asked, at the bottom of that stair and not by the room, what a person is for when the only
thing anybody is going to use them for is to have gone past. HE DOES NOT ANSWER IT, AND THE NARRATOR
DOES NOT SAY WHAT HE THOUGHT, AND NO CHAPTER OF THIS VOLUME ASKS HIM WHAT THE MISSING NAME IS.** The
space about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two on his own board is not
mentioned on day 748 and is on nobody's page.

**The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.** A board standing face in against his own trestle
with the bare back of it turned out into the row behind his shoulder, a front two stalls along, and a
piece of chalk he does not use. **The chalk has not come out of his own inside breast pocket since the
six hundred and seventy-eighth day and is in that pocket at every hour of all fifty days including day
750. NO CHAPTER TAKES IT OUT.** He is the one person in this city a room could have sent to find out
what one woman did with her own feet, and he was not sent. **He is the man who ruled out the row out
loud, and he paid for it in his own mouth in seven words, and was thanked by nobody.**

**The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register.** Her second slate, and a column on the
right of one page with a word in it near the head of it that is not hers, and **the front door of the
building she goes in at every morning.** **ON DAY 745 SHE DOES NOT GO IN AT THAT FRONT, AND THE CHAPTER
MAY NOT SAY WHY SHE KNEW ANYTHING.** She has paid two costs in this volume — the first on day 727, the
second on day 747 in her own words in front of about nine people with nothing done about it at any hour
of that day. **What is proved about her and the row is only that she has never been on it.**

**The woman of about fifty-two.** A door she is not asked to open, and a stool she carried out of a
room and left against a far wall. **A man sat down on that stool on day 741 and did not know whose it
was, and she said nothing, and it was left where his weight had turned it.**

**The man of about twenty-seven.** A bucket and a rag, and a wall he washed once and has not washed
since. **On day 750 he washes the store's board once and not twice, and says out loud that nobody has
ever asked him why he washed that one once and stopped.**

**The man of about thirty-eight at the wharf.** A tally-board, a knife, a barrow, and a figure on the
side the sun gets to. **He loses the morning of day 743 to a bar he cannot get a board past and takes
that board off up the market on his shoulder at the tenth hour with his barrow standing empty behind
him.**

**The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living.** A satchel with a flap, and one thing
he knows what is in. **No chapter of this volume opens it, and no chapter says why he opens nothing he
carries.**

**The man of about fifty-seven.** A length of new rope he has not used. **He stands in his own doorway
with his hands on his knees and NO HAND IN THIS VOLUME LIFTS THAT ROPE, AND HE IS NOT ASKED ANYTHING
ON ANY OF THE FIFTY DAYS.**

**The man of about sixty.** A hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, a coil of rope that has been
used, and a handcart. **He put the fence up and it is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies and it is
never measured and no post moves.**

**The man of about forty-one in a very good coat.** A document inside the coat. **The man of about
fifty-nine.** A hurdle he made and a bar he replaced. **The woman of about sixty-nine.** A tin.
**Tamsin Quill.** A folded sheet of road with about nine places on it.

## THE HANDLES THAT ARE NOT PEOPLE, AND MAY NOT BE GIVEN A FACE

**THE WOMAN WHO WALKED A MARKET** is a slate under an arm and a tin in her two hands, no age, no number
and no descriptor on any page, **and she is on no page of Volume 15 at all. A ROW IS THE WORST PLACE IN
THIS MANUSCRIPT TO PUT HER, AND DAY 750'S LAST IMAGE IS A ROW.**

**THE ONE OF ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE A DAY WHO STOPPED AT THAT DOOR ON DAY 695 IS A HANDLE AND NOT A PERSON.**
No chapter of this volume gives that person a word, a face, a line, an age, a number, a name, a
descriptor or a second appearance, and no chapter joins that person to anybody. **ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE A DAY
IS A CENSUS AND NOT A PERSON AND MAY NOT BE GIVEN A DESCRIPTOR, MAY NOT BE REDUCED TO *FOUR PEOPLE A
DAY* — THE WORD *ABOUT* IS THE INSTRUMENT AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL — AND MAY NOT BE USED AS A COUNT OF
ANYBODY.** The person who stops in a chapter of this volume is a different person and is also a handle
and not a person, and a chapter that makes the two the same person has invented a person and resolved
the census at the same time.

## THE STANDING DISAGREEMENTS, CARRIED AND NOT SETTLED

**THE STALLKEEPER OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR IS TWO PEOPLE IN THIS REPOSITORY AND NO PAGE HAS EVER SAID SO.**
The plan and the pages say *man*. The state layer and two close records say *woman*. A third close
record says the man is a fifth person distinct from four women of about thirty-four. **NO CHAPTER PUTS
THE TWO IN ONE ROOM, MAY ASK EITHER ABOUT THE OTHER, OR MAY USE A DESCRIPTOR THAT SETTLES IT. The
person this volume spends a thing on is one of them and nobody knows which, and neither does the plan.
Nine volumes have not settled it and this volume did not.**

**THE TWO MEN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT ARE TWO PEOPLE.** A chapter may not merge them or settle them in
narration. **THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN IS NOT AVAILABLE TO ANYBODY.** The man who trades on the
board is thirty-nine, the man who keeps a stall is thirty-four, and the person of about fifty-two is a
woman. **THE TWO WOMEN OF TWENTY-NINE ARE NEVER IN ONE ROOM AND NOTHING SETTLES WHETHER THEY ARE ONE
WOMAN.**

## WHAT THE REVIEW REPAIR PASS ON DAYS 741 TO 750 DID TO THE PEOPLE ON THIS PAGE

**NOTHING ABOVE MOVED. NOBODY CHANGED. NOT A DESCRIPTOR, NOT AN AGE, NOT A NUMBER, NOT A PLACE, NOT A
NAME, NOT A HANDLE, AND NOT ONE OF THE STANDING DISAGREEMENTS WAS SETTLED.**

is where she keeps them, and a checkpoint pass had briefly put them *flat against the sides of it*,
which is not a thing a hand can do.**
