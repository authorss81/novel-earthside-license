# Character State

Compact live state for the next batch. Updated after Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20). Private want, current pressure, physical state, and what the last ten chapters cost them. The full character bible is `bible/characters.md`; the long arc is `bible/premise.md`.

## Adrian Vale — 32, protagonist

- **Stage 1, Witness.** Perceives the nearest join-line and understands one direct Veyran promise at a time. Cannot create a working, open a threshold, or hold a place. **Unbound — no workway, by choice, twice refused in rooms with witnesses.**
- **Mark:** the pale fragment-mark is in the maintenance seam; the palm keeps a thin trace running out toward the wrist. Under it, separate from it, is a **thin black line two inches long** that opened in Chapter 8, has not moved in three weeks, and is invisible to everyone because nobody steps over a hand. No return mark. No route. No way home.
- **Seat:** named holder of the pull on an instrument that is entered, costed, inspected, signed, and **formally unrecognised**. A seat, in Nell Bray's sense: *a place to be angry at. A seat has to be legible to a person who does not like you.*
- **Debt:** a season of the bottom field, enforceable, reviewable on the last day of the salt month — and it is **an open account, not a fine**, and it is still being spent four miles downstream with nobody in the field.
- **Post:** for as long as the ground under the chapel is open, the ground does not open without him. Not money. Not useful. No end of it in sight.
- **Public record:** the first complaint is entered on page one against his name, with the words *and he was asked in public*. The roll of strangers in Aurel says **Unbound**. His public answer to *what is the Narrow Mark for* is *I do not know*, and he asked to be asked it again in a month and again in a year.
- **Body:** cut hand from the Chapter 2 spike. Left ear still ringing. A headache behind the left eye whenever the reading runs. A nosebleed four minutes into a hole in Chapter 15, the first return of the Chapter 4 cost. He has not slept properly in three weeks.
- **What he wants now:** a way home for six people who are not his; an answer to where the sentence in the voice came from; and to be told he was wrong about the lock's ordinary work before somebody else is hurt by it. He has stopped looking for the pale field and written down the date he stopped.
- **What he has learned about himself:** that a refusal is a loss of capability first and a gain of standing second, in that order. That the thing he is best at is finding a cheap reversible action, and the dangerous half is always the two clauses downstream. That arranging other people's work is a kindness for him, and that the test of a rota is whether it survives him. That holding a seat is not running anything, and that being legible to a person who dislikes him is the whole dignity of it. That he is frightened of the correct thing, and that Vask is the only person in this world frightened of the same object.
- **Voice:** flat, technical, deflects with design language. Says "in the flat voice" as a tic. Crosses out his own conclusions rather than erasing them, and deliberately kept nine crossed-out drafts of one answer. Says "Ask the people who maintain the gate" at the bottom of most pages.

## Tamsin Quill — 29, anchorwright

- **State:** working partner, not lover. She checks him and told him she will go on checking; he has been checkable in public for three weeks and it has cost him twice.
- **What she did this batch:** read the **eleven-line boundary table** aloud in a yard for the first time in seven years, which is the act that reframes Ivenn's offer; said out loud that she is the only person in this city who can say his name in a way a stranger understands, and that everything he has said for three weeks has gone through her mouth into a market; refused to translate one of Ivenn's sentences in the local language on purpose, to make it plain what it was; and told him in front of the room that she had predicted the thing he was about to get wrong.
- **Trust ledger:** he let her correct him. He asked her for the timeline out loud where the others could hear it. He did not fill in the Crown's form. He wrote the four rules under the public statement at her request. She is the one he would put in a chair if he ever took a chair, and he told her so on a road in the dark.
- **Her exposed flank:** the boundary table has her name on line seven, from when she was twenty-one, and she has now read it out in public. That is a document later chapters can use.
- **Voice:** short sentences, level voice that gets worse when she is least angry. Says a thing once and does not repeat it.

## Mina Okoye — 29, first-cohort medic

- **Injuries:** a four-inch thigh graze from the wall, dressed and closed. Working.
- **What changed:** she has been **re-taught by Hesper Saye** in the low room by the salt wharf, found out she had been washing in river water on the strength of an Earthside textbook, and now works four days a week in a room where she can be wrong in front of people who can correct her. SURVIVABLE is still in her book in capitals.
- **Pressure:** her rule now runs in two parts — every number as *what we said*, never as *what it was*; and every name as *what the person said about themselves*, never as *what the room knows*. She took the six fears off a public wall in Chapter 12 and she was right, and Adrian has accepted that she overruled him.
- **Want:** to get back to her family and to prove practical knowledge beats a spectacular class. **In Chapter 16 she argued in a room of witnesses that Adrian should take the workway** — because a man with a duty is safer than a man with nothing owed to him — and she asked for it to be entered in her own words. Fear: being unable to tell a patient she can save from one she has already failed.
- **New:** Adrian chose to tell her something frightening at half past six, before the other five, because she was the person who had overruled him and been right.

## Dante Kuo — 35, Hearthguard captain in everything but name

- **Injuries:** **right forearm reduced** on the Thursday of Chapter 14, three days late, by Hesper Saye, with the arm held against his chest for three days. Saye's rule: the shoulder has been doing the arm's work for three days without being asked, and in three weeks he will lift a sack and something will go that is neither the arm nor the sack. Eleven days of care, then he stops being careful. He is on about day six.
- **Pressure:** he learned from Renna Ord that a crew is a **chain of asking**, not a chain of command, and that asking costs him the right to ask again for nine days. He works by rota now. He works by rota because a rota is a set of people who have already agreed to stand in a place at an hour, and everything Adrian builds has to look like that or it is not a rota.
- **Want:** keep the seven alive and prove that a disciplined plan can beat panic. Fear: being remembered as the man who ordered others into a disaster. The rescue argument with Adrian is still owed.
- **New:** in Chapter 14 he said "yeah" to Adrian admitting he had been assigning work for nine days without asking, and did not finish it, and Adrian let it stand.

## Priya Shah — 24, first public record

- **Injuries:** forearm gash, four stitches with a sewing needle and boiling water.
- **What changed:** she wrote the six names in Chapter 5, the sheet went up in Chapter 6, and in Chapter 12 a man in a salt hall said her name at the back of a room. The investigation found the mechanism was never a leak: **Tamsin read the whole sheet aloud in a market eleven times on the Wednesday**, because the rules do not work if you only know them in your own language, and a genuine three-hundred-and-ten-year-old customs document listing six things that came out of a hole made new gossip sound like a roll. Priya wanted it suppressed and Jonah wanted it run and Adrian was wrong about the timing. **Mina overruled him and took the fears off the wall and left the names up.**
- **Standing:** her log has a refused gatekeeper offer, a refused Brass Measure deal in one sentence with Jonah's name on it, Owen's offer on the relic question, Adrian's refusal of the workway twice, a public statement she did not write and cannot retract, and now a third refusal — hers — on the workway, in which she says she wanted it and refused because Owen is the only person in the room who cannot be got to do anything.
- **Fear:** that the whole thing was a bit and she made people laugh and now somebody is dead. Now also: that her own name is the one in the market.

## Jonah Pike — 42, the colonial mirror

- **Injuries:** none. He arrived dry.
- **What changed:** the Brass Measure refused **in one sentence in a yard**, at Priya's instruction, after he had done the sums and wanted it enormously and told the room so — *and in about eleven months I would walk into the chandlery on the upper road with three duties and a settlement behind me, and every one of the five of you would be proud of me, and I would not have noticed the day it stopped being for the group.* He put the sentence in Priya's log with his own name on it and has not mentioned it since. He is also the only person who says publicly that the route is worth something, and the only one who understood in Chapter 19 what could be done with Adrian's measurement — write to Aurel and end it, permanently, for everybody — and took **two hours** to decide not to, and asked for that to be logged.
- **Want:** a safe, recognizable settlement where the seven can live without depending on an indifferent Earth. Fear: another failed attempt at making a home for people who have already lost one. **The White Ledger is four volumes away and this is where it starts.**

## Noor Haddad — 27, quartermaster

- **Injuries:** dust inhalation, aspirating, sick twice, recovered.
- **What changed:** she added the column nobody adds in the first week — *who gave it to us and what they expect* — and found the group had been eating out of the market's widows' box for nine days without knowing, carried up a stair in a cloth at six in the morning by a woman who could have said. She made Sesh Brack say it out loud in front of her cousin, and entered it as *unrecorded* and *she chose to be found*. **Her principle: you do not get to decide what somebody's kindness cost. You ask them. Then it goes in a column and it is a debt and not a rumour.** In Chapter 16 she asked the best question of the batch — *who does the job?* — because a workway is a duty with a shape and a duty with a shape displaces a duty, and nobody had asked the man it lands on.
- **Telling:** her gift is noticing who has been left out of a distribution. She is the reason the trial license will have a public cost record.

## Owen Park — 36, security contractor

- **Injuries:** cut scalp from the wall, from the moment he came through it. Now diagnosed by Hesper Saye as **unclosed** — nine days of clean skin over it, nothing happening underneath, and her word for it is *unclosed*, which is a promise with a witness on it that nobody has taken up. **His shoulder is untouched and is reserved for Chapter 25.** He has a cold and no other complaint.
- **Private light:** he alone perceives a relic-shaped light through the damaged wall, coin- or seal-shaped, standing on its edge. It walked away from the wall after the arrival. His statement is in the public ledger marked **unverified** and stays there.
- **What changed this batch:** three times.
  - He tried to buy the freehold of the building the loft is the top of, and was stopped by a widow with a nine-year-old in her own doorway, calmly, in a way that could not be handled as a fight. She offered a twelve-month lease on the two upper rooms in his own hand with two conditions: tell Sesh Brack in her kitchen with her in the room, and go up the street and ask Sesh whether the roof over her head is the roof being sold. **Neither condition is met.**
  - He learned the keeper's four words and the fifth, and says them in the yard every morning at six in the rain, with a mason correcting him every eight seconds. The first attempt of the morning does not work. The second does. Both facts are on the board.
  - He said the true thing about the lock: it is not the stone, it is the words forty men say twice a day under the near gate; the man in the join did not build that pattern, he found it. **So the field is a toll and somebody is being charged, and the only question left is by whom and whether they agreed to it.**
  - In Chapter 17 he was the only person who backed Adrian's public "I do not know", and asked for the story of the missed Saturday inspection to be told accurately, because in a month one of them will tell it wrong.
- **Planned death, Chapter 38, permanent.** It must carry three causes: Adrian's underestimated crossing cost, the Chapter 25 shoulder no healer could set, and Owen's own refusal to follow instructions. He is alive at the end of this batch, with a scalp that will not close and a lease he has not signed.

## Ivenn Marrow — 41, Quiet Choir's First Reader

- **Public face:** Crown Law scholar from Aurel, six weeks and then some in the city, the man who arrived before the Crown's clerks. Charming, patient, useful, and the only person in Saltwake not frightened by the wall.
- **What he actually did this batch:**
  - Read the omitted cost of the workway out loud in a room with witnesses, accurately, unasked, and then argued that the cost was worth paying — and that Adrian had no standing to refuse on behalf of six people.
  - Told a room of nine on the Wednesday of the second week that the three-hundred-and-ten-year-old customs document was a fraud, when it is genuine, and let them believe the whole of it was fake.
  - **Put his own open offer in chalk on the keeper's board, in his own hand, with a date on it**, expiring on the last day of the salt month, and said that an offer with no shape in it is a rumour a decent man can walk away from.
  - Said the true thing about why he wants Adrian to take the workway: not control, but **tethering** — a man who belongs to a place can be asked questions by that place, and Adrian is a man nobody in Saltwake is owed anything by and can therefore always be talked round. And he dressed it as a duty because in this city a chain sounds like an insult and a duty is a trade.
- **What nobody suspects:** that the prompts are his. He is the only man in Saltwake with both reason and access. **His complicity must stay plausible and unproven to the end of the volume and he must stay publicly useful. No public scene may convict him.**
