## VOLUME 14 BATCH 0002 — WHAT THE REVIEW REPAIR DID TO WHAT EACH OF THESE PEOPLE IS, AND WHAT IT DID NOT DO

**The second handle survived all nine of its rewrites. That is the single most important sentence in this block: the identifying content — age, trade, and the objects that belong to that person — is in every chapter it was in before, and only the sentence that carried it moved. A character who is identified in the same nine places with the same nine objects is the same character.**

- **The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.** Handle: a board standing face in against his own trestle, and a piece of chalk he does not use. **The handle now appears in five different sets of words across 0661, 0663, 0664, 0665, 0667, 0668 and 0670 and is not in 0662.** He never loses the chalk, never has it out, never has it in one hand with the store's chalk. His two bolded turns in 0668 and 0670 are unaltered word for word. **What the repair changed is that he now exists in a row where somebody stopped to shift a basket and he chose not to turn the board round while they were standing there, and nobody remarked on it either.** That is a small act inside a man who is not asked anything and it is not a decision of record and it does not go near the space under the date.
- **The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register.** Handle: her second slate, and the column on the right of one page with a word in it near the head of it that is not hers. **The handle stands in 0663, 0664, 0667, 0668 and 0669 and is in no other chapter.** Her two bolded turns in each of 0663 and 0669 are unaltered. **What the repair changed is that her two descriptions in 0663 and 0669, which had been word for word identical, are now two descriptions of the same facts, and that she now carries a pail out to the door in 0669.** She is still not asked what the column is for, what the word means, or what the space under the date is for, on any of these ten days, and she is still not on a page where anybody answers any of it.
- **The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board.** Handle: a bucket, a rag, and a wall washed once in the whole of this flood and not since. **The handle now appears in four different sets of words across 0661, 0662, 0667 and 0670 and is not a single sentence in any of them.** His two bolded turns in 0662 and 0667 are unaltered. **What the repair changed is that in 0667 he moves a stranger's crate off the channel run without either of them saying anything, and the chapter still washes no wall and still puts no hand on one.**
- **The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board.** Handle: a board under his arm, nineteen rates, one name not on it. **Unaltered in substance and reworded in 0663 and 0666.** His two bolded turns in 0666 are unaltered word for word. **What the repair added is one beat of waiting: in 0666 the wharf man does not move his barrow until the tradesman has finished, and after the tradesman has gone round the corner the wharf man puts his own hand on the belt where his knife is and takes it off again.** Nobody asked either of them anything and neither is thanked and the missing name is not printed.
- **The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living.** Handle: a satchel with a flap and one thing he knows what is in. **Reworded in 0664 and 0669 and identical in substance in both.** His two bolded turns in 0664 are unaltered word for word. **What the repair changed is his closing: he now holds the strap out in front of him going down the stair so that his own back hides the satchel from anybody coming up, instead of a sentence about keeping the shut.** He opens nothing. The reason he opens nothing is still not given and is still on no page in that room and nobody asks him for it.
- **The man of about thirty-eight at the wharf.** Handle: a tally-board, a knife, a barrow, a figure on the side the sun gets to. **Reworded in 0661, 0665 and 0666 and identical in substance in all three.** His two bolded turns in each of 0661 and 0665 are unaltered word for word. **What the repair added is a beat of waiting and a beat of touching a belt in 0666.** He still wipes nothing, counts nothing, marks nothing on his tally-board, prints no figure, traces no line to a hand and is asked nothing.
- **Adrian Vale.** **Not edited in any respect and it is published that he was not, because a repair that improves a chapter must not go looking for the protagonist to make him better.** Stage 2 on all ten days, in two of them, 0661 and 0665, as the map's column says. A shifted stack with a rolled barrow caught in one, a board leaned on the wrong stack in the other, thanked in none, told he was right in none, offered no workway in either, and both wants not obtained.
