# Volume 02 Close Record — *Salt and Silver*, Chapters 51–100

Day 1 was the Tuesday Adrian Vale came out of the wall and **day 100 is the hundredth day of the flood and a Wednesday**, and he is standing four feet back from the wall of the second bay in an empty gallery at about the ninth hour of the evening having said a number out loud to nobody. The undertaking of the Lantern Concord is on a nail, dry, unmarked, with the string untouched, and it went down a river in a wooden case three days before it was voided — out on the sixty-fourth day of the new month, which was the eighty-ninth day of the flood, and void on the ninety-second — and a hearing is about eight weeks off. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid. A woman of forty-eight in a fourth house at Nine Elms has been clearing a drain by herself every morning since the twenty-ninth day of the new month and has not been asked whether she will carry any part of the standing charge, and this city chose not to ask her and wrote the choice on a stone with a date on it.

This is the one page the next phase has to read before it plans anything.

---

## 1. What Volume 02 answered

**The shape got a name, and the name is two words, and it was said in a room in daylight with a date on it by the most useful man in the city, four days early and in a room that was not his, and he entered that he cannot tell anybody afterwards whether he said it to help this city or to file it.** *Single Witness* is the patch. *First Grammar* is what carries it. He has never seen the second written in a book. **He is not convicted of anything, is not going to be, and is not made useless in any scene, and his room is in about fourteen weeks and he has still not mentioned the letter to Hattie Park after sixty days.**

**Ivenn's false oath was voided by a local debt, by nine witnesses, and by a page that cannot say why.** The undertaking of the Lantern Concord made a body of about a hundred and forty people the permanent holder of the licence in exchange for taking the whole standing charge off a private debtor, at no cost to anybody, and every clause in it is a sentence somebody in this city said out loud in the preceding eighty-four days. **Its own condition was in its own first sentence and the condition was not met, and the whole of it took about nineteen minutes in a room with about two hundred and forty people in it.** Nobody was convicted. **The Concord did not lose the lock, the roll, the notary or the gauge and gained a term it has never had: a day on it that it did not choose, on which somebody else ended a thing it had agreed to.** It is a loss and a notary of fifty gave it to them anyway. The undertaking is on a nail, unmarked. The only place the voidness exists is a notary's record.

**The cost of that was a person, and the person is Adrian Vale, and the batch says so on the page.** **He is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing and not in a ceremony.** He can open a single-holder threshold: one named living person per opening, one return mark carried, willing local witnesses who do not live with the person, a receiving community that has agreed, a fresh inspection, and **the same route may not be opened twice in a row by him.** **No threshold was opened in this volume. He does not reach Stage 3 in it.** What he lost is **the knowing and not a road**: the idea that if he ever wanted to go back there was a way to work it out, gone in about four minutes in a room, and not coming back. **He has no return mark, he is on no roster of anything, and the route home goes through a room with no door.** He perceives a third line in the lower gallery that goes eleven inches from the dressed edge and stops, and may not use it and may not stop seeing it. **The easy route home is spent once and is not offered again in this series.**

**A person at the top of a page cannot be asked whether they will have it, and Adrian Vale is the first person in this world to find that out the hard way, and nobody may rescue him from it.** The only instrument that could have asked him is the page that has him in it, and that page does not ask, and nobody can make it ask, and that is not a fault in the page. **Any later instrument that puts a person at the top of a page inherits the same hole, and a writer who wants a working performed by a named holder must find a different instrument and say how the asking happens.**

**Two Wayhouses and one Open Hand exchange stand under local law, and none of them is his.** A bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, on a levy on the road's trade agreed among the traders, at a price of a quarter of a pound of salt and an hour of washing, **open to a list of nobody**, closable by its own household for its own reasons on a date on the page, and not asked why twice. One is on the upper road, kept by **Ilsa Roke, fifty-one**; one is at the fork at Four Elms, kept by a household that will not put its name on anything. **A refusal was exercised in public, in front of about nine people, for a reason on a page, and nobody overrode it, and the person refused came back the next morning to read his own refusal out in his own words.** The Open Hand has **no share, no interest, and a right of refusal that cannot be bought out**, and a man of forty-two argued that the third of the three cannot be administered and was right, and it is still the only one of the three worth having, because **it is the first rule anybody in this city has brought that nobody is going to check, and a rule nobody checks is a rule that everybody can get out from under.**

**The volume's central refusal is dated, and that is the last and best fact in it.** A holder of a workway refused the second asking and not the work, in her own words, before anybody asked her anything, and put one line on a page and would not let anybody make it a form. **Nobody may ask her about it again.** A notary entered that she drafted the procedure and drafted the amendment and may not grade her own paper. And then, at the flood-season review of the licence, the four words *nobody has asked them* — which for twenty-four days had been a refusal, and a refusal is not entered anywhere — went onto a page with a heading and a date. **A refusal with a date on it is an answer. An answer is a description. A description is a removal with a column for where the person is. There is now a column for where the seventeen are with a space under the date for an officer's hand nine hundred miles away. Nobody undid it and nobody could.**

**And the volume's gains are the volume's bills.** The fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the forty-first time and now has a reason. The cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth, **because the join stopped being worked on the first of March and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason.** The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand, one ruled line left, **and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken on the first of March, on the ninth day of the month after this one, or on any day in this volume.** The door has been opened twice in a hundred days and the hundredth day was not one of them.

## 2. What Volume 02 deliberately did not answer

None of these is a gap. Each is a live question and each was protected in prose.

- **Whether a page that ends a document can be understood.** It cannot, and the page does not say why, and the inside was folded twice and there is no second copy, and the man who carried it into the room did not read it out. **A page that cannot say why it worked is the most dangerous page in this book and it stays that way, and no chapter before Volume 06 may produce a mechanism for it.**
- **The room at the far end of the Narrow Mark.** A room with no window and no door anybody has found. About eleven people have stood in it. **It is being knocked on.** Three knocks on the night of the twenty-ninth day of the new month and four on the thirty-fifth, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody opened anything and nothing in this city was done at either hour and **neither was used as an instrument, a signal, a proof, a rescue or a reason to open anything.** Nobody has asked. *Active, and it is the series' engine.*
- **Whether there is a person on the other side of the chapel floor.** Untouched. The boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. **The form on the nail has four items, a price, and a fifth item, and none of the five can say who is on the other side or where a residue goes, and may never be made to do either.**
- **The voice.** The black line in the chapel stone said nothing on the twenty-third, forty-ninth or fiftieth day of this flood, said nothing on any day of the new month, and **did not speak in Volume 02 at all**, and it is not repaired.
- **Why do Crown records describe earlier Bright Pilgrims when no living local can say where they went?** The volume's question, and it is now sharp in a specific way. **The eighth column of the Concord's quarterly description went down a river in a wooden case on the eighth day of the month after this one carrying a question and not a name**: to which gate, in which year, on whose authority was the count of eight continued after that gate came off the roll, and what are the eight names in it. One of the eight is cut on a stone nine miles up the upper road in a Hearthguard's grandfather's hand about a hundred and forty years old. **Seven of the eight have never been looked at. Nobody living can say where the rest went.** A clerk of that office may not ask a question and may not refuse one, and a person named in a question is not told the question exists. *Active, and it is the Volume 03 question with a date on it.*
- **What is on the nineteen stones.** The copying is finished: all nineteen are in four slates that four different people can read aloud. **The meaning is unwritten and nobody has written anything on the wall.** About nine people are going up a nine-mile road on the nineteenth day of the month after this one, in a month that is not March, **because anybody may pick the work up, and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read has one of the four slates and is not repaid and is not on a roll.**
- **Marrow.** Publicly useful, privately dangerous, unaccused, and **he has handed this city the only thing it will ever have against him and has entered that he cannot tell whether he gave it to help or to file.** His room is in about fourteen weeks. He has still not mentioned the letter to Hattie Park and the count is sixty days.
- **March.** The bottom field, the woman in Corrow, and the four of the forty who will be hurt before the next year of certainty, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, in no working, and no instrument in this world can stop it.
- **Two letters.** Nora Vale in Camden and Hattie Park in Dunedin. **There is no route in this world that a letter can travel, and the other world was not named once in a hundred days of prose.**

## 3. What is live into Volume 03

1. **The next volume's question: what happened to the people whose names were removed from the first records?** The trail is laid and it is physical. The eighth column of a quarterly description is a question about eight names in a count that ran nineteen times for a gate that came off the roll, it is in a wooden case on a river, a clerk may not ask it and may not refuse it, **and a hearing is about eight weeks off and there is one hearing and one clock and the second consequence of the honest useless form is still reserved.**
2. **A page that ends one document has been used once and cannot be used again**, and about nine people in this city are going to spend four months failing to explain it, and **they must not succeed.**
3. **A name is not a refusal, and this city has described the thing that was doing the describing.** A name is a description; a description is the column at the end of the page; a column is a roll; a roll goes to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year. **A shape with a name is easier to write about than a shape without one, and about nine people in this city know it.**
4. **A person at the top of a page cannot be asked whether they will have it.** Open, unaddressed, and unresolvable by the instrument that caused it.
5. **A page in this world is a description, and about nine people have just written down where eight people are.** A slate in the lower gallery, eight sentences in eight hands, and one of them is *nobody in this city was told what he had*, and that man is to be asked again on a different day.
6. **A protection with a date on it is a description with a date on it.** The seventeen now have a column with a space under the date for an officer's hand, and the review that put it there cannot be undone.
7. **Nine Elms has not refused a fourth time and nobody has said what happens if it does**, and the water is not received and the quarter is gone and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, and the document that would have paid for it is void.
8. **A field is not paid.** Ninety-one acres, the join stopped being worked on the first of March, and **no twelfth entry and no figure on the end of any of the eleven.**
9. **Two Wayhouses and one Open Hand exchange, none of them his, all of them refusable by the people who keep them, and none of them answering for another.**
10. **The room at the far end of the Narrow Mark, the boards under the chapel, the chapel stone, the scar, and the knocks.** All untouched. All still open. The door has been opened twice in a hundred days and **the hundredth day was not one of them and the licence's review authorised no opening and shut nothing either.**
11. **A man from another world is Stage 2, is on no roster of anything, holds no title, no land right, no workway, no seat and no mark of his own, has no road, and is the man who reads the number off a wall four feet back from it and says it out loud.** The black line in his palm is ninety-nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.
12. **The interface has made twenty-seven offers across eleven batches and has now registered a refusal for the first time.** The count of refusals in a second book kept at home is twenty-three; the count at Aurel is thirteen; the difference of ten has never been made smaller. **Eleven silences in a row.**
13. **The archive of this city is nine inches of stone and a household that is nobody's servant, and it now holds three pages**, and the keeper is about sixty-one and argued for the third on behalf of a stranger who will have to keep it in about four years.

## 4. What must not be re-litigated

- **Owen Park is dead and buried and is not mentioned once in Chapters 81–100, by name or otherwise, and nothing in Volume 03 may mention him, soften him, summarise him, re-open him or turn him into a lesson. Nobody may write *he died because*.** A form may not shorten a record because a form is small. **Dante Kuo's right shoulder went on the thirty-first day of this flood, was reduced twice, cannot be set, did not go, was not made whole, and is on a page in a stone as a debt with no line that will mend it.** The struck-off name on the roll of the forty is deliberately unnamed in this series and may stay that way, and Ovin Sarr may not be put in a sentence with Dante Kuo.
- **The measurement column is not continuous.** Twenty-four readings, eight and seven-eighths, two steps, one ruled line left. **A later writer may not give it a figure that is not in `state/continuity.md`, may not move a step, and may not take the twenty-fifth reading without a reason on the page.** A dressed join does not narrow; it gets dressed.
- **One date-form set, three forms only:** the Nth day of this flood, the Nth day of the new month to the sixty-fifth, and the Nth day of the month after this one from day 91. **Never *of this month*, *of the month*, *of next month*, or a spelled-out impossible ordinal. *March* is a month and is never a date number.** Day 1 is the Tuesday Adrian came out of the wall. Day 50 is the Tuesday. The new month began on day 26. **The Nth day of the month after this one is day 90 + N, so day 101 is the eleventh.**
- **The five lock sites stay apart**, and three of them did nothing in this volume.
- **No crowd ever crosses.** One named person, one opening, a fresh inspection, two witnesses who are not of the crew, and a receiving community. **There is no working route in this world and Adrian's own Stage 2 cannot be the first one.**
- **A rescue is a working, a pattern is a toll, and a cost is a promise.** Keep those three apart in every scene.
- **Six Crown-side shapes and a seventh, and they never collapse into each other.** The Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood; the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month; the second answer of the twenty-second; the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first, which is a table and not a version of the others, with a fee of eleven ounces of silver; **and the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month, which is the fourth shape reissued with one more column — and those are two different counts.** The answer of the forty-second day accepting salt is the schedule of crossings and not a sixth. **The undertaking of the Lantern Concord is a seventh shape and belongs to this city and is now void and is not withdrawn.** **The Quiet Choir's design is a sixth shape and is not the Crown's, and it now has two names on the page and one of them is the reason Volume 02 went the way it did.**
- **The name *Single Witness* and the name *First Grammar* are on the page and may be used in prose from Chapter 101 onward, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and may not be guessed aloud by a character who has not been given them and may not be explained by Ivenn Marrow.**
- **A seat is vacant and a vacant seat is not a fault. There is nothing here to resign from and a man with nothing to resign is not a man being let go. A later chapter that wants Adrian Vale relieved of something must first give him something.**

## 5. The one thing the next phase should know about the shape of the book

Nobody in this volume got stronger. A document was ended by a page that a man drew in eleven minutes and could not explain, and the city that ended it got its water back for one day of argument and then did not, and the man who ended it lost the idea of going home. A woman of seventy said twenty-four words twice. A docker of fifty-eight who cannot read stood behind a page and asked to have her name taken back and nobody took it. A girl of seventeen refused the wall, in under a second, in the local language, and the count of refusals in a second book went to twenty-three for the first time in eleven batches. A woman who keeps a stall by the wharf and is named on nothing said the three sentences that will still be here in four months, and a man of twenty-six read his own refusal out in a road and was not angry.

**The gains are a name, a spent page, a stage, a lost road, two beds, a ledger, a question in a column, and eleven households keeping nine miles of road. None of them is a power, one of them is the price of a power, and all of them are heavier than a power.**
