## VOLUME 15, BATCH 0005 — CHAPTERS 0741 TO 0750, DAYS 741 TO 750, ONE CHAPTER TO ONE DAY

**These ten entries were written before the review repair pass and they describe the days. The
entries below them at the foot of this block record what the repair pass changed, and they are the
newest statement about these ten pages.**

**0741 (741, Sunday, the four hundred and twenty-sixth, character).** The man of about thirty-nine who
trades on a board came up the back stair with his board under his arm and sat down on a stool at the
side of the bench after turning it about, and nobody in that room told him whose stool it was. He said
out loud that he was not going to ask. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the
front at the seventh hour and said that about nine people had gone up that stair that morning carrying
nothing and that there is no reason anybody in this city could be shown for that. About four people a
day went past the row outside. The woman of about fifty-two came up at the eighth hour, saw the man on
her stool, and said nothing. He left at the tenth hour. Change: a stool is left where a man's weight
turned it, which is not where she put it.

**0742 (742, Monday, the four hundred and twenty-seventh, political).** The man of about thirty-one
who carries things for a living sat at the far end of the bench with his hand on the flap of a
satchel on the floor behind it and did not open it. The woman of about fifty-two asked the room, and
not anybody in it, whether a sheet may be carried out of that building in a person's own hand, and
nobody answered her. The stallholder answered a question that had not been put to him: nobody in that
room is going to find out what leaves that building. The man of about twenty-seven came up at the
ninth hour, looked at the one-place form on its shelf, and did not touch it. Change: the question was
asked in front of nine people and the room produced two answers to a different question.

**0743 (743, Tuesday, the four hundred and twenty-eighth, physical, ADRIAN NOT OBTAINED).** Adrian
Vale worked a bar in the sockets on the far side of that door three times and it came down into the
same two sockets. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf could not get a length of board past
it and left it standing against the outside wall, and said out loud that he was not going to stand in
the road working out whose hands were on the bar. The man of about twenty-seven put his hand on the
face of that door and said he could feel it was shut on something. The board went off up the market on
the man's shoulder at the tenth hour and nothing had gone past that door all morning. **Adrian Vale did
not take that door off its hinges at any hour of that day, nobody in this city asked him to, and nobody
thanked him for not doing it. THE WANT IS NOT OBTAINED.**

**0744 (744, Wednesday, the four hundred and twenty-ninth, discovery).** The man of about fifty-seven
stood in his own doorway with his hands on his knees and a length of new rope over the back of a chair
behind him that no hand had ever gone near. The woman of about fifty-two came up the shut road on foot
and said out loud that the rope was new and that she was not going to ask what it was for. He neither
agreed nor disagreed. The man of about sixty went past with his handcart and came back and said
nothing. Change: at the tenth hour she went back down the road and could still see his hands on his
knees from the top of it, because he had not gone in.

**0745 (745, Thursday, the four hundred and thirtieth, THE RESOLUTION).** The woman of about
twenty-nine who keeps a public register came round the front of that building to the row at the foot of
the stair instead of going in at the front, went up, sat at the near end of the bench and opened that
book. About four people went past that door that day and about four of them were her. Nobody in that
room was told and nobody asked her how she came in. Nothing was decided, nothing was written, nobody
was rescued and nobody was defeated, and there is no eighth notice in this flood. The man of about
thirty-nine and the stallholder each said one thing that was not about the row. **The only person who
could have found out what she did is the man who put her there by deciding the row is not to be asked,
and he went back to his front two stalls along and was not sent.**

**0746 (746, Friday, the four hundred and thirty-first, character).** The man of about sixty took a
handcart up onto the stones of a shut road and set it down past the near end of a fence of sixteen
willow posts and eleven withies. The woman of about fifty-two said out loud that the cord on those
withies is one cord and not eleven, that she had not counted anything, and that nobody in this city
knows where that fence stops, including the man standing at this end of it. He put both hands on the
near post where hers had been and pushed once and let go. Change: he turned the cart about and walked
it back off the stones and down the road, and no post moved and no withy came off.

**0747 (747, Saturday, the four hundred and thirty-second, THE THIRD COST, AND THE SECOND COST OF THE
PERSON WHO PAYS IT).** At the fifth hour the woman of about twenty-nine said out loud in her own mouth
in front of nine people what it had cost her, before she said anything else. Nobody thanked her,
nobody improved on one word of it, and nobody in that room did one single thing about it at any hour
of that day. The stallholder came in and said he had heard her and was not going to answer it and was
not going to tell her she was right or that she was wrong. The woman of about fifty-two came up at the
ninth hour, saw the stool, and did not move it. Change: at the tenth hour she shut the book on that one
word and set it down square on the shelf in front of the second slate, and took her hands away.

**0748 (748, Sunday, the four hundred and thirty-third, THE NEW QUESTION).** At about the sixth hour the
man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went out of that room and down that stair and asked the man
of about thirty-nine who trades on a board **what a person is for, when the only thing anybody is going
to use them for is to have gone past.** He did not ask it to that room. **He was not answered. Nobody in
that room heard it and nobody in that market heard it, and the chapter says so in the next line.** He
was thanked by nobody for not answering and nobody argued with him about it, and the narrator does not
say what he thought. He went back up and said something else to the room. She was asked nothing, and
the question does not come from her and is not about her. At the tenth hour the woman of about fifty-two
came in and sat down on her own stool and said one word to nobody.

**0749 (749, Monday, the four hundred and thirty-fourth, character).** Nobody came up that stair that
morning and nobody went down it, and the chapter says so in narration and does not say why. About four
people went past the outside of that door without going up it. At the eighth hour she said one thing out
loud to the middle of the floor and there was nobody in that room to hear it. At the ninth hour she
carried the book the length of the room, set it square on the shelf in front of the second slate, stood
with both hands on the shelf, then carried it back and put it on the bench and sat down in front of it.
Change: the book went to the shelf and came back, and about four people went past while she was
carrying it.

**0750 (750, Tuesday, the four hundred and thirty-fifth, THE VOLUME'S LAST IMAGE).** The man of about
twenty-seven washed the board on the outside wall once and not twice, and said out loud that there is a
wall above the top edge of it that he washed once in the whole of the flood and stopped. The
stallholder said out loud that he has not taken the chalk out of his pocket on one of the mornings of
the flood and is not going to start this one. Then the plan's own list of objects, in this order: the
room and its shelf and the one-place form and the register and the second slate; the wharf board face up
under two lines of grey grit with a clean piece at the near edge; the fence of sixteen willow posts and
eleven withies; the new rope over the back of a chair and the man in his own doorway at the ninth hour
with his hands on his knees; the door at the foot of that stair with a date in chalk and a bare piece
of board about as wide as a hand under it that means nothing whatever; **and a strip of ground at the
foot of that stair with about four people a day's feet on it and nothing on it and nothing said about
it.** No panel on any of these pages; the one panel of this volume is on Chapter 0740.
