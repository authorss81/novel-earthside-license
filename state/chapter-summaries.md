# Chapter Summaries

## Volume 01 — The First Door

### Chapter 0001 — The First Rule
Earth, Morrowglass Interactive workroom, a little after 2 a.m. on a Tuesday. Adrian is alone finishing the onboarding permission screen for *A Crown of Ash* after the project was cancelled out from under him; the build server is already gone, the ticket does not exist, and an all-hands about the cancellation is waiting at nine. A pale permission field appears on a dead monitor with no publisher, no target, no date, and no way to run it twice, offering a "limited Earthside test." Adrian reasons that an unaimed, unschedulable, un-previewable one-shot is not a service but a trap, and accepts it with his legal name anyway because he cannot find an audit trail and has decided the risk is his. The lamp dies, running water sounds close, and a small warm branching mark appears on the heel of his palm. He is now a **fragment-bearer**; nothing else has been granted.

### Chapter 0002 — The Wrong Kind of Loading
The Lock of Salt, dawn, a rising flood tide. Adrian wakes bruised and bleeding on a maintenance ledge in a barrel-vaulted chamber under the river, cut off from a pale seam in the wet black stone that is a depth rather than a doorway. Three dock workers — a woman in a soaked boat-coat, a young man called Ovin Sarr, and an older woman in a felt hat called Bray — come at him with hooks because he is a *skyborn*, a word that was a joke and has hardened into an accusation. The one System prompt in the chapter warns that a witness is not a servant. Adrian uses a mooring line and a fallen iron spike to reach a ledge, returns a loose copper lock-token to the water in front of the woman rather than keeping it, and hauls Ovin out of a hole that opens under his boot at the pale edge. He perceives the harsh light in the join and gets no direction, no language, and no usable action from it. Tamsin Quill steps between him and the hooks and asks him a question he cannot answer.

### Chapter 0003 — Names Are Prices
The maintenance yard and the lock chapel, midmorning. Adrian is put through a Hearthguard interrogation by Hettis Roke, who speaks English and wants a written confession that Adrian cannot physically give, because nobody in Saltwake writes Latin. Adrian kneels in wet gravel and draws his palm mark in the dirt; Tamsin says it is exact. In the chapel he calls the keeper's daily binding rite a **tutorial** and is told, flatly, why that word cannot be taken back. He learns Veyran usage — a working needs a pattern, a hand, and a willing witness, plus a cost, and the residue always belongs to the place; the mark on his hand is a **bond**. He apologises, returns the copper token to its numbered place on the lock board, and offers to work under a public obligation rather than demand a reward. Tamsin sets her three conditions: do not touch a working, do not use a local name, ask before you take a thing. The chapel floor opens a hairline seam near the foot of the arch and water stands up and climbs the stones uphill toward it.

### Chapter 0004 — A Door in the Lock
The maintenance tunnels, late morning. Adrian treats the flood as a pressure problem, gets nine minutes of useful work out of a chalk diagram on a slate, and is refused by the dockers, who will not take payment from a stranger. He proposes the only move he has: get the people who actually maintain the lock to say the maintenance oath in the open, which is a real promise rather than a man making noise. The lock is a resonant site because three jurisdictions repeat the same oath; a promise nobody accepts is not a working. While the dockers hold the near gate — a different structure from the arriving door — Adrian gains a **provisional join-reading** and can follow one join-line and say what he sees, at the cost of a headache and a nosebleed, the only time that cost appears in the batch. He sequences the repair to send the load off the far arch into the outer channel and is right. Nobody is watching the outside of the lock, because Hearthguard Brann is in the yard shouting, and the unwatched joint beside the outer channel opens. The air that comes out smells of asphalt and engine oil, and a man shouts in English about his legs.

### Chapter 0005 — Seven Names, One Door
Midday, on the walk-round. The outer tear takes six strangers out of the wall: Mina Okoye with a thigh graze, Dante Kuo with a dislocated forearm, Priya Shah with a forearm gash and a phone with no signal, Jonah Pike entirely dry and counting heads, Noor Haddad aspirating dust with a bakery bag in her fist, and Owen Park bleeding from a calm, competent head. Locals read the opening as divine judgement and crowd in. Adrian chooses the crowd problem over the door problem: he uses Tamsin's thin translation and Mina's triage to sort the injured from the curious, tells them the plain truth, and asks each person for a name and one thing they are afraid of, writing both down. He searches for a status display, a quest line, a party screen, and finds nothing, which is the real horror of the day. His provisional reading gives him a direction and then dies the moment Tamsin's crew lets go, leaving him unsteady in front of six frightened strangers who are watching him for an answer. Owen says a relic-shaped light is visible through the wall to him alone, and the lock chapel's window lights up.

### Chapter 0006 — No Respawn
The dry-goods loft above the river market, Tuesday afternoon into Wednesday at six. Adrian works out the batch's central fact: the things they brought are useful and are nothing else, because Veyran work is bound to relationships. Jonah Pike makes the most sensible proposal anyone has heard — exclusive shelter, exclusive information, one trusted mouth — and Adrian recognises it instantly as a gate, because he has built the same thing four times for money and watched every one of them become a locked door. Tamsin asks the question Jonah has not asked, who is not in the six, and Jonah withdraws the proposal within the hour and asks what the alternative is. A repair goes beautifully and leaves a residue that takes eleven stalls out of the day's water line. Jonah then breaks a stallholder's witnessed front tie with a multi-tool, and Sesh Brack cuts her own hand on the shutter trying to work the arm alone. At four o'clock Owen puts his coat on over the relic-shaped light, says he can be at the wall before it goes, and is called a crank by the room; the group turns on him because *one man and a rope* was said out loud in front of six people who were taken without asking. He gets as far as the yard, where Sesh stops him by naming the cost of each direction — a Hearthguard with a stick east, the second bay west — and sends him to a notary in a room with a clerk in it. Adrian refuses to tell Owen the light is not there, and instead makes the retrieval **a question the people who own the wall get asked in public**, which is a reason to stay that is not an order. Owen puts his name on the question and his offer in Priya's record. Adrian posts the three conditions verbatim on the wall and adds a fourth of his own — tell Mina about every injury, including the ones that look survivable — notices himself legislating for people instead of asking them, gives authority over the medical list and the inventory to other people, and surrenders his cash and his phone to Noor's ledger. The Concord's crier then sets the question the city will actually ask: can the lock be worked by more than one person so that nobody can open, hold, close, or refuse it alone? She gives a date — first light on the day after Thursday's tide, which is Friday.

### Chapter 0007 — The First Quest Is a Trap
The lock chapel, evening, at a preliminary chapel audience. Ivenn Marrow introduces himself as a Crown Law scholar from Aurel, quotes Adrian's own unpublished design note back at him — a sentence Marrow says he has only ever heard through a wall — and makes a partial, edited offer: a Wayfinder workway and a route to a displaced Anchorstone under the chapel before the next tide, with no cost in it anywhere. Mina says out loud that a route shown is not permission to use it. Tamsin names the omitted local cost in front of witnesses: the stone holds the old river boundary, and touching it lets the water choose a new one, and the Reed margin is lower by somebody's decision. Adrian asks Marrow to state the cost in front of local witnesses; Marrow declines. Adrian refuses the template, is recorded as refusing it, and **stays Unbound** for the whole volume. He asks Tamsin to hold the arch for a route-reading test, she withdraws her feet rather than stand in a promise he never offered her, the pattern goes out, and the promise he made with nobody standing in it lands as a **black line in the chapel's threshold stone** — a separate pale slab at the foot of the arch, not the floor, and not the Chapter 3 hairline seam four feet away. He puts the line in the public record under his own name. Marrow advises him never to take a chair. A voice then speaks the old form of the maintenance oath out of the black line, the lantern over the chapel door goes out, and the voice says his name.

### Chapter 0008 — The Price of Translation
The lock chapel and the river tunnels, Wednesday night. Tamsin wins the argument that the chapel must not be sealed, because a room that stops being on the public roll is a room that can be done to. Owen wants to reach into the line; Adrian tells him plainly that the line is not a door, that the bright edge on the wall belongs to the new and old stone and is visible to anyone in daylight, and that a relic cannot be retrieved by reaching for it. Vask's masons hold the arch so Adrian can use his provisional reading once, and he finds that the pressure in the whole lock rides on the arch while a second join at the yard wall runs thin and stops — which is what the Ivenn offer is really for. Adrian refuses to answer the voice even when it says his name, and states on the record that he did not. The cost is a dull ringing in his left ear and a fine cut opened along the mark on his palm — and **underneath that cut, a thin black line about two inches long**, which Vask identifies as a black line on a person: not a punishment, not a reward, a stone telling him what he is standing on, unfixable by work and money, and invisible because nobody steps over a hand. The voice repeats the old oath with a fifth, older syllable, and Adrian works out from nine pressure readings that the bottom fields are being drained an hour at a time by whatever is standing in the lock join.

### Chapter 0009 — Seal or Spend
The outer channel, pre-dawn **Thursday**, the second tide. The whole argument comes to the wall: Tamsin and Roke want the tear sealed, the cohort want a way home, Marrow argues in public that closing it is an act done to a person, and Vask states that a quorum can close the tear or the city can leave a socket that a door might one day be built into, and that either way the bottom field pays a season. Adrian finds the failing join and holds it visible — this time with nobody owing it to anybody — and tells four of the five witnesses where to put their hands. He has been the only one who can see for two hours, he has got used to it, and when a noise distracts him he tries to take the working off five people by promising it to the stone. The promise has nobody standing in it and rebounds; he goes down on the walk-round with blood from his ear and ninety seconds of blindness. He gets up, moves out of the way, and watches the quorum finish without him at about half past ten. He leaves the **main breach** explicitly for later and refuses to let anyone pretend the gate is fixed — in daylight the pale edge of the join in the gate has widened by the width of a finger since Tuesday. Sera Vail arrives at two in the afternoon, enters him as holding an enforceable public debt for the season of the bottom field with a review date of the last day of the salt month, and says the hearing is at first light tomorrow, in about sixteen hours. The closure leaves a maintenance seam in the outer wall whose branching is the **exact proportion** of the mark on his palm at one-twentieth — the mark is a thumbnail, the seam is eighteen inches by three fingers, and a thumbnail laid over the lip at one end of it covers the lip exactly. His palm is smaller for it, and its centre has gone clean, and the **black line underneath has not moved at all**.

### Chapter 0010 — The Narrow Mark
The Lantern Concord hall, first light **Friday**, the day after the second tide: two hundred and eleven people, one chained ledger. Sera Vail reads the five parts of a controlled threshold aloud and enters them: the site is the Chapter 9 maintenance seam; the holder is the person who has agreed to pull the rope, named as Adrian; the witnesses are Tamsin Quill, Ovin Sarr, Nell Bray and Hettis Roke, each signing for himself; the receiving community is the Reed Commons for a reduced area, with the refusal of the nine households of **Nine Elms** entered by name at the word of the household and not the assembly; the cost is posted as opened salt, the season of the bottom field, and a monthly shared labour shift. The single System message in the batch is Marrow's lure: the closed place can be opened and kept, it will carry one living person, it will need no witness and no community, and **the cost has been paid, there is nothing further to pay**. Adrian refuses it as the cheapest offer he has ever had, on the record, and understands that the cost it hides lands on Nine Elms in about nine months. Tamsin establishes that nobody may fill the seat, and Adrian then accepts it aloud as a duty with an inspection, a review, and an enforceable debt, and is given no chair. The working is performed by the locals — Quill and the two dockers on the anchor-side pattern, Roke on the inspection line, Vask and her masons, and Vail pressing the fragment-mark into the seam — while Adrian only reads and marks the join-line. He gives up the mark, keeps a thin pale trace, and could have taken it back and knows that nobody present could have stopped him. The thin **black line** is not part of what went into the stone: it is where Vask found it on Wednesday night, exactly as wide, and Vask's word had been *until* and not *unless*, and it will be there at the field's review and every time Tamsin checks. Vail rules the recognition **incomplete**: the instrument is written, argued, costed, posted, signed, and cannot be used, and the Concord has opened the record on whether a first license may exist at all. Owen's unverified statement about the light goes into the book; he calls himself the second of the six, and Mina and Adrian correct him at the bench in front of the room, because he was the **first** out of the wall, and the ledger gets the true sentence. Marrow tells Adrian privately that the narrow mark in the seam is only the first one to remember his name.

### Chapter 0011 — The Second Sitting
The Lantern Concord hall, the Friday after the second tide. Sera Vail refuses to construe the terms she has read, and a salt factor called Marek Osley wants a date in a box so he can carry a schedule instead of a rumour to eleven villages; Adrian tries to write a rota and learns from Renna Ord that a crew is a man who has owed somebody a shift, and from Ovin Sarr that an inspection is four people turning up in the wrong weather. One System prompt tells him a holder who does not come may be *replaced* at the next sitting — which is not what the ledger says, and he notices the gap himself and tells nobody. Brann walks the length of the hall and says the true thing about him twice, for Tuesday and for Wednesday night, and asks for nothing. A woman of Silt Row named Wenna Cray asks whether the seat does anything about the water, and the complaint goes in on page one against his name with the words *and he was asked in public*. Nell Bray, in front of two hundred people, defines a seat as a place to be angry at and says a seat has to be legible to a person who does not like you, anything else is a chair. Adrian writes the first inspection date, which takes four minutes and feels like nothing. At the back of the hall a salt-carter says Priya's name out loud to a man beside him.

### Chapter 0012 — The First Use of the Record
The market and the loft, the next day. Priya wants the sheet off the wall; Adrian cannot work out how a name got into a market, because he believes information moves because somebody carried a document. It did not. Tamsin read the whole sheet aloud in the market eleven times on the Wednesday, because the rules do not work if you only know them in your own language, and the group is one interpreter wide. At the tally-house, Halvard Ketch produces a genuine customs clerk's report from about three hundred and ten years ago listing six things that came out of a hole in descriptions and **no names at all** — and says Ivenn Marrow asked for it in the first week and told a room of nine people it was a fraud. The danger was never the mouth; it is that a real old roll makes new gossip sound like a roll, and a count in Saltwake is a thing you do *to* people. One System prompt offers him the cheap fix — the paper can be taken down tonight, nobody has said that you may. Mina Okoye overrules him for the first time and is right: the **names stay up and the fears come down**, because six people gave a fear to a man on a piece of road, not to a wall. The group publishes a statement Adrian did not write and cannot retract, containing one sentence he disagrees with, and he adds the only line on that board that will still be true in February.

### Chapter 0013 — The Crown's Handwriting
The Concord hall, the following Wednesday. A courteous four-page letter from the Office of Crossings and Tides at Aurel asks who is answering for the bridge and encloses **Schedule Four: Answerable Party**, a form with one field, a line for a name, and no box that says no. The Concord's assessor of strangers explains that a Crown registration would not own the bridge, it would take the *jurisdiction* — and it would receive for the whole realm, which deletes the refusal of nine households by writing. Marrow argues the true thing, which is that a registered crossing could put a Crown warrant in the join and stop the man spending the river. The interface offers nothing at all this morning, which Adrian notices and files. Adrian refuses, on the grounds that the only thing worth anything he has found in a week is a thing with a way of being refused in it, and the whole of his answer is that the form has no such box. Tamsin adds the fifth cost: a registered crossing cannot be suspended by anybody in Saltwake at all. The refusal costs the group the only protection on offer, including the Crown's compensation for the bottom field, and it goes into the ledger with the reasoning at page twenty-three. **The letter's courier is a person and not an official**, and her question — has the Reach been told this letter exists — changes the document.

### Chapter 0014 — Roles
The loft and the market, a Thursday and a Friday. The six take on local work and each is bad at the first version of it. Mina is re-taught by Hesper Saye, a bone-setter by the salt wharf, who opens Owen's scalp in front of nine people and says the word *unclosed*, and who sends Mina to her room four days a week to be wrong where she can be corrected. Dante has his forearm reduced three days late and learns from Renna Ord that a crew is a chain of *asking*, not a chain of command. Priya starts a local record and hits the wall from Saturday. Noor adds the column nobody adds in the first week — who gave it to us and what they expect — and finds the group has been eating out of the market's widows' box for four days without knowing, carried up a stair in a cloth by Sesh Brack, who says yes she would do it again and that is exactly why you should know. Jonah negotiates a very good price with the Brass Measure and gets refused by Priya in one sentence. Owen tries to buy the freehold of the building the loft is the top of and is stopped by a widow with a nine-year-old in her own doorway, calmly, in a way that cannot be handled as a fight; she counters with a lease and two conditions he has not met. Adrian says out loud that he is not the person who assigns them. The chapter ends with a question on the loft wall that he cannot answer, and two answers underneath it that are not his.

### Chapter 0015 — What the Anchorstone Holds
The yard under the lock chapel at low water, the second Saturday, day 12 of the flood. Nyla Ferrow's fifth word turns out to be the keeper's word and not part of the oath, and the voice in the lock join repeats it half a second after she says it, out of the black line in the chapel stone. Tamsin reads the **eleven-line boundary table** aloud in the yard for the first time in seven years: line one is the only attempt ever carried out, by the three jurisdictions together, at a public act, with witnesses, and it put four hundred people in new houses. Ivenn's route was true in every particular except the one that mattered — *bring it back* has no place in it, because there is no *back*. One System prompt offers to show him which boundary the water is using, and he refuses, because it would be true and there would be no page, no witness and no argument, which makes the holder a singleton. The keeper names her price and it is not money: for as long as the ground is open, the four words and the fifth are said in the open, in the yard, twice a day, in any weather, by whoever is nearest. Vail enters it as a labour on a rota with no name at the top. The terms are read and entered, Vask posts her list of what may not come out of the hole — and the fourth item is *anything that answers* — and Adrian is put on a rope afterwards and finds the edge of the void and nothing above it, and says so in public.

### Chapter 0016 — The Offer, Again
An evening in the disused Salt Weavers' chapel two floors above the market, with Marrow present and the keeper in the room. Adrian calls the meeting so the offer can stop being attractive by being vague. Marrow reads the omitted cost out loud accurately, unasked — repeated route work taxes memory and balance, and a wayfinder cannot refuse to carry a road, which is why the offer was made to Adrian and not to Tamsin — and then argues the cost is worth paying and that Adrian has no standing to refuse for six people. The six are asked separately, in their own words. Noor asks who does the job, because a workway displaces a duty and nobody has asked the man it lands on. Jonah wants it, says exactly why, and refuses. Priya wants it, refuses, and asks for the wanting to be logged. Dante refuses so that a thing that goes wrong is a man and not a shape. Mina argues **for** taking it, because a man with an obligation is safer than a man nobody is owed anything. Owen refuses because if he takes a workway he will never know whether he is seeing the light or doing it. Adrian refuses on **obligation**, not risk, and Vask immediately asks him for the safe line and he says no, in public, to leave her a better man for the job. The refusal goes into the roll of strangers as **Unbound**, in a document a Crown clerk will find; Marrow then puts his own open offer in chalk on the keeper's board with a date on it, and tells Adrian the true reason, which is tethering, not control.

### Chapter 0017 — The First Dissent
The first inspection, a Wednesday at low water, four days after the second Saturday, when the Saturday it was supposed to happen on did not happen. Ninety people come, angry, because nine came to the last one and left in four minutes. Marek Osley argues with a written column that the instrument is a hazard of unknown size in a load-bearing wall and offers a number for cutting it out. Jonah Pike is the only person who says publicly that the route is worth something. The nine households of Nine Elms send Ilda Marsh and Petra Oake, and Petra asks the question Tamsin predicted in the rain twelve days ago — is a refusal given at a hearing the same as a commitment — and Adrian agrees with them, in public, at the cost of the instrument, which is formally declared unrecognised until somebody accepts the whole of the receiving area. The interface offers nothing when ninety people ask what the Narrow Mark is for, and Adrian answers the question in a clerk's voice for nine minutes: he does not know what it is for, and the only thing he can say is what it is not for, and it is a seat somebody can be angry at. Owen backs him in public and asks for the story of the missed Saturday to be told accurately. Vail records the inspection as passed on the term, so the instrument survives because it did nothing. Then a woman of sixty from the upper road walks down two hundred wet steps to say she is not here for the seam, and asks whether a man who reads walls might one day read nineteen stones.

### Chapter 0018 — The Cost of the Field
The bottom field and the Reed Reach road, a Thursday. Wenna Cray takes his notebook off him at the second stile and tells him the loss is not a disaster: nothing is dead, the field will be replanted, and what has gone is the *ability to promise* — because the ditches are level, the guess has been taken out of the water, and in the Reach a household that cannot promise a gate date does not keep its share. Elsa Skell of Corrow asks him a number and a date, and the number is a measure of winter grain, and the date is the last day of the salt month, and nobody can give her either. His notebook is the wrong instrument and she tells him so. He finds a faint gradient in the ditch and works out that the drain is still running fourteen days after the tear was shut with nobody in the field at all, which means the debt is an open account and Vail entered it at its smallest. The interface is silent and he notices that the messages come out of places and that the one place they have never come from is the gate. On the walk home they find four things nobody ordered: a miller putting flour on the banks of a ditch in the dark, a Hearthguard carrying salt to Silt Row on somebody else's horse, a gatehouse using its own thatch, and Owen Park standing at the end of a field for an hour, four mornings running, telling nobody. Adrian nearly makes it a rota and Tamsin stops him, because a rota has witnesses in it and witnesses are what kills it.

### Chapter 0019 — The Second Cohort Does Not Arrive
The walk-round and the main breach, a Friday night, day 18 of the flood. The group has a rota with thirty-one names, a ledger with a new column, a lease, a room by the wharf, and a functioning life, and nobody believes anything will happen tonight. The walk-round is thin because the gate crew's day went to the instrument's labour shift, and the line in the book with his name on it is the reason. He measures the main breach at one in the morning from the lower gallery, at three chalked marks in the join in the gate: three inches in twenty nights, in four steps of three-quarters of an inch, and a crack does not step. The four steps are the four days the lock did **unusual** work. He concludes that the ordinary maintenance of the lock is the mechanism, that the near gate and the breach are nine feet apart in the same course of stone, and that the only lever is to stop working the lock, which drowns eleven villages. The measurement is good and there is no answer attached to it. At the fourth hour a voice comes out of the black line in the chapel stone and says three sentences in English, the last of which is a line Adrian wrote at four in the morning at Morrowglass, underneath a joke underneath a rule, and then **deleted the section of** — so it is not on the laptop and there is no file, and either something has been inside him since Tuesday or something read a document that no longer exists. Marrow is not present and nobody admits to having done it. He tells **Mina first**, at half past six, then Tamsin at seven because the voice came out of her room's threshold stone, then the other five at nine. Jonah sees in a minute what he could do with it — write to Aurel and end it, for everybody — and takes two hours to decide not to. In the middle of the argument Jonah uses the word **quest** out loud for a thing in a wall, and Adrian understands two things at once: that he has been waiting all day for a pale field to tell him what to do next and it is not going to, and that he is the only one in the room who knows what the word is supposed to cost. He says *do not use that word in this room again* and writes the date down. **This is the moment he stops expecting System output, and it is on the page here, not in a notebook.** Owen then says the true thing: it is not the stone, it is the words forty men say twice a day under the near gate, so the field is a toll and somebody is being charged.

### Chapter 0020 — The Road Down
The yard under the lock chapel, the next Saturday at low water — day 19 of the flood — at the top of the dig, and the notebook at the end of it is stamped *the nineteenth morning*. Owen says the four words in the yard and the first attempt does not work and the second does, and Vask makes him write the hour on the board. The terms are read out at the north door and Roke reads his own clause: nobody is going down to fetch anybody, because there is nobody to fetch. The dig is ordinary, expensive work on a real rota and it goes wrong in small ways about a hundred times — a string line pinched with a thumb, a flooding lantern, a boy of eleven through a flagstone, a water table that comes up higher than the bar said. Adrian offers to go down and is refused in front of a yard; the keeper puts him four feet back from the lip of the hole and will not tell him why. At four feet Nell Bray finds a dressed face with her fingers: a wall laid on edge, three putlog holes in a line, a joint opened and looked at and closed again two hundred years ago and closed well, sitting directly under the chapel's own hairline seam. **It is dry.** Vask has never in thirty-four years lifted a stone out of a bank and found it dry. It is the edge of something larger running under the river bank where nobody has ever dug, and Renna Ord stops the dig by naming a cofferdam, a manned pump and three more men. The ground is boarded, the terms are entered, and nothing is identified, because a man with a quarter of a thing who says the whole of it is a man selling. Nyla Ferrow names Adrian's post: for as long as the ground is open, the ground does not open without him, and it is not money and it is not useful. Sera Vail comes up into the yard with a slate and gives the city two dates at once, because a man who is given one date plans the first one: the field review in six days, the next tide in nine, the hearing three days after that.

### Chapter 0021 — Two Dates in One Yard
The day after the dig stopped — Sunday the twentieth, into Monday the twenty-first. Owen is late for the six o'clock binding and the keeper makes Adrian say the five words himself, badly, twice, and the arch takes the second attempt, and both facts go on the board. Vask, Sesh Brack, the keeper and a mason of nine-learners teach the words to as many people as will learn them, so that the rule *whoever is nearest* has more than one candidate: it is entered as a widening, not a rota, and there is no name at the top. Renna Ord takes Adrian to pieces for putting a man who works under the near gate onto it, and says the gate crew is the thinnest thing in this city. Sera Vail teaches him the batch's spine: **a date in a book is not a plan, it is a place a plan has to be legible in**, and makes him write down, in front of witnesses, who is answerable for what on each of the next twelve days — forty-one lines, of which nineteen are people he has never spoken to, and the difference between a plan he can execute and a plan this city can execute is that he cannot reach the telephone in any of them. She also names the other half of the seat: when you are at the counter you are also the man who finds out that somebody has not turned up. Elsa Skell of Corrow refuses to speak to him and says, in a gateway, that a man who owes a season has no business putting a number in a village's book. A man in a good coat has a date and has told a nine-year-old on a step about it rather than the room. The chapter ends with a message from the Reed Reach asking for *a person* to come and stand in a room, without naming one.

### Chapter 0022 — The Second Set of Hands
Two days on the Reed road and the reed channels, days twenty-two and twenty-three. Tamsin is more useful in the first hour than in the first year, because her trade is a count of who speaks to her and in what order. The Commons does not want Adrian: it wants **Owen Park**, and it says so to his face, because a man who has asked for nothing in a room is a man you can send, which is Hessa Ruun's whole vocabulary of value. Adrian goes as a witness and is treated as a witness. Nine Elms' Ilda Marsh, who has refused twice, is the one who sees the trap: if the season becomes a standing charge on the instrument, their refusal stops being a *no* and becomes a **non-payment**, and a non-payment is a debt a village can be brought to a meeting-ground about — and she forces through a fourth line that lets any village refuse the charge and return the whole to the private debtor. The demand Adrian carries home is worse than a refusal and better than a plan, and Hessa Ruun warns him that the concession he has just been handed is a document a Crown scholar can read, and that Nine Elms' refusal will be tested in a month by somebody using his own gift. Owen, changed, asks Adrian for the one thing he has never asked anybody for: not the wall — a stake in the bottom field at four in the morning, to see whether he can see a light six other people cannot.

### Chapter 0023 — The Unverified Man
The night of the twenty-third into the morning of the twenty-fourth, and the walk-round, and the low room by the wharf. Owen has stopped asking to go anywhere and has started making other people go — a mason over lime, a boy of nineteen with a flask of tea at half past three in the morning — and Renna Ord explains that he has *spent* them, and Adrian admits in public that he watched it happen for four days and did not stop it. Hesper Saye will not look at the arm, says the wound is unclosed and will stay that way, and asks Owen whose head it is. A light seen by one man cannot be put in a record, and the only way to make it legible is a document in the maker's own hand, which a court will treat as a symptom. So Adrian invents a form for a claim that cannot be tested: a witness chain of three who must be asked separately, a stated standing (*this is a record that a person said it, and it obliges somebody to answer it, and it obliges nobody to believe it*), a refusal clause, a review date, numbers entered as *what we said*, and a bar on any office using it as grounds for a search, a price, or an entry onto anybody's ground. Hesper Saye writes the sixth line herself: a person who has entered a statement is not to be harmed, refused work, refused water, or moved for it, and that is a clause against a city. The same form is immediately used on Adrian himself — his black line in the palm, with no witness chain at all — so a second person in Saltwake is now documented as being owed an answer, and the form has no seal, no authority and no precedent and says so at the top. It goes on the loft wall beside the four rules.

### Chapter 0024 — What the Dig Found
The yard under the lock chapel at low water on the twenty-fourth. Four days of arguing and one day of digging, and the cost is entered by the salt factor: the gate crew is manned on a day it is not manned, because a floor is open. Vask will not dig under the bank and takes eleven flagstones instead, which means six more days of yard binding and more strangers standing in the rain at six. The joint does not end; it **turns**, at a dressed right angle, and makes the L of a doorway, and stops square under the north wall — a threshold, not a wall, with the chapel's own footings laid clear of it. Tamsin reads the eleven-line boundary table against the evidence and the answer is ugly and simple: the stone in the yard-side landing and the joint under the chapel floor are **two pieces of one line**, set in the same year by the same three jurisdictions, one liftable and one not, and somebody lifted the liftable one a hundred yards downstream in public and left the other where the water could find it. The dry thing under the chapel is a record, not a mystery. Vask refuses to name it — *a quarter of a thing is not a thing* — and it is entered as a description in her words and Quill's, in front of sixty people. The interface offers to let Adrian **name** it; he refuses, because a name in one man is a singleton. The ground is re-boarded, the dig is finished for this season, and by the following morning the identification is in four mouths in a city of eleven hundred people — not a leak, and Adrian recognises that the fix is not quietness, the fix is that the thing that moves should be true.

### Chapter 0025 — The Second Cohort Arrives Anyway
The twenty-fifth day, the last day of the salt month, at the top of the water, at the seventh hour, in the lower gallery. The volume midpoint. Six terms are read in the yard in the rain by Vask, because the terms will be repeated in a hundred yards for a year: the gate crew consents by a count, thirty-one for and eleven against with the foreman's name entered; the cost is eleven measures of opened salt, a day of the day crew, a season of the bottom field, and the yard labour doubled; the four witnesses sign; **Adrian reads and marks and performs nothing**; and the person with authority to stop the working is Renna Ord and not him. The crew says the maintenance oath **at its ordinary volume**, at Owen's insistence and at a docker's, so that the day cannot later be called a thing a crowd did. The working succeeds: the pale edge of the main breach steps nine inches *toward the near gate*, in the safe direction, and the four sign and Vail enters it. Then, at the eleventh hour and eleven minutes, the boards over the dig rise flat, a wall of grey water comes up the yard and stops at the threshold slab, and the chapel-floor hairline opens **across** rather than along, for four feet, with a flagstone stood on end. No consent, no witness, no cost, nobody standing in it. Out of the black line in the chapel stone — not the floor, not the arch — a flat voice reads out one line of Adrian's own deleted design note, and Ottoline Vask looks at her own list, item four, and says one word. Mina names it: **a hold is a working without a witness, and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.** Marrow is the most useful man in the yard and says aloud that this was a good working's residue arriving where it was not sent, and Adrian underlines it twice. The field review is adjourned on the day it was set for, with the reason and both dates entered, and Vail stops two hundred people from opening the seal. The interface says nothing all day. Roke enters Adrian's answer to *is this a game* — *there is no* — in full, in his own words, unsoftened, and Adrian checks his palm in the lamplight: the black line has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.

### Chapter 0026 — The List
The morning after, in the hall, then the loft. The hall spends six hours on the second list and Adrian is stopped four times. Jonah argues ninety per cent right for an immediate structure, and the tenth per cent is Renna Ord's: a list of people with no shift on it is not a rota, it is an order. Priya Shah makes him stop saying *cohort* in public, on the grounds that the first six came out of a hole in a numbered order too. The eleven are sitting at the back and Adrian understands at last that the obstacle is not language — it is that he has been translating into design terms. Tamsin tells him to use the one language all of them share. Vail enters the eleven as **persons who came through without asking**, in the same words used for the six, separately witnessed, with a refusal clause letting any of them strike their own name, and with an explicit bar on the words *order, company, party, class, cohort, settlement, faction, rescue*. The six are asked to testify. Eli Mercer refuses to join them and refuses to speak for them, in the record, in front of two hundred people. Adrian realises that the premise he has been privately embarrassed by for three weeks is now a public asset being used against him, and that the only sentence he has is Hesper Saye's question from a room by the wharf: *what happens when one of them does not want to tell?*

### Chapter 0027 — The Missing Child
Sunday the twenty-seventh and the morning of the twenty-eighth. Owen gets his four in the morning; Adrian stands on the stake at the end of the third ditch from ten past four until about twenty to six — ninety minutes — and sees nothing, and spends most of that time failing to notice that Owen is in pain, and says so afterwards, in a hall, to a woman of sixty from the upper road who did not ask. At twenty to six they come up the field and Sesh Brack is at the top of the stair with a lamp: a child is gone. **Sefa Rood**, eight, of Four Elms, walked out at the second hour of the night following a dog, on paths she has known for three years, into a wetland whose water is not where it was. Adrian works out the mechanism in forty minutes — the twenty-fifth day's big working took the water out of four villages' ditches, and a household that cannot say when its gate opens does not keep its share. Marda Rood, her grandmother, walks eleven miles and refuses a bench for ten minutes. The argument about the **first opening** of the narrow route takes five hours and is resolved by six people who are not Adrian. Tamsin: a door is not a search party. Mina: the first opening will be remembered as the one spent on the day a child was lost. Jonah: send somebody with nothing here. Dante, in nine words: *you'd be sending them because they're cheap.* Renna Ord: an opening is a fresh inspection by four people in the wrong weather, and that is the work. Saye will not have Owen on a stair. Adrian then says the hard thing out loud, with the grandmother in the room: **he does not know whether the mark he gave into the wall in the first week could have brought anybody home**, nobody will ever be able to tell, and the only way to find out is to take back the thing he gave away. The route's terms are broken in public; a return mark is set on a living person at a cost; a fresh inspection is done in the rain by four people who did not want to be there; and at the sixth hour on the twenty-eighth, at the top of the tide, **Eli Mercer**, who asked eleven times and was refused eleven times, walks into the seam and is out in eleven seconds. The child is found at four o'clock on the twenty-seventh by four people from Long Water, on foot, on a reed path, because she was where the water was not. Marda Rood asks the boy a question at the foot of the stair and he tells her the truth: he was not looking for her. Nell Bray asks Adrian, in the rain, in front of a hundred and forty people, **what was it for.**

### Chapter 0028 — What the Scout Is
The Concord hall and the loft, the twenty-ninth. The account of the other side is taken over six hours and Vail runs it her way: the paper is entered as *a paper carried by a person out of an opening and brought back into this one*, not as a document of another world, and nobody may ask a man to read it out until he has been told he may refuse. What comes out is a room with no window, four openings in one wall at different heights, of which the fourth was already open, a sound like a page turning every forty seconds, and — from one man only — a smell of wet stone in a room with no stone in it. The eleven disagree about the size of the room and about which opening they came out of, and the disagreements are not confusion and not lying. Ivenn Marrow translates for two hours, word for word, adding one sentence of his own: *I have not removed the four disagreements, because in nineteen years of reading other people's accounts I have never once read one and been asked to keep the wrong parts.* Adrian realises that the most frightening version of the man yet is that he is reading the account **as a professional and not as a recruiter**, and that this is what makes him genuinely useful to the people he wants to use. Jonah wants the account in a market and is argued out of it by the person he was arguing with, and withdraws it in the room and asks for the withdrawal to be entered. Peth Lammay asks the standing question from Chapter 13 — does the community the document is *about* know it exists, and in what hand — and the page goes down the Reach road on **the Friday, the thirty-second** in her own hand, ahead of the Crown's mail case, and Adrian has to go with it. Vail refuses to reconcile the four accounts and enters *doubtful* into the ledger for the first time in twenty-six years. Teodor Blaz then gives the detail only he can account for: the heights of the four openings, in order, each about two thirds of the one above it — roughly four foot, two foot eight, a foot and nine, a foot — with the last two inches and a quarter under the rule. Adrian recognises the order and the ratio and finds them, in his own hand, on **page forty-one of a paper notebook he has shown to nobody**, written at four in the morning on the fourth night of the flood, forty feet from the voice that said the other sentence. He writes four readings down in order of how much they frighten him, and hands the page to the notary, and admits that this is the first time he has given somebody the thing he was frightened of *without telling anybody first*.

### Chapter 0029 — The Review
The thirtieth day, at first light, in a hall of about three hundred and ten. The review was set for the twenty-fifth and was held five days later, and both dates and the reason are entered and read out, and Vail says the other half: a date that moves five days is a date that can move nine. She reads the three questions exactly as she wrote them on the ninth morning of the flood. **Elsa Skell** stands up before Adrian answers and gives a second number: not a season in measures, but nineteen unnamed people from four villages putting barrowloads of flour on ditch banks at four in the morning, and the miller at Silt Row counted *by accident*. Marek Osley insists a kindness is not a cost and will not have a notary enter one in a cost column. Adrian answers question two in nine words and then admits in front of the room that he used a true sentence in a yard in the second week to get four witnesses out of a gate crew, and that it has cost that crew an hour a day ever since. **Kirsty Lund, sixteen, four days in the world**, answers question three from the back of the hall: the households out there cannot be told they have been paid by a book, because everybody out there knows what a book was worth last time somebody was hungry, so *somebody has to knock on their door* — and it cannot be him. The interface offers, for the first time, to let him **close** the debt, and the fourth line is a cost sentence in the place a cost goes. He refuses, and asks to be **continued as a creditor**: the debt stays open, growing, enforceable, named, with a new review, on the grounds that a debt that can be closed is a debt that can be traded out, and a debt that is closed by somebody else is a favour, and a favour is a thing there is nobody left to be angry at. It passes by the first count in the Concord's history, 201 for and 78 against, and the seat is readmitted for a further term with a new cost column: the yard labour twice a day with the holder as nearest within four hundred yards, a new page for the eleven, and a review on a named date. Adrian says out loud, once, in a room, why he is staying Unbound — that a workway would have given him the ability to be asked, and he got that in a yard where anybody can watch. And in the evening Marrow takes his own expired chalk off the keeper's board in daylight and writes under it, in his own hand, that he withdraws the offer because the holder's answer is better than the one he would have given, and asks for the true reason to be written on the board in Adrian's own hand, and adds: *he will do it.*

### Chapter 0030 — The Hearing
The thirty-first day, at first light, four hundred and ten people, the largest attendance in the Concord's history. A second letter from Anwen Kroon at Aurel is read in full by Renner Sallow, the Registry's second clerk, who has sat beside Vail since the first sitting and whom nobody has heard speak at length: it opens a file, notes the use of the instrument without accepting it, and requires either **a registered holder with a Crown-recognised workway and a Crown advocate** or **a statement of permanent closure**; and it says, in a sentence that is not a threat, that a seat with no workway is not a form the Registry can accept, and that a position may be filled by a person who can hold it. The Registry is not represented. The dressed joint under the chapel floor is entered as **scarred** — the first place in the city's history entered as damaged and not closable — in the local phrase *a black mark in a place*, entered beside the chapel stone's line and expressly distinguished from it. Wenna Cray names the question that is actually in the room: not whether there is a door, but **who may be angry at whom, and how long for, and what happens on the day he stops being angry-able.** Adrian, asked for the third time in a month what the mark is for, gives a smaller and worse answer: it is a place where one person at a time can be named out loud by other people before they go somewhere, and the price of a door in this city is the crew, and the people who pay it are not in the room. The hearing does not license anything. The instrument is **continued and may not be used**, with the cost column, the receiving community's standing and both refusals entered together on the same page, neither above the other. Its real product is a **statement of error**: any person may enter an error against any entry, in their own words, without reason or cost; the person named must answer in public within a term; an entry against which a statement stands unanswered may not be used, relied on, or carried to a court; and the holder may not enter one against his own seat. Tamsin names it: **say it out loud again.** The first statement of error in the Concord's history is entered at the tenth hour by Osten Fenn, 22, of Long Water, **against the record of the sixteenth day's inspection — the Wednesday one, and not the Saturday before it, which is also in the book and produced no inspection at all** — and it may be correct. In the afternoon Dante Kuo's shoulder goes while he steadies a door frame, exactly as Saye said it would, and Saye says it is never the heavy thing, and Marrow's decision to put him on paper work is the one useful thing nobody has entered in a book. In the evening Adrian reads the last column of the tide table: **the thirty-third is a Saturday** and it is an ordinary worked day, forty men under the near gate, twice, and the last cell in the last column is still empty. Renna Ord tells him the truth about who decides, and that whoever holds the chalk that day decides it, and it is a Saturday. **The last act in this city is a man with a stick of chalk, on a wet morning, writing the ordinary word in a box.**

### Chapter 0031 — What a Date Is For
Day 32, the Friday, which is the seventh day of the new month, and the seventh hour, and about two hundred words on a keeper's board. Ivenn Marrow's date is honoured literally: he does not ask in a yard, he asks in a room, because a refusal said in a yard produces a shorter sentence than the true one, and Adrian cannot refuse him in front of a keeper anyway. What he wants is the **true** reason for refusing a workway, in Adrian's own hand, on a keeper's wall — and the true reason is *I refused it because it worked. I liked saying no, in a room, in front of people, in the rain, with witnesses.* It is a worse sentence than the one in the book and Tamsin refuses three words of it and gives one back, and a mason of fifty reads it aloud in the local language and puts his own name under it, because he learned the keeper's fifth word on the twenty-first day and put it in the wrong place for three weeks. On the same afternoon the account of the other side is read at Verrow before a clerk in Aurel knows it exists, and **Petra Oake asks a question about a person** — *is there a person on the other side of it* — having asked it of two other people in the Reach and been answered by neither, and Adrian does not answer it and says so on the page. At one in the morning on the thirty-third, the twenty-first reading of the pale edge of the join in the gate is **eight and five-eighths**, three-quarters of an inch in fifteen days in one step, and the step fell on the twenty-fifth; and Renna Ord, a docker, has written the ordinary word in the last column of the tide table, and nobody asked her what she decided.

The Monday: Vail answers the Crown's demand in nine lines that are **not the form required**, and Adrian says in a hall that the Crown wants a man in a chair and Tamsin corrects him in public — *it is a seat, a chair is what a person sits in.* Ottavia Reek returns with four sheets, and the fourth term is that the dockers' labour be drawn from the Brass Measure's own list, entered as a standing order and not an agreement. ### Chapter 0033 — Nine Elms, a Second Time

### Chapter 0034 — The Man Who Was Available

### Chapter 0035 — A Mark for a Man Who Asked
Day 36 at the eleventh hour at night, and day 37, the Wednesday. ### Chapter 0036 — The Terms of a Rescue
Day 37, the Wednesday evening, the walk-round and the yard. Adrian writes the rescue terms between the seventh and the tenth hour, and the terms are the six items of the twenty-eighth with one preposition changed, and he cannot see the change. He also writes the site as *a dressed joint, described on the twenty-fourth day and not named*, which is true of two places in this city, and one of them is a scar in a place, and a description is not a place, and *a quarter of a thing is not a thing* was his own rule. The pale field comes up a second time and offers the best of the fourteen prompts: **the terms of a crossing may be written by the person who will be carried through it, and a person who is carried through a working is not answerable for the working** — and Adrian refuses it in under a second and can say why: a working with no person answerable in it is a working with no cost in it, and that is a good day's work at a lock at the top of a tide. That sentence goes into the terms as the fifth item, in his own words, read in the yard. **Vask reads the first two items and stops.** The changed word is *before*, changed to *when there is time*, and she says the true thing — a rescue is a working, a working needs a pattern, and a floor that has come open once under three hundred and ten people is not a pattern, it is a hole — and is outvoted in four minutes by a family and a boy of nineteen, and her refusal is entered in her words, and it is the only reason the page survives the next day. Roke says his sentence, *nobody is going down to fetch anybody*, about a man this time, is asked who he is saying it to, and does not have an answer. At the second hour, Owen is at the top of the walk-round with a mark in his hand, waiting for a tide that is not the right tide.

### Chapter 0037 — The Reach of the Step
Day 38, the Thursday, before light and after. Nyla Ferrow puts Adrian four feet back for the fourth time and tells him why, and the reason is not about him: a keeper's whole job is giving a man a place that is not the hole and standing near it, and the ground under that yard now answers to two men. At half past one, four people who were not sent find **Ceri Vaughan alive**, eleven miles the wrong way, on foot, in the dark, having deliberately walked the wrong water for eleven hours rather than be a body somebody found in a morning. Vail enters the finding and the terms on facing pages four hours apart, and enters that the holder of the pull was in the room and did not say the word, and she does it herself because he is barred from entering a statement against his own seat. Adrian says, in a room with eleven people in it, that he has been trying to say that one word to a room since Sunday and that the reason is that he has wanted so much to be a person who is answerable in a place that he would rather put a man into a hole than find out. Owen says he is going, and knows the man was found, and says why in six sentences. At ten to four, **the list on the chapel door is read in the yard**, and Vask reads it because the clause as it stands says *before*, and then says she is not going to be a woman who says *when there is time* and does the other thing when there is not. At four minutes past the fourth hour the floor comes up along four feet of dressed face and takes him sideways, right arm first, and there was no join to read and there were **two men on that floor and one of them was not in the terms.** He comes up out of it at twenty past the sixth hour, alive, badly hurt on top of the twenty-fifth, and the mark in his hand is **the first black scar in a person in this world** — a line in a place can be mended with work and money, a mark in a person cannot, because nobody steps over a hand.

### Chapter 0038 — Unclosed
Day 38, the Thursday, the morning and the afternoon, in the low room by the salt wharf. The volume's only death, at full length. Hesper Saye explains in about a minute what she meant by *unclosed* in a porch on the fourteenth day: a thing with a witness on it stays open until the witness comes back, it does not mean the wound is not killing him, and **the thing keeping it open is the thing that is killing him** — and the witness was Owen's own statement on the loft wall, entered in Adrian's form with an empty chain of three names because he had not told anybody, and the form does not allow a witness who has not been asked. ### Chapter 0050 — The Trial Licence

### Chapter 0054 — The Word He Wrote Down

### Chapter 0055 — The Man Who Did Not Say

### Chapter 0056 — What the Description Is For
Day 56, the Monday, the thirty-first day of the new month. **The fourth Crown instrument arrives and it is a table, and Sera Vail says out loud, before anybody has finished reading it, that it is a fourth document and not a version of the other three, and that it is the first Crown instrument in this city that does not want a person in it.** A **schedule of crossings**, quarterly, in a form required, complete or not received, a summary is not a schedule, a figure may not be rounded, **at a fee of eleven ounces of silver**, and the fourth line says *a fee is charged in respect of the office's work, and an office that is not paid does not work.* **Renner Sallow, who has been at a table in this city since the fourth day of the flood and has never been asked to stop, gives the volume its politics in fourteen words: a fee is a thing that tells you what an office thinks you are** — and that the office not working is a condition and not a punishment — and then adds, in a second sentence, that an office that is not paid does not collect the ounce either, and that he did not mean to. ### Chapter 0057 — The Nineteenth Field
Day 57, the Tuesday, the thirty-second day of the new month. **The nineteenth field comes up in a room over a chandlery in front of about nine people and one of the nine is asleep, and Perry Lask, forty-one, of the second list, says yes in about four seconds after saying one sentence first: that she has been waiting since the ninth day of this flood for the wall to say something she recognises, and that she is not going to pretend to a room that she checked it first.** She says the rest of it out loud, and it is her sentence and it is the volume's: **a rule that only covers the thing you can see is a rule about a thing and not about the world**, and she has read a licence page in four languages in her life and has broken every one of them. **The city then discovers in about eleven minutes that it has no procedure for a workway and cannot have one**, because a workway is a job and a job means somebody is entitled to expect a thing of the holder, and there is no office, no guild, no bench and no Crown instrument in Saltwake that can say what a holder is expected to do. Day 180, the Saturday, the twenty-fifth day of the Long Month, the flats. **About nine people come down eleven miles on foot with bars and none of them was asked and none was sent, and a hundred and forty yards of channel takes nine people a day and a half, and the finding is that nobody came to take anything and there is nothing to be angry about.** The queue is a queue; the boy rang a bell four times; the bed is not a gain and was there in the second month. **A woman of about fifty who counts water comes to the gate and says out loud that the Reach's list is the only list in four hundred miles that has never been wrong about a person and that she does not know whether that is a good answer, and writes nothing down.** A woman of about thirty-four says at about the seventh hour that it is still here.

### Chapter 0177 — Four Legs of Unequal Length
Day 181, the Sunday, the twenty-sixth day of the Long Month, a kitchen at the bottom of a channel. **A recovery with no form in it: a man of thirty-two walks eleven miles with nothing in his hands to be useful at something, says he is sorry, is told no, stands up, and writes nothing down.** The keeper says the whole of a gate in nine words, and it is nine years of hers and he has had it for about a week. **A woman of about fifty-nine who cannot read works out the days since the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one — twenty-seven — and tells him he has not been in a room that wanted him, and says that the not being asked is what she has noticed since about the eighth hour that morning.**

### Chapter 0178 — Nine Hundred Measures
Day 182, the Monday, the twenty-seventh day of the Long Month, the flats and a causeway of four miles. **The first full load of salt goes out of the Reach, nine hundred and nine measures on a sledge, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board walks the causeway nine times over and finds that the only instrument that can stop a road is the ground it is on, and that the ground belongs to a woman of thirty-eight whose days can be given back.** A gate is not owned and a road is not a gate. Nine hundred measures a week go to one buyer nine hundred miles away and that is the whole of the settlement's money. The keeper stands at the gate for the whole day with the board in her left hand and says one sentence to a man of about thirty-four and it is that he said it and not her.

### Chapter 0179 — A Question About a Term
Day 183, the Tuesday, the twenty-eighth day of the Long Month, a market and a gateway with a door that shuts. **The first wage goes out of a store in nine sacks and a man of about thirty-two asks a real, correctly framed question about a term of the trial licence and a clerk of the fourth desk says there is a column for it and that the column goes to a desk and the desk is a room and the room is nine hundred miles away, and that the answer will be correct in four months and worth nothing to a household.** **The fourth channel at the Nine Elms junction has been shut seventy-one days and that junction has not taken water in a hundred and fourteen days.** Adrian puts his two questions next to each other for the first time in twenty-nine days and works out that the reason the first is worth nothing is the second.

### Chapter 0180 — Nine Miles Up the Road
Day 184, the Wednesday, the twenty-ninth day of the Long Month, a workroom with a date in chalk on the door. **A man of about forty-two offers, in nine sentences, to pay four months of the wage of a person with a standing so that the question can be put in the seventh column, and says the reason out loud on purpose, and it is that he would like to stop being the only person in four hundred miles who pays people on a day.** A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf names the price: about a hundred and forty people who get paid would find out in four months that the wage was on a list, and there is no instrument that takes a person off a list they were not put on. **Adrian refuses it in about nine minutes, which is the third time a refusal has cost anything, and the sentence he uses was said in the second month of this flood and is the oldest one in the room.**

### Chapter 0181 — Disappointed
Day 185, the Thursday, the thirtieth day of the Long Month, a store on the upper road. **A man of about forty-two says out loud in a yard that he is disappointed, and that the sharpest thing he has got is that there is nothing in it he can be argued out of.** **Ninety** is the count of days this city has got nothing out of a wall, and a girl of seventeen says it in a store and says that nobody checks hers. A rota is seven days and nine days of a week is not a week. A woman asks what is the date and a man of forty-two finds out he has been running a settlement on a word for nine weeks.

### Chapter 0182 — What They Will Not Do
Day 186, the Friday, the thirty-first day of the Long Month, a yard with a market under it. **The coalition of the seventeen splits, between people who want a wage and people who want a name, and neither side is wrong, and there is no roll, no count and no vote, and four people say out loud what they will not do.** A woman of about twenty-four who has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of the flood says no to writing any of it down and says why, and it is the first time she has chosen not to write one down because she wanted to. **A man of about thirty-two says nine sentences and does not try to hold it together and says he has not found another instrument and is not going to pretend to two hundred and forty people that he has.**

### Chapter 0183 — What a Warden Is For
Day 187, the Saturday, the thirty-second day of the Long Month, the low end of a causeway. **Water takes the bottom four hundred yards and about nine people from the store and about nine from the flats pull the same rope for two hours without discussing it, and the store pays the nine from the flats, and the finding is that the thing which has not happened in a hundred and eighty-seven days is a wage going the other way.** A woman of about thirty-eight comes out of the gateway with a bar because she took a groundkeeper's workway on the seventh day of the Long Month and the fourth line of it is that she does not get to walk off claimed ground because the weather has turned. Two pairs of boots are a favour and a favour stops.

### Chapter 0184 — The Seventh Day From Now
Day 188, the Sunday, the thirty-third day of the Long Month, a workroom with a date in chalk on the door. **There is one day of the quarter left and it is tomorrow, and two men in a room settle a date and neither of them wins it: the seventh day from now, a Sunday, the fortieth day of the Long Month, on which the store shuts, and both of them arrived at it from opposite ends and a man of about thirty-nine says nobody chose it.** A man of about forty-two refuses to hand over a page with four days on it and puts a date on one instead, because a page with a post in it is a thing with a post in it. A man of about forty-four says nine words: he is going to stand at the back.

### Chapter 0185 — The Thirty-Fourth Day of the Long Month
Day 189, the Monday, the thirty-fourth day of the Long Month, a room over a market. **THE QUARTER RUNS OUT, thirty-five days after the hearing on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, and nothing can be sent and nothing could be prepared and there is no road that reaches that office, and about nine people spend the whole day in a room with nothing to do.** **A return was due today and it does not arrive today, and the finding of the day is a man of about thirty-nine's: a thing that was going to arrive on a day and did not is a different kind of information from a thing that arrives.** **Ninety-four** is the count of silences and **a hundred and four** days is what the thirty-nine men have gone unpaid. Adrian's twenty-five days end at the last hour of the evening and about nine people do not count that.

### Chapter 0186 — A Figure With Nothing Under It
Day 190, the Tuesday, the thirty-fifth day of the Long Month, a room over a market. **A cart comes up the Reach road on its way somewhere else with a return in it, and a girl of seventeen reads four lines out, and the return agrees with the twelfth shape on a wall in a converted salt store, and the reading of that number is not Adrian's and never has been.** **A woman of about thirty-four says the whole of it in four sentences: a quarterly return is not a change, and a thing that is not a change is the most dangerous kind of thing there is, and nobody can be stopped by it, and four times a year.** A woman of about seventy of a village of nine households is asked about it in a yard and answers in four words and is not asked again. A man of about thirty-nine says on a road that it came in a cart that was going somewhere else and that is the sharpest thing anybody has said in a hundred and ninety days.

### Chapter 0187 — Ninety-Five
Day 191, the Wednesday, the thirty-sixth day of the Long Month, a room over a market. **The wall speaks and says four short things: the days on a gate can be given back by the household alone; no other party may do it; a person who is findable can be sent into any room; a person who is findable cannot be sent to a gate; nothing is owed, nothing is given, it can be done in a room in daylight.** Adrian refuses it before it has finished going away. **The count is ninety-five and it was ninety-five yesterday and it does not move, and the wall spoke on the eighteenth day of the Long Month and this is the second time it has put two halves of the same fact in one field.** A woman of about thirty-four names the cost and it is the name he did not choose. It gives no date, no room, no person and no instruction.

### Chapter 0188 — Nine Sentences About a Cost
Day 192, the Thursday, the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month, a room over a market with nothing on the table. **About nine people each say what it has cost them, out loud, in their own words, in no order anybody chose, and nobody names a price first and nobody improves on any of it.** The instrument is four sentences long and it was built in about nine minutes. **Tamsin Quill says hers in six sentences and refuses, in a room with about nine people in it, the one thing everybody in the room has been waiting for her to say about the man she has been checking for a hundred and ninety-two days**, and says that the cost of a check is that a person who is needed is a person who can be found and she has never once been asked whether she would rather be a person in a room. A woman of about twenty-four says hers last: she is the only person in this city who can be checked.

### Chapter 0189 — A Question With No Answer In It
Day 193, the Friday, the thirty-eighth day of the Long Month, a workroom nine miles up the upper road. **A man of about thirty-two gives a four-sentence question in his own hand to about nine people four days before a meeting-ground, and the instrument is used for the third time and on a body for the first time, and one person cannot be given it and is not going to be in the room, and he writes her refusal into his own book with the date on it.** A man of about forty-two cannot answer the question and will not refuse it and will be in a yard on the Sunday and not on the raft. A man of about twenty-nine says that a person who is findable is a person who can be sent and a person who can be sent is a person who cannot go. A woman of about twenty-nine says a meeting-ground is a number with legs and a question is better than a delegation. A share with the post taken out is still in a coat and is unresolved.

### Chapter 0190 — At the Back
Day 194, the Saturday, the thirty-ninth day of the Long Month — THE BATCH'S CLIFF, and the first of two chapters on that day. **A question in four sentences is read out in the local language in daylight with a date in chalk on the door in front of about nine people and about two hundred and forty who can hear, and a girl of seventeen reads it back for the people at the back from a slate and not from her own, and a man of about thirty-two stands at the back for four hours and does not answer it, and about two hundred and forty people hear him not answer it.** A woman of about thirty-four says out loud that the question is asking the wrong body, because a gate is not owned and the days are the household's and only a household can give them back. **He knew, since the twenty-sixth day of the Long Month, and wrote it anyway, and gave it in writing four days beforehand, and it is too late to change it, and the finding is that a room asked a question it cannot answer has to find the person who can, and the rule of the Reach is four hundred years old and has no name on it.** A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf will not put the question on a slate and carries it in her hand up eleven miles in the dark.

## Volume 04, Batch 0003 — Chapters 0191 to 0200, days 194 to 200

### Chapter 0191 — A Yard With Nothing In It
Day 194, the Saturday, the thirty-ninth day of the Long Month, the night before a meeting-ground is poled. **The store on the upper road is shut in the morning and about a hundred and forty people have a day they have not had since the seventh week of this flood, and the shut is in chalk on a door because a door is not a page, and about nine of them are going to walk up the road past this city.** A girl of seventeen says **ninety-eight** and gives the rule. Nine hundred miles away a man of forty-one in a very good coat is told a figure he did not ask for. A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf walks the question eleven miles in the dark in her hand, would not put it on a slate, and hands it to a water-count on a bank at about the ninth hour of the evening.

### Chapter 0192 — Nine Feet Of Standing Room
Day 195, the Sunday, the fortieth day of the Long Month. **A meeting-ground is poled four hours to the flats at Redroot and the question in four sentences is read out on a flat by a water-count with them on the flat of her left hand, and about nine people are standing there and not one of them was asked to be.** A man of forty-two stands in his own yard and watches about nine people walk up the road past this city and asks none of them where they are going. The raft says it holds no days on any gate, that it is four hundred years old that it will not name a household, and that there is no other date in four hundred miles this month it could have been poled to. Adrian is not there because a person who is findable cannot be sent to a gate.

### Chapter 0193 — It Will Not Name A Household
Day 196, the Monday, the forty-first day of the Long Month. **The rule is said out loud in four sentences and the water-count refuses to name it or say who built it, and then the raft refuses to name the household — not out of preference but because it does not know, and because asking nine people one at a time in front of the other eight would leave a mark on this water in about four months.** A man of about thirty-nine says the raft is right for his reason and not the raft's. Nine people then work out for themselves who is affected, for two hours, with no vote and no count. **The four sentences are legible on a wet hand until about the eleventh hour and then they are not, and about four days later nobody in four hundred miles can check what was asked.** The keeper comes out at the eleventh hour and says she is not going to be asked and is going to say it anyway.

### Chapter 0194 — A Date In Chalk On A Rail
Day 196, the Monday, the forty-first day of the Long Month. **A woman of about thirty-eight gives nine days of a week back, unasked, in daylight, on a flat, having given them in a kitchen with a date in chalk on a door seventeen days before, and she is seventeen days into a year of being angry and nobody is going to argue her out of the rest of it.** Nobody gave her anything and nothing is arriving with her name on it, and a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house says she is the one who has to live in it for the rest of the year. The days go back into a rota on a board in a fifth hand, in one copy, not copied. Adrian stood on a bank eleven miles above the cut and did not come down. **And the volume's one sentence is not said again and a gate is not owned stands at zero in all fifty chapters.**

### Chapter 0195 — Four Miles Of Causeway
Day 197, the Tuesday, the forty-second day of the Long Month. **The salt stops and nobody has shut anything: the racks stand in the channel, the channel takes its water from a gate, a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and nine hundred measures stand in a yard at the foot of a four-mile causeway that took forty days to build.** A man of about thirty-four says the whole of it is that the road is not shut and a gate is eleven feet away from it, and about four hundred and forty people dry salt for a buyer nine hundred miles away and there is no route in this world that reaches him. **The loss falls on the settlement and not on the Reach, that sentence is the reason nine people on a flat were able to do it, and a wage that stops is not a favour that stops.** **A hundred and twelve days is what the thirty-nine men under the near gate have gone unpaid, and it is a different debt and it did not change today.**

### Chapter 0196 — One Hundred And Fifty-Eight
Day 198, the Wednesday, the forty-third day of the Long Month. **The trestle count is read out in a room over a yard, in daylight, with a date on the door, by the man who has been keeping it, in his own hand, and it is one hundred and fifty-eight, counted one a day from the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else.** It was one hundred and fourteen on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one and eighty-nine on the thirty-ninth, and forty-four days separate the hearing from the room and there are forty-four in the difference. He does not know what a day of it means and has never pretended to. **Adrian was asked whether the day suited him and said no, and said out loud that he will never know who checked a number he was not in the room to hear.** A man of about thirty-nine says a person who is not findable cannot be checked and a person who is findable is a place to be found in and Marrow is neither, and a woman of about twenty-four writes all three down separately.

### Chapter 0197 — Nine And Eight
Day 198, the Wednesday, the forty-third day of the Long Month. **The number goes onto the wall of the room it was read in, in a column with a date at the top, in a hand that is not the hand that keeps the book, and about four people in that room had wanted it not to.** A woman of about thirty-four says a correct page is a page nobody can argue with and a page nobody can argue with is a page nobody can stop. A man of about forty-four recites his cost column once and says the two of those things are the same thing. **And about nine of the seventeen say one sentence each in a yard and about eight say another, so the split is nine and eight, and nobody made a count and nobody voted, and the finding is not the split: eight of them read a number they could not understand and decided the man who can get forty people a week out of a city that cannot get one out is the safer answer to it.**

### Chapter 0198 — A Person May Be Found
Day 199, the Thursday, the forty-fourth day of the Long Month. **A posting goes into a page: the ground stays with the households, no land right transfers, no workway is a term, the labour is shared and posted, a right of refusal that cannot be bought out is on it, and a man of about thirty-nine's own objection goes in with his name and the date on it because he is a household affected by a change.** A man of forty-two takes a share on the same posting as everybody else and says nine sentences, the ninth of which is the word *order*, and Renna Ord is at the back of that room and says nothing and about four people know the word is hers. **And the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf names the price before she says yes: for nine years nobody has known what she looks like while she is arguing, and from today there is a date and a stall and a name on a page. She asks in advance not to be thanked, and nobody thanks her.**

### Chapter 0199 — Eleven Lines
Day 200, the Friday, the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and the volume's resolution. **A land-and-labour compact of eleven lines is read out in the local language in daylight in a room over a market with a date in chalk on the door, before about nine people and about two hundred and forty who can hear, which is the sixth time two hundred and forty people have heard a thing said in that room.** A girl of seventeen reads the whole of it back twice from a plain slate and not from her own. **The ninth line is a person and it is *a person may be found*, and it is not the fifth line of the compact of eleven lines, and a woman of about twenty-nine says in five sentences that a document ended by the asking of a person and a document requiring a person to be findable are not the same instrument and neither is a copy of the other.** The name at the front of it is said once in a room and is deliberately not on any page you can read. The keeper is not in the room and nobody went and got her and that is entered as a failure.

### Chapter 0200 — At The Back Of His Own Queue
Day 200, the Friday, the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and the last chapter of Volume 04. **Out on the flats the four-mile causeway is standing open and empty with no bar on it, the salt has not stopped drying, a boy of eight rang the bell twice, and there is nothing in the day to tell anybody a story about.** In the market under the room, about two hundred and forty people have two sentences between them about a compact nobody can check and a list anybody can be angry at, and neither was said to the other. Nine miles up the road the store is open and the queue worked out of a chest that has two weeks in it, a man of about thirty-four said a hundred and fifteen days and said at the front of his own queue in daylight with a date on the door that he did not shorten it, and **at about the sixth hour of the evening a man of forty-two stands at the back of his own queue with his hands in his pockets and nobody turns him out.**
