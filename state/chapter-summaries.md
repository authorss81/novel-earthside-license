## VOLUME 15, BATCH 0005 — CHAPTERS 0741 TO 0750, DAYS 741 TO 750, ONE CHAPTER TO ONE DAY

**These ten entries were written before the review repair pass and they describe the days. The
entries below them at the foot of this block record what the repair pass changed, and they are the
newest statement about these ten pages.**

**0741 (741, Sunday, the four hundred and twenty-sixth, character).** The man of about thirty-nine who
trades on a board came up the back stair with his board under his arm and sat down on a stool at the
side of the bench after turning it about, and nobody in that room told him whose stool it was. He said
out loud that he was not going to ask. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the
front at the seventh hour and said that about nine people had gone up that stair that morning carrying
nothing and that there is no reason anybody in this city could be shown for that. About four people a
day went past the row outside. The woman of about fifty-two came up at the eighth hour, saw the man on
her stool, and said nothing. He left at the tenth hour. Change: a stool is left where a man's weight
turned it, which is not where she put it.

**0742 (742, Monday, the four hundred and twenty-seventh, political).** The man of about thirty-one
who carries things for a living sat at the far end of the bench with his hand on the flap of a
satchel on the floor behind it and did not open it. The woman of about fifty-two asked the room, and
not anybody in it, whether a sheet may be carried out of that building in a person's own hand, and
nobody answered her. The stallholder answered a question that had not been put to him: nobody in that
room is going to find out what leaves that building. The man of about twenty-seven came up at the
ninth hour, looked at the one-place form on its shelf, and did not touch it. Change: the question was
asked in front of nine people and the room produced two answers to a different question.

**0743 (743, Tuesday, the four hundred and twenty-eighth, physical, ADRIAN NOT OBTAINED).** Adrian
Vale worked a bar in the sockets on the far side of that door three times and it came down into the
same two sockets. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf could not get a length of board past
it and left it standing against the outside wall, and said out loud that he was not going to stand in
the road working out whose hands were on the bar. The man of about twenty-seven put his hand on the
face of that door and said he could feel it was shut on something. The board went off up the market on
the man's shoulder at the tenth hour and nothing had gone past that door all morning. **Adrian Vale did
not take that door off its hinges at any hour of that day, nobody in this city asked him to, and nobody
thanked him for not doing it. THE WANT IS NOT OBTAINED.**

**0744 (744, Wednesday, the four hundred and twenty-ninth, discovery).** The man of about fifty-seven
stood in his own doorway with his hands on his knees and a length of new rope over the back of a chair
behind him that no hand had ever gone near. The woman of about fifty-two came up the shut road on foot
and said out loud that the rope was new and that she was not going to ask what it was for. He neither
agreed nor disagreed. The man of about sixty went past with his handcart and came back and said
nothing. Change: at the tenth hour she went back down the road and could still see his hands on his
knees from the top of it, because he had not gone in.

**0745 (745, Thursday, the four hundred and thirtieth, THE RESOLUTION).** The woman of about
twenty-nine who keeps a public register came round the front of that building to the row at the foot of
the stair instead of going in at the front, went up, sat at the near end of the bench and opened that
book. About four people went past that door that day and about four of them were her. Nobody in that
room was told and nobody asked her how she came in. Nothing was decided, nothing was written, nobody
was rescued and nobody was defeated, and there is no eighth notice in this flood. The man of about
thirty-nine and the stallholder each said one thing that was not about the row. **The only person who
could have found out what she did is the man who put her there by deciding the row is not to be asked,
and he went back to his front two stalls along and was not sent.**

**0746 (746, Friday, the four hundred and thirty-first, character).** The man of about sixty took a
handcart up onto the stones of a shut road and set it down past the near end of a fence of sixteen
willow posts and eleven withies. The woman of about fifty-two said out loud that the cord on those
withies is one cord and not eleven, that she had not counted anything, and that nobody in this city
knows where that fence stops, including the man standing at this end of it. He put both hands on the
near post where hers had been and pushed once and let go. Change: he turned the cart about and walked
it back off the stones and down the road, and no post moved and no withy came off.

**0747 (747, Saturday, the four hundred and thirty-second, THE THIRD COST, AND THE SECOND COST OF THE
PERSON WHO PAYS IT).** At the fifth hour the woman of about twenty-nine said out loud in her own mouth
in front of nine people what it had cost her, before she said anything else. Nobody thanked her,
nobody improved on one word of it, and nobody in that room did one single thing about it at any hour
of that day. The stallholder came in and said he had heard her and was not going to answer it and was
not going to tell her she was right or that she was wrong. The woman of about fifty-two came up at the
ninth hour, saw the stool, and did not move it. Change: at the tenth hour she shut the book on that one
word and set it down square on the shelf in front of the second slate, and took her hands away.

**0748 (748, Sunday, the four hundred and thirty-third, THE NEW QUESTION).** At about the sixth hour the
man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went out of that room and down that stair and asked the man
of about thirty-nine who trades on a board **what a person is for, when the only thing anybody is going
to use them for is to have gone past.** He did not ask it to that room. **He was not answered. Nobody in
that room heard it and nobody in that market heard it, and the chapter says so in the next line.** He
was thanked by nobody for not answering and nobody argued with him about it, and the narrator does not
say what he thought. He went back up and said something else to the room. She was asked nothing, and
the question does not come from her and is not about her. At the tenth hour the woman of about fifty-two
came in and sat down on her own stool and said one word to nobody.

**0749 (749, Monday, the four hundred and thirty-fourth, character).** Nobody came up that stair that
morning and nobody went down it, and the chapter says so in narration and does not say why. About four
people went past the outside of that door without going up it. At the eighth hour she said one thing out
loud to the middle of the floor and there was nobody in that room to hear it. At the ninth hour she
carried the book the length of the room, set it square on the shelf in front of the second slate, stood
with both hands on the shelf, then carried it back and put it on the bench and sat down in front of it.
Change: the book went to the shelf and came back, and about four people went past while she was
carrying it.

**0750 (750, Tuesday, the four hundred and thirty-fifth, THE VOLUME'S LAST IMAGE).** The man of about
twenty-seven washed the board on the outside wall once and not twice, and said out loud that there is a
wall above the top edge of it that he washed once in the whole of the flood and stopped. The
stallholder said out loud that he has not taken the chalk out of his pocket on one of the mornings of
the flood and is not going to start this one. Then the plan's own list of objects, in this order: the
room and its shelf and the one-place form and the register and the second slate; the wharf board face up
under two lines of grey grit with a clean piece at the near edge; the fence of sixteen willow posts and
eleven withies; the new rope over the back of a chair and the man in his own doorway at the ninth hour
with his hands on his knees; the door at the foot of that stair with a date in chalk and a bare piece
of board about as wide as a hand under it that means nothing whatever; **and a strip of ground at the
foot of that stair with about four people a day's feet on it and nothing on it and nothing said about
it.** No panel on any of these pages; the one panel of this volume is on Chapter 0740.

In that room over that market the woman of about fifty-two says the room's own figure out loud in the ordinary course — about nine of them — and nobody improves on one word of it and nobody asks her where the figure came from. **The trough stands at the level another man left it at and nothing on it says any of it happened.**
2. **0752, Thursday, the four hundred and thirty-seventh, character, Adrian one of three.** He carries a length of board up eleven miles to lay it along the sill of the one window in that room and the sill is a hand's breadth deep and it will not go, and he leans it against the outside wall at the foot of that stair instead — **not obtained.** He then stands at the foot of that stair from the fifth hour to the ninth hour and watches the whole length of that strip of ground and **cannot afterwards say how many he saw go over it**, and he asks nobody anything. He lifts one flagstone at the foot of that stair and sets it back in the same bed with the same face up. At the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight sets his own board against that wall beside Adrian's, and neither man says a word to the other. **Two boards lean against that wall at the tenth hour and one of them is his and one of them is not.**
3. **0753, Friday, the four hundred and thirty-eighth, physical, Adrian not in it.** The man of about thirty-one comes back, empties that trough into the low place in the stone, and fills it higher than any hand had left it. The man of about thirty-eight finds the wet stone, says out loud that somebody has been at that trough twice and that he is not going to ask about either time, and neither of the two men says a word to the other. In that room the man of about thirty-four gives the room's own figure out loud and says he has never once written it down and has never asked where the first of them came from; his chalk stays in his pocket. **The trough ends the day a hand's breadth further from the step and the second slate on the shelf behind her has never been picked up, turned over or written on.**
4. **0754, Saturday, the four hundred and thirty-ninth, character, Adrian two of three.** He stands a board upright against the bare back of the stallholder's own board because a board standing there is a thing people can see, and nobody in that row looks at it. The stallholder gets behind his trestle, pulls his own board a finger's width, and does not turn round. At the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine stops at that front and stands there a minute, and hears the room's own figure said behind him — and **at the end of that morning he does not go up that stair with his board under his arm, for the first time in the whole of this flood, and nobody in that row asked him where he had gone.** Adrian puts his board back against the same back at the tenth hour.
5. **0755, Sunday, the four hundred and fortieth, recovery, Adrian not in it.** The man of about fifty-seven tries to mend the gate of his own yard with a length of cord and a piece of iron with a hole in the end of it, gets it to take, and takes the whole of it back off again and hangs the iron on a peg inside his own door. He goes out along the top of that shut road about forty paces, stops twice, and turns back before the fence. The length of new rope lies on the chair where it lay at the fourth hour and no hand in this flood has gone near it. **He handles the cord and the iron twice with his hands three feet away from that rope and neither time near it.**
6. **0756, Monday, the four hundred and forty-first, political, Adrian not in it.** About nine in one mouth and eight in another in the same morning, and the difference between the two figures is one person and no mouth in that room can put a name to it. **At the sixth hour the bar comes out of its sockets on the far side of that door and that door stands open into the room all morning and nobody in that room knows who opened it.** The man of about thirty-four turns the bar over on the flags and puts it back. About four people go past the outside of that door and none of them goes up. **Nothing on that ground says how many were in the room above it.**
7. **0757, Tuesday, the four hundred and forty-second, discovery, Adrian three of three.** He gets to that trough at the fourth hour to fill it before anybody else has to and it is already full, and he does not ask one person in this city who filled it. Up in that room the man of about thirty-nine says out loud that he has come up that stair on every morning of this flood and cannot tell which of them was the first and has not got two of them in an order that means anything, and **nobody asks him a second question about it at any hour of that day.** At the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight has to turn his barrow on its side to get past the two boards leaning at that stair. **The trough stands where he left it and the man who filled it said nothing.**
8. **0758, Wednesday, the four hundred and forty-third, character, Adrian not in it.** The woman of about twenty-nine carries that book the length of the room twice and sets it down out of the light that comes over the roof at the ninth hour, and nobody thanks her and nobody asks her whose hand is not hers on one of those five figures. The man of about twenty-seven comes up that stair with his bucket and looks at that shelf and says nothing at any hour of that day. **The narration says in plain words that about nine people were in that room over the course of that morning and not one of them said out loud how many of them there were, and that has not been done on every morning of this flood before.**
9. **0759, Thursday, the four hundred and forty-fourth, physical, Adrian not in it.** The coil of rope is off the handcart and a piece of it is bound round one of the eleven withies on that fence, and the cut end of it is clean. The man of about sixty says out loud that he put that fence up and will not take it off and is not going to be asked, cuts the piece off, puts his own cord back on, ties all eleven withies in the order he keeps them in and draws each knot tight, and does not measure that fence and does not move a post. **In that room the woman of about twenty-nine says that room's own figure out loud for the first time in this flood and nobody asks her where it came from.**
10. **0760, Friday, the four hundred and forty-fifth, political, Adrian not in it. THE BATCH'S TURN.** The bar has gone back into its sockets and that door is shut and nobody in that room knows who shut it. **The woman of about fifty-two says out loud that that figure is the only thing that room has ever said about itself, that it has never been on a page or on any of the seven sheets that have gone up that road, and that she has been one of the mouths it goes out of every morning.** Nobody thanks her. **The man of about thirty-nine then says out loud that he has been the mouth it goes out of more often than anybody, that it has never once been written down anywhere he has ever been, and that he is saying he has noticed that and is not saying what he is going to do about it.** Nobody asked him what he is going to do about it. **That figure is in about nine mouths and on no page at all.**

## VOLUME 16, BATCH 0002 — CHAPTERS 0761 TO 0770, DAYS 761 TO 770, ONE CHAPTER TO ONE DAY

**Ten days, one chapter to a day, from a Saturday to a Monday, the four hundred and forty-sixth to the four
hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month. Read `outline/volume-16.md` before this block. The ten cards are
at the head of `state/batch-summaries/volume-16-batch-0002.md` and this block is a summary and not a plan.**

1. **0761, Saturday, the four hundred and forty-sixth, character, Adrian one of five.** He turns that chair at the far side of that room about on the flags and walks it back until its back stands against that wall, because a man sitting in it where it has stood since the flood came would have his own back to that door. He sits in it and finds that from there he can see the near end of that bench and the flags and no more, and he gets up off it before the fifth hour. **The man of about thirty-nine comes up at the fifth hour, finds the chair where it has not been, sits down in it, says out loud that he is not going to ask which of them put it there, and nobody in that room says a word.** **NOT OBTAINED.** About nine people are in that room over the morning and not one of them puts how many of them out loud, and nobody notices.
2. **0762, Sunday, the four hundred and forty-seventh, discovery, Adrian not in it.** The woman of about twenty-nine gives that figure out loud at the fourth hour; the man of about thirty-eight comes up at the seventh hour and gives eight. **The woman of about fifty-two says out loud that the two figures are not about the same morning, or not about the same part of one, and that she has been saying that figure for the whole of this flood without knowing what hour she was saying it for, and that is this batch's discovery.** Nobody improves on it, nobody puts a question to her, and no name for the person the difference comes out of any mouth in that room. **The man of about thirty-eight stands that barrow along the near wall at the eighth hour and about nine people go round it all morning and he does not move it.** The man of about thirty-nine is not in that room at any hour and no mouth connects his absence to the difference.
3. **0763, Monday, the four hundred and forty-eighth, character, Adrian two of five.** He picks the satchel off the bench, will not put it on the shelf that carries the sheet with its heading and its one ruled place, takes it up the eleven miles and brings it back, and sets it on the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder at the seventh hour with the strap over the front edge. He stands there with his hands on the shelf for two hours. **At the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one comes up, puts his own hand on the strap over Adrian's own hand, takes the satchel off that shelf and goes back down that stair.** The flap is never turned up, nothing is opened, and no mouth in that room asks what is in it. **NOT OBTAINED.**
4. **0764, Tuesday, the four hundred and forty-ninth, COST — THE FIRST COST OF THIS VOLUME, Adrian not in it.** In that room, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair and about nine people in it over the morning, the woman of about fifty-two says what it has cost her before she says anything else: that she is the one person in that room who knows who is not in it, that she has never once said that out loud, that nobody in that room has ever asked her how she knows, and that this is the whole of what she has that is hers. **Nobody thanks her, nobody improves on a word of it, nobody argues with it, she is not asked how she knows, the man of about thirty-nine hears all of it and says nothing, and nothing in that room is done about it at any hour of that day.** At the seventh hour she sits down on the stool she carried out of that room and left where anybody could sit on it, which she has not done on any morning of this flood, and the man of about thirty-four moves a forearm's length along that bench without being asked.
5. **0765, Wednesday, the four hundred and fiftieth, physical, Adrian three of five.** He wrings that rag out in that trough, wipes the whole of that stone lip at the fourth hour, wipes it three times, and leaves that rag on the wet end of it with the wet side up, because the man of about thirty-eight puts his own hands on that stone at the seventh hour before he does anything else. **That lip is dry by the seventh hour** and the man of about thirty-eight says out loud that somebody has been round it with a wet thing and that he is not going to ask. **NOT OBTAINED.** The grit and salt off that lip is in the water at the low place in that stone at the tenth hour and standing in a shape a foot wider than it stood in at the fourth hour.
6. **0766, Thursday, the four hundred and fifty-first, character, Adrian four of five.** He lifts the top two boards off that stack at the fourth hour and stands them on their ends against that yard wall, and the whole of the face of the board that has lain face up on that stack since the flood came is standing in the open with nothing over it. Rain comes at the seventh hour and runs across the clean piece at the near edge and darkens it a hand's breadth. **He does not turn that board and he does not put either of those two boards back on the top of that stack. NOT OBTAINED.** The man of about thirty-eight finds the stack two boards shorter at the eighth hour, says out loud that he is not going to ask about either of them, and stands his own barrow a hand's breadth further off that wall for the rest of the morning without saying so.
7. **0767, Friday, the four hundred and fifty-second, political, Adrian not in it.** The man of about thirty-four says out loud, in the ordinary course, that this room has been giving that figure on more mornings of this flood than he has said anything else and that nobody in this room has ever asked anybody where it came from, **and nobody in that room asks him.** The man of about twenty-seven comes up with his bucket and says out loud that somebody put a date in chalk on the outside of that door and that he does not know who and is not going to find out by asking anybody in that room; the woman of about twenty-nine says she does not know either and the man says nobody in that room ever has. **Nobody says a word to either of them about either answer.** At the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine lays his own board flat on that bench face up for about the length of a minute with about nine people in that room and the part of it where no name is standing up in the light in front of all of them, and **nobody says one word about what is on that bench**, and he takes it back under his arm and goes down the stair.
8. **0768, Saturday, the four hundred and fifty-third, discovery, Adrian five of five.** He puts his head below the line of that one sill to look down at the whole length of that ground, and every time he raises his head to see the other half his own face goes into the glass. **THE DISCOVERY: nobody who goes over that ground looks up at that window — every one of them looks up at that stair and the door at the bottom of it — so a man at that sill is unlooked-at for as long as he likes, and he can see the top of every head that crosses and not one face.** At the seventh hour he hears the stair, looks down at it, and nobody comes up it. At the ninth hour the weather puts a mist on the outside of that glass and takes his face off it and leaves a wet print the size of a man's face, which is going out of the glass while he watches it. **NOT OBTAINED.**
9. **0769, Sunday, the four hundred and fifty-fourth, character, Adrian not in it.** He comes up at the fourth hour with that board under his arm, puts it where he puts it, and sits at the far end of that bench, **and not one word is said to him at any hour of that day and he says none back.** That register lies open on that bench from the fourth hour to the tenth hour. The woman of about fifty-two says that figure out loud in the ordinary way at the fifth hour and nobody looks along that bench at the far end of it while she says it. The man of about thirty-eight carries a length of board up and stands it on the near wall on the spot where he stood his barrow on the Sunday before. **At the ninth hour he shifts that board out from under the light with the flats of his own boot, about a foot along, and puts his own right hand back on it.**
10. **0770, Monday, the four hundred and fifty-fifth, character, Adrian not in it. THE BATCH'S END.** The man of about thirty-nine says that figure out loud at the fifth hour by himself and nobody takes one word of it up. At the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four says out loud into that room, to nobody in it, that he cannot tell since which morning the man with the barrow began coming up that stair and that he has been at it for about an hour. **About four people in that room say out loud that they do not know in four different words, and about four say nothing at all.** At the tenth hour he puts it to the woman of about twenty-nine that not one of them can say what that figure had been at the fourth hour, and she says she cannot, and the two of them stand at that bench with a forearm of wood between them and neither of them can. **Four days before the volume's one decision, and the batch ends there.**

**AND WHAT NONE OF THE TEN DOES: no decision of record, nothing decided in that room, no second cost, no panel,
no document, no notice, no name spoken, no question asked of the room and none answered, no new person, no new
descriptor, no route opened, nobody rescued, nobody defeated, nobody stronger, no relationship milestone, no
repair of the consent fracture, no figure off any object in the inventory, and no day after 770. The room's
own figure is said aloud on the five days Adrian Vale is not in and in no mouth on the five days he is, which is
`outline/volume-16.md` §9 item 3 and not a choice.**
