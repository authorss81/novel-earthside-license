# Continuity State

## Bootstrap canon

- **World:** Veyra is a real, inhabited medieval-fantasy world, not a simulation. Locals have ordinary work, law, food systems, political disagreements, and a history that continues independently of Adrian.
- **Magic:** Veyran Weave changes existing patterns and requires a pattern, a working, a genuine witness, and a concrete residue. Consent changes power; refusal is a valid result.
- **Crossing:** The Earthside License is a bounded permission interface laid over a fragment of the First Grammar. It can mark travelers, translate direct promises, show limited permissions, and open a threshold only through physical anchors and local agreement.
- **Fragment vs recognized License:** the prompt Adrian accepts in Chapter 1 is an unanchored permission **fragment**. A fragment can take a name, leave a mark, and pull an arrival, but it has no resonant site, no willing Veyran witness, and no receiving agreement, so it is not a recognized License and cannot open a controlled threshold. Adrian is a **fragment-bearer**, not a recognized License holder, from Chapter 1 until Chapter 10.
- **Return marks:** each mark belongs to one named living person. Controlled routes carry one named person per opening, require a new inspection before reuse, and can be suspended by the receiving community. A route's capacity never grows because more witnesses stand behind it. No automatic respawn or inherited ability exists.
- **System limits:** no infinite inventory, health bar, resurrection, mind reading, obedience, or guaranteed return. Prompts can be incomplete or edited. Ivelin's altered prompts are a known long-term danger, but the protagonists do not know that in the opening.
- **First Grammar:** built before the First Sundering as a plural device for keeping a shared law coherent. It broke into Anchorstones after the disaster. The Quiet Choir later rebuilt fragments and adapted one into a cross-world bridge.
- **First Sundering:** caused eight centuries ago by a Crown attempt to force one false harvest record over contradictory testimony. It removed or displaced people, roads, and memories.
- **Earlier crossing:** a narrow crossing occurred near the Saltwake lock roughly three centuries ago and became the local Bright Pilgrims tradition. Nineteen stones on the upper road carry writing nobody living can read. The present breach reuses that damaged resonant site.
- **Adrian Vale:** male, 32, game designer at Morrowglass Interactive. His want, need, flaw, fear, relationships, and growth constraints are defined in `bible/characters.md`.
- **Tamsin Quill:** local anchorwright and Adrian's slow-burn romantic partner, not an instant love interest. Her consent and independent political agency are required throughout.
- **Central antagonist:** Ivenn Marrow and the Quiet Choir's Single Witness faction. Ivenn is introduced in Volume 1 and remains the final human antagonist; the First Grammar is the established mechanism, not a new final enemy.
- **Ending:** Adrian publicly rejects both the permanent game lie and a single-author history, distributes the license among independent witnesses, and accepts an accountable bridge that can work without him. His personal return mark is spent once, at Stage 5; the finale surrenders the recognized seat of first holder and his last hold on the Grammar's record, not a second return mark.

## Volume 01 author decisions (settled during the outline review)

- **Site vocabulary for the lock:** five names, one site each. The **main breach** is the damaged opening in the gate itself — the Chapter 1 arrival site, open and under watch until the closing working. The **outer tear** is the smaller join beside the outer channel; it opens in Chapter 5 at the one joint the Chapter 4 repair leaves unwatched, is closed in Chapter 9, and leaves the **maintenance seam**. The **near gate** is the inner working gate the dock crew holds during repairs. The **chapel-floor hairline seam** opens in Chapter 3 and is the visible edge of the older damage exposed in full in Chapter 24. The **scarred outer threshold** is that older Anchorstone, exposed in Chapter 24 and tearing in Chapter 25. No event happens at two of these sites.
- **Chapter 25 has one cause:** a deliberate, consented, locally witnessed working fails at the outer threshold it could not reach. Owen does not force anything open there; he is a bystander who reaches for the missing child and injures a shoulder.
- **The volume's closing distributed working is Chapters 48–50.** Chapters 41–47 are the choice and the commitment to it.
- **System usage is capped at one message per chapter.** Exactly four prompts appear in Batch 0001 — one each in Chapters 1, 2, 7 and 10. Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9 carry none; Chapter 8 re-reads the Chapter 7 fragment. The Chapter 10 five-part threshold description is a civic document read and entered by the notary, not a panel, and the Chapter 7 route-reading test is a local working rather than a second message.
- **Adrian never performs a working.** The Chapter 10 working is performed by Tamsin, the Chapter 9 dock crew, Hettis Roke, and the Lantern Concord notary Sera Vail; Adrian reads and marks the join-line. He has the Witness stage from Chapter 9 and no workway.
- **Adrian stays Unbound through Volume 1.** He refuses the Wayfinder workway in Chapter 7 and again in Chapters 13–20, and the volume ends with him holding no workway at all.
- **One hearing, not two.** Chapter 7 is a preliminary chapel audience that ends with the Concord setting a date. The formal hearing convenes at first light on the day after the Chapter 9 working and is where Chapter 10 happens.
- **Chapter 10 records, it does not ratify.** The route's terms are entered in the public ledger, but the recognition is **incomplete**: one Reed village refuses, so the receiving community has accepted a reduced area rather than the whole, and the Concord has not yet ruled on whether a first license may exist. A reduced receiving area is not a smaller recognized License, so the instrument is **pending** — inspectable, arguable, and unusable. The fragment is the mark on Adrian's palm, not an object he carries; Chapter 10 presses that mark into the seam and he keeps only a thinner trace.
- **The premise is deferred, on purpose.** The "Earthside arrivals believe they joined a game" half of the premise is not established in Chapters 1–10 and becomes explicit later, when larger groups of players cross expecting a game. Which volume carries that shift is deliberately unfixed. Chapters 1–10 do not have the cohort assert it.
- **Registering names:** *skyborn* is a Saltwake dock-worker term of suspicion, not a species or a rank. *Sera Vail* is the Concord notary and *Hettis Roke* the Hearthguard signatory; both are working cast, not leads. There is no "Witness mark" as a rank — the marks are the palm fragment-mark, a return mark, and a black line.

## Canon established in Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10)

### Geography and sites

- **The Lock of Salt** is a public tide gate with a barrel-vaulted **chamber** above the **lower gallery**, three arched bays, a central channel, and the **near gate** — a black timber and iron slab the dock crew stands under on a working rotation.
- The **outer channel** runs below and outside the lock, reached by a two-hundred-step **walk-round** with a half-rotten rail and a low parapet. The Chapter 5 outer tear is in the outer wall of that channel.
- The **lock chapel** stands in the yard above the lock, with a low arched window, an arch where three jurisdictions meet, a daily renewed patch of lime and clay, and a keeper's board. It is called the **lock chapel**, not the old chapel.
- The **chapel stone** is a single pale slab laid *across* the flagstones at the foot of the arch, raised four inches, worn into a shallow bowl by four hundred years of boots. Every person who enters the chapel steps over it. It is the threshold stone and it is **not** the floor.
- The **chapel-floor hairline seam** is a hair-thin dead-straight black line between two long flagstones near the foot of the arch, about a hand's width long, dry since Chapter 3, and four feet from the chapel stone. Water rose out of it uphill in Chapter 3. It is the visible edge of older damage beneath the chapel.
- The **maintenance seam**, called the **Narrow Mark** in the ledger, is an eighteen-inch, three-finger-wide closed seam in the outer wall of the outer channel, under the cap of the old landing on the walk-round. It is not a door and it does not carry anybody.
- **Scale rule for the mark and the seam:** the fragment-mark on Adrian's palm is the size of a thumbnail and the seam is eighteen inches by three fingers. The two are the same **proportion**, not the same size — every branch of one is in the same ratio as the other, the way a coin fits the die it was struck from, which is why a thumbnail laid over the lip at one end of the seam covers it exactly. Established on the page in Chapter 9. Never describe them as the same size.
- The **displaced Anchorstone** that Ivenn's Chapter 7 fragment points to lies *under the lock chapel's own yard-side landing*, four steps left of the yard. It is dry, set in the footing, and is a **different place** from the Narrow Mark. Nobody has moved it.

### Clock of Batch 0001

Four days, and the tide drives everything. Get this right before writing a line of Chapter 11 or later.

- **Tuesday.** Chapter 1 is 2:40 a.m. on Earth. Adrian arrives at dawn; Chapter 3 is midmorning, Chapter 4 late morning, the outer tear opens at midday in Chapter 5, and Chapter 6 is that afternoon and evening. The chapel window lights at the end of Chapter 5.
- **Wednesday, 6 a.m.** The crier brings the Concord notice at the close of Chapter 6. She says the next tide is before light on **Thursday**, and that the hearing is at first light on **the day after that tide**, which is **Friday**. The Concord crier is a civic official and reads the date in a flat clerk's voice; do not have anyone else contradict her.
- **Wednesday evening.** Chapter 7, the preliminary chapel audience. Tamsin withdraws the oath mid-test; the black line opens in the chapel stone. Marrow leaves, the lantern goes out, and the voice speaks the old oath and then says Adrian's name.
- **Wednesday night into Thursday.** Chapter 8. The voice returns at the fourth hour with the fifth, older syllable. Adrian's palm opens its black line. They go out at dawn.
- **Thursday, pre-dawn.** Chapter 9, the second tide surge. The crew goes out along the walk-round at 4:20 a.m., the outer tear is shut by about half past ten, and Sera Vail arrives at two in the afternoon and says the hearing is **at first light tomorrow, in about sixteen hours**. The breach has not narrowed and the pale edge of the join in the gate has widened by the width of a finger since Tuesday.
- **Friday morning.** Chapter 10, the day after the second tide. The seam is bound at eight o'clock and the mark is pressed into it at eight twenty, and the hall scene with Vail's read-back, Owen's statement, and Tamsin in the rain follows the same morning.

### Facts about the world

- Adrian is **not** a Veyran speaker. He has a handful of maintenance-oath words and everything runs through Tamsin, which makes the group one interpreter wide.
- A working requires a **pattern**, a **hand**, a **willing witness**, and a **cost**; the residue always lands in the place. A promise nobody accepts is not a working. The **old form** of the maintenance oath additionally binds the gate *and the man standing in it*, and was stopped sixty years ago.
- The lock is a **resonant site** because three jurisdictions repeat the same maintenance oath there. The chapel's arch and its daily lime-and-clay patch are both working sites; the chapel stone is a threshold, not a working.
- **Phones have no bars and their clocks have stopped at 11:52.** Earth money is useless locally. A folding spanner, a multi-tool, a tape measure, and a lighter are the group's whole material advantage, and the multi-tool is the most dangerous object in the building.
- **Reverberation on the page:** the market cart was mechanically fixed and lost a day of trade because its lashing could no longer be sworn to; two men lost forty minutes each; Adrian's ear rang for a day; the bottom field lost a season.
- The breach out of the chapel's black line **carries a voice** but has never carried a body. It repeats the old oath with a fifth, older syllable — *answer* — and it knows Adrian's name.
- Nine Elms, Four Elms, Long Water, Silt Row, Corrow, Black Sluice and the two gatehouses are the receiving area the Reed Commons has accepted. **Nine Elms' water is not received**, at the word of the nine households rather than the assembly, and may be withdrawn on any day the household chooses.

### Adrian's state

- **Fragment-bearer** from Chapter 1 to Chapter 10. **Named holder of the pull** in the Chapter 10 ledger entry from Chapter 10 on, which is a recorded duty on a pending instrument, not a License holding. He is a holder of a recognized License only at the trial license in Chapters 48–50.

- **Witness stage** earned in Chapter 9: he perceives a join without anybody owing it to anybody, understands one direct promise at a time, and still cannot create a working. Cost: sensory bleed, and a duty to a place he cannot clear.
- **No workway. Unbound.** He refused the Wayfinder profile in Chapter 7 in front of witnesses and Marrow wrote the refusal down. He does not hold a return mark and has no way home.
- **Two separate marks on the right palm, and they behave differently.** The **pale fragment-mark** is in the seam. The **thin black line** is *not* part of it: it opened in Chapter 8 in the heel of the palm, underneath the cut that ran along the pale mark when Adrian spent four hours of borrowed join-reading and did not answer the voice. Vask calls it a black line on a person, an overload warning, and tells him it is not a punishment and not a reward but a stone telling him what he is standing on. It does not thin, fade, or warm; it is exactly as wide in Chapter 10 as it was in Chapter 8; it does not go into the stone with the pale mark; and nobody else can see it, because nobody steps over a hand. It is **not** a return mark and it does not deepen into a black scar in a person in Volume 1 — that is reserved for Owen's return mark in Chapter 38. Do not merge the two marks and do not let the pale mark's decay be described as the black line's.
- **Chapter 10 leaves him a trace, not an absence.** What remains on the palm is a thin pale ghost running out toward the wrist. He could have taken the mark back and knew that nobody present could have stopped him or proved it afterwards.

- The four rules on the loft wall are: Tamsin's three conditions verbatim, then Adrian's fourth — tell Mina about every injury, including the ones that look survivable. Mina's addendum: every number the group states about how bad something is is written as *what we said*, never as *what it was*.

### Documents and institutions created

- **The roll of strangers.** The Concord's quarterly description of everybody in Saltwake who is not from it, sent to Aurel four times a year in a wooden case on a river boat. Chapter 16 enters Adrian as **Unbound** in it, in the words *offered on the second day and again on the twelfth, and refused twice* — the second day being the Wednesday of the chapel audience and the twelfth being the second Saturday, so the roll and Marrow's chalk on the keeper's board date the same offer to the same day. It is a Crown-readable sentence about him that will outlive the working record.
- **The keeper's board.** Nyla Ferrow's board on the chapel door: her four words and her fifth word in chalk on one side, four inches high; Vask's list of *what may not come out of this hole* on the other. It is not a public roll, and both women say so.
- **The keeper's fifth word.** The syllable the old oath has had put into the middle of it since Chapter 8, which Tamsin renders in English as *answer*. It is **not in the oath**. It is the keeper's word — *we keep the third thing* — and Nyla Ferrow says it at the arch every morning, has said it for thirty years, and says it as a *keeping* and never as a *saying*. Her mother and her mother's mother said it. **The voice in the lock join is using a form that combines the old maintenance oath with a keeper's word that has never been in the oath**, and both families are standing in the same yard.
- **The binding in the yard.** The keeper's price for opening her ground: for as long as the ground is open, the four words and the fifth are said **in the open, in the yard, twice a day, in any weather, by whoever is nearest.** Entered by Vail as a **labour, not a payment**, on a rota in the open with no name at the top, and she will strike out any name put at the top. Owen Park learns them and says them; the first attempt of the morning does not work, the second does, and both facts go on the board. It began when **Vask's own bar** went in under the north flagstones on the Saturday of Chapter 15 — the keeper set the price over the probe, not over the Chapter 20 dig — and it is a season's obligation, not less.
- **Vask's list: what may not come out of this hole.** (1) the stone in its place; (2) water, in any vessel, for any reason; (3) any writing; (4) **anything that answers.** Vask's reason is a hole she has been in once before, not a voice, and she has never read of one in the chapel's register.
- **The dig terms**, five items, read in a yard and entered: the cut is in the floor **at the hairline in the flagstones, four feet beyond the threshold slab at the foot of the arch**, and not in the threshold slab; the dig stops the moment the void is found; it is not a rescue; it is not a door; the binding is done in the yard and before the bar goes in.
- **The eleven-line boundary table.** The Concord's own list of every attempt to move the old river boundary, which Tamsin has kept a private copy of since she was twenty-two and has never read aloud in a yard. Line seven is hers, from when she was twenty-one. **Line one is the only attempt ever carried out**, in living memory, by the three jurisdictions together at a public act with witnesses; the stone was set one hundred yards downstream and the cost column says the bottom field failed and four hundred people were rehoused. She read the table out in Chapter 15. It is now a public document that Chapters 21–50 can weaponise against her, and she knows that.
- **The Chalk and Clay bound**: the Crown's **Schedule Four: Answerable Party**, a form with one field and no exit. Refused and entered as refused in Chapter 13, with the Crown's compensation declined with it.
- **The group rota** on the loft wall: thirty-one names, four columns, and the fourth column is what the **work** is for, not what the person is for, on Sera Vail's ruling.
- **The Salt Weavers' chapel**, two floors above the market in the same building as the loft, a disused guild chapel Sesh Brack has the key to, given to the group on condition that they take the plaster off the north wall at the end of it. The plaster is still on. This is where the group meets.
- **The public statement** of Chapter 12, on a board in the river market: the six names stay on the wall, the **fears come down**, and under a sentence Adrian disagreed with — *nobody from the wall has asked this city for anything* — a line he insisted on, which is the only sentence on that board that will still be true in February: *what the six were asked for, and by whom, and what they were told it would cost, is in the Concord's book, and any person in this city may come and read it.*

### Political weather

- **The Glass Crown has answered nothing and asked for everything.** The first letter is from the Office of Crossings and Tides at Aurel and asks, in courteous language, *who is answering for the bridge*. Adrian refused it in Chapter 13. The refusal is entered, with the reasoning, at page twenty-three. It **costs the group the only protection on offer**: no Crown advocate, no Crown-funded labour crew, no stipend against the bottom field, and a written refusal with Adrian's name on it in a file in Aurel. It also leaves Nine Elms' refusal standing, because a Crown registration would receive for the whole realm.
- **A second Crown letter exists and Adrian did not know about it**: a formal notice to the Reed Reach's meeting-ground and gatehouses, and to Nine Elms by name, carried by Peth Lammay in the same mail bag. **It is read into a book in front of the nine households the morning after Chapter 13.** Any reply to Aurel now has to carry notice to the Reach, and Vail's entered reply says so.
- **Jonah Pike has refused the Brass Measure in one sentence in a yard**, in front of eleven people, on Priya's instruction, with the sentence in her log, and has not mentioned it since. He is a no for the lock priority, and four hundred measures of flour and a week's notice is what he got instead.
- **Noor Haddad's ledger has a new column with a small square round it: who gave it to us and what they expect.** Sesh Brack's unrecorded gift out of the widows' box is entered in it, as *unrecorded* and *she chose to be found*.
- **Owen Park holds a twelve-month lease on the two upper rooms, in his own hand**, with two conditions attached that he has not yet met. Sesh Brack's status in the building is now an open question he is obliged to ask her about.

### The new and hard technical facts

- **A workway's real cost, read out by Marrow in Chapter 16 and confirmed by nobody but him:** repeated route work taxes memory and balance, takes the road's name into the place where you keep your name, and is an *erosion*, not a punishment. The obligation and the temptation are the same object: a wayfinder cannot refuse to carry a road, so a wayfinder is the obvious person to be asked to take the boundary stone down to the water. That is the design, and that is why the offer was made to Adrian and not to Tamsin.
- **The interface has no state.** The Chapter 7 offer (day 2) and the Chapter 16 offer (day 12) both end *it is not yet accepted*, ten days and a second refusal apart. There is no record of a refusal in it. Adrian's whole working assumption — that a permission is a thing with a state — is wrong, and he has written that down and does not know what it means.
- **Adrian's third and fourth prompts.** Chapter 11: the holder may be summoned, and a holder who does not come **may be replaced at the next sitting** — which is *not* what the ledger says. The ledger says the seat falls vacant and stays vacant. Adrian notices the gap himself; nobody certifies the panel. Chapter 15: the old boundary is held in two places, one the stone and one the table, and **a holder may be shown which one the water is using** — refused, because it would be true and there would be no page, no witness, and no argument, which makes the holder a singleton. Chapter 12: the paper can be taken down tonight; nobody has said that you may.
- **There are four messages in Batch 0002: one each in Chapters 11, 12, 15 and 16, and none in 13, 14, 17, 18, 19 or 20.** The absence in 13 and 17 is on the page and is information: the wall said nothing at all when a Crown letter put the week in front of him, and nothing at all when ninety people asked the instrument what it was for. In Chapter 18 Adrian notices that the messages come out of *places* and that the one place they have never come from is the gate itself.
- **The main breach is being measured, not spoken from.** Twenty nightly readings taken at one in the morning by three chalked marks **in the pale edge of the join in the gate itself**, reached from the lower gallery, not from the walk-round. They run from four and seven-eighths to seven and seven-eighths: **three inches in twenty nights, arriving in four steps of three-quarters of an inch each**, and a crack does not step. Vask's rule: a press is a curve and a work is a decision. The four steps fall on the four days the Lock of Salt did **unusual** work; the tide and the flood did nothing at all. The conclusion Adrian reaches and cannot act on: **the ordinary maintenance of the lock is the mechanism**, the near gate and the breach are nine feet apart in the same course of stone and are not separate facts to the stone, and the only lever is to stop working the lock — which drowns eleven villages in the next flood instead. The measurement is good and the answer is unavailable. **The main breach did not open in Chapter 19 and the gate did not speak.**
- **Owen Park's account, and it is the batch's sharpest:** it is not the stone, it is the four words forty men say twice a day under the near gate; the man in the join did not build that pattern, he found it; so the field is a **toll**, and somebody is being **charged**, and the only question left is by whom and whether they have agreed to it — and they have not.
- **The old customs document, and Marrow's part in it.** Halvard Ketch's genuine leaf, about three hundred and ten years old, from a Saltwake customs clerk whose boat was held eleven days at the river mouth, listing **six things that came out of a hole** in descriptions rather than names, and entered in a file that was not important for three hundred years. It is genuine. It contains no name, and it never did, because the manifest burned. **Marrow told a room of nine people on the Wednesday — day 2, the night of the chapel audience — that it was a fraud, and let them believe the whole of it was fake.** Ketch has been turning it over for three days by Chapter 12. He found it in the first week. He has reason and access and is the only man in Saltwake who has both. **No public scene convicts him and none may.** His complicity stays plausible and unproven to the end of the volume, and he stays publicly useful.
- **The leak was never a leak.** The six names Adrian asked for on the first morning, written by Priya, went up over a stall in the river market on the Tuesday night beside the four rules, and **Tamsin read the whole sheet out loud in the market eleven times on the Wednesday** because the rules do not work if you only know them in your own language. The group is one interpreter wide, and the interpreter is the channel. What made it dangerous is that a genuine old document puts six things that came out of a hole into circulation, so new gossip sounded like a roll, and a count in Saltwake is never a list of people — it is a thing you do *to* people.
- **Adrian's vocabulary, and the sentence that is his.** In Chapter 19 the voice, out of the black line in the chapel stone, answers a question nobody asked aloud, in Adrian's own words, using **a chair is a system that has stopped asking whether it is needed** — a line he wrote at four in the morning in `ACOA_PERM_LEDGER_LOCAL_ONLY`, underneath a joke underneath a rule, and then **deleted the whole section of**. So it is not on the laptop, and there is no file. Either the thing has been inside him since Tuesday, or something read a document that no longer exists. He wrote the second version out in a keeper's own words and had Nyla Ferrow chalk it on the chapel door under Vask's list. He tells **Mina first**, at half past six, before the other five, and Tamsin at seven because the voice came out of that room's threshold stone and the keeper is owed it by the hour.
- **A Veyran-year of certainty is a year of promises.** The bottom field's damage is not visible: the shoots went back under, the field will be replanted, the soil is the same. What is gone is the ability to **promise** — in the Reed Reach a household that cannot promise a gate-opening on a date does not keep its share of the water, and that is not poverty. The ditches are *level*, which is the damage: the guess has been taken out of the water, so the thing a promise is made of is gone.
- **The debt is an open account, not a fine.** It is still being spent — the effect is running four miles downstream in Corrow fourteen days after the tear was shut, with no man in the field at all. The season Vail entered on the third day, the morning after the second tide, was the smallest it will ever be. Adrian's own note: the field review is the day a woman decides how much of her life to put in the ground.
- **The first descent's cost, and the post.** Chapter 20 opens the ground under the keeper's terms and finds, at four feet, a **dressed wall laid on edge, three putlog holes in a line, a joint opened and looked at and closed again two hundred years ago and closed well** — the same line as the hairline seam in the floor above, which was its top. **It is dry.** Vask: in thirty-four years she has never lifted a stone from a bank and found it dry. It is the **edge** of something larger that runs **under the river bank, where nobody has ever dug**, and Renna Ord stops the dig by naming the price: a cofferdam, a manned pump, three more men, and the river's own bank. **The dig stops. The ground is boarded. The terms are entered. Nothing is identified** — Vask refuses, in front of a yard, to say what four feet of a thing is when she has got four feet of it and a bank, because a man with a quarter of a thing who says the whole of it is a man selling. The identification is reserved for Chapter 24.
- **The post, not a debt.** Nyla Ferrow names the price of keeping the ground open in Adrian's own case and refuses to name a man: a man whose mark is in the stone of that building is not a visitor to it, and a visitor can leave. So **for as long as the ground is open, the ground does not open without him — every beginning, he is there or it does not begin.** It is not money, it is not a fee, it is not useful, and there is no end of it in sight. He accepted it in the local language and Vask corrected the verb and let the rest stand.
- **A seat, defined.** Nell Bray, Chapter 11, in front of a hall: *a seat is a place to be angry at. It is not an office. There is no office. A seat has to be legible to a person who does not like you. Anything else is a chair.* The four witnesses exist so that when the thing goes wrong there are four people who have to be angry and not one, and if all four agree they have not been asked, they have agreed, and then the holder is decoration.
- **A crew is not a list.** Renna Ord: a crew is a man who has owed somebody a shift. You ask a man to come; if he comes you owe him; if you do not pay you do not get to ask him again for nine days. The order is not written down, it is *known*, and it is older than the writing. Ovin Sarr: an inspection is not a form, it is four people turning up in the wrong weather.
- **Adrian stops looking for the field, and it is dramatised rather than recalled.** In Chapter 18, on the Reach road on the Thursday, he looks for the pale field and there is nothing, and he begins to understand that the messages come out of places. In Chapter 19, on the Friday night in the Salt Weavers' chapel two floors above the market, **Jonah Pike uses the word *quest* out loud for a thing in a wall**, and in the middle of the sentence Adrian understands two things: that he has been waiting all day for a pale field to tell him what to do next, and that he is the only one in the room who knows what the word is supposed to cost. He says *do not use that word in this room again* and writes the date down. Chapter 20 looks back on that night and not on any other.

### The voice, and the one rule about it

**The voice has one home and it is not the gate.** It comes out of the **black line in the chapel stone** — the raised pale threshold slab at the foot of the arch, four feet from the hairline seam in the floor — and it has never once come out of the arch, the floor, the outer channel wall, or the main breach. In Chapter 15 it repeats Nyla Ferrow's fifth word a half-second after she says it, which means it is standing *in* the binding. In Chapter 19 it says a sentence in English out of the black line. The working behind it is being done by a man in the **lock join** below. **The chapel stone is the channel. The lock join is where the work is.**

### Injuries

- Nobody dies. **Owen Park's scalp is an *unclosed* wound** — nine days of clean skin over it and the thing underneath going nowhere — and Hesper Saye says the word for it out loud in Chapter 14. It will not close and it is not his Chapter 25 shoulder. **His shoulder is untouched and is reserved for Chapter 25.**
- **Dante Kuo's right forearm** is reduced by Hesper Saye on the Thursday of Chapter 14, day 10, three days late, with the arm held against his chest. Saye's rule: the shoulder has been doing the arm's work for three days without being asked, and in three weeks he will lift a sack and something will go that is neither the arm nor the sack. Eleven days of care and then he stops being careful, which is **day 21**; the thing that goes is three weeks after the reduction. **Neither has happened yet.**
- Adrian: the cut hand from the Chapter 2 iron spike, a headache behind the left eye whenever he uses the reading, and **a nosebleed on the ladder in Chapter 15**, four minutes into a hole, the first time the Chapter 4 cost has returned.

### What Adrian holds at the end of the batch

**Stage 1, Witness, unchanged.** He can perceive the nearest join-line and understand one direct Veyran promise at a time. He **cannot** perform a working, open a threshold, or hold a place. He is **Unbound** and has now refused the Wayfinder profile twice in rooms with witnesses, the second time **on obligation** — an open-ended duty with a shape would be the one that pays, and his is not. He holds **no workway, no return mark, and no route.** The route is still empty and there is no mark on any living person in this world that would take one home. The pale fragment-mark is in the seam and the palm keeps a thin trace; the **thin black line underneath is separate, has not moved since the second morning, and is now seventeen days old.**
