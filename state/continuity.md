## THE THREE STATES THAT CHANGED ON DAYS 981 TO 990, AND WHAT EACH OF THEM COSTS A LATER WRITER

**A state changing is not a thing being answered, and none of these three is closed by any later chapter.**

**1. THERE IS A SHEET ON THAT BENCH, AND IT IS NOT ONE OF THE TWO BOOKS.** It was not there on 980. On 981 the keeper took a
pencil out of the pocket of her dress, squared it with her own hand, and wrote three lines on it and no more: the day, the
person it was about, and the putting-to. **The three lines stand a hand's width in from the right edge of that sheet, because
Adrian Vale's two hands were on the wood at the left of it from the fourth hour and she did not ask him to move them and he
did not move them.** The sheet lies on the wood of that bench at the far end of that room, a hand's width from the register,
with the two books open beside it. **The space below the third of those lines is bare and it stayed bare through day 990, and
it is the only place in this volume on which anything is written, on any of the fifty days.** It is not a notice, it is not a
form, it is not a document, nothing is written out on it for anybody to fetch, and **the fourth hundred yard of that market,
the eleven miles of flats and the about nine hundred paces are the plan's own distances and nothing else has been written on
anything in this volume since the flood came.** A chapter that stages that room after 981 stages the sheet on that bench; a
chapter that puts a second entry on it has changed the world and its record must say so.

**2. THERE IS A BLOCK OF WRITING ON THE WALL ALONG THE FAR SIDE OF THAT ROOM, AND NOBODY HAS SAID A WORD ABOUT IT.** It stood
by itself on 983 from about the height of a person's chest to about the height of a person's shoulder, written in one piece
without a break in it and without a line ruled above or under it and with no head on it, and there was no mark on the face
of it or on any other part of it, and the plaster either side of it was the colour it had been for eleven days. **It was not
there at the light on that morning and nothing whatever was made of it at any hour of any day of days 983 to 990 that are on
disk, and no mouth in that room said it was right and no mouth said it was wrong.** The woman of about fifty-two went across
that room at about the seventh hour and stood in front of it alone for about four minutes, with the rest of that room at the
other end of the floor and none of it coming over to the wall, and came away and stood where she had been standing. **She was
the only person on any of these ten days who went near it, and no hand touched it at any point of those four minutes or at
any hour after them.**

**3. THE DRY CLOTH IS ON THE HEAD OF THE SECOND BOOK'S LEAF.** At about the tenth hour on 990 the keeper came back up that
stair and wanted to lift the topmost page of her own leaf at its outer corner, and Adrian Vale's own shoulder was in the way of
the wood of that bench where her own hand wanted to go. She waited about as long as it takes to read a thing twice, then
reached across him with her own left hand, took the dry cloth off the head of her own leaf, and laid it along the head of the
second book's leaf and squared it against the crack in the column of that leaf with her own thumbnail. **It was on that leaf
and not on the slab at the going of the light, and the slab lay drawn back off the leaf under it in the place it lies in.**
A chapter that stages that room after 990 stages the cloth on the second book's leaf until somebody picks it up. **The second
book is a slate and not a document, and no hand has written on it on any day of days 971 to 990.**

## THE STATES THAT CHANGED ON DAYS 961 TO 970 AND ON DAYS 971 TO 980 ARE STILL WHERE THOSE TWO SETS OF DAYS PUT THEM

**The woman of about fifty-two is at the far end of that bench and not against her own far wall.** On 971 at about twenty
minutes past the seventh hour she came off that wall without a word to anybody and went the length of that room and stood at
the far end of that bench, past the second book, with her own shoulder to the wood behind the far end of it and the window at
her back. **She did not go back to that wall on any of the nineteen days from 972 to 990 and a chapter that stages that wall
after 971 stages it with her at the bench.** Her stool has not moved and still stands a foot further out from that wall than
it did before she carried it there, and the clear floor between the stool and the wood of the wall is about the width of a hand
the whole length of it, with the grit in the joints of the flags inside that strip standing up where nothing has walked on it
at any point of that flood. **On 980 she was in the market row and not in that room, and on 988 she went out at the middle of
the afternoon and along that row to the store's end of it and stood there and then went on out past the store's end, and the
stool stood where she had left it.** The man of about thirty-four and the man of about twenty-seven carry an exchange about a
hoop made on 975. The stool a foot further out with a clean square on the flags under where its near leg was. The gap at the
head of the keeper's leaf closed and the dry cloth with nowhere to go. The empty second hole in the outside wall soft a whole
finger's width from the hole all the way round, and now soft a whole finger's width from the hole all the way round after the
rain of the night of 974 stood in it and went down over an hour of afternoon sun. A clean arc about the width of a hand on
that gate's hinge, with the gate standing open.

## WHERE THE STANDING OBJECTS STAND AT DAY 990

1. **THE BENCH IN THAT ROOM. Moved on 953, about a foot further along toward the wall, and not put back.** Under the place it
   came off there are four squares of clean flags about the size of a hand and the grit in the joints inside them is pressed
   flat and has not come back up. On 975 the dry cloth was carried off the head of the keeper's leaf and laid on the bare wood
   of that bench for about half the morning and put back, and the wood under the place where it lay stands a shade paler than
   the wood either side of it along the whole length of the cloth and about a finger's width wide. **The two books went with
   the bench and are still open side by side on it, and the sheet lies beside them.** The keeper went at the near end of that
   bench on 982 and at the far end of it on every other morning.
2. **THE WEAR IN THE MIDDLE OF THE NINTH STEP FROM THE TOP OF THAT STAIR. Opened on 959.** The stone shows about the size of a
   hand and a shade paler than the stone either side of it, and the flakes are in a heap at the foot of that step about a
   foot to one side of the middle of it against the riser, and no boot goes near that heap. Nobody fetched a heat for it,
   nobody broke anything at it and nobody measured it.
3. **THE TROUGH AT THE TOP OF THAT MARKET STEP, ITS CRACK AND ITS ICE.** The thread of water out of the crack in its near end
   still crosses the flags. **The ice is still whole, about the depth of a thumb, and no heat was fetched on any day of days
   971 to 990 and nobody offered.** On 985 the man of about twenty-seven told the tradesman that the crack had gone a hand's
   width further along since the water came, and the tradesman said he had not looked at it and did not intend to.
4. **THE CHAIR AT THE FAR SIDE OF THAT BENCH AND THE SHEET OF ROAD ON IT.** Moved against the side wall on 953 and standing
   there with the fold along the middle. **No hand lifts the new rope over the back of it**, and **no chapter of days 971 to
   990 names that chair at all**, because nothing about it changed and the standing-object rule is that a named object must be
   named with what is different about it.
5. **THE BARE PIECE OF BOARD UNDER THE DATE. UNCHANGED ON EVERY DAY TO 990.** One mark, thick at the top and thin at the
   bottom, and **nothing went under that date and nothing scraped, washed out or altered it.** The chalk of the day crosses the
   joint between the two planks the outside face of that door is made of and stands broken along it, and about a finger's
   width of that writing is lost down in the groove of the joint, with the finest and palest of that chalk lying in the
   bottom of the groove in a line about the length of a hand. The mark stands about two fingers in from the near edge of its
   own board, clear of the break. **Nobody asked anybody anything about it on any day to 990, the hand that put it there is
   not named on any of them, and it is not a day of the Bare Month.**
6. **THE LINES IN THE GRIT AT THE STORE'S END OF THAT ROW.** Made again on 952 and again on 966, made none at all on 978, and
   **made none on any day of days 981 to 990, and the woman of about sixty-nine with her tin is in one of those ten days
   only, on 981, going up that stair with the tin under her arm and the stick in her own hand.** Not counted by anybody on any
   of them. Nobody asked her what they are for on any day to 990 and she did not offer it.
7. **THE BOARD ON THE OUTSIDE WALL AT THE FAR END OF THAT MARKET, ITS ONE NAIL, AND THE EMPTY SECOND HOLE.** It has drawn
   another fraction out at the head of its nail and has taken a line of shadow about the width of a card with it. **On 974
   the rain of the night stood full in that hole and went down over the better part of an hour of afternoon sun without running
   out anywhere, and the wood around the lip of it is now soft a whole finger's width from the hole all the way round. No
   second nail went into that wall on any day to 990 and nothing was written on that board.** There is pale dust in the gap
   behind the bottom edge of that board about the depth of a grain of rice, along the whole length of it.
8. **THE SECOND BOOK AND THE REGISTER, THE CRACK IN THE COLUMN, THE SIX STROKES, AND THE TWO ACCOUNTS OF ONE DAY. UNCHANGED.**
   **No entry of any kind was made in either book on any day to 980, and on days 981 to 990 the one thing written anywhere in
   this volume is the three lines on the sheet, and no seventh stroke went into any column of either book on any day of them,
   and no leaf of either book was picked up, turned over, closed or written on — with one thing standing, and it is that on
   990 the keeper lifted the topmost page of her own leaf at its outer corner and laid the dry cloth on the head of the
   second book's leaf.** The filled crack has taken the colour of the stone round it and stands a hair proud, so the last of
   the light catches along its whole length. **The two accounts of one day were never compared and no mouth in that room said
   which of them is the one that happened.**
9. **THE SATCHEL, THE TIN, THE TALLY-BOARD, THE ROPE, THE FENCE, THE GATE, THE STOOL, THE SILL AND ITS TWO SHAPES, THE
   TWO-FINGER SPACE, THE TRADESMAN'S NINETEEN RATES, THE BUCKET, THE BOX OF CHALK, THE TRESTLE FOOT.** **All unchanged on
   every day to 990, and the fence of sixteen willow posts and eleven withies is not measured and no post of it moves.** The

   are still undated and no chapter of days 971 to 990 dated either of them or named the hand that put either down, and Adrian

   stands up on its edge about the depth of two fingers, on the side away from the trestle, with the pale damp packed earth of

10. **THE CENSUS AT THE FOOT OF THAT STAIR.** Printed with the word *people* inside it, **never reduced, never given to
    anybody, never used as a count of anybody, and never counted in any room.** It is printed in **eleven** different

11. **THE HEADCOUNT OF THAT ROOM.** **Not on any day to 990, in any mouth and in no narration, and no mouth in that room

## THE TWO RULES OF THIS VOLUME THAT THE FIRST BATCH BROKE AND THAT HOLD, AND WHICH A WRITER WHO READS ONLY THE PLAN WILL NOT SEE

**§17.5 IS BINDING AND THE MEASURED COUNT BEHIND IT IS ZERO.** A card may not make an attribution restate a person's whole
handle every time that person speaks. The second handles this volume has and may use are **the keeper, the tradesman, the
stallholder, the carrier, and the man of about thirty-eight**, and each is beside the age and the trade on first appearance in
a chapter and never instead of it. **Full-descriptor restatements across days 981 to 990: 0, and no person speaks twice in any
chapter of these ten.**

**A SCENE'S STATE IS PAST TENSE AND A GENERAL TRUTH IS NOT, AND THE LINE IS PUBLISHED SO IT CAN BE HELD.** A verb describing
the state of a specific located object on that morning is in the past. A verb describing how a material behaves stays in the
present. **Days 981 to 990 were written in that tense and converted nothing after the fact, and the gnomic present was not
touched once.** The whole-column and whole-room statements are of the second kind and are not faults.

## THE GEOMETRY OF THAT STAIR, WHICH IS SETTLED GEOMETRY AND NOT A FIGURE ANYBODY MAY RECOUNT

**THAT STAIR HAS ELEVEN STEPS IN IT. The ninth step down from the top is neither the bottom step nor the top step, and there
are two steps under it before the flags.** It follows from three things already on disk: the plan puts **the ninth step down
from the top of that stair** as the place Adrian Vale's hands are on on day 959 at §4.1 and as a card in two separate days;
**the top step of that stair, which is not the bottom step**, where the bar lies out of its sockets, is §5; and 0953 had a
man at the ninth step get up and **come the last two steps**. **Measured across days 981 to 990, `nine steps` stands at 0, and
no person in those ten chapters stands on a step that does not exist.** The **ninth step from the top** carries the single
worn place in the middle of it. The **bottom step**, the eleventh and the last, is where the strip of ground begins. The
**top step**, where the bar lies and the trough stands, is nine risers above the ninth and is not near it.
