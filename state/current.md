## Canon after Chapter 0020

Active threats: The **main breach** in the Lock of Salt gate is open, watched, and **three inches wider than it was the night Adrian woke on the stones**. It has been measured twenty nights running, at one in the morning, at three chalked marks in the pale edge of the join in the gate: three inches in **four steps of three-quarters of an inch**, and a crack does not step. The four steps fall on the four days the lock did **unusual** work and not on any tide or flood. Adrian's conclusion, which nobody can act on: **the ordinary maintenance of the lock is the mechanism**, and the only lever is to stop working the lock, which drowns eleven villages in the next flood. The **black line in the chapel's threshold stone** is unhealed and will stay that way for the rest of the volume. The **thin black line in the heel of Adrian's palm** is three weeks old, has not moved, and is not a return mark. The **chapel-floor hairline seam** is dry and unmoved — and Chapter 20 establishes it is the *top* of a nine-foot dressed joint four feet below, which was opened, looked at, and closed again two hundred years ago. The **void** under the north flagstones is a straight-sided room's worth of nothing, and it continues under the river bank, where nobody has ever dug, and the bank is the river's own. A voice using the old form of the maintenance oath **still speaks only out of the black line in the chapel stone**; in Chapter 15 it repeats the keeper's fifth word half a second after Nyla Ferrow says it, which means it is standing inside the binding. The bottom field has lost a season and is **still losing**, and the loss is now legible as a level ditch four miles away in Corrow with nobody standing in it. Ivenn Marrow's altered prompt is live in the interface, nobody knows he is editing it, and he has told a room a three-hundred-and-ten-year-old customs document is a fraud when it is genuine and contains no name.

Active promises: The Concord hearing on whether a first license may exist in Saltwake is **dated — three days after the next tide, and the next tide is nine days out.** The bottom-field review is the **last day of the salt month**, six days out, and it is the day a woman in Corrow decides how much of her life to put into the ground, the day Adrian answers three public questions about an account that is still growing, and the day Marrow's written workway offer expires. The Chapter 10 route is recorded, costed, inspected, formally unrecognised, and **cannot be used**: no return mark exists for anyone. The nine households of **Nine Elms have now given their refusal as a standing commitment**, may withdraw it on any day, and the instrument is therefore dead until a receiving community accepts the whole of the area. Adrian holds an enforceable public debt for the bottom field, and it is an **open account, not a fine**. Owen's unverified statement about a relic-shaped light is in the book. A Crown letter of refusal with Adrian's name on it is in a file in Aurel, and the Concord's roll of strangers records him as **Unbound**. The group has a rota, a ledger with a new column, a statement on a market board, and a **post**: for as long as the ground under the chapel is open, the ground does not open without him.
