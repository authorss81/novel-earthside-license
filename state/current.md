## THE BATCH THAT WROTE CHAPTERS 0981 TO 0990, DAYS 981 TO 990

**WHAT THE TEN DAYS ARE.** Days 981 to 990, Tuesday to Thursday, Bare-Month ordinals 666 to 675, **derived from two figures
and two figures only — day one is a Tuesday and the ordinal is the day less three hundred and fifteen.** Chapter equals day on
all ten and weekday is correct on all ten. Pressures as the plan's own column: cost, decision, discovery, recovery,
discovery, physical, character, recovery, cost, character. **Adrian Vale is in 0981, in 0985 and in 0990 and in no other of
the ten: he obtains the volume's one obtained thing on 985 and nothing on the other two.** Ten frames out of eighteen, all ten
distinct, nine of them frames the three batches behind also drew and all nine are named in
`reviews/volume-20/batch-0004.md` §5 because §17.20 requires a repeat to be named rather than hidden, **and frame 12 on 0989
is the last frame in the whole volume that had never been drawn.**

**WHAT IS NEW IN THE WORLD AS A RESULT OF THESE TEN DAYS, AND IT IS THREE THINGS, TWO OF THEM INSTRUMENTS AND ONE A CLOTH.
NONE OF THE THREE IS A THREAD ANSWERED.** **1. THERE IS A SHEET ON THAT BENCH.** It was not there on 980. From 981 it lies on
the wood of that bench at the far end of that room, a hand's width from the register, with three lines on it a hand's width in
from its right edge — the day, the person it is about, and the putting-to itself — **and the space below those three lines is
bare, and it is the only thing written on any surface on any of the fifty days of this volume.** It is not one of the two
books, not a notice, not a form, and nothing is written out for anybody to fetch. **2. THERE IS A BLOCK OF WRITING ON THE WALL
ALONG THE FAR SIDE OF THAT ROOM**, standing by itself from about the height of a person's chest to about the height of a
person's shoulder, in one piece without a break in it and with no head on it, and it was not there at the light on 983 and
nobody has ever said a word about it. **3. THE DRY CLOTH IS ON THE HEAD OF THE SECOND BOOK'S LEAF**, laid there on 990 at about
the tenth hour because Adrian Vale's shoulder was in the way of the keeper's hand, and still there at the going of the light.

**AND THE THREE THINGS THAT WENT OUT OF THAT ROOM AND CANNOT BE GOT BACK.** On 981 the keeper put one thing on a page before
she put it to anybody and the woman of about sixty-nine with her tin read it before she answered, **and the chapter prints
the putting-to in full and prints nothing of the answer, and the answer is on no surface in this city.** On 982 the keeper said
out loud once that the page carries the putting-to, the person and the day and carries nothing back. On 985 the man who came up
that stair said one word out loud once in that room in daylight, **nobody asked him what it meant, nobody said it back to him,
nobody improved on it, and it is not explained in that chapter or in any other chapter of this volume.**

figures and no seventh, and **no seventh stroke went into any column of either book on any of these ten days, and no leaf of
either book was picked up, turned over, closed or written on — with one thing standing, and it is a thing a later chapter may
undo: on 990 the keeper lifts the topmost page of her own leaf at its outer corner, as she has done every morning of that
flood, and lays the dry cloth on the head of the second book's leaf.** Two books on one bench holding two accounts of one
day, neither compared. The bar on the top step out of its two sockets, out of the weather, not put back, on all ten mornings,
and **no chapter of these ten counts that stair from the top step down to the flags, and no person stands on a step that does
not exist.** The bare piece of board under the date carried its one mark on all ten and nothing went under that date and

## THE THREE THINGS THE FIX PASSES ON THE TWO BATCHES BEHIND FOUND, AND THAT STILL HOLD

1. **THAT STAIR HAS ELEVEN STEPS IN IT AND IS NEVER COUNTED END TO END.** The ninth step down from the top is neither the
   bottom step nor the top step, and there are two steps under it before the flags. The bar and the trough are nine risers
   above the ninth. **A writer must not put a number on that stair at all, and must not put a person on a step that does not
   exist.** Measured across days 981 to 990: **`nine steps` at 0.**
2. **A SCENE'S STATE IS PAST TENSE AND A GENERAL TRUTH IS NOT.** A verb describing the state of a specific located object on
   that morning goes to the past. A verb describing how a material behaves, how a boot goes on a wet step, what a hole does
   after a nail has been in it a long time, or how a tin came to have its shape, stays in the present. The gnomic present is
   not a fault in this manuscript. **Days 981 to 990 were written in that tense and converted nothing after the fact.**
3. **AN ATTRIBUTION DOES NOT RESTATE A PERSON'S WHOLE HANDLE EVERY TIME THAT PERSON SPEAKS.** The second handles this volume
   has and may use are **the keeper, the tradesman, the stallholder, the carrier, and the man of about thirty-eight**, and She
   and He where a person has been established in the chapter. **Measured full-descriptor restatements on days 981 to 990: 0,
   and no person speaks twice in any chapter of these ten.**

**AND TWO HOUSE FIGURES THAT ARE NOT DEFECTS AND THAT NO REVIEW SHOULD RE-RAISE AS NEW.** True contractions stand at zero in
this batch and at zero on days 951 to 960, 961 to 970 and 971 to 980, all four measured with the same instrument in the same
run, so the absence is the register of the book; and *his own* stands at **140 in 13,896 words here against 89 in 13,059 on

## THE ONE PLACE TWO CLAUSES OF THE PLAN HAD TO BE READ TOGETHER, AND THE READING IS CARRIED HERE

**§6.5 says every question put to anybody in that room is written down before it is asked. §6.4's own table says 987 and 989
are written down before they are asked — no. §17.20's frame sixteen, which the card gives to 990, is *a page with one thing
on it and the space below that thing left empty*. Those cannot all be obeyed at once.** The reading this batch took is that
§6.4's per-day column governs the two days it names, so on 987 the asking happened in the market row and not in that room,
and on 989 the woman of about fifty-two's own hand went to the pocket of her own dress after naming her cost, came back
out with nothing in it and went back to the side of it — **and the consequence is rendered as a room that announced a rule on
975 and does not keep it, which is what this manuscript does.** **The consequence for a later writer is on the sheet: it
carries one entry and the space under that entry is bare, and if a later chapter writes a second entry on it, that chapter has
changed the world and its record must say so.** The two cases an outside reader could argue the other way, and the correction that belongs
to a phase which owns the plan, are at `reviews/volume-20/batch-0004.md` §3.2.
