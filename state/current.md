## THE STORY OF VOLUME 15, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND IT COMES FIRST BECAUSE THE COUNTS COME SECOND

A room over a market has nine people in it and a wall along its far side with nothing on it. Nine
steps lead up to that room from a strip of ground at the foot of them, and about four people a day walk
on that strip, and nobody in this city has ever asked any of them what they were doing. Over fifty days
that room works out what it is being asked on the morning it is asked it, decides, and puts the decision
on nobody's page: the people who walk on that row are not to be asked what is said in that room. A
man rules out the row out loud and pays for it in his own mouth eight days later in about eight words
and is thanked by nobody. A woman keeps a book with five figures in one column and one word near the
head of a column beside them that is not in her hand, and a second slate stands behind that book on the
same shelf with a day cut across its head and nothing under it, and it is never picked up and never
turned over. On the resolution day she comes round the front of that building to the row instead of
going in at the front and walks on the instrument herself, and nobody finds out and nobody is told. And
a man asks another man at the foot of that stair what a person is for, when the only thing anybody is
going to use them for is to have gone past, and it is not answered.

**THE FINDING, AT THE PLAN'S §12, AND IT IS ABOUT A CLASS OF PERSONS AND NOT ABOUT A PERSON: a row is
not a witness, and the difference between a row nobody has ever asked and a row a room has decided not
to ask is on neither of them.** A place that has been decided about is a place that cannot be shown,
and nothing is lost that anybody had. A row that has been ruled out of being asked is a set of about
four people a day who can no longer be asked whether they were ever there, and something is lost that
somebody had, which is the fact that they were.

**WHAT IS PROVED, AND WHAT IS NOT. It is NOT proved by the fifty that the only person who could have
said what the row was for is the woman who keeps the book. What is proved, on day 740, is that she has
never been on it.** No chapter of this volume says or implies that she knew what the row was for, or
that she knew it because she kept a book, or that anybody knew. The row's own subject is that nobody
knew.

## WHAT IS ON THE TABLES AND THE SHELVES AT THE END OF DAY 750, AND EVERY ITEM HOLDS

- **The register.** Five figures down the middle of one page, a day against each, none of the five
  struck, no sixth entered and none taken out. One word near the head of the column on the right of
  that page and nothing anywhere else on it, not in the keeper's hand, put there in another hand on a
  day that is not one of this volume's fifty. It has never been printed in any chapter of this volume
  and no mouth in this city defines it.
- **The second slate.** Behind the register, on the same shelf, not on a shelf of its own. A day cut
  across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Never picked up, never turned over,
  nothing written on it. No hand but the keeper's ever went near that shelf.
- **The first one-place form.** On its own shelf at the height of a person's shoulder. A heading, one
  ruled place, a figure that is not printed here. Never returned, never withdrawn, never written in,
  never read out.
- **The store's board on the outside wall.** Three sets of figures of this city's own, none rubbed
  out, not one carrying a line of words under it. Washed once at the fourth hour on day 750 and not
  twice. The wall above its top edge is a different thing and was washed once in the whole of the
  flood and by no second person.
- **The door at the foot of that stair.** A date in chalk, and under that date a bare piece of board
  about as wide as a hand. It means nothing at all on all fifty days and it is not this volume's spend.
- **The row.** A strip of ground from the bottom step to wherever the paving gives out. About four
  people a day. Never measured, never cleared, never paved over, never widened, never narrowed.
- **The fence.** Sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound on with the same cord, along the top of
  a shut road out past the last named house. Never measured, no post moved, no withy taken off.
- **The new rope.** Over the back of a chair in a house out past the last named house, new, every
  morning of the flood. Never used, never cut, never taken up, never lifted.
- **The satchel with a flap.** One thing in it that its owner knows what is in. Never opened, and the
  reason he opens nothing he carries is not given on any page.
- **The wharf board.** Face up on a stack of boards under two lines of grey grit, a clean piece at the
  near edge. No third line of grit. Not turned face down again at any hour, and cannot be.
- **The stallkeeper's chalk.** In his inside breast pocket at every hour of all fifty days including
  day 750. It has not been out of that pocket since the six hundred and seventy-eighth day.
- **The stool.** Carried out of a room by the woman of about fifty-two and left where anybody could
  sit on it.
