## THE STORY OF VOLUME 15, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND IT COMES FIRST BECAUSE THE COUNTS COME SECOND

A room over a market has nine people in it and a wall along its far side with nothing on it. Nine
steps lead up to that room from a strip of ground at the foot of them, and about four people a day walk
on that strip, and nobody in this city has ever asked any of them what they were doing. Over fifty days
that room works out what it is being asked on the morning it is asked it, decides, and puts the decision
on nobody's page: the people who walk on that row are not to be asked what is said in that room. A
man rules out the row out loud and pays for it in his own mouth eight days later in about eight words
and is thanked by nobody. A woman keeps a book with five figures in one column and one word near the
head of a column beside them that is not in her hand, and a second slate stands behind that book on the
same shelf with a day cut across its head and nothing under it, and it is never picked up and never
turned over. On the resolution day she comes round the front of that building to the row instead of
going in at the front and walks on the instrument herself, and nobody finds out and nobody is told. And
a man asks another man at the foot of that stair what a person is for, when the only thing anybody is
going to use them for is to have gone past, and it is not answered.

**THE FINDING, AT THE PLAN'S §12, AND IT IS ABOUT A CLASS OF PERSONS AND NOT ABOUT A PERSON: a row is
not a witness, and the difference between a row nobody has ever asked and a row a room has decided not
to ask is on neither of them.** A place that has been decided about is a place that cannot be shown,
and nothing is lost that anybody had. A row that has been ruled out of being asked is a set of about
four people a day who can no longer be asked whether they were ever there, and something is lost that
somebody had, which is the fact that they were.

**WHAT IS PROVED, AND WHAT IS NOT. It is NOT proved by the fifty that the only person who could have
said what the row was for is the woman who keeps the book. What is proved, on day 740, is that she has
never been on it.** No chapter of this volume says or implies that she knew what the row was for, or
that she knew it because she kept a book, or that anybody knew. The row's own subject is that nobody
knew.
