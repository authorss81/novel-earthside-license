## HOW THE PROSE IN THIS VOLUME HAS BEEN FAILING, WITH THE FIGURES BOTH SIDES

**These are the four things the review of the batch before yours found, and they are all the same habit wearing
different clothes. Each figure below was measured on `chapters/volume-17/chapter-0831.md` to `chapter-0840.md` as
they stood after the repair, and the comparison is Volume 16 whole and the batch that came before this one.**

1. **The word *that* was carrying the prose.** The batch ran 45.0 uses per 1,000 words before the repair, against
   30.6 for Volume 16 whole, and the repair brought it to 22.0. *That store door* and *that yard* and *the far end
   of that bench* were standing in for nouns the sentence already had. **Where a demonstrative is doing the work of
   a noun, cut it or give the noun. Where it is pointing at something the reader cannot see yet, keep it.**
2. **People were being re-introduced instead of referred to.** §17.9 requires every person who carries the volume
   to have a second handle beside the age and the trade, in every chapter that person is in, and it does not ask
   for the handle to be repeated. **Give each person the full handle from §7.2 on their first appearance in a
   chapter, and let them be *he* or *she* for the rest of it.** The census handle is the exception and is a
   different rule: *the woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress* is a fixed string and
   stands word for word wherever it stands, and what you vary is the verb in front of it and the clause behind it.
3. **Dialogue went missing, and then the room's answer to a man's words became a list.** Two chapters of the last
   batch had no marked speech at all, and the climax answered a man's confession with four sentences beginning
   *Nobody*. **Every chapter of yours needs speech, three paragraphs or more, and never more than four consecutive
   speech paragraphs between the same two people. And a moment somebody says something that costs him has to be
   answered with what hands and bodies do, not with a roll-call of what nobody did.** The motif stays; the
   catalogue goes.
4. **Ten chapters on one template read as one chapter ten times.** The same opening move, the same date sentence in
   the same position, exactly two separators in every file, and a closing still-life keyed to a clock hour. **Vary
   the separators, vary where the day and the hour sit, and let at least two of your ten close on something that
   arrives, dries, opens, is found out, is put down or is taken away rather than on an object standing still.**
   §17.17 caps closings that state nothing changed at two in any ten, **days 831 to 840 counted none, so your ten
   may carry up to two, and not one of days 841 to 844 may close that way and 0850 may not either.** The reading
   and the per-chapter rulings are at `state/batch-summaries/volume-17-batch-0004.md`. §17.18 also puts six shapes
   off limits for a closing, and your ten closings must be ten different constructions, read side by side by you
   before you finish.

**Two instruments, and both are cheap.** Run the pair-against-pair run over your ten files before you finish, and
against the volume's earlier thirty as well as inside your own ten, and reword what it finds: **the longest run of
consecutive words any two of days 831 to 840 share is eight words and it is the census handle, down from sixteen
before the repair.** The batch before that one found seven sentences that were verbatim copies of sentences already
on disk in closed batches of this volume, and every one of them came from a sentence that read well on its own and
from a writer who had not run the instrument. **And publish every figure with its reading and scope on the same
line, in both directions: an instrument that returns a number where you expected a fault is the same failure as one
that returns nothing where you expected a number.**
