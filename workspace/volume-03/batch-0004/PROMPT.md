## The climax, in the plan's own words, and what it costs

**Adrian refuses to become a Crown-appointed interpreter with unlimited authority.** That is `outline/series.md` and it is binding, and it is the volume's last movement, and it happens in a room, in daylight, in the local language, with a date on it, in front of about nine people and about two hundred and forty other people who can hear it.

**The refusal is not the climax's outcome. The compact is, and the refusal does not produce it.** A chapter that has Adrian refuse and then win is a chapter that has written a rescue. The shape that works is: **the compact is going to exist whatever he does, because the compact is a form and a form exists whether or not anybody in this city will stand in it, and the only question in the room is who will be in the places in it. He refuses the post. The form does not change. Somebody else reads it out, in the local language, in a room, in daylight, and that person is exposed, and the exposure is the cost of the page, and he is out of it.** The volume's mature relationship is exactly that: two people doing separate work with separate loyalties, and neither of them rescuing the other.

**What the refusal permanently costs him, and the batch must say all of it on the page:** he gives up the only thing he is good at as a way of being needed; he is outside an office and has no standing and no roster and no mark and no seat; the knowing does not come back and the route home does not come back and neither was ever going to; and **the answer to the eighth column's question, when it comes, will be read by somebody else or by nobody, and he will not be the instrument, and he chooses that in a room and nobody praises him for it.** A man with no mark, on no roster, outside every office, is the most findable-unfindable person in this world, and that is not a safety and he should know it.

**The one shape he may not be given:** a name, a title, a standing, a workway, a mark, a seat, a wage, a roster, a column, or any arrangement in which his reading is the price of somebody else's place. **A later offer that looks like the post and is not the post is a legitimate escalation, and it must be refused in the same breath and for the same reason, and the reason must be stated once and not improved on.**

## The resolution, in the plan's own words, and what it costs

**The bridge is recognised provisionally and a delegation is sent to the First Landing ruins.** Two things, and they are different things.

**The recognition is a recognition, not an opening.** **The Narrow Mark may not be opened by Adrian Vale in this volume at all:** Stage 2 requires a named local holder with a return mark, a receiving community that has agreed, a fresh inspection, two witnesses who are not of the crew, and the same route may not be opened twice in a row by him, and he holds no mark and has no workway and is on no roster. **A later chapter that opens the door casually undoes the last two volumes. A batch that wants the bridge recognised must recognise it in a room and nowhere else.**

**The delegation goes and is not resolved.** `bible/world.md` §4: roughly three centuries ago a fragment near the Saltwake lock opened a narrow crossing and a small number of Earthside people crossed without knowing they were entering a real world, and their stories became the Bright Pilgrims tradition. **The First Landing is a place and its ruins are a place and nothing about them is on the page in a hundred and thirty days. A batch may name where the delegation is going and who is in it and what it has been asked to find out, and it may not find out, and it may not turn the delegation into a rescue, and it may not make the First Landing a door, a monster, a court, or a place where anybody in this book was already wrong.** The delegation is a promise with people in it and the chapter that sends it should be honest about that.
