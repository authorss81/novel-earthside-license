## Where the story is

First light on the day after the second tide. Two hundred and eleven people have just been told, by a notary, that a shape exists in a wall and that nobody may use it. The **Narrow Mark** is in the ledger with a named holder, four witnesses, a reduced receiving area, a posted cost, and **incomplete recognition**. There is no return mark in it for anyone. The **main breach** in the gate is open, watched, and wider than it was on Tuesday. The **black line** in the chapel's threshold stone is unhealed. The bottom field has lost a season and the debt is reviewable on the last day of the salt month. The Concord has **opened the record** on whether a first license may exist and has given no date. The nine households of Nine Elms have refused receiving and may withdraw on any day. Ivenn Marrow's workway offer is still open, cheap, and pointed at Aurel. Owen's statement about a relic-shaped light is in the book marked unverified. A voice in the lock join is still spending the river and still knows Adrian's name.

Seven people. Adrian is a Witness with no workway, a duty he cannot clear, and a thin pale trace in his palm.

## Batch goal

Turn a public defeat into ordinary work. The hearing has to become a process, not a verdict; the cohort has to acquire local roles and pay for them; the Crown's first letters have to arrive and change the political weather; and the descent toward the chamber beneath the chapel has to begin as a job with a cost rather than a quest. Adrian must learn that holding a seat is not the same as running anything, and must spend the batch discovering that the interface rewards him for the version of events that is easiest to sell.

## Hard constraints

- **System messages: at most one per chapter.** A message is a pale field in Adrian's environment, in plain language, in obligations rather than mechanics. It never rewards good behaviour. Ivenn's prompts are the batch's designed danger: attractive, partly true, and missing a social cost. Do not let the narration repeat a prompt's claim as fact. Chapters where nothing needs a message should carry none.
- **Adrian never performs a working.** He reads and marks. Locals work.
- **Adrian stays Unbound.** Ivenn offers the Wayfinder workway again in this range and Adrian refuses it again, out loud, for the record, and says why the refusal is about obligation rather than about danger. He does not collect templates.
- **No crowd ever crosses.** One named person per opening, one opening at a time, a fresh inspection, a receiving community that can suspend. If no return mark exists, no one travels at all.
- **Nobody dies in this batch.** Owen's Chapter 38 death is the volume's only death and it must arrive intact and permanent. No respawn, no return, no inheritance of a mark.
- **Do not have anyone state that this is a game.** The premise half is still deferred. Adrian's own vocabulary may slip — status display, quest, respawn, tutorial, a chair — and the slip is the point, but the cohort and the narration must not assert it, and no Veyran may be called an NPC.
- **Keep the five lock sites apart.** Main breach, outer tear/maintenance seam, near gate, chapel-floor hairline seam, chapel stone. Nothing happens at two of them.
- **The voice has one home and it is not the gate.** Since Chapter 7 it has come *out of the black line in the chapel stone*, and its physical source is a man standing in the **lock join** below. The chapel stone is the channel; the lock join is where the working is being done. If a chapter needs the voice, it arrives in the chapel stone. It does not relocate to the main breach, and the main breach does not speak.
- **The 211 people, the ledger, and the field debt are real.** When a promise has a cost, put the cost in the book.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0011 — The Second Sitting
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the Lantern Concord hall, later the same morning.
- **Goal:** get the Chapter 10 instrument translated into things a person can actually do this week — an inspection rota, a cost schedule, a way for Nine Elms to withdraw — before the room empties and the work evaporates.
- **Resistance:** the notary who wrote the terms will not interpret them; the two hundred people want a decision and there is none; the first person to try to turn the pending instrument into a licence by ordinary administrative drift is not a villain, just tired and reasonable. Adrian's name in the book makes him a target for people who want to be angry at somebody specific.
- **Turn:** a working local explains, without any malice, what a *seat* is in this city — that a holder is a person other people can be angry at when a thing goes wrong, and that the whole point of four witnesses is that they are the ones who have to be angry. Adrian understands that the dignity of the role is that it is legible to a person who dislikes him.
- **Consequence:** he spends the morning doing nothing that feels like progress and everything that makes the instrument real, including writing the first inspection date himself. The first complaint about him enters the book on page one, from a woman whose lower field is under water.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The cost of the seat is made concrete: he now has a queue of people who want something from him.
- **Ending:** a name is read out at the back of the hall — an Earthside name, spoken by somebody in Saltwake who should not know it.

### Chapter 0012 — The First Use of the Record
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the market and the loft, the next day.
- **Goal:** find out how the leaked name got into Saltwake's hands before whoever said it can use it, without turning a public emergency into a private panic.
- **Resistance:** Priya wants it suppressed, because a name in a market is a name in a street — and she is the reason it is a name in a street. Jonah wants it run, because a frightened crowd that can see a threat is a crowd that can be organised. Adrian is caught between the two and is wrong about the timing.
- **Turn:** the name is not in Saltwake because of a leak. Adrian asked each arrival for their name in Chapter 5, Priya wrote all six down in Chapter 5, and in Chapter 6 that sheet went up on the loft wall above the river market beside the four rules, where any market woman could read it for nothing. The person who said the name at the back of the hall in Chapter 11 read it off a wall. What makes it dangerous is not the mouth. It is the customs clerk's report: Marrow really did find it weeks ago, it really is genuine, and it really does prove that names crossed here about three hundred and ten years ago and were written down at the time. A genuine old document makes new gossip sound like a roll. The report is a period description of a breach in a dead clerk's hand and contains no Earthside name, and Marrow knows that and let the room believe otherwise. So the leak is old information plus a new mouth, and the damage is already done before anyone in the room understands what has happened. Keep Marrow's complicity **plausible and unproven** in public: he is the only man in Saltwake with both reason and access, and he must remain publicly useful to the end of the volume. Do not let a public scene convict him.
- **Consequence:** a decision is made under pressure about what the group says publicly, and it is a bad one, entered by Mina, who overrides Adrian for the first time and is right. Adrian learns that the record has a tempo he does not control, and that the most dangerous document in Saltwake is the wall of his own loft.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The value of the public book is shown working against its holders.
- **Ending:** the group's own public statement, which Adrian did not write and cannot retract, is read back to him with one sentence he disagrees with in.

### Chapter 0013 — The Crown's Handwriting
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the Concord hall, midweek.
- **Goal:** answer a letter from Aurel that asks, in courteous language, who is answering for the bridge, and keep the answer from becoming a claim of ownership.
- **Resistance:** the letter is not a threat. It is a request for a form, and the form exists, and filling it in would register the crossing under a single authority. Solenne Dary is not on stage and does not need to be.
- **Turn:** the Concord's assessor of strangers explains exactly what a Crown registration would do to a legal instrument that is currently pending, and Adrian understands that the most dangerous document in the city is a piece of stationery he is being asked to complete because it is polite.
- **Consequence:** the answer is drafted, read aloud, and entered — and it is a refusal that costs the group something concrete, because the reply says no to the only authority with the power to protect them.
- **Power:** nothing gained; the interface offers nothing at all in this chapter, which is itself information.
- **Ending:** the letter's courier is a person, not an official, and has a question of her own that nobody thought to ask her.

### Chapter 0014 — Roles
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the loft and the market, over two days.
- **Goal:** let the six take on local work, because a group with no jobs in a city with a flood will be a group that gets told what to do.
- **Resistance:** each role costs something and each person is bad at the first version of it. Mina works with local healers and is re-taught by them. Dante organises supplies and discovers that local labour is a rota, not a chain of command. Priya begins a local record and has to be told, by Nora's absence and Priya's own judgement, what naming a vulnerable person costs. Noor audits food and finds the group was being given food by somebody with a claim on them. Jonah negotiates with the Brass Measure and gets a good price, which is the problem.
- **Turn:** Owen tries to secure the loft and is stopped by a nine-year-old's mother, calmly and in public, in a way that is not a fight and cannot be handled as one.
- **Consequence:** the group acquires roles, debts, and obligations that belong to nobody in particular, and Adrian has to say out loud that he is not the person who assigns them.
- **Power:** Adrian's borrowed route-reading is used by others for the first time and it is not enough for anybody.
- **Ending:** a local healer asks Adrian, in front of people, a question about consent that he cannot answer in a language he speaks.

### Chapter 0015 — What the Anchorstone Holds
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the yard under the lock chapel, at low water, daytime.
- **Goal:** find out what the displaced stone is and why the interface wants it moved before the next tide, using people who know the ground rather than a prompt.
- **Resistance:** the chapel keeper's ground is not available without her reason and a cost. Vask says the dig is possible and dangerous and will cost the chapel its floor. The old oath's fifth syllable is the same word the keeper's mother used. The interface offers something here, and it is not the same something.
- **Turn:** Tamsin, reading the boundary table aloud in the yard, establishes that the stone and the old river boundary are the same fact, and that Ivenn's route was true in every particular except the one that mattered.
- **Consequence:** the work is agreed, costed, and entered, and the chapel keeper names a price that has nothing to do with money.
- **Power:** Adrian's provisional reading is used to find the safest line and fails halfway, and he says so in public.
- **Ending:** the first descent is scheduled, and the first descent is not a rescue and not a door, and someone has written a list of what may not be taken out.

### Chapter 0016 — The Offer, Again
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** an evening in the loft chapel, with Marrow present and locals present.
- **Goal:** establish, once and for all, in a room with witnesses, exactly what a workway is and exactly what it would cost, so that the offer stops being attractive by being vague.
- **Resistance:** Marrow is at his most useful and most dangerous. He reads the omitted cost out loud, accurately, without being asked, and then argues that the cost is worth paying and that Adrian has no standing to refuse on behalf of six people. Adrian has no standing either. That is the argument.
- **Turn:** Adrian refuses the template for the second time and states the reason as obligation rather than risk, and the six are each asked separately, in their own words, and five of them say something Adrian would not have chosen.
- **Consequence:** Adrian is recorded as Unbound in a public document that a Crown clerk will one day be able to find, and Marrow's offer remains open in the ledger, in his own hand, with a date on it.
- **Power:** Adrian's refusal is a loss of capability and a gain of standing, in that order, and the chapter should not pretend otherwise.
- **Ending:** Marrow says the true thing about why he wants Adrian to take it, and it is not the reason Adrian assumed.

### Chapter 0017 — The First Dissent
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the hall, several days on.
- **Goal:** keep the Narrow Mark's inspection from becoming the ritual everyone expects it to be.
- **Resistance:** the first inspection draws a crowd that wants to watch a door, and a door that does nothing is a worse spectacle than no door. A local faction argues that the instrument is dangerous and should be scraped out of the wall. Nine Elms uses its refusal, as Tamsin predicted, to renegotiate. Jonah is the only one prepared to say publicly that the route is worth something.
- **Turn:** Adrian is asked by the Concord to state, on the record, what the Narrow Mark is for, and he has to answer a question about the return of people through it, and the honest answer is that he does not know whether the mark he gave up could have brought anyone home.
- **Consequence:** the first inspection passes on a technicality, the instrument survives, and Adrian has publicly admitted a limit he would have preferred to keep.
- **Power:** no gain. The loss is reputational and specific.
- **Ending:** the crowd at the inspection includes someone from the upper road who has come to look at the nineteen stones.

### Chapter 0018 — The Cost of the Field
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the lower field and the Reed Reach road, daytime, with a farmer who is not a named character yet.
- **Goal:** see what a season of a bottom field actually is, in the field, in front of the people it happened to.
- **Resistance:** the loss is not visible. Nothing is dead. The shoots went back under and the field will be replanted and the damage is a *year of certainty* in a place where certainty is the product. The farmer wants a number and a date and does not care whose fault it was. Adrian's notebook is the wrong instrument and he is told so.
- **Turn:** the same field is still losing pressure, quietly, and the working in the lock join is not finished, so the season is not the only cost — it is a first payment.
- **Consequence:** Adrian understands the debt is not a fine to be paid but a relationship to be kept, and the review date becomes a scene rather than a line in a ledger.
- **Power:** nothing gained; the cost of the place is made legible, which is not a power but is the only tool he has.
- **Ending:** someone has begun paying the debt in small ways that Adrian did not authorise, and it is not the group.

### Chapter 0019 — The Second Cohort Does Not Arrive
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the walk-round and the main breach, several nights later.
- **Goal:** establish that the main breach is still changing, without using a System message to tell him.
- **Resistance:** the group now has jobs, a rota, a ledger, and a functioning life, and a life is a terrible thing to interrupt. Nobody believes anything will happen tonight. The measurement Adrian can make is bad in a way nobody can act on.
- **Turn:** the breach does not open, and does something worse: a voice comes out of the black line in the chapel stone — the same channel it has used since Chapter 7, and the gate itself does nothing at all tonight and is only being measured — and it answers a question nobody asked aloud, in Adrian's own words, using a sentence from the design ledger he never showed anyone. Marrow is not present. Nobody admits to having done it.
- **Consequence:** Adrian has to decide whether to tell the group that the thing can use his vocabulary, and he tells Mina first, before the others, which is a choice about who he trusts with fear and is a real change in how the group works.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The uncertainty becomes a shared cost.
- **Ending:** the sentence the voice used is one Adrian abandoned in a document at Morrowglass, and the question it opens is who else on that side of the wall was reading his files.

### Chapter 0020 — The Road Down
- **POV:** Adrian Vale.
- **Location/time:** the yard under the lock chapel, the next morning, at low water, at the top of the dig.
- **Goal:** open the ground the chapel keeper has agreed to open, under the terms she set, with the list of what may not be taken out posted where the diggers can see it.
- **Resistance:** the descent is ordinary, expensive work with a real crew on a real rota, and it goes wrong in small ways a hundred times before it goes wrong in one large way. Adrian cannot read the join under the chapel floor and has to be told, by Vask and by the keeper, where it is safe to stand. The cost named by the keeper is a labour, not a payment, and it is a labour that will be needed for months.
- **Turn:** at the bottom of the first cut they find that the damage under the chapel is not one thing. It is a worked-out seam, old, and it is the *edge* of something larger that runs under the river bank where nobody has ever dug, and it is dry, and it has been dry for a very long time.
- **Consequence:** the dig stops, the ground is boarded, the terms of the descent are entered, and Adrian accepts a duty that is not a debt and cannot be paid with money.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The batch ends with Adrian in a Witness's body, holding a notebook, owing more than he did ten chapters ago, and with a route that is still empty.
- **Ending:** the formal Concord hearing on whether a first license may exist has set a date, and the date is three days after the next tide, and the next tide is in nine days.
