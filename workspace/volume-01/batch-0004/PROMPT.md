## Where the story is

**Chapter 31 opens on day 32**, the Friday, roughly the eighteenth day of the salt month. Three live things happen on that day: a man in a good coat has a date he told a nine-year-old on a step and nobody else; a woman who carries the Crown's mail takes the account of the other side down the Reach road ahead of the Crown's case, and Adrian has to go with her; and the Registry's demand for a registered holder or a statement of permanent closure is live and dated to a quarter. **Day 33** is the next tide and it is an ordinary worked day: forty men under the near gate, twice, at ordinary volume, and about three-quarters of an inch in the pale edge of the join in the gate at one in the morning, and a hundred and forty acres four miles away losing another hour. **Day 34 onward** is the week before the closing working, which is not in this batch.

## Hard constraints — carry these from Batch 0003 unchanged

- **System messages: at most one per chapter.** A message is a pale field in Adrian's environment, in plain language, in obligations rather than mechanics. It never rewards good behaviour. Ivenn's prompts are the designed danger: attractive, partly true, and missing a social cost. Do not let the narration repeat a prompt's claim as fact. **Batch 0003 carried four, in Chapters 22, 24, 27 and 29, and none in 21, 23, 25, 26, 28 or 30** — so the interface has now been silent for four chapters again, and **Chapter 25 proved that the loudest event in the volume can produce no message at all.** Its silences are usable. Adrian has four prompts in his notebook and has learned the *shape* of an offer rather than its content; do not let him pass one.
- **Adrian never performs a working.** He reads and marks. Locals work. In Chapter 25 he was asked in front of eleven men whether he was in the working and said no; in Chapter 27 his whole part in the first crossing was to read a line and mark it in three places with a stub of chalk, and Hesper Saye made him say so out loud afterwards **so that it would be in a book before it was a story.** That motif is available and should be used at least once more, and never parodied.
- **Adrian stays Unbound.** He refused the Wayfinder profile three times, the third out loud in a hall of three hundred people, and the reason is in the roll of strangers with two dates on it. He collects no templates. He also has no return mark of his own and never will in this volume.
- **No crowd ever crosses.** One named person per opening, one opening at a time, a fresh inspection before reuse, and a receiving community that may suspend. **One return mark exists in the world and it belongs to Eli Mercer**, established on day 27 at a cost and a date, and the route that could use it is **continued and may not be used** until a fresh inspection, no suspension, and an answer. Anything that looks like a rescue in this batch is a **breach**, and a breach is defined by missing infrastructure, not by being a door.
- **Owen Park dies in Chapter 38 and it is permanent.** It is the volume's only death. No respawn, no return, no inheritance of a mark. It must arrive with three causes, all three of which are already on the page: **Adrian's underestimated crossing cost**, **the Chapter 25 shoulder that no healer could set**, and **Owen's own refusal to follow instructions.** He is hurt, he is exposed, he is the author of his own death in the specific and limited way the outline requires, and Adrian is the man who let it happen. - **Do not have anyone state that this is a game.** The premise surfaced in Chapter 25 and is now a public asset: the word *player* is on a market board, four Veyrans repeated it in a market on day 26, two men from a chandlery came to a hall, and one of the second group wrote *this is a closed beta* in a yard and asked for it to be entered that she said it first. Adrian's own vocabulary may still slip — a status display, a respawn, a tutorial, a chair, a cohort, an onboarding — and the slip is the point, and at least one slip should be corrected in public.
- **Keep the five lock sites apart.** The **main breach** in the Lock of Salt gate, open, watched, nine inches nearer the near gate than it was, and *stepping* when the city does unusual work; the **outer tear's maintenance seam**, the **Narrow Mark**, in the outer channel wall, used once on day 28 in breach of its own terms; the **near gate**, the dockers' inner working gate; the **chapel-floor hairline seam**, which is the top of the dressed joint and which **tore across** on day 25; the **chapel stone**, the raised pale threshold slab at the foot of the arch, which **said nine words of English out loud in a yard on day 25** and is the voice's only channel. Derive every new date from the table in `state/continuity.md` and put any new date in the notebook before you write it. ### Chapter 0031 — What a Date Is For
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the loft and the yard on day 32, then the Reach road to Verrow, then the lower gallery at one in the morning on day 33.
- **Goal:** find out what Ivenn Marrow wants on the eighteenth day of the salt month, and keep the account of the other side out of a Crown mail case.
- **Resistance:** Marrow's date is not a demand, it is an **invitation to write something on a keeper's board**, and Adrian cannot refuse it in a yard because refusing it in public is itself a sentence. Peth Lammay's boat goes on the thirty-second and the account goes in her hand and **Adrian has to sit in a room in the Reach for four hours with no notebook**, which is the thing he is worst at. The Reach asks him a question he cannot answer, and it is not about a door.
- **Turn:** Adrian discovers that the thing Marrow wants written is not an excuse. It is the **reason**, and the reason is that Adrian's refusal was the first refusal in a month that a man in a good coat could not improve on, and Marrow wants it in a keeper's hand on a keeper's wall **because a reason in a good coat's hand is an argument and a reason in Adrian's hand on a keeper's board is a rule.** Adrian writes it. It is a *worse* sentence than the one he gave in the hall.
- **Consequence:** the account of the other side is read at Verrow before a clerk in Aurel knows it exists, and the first thing the meeting-ground does with it is ask a question about a person rather than about a place, and that question is entered. At one in the morning on the thirty-third, the number in the gate is read for the last time by Adrian alone, and he enters it, and the chalk in the last column of the tide table is written by somebody else.
- **Power:** nothing gained. A sentence he did not want to say.
- **Ending:** the measurement, and the ordinary word in the box.
