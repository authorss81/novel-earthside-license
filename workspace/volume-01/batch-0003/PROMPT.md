## Where the story is

Six days to the last day of the salt month, which is the field review, which is also the day Marrow's chalk expires. Nine days to the next tide. Twelve days to the Concord hearing on whether a first license may exist in Saltwake. The main breach is three inches wider than the night Adrian woke on the stones and it is *stepping* — four steps, all of them on days the lock did unusual work, which means the city's ordinary maintenance is the mechanism and the only known remedy is a flood. The ground under the chapel is boarded, and a nine-foot dressed joint four feet down is dry, and it runs under the river bank where nobody has ever dug. Adrian is a Witness with no workway, a seat on a formally unrecognised instrument, an enforceable debt that is still growing, and a post: for as long as the ground is open, the ground does not open without him.

Seven people. A nine-year-old who follows Owen at six feet. A woman of sixty with nine years of paper about nineteen stones. A keeper whose fifth word has turned out to be a word the voice is using. A mason who is frightened of the correct thing. A man in a good coat whose chalk is on a board and whose complicity must stay plausible and unproven.

## Batch goal

Turn the post into a season, and the hearing into a real political fight with something at stake in it. The board under the chapel has to go down further. The voice channel has to become two-way in a way nobody can control. The field review has to happen as a scene, not a line. A child has to go missing and the cohort has to argue about the one opening of the narrow route. And at the end, a controlled and properly witnessed working has to succeed at what it was for and still go catastrophically wrong somewhere it could not reach, and the second group of arrivals has to come out of the dark.

## Hard constraints — carry these from Batch 0002 unchanged

- **System messages: at most one per chapter.** A message is a pale field in Adrian's environment, in plain language, in obligations rather than mechanics. It never rewards good behaviour. Ivenn's prompts are the batch's designed danger: attractive, partly true, and missing a social cost. Do not let the narration repeat a prompt's claim as fact. Chapters where nothing needs a message should carry none. Note that the interface has been silent for six chapters, it has no memory of a refusal, and it has never once come out of the main breach — its silences are usable.
- **Adrian never performs a working.** He reads and marks. Locals work. In Chapters 15 and 20 he was refused entry to the hole in public and put four feet back; keep that posture and do not let a new power quietly dissolve it.
- **Adrian stays Unbound.** Marrow's chalked offer expires on the last day of the salt month. He does not take it. He says out loud, in a room, that he is staying Unbound and why. He collects no templates. He also does not collect a return mark.
- **No crowd ever crosses.** One named person per opening, one opening at a time, a fresh inspection before reuse, and a receiving community that can suspend. **If no return mark exists, no one travels at all** — and at the start of this batch no return mark exists for anyone, so the first person to travel requires a separate, expensive, witnessed act to establish one, and that act is a plot event with a cost and a date.
- **Nobody dies in this batch.** Owen Park's Chapter 38 death is the volume's only death and it must arrive intact and permanent. No respawn, no return, no inheritance of a mark. He is hurt, he is exposed, and he does not die. Keep his shoulder untouched until Chapter 25, where it breaks.
- **Do not have anyone state that this is a game.** The premise half is still deferred. Adrian's own vocabulary may slip — status display, quest, respawn, tutorial, a chair — and the slip is the point, but the cohort and the narration must not assert it, and no Veyran may be called an NPC. The newly arrived group in Chapter 25 is the first place the mistaken-belief premise may begin to surface **in the arrivals' own mouths only**; a Veyran reacting to them is not the same as the Veyrans believing it, and Adrian must find it unbearable and be right to.
- **Keep the five lock sites apart.** The main breach in the gate; the outer tear's maintenance seam (the Narrow Mark) in the outer channel wall; the near gate, the dockers' inner working gate; the chapel-floor hairline seam in the chapel flagstones; the chapel stone, the raised pale threshold slab at the foot of the arch. Nothing happens at two of them.
- **The voice has one home and it is not the gate.** Since Chapter 7 it has come *out of the black line in the chapel stone*. Its physical source is a man standing in the **lock join** below. The chapel stone is the channel; the lock join is where the working is being done. If a chapter needs the voice, it arrives in the chapel stone. It does not relocate to the main breach, and the main breach does not speak.
- **The 211 people, the ledger, and the field debt are real.** When a promise has a cost, put the cost in the book.
- **Marrow stays publicly useful and unaccused.** No scene in this batch may convict him, and none may make him useless either.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0021 — Two Dates in One Yard
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the yard under the lock chapel, the day after the dig stopped, then the three days after that.
- **Goal:** get from "we have twelve days and two of them are in other people's hands" to a plan that other people can act on, before the post consumes him entirely.
- **Resistance:** the keeper's rota for the twice-daily binding has a hole in it every second morning because Owen cannot always be there and nobody else has learned the words, and **the rule is *whoever is nearest*, which means the rota is not allowed to exist and Adrian is the one who has to work out how to run a thing that must not be run.** A second resistance: the four witnesses each have a different day off, and the cost column puts a day's labour on the gate crew every month, and Renna Ord tells Adrian that the crew is already the thinnest thing in the city.
- **Turn:** Sera Vail teaches him the only thing she knows how to teach, which is that a date in a book is not a plan, it is a *place a plan has to be legible in*. She makes him write down, in front of witnesses, who is answerable for what on each of the next twelve days — and the list he produces is longer than the room and mostly consists of people he has never spoken to.
- **Consequence:** he learns that being at the counter means he is also the man who finds out that somebody has not turned up, and the first name on his list of twelve days is a woman in Corrow who has decided to stop speaking to him.
- **Power:** nothing gained.
- **Ending:** a message from the Reach — not the Crown's, the Commons' — asking for a person to come and stand in a room, and the question of who goes being the first thing in the batch that the group cannot answer.

### Chapter 0022 — The Second Set of Hands
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the Reach, two days, by boat and on foot.
- **Goal:** go where the water is, meet the Commons properly, and find out what a receiving community actually wants, now that it has learned it can say no twice.
- **Resistance:** the Commons does not want Adrian. It wants **Owen Park**, and it says so, because Owen is the only one of the seven who has not asked for anything in a room and the Reach has noticed. Adrian goes as a witness and is treated as a witness, which is a form of being put in a small boat at the front. Tamsin comes and is more useful in the first hour than he is in the first year. A Reed speaker puts the standing question: if a receiving community may suspend a route, what happens to the people it has already let in?
- **Turn:** the Commons gives Adrian something worse than a refusal, which is a **demand**: it wants the route's cost schedule to be paid by the thing that benefits, and it wants the eleven villages' season to be a standing charge on the instrument rather than a private debt on one man — which would make his debt communal, and would make it much harder for him to clear and much easier for him to be angry at.
- **Consequence:** Adrian brings back a term sheet that the Concord has to answer, and a warning that Nine Elms' refusal will be tested in a month by somebody who will use his own concession against him.
- **Power:** nothing gained; a relationship entered.
- **Ending:** Owen comes back changed, and asks Adrian for the one thing he has never asked anybody for.

### Chapter 0023 — The Unverified Man
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the walk-round and the low room by the wharf, a night and a morning.
- **Goal:** take Owen's light seriously enough to act on it without sending anybody into a hole in a flood, and keep a man with an unclosed scalp from becoming the next name on a failure record.
- **Resistance:** Owen has stopped asking to go anywhere and has started making *other people* go, which is the most dangerous thing he has ever done and he does not know it. Hesper Saye will not treat him in front of the six. And the physical evidence question is real: a light seen by one man cannot be put in a record, and the only way to make it legible is to make the record in **Owen's own hand**, which is a piece of paper that a court will treat as a symptom.
- **Turn:** Adrian does the thing he has never done — he designs a **procedure for a claim that cannot be tested**, the local equivalent of a form for a haunting, with a named witness chain, a stated standing, and a refusal clause, and it is immediately useful for something he did not expect, because the same form works for a man in a join and for a mark on a palm.
- **Consequence:** the form goes into the wall of the loft beside the four rules, and a second person in this city is now documented as being owed an answer. Adrian has invented a civic instrument and has no authority to invent one, and says so in the entry.
- **Power:** nothing gained, except a tool.
- **Ending:** Owen's request, which is not to go to the wall.

### Chapter 0024 — What the Dig Found
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the yard, at low water, over four days, with the board and the bank.
- **Goal:** get enough of the joint to identify it — and find out whether the dry thing under the chapel is the same fact as the boundary stone in the yard-side landing, or two facts that have been standing next to each other for two hundred years.
- **Resistance:** Vask will not dig under the bank and Renna Ord will not let her. The work has to be done from the chapel side, which is slower and means taking up more of the floor, which means more days of the yard binding, which means more people standing in the rain at six in the morning. **Ottoline Vask insists on the honest rule: a quarter of a thing is not a thing, and the identification is entered as a description and not as a name.**
- **Turn:** Tamsin reads the boundary table again against the new evidence and finds the answer is uncomfortable and simple: the stone in the landing and the joint under the floor are **two halves of one boundary**, set in the same year, by the same three jurisdictions, and one of them was moved. The dry thing is not a mystery. It is a *record* — somebody wrote the boundary into the ground and then moved half of it and left the other half where the water could find it.
- **Consequence:** the **scarred outer threshold** is uncovered and **identified on the page**, by description, in Vask's and Quill's words, in front of witnesses — an older damaged Anchorstone beneath the chapel floor on the bank above the outer channel, out of reach of Adrian's Chapter 4 repair, which carries a voice and cannot be controlled. The ground is re-boarded. The dig is finished for this season.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The thing it identified is not under his hand and he cannot touch it.
- **Ending:** the identification goes into the public record, and within a day somebody in the city who was not in the yard knows what is under the chapel floor.

### Chapter 0025 — The Second Cohort Arrives Anyway
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the lock, four days later, in daylight, at high water.
- **Goal:** hold a controlled, locally witnessed working that stabilises the main lock and keeps the standing route intact — done properly, with witnesses, with a cost, and with the gate crew saying the oath at its ordinary volume.
- **Resistance:** everything is right except one thing. The working is consented to, costed, witnessed, and performed by locals; the four witnesses sign; Adrian reads and marks; Adrian does not touch. And the **older damaged Anchorstone beneath the chapel floor** — the outer threshold, out of reach of the working, in the wall of a building where the crew have doubled for the crowds — **tears open as a breach without consent and without witnesses and takes people who were standing there.**
- **Turn:** this is the **volume midpoint reversal**, and it must be unambiguous. It is *not* the Chapter 5 mechanism: there is consent, there is a quorum, there is a cost, the working succeeds at the work it was set to do, and the failure is at a site the working could not reach. The grab takes a **second, unrequested group of arrivals, including a nineteen-year-old called Eli Mercer**, and leaves the outer threshold as the first **black scar in a place**.
- **Consequence:** Owen Park is at the exposed site as a bystander, reaches for the child, and **injures a shoulder that no local healer can fully set**. Adrian learns that controlling one seam was never the same as controlling the site. The cohort's immediate goal changes from *find a way home* to *keep the people who came through with him alive without turning them into a sacrifice for his return.*
- **Power:** nothing gained. This is the cost the volume extracts.
- **Ending:** the arrivals are in the hall, and one of them is asking Adrian, in front of two hundred people, whether this is a game — and Adrian has to answer a question he has never had to answer in his own voice.

### Chapter 0026 — The List
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the hall, then the loft, the day after.
- **Goal:** work out who the second group actually is, and stop the room from making them into either an invasion or a rescue, in that order.
- **Resistance:** the second group is not strangers to each other and not strangers to a genre. They have come expecting a game and they say so, out loud, in the open, and Adrian cannot speak their language any better than he can speak Vey's, which turns out not to be the obstacle. Priya is the only person in the room who recognises what they are saying and she nearly says it in public. Jonah wants them organised immediately and is, for once, arguing a case that is ninety per cent right.
- **Turn:** Adrian does the thing that costs him most this volume. He **names the new arrivals in the public record as people who came through without asking, exactly as the six were named in Chapter 5**, refuses the word *player* in the hall, and enters the second group as a **separate list** with its own witnesses — including a refusal clause for any of them who does not want to be in it.
- **Consequence:** the six are put in a position where they are asked to testify against the framing that would have helped them, and Priya's log becomes the document the room argues about for a week. One of the arrivals, **Eli Mercer**, refuses to join the six and refuses to speak for them, and says so in the record.
- **Power:** nothing gained.
- **Ending:** Adrian realises that the premise he has been privately embarrassed by for three weeks is now a public asset being used against him, and that the only thing he has is the sentence a woman from a wharf said in a room in Chapter 14.

### Chapter 0027 — The Missing Child
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the Reach, the river, and the market, four days.
- **Goal:** find a child who went missing on the second night after the arrivals came through, and do it without spending the one thing that cannot be spent twice.
- **Resistance:** local water pressure has dropped across four villages, a child is missing on the reed paths, and the argument is between Tamsin, Mina and Adrian about the **first opening of the narrow route** — which means establishing a return mark for a named person, which means breaking the standing terms in public, which means either a rescue or a first possible homecoming and not both.
- **Turn:** Adrian learns the hard thing the volume has been setting up: **he does not know whether the mark he gave up in Chapter 10 could have brought anyone home.** He says so out loud, in front of the child's family, in the middle of the argument, and the room goes quiet. The mark is in a wall in another channel and nobody can get it back, and the honest answer is that the question cannot be answered in his lifetime, and that answering it would require the very thing he gave up.
- **Consequence:** the route's standing terms are broken in public to make a rescue possible, and the rescue is not the disappearance — the child is found by **locals**, on foot, on the reed paths, by people who know the ground — while the opening is spent on something else entirely, chosen by the person whose mark would have been used. The batch's argument about which is the first opening is resolved by the six arguing it out, not by Adrian.
- **Power:** nothing gained. A duty is paid and a term is broken.
- **Ending:** the person who is sent first is not Adrian, and Adrian's only part in it is to read a line and mark it, and to be the one who is asked afterwards what it was for.

### Chapter 0028 — What the Scout Is
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the Concord hall and the loft, several days on.
- **Goal:** establish, on the record, what the second group actually saw on the other side of the Chapel 25 tear, and stop it being told as a story.
- **Resistance:** one of the arrivals describes a place with a shape Adrian recognises from a design document, and describes it in the wrong order. Ivenn Marrow is the only person in the room who has the vocabulary to translate the description, which is both useful and unbearable. Jonah wants the account published in a market. And the second group is already disagreeing with each other about what they saw, in a way that is not confusion and is not lying.
- **Turn:** Marrow translates, and the translation is accurate, and Adrian understands that **Marrow is reading their account as a professional and not as a recruiter** — for the first time in twenty chapters — and that this is the most frightening version of the man yet, because it means he is genuinely useful to the people he wants to use.
- **Consequence:** the account is entered in the public record with its disagreements preserved rather than reconciled, and the second group is given standing to correct it. **Preserving a contradiction instead of resolving it** is the first visible act of the plural principle anywhere in the volume, and Adrian recognises that he has learned it from a notary and not from a teacher.
- **Power:** nothing gained.
- **Ending:** in the disagreement, one detail that only one of the arrivals can account for is the same shape Adrian's own design ledger describes, and it is described in a sequence that a person could only know by having read the document — and Adrian has never shown the document to anybody.

### Chapter 0029 — The Review
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the last day of the salt month, in the hall, and afterwards in a yard.
- **Goal:** answer the three questions about a bottom field in front of the same people who asked them, with a number that has been growing every hour since the morning after the second tide.
- **Resistance:** the debt is not a fine and cannot be paid. Sera Vail's three questions are the ones she wrote in Chapter 9 and they have not been softened by a month. The eleven villages have their own account, and the two accounts do not agree, and **the only way to make them agree is for Adrian to accept a number he knows is too small.** Nine Elms' refusal is on the table, and so is the Crown's refused compensation, and so is Marrow's chalk, which expires on this day.
- **Turn:** Adrian does the thing the whole batch has been building toward and refuses to be the man who settles it. He asks to be **continued as a creditor** — to keep the debt open, growing, and enforceable, with a new review, on the grounds that a debt that can be closed is a debt that can be traded, and that the field's real cost is not a season and never was. It is the most humiliating available option and the only one that does not require anybody to lie.
- **Consequence:** the review passes, in public, with the debt **continued and enlarged**, and Adrian is readmitted to the seat for a further term with a different cost column: the standing labour, at two a day, in the open, and a new page in the ledger for the second group.
- **Power:** nothing gained. He is deeper in the book than he was.
- **Ending:** Marrow's chalk comes off the board that evening, in his own hand, and what he writes underneath it is not an offer.

### Chapter 0030 — The Hearing
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** three days after the next tide, the Concord hall, first light.
- **Goal:** get the Concord to answer the question it opened — whether a first license may exist in Saltwake — without letting the answer be an answer to a different question.
- **Resistance:** the hall is now a room with a scar in a place, a second cohort in it, a Crown letter refused, a village that has said no twice, a man whose shoulder will not be set, and a man in a good coat who has spent a month being nothing but useful. The Crown sends a second letter, and this one is not polite. And the honest question of the hearing is not *should there be a door* but *who may be angry at whom*, and nobody in the room wants that question answered.
- **Turn:** Adrian, asked for the third time in a month what the Narrow Mark is for, gives a different answer from the one in Chapter 17, and it is not better or worse — it is smaller and more precise, and it names the cost in terms of *people who are not in this room*. The instrument is not licensed. It is **continued as a thing nobody may use**, with the cost column, the receiving community's standing, and the two refusals all entered together, and the hearing's actual product is a **procedure for being wrong in public** rather than a permission.
- **Consequence:** the first volume's political question gets a real answer that does not close anything, and the thing Adrian is left holding is a duty with a review date and a document nobody can use. This is where the volume hands off: a **narrow quorum route that survives as a costed, refusable, unusable instrument**, a second group in the city on separate terms, an outer threshold that is now a black scar in a place, and a man in a gate that steps when the city works it.
- **Power:** nothing gained. The volume's promise for Chapters 41–50 is now unmistakable and it is a *choice about the closing working*, not a discovery.
- **Ending:** the next tide, and the fact that the volume's last act will be somebody deciding whether to keep working a lock.

## Batch pacing

Chapters 21–24 are consequence, travel, procedure and excavation, with Chapter 24 the batch's discovery and the identification that Chapter 25 needs. Chapter 25 is the **volume midpoint reversal** and it is the loudest, most frightening and most consequential chapter in the volume; it must be unambiguous about consent, witnesses, cost and site. Chapters 26–28 are the human aftermath, the new group's arrival, the missing child and the first opening of the narrow route. Chapters 29–30 are the field review and the hearing, and they end on a decision and a clock. The batch ends on the hearing's answer and the two dates behind it, not on a cliffhanger.
