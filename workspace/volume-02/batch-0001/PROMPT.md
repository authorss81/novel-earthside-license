### Chapter 0051 — The Room and the Road
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the loft above the market, the yard under the chapel, the walk-round, and the office end of a Crown letter, day 51, the Wednesday, the twenty-sixth day of the new month.
- **Goal:** find out whether the licence can do the one thing seventeen people have asked him for, which is put a person back where they came from.
- **Resistance:** **it cannot, and the reason is on the licence's own page.** A controlled opening needs a receiving community that understands the purpose. **There is no receiving community on the other side of that seam and there is no instrument in this world that can make one from this side**, and the far side is a room with no window and no door anybody has found, and about eleven people have stood in it. Adrian spends the day being asked the question by people who are entitled to ask it and gives the true answer nine times in nine different rooms, and about nine people hear the difference and about two hundred and thirty do not.
- **Turn:** the second list is asked, one person at a time, in a room with the door shut, and **eight of the eleven want the same thing and three do not**, and one of the three is a woman of sixteen who wants to know what the stones on the wall above the market say, and the discovery is that the answer to her question is a page and the answer to the other eight is a sentence with no page under it. And a Crown channel that has been on the table since the fortieth day of the flood is finally asked about — **by somebody other than Marrow**, by a licensed carrier of Crown mail who has carried documents into places with no Crown documents for eleven years and who says the sentence that opens the letter problem: **a letter is a thing that travels along a road, and there is no road, and a man in a good coat has offered me nothing for eleven days.**
- **Consequence:** **the first request for an opening is entered as a request** — it has a name on it, a date, a person who asked, and an answer that is a reason and not a refusal of the person — **and the reason is the instrument's own condition, and the answer is *there is no receiving community and this instrument cannot make one*.** Wenna Cray asks the fact-or-fault question and is told it is neither: it is a fact about the shape of the world and the shape of the world cannot be entered as a fault. Adrian writes, in his own hand, that he made a promise of fair access in the first week of this flood and **the promise was fair and it was not possible, and those are two different sentences and he has been saying them in the same breath for fifty days.**
- **Power:** nothing gained. A page with a reason on it and nine true answers.
- **Ending:** the review date is read off the slate on the nail under the chapel arch in front of about two hundred and forty people, and somebody asks how many times the door has opened, and the answer is twice in fifty days and one of them was a breach of its own terms, and the room does not find that funny and the notary enters it.

### Chapter 0052 — Salt
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the salt works on the upper road, the market, the yard under the chapel, and the two hundred steps, day 52, the Thursday, the twenty-seventh day of the new month.
- **Goal:** pay the price that is written on the nail, which is the price of a form that watches a hole and cannot mend it.
- **Resistance:** **salt is Saltwake's trade and it went up a third when the season turned, and a keeper of about seventy has a bucket and eleven weeks in it.** The Brass Measure does not sell salt and does not need to: **Ottavia Reek offers the salt anyway, on credit, against the crew's shift list**, which is the fourth term in a good coat, and it is refused in a yard in about eleven words by a rule that has been in the book since the fortieth day of the flood: *a thing that cannot be paid is a tax, and a wage drawn from another list is not a wage.*

### Chapter 0053 — A Hand That Will Not Close
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** Saye's room above the salt weavers' chapel, the loft, and the yard, day 53, the Friday, the twenty-eighth day of the new month.
- **Goal:** keep a man of thirty-one out of a decision that his own hand is making for him.
- **Resistance:** **the eighteenth field, and it is the cruelest shape yet, because it puts the whole of the cost on a named person and calls that fair, and the sentence is true.** Teodor Blaz, 31, of Leeds, warehouse supervisor, has not been able to make a fist since the twenty-fifth day of this flood, and in a town where a hand is the work a man without a hand is a man without a wage, and nobody has said that to him out loud and everybody has behaved as though they had. The field offers a workway, states the benefit plainly, says the person is the whole of the cost, and says the cost is entered. **It is the old form of the oath with the words taken out and a person in it, and it is the most attractive of the eighteen, and Adrian recognises the shape in under a second and is the wrong man to be the one who refuses it.**
- **Turn:** **Mina Okoye asks the standing question, and it is hers:** *what is the work?* Because a workway is not a power, it is a social role, and a role means that somebody in the world is entitled to expect a thing of the holder. And the room discovers, in about eleven minutes, that **there is no office in this city for a hand that holds things.** Every other work somebody could name had a door: the anchorwright has a shop, the water-count has the Reach, the bone-setter has her room. **A workway in this city is a claim on a job that does not exist, and the cost of it is not the work. The cost of it is that a man with a standing is a man who has been given a reason to be angry, and the standing is a seat, and a seat is only ever got rid of by the people who are angry at it.**
- **Consequence:** Teodor refuses, out loud, in a room, and says the true reason, which is not the noble one. And afterwards, in the yard, in private, he asks Adrian the question nobody has asked him in a month: **if I had said yes, who would have been angry?** And Adrian writes the answer down and it is **nine names, and not one person**, and he gives it to him, and Teodor Blaz reads it and says the sentence that makes the middle of this volume possible: **then it is not about me.** Renner Sallow's count is entered. The count of refusals is still **none**.
- **Power:** nothing gained. A list of nine names in a notebook, and the discovery that the licence's own bar bars the instrument and not the world.
- **Ending:** Adrian writes, in a notebook, in his own hand, the sentence he has been avoiding for six weeks, and it is that **the thing he refused three times was the only thing he was ever going to be offered, and he refused it because it worked, and a man with a hand that does not close has not got that luxury and nobody in this city has thought about what he gets instead.**

### Chapter 0055 — The Man Who Did Not Say
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the yard under the lock chapel, a room over a chandlery, and the ordinary ground behind the ordinary wall, day 55, the Sunday, the thirtieth day of the new month.
- **Goal:** get the winter's ordinary work done and find out who is carrying the cost of it.
- **Resistance:** **the batch's centre, and the volume's mystery opening its second half, and it happens because a procedure invented in Volume 01 catches a man who has been walking past it for nine years.** The **right to be asked** — the fifth item on the slate on the nail, the one added on the forty-ninth day — obliges somebody to be told and asked before the ground begins, refusably, with the refusal entered, and it is a **question and not a duty** and the day it becomes a duty somebody is to take it off the wall in daylight and say so. And in practice, on a Sunday, when the keeper's board is washed and the four words and the fifth go back on, **the form asks a Hearthguard of nineteen years whether he has anything in the ground behind the ordinary wall that a person has ever asked him about**, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and he cannot refuse it, because **a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused.**
- **Turn:** **he answers, and the answer is that the names were written down once.** All eight of them, in a book, in his grandfather's hand, in the year the ground was made, because a Hearthguard's grandfather who held a gate was required to give a roll of the dead of that gate to the Crown twice a year. And the stones stayed. **And the book went to Aurel in a wooden case, four times a year, on a river boat, the same case the roll of strangers goes in.** And there is no record in Saltwake of any dead of this gate since that year, and there has not been for nine years, and **nobody noticed, because the ground behind the ordinary wall has had a Hearthguard's grandfather in it the whole time and a grave with a slab and a name cut on it twice looks like care.**
- **Consequence:** **the room understands, in about one afternoon, that a description is a removal with a column for where the person is.** And the seventeen are on a roll. And the seventeen's page is sent four times a year. And a woman of twenty-four asks, in a room of two hundred and forty people, the question that reorganises the second half of this volume: **what is the column for where the person is, and who fills it in, and what happens to a person who is in a column and not in a country?** And the answer, from a notary of fifty who has never been asked and who answers in nine words, is that **the column is for the office and the office is nine hundred miles away and has never once sent anybody to fill it in.** Adrian asks, out loud, in a room, whether anybody in this city has ever seen a name come back out of one of those cases, and about two hundred and forty people understand that nobody has.
- **Power:** nothing gained. One question answered in nine years, and a machine that four hundred people have been inside without knowing.
- **Ending:** a woman of sixty-one who cannot read is told, by a woman of twenty-four, what a man said in a yard about eight graves, and she says the number nineteen out loud, twice, and nobody has asked her why, and the yard empties.

### Chapter 0056 — What the Description Is For
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** a converted salt store, the yard, and the office of the Concord at the salt wharf, day 56, the Monday, the thirty-first day of the new month.
- **Goal:** answer a Crown document without lying and without handing anybody over, and decide whether his own name goes in the answer.
- **Resistance:** **the fourth Crown instrument, and it is new, and it is not the other three.** A **schedule of crossings**: every opening, the date, the hour, the seam, the person, the two witnesses, the four who inspected, and the cost, quarterly, in a form required, to a building in Aurel, **with a fee in silver for the copying and the sealing, and this city has no silver.** The notary says out loud, in a room, that it is a fourth document and not a version of the other three, and Renner Sallow — who is a clerk of that office and has been at a table in this city since the fourth day of the flood and has never been asked to stop — says the sentence that makes the politics legible for the next four volumes: **a fee is a thing that tells you what an office thinks you are.**
- **Turn:** **the room invents the honest useless form.** They fill the schedule in truthfully and in the truth's own shape: every column filled, nothing omitted, no rounding, no summary, the true hour, the true cost, the true number of men, and **nothing in it that the office can act on** — because a form completed honestly by people who have decided to be unhelpful is a form that is complete and carries nothing, and the Crown cannot refuse a form it asked for and cannot do anything with it either. Vask supplies the technical condition, Priya Shah supplies the sentence, and Sera Vail enters that this is the first instrument in the history of this book that was designed to be useless and that **she has been a notary for twenty-six years and has never before entered a page whose purpose is to be unhelpful.**
- **Consequence:** **compliance is dangerous, and it is dangerous in the exact way Adrian has been warning about for fifty days.** The honest form records, in a column the office will read, that eleven named people have asked to be returned to the place they came from and that there is no receiving community and no route. **That sentence now exists four times a year in a building nine hundred miles away in a wooden case.** And the fee in silver has to be found, and the only winter money in Saltwake is the Brass Measure, and Adrian is put in front of the choice: put his own name in the schedule as a person to be asked, which is the first thing he has been offered in this volume that is not a seat and is the cheapest possible form of visibility, **or keep his name out of it and let the page be a page about a lock.** And he puts it in, and Priya Shah tells him what he has done in nine words, and he writes them down.
- **Power:** nothing gained. A form designed to be useless, a fee owed, and a name on a page in a column headed *person to be asked*.
- **Ending:** the fee is entered as owing, with no date, and Renner Sallow enters, in his own book and in ours, that the office does not charge a fee it cannot collect and that the fee is therefore not about silver, and nobody in the room works out what it is about, and the reader is allowed one better guess than the room.

### Chapter 0057 — The Nineteenth Field
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the loft, the yard under the chapel, and a room over a chandlery, day 57, the Tuesday, the thirty-second day of the new month.
- **Goal:** get through a Tuesday, and stop thinking about a form.
- **Resistance:** **the nineteenth field, and for the first time in this book's history the person who accepts one is not the man who has spent six weeks refusing them.** **Perry Lask, 41, of the second list, who said *this is a closed beta* in a yard on the ninth day of this flood and asked for it to be entered that she said it first, and who has been careful for fifty days and has asked for nothing, accepts a workway** in a room of about nine people, in about four seconds, after saying one sentence first, which is that she has been waiting since the ninth day for the wall to say something she recognises.
- **Turn:** **this city has no procedure for a workway, and it cannot have one, and the reason is on the licence's own page: a workway is a social role, and a role means somebody in the world is entitled to expect a thing of the holder, and in Saltwake there is no office, no guild, no bench and no Crown instrument that can say what a holder of a workway is expected to do.** So the holder of a workway is a person with a standing and no job, in a city where a standing is the old form of the oath with the words taken out. Vask's sentence: **a workway is a post with a person in it and the post has not been through a room.** Priya Shah's sentence, which is the chapter: **then put it through a room.**
- **Consequence:** **they write a procedure in about a day and it is bad, and its weaknesses are entered with it, and the entering is the point.** It has no power to compel and no power to revoke and no list of what the holder is expected to do, and the only thing it can do is require the holder to say, in a room, in daylight, with a date, what they think the work is, and to have that said in a book, and **to be asked again in four months by somebody who is not the person who wrote it.** Adrian discovers, on the page, and says out loud, that **his own bar is not broken.** The bar says no workway may be offered to any of the seventeen *by this instrument or by anybody using it*, and **a pale field in a room is not the instrument and nobody used it, and the page is perfect and it does not reach.** He says the sentence in about nine words and it is the worst sentence of his life and it is true: **I wrote a rule about a thing and I did not write a rule about the world, and I have spent fifty days telling a city that the difference is pedantry.**
- **Power:** nothing gained. One workway in a city of about eleven hundred people, one bad procedure with a date, and a bar that does not reach.
- **Ending:** the girl of seventeen enters, in the day book, in her own hand, that the holder of the workway has asked what she is expected to do, and that nobody in the room could answer her, and that **the entry is four lines long and the column is four lines high and the width of the column is entered as a fact for the fourth time in the same year.**

### Chapter 0058 — The Day the Crew Did Not Turn Up

And Renna Ord, who named the price and who is owed a season herself, says one sentence, and it is four words, and it is the chapter: **it is the same price.** And a docker with a barrow asks, out loud, for the first time, whether the number four is a number or a prediction, and it is entered as **a number entered by a person and a prediction entered by a person, and the two are on the page together, neither above the other, and both stand.**
