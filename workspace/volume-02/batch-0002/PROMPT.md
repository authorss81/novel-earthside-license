### Chapter 0061 — What a Person With No Work Is For
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the loft, a converted salt store, four kitchens, a chandlery, and the walk-round, day 61, the Saturday, the thirty-sixth day of the new month.
- **Goal:** do the thing he was told to do on a stair, which is to go and ask the city what a person with no work is for, and take the answer.
- **Resistance:** **he cannot ask the question as he means it.** The first four attempts produce a room full of jobs that are not jobs — a woman offered a seat, a boy offered a day's labour, a docker offered a place in a queue, and a man who asks him whether he means *work* or *a wage*, and who is right that they are not the same and that the page of the seventeenth day of the new month banned the second and did not mention the first. **He has to say the question in the words the room uses, and the room's word is not *work* and it is *turning up*.**
- **Turn:** **the answer is a number and not a list of jobs, and it is nineteen lines long, and it is not a rota.** Nineteen lines that say what a person in this city may be asked to turn up for, at what notice, at the ordinary rate, on an ordinary morning, with no name at the top of anything, and **with a line that says a person may refuse and the refusal is entered and does not stop the line from being offered again on a different day.** Vask writes it in about nine sentences and says the second sentence of it out loud: **a working can be made cheaper without being made private, and this is the second time anybody has said that sentence in this city and the first time it has been said about a person instead of a door.**
- **Consequence:** **the two beds in Silt Row are the test case and they fail the first time they are used**, because a household that has offered a bed under a page with a date on it is a household that has offered it to two people, and one of the two does not turn up, and **the page does not say what happens and nothing in this city has ever said what happens**, and the finding is written down in about nine lines by a girl of seventeen and it is the most uncomfortable page written in sixty days.
- **Power:** nothing gained. Nineteen lines and a failure on the first morning.
- **Ending:** the first of the nineteen lines is read out in about two hundred and forty people and about nine people understand that it is a widening and about two hundred and thirty think it is a job.

### Chapter 0062 — The List That Did Not Go
- **POV:** Adrian Vale. **Location/time:** the rope store above the chandlery, the yard, and the office of the Concord at the salt wharf, day 62, the Sunday, the thirty-seventh day of the new month.
- **Goal:** find out why the second list of eleven has not gone into a wooden case since the third week of the new month.
- **Resistance:** **the answer is that it is not a conspiracy and it is a box.** A case goes when a person goes, and eleven people did not go, and the case is a wooden box that a licensed carrier of Crown's second-table mail carries once a quarter, and **a case with nobody in it is a case nobody can charge to carry, and a carrier is paid by the person whose description is in it.** The two reasons are opposite and both are true: **a roll is the only thing in this world that makes a person findable**, and being on no roll is simultaneously the protection and the disappearance, and a person cannot have one without the other.
- **Turn:** **Nadia Brandt, twenty-nine, of the second list, works it out in about an hour and does not tell anybody for two days**, and then tells a woman of twenty-four and not the man, **and the reason she told the woman and not the man is entered and it is that she does not yet know whether she is complaining or asking.** And the two people on no roll at all — a girl of sixteen and a woman of thirty-eight — are named, and **neither has been asked, and the asking is a form with a date on it and about nine people signed it and about nine people did not.**
- **Consequence:** **the roll of strangers is entered for the sixteenth time with a page that says eleven people of the second list are on it and are not in the case, and a clerk at Aurel is going to have a page with a hole in it.** The girl of seventeen writes the sentence and it is nine words: *a hole in a page is not a smaller number, it is a place where a number used to be.* And Vask enters the finding, which is that **this city has spent sixty-one days protecting a set of people by describing them, and the describing is the whole of the protection, and there is no second thing.**
- **Power:** nothing gained. A page with a hole in it and a form with nine signatures on it.
- **Ending:** on the day book for the thirty-seventh day of the new month, in a narrow column, four lines high, in a girl of seventeen's hand, an entry that is not about the roll: **that a woman of twenty-nine worked a thing out in an hour and told a woman of twenty-four and not the man, and that the reason is entered, and that the reason is a sentence about whether she was complaining or asking.**

### Chapter 0067 — A Price on Good Faith
