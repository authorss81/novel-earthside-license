## HOW THE PROSE IN THIS VOLUME HAS BEEN FAILING, WITH THE FIGURES AND THEIR READINGS

**These are the four things the last ten chapters on disk do well and the four things that have been failing in
this manuscript for five volumes. The first four figures were measured on `chapters/volume-17/chapter-0841.md` to
`chapter-0850.md`; the reading is whitespace tokens with the heading line out, whole files, both orders.**

1. **The demonstrative was carrying the prose and now is not.** The last ten days run *that* at **4.5 uses per
   1,000 words** against **20.4 for Volume 17 whole**, **24.3 for its first forty days** and **30.6 for Volume 16
   whole.** *That store door* and *that bench* standing in for nouns the sentence already had is the habit. **Where
   a demonstrative is doing the work of a noun, cut it or give the noun. Where it is pointing at something the
   reader cannot see yet, keep it, and in these fifty days the bench and the bar and the trough have all been on a
   page already, so it can nearly always be named.**
2. **People were being re-introduced instead of referred to.** §17.9 requires every person who carries the volume
   to have a second handle beside the age and the trade, in every chapter that person is in, and it does not ask
   for the handle to be repeated. **Give each person the full handle from §7.2 on their first appearance in a
   chapter and let them be *he* or *she* for the rest of it.** Two handles are fixed strings and stand word for
   word wherever they stand — *the woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress* and *about
   four people a day* — and what you vary is the verb in front of the handle and the clause behind it.
3. **Dialogue goes missing and then a room's answer becomes a list.** **Every chapter of yours needs three or more
   marked speech paragraphs, and never more than four consecutive speech paragraphs between the same two people.**
   And a moment somebody says something that costs them has to be answered with what hands and bodies do, not with
   a roll-call of what nobody did. The motif stays; the catalogue goes.
4. **Ten chapters on one template read as one chapter ten times.** **Vary the separators, vary where the day and
   the hour sit, and let at least two of your ten close on something that arrives, dries, opens, is found out, is
   put down or is taken away rather than on an object standing still.** §17.17 caps closings that state nothing
   changed at two in any ten and §17.18 puts seven shapes off limits for a closing — the room's figure, *about
   four people a day*, the strip of ground, the bar, the sockets, a page that says a number, and **the space on that
   board, which is new in this volume and which you may not close on because it is not yours yet.**

**ONE INSTRUMENT, AND IT IS CHEAP.** Run the pair-against-pair run over your ten files before you finish, and
against Volume 17's fifty as well as inside your own ten, and reword what it finds. **The longest run of
