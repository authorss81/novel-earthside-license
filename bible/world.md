# World Bible: Veyra and the Two Sides

## 1. The shape of the world

Veyra is a dense, agrarian world with a medieval level of social organization and a much deeper history of magical law. The world is not a disposable simulation. Its people grow food, bury the dead, maintain roads, argue over inheritance, and change their customs when a crisis exposes an old compromise. The regions below are used as active pressure systems rather than as a checklist of scenery.

### The central river and Saltwake

**Saltwake** is a river city at the mouth of the **Vey**, where saltwater tides meet the inland current. Its locks, salt warehouses, and floating markets make it the first major crossing point for Earthside arrivals. The city is governed by the **Lantern Concord**, a civic council of dock families, craft guilds, healers, and public notaries. Saltwake is neither especially welcoming nor especially cruel; it is practical, crowded, and very good at remembering who owes whom.

The present breach opens at the **Lock of Salt**, a public tide gate where several jurisdictions already repeat the same maintenance oath. That repetition makes the lock a resonant site, even though the site was used by an earlier crossing centuries ago. A damaged opening there can move water, traffic, sound, and remembered events between nearby places.

### The Hearth Marches

The Hearth Marches are farming villages, orchards, and small market towns west of Saltwake. Their people maintain **Hearthguard** companies: local militia, cooks, herbalists, and roadkeepers who protect a settlement rather than a flag. The Marches are the first place where Earthside medical knowledge and Earth tools meet local seed, soil, and weather. They are generous to useful guests and hostile to anyone who assumes a local resource is free.

### Reed Reach

Reed Reach is a broad wetland where villages trade by canoe and maintain shared water gates. Its **Reed Commons** has no single ruler. Decisions are made at floating meeting-grounds where every household affected by a change must be heard. The Commons become a crucial counterweight to the Glass Crown and to player settlement schemes. Their version of law is practical rather than universal: a rule must leave the wetland usable by the next generation.

### The Glass Steppe

The Glass Steppe is a high, dry plateau scarred by ancient Anchorstones. The stones are not ordinary ruins; each is a place where a public law, natural cycle, or social promise was once held in common. At noon, the pale seams in the plateau throw hard light across the grass. At night, discarded routes sometimes appear in the same places as roads that have not been built.

The **First Room** lies beneath the Steppe in a network of buried chambers. It is not a wizard tower. It is a chamber built to let multiple communities keep a rule alive through disagreement. The current state of the Steppe is a visible consequence of the First Sundering: places that were promised different outcomes no longer agree on which outcome is physically present.

### Aurel and the Glass Crown

**Aurel**, the capital of the Glass Crown, is built on a white limestone stair above the upper Vey. Its courts keep a single official copy of inheritance, road, and Crown Law. Regent Solenne Dary rules through clerks and judges rather than open conquest. Aurel is wealthy, literate, and capable of genuine public works, but its central record system is vulnerable to the same problem as the First Grammar: the person who owns the official version can begin to own reality.

The Crown’s relationship to Earthside arrivals changes from curiosity to security crisis, then to constitutional conflict. The Regent is a recurring political opponent and, later, a reluctant co-signatory. She is not the final villain.

### The Cinder Coast

The Cinder Coast has salt ports, shipyards, and the first stable landing sites for repeated crossings. It becomes the diplomatic and commercial hinge between Veyra and Earth. Early arrivals treat the coast as a map pin; later communities treat it as a place with queues, families, tariffs, and the political burden of deciding who may enter.

### The Quiet Provinces

The Quiet Provinces are older than the Glass Crown. They contain monastery roads, abandoned witness courts, and villages whose names were removed from official records during the First Sundering. The Quiet Choir operates from hidden chapter houses here, but not every person in the region supports it. Some residents welcome a bridge that can restore lost names; others fear that outsiders will repeat the old erasure with newer paperwork.

## 2. What magic is

Veyran magic is called **Weave**. It is a practical discipline of changing accepted relationships among matter, place, memory, and witnesses. It is not mana and cannot create a person, food, or infinite energy from nothing.

Every Weaving has four parts:

1. **A pattern:** a real physical or social relationship already present, such as a road used by travelers, a seed planted by a farmer, a boundary watched by neighbors, or a promise made in public.
2. **A working:** a skilled action that changes, reinforces, reveals, or redirects the pattern.
3. **A witness:** a person or community that observes and accepts the change. A forced witness weakens the working and can rebound.
4. **A residue:** a consequence left in the world. The residue may be fatigue, injury, altered weather, a changed memory, a broken road, a debt, or a new local rule.

### The four common forms of Weave

- **Matter Weave:** bends heat, pressure, metal, stone, water, and living tissue. It is precise, physical, and dangerous to improvise.
- **Path Weave:** reads, stabilizes, or redirects routes, borders, messages, and thresholds. Path Weavers are local roadreaders and anchorwrights.
- **Witness Weave:** works through public speech, records, names, and shared memory. It is strongest where a community already recognizes a rule.
- **Life Weave:** tends bodies, crops, animals, and long-lived landscapes. It cannot cure every wound and cannot separate health from social conditions.

These forms overlap. A healing can require a medical diagnosis, a patient’s consent, a remembered promise, and a favorable room. A bridge can be a physical gate, a legal permission, and a path through memory.

### The rules that never change

- **Nothing is created from nothing.** A working rearranges what exists and pays a cost somewhere else.
- **Consent is not a moral decoration.** Consent changes the quality and direction of a working; refusal is a legitimate result, not an obstacle to be removed.
- **The place remembers.** Large workings leave a residue that can alter crops, weather, roads, dreams, or legal recognition. “Remembering” is a physical effect, not a claim that every ghost is intelligent.
- **A promise is material only when it is understood and accepted.** Coerced oaths are unstable and often rebound on the coercer.
- **A rule needs witnesses over time.** One person can start a working, but a durable rule requires continued public recognition.
- **Power does not erase consequences.** Increased capacity increases the size of the person, place, or institution that can be harmed.

### Local magic vocabulary

Veyrans say **weave**, **working**, **witness**, **residue**, and **join**. Earthside players initially use “magic,” “class,” “skill,” and “buff,” which are imperfect translations. The language changes throughout the story as Adrian learns the differences; the glossary in `terminology.md` marks terms that should not be casually interchanged.

## 3. The First Sundering and the First Grammar

Eight centuries ago, a river famine followed a failed treaty. The Crown attempted to repair the crisis by forcing a single official harvest record. The record was convenient, decisive, and false. The attempt to make one witnessed version suppress every contradictory version tore a seam through the world.

The **First Sundering** did not simply move mountains. It divided commitments:

- A village could be present in one valley and absent from the next valley’s record.
- A road could be used by people who could not remember who granted permission.
- A child, worker, or dissenter could be “unwritten” from a legal roll while remaining physically alive.
- A promise could survive as a ghost of an oath with no living person able to finish it.

The **First Grammar** was the emergency device built before the collapse. It was meant to let several communities keep a law coherent by recording what each community could witness. Its central rule was plural: no single witness could command the whole design.

After the Sundering, the device broke into Anchorstones. A hidden order called the **Quiet Choir** later gathered the fragments and reinterpreted the emergency tool as a controlled bridge. The Choir is not the original creator of the world’s magic and does not have an unbroken monopoly on it. It is a group of custodians, historians, and ambitious politicians who believe their version of order is the only way to prevent another catastrophe.

## 4. The First Landing and the current breach

Roughly three centuries ago, a fragment near the Saltwake lock opened a narrow crossing to the other side. A small number of Earthside people crossed without knowing they were entering a real world. Their technology decayed, their language changed, and their stories became the local **Bright Pilgrims** tradition. Later generations described them as divine refugees, ancestors, or monsters depending on the political need.

The present breach occurs when Adrian’s design work intersects with the same damaged Anchorstone and the Choir’s modified bridge. The Earthside interface appears because the ancient device needs a familiar rule-language to make a dangerous crossing survivable. Adrian’s mind supplies the vocabulary of permissions, classes, and quests even though he never consciously created the portal. The interface he is answering is a **fragment** of the Grammar rather than a recognized License, which is why it can speak to him before anything on the Veyran side is able to answer back.

The opening is initially unstable. It pulls a limited group of Earthside people, damages local water and memory, and leaves residue that can be felt for miles. The first civil goal is therefore not “reach level ten.” It is to establish who can cross, who must answer for the crossing, and whether the door can be closed without abandoning people already on either side.

## 5. The two societies

### Veyran life

Most Veyrans live within a few days’ walk of a river, road, shrine, or market. Their calendars follow the **Salt Year**, divided into flood, root, harvest, and ember seasons. Laws are taught through work: a child learns boundary stones before reading, a bridge keeper learns the names of flood years, and a healer learns which promises a patient has already broken.

The arrival of Earthside people disrupts ordinary systems immediately. A player who treats a ruined farm as a resource camp changes the local balance. A metal tool can save a life and also make a local craft obsolete. A healing spell can be affordable only if the patient accepts a public record. A player who dies leaves a body, a family, an economic loss, and a political question.

### Earthside life

The bridge affects real families, workplaces, laws, and governments. People disappear without conventional proof of death. Companies try to patent inter-world materials. States argue over whether the crossings are migration, trade, military intervention, or a public-health emergency. Families organize for information and return rights. Earthside governments do not instantly become fantasy villains; most are frightened by missing citizens, mass communication, novel materials, and the possibility that a person can return with a weapon or a contagious illness.

Adrian’s studio, **Morrowglass Interactive**, is not secretly responsible for the world. Its canceled historical-fantasy project supplied Adrian with the professional habit of designing permissions, but the bridge’s source is Veyran. This distinction prevents a familiar human conspiracy from replacing the original Veyran mystery.

## 7. Portal effects on Veyra

Every controlled threshold has five requirements:

1. A resonant place, such as a lock, boundary shrine, gate, or historically repeated road.
2. A named Earthside person who is the **recognized holder** of a **recognized License** — a fragment installed in a resonant site and witnessed locally, not merely a person who once accepted a fragment.
3. At least one willing Veyran witness.
4. A receiving community that understands and accepts the purpose.
5. A material cost such as opened salt, repaired stone, food, fuel, medicine, or a shared labor shift.

A **breach** is the failure state: a damaged Anchorstone tears open without a resonant site under anyone’s control, a willing Veyran witness, or a receiving agreement. The old maintenance oath makes the Lock of Salt a raw resonance, but no living Veyran has knowingly witnessed or received the crossing. A breach can pull an arrival because the rule is broken, which is why it also causes the heaviest immediate residue, memory bleed, and black-line scarring. It is not a valid passage and cannot be treated as a normal route.

Adrian’s Chapter 1 acceptance does not change this. He knowingly takes a name into an unanchored permission **fragment** and becomes a fragment-bearer, which makes him responsible for his own arrival and for what the fragment then does to other people. Responsibility is not recognition. The moment the fragment stops being unanchored — because a quorum installs it at the lock with witnesses and a receiving community that accepts the whole receiving area — it becomes a recognized License, and that is a later, earned event in Volume 1. The Volume 1 quorum route in Chapter 10 does not reach that bar: one receiving village refuses and the Lantern Concord has not ruled, so it is installed and posted but not recognized, and it cannot be used.

A controlled crossing can be delayed, narrowed, redirected, or closed by changing any of its requirements. It cannot be forced into existence simply because Adrian wants an item or an escape route.

### Reverberation

The immediate cost of a crossing is called **reverberation**. It can alter wind, water, seed growth, dream patterns, or the local sense of distance. Reverberation is not an abstract mana bill. A portal opened above a city may damage a bridge, flood a granary, make a bell ring at the wrong hour, or make two families remember the same room differently. A small passage can be safer than a large one, but even a small passage requires preparation and aftercare.

## 8. Ordinary-life anchors

The story must continue to show work that has nothing to do with combat:

- Saltwake clerks copy hand, repair locks, and argue over tide tables.
- Hearthguard cooks rotate grain into emergency stores.
- Reed Common speakers record who may use a water gate.
- Earthside medics learn which local medicines interact with modern assumptions.
- Players repair boots, bargain for grain, translate insults, and bury friends.
- The System’s prompts are most useful when they clarify a hard choice, never when they replace the conversation required to make it.

## 9. Continuity constraints

- Veyra is a real world with real inhabitants. Do not call it a dream, a game, or a disposable simulation after the first reveal.
- The First Grammar is the established historical mechanism. Do not add a second ancient god or a newer hidden simulation layer.
- The Quiet Choir and Ivenn Marrow are the final human antagonistic thread. New factions may arise, but no new final enemy replaces them.
- Earthside passage is bodily. Injury, death, pregnancy, age, and time delay are real consequences.
- A Veyran character may become Adrian’s ally without becoming a player, a template recipient, or a human quest-giver.

  - **THE MEETING-GROUND IS POLED ON THE FORTIETH DAY OF THE LONG MONTH, day 195, a Sunday, and the store is shut on it, and a question in four sentences is on its way up the Reach road in a woman's hand because she would not put it on a slate.** A meeting-ground is a raft with a roof on it, nine feet of standing room and a rail, it cannot be summoned, it has to be poled to where the people are, and **a date nobody chose is the only instrument it can be poled to.** Its own four-hundred-year-old rule is that every household affected by a change must be heard, **and it has a hole in it shaped exactly like a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house at Nine Elms, and the hole was found in Chapter 0167, was not filled on the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month, and that household is not mentioned once in Chapters 0171 to 0190.**
  - **A DATE IS THE ONLY THING IN THIS WORLD THAT TRAVELS ON ITS OWN,** said by a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf on the twentieth day of the Long Month, **and a board on the flats is a thing anybody can walk up to and a person is not, and a person is findable, and eleven miles of the Reach knew a date in chalk by the second hour of one Monday afternoon and nobody chose the eleven miles.**

  - **THE DAYS WENT BACK ON THE FORTY-FIRST DAY OF THE LONG MONTH, day 196, in daylight, on a flat at Redroot, with about nine people on it, and NOBODY ASKED THE HOUSEHOLD.** The household heard itself and spoke first. All nine days of a week went back, for the rest of the season, starting then. **The same household gave the days in a kitchen with a date in chalk on a door on the twenty-fourth of the Long Month and gave them back on a flat with a date in chalk on the rail of a board, in a fifth hand, and the instrument is not the door and not the rail.** The rota went up in one copy and was not copied, because the four copies on the upper road say nine days a week for a store and altering them would put a person nine miles away on a rota that is not the one on the board. *Gives the days back means the gate never went anywhere.   - **THE RACKS STAND IN THE CHANNEL AND THE CHANNEL TAKES ITS WATER FROM A GATE, AND A RACK THAT HAS NOT BEEN WORKED DOES NOT COME OFF.** The salt stopped on the forty-second day of the Long Month and **nothing was shut: there is no bar on the four-mile causeway and there is not going to be one, and a man of forty-two refused in front of about nine people to let anybody call it a bar, because a bar is a person standing in the way and there was not one.** About nine people had been putting that water down that channel on nine days a week since the seventh week of this flood. **The loss falls on the settlement and not on the Reach, because the ground under the causeway was taken in the seventh week without asking, the racks are his, the people on his rota in four hands are nine, and the salt is his until it is somebody's and it is nobody's — and that sentence is the reason nine people on a flat were able to do it in daylight.**
  - **ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY PEOPLE DRY SALT FOR A BUYER NINE HUNDRED MILES AWAY.** Nine are on the settlement's rota; the rest have never been on it and are paid in salt and a place at a fire. **There is no route in this world that reaches that buyer and no page that tells him anything, and the people who are not on the rota were not in either room on any of the seven days, and a channel with no water in it will have nothing in it that a rack can go into in about four weeks.**
  - **THE SIXTEENTH SHAPE, dated the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, day 200, a Friday, a land-and-labour compact between the Redroot Commons and the Reed Commons, eleven lines, local, read out in the local language in daylight in a room over a market with a date in chalk on the door.** A posting is bound to it with a date on it and a price under every line. **The ninth line is a person and it is *a person may be found*, and it is not the fifth line of the compact of eleven lines: the eleventh shape's fifth line is a guest who may ask to stop being a guest, and a document that can be ended by the asking of a person and a document that requires a person to be findable are not the same instrument and neither one is a copy of the other, and the compact of eleven lines is not ended and is not touched.** **The compact carries its own dead line: the fourth line cannot be administered, and it is in the compact in the words of the man of about thirty-nine who wrote it against it, with his name and the date on it, because the Commons' own rule requires every household affected by a change to be heard and a man who has argued against them since the seventh week of this flood, which is day 49 and a hundred and fifty-one days before this one, is a household affected by a change.** *Nobody has run it for a day. Nobody has tested the store's queue being on the same posting as everybody else's. Nobody in this city knows how an unbought right of refusal is administered except the man who says it cannot be.*
  - **A CORRECT NUMBER WENT ONTO A WALL IN A COLUMN WITH A DATE ON IT, in the room it was read in, in the ordinary course, in a hand that is not the hand that keeps the book.** It is one hundred and fifty-eight, counted one a day from the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else, and it was one hundred and fourteen on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one and eighty-nine on the thirty-ninth, and **forty-four days separate the hearing from the room and there are forty-four in the difference.** Nobody in four hundred miles knows what a day of it means and nobody can stop it. *There is one other line in the same book and it is not a number, and it has not been sent, and no office knows it exists, and the man keeping the number has still not mentioned it.*
  - **THE LAST IMAGE OF VOLUME 04 IS ONE THING.** Out on the flats the salt road is standing open and empty and the racks are drying.
