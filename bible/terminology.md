# Terminology and Naming Bible

Use these terms consistently. Local characters may use different words for the same practice, but a narrator or Adrian should not blur terms without making the difference part of the scene.

## Earthside terms

### Earthside

A person, place, document, or event from Earth. “Earthside” is the broad cultural term; it does not mean every Earth person shares the same values or government.

### Player

The Earthside name for a person who has crossed or been offered a License. The word is useful as a mistaken or temporary label but should not appear in an official Veyran document. When used, ask who is speaking and what they assume.

### License

The living permission mark that permits a named traveler to cross between worlds and receive limited translation. It is not a legal certificate issued by Earth and not a game account. The word became Adrian’s translation of the ancient **bond** because his design vocabulary was already shaping the interface. “License” in the strict sense means a **recognized License**; see the next three entries before using the word loosely.

### Fragment

A piece of the Grammar’s permission language that has separated from a resonant site. A fragment can take a name, leave a mark on a palm, and pull an arrival toward a damaged site. It cannot open a controlled threshold, cannot be aimed, and cannot be repeated on purpose. Chapter 1’s prompt is a fragment, which is why accepting it is Adrian’s own act and still not a legal crossing.

### Recognized License

A fragment installed in a resonant site, witnessed by at least one willing Veyran, and accepted by a receiving community. A recognized License has a named holder, a cost, a review, and someone able to suspend it. Establishing one is a public act, never a reward.

### Holder

A person recognized *by a recognized License* as its named holder. A **fragment-bearer** is someone who has accepted a fragment without a local witness; Adrian is a fragment-bearer from Chapter 1 until a local quorum gives him standing in Volume 1. Do not call him a holder, a bearer of the License, or a licensed player before that point.

Being a named holder is accountability, not power: it is the person the witnesses will come to when an opening goes wrong, and it carries the duty of inspection. Adrian becomes the named holder of the Volume 1 quorum route in Chapter 10 while he is still only a Witness. **Passage holder** is a different and later thing — the Stage 2 permission to open a threshold himself with two willing local witnesses. Never use the two interchangeably.

### Threshold

A temporary physical crossing. A threshold has a resonant site, a named holder, willing witnesses, a receiving community, and a cost. It is not a teleportation spell.

### Capacity

How many people one opening may carry, counted per opening and never per crowd. A breach grabs whoever is nearest. A single-holder threshold and a quorum route each carry one named living traveler per opening, and a quorum’s extra witnesses buy durability and legitimacy, not throughput. Only an inspected civic threshold moves a small posted queue, and a regional network moves many people across many separate openings. See the capacity table in `power-system.md`.

### Return mark

The anchor that lets a living traveler seek a return crossing. It is not a guaranteed button, an inherited ability, or protection from death.

### Workway / template

A permission profile that helps a traveler use one branch of Veyran Weave. Adrian sees templates as classes; Veyrans call them workways. A workway carries an obligation and a limit.

### Reverberation

The physical, social, or perceptual disturbance left by a threshold or large working. Reverberation is local and specific; it is not a generic mana cost.

## Veyran terms

### Veyra

The local world. **Veyran** is the adjective used by its people. The name is not a translation of Earth and predates the current crossing.

### Weave

The local practice of changing patterns among matter, path, witness, and life. “Magic” is a useful Earthside shorthand but hides the consent and residue rules.

### Working

A single act of Weave with a pattern, witness, and residue. A working may be a spell, a repair, a legal ritual, a healing, a road agreement, or a craft process.

### Witness

A person or community who observes, understands, and accepts a working. A witness is not automatically a victim or servant. A coerced witness weakens or reverses the result.

### Join / join-line

The perceptible relationship between a pattern, a place, and the people who repeat it. A roadreader can sense a join; a damaged join can produce a black scar or a false route.

### Anchorstone

A physical fragment or construction that holds a Crown Law, route, boundary, or public promise. Anchorstones are infrastructure and memory, not automatically magic weapons.

### Crown Law

A public rule recognized and repeated by a community or the Glass Crown. Crown Law can protect people and concentrate power. It is not the same as a natural law.

### The Hearthguard

Local village and road militias that protect homes, travelers, water, and local decisions. The Hearthguard has no single commander and can refuse the Glass Crown.

### The Reed Commons

A wetland network of villages that governs shared water through local assemblies. A Commons decision is binding only when the affected people recognize it.

### The Quiet Choir

A hidden order that collects and guards Anchorstone fragments. Its oldest members preserve plural witness; Ivenn Marrow’s faction has turned the order toward a Single Witness design.

### Single Witness

The Choir’s proposed answer to another Sundering: force one official version of a disputed law or history into the world. It is attractive because it prevents contradictory records and horrifying because it makes disagreement itself a target.

### The First Grammar

The ancient, partly broken device beneath the Glass Steppe. It was designed to let different communities witness a shared rule. The present License is a visible fragment of it, altered by the Quiet Choir and adapted to Earthside minds.

### The First Sundering

The historical catastrophe caused by forcing a single harvest record during a famine. It removed people, roads, and memories from one another’s versions of reality. It is the central historical wound behind the current political conflict.

### Bright Pilgrims

The local name for the earlier crossing from Earth. The tradition mixes genuine memories, religious stories, and political invention. Do not use it as a neutral historical term.

### The Unwritten

People, places, or obligations removed from a public record. The Unwritten may be living, missing, or legally invisible. “Unwritten” is a condition of the record, not a supernatural species.

## License and crossing states

### Resonant site

A place where a public rule or labor practice repeats often enough for a threshold to form. Examples: the Lock of Salt, a gate used by several jurisdictions, a treaty stone, or a road kept alive by daily maintenance.

### Breach

An involuntary threshold produced by a damaged Anchorstone or a failed permission. A breach has a raw resonant site but no site under local control, no willing Veyran witness, and no receiving agreement. It can pull people through because the rule is broken, always causes severe reverberation, and must be converted into a controlled threshold or closed. It is not a legal crossing. A breach is defined by missing local infrastructure, not by the absence of a knowing human: Adrian knowingly accepts a fragment in Chapter 1 and the crossing is still a breach.

### Black line

A thin precursor to a black scar: a place or person has registered a false promise, overload, or incomplete witness, but the route has not yet torn fully. A black line can be repaired or can deepen into a black scar if the underlying working is repeated or abandoned.

### Black scar

A corrupted join or route caused by forced consent, false promises, overload, or an abandoned working. A scar can close a crossing, displace a road, or mark a person. It is not a moral stain but a physical and political event.

Scar **where** and scar **in whom** must both be stated. The chapel stone takes a black line in Chapter 7; the outer threshold takes the first black scar **in a place** when Ivenn’s instructions overload the join in the black-scar chapters; Owen’s return mark takes the first black scar **in a person**, which is the first one that kills. Use those phrases rather than saying “the first black scar” on its own.

### Quorum

A minimum of independent willing witnesses required for a stable regional permission. The exact number changes with the working; the License never declares a universal number because context matters.

### Version lock

A permission that makes one version of a record override all other versions. The Choir calls it Single Witness; Adrian later calls it a version lock to describe its effect. A version lock does not make every person agree; it makes disagreement unable to produce a physical result.

### Public record

A record whose terms are visible to the people affected and can be challenged, witnessed, amended, or withdrawn. The opposing form is a sealed or single-author record, which may still be powerful but is not a public record even if a ruler calls it one.

## Usage rules

- “System” is Adrian’s and the player cohort’s term. Veyran characters use “the License,” “the bond,” or “the mark” according to their culture.
- “NPC,” “quest,” “spawn,” and “respawn” may appear in Earthside dialogue early, but should become embarrassing or inadequate terms as characters learn the real world.
- “Magic” is a broad Earthside word. Use “working” or “Weave” when the consent, witness, or residue rules matter.
- “God” is a local interpretation, not a confirmed objective fact. The story may show what people believe without confirming a divine creator.
- “History” is contested in Veyra. A document can preserve an account without proving that the account is the only version that happened.
- Names are meaningful and should not be casually translated. A System may offer a phonetic approximation, but Tamsin and other locals retain their own names in the record.
