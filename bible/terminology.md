# Terminology and Naming Bible

Use these terms consistently. Local characters may use different words for the same practice, but a narrator or Adrian should not blur terms without making the difference part of the scene.

## Earthside terms

### Earthside

A person, place, document, or event from Earth. “Earthside” is the broad cultural term; it does not mean every Earth person shares the same values or government.

### Player

The Earthside name for a person who has crossed or been offered a License. The word is useful as a mistaken or temporary label but should not appear in an official Veyran document. When used, ask who is speaking and what they assume.

### License

The living permission mark that permits a named traveler to cross between worlds and receive limited translation. It is not a legal certificate issued by Earth and not a game account. The word became Adrian’s translation of the ancient **bond** because his design vocabulary was already shaping the interface. “License” in the strict sense means a **recognized License**; see the next three entries before using the word loosely.

### Fragment

A piece of the Grammar’s permission language that has separated from a resonant site. A fragment can take a name, leave a mark on a palm, and pull an arrival toward a damaged site. It cannot open a controlled threshold, cannot be aimed, and cannot be repeated on purpose. Chapter 1’s prompt is a fragment, which is why accepting it is Adrian’s own act and still not a legal crossing.

### Recognized License

A fragment installed in a resonant site, witnessed by at least one willing Veyran, and accepted by a receiving community. A recognized License has a named holder, a cost, a review, and someone able to suspend it. Establishing one is a public act, never a reward.

Acceptance is the part that is easy to fake and must not be. A community that has agreed to part of the receiving area has not agreed to the whole, and a reduced receiving area is an **incomplete recognition**, not a smaller recognized License. The Volume 1 route in Chapter 10 is exactly this: site, holder, witnesses and cost are entered in the ledger, but one Reed village refuses and the Lantern Concord has not ruled, so the instrument is pending. A pending instrument can be inspected, argued over and costed; it cannot be used.

### Holder

A person recognized *by a recognized License* as its named holder. A **fragment-bearer** is someone who has accepted a fragment without a local witness; Adrian is a fragment-bearer from Chapter 1 until a local quorum gives him standing in Volume 1. Do not call him a holder, a bearer of the License, or a licensed player before that point.

Being a named holder is accountability, not power: it is the person the witnesses will come to when an opening goes wrong, and it carries the duty of inspection. Adrian stops being a fragment-bearer in Chapter 10, when a local quorum gives him standing, and he is the **named holder named in the ledger** of the Volume 1 quorum route from that chapter while he is still only a Witness. Standing and recognition are separate: because that route’s recognition is incomplete, the named-holder role there is a **recorded duty, not yet a License holding**. He becomes a holder of a recognized License only at the trial license in Chapters 48–50. **Passage holder** is a different and later thing — the Stage 2 permission to open a threshold himself with two willing local witnesses. Never use the two interchangeably.

### Threshold

A temporary physical crossing. A threshold has a resonant site, a named holder, willing witnesses, a receiving community, and a cost. It is not a teleportation spell.

### Capacity

How many people one opening may carry, counted per opening and never per crowd. A breach grabs whoever is nearest. A single-holder threshold and a quorum route each carry one named living traveler per opening, and a quorum’s extra witnesses buy durability and legitimacy, not throughput. Only an inspected civic threshold moves a small posted queue, and a regional network moves many people across many separate openings. See the capacity table in `power-system.md`.

### Return mark

The anchor that lets a living traveler seek a return crossing. It is not a guaranteed button, an inherited ability, or protection from death.

### Workway / template

A permission profile that helps a traveler use one branch of Veyran Weave. Adrian sees templates as classes; Veyrans call them workways. A workway carries an obligation and a limit.

### Reverberation

The physical, social, or perceptual disturbance left by a threshold or large working. Reverberation is local and specific; it is not a generic mana cost.

## Veyran terms

### Veyra

The local world. **Veyran** is the adjective used by its people. The name is not a translation of Earth and predates the current crossing.

### Weave

The local practice of changing patterns among matter, path, witness, and life. “Magic” is a useful Earthside shorthand but hides the consent and residue rules.

### Working

A single act of Weave with a pattern, witness, and residue. A working may be a spell, a repair, a legal ritual, a healing, a road agreement, or a craft process.

### Witness

A person or community who observes, understands, and accepts a working. A witness is not automatically a victim or servant. A coerced witness weakens or reverses the result.

Adrian’s Stage 1 in `power-system.md` is also called **Witness**. The stage and the person are different things and the prose should not blur them: a person witnesses a working, while a stage is what Adrian has earned. A local witness can refuse to recognize that Adrian has reached the stage. There is no “Witness mark” as a separate rank; the marks in this story are the fragment-mark on a palm, a return mark, and a black line.

### Join / join-line

The perceptible relationship between a pattern, a place, and the people who repeat it. A roadreader can sense a join; a damaged join can produce a black scar or a false route.

### Anchorstone

A physical fragment or construction that holds a Crown Law, route, boundary, or public promise. Anchorstones are infrastructure and memory, not automatically magic weapons.

### Crown Law

A public rule recognized and repeated by a community or the Glass Crown. Crown Law can protect people and concentrate power. It is not the same as a natural law.

### The Hearthguard

Local village and road militias that protect homes, travelers, water, and local decisions. The Hearthguard has no single commander and can refuse the Glass Crown.

### The Reed Commons

A wetland network of villages that governs shared water through local assemblies. A Commons decision is binding only when the affected people recognize it.

### The Quiet Choir

A hidden order that collects and guards Anchorstone fragments. Its oldest members preserve plural witness; Ivenn Marrow’s faction has turned the order toward a Single Witness design.

### Single Witness

The Choir’s proposed answer to another Sundering: force one official version of a disputed law or history into the world. It is attractive because it prevents contradictory records and horrifying because it makes disagreement itself a target.

### The First Grammar

The ancient, partly broken device beneath the Glass Steppe. It was designed to let different communities witness a shared rule. The present License is a visible fragment of it, altered by the Quiet Choir and adapted to Earthside minds.

### The First Sundering

The historical catastrophe caused by forcing a single harvest record during a famine. It removed people, roads, and memories from one another’s versions of reality. It is the central historical wound behind the current political conflict.

### Bright Pilgrims

The local name for the earlier crossing from Earth. The tradition mixes genuine memories, religious stories, and political invention. Do not use it as a neutral historical term.

### Skyborn

A dock-worker word for anyone who arrived out of the opening: literally “born of the sky.” In Saltwake it is first said as a joke and then hardened into suspicion, so it works as a label for how a crowd turns an accident into an accusation. It is local slang, not a species, not a religious rank, and not the same as the later Crown term “arrivant.” Use it the way the speaker uses it, and let characters notice when someone else uses it as a verdict.

### The Unwritten

People, places, or obligations removed from a public record. The Unwritten may be living, missing, or legally invisible. “Unwritten” is a condition of the record, not a supernatural species.

## License and crossing states

### Resonant site

A place where a public rule or labor practice repeats often enough for a threshold to form. Examples: the Lock of Salt, a gate used by several jurisdictions, a treaty stone, or a road kept alive by daily maintenance.

### Breach

An involuntary threshold produced by a damaged Anchorstone or a failed permission. A breach has a raw resonant site but no site under local control, no willing Veyran witness, and no receiving agreement. It can pull people through because the rule is broken, always causes severe reverberation, and must be converted into a controlled threshold or closed. It is not a legal crossing. A breach is defined by missing local infrastructure, not by the absence of a knowing human: Adrian knowingly accepts a fragment in Chapter 1 and the crossing is still a breach.

### Black line

A thin precursor to a black scar: a place or person has registered a false promise, overload, or incomplete witness, but the route has not yet torn fully. A black line can be repaired or can deepen into a black scar if the underlying working is repeated or abandoned. That describes the general possibility, not any one line: the chapel stone’s line is **not** repaired in Volume 1, and a line that has never been used to carry anyone has nothing spent on it.

Two lines are on the page in Volume 1 and they are **different objects**, so never merge them. The chapel stone’s line is a black line *in a place*, opened in Chapter 7 when Adrian made a promise on the arch that Tamsin refused to stand in, and it has never carried a body. Adrian’s is a black line *in a person*, opened in Chapter 8 in the heel of his palm, underneath the cut that ran along the pale fragment-mark when he spent four hours of borrowed join-reading and did not answer the voice. It is an overload warning, not a return mark, and it is exactly the same width in Chapter 10 as it was in Chapter 8. The two behave differently because the work that made them was different, which is the point Vask makes to him: the chapel line can be mended by work and money, and the palm line cannot, because nobody steps over a hand.

### Black scar

A corrupted join or route caused by forced consent, false promises, overload, or an abandoned working. A scar can close a crossing, displace a road, or mark a person. It is not a moral stain but a physical and political event.

Scar **where** and scar **in whom** must both be stated. The chapel stone takes a black line in Chapter 7; the outer threshold takes the first black scar **in a place** when Ivenn’s instructions overload the join in the black-scar chapters; Owen’s return mark takes the first black scar **in a person**, which is the first one that kills. Use those phrases rather than saying “the first black scar” on its own.

### Quorum

A minimum of independent willing witnesses required for a stable regional permission. The exact number changes with the working; the License never declares a universal number because context matters.

### Socket

A join that has been **closed on purpose** and has left behind the shape a door would go in. Vask introduces the word in Chapter 9 for the maintenance seam the outer tear left in the wall of the channel. A socket is not a door, is not openable, and has no standing of its own; it is the negative of a shape, waiting for a city that may never want a door there. The proof that Adrian's fragment-mark belongs to it is a **proportion**, not a size: the seam is eighteen inches long and three fingers wide, the mark on his palm is the size of a thumbnail, and every branch of the one is in the same ratio as the other, the way a coin fits the die it was struck from. State it that way. Never claim the mark and the seam are the same size.

### Narrow Mark

The name Vail enters in the Chapter 10 ledger for the Chapter 9 maintenance seam once it has been given terms. It is the name of an **instrument**, not of a person and not of a person’s rank, and Adrian is the instrument's named holder while holding no workway and no License. "Narrow" is doing real work in the word: the route carries one named living traveller per opening, needs a fresh inspection before any further use, and has a receiving community that may suspend it. The shape in the wall and the shape of Adrian's mark are the same, which is why both are called the same thing, and the reason Marrow tells him at the end of Chapter 10 that he will be "the narrow mark in the seam". Do not use the term before the Chapter 10 ledger entry, and do not let it drift into meaning a small person.

### Holder of the pull

The local, plain way of saying **holder**, from the bell-rope: the named holder is the person who agreed to pull, and the agreement is the whole of what makes the rope a rope. Sera Vail uses the phrase in the Chapter 10 ledger entry, and Chapter 11 turns on a local explaining what a **seat** is: a holder is the person other people can be angry at when a thing goes wrong, which is why four witnesses exist — they are the ones who have to be angry. A holder of the pull is not a Passage holder, not a Witness, and not a Licence-holder, and the three must stay distinct.

### Version lock

A permission that makes one version of a record override all other versions. The Choir calls it Single Witness; Adrian later calls it a version lock to describe its effect. A version lock does not make every person agree; it makes disagreement unable to produce a physical result.

### Public record

A record whose terms are visible to the people affected and can be challenged, witnessed, amended, or withdrawn. The opposing form is a sealed or single-author record, which may still be powerful but is not a public record even if a ruler calls it one.

## Usage rules

- “System” is Adrian’s and the player cohort’s term. Veyran characters use “the License,” “the bond,” or “the mark” according to their culture.
- “NPC,” “quest,” “spawn,” and “respawn” may appear in Earthside dialogue early, but should become embarrassing or inadequate terms as characters learn the real world.
- “Magic” is a broad Earthside word. Use “working” or “Weave” when the consent, witness, or residue rules matter.
- “God” is a local interpretation, not a confirmed objective fact. The story may show what people believe without confirming a divine creator.
- “History” is contested in Veyra. A document can preserve an account without proving that the account is the only version that happened.
- Names are meaningful and should not be casually translated. A System may offer a phonetic approximation, but Tamsin and other locals retain their own names in the record.

## Terms established in Volume 01 Chapters 11–20

These are on the page and are binding from Chapter 11 onward. Do not use them before the chapter that establishes them.

### Seat

The local, plain word for the holder's position, and it is **not an office** and **not a chair**. Nell Bray defines it in Chapter 11 in front of a hall: a seat is a place to be angry at; the four witnesses exist so that when a thing goes wrong there are four people who have to be angry and not one; and a seat has to be **legible to a person who does not like you**. Anything else is a chair. Use *seat* for the holder's position and *chair* for the thing Adrian wants, and never swap them.

### Crew

Renna Ord, Chapter 11: **a crew is a man who has owed somebody a shift.** Not a list, not a chain of command. A crew is a **chain of asking** — you ask a man, and if he comes you owe him, and if you do not pay you do not get to ask him again for nine days. The order in which people are asked is *known*, not written, and is older than any page. Ovin Sarr: an inspection is not a form, it is four people turning up in the wrong weather. A rota in Veyra is a set of people who have already agreed to stand in a place at an hour; a rota that has witnesses in it kills the thing it is meant to organise, which is a cost Chapters 18 and 19 depend on.

### Holding the pull / the post

*Holder of the pull* is the Chapter 10 bell-rope term and is unchanged. **The post**, named by Nyla Ferrow in Chapter 20, is a different thing: a place a person stands, as opposed to a place taken. A man whose mark is in a building's stone is not a visitor to it, and a visitor can leave. The post is not money, is not useful, and has no end of it in sight. Do not use *post* for the seat.

### Keeper's fifth word

The one syllable the old maintenance oath has had put into the middle of it since Chapter 8, which Tamsin renders in English as *answer*. It is **not in the oath**. It is the keeper's word — *we keep the third thing* — said at the arch every morning by Nyla Ferrow, her mother, and her mother's mother, and it is a *keeping*, never a *saying*. The voice in the lock join is using a form that combines the old maintenance oath with a keeper's word that has never been in the oath, and in Chapter 15 it repeats that word a half-second after the keeper says it. Never let it be called a fifth word of the oath.

### Unclosed

Hesper Saye's word, Chapter 14, for a wound with clean skin over it and nothing happening underneath: a thing with a witness on it that has not been taken up. Owen Park's scalp is unclosed. It is not the same as a black line and it is not the same as a black scar.

### A year of certainty

The Reed Reach measure of loss, Chapter 18. A household that cannot promise a gate-opening on a date does not keep its share of the water. A bottom field with level ditches has lost the thing a promise is made of, not the thing a crop is made of. Use this whenever a Veyran village's damage is described, and do not substitute "a bad harvest" for it.

### Marrow's chalk

Ivenn Marrow's own hand on the keeper's board, Chapter 16: an open offer of the Wayfinder workway, made on the second Saturday of the flood and standing until the last day of the salt month, which is day 25. An offer with a shape in it is a promise; an offer with no shape in it is a rumour. This is his own method turned against his own product and it is the only document in the batch that is his.

### The roll of strangers

The Lantern Concord's quarterly description of everybody in Saltwake who is not from it, sent to Aurel four times a year in a wooden case on a river boat. A public record, in the sense that anyone may ask to read it, and the one document in this volume that a Crown clerk will find without asking a question. Adrian is entered in it as **Unbound** in Chapter 16, on the Monday after the second Saturday, in the words *offered on the second day and again on the twelfth, and refused twice* — the two dates agreeing exactly with the chalk on the keeper's board.

## Terms established in Volume 01 Chapters 21–30

These are on the page and are binding from Chapter 21 onward.

### A black mark in a place
The local phrase for a place that is scarred, entered in the ledger on the twenty-sixth day of the flood. The first place in the history of Saltwake entered as damaged and not closable, and it is the dressed joint beneath the north flagstones of the lock chapel's yard. It is **not** the same object as the black line in the chapel stone at the foot of the arch, and Vask has entered that they do not behave the same way: a line in a place can be mended by work and money, a mark in a person cannot, because nobody steps over a hand. Say *a black mark in a place* and *a black line in a person* and never either without its article. The chapel stone's line is **not repaired in Volume 1** and carried twelve words of English in the open on the twenty-fifth day.

### Say it out loud again
The local phrase Tamsin Quill gives the Lantern Concord's first procedure, on the thirty-first day, and it is the name in the ledger. A keeper's phrase for what a keeper says when two witnesses disagree about a thing that happened at an arch: the keeper does not decide, the keeper makes both accounts hearable in a room and puts both in the book. The procedure is a **statement of error**. Never call it a challenge, a complaint, an appeal or an objection in a Veyran character's mouth.

### Statement of error
The Concord's standing correction, entered on the thirty-first day. Any person may enter, in the Concord's book, a statement of error against any instrument, entry, count or record, in their own words, with their name on it, and without giving a reason and without a cost. The person named must answer it in public, in a room, within a stated term, or withdraw it, or say publicly that they stand by it and can say no more. **No entry of this kind may be removed except by the person who entered it.** An instrument, entry, count or record against which a statement stands unanswered **may not be used for any purpose, be relied on by any person, or be carried to any court.** The holder of the pull may not enter one against anything on his own seat, nor may anyone in his household or whose standing derives from his.

### The statement of what a person said they saw
Adrian's form, invented in the loft on the twenty-fourth day, for a claim that cannot be tested. Nine clauses and a sixth clause written by Hesper Saye. Its standing clause reads: *this is not evidence that the thing was seen. It is a record that a person said it was seen, and the record obliges somebody to answer it, and it obliges nobody to believe it.* It may not be used as grounds for a search, a seizure, a price, a wage, a passage price, or any other act against a person or place; numbers in it are entered as *what the person said*; a person may strike their own name at any time; and it may not be performed by anybody who cannot speak the language of the room it is entered in. It has no seal, no authority and no precedent, and it says so at the top. Veyrans call it nothing; it is referred to in the room above the market as *the form*.

### A black mark in a person
Do not use. Adrian's thin black line in the heel of his palm is an **overload warning**, is thirty days old by the end of the batch, and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch through any of it. The first black scar **in a person** in the series is Owen Park's return mark in Chapter 38 and it kills him.

### A widening
The keeper's refusal to have a rota. The binding in the yard is said twice a day by whoever is nearest, with no name at the top, and the batch's repair was to teach the five words to as many people as will learn them so that the rule has more than one candidate. Vail's phrasing is that the thing is *a widening, not a rota*, and that a list of people with no shift attached to it is **an order**. Use *widening* in a Veyran character's mouth; reserve *rota* and *order* for the pair of things it is not.

## Rules established in Volume 01 Chapters 21–30

These are additions to the sections above, not replacements, and they are binding from Chapter 21 onward.

1. **A consent-less event cannot be blamed on the person nearest to it, and the person nearest to it is not the author of it.** Chapter 25 is the proof. A working at the Lock of Salt, performed with consent, at a cost, with four witnesses, by locals, on a known tide, **succeeded** and its own residue arrived under a chapel three hundred yards away, at a time nobody chose, and opened a site the working could not reach. The residue is the residue of a lawful act. Adrian's entry, Marrow's account in a yard, and the ledger all say the same thing and all three must keep saying it. The villain, if there is one, is not in that scene.
2. **A hold is a working without a witness, and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.** Mina Okoye, Chapter 25. A bystander who takes a child's weight off a moving stone in a resonant field has performed an unwitnessed working with himself as the instrument. It is the most ordinary thing a strong person does and it is exactly what a flood city does to itself daily. This is the volume's argument about consent in one sentence and it should be reused, not restated.
3. **A working that succeeds is not thereby safe, and a route that has been travelled is not thereby a road.** Both sentences are in the Registry's second letter and both are in the ledger entry of the twenty-eighth. Capacity has never been purchased by use. One opening, one named person, always, and a use in breach of terms is entered as a breach and is not a precedent.
4. **A debt that can be closed is a debt that can be traded out.** Adrian, Chapter 29. Not traded between people: closed by somebody else paying it, which turns an obligation into a favour, and a favour is a thing there is nobody left to be angry at. The general test for any instrument that can be discharged by a third party: *who is left with nothing to be angry at, and does the person who was owed still have a standing?* A debt that is continued, named, and reviewed is a relationship. A debt that is closed is a receipt.
5. **A record must be able to be wrong in public, and the person it names is the one who has to answer.** The statement of error is the first institution in the series that makes being wrong expensive and visible rather than fatal, and the holder of the seat is barred from using it. Any later volume's mechanism for dissent should be built out of this and not out of a new one.
6. **A document that is internally consistent and can only be checked by one person is not a record.** Sera Vail and Priya Shah, Chapter 28, independently. When accounts of the same thing disagree, enter the disagreements side by side with names on and reconcile nothing. The plural principle's first visible act in the series is a notary refusing to do her job.
7. **The interface's later constructions are the same shape.** **Three prompts in this batch, in Chapters 24, 27 and 29** — and **Chapter 22 carries none**, its four clauses being the Reed Reach's term sheet, a document. Every prompt is true, and every one places the cost in the grammatical position where a cost goes. Adrian has now read the *shape* of an offer rather than its content, which is the standing test the Bible requires and the thing the Choir is counting on. Do not let him pass one.
8. **The message channel is two-way and it cannot be stopped.** On the twenty-fifth day the chapel stone said twelve words of English in front of about three hundred and ten people, and not one word of it was understood by anybody in that yard except one man. It is the only time the stone has carried anything but the old form of the maintenance oath. The chapel stone is still the voice's only home; the main breach still does not speak; and a channel that is audible in public cannot be closed by anybody, which is the batch's answer to the question of whether the voice can be controlled. It cannot.

## Terms established in Volume 01 Chapters 31–40

These are on the page in Chapters 31–40 and are binding from Chapter 31 onward. Do not use them before the chapter that establishes them.

### A description is not a place
A rule from the twenty-fourth day, turned against its author on the thirty-seventh. A site named in a document by **what has been written about it** — *a dressed joint, described in the words of the mason and the anchorwright, entered in this book and not named* — may be true of more than one place, and this city has two dressed joints, and the second crossing of Volume 1 went to the one that had never been named. Use it whenever a local document identifies a site by description and a later writer needs the reader to feel the difference between the two.

### A silence in a yard is not a refusal
A third thing, and this city does not have a word for it. Entered by Sera Vail on the tenth day of the new month about the holder of the pull: *a silence entered in a yard is not a refusal and is not a consent, and is a third thing, and this city does not have a word for the third thing and has had four hundred years and could not be bothered.* The phrase is Vail's and is in the ledger.

### A man with a shoulder is a man who is not available
Adrian's, in a notebook, on the thirty-sixth, in nine words, after a day on which nobody in a city of eleven hundred people asked a man with an unset shoulder to do anything and he was so relieved he did not look at it. **Availability is the resource this city has been quietly redistributing for a month** and it is the resource the whole batch turns on. Use it in a local mouth only if the local would say it; in Adrian's mouth it is a designer's sentence about a man's body and that is the point.

### A record is not a fence
Nell Bray, docker, in a yard of about two hundred and ten people, on the fortieth day, against the holder of the pull and against a Hearthguard of nineteen years. A record tells you afterwards. A fence is there before. Entered as a statement of error against two things said in the same yard. **It is the volume's thesis in nine words and the next ten chapters have to answer it.**

### a man's name on a floor
The third line of the entry **Nyla Ferrow**, the keeper of that ground, wrote in her own hand on her own board at a man's request on the thirty-seventh day, four inches high: **it is a rule and it is a man's name on a floor.** The whole of the three lines is the keeper's, not a mason's — Ferrow wrote the first line and the second and this one in one go and took about two minutes and did not ask him to say it twice. It is the local phrase for the keeper's rule *the ground under this yard does not begin without Owen Park*, and it comes off on the Sunday, in daylight, and **the keeper washes it herself with a cloth and a bucket and reads out what comes off.** It is the exact opposite of a *seat* and it is the failure mode of every rule this book has made about people, and it is a phrase and not an epigram.

### A record is not a finding
Priya Shah's use of the standing clause of Adrian's own form, in a hall of about three hundred and fifty on the thirty-ninth, and the reason the entry of a death in the Concord's book is four pages long. A record obliges somebody to answer it and obliges nobody to believe it, and it is not a finding and it cannot be used as one, and therefore nobody may write *he died because*. Never call a statement of error a challenge, a complaint, an appeal or an objection in a Veyran character's mouth — that rule still holds — and note that a *finding* is a new word in this world and it is an English one and it is used by a woman from another world about her own document.

### The Reach's fourth line
Ilda Marsh's, forced through on the twenty-third day at the meeting-ground at Verrow over about nine days, and **exercised on the thirty-sixth by the woman who wrote it.** A village may refuse the standing charge, and on a village's refusal the whole of the charge returns to the private debtor. Hessa Ruun warned on the twenty-third that a concession is a document a Crown scholar reads and that a village which has refused twice can be brought to a meeting-ground about a bill; both halves of that came true in nine days, and the bill was brought against the man, not the village. The whole of the Reed Reach's season is now on a man from another world with no money, and the door is shut, and both of those are correct.

### Nineteen
A number nobody put on a board. On the fortieth day, in a market, four versions in six mouths: arrivals; stones on the upper road with writing nobody living can read; graves; and people who are going to go down. **The fourth is the one a city acts on.** Nineteen stones and eleven graves and eight of them without a name stone is Roke's and Sabra Vint's and is now a page in the book: nineteen of the shapes are the same and eight are not, and the eight are further down the road, and they are in a different order.

### The last column
The tide table in the lower gallery has one word in it for every tide of the salt year and the word is *works* or it is blank. Whoever holds the chalk that day writes it. **The word against the thirty-third is *works*, written on a Saturday morning by a docker with a barrow, in a hand that is neither his nor a clerk's, and the *k* is pressed too hard, and nobody has asked her what she decided.** The next tide is the forty-third day, a Tuesday, and its box is empty. The last act of Volume 1 is not going to be Adrian's and the book says so out loud.

## Terms established in Volume 01 Chapters 41–47

These are on the page in Chapters 41–47 and are binding from Chapter 41 onward. Do not use them before the chapter that establishes them. **The name Single Witness and the name First Grammar are not used in prose anywhere in this volume and must not be used in Chapters 48–50.**

### Standing
Tamsin Quill's one word, Chapter 0042, the seventeenth day of the new month, in a converted salt store with about two hundred and forty people in it, after she read the entry of the fortieth day aloud twice. **The gloss Adrian Vale wrote in a notebook is: *a thing that has a standing is a thing whose answer cannot be answered.*** It is in the **third line of the old form of the maintenance oath, which binds the gate and the standing of the man in it**, and that form was taken out of the oath in Saltwake **sixty years ago** after a man in it died — the standing was a man, the man could not be got, the gate did not open, about four hundred people were on the wrong side of a river in a flood month, and there is no stone anywhere and the list is in a building in Aurel. It is also in a Crown procedure of about two hundred years, in the fourth item of the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of the flood, in the fourth clause of the Crown's third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month, and in a hundred and thirty words a man in a good coat said in a yard. **It is not in one office and it is not in one office's coat.** Four generations of women keeping the lock chapel have said four words and a fifth for sixty years and **the fifth is not that word.** The keeper's fifth word is *answer* and the two must never be confused. **It is not Marrow's coinage and it is not a faction's name**, and the whole of Chapter 0047 is the argument that it is a thing that is a hundred and forty years older than a coat.

### The old form of the oath
The form that bound **the gate and the standing of the man in it**, stopped in Saltwake sixty years ago. It is on the page in full in Chapter 0042 for the first time, in Tamsin Quill's mouth, and about nine people in Saltwake can still say it and four of them were in that room and nobody asked them. It is the shape of every instrument this volume's antagonist proposes, with the words taken out: Marrow's private offer of the nineteenth day of the new month is the Crown's third instrument with the Crown removed and the *answer* clause left in, and it costs this city nothing at all. **A rule that names a man is a chain, and a chain is a thing you ask a man to hold by name, and on the thirty-seventh day of the flood a man held one in a yard in front of two hundred and forty people and four hours later he was under a floor.**

### A widening, applied to the lock
Ottoline Vask, Chapter 0047, in a yard, about nine seconds, entered over the ninth page of the instrument the next morning, and it is **her line and not the notary's and not his**: *it is the same shape as the five words in that yard; it is a widening and not a rota; and if a name goes at the top of it I will read it out in a yard.* The keeper's **widening** (Chapter 0021) and the lock's standing working are therefore the same kind of object: a labour with no name at the top. **A rota of the lock with a name at the top of it is the Brass Measure's fourth term and it is what Jonah Pike wanted and it is what the single-author containment is.** Say *widening* in a Veyran character's mouth; reserve *rota* and *order* for the pair of things it is not.

### A roll with no name at the top
The fifth of the six conditions, and the local form Renna Ord insisted on in Chapter 0046: forty names of the day and flood crews, **nobody's name above them, and a column of what it is for**, like the yard, like the five words. **A man who has not been paid does not come the next tide and there is no entry and no fault and nobody goes to anybody's door.** Sera Vail will strike out any name put at the top of it, exactly as she would on the yard's rota. Adrian Vale is **not on the roll of persons who may open the instrument**, at his own request, in his own hand, with the date on it, and **nobody whose standing comes from him may open, inspect, sign or suspend it.**

### The owing is the mechanism
Renna Ord, Chapter 0046, under the near gate, at about the seventh hour of the evening: **a crew is a man who has owed somebody a shift; you ask a man to come, and if he comes you owe him, and if you do not pay you do not get to ask him again for nine days.** Therefore **a thing nobody has asked for cannot be owed, and a thing that cannot be owed cannot be paid, and a thing that cannot be paid is a tax.** And the sentence that is the whole of the batch: **the owing is not the fairness, the owing is the mechanism.** Men do not stand under a gate at six because it is right; they stand under it at six because somebody owes them and they have not been paid yet. **This is why the crew refused the first version and accepted the second, and it is Renna Ord's rule and not Adrian's and not a mason's.**

### The price of a door
Renna Ord, Chapter 0045, the walk-round, in the wrong weather: **the price of a door in this city is the crew and the crew is the wall**, and **a wall cannot be a price for very long** (Adrian, Chapter 0047, in a hall). The named price is: nineteen men of the day crew and twenty-one of the flood crew, twice a day, at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, on every tide of the flood season and once a month after; **about four of the forty will be hurt before March, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, and it will not be in a working, and no instrument in this world can stop it**; four named persons inspect before every tide at a falling tide with a gauge, in whatever weather there is, and if the gauge reads what it read on the twenty-seventh day of this flood the tide is not worked; and **the day the bottom field is not paid in March, this stops, and the join stops being worked, and the join will be where it is.** And: **you do not get a door that is not somebody's field.**

### Nobody can open it
The condition that makes the standing working plural, Chapter 0045: there is **no opening in it, no hour, and no man in it whose business it is**; **forty men turn up or it does not happen**; the only way anybody in Saltwake can stop it is **nine named persons saying one word, one of whom is named by herself**; and the result is a **door with no handle in it**. **A person can be stopped from doing a thing; a process cannot be stopped in a yard, it can only be filled in** — which is why the Chapter 0046 field is the most dangerous of the sixteen and is refused in under a second.

### The toll and the pattern
Ottoline Vask, Chapter 0045, the walk-round: **the forty words under the near gate are the toll and they are the pattern, and a city cannot spend a thing and be its own foundation at the same time.** Three jurisdictions repeat the same maintenance oath at that lock, the arch is where three jurisdictions meet, and that is the whole reason the place is a resonant site. Take the words out and there is a river with a gate in it. Leave them in and there is the ground of a city. **The cost of them is not a season and never will have a figure: it is the year of certainty gone out of a hundred and forty acres, and the four villages whose gates cannot be promised are four miles away and are not in the gallery.** Any later chapter that proposes a working built on the lock's own ordinary labour must carry this objection and must not resolve it.

### The forty-ninth day
The day the working is on the board for. It is a **Monday**, it is the next tide, and the box in the last column of the tide table is empty against it. It is also the **tenth day of eleven** on the loft wall rota. The post's final term ends on it. **Nothing is performed in Chapters 41–47 and nothing may be performed before it in Chapters 48–50.**

### A pattern is not a hole
Repeated from Chapter 0025 and now load-bearing for the whole closing sequence: **a floor that has come open once under three hundred and ten people is not a pattern, it is a hole**, and **a quarter of a thing is not a thing**, and **a description is not a place**. The three together are why the closing working can be the ordinary work of a lock and cannot be a new rite, and why the site of it is described by what is at it and not by what has been written about it.

### A man is a scar in a person
Owen Park's own sentence, the eleventh item of the four-page account he dictated on the thirty-eighth day of the flood, read at his graveside on the sixteenth day of the new month and said out loud by **Hettis Roke**, with **Ottoline Vask** supplying the mason's reason: **a line in a place can be mended with work and money and a mark in a person cannot, because nobody steps over a hand.** It is the sentence the volume inherits and it is never to be turned into a lesson, a consolation, or a rule about anybody else.
