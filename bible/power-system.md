# Power System: The Earthside License and Veyran Weave

## 1. The two interacting systems

Veyra already has magic before Adrian arrives. Its practitioners use **Weave**, a consent-sensitive discipline that changes patterns in the world. The **Earthside License** is a separate interface laid over that world by a fragment of the ancient **First Grammar**. It does not replace Weave. It translates, limits, and records a crossing so that a person from Earth can survive contact with it.

The distinction matters throughout the story:

- **Weave** is local, embodied, relational, and shaped by history.
- **The License** is cross-world, mechanical in its effects, limited by physical anchors, and capable of being edited.
- A player can receive a template from the License and still need years of local practice to use it.
- A Veyran can be a powerful Weaver without ever receiving a template or seeing a System panel.

## 2. What the License can do

The License is a living permission record. It can do the following, and nothing beyond this without a new established rule:

1. **Mark a traveler:** identify a person’s chosen name, body, origin, and return mark so the crossing can recognize them.
2. **Translate a declared act:** turn a direct promise into a Veyran working, such as opening a threshold, identifying a route, or repairing a damaged join.
3. **Show local permission:** display whether a place, person, or object accepts, refuses, or cannot safely receive a working.
4. **Offer a workway:** provide a class-like template that narrows a traveler’s possible magic and states its obligations.
5. **Limit passage:** set a duration, destination, number of people, and condition for a threshold.
6. **Preserve a return mark:** allow a living traveler to seek the return crossing if the mark and the receiving community still consent.

The License does not create a body, resurrect a dead person, grant combat skill, store unlimited items, read thoughts, compel obedience, erase memory on command, or make a person invincible. It cannot safely make a destination that has no local witness.

## 3. What the interface looks like

The License appears as a pale line of text or a small rectangular field when Adrian looks at a mark he understands, speaks a direct promise, or enters a dangerous threshold. It usually gives one prompt, one consequence, and one choice. It never prints a large skill table.

Typical prompts use the language of obligations rather than game mechanics:

> A door needs two willing names: one to leave, one to receive. If you close it from the far side, the person you leave behind keeps the scar.

> This working can change the road, not the history of the road. Choose a new route or accept the old loss.

> You may give one person a workway. The person must be told the cost before accepting.

A panel may be helpful, incomplete, or deliberately misleading. Ivenn’s altered prompts tend to omit a social cost while displaying a clear reward. Adrian and Tamsin learn to read every offer for three things: who is named, who pays, and who can refuse.

The System uses familiar Earthside words because Adrian thinks in those words. Veyrans may call the same thing a **bond**, **crossing**, or **working**, and they do not see a floating panel. The same permission can therefore look like a quest to Adrian and like a dangerous public contract to Tamsin.

## 4. Threshold rules

A **threshold** is a temporary crossing between Earth and Veyra. It is a physical event with social infrastructure, not a personal teleport.

### The three words that must not be blurred

- **Fragment:** a piece of the Grammar’s permission language that has separated from a resonant site. A fragment can take a name, leave a mark on a palm, and pull an arrival toward a damaged site. It has no local anchor, no witness, and no receiving community, so it cannot open a controlled threshold and cannot be aimed, scheduled, or repeated. A fragment is what the first breach is built out of.
- **Recognized License:** a fragment that has been installed in a resonant site, witnessed by at least one willing Veyran, and **fully** accepted by a receiving community. A recognized License is a standing permission with a named holder, a cost, and a review. Only this can hold a route open on purpose. If the receiving community accepts only part of the receiving area, or the local authority has not ruled, the recognition is **incomplete**: the terms can be entered, posted and inspected, and the instrument still cannot be used. Volume 1’s Chapter 10 route is in that state until the trial license in Chapters 48–50.
- **License holder:** a person recognized *by a recognized License* as its named holder. Accepting a fragment does not make someone a holder. Adrian is a **fragment-bearer** from Chapter 1 until a local quorum gives him standing in Volume 1; the narrator must not call him a holder before that.

The distinction is the moral engine of the opening. Adrian does something real and knowing in Chapter 1 — he accepts a fragment and puts his legal name into it. That makes him responsible for his own arrival. It does not make the crossing legal.

### Entry conditions

Every **controlled** threshold requires:

- A resonant place: a lock, gate, boundary shrine, old road, or place where a public law is regularly repeated
- A named Earthside holder
- At least one willing Veyran witness
- A receiving community that understands the purpose
- A material cost such as opened salt, repaired stone, food, fuel, medicine, or a shared labor shift

A **breach** is the exception created by a damaged Anchorstone, not a loophole Adrian can use. What a breach lacks is not a human intention — it is the local infrastructure. There is no resonant site under anyone’s control, no willing Veyran witness, and no receiving agreement, so a fragment in the breach can only grab at whoever is nearest. A breach can pull an arrival because the rule has failed. It cannot be opened on purpose, it always leaves severe residue, and the first task is to turn it into a controlled threshold or close it.

### Passage limits and capacity

Capacity is always counted per opening, never per crowd. The table below is the whole capacity system; any number in the prose must match one of these rows.

| Kind of opening | Who can pass in one opening | How often it can be opened | What raises the number |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breach** (damaged site) | Nobody chooses. It grabs at most the few people nearest the opening, and cannot be aimed. | Once. A breach either tears wider or is closed. | Nothing. This is why it is a catastrophe. |
| **Single-holder threshold** (Stage 2 onward) | One named living traveler, plus only what that traveler can carry. | Only after a new anchor or a new local agreement; the same route cannot be opened twice in a row by one holder. | A new anchor and new witnesses, which is how Volume 2 improves a route at all. |
| **Quorum route** (the Volume 1 trial) | One named living traveler per opening, carrying a return mark. | Sequentially, one opening at a time, and only after a fresh inspection of the seam by the witnessing group. | Witness count buys durability and legitimacy, never throughput. Ten witnesses still move one person. |
| **Inspected civic threshold** (later volumes) | A small queue, set by local inspection and posted publicly, counted as people per opening. | A posted schedule tied to tide, labor shift, and inspection. | Construction, political agreement, and an agreed schedule — the only route that ever moves more than one person at a time. |
| **Regional network** (Stage 3 onward) | A whole network moves a whole volume’s worth of people **across many separate openings**. A single opening still moves a single opening’s worth. | Whatever the weakest anchor can sustain, which the story shows as a failing network rather than a bigger door. | Independent anchors added by other communities, never by Adrian alone. |

The first crossing carries Adrian alone. The first failed expansion carries six more because the breach is grabbing, not transporting. No opening in the series ever carries a crowd at once; when the story needs dozens of people moving, it shows dozens of inspected openings, a queue, a ledger, and a labor schedule.

A passage is limited by the weakest of its anchors, not by Adrian’s desire.

The following are hard limits:

- A dead traveler cannot return.
- A missing or broken return mark can be repaired only through a new witnessed act, not automatically.
- A threshold can be closed from either side, and a person on the wrong side may be stranded.
- Travel through a closed or corrupted route causes body injury and local residue; it is not a clean reset.
- Earthside time and Veyran time are not identical. Most early crossings differ by hours, but later events can move the return date by days or weeks.
- The License can carry living people, ordinary cargo, and carefully limited records. It cannot safely carry an unlimited army, a contagious substance, or a forced prisoner.

### Reverberation

Every opening causes **reverberation** in the local world. The cost is concrete and local:

- water shifts and a nearby lock loses pressure
- crops germinate at the wrong time
- bells, songs, or memories repeat out of order
- a road is briefly visible where no road exists
- animals flee or become aggressive
- a person near the site forgets a short but important interval

A passage can be physically successful and socially disastrous. A village may vote to close a bridge because the bridge keeps working but the surrounding fields no longer produce stable harvests.

## 5. Return marks and failure

A return mark is a physical and social anchor, not a button labeled “return.” It may be worn as a mark, carried as an iron token, or established through a place-bound ritual. The mark contains the traveler’s chosen identity, not their complete history. Each mark belongs to one named living person; a route never lends its mark to a group. A person without a mark cannot cross through a controlled threshold unless the receiving side first establishes a temporary mark through a public working.

A quorum route carries one named living traveler per opening, and the number of witnesses standing behind it never changes that. Passages are sequential, never simultaneous, and each opening requires the witnesses to confirm the traveler’s name, destination, medical condition, and right to refuse. The route can be used again only after a new inspection and local agreement. This limit prevents a single mark from becoming a shared ticket.

If the receiving side refuses future passage, the mark becomes difficult to use. If the route is broken while open, the named traveler is the person most likely to be stranded; the other travelers are not automatically dragged through. A short written message can be carried through an anchored token, but it consumes the same one-person capacity and is not a safe substitute for a return.

### Failure modes

- **Wrong witness:** If a working is performed without a genuinely willing witness, the residue rebounds. A forced bridge can fold travelers into the nearest remembered route, produce a body at the wrong age, or leave a person unable to remember a destination.
- **False promise:** If a person signs a permission they do not understand, the mark develops a black line. Repeated abuse can create a **black scar** on a route or a person.
- **Overload:** If too many permissions share one anchor, the route tears. The local place may lose a room, a boundary, a family relationship, or a named season.
- **Unclosed working:** A person who accepts a duty and disappears transfers part of the cost to the people who remain. The world experiences this as broken roads, strange inheritance, or a public oath that points at the wrong heir.
- **Death:** A dead traveler’s permission does not transfer automatically. If the return mark is still active, it may attract attention from people who want to use it; if it is broken, the route becomes a black scar until repaired by witnesses.
- **False System prompt:** A corrupted prompt can lead a player into a working that appears beneficial and is socially destructive. The person still chooses; responsibility does not vanish because the wording was false.

The story should show the aftermath of failure, not merely announce a penalty. A failed crossing may require a funeral, a repaired lock, a changed harvest, a public apology, or a community vote.

## 6. Workways and class templates

The License calls its workways **templates** because Adrian’s mental model needs that word. Veyrans call them **workways**. A template is a permission profile, not a complete skill tree.

| Earthside display | Veyran meaning | Benefit | Limit and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wayfinder** | roadright / path reader | Sense joins, read a route’s recent choices, find a safe passage through difficult terrain | Must personally walk or bear witness to a route; cannot teleport; repeated route work taxes memory and balance |
| **Hearthkeeper** | shelter-weaver | Share basic recovery, stabilize water and shelter, recognize household needs | Cannot impose treatment; must accept the patient’s consent; leaves a residue of the patient’s strongest fear or memory |
| **Artificer** | material speaker | Repair tools, work metal, build anchors, make compatible parts | Materials are not conjured; each work records a debt to material, maker, and user; a failed object can injure its maker |
| **Listener** | oathreader | Hear changes in names, contracts, and spoken intent | Cannot know unexpressed thoughts; prolonged use makes ordinary lies difficult to distinguish from truth; social isolation is common |
| **Courier** | threshold runner | Move quickly between marked crossings and carry urgent messages | Cannot conceal a living passenger or undeclared magical cargo; exhaustion and exposure accumulate |
| **Warden** | groundkeeper | Reinforce a claimed place, organize defense, resist involuntary displacement | Must defend the place and its residents; abandoning a claimed ground causes a physical and social backlash |
| **Scribe** | witness-copyist | Translate local law, preserve a disputed record, make consent legible | Cannot sign on another person’s behalf; forged or incomplete copies create dangerous absences |
| **Unbound** | no named workway | Move between specialties and avoid one template’s fixed obligation | Has no special protection and must learn each practice from local teachers; the most dangerous route for a desperate traveler |

Templates are learned through practice, failure, and relationships. A player cannot collect every template and use them simultaneously. Choosing a workway is choosing a social role that other people can reasonably expect the holder to perform.

## 7. Adrian’s power stages

The stages below are narrative milestones, not levels displayed as numbers. Each stage adds options and responsibility.

### Stage 0: Unlicensed body

Adrian can see an occasional pale prompt and perceives harsh light in a join when he is injured or frightened. He has no stable template and no reliable return. He can survive by observation, tools, negotiation, and the first people who help him.

**Cost:** ordinary injury, exhaustion, language loss, and immediate social suspicion. A wrong choice can kill him without a dramatic warning.

### Stage 1: Witness

Adrian can perceive the nearest join-line, understand one direct Veyran promise at a time, and mark a safe route for himself. He does not create a working; he can only make an existing pattern visible enough for others to act on.

**Earned through:** helping the Lock of Salt during the first flood, accepting a public debt to a place he cannot clear by himself, and failing in front of witnesses to close the outer tear alone. All three must happen. Until the debt and the failure, he has a **provisional join-reading** — he can follow one join-line for as long as somebody else is holding the working, and it leaves a headache and a nosebleed. A provisional reading is not a rank, carries no duty, and cannot be refused by a local witness because it was never granted to him by one.

**Cost:** headaches, sensory bleed, and a responsibility to the place he has witnessed. A black scar can attach to him after a failed working.

### Stage 2: Passage holder

With two willing local witnesses and a named destination, Adrian can open a single-holder threshold: one named living traveler per opening, carrying only what that traveler can hold. He can carry one return mark and one basic workway template. This stage is **not reached at the end of Volume 01**; the first trial route is operated collectively, and Adrian remains a Witness until later practice, local trust, and a failed return earn him the Passage permission.

**Earned through:** a later controlled rescue, Tamsin’s and Mina’s trust, and the sacrifice of an easy return route.

**Cost:** each opening causes reverberation and physical strain. A failed passage can injure another person. Adrian cannot open the same route twice without a new anchor or a new local agreement.

### Stage 3: Coven steward

Adrian can connect several independent witness marks into a small network, translate consent for a shared work, and help a willing player choose a template. He can repair a damaged threshold with a team, but cannot command the team.

**Earned through:** the first settlements, the White Ledger conflict, and a public failure that forces him to share authority with Tamsin, Mina, Dante, and local leaders.

**Cost:** Adrian becomes responsible for the network’s weakest link. A remote failure can send a shock, a false quest, or a black scar back to him. He must disclose the network’s limits.

### Stage 4: Rule speaker

Adrian can translate between Earthside and Veyran legal language, read a Crown Law’s history, and propose a public correction to a disputed record. He can open a regional network of thresholds only when multiple local witnesses agree.

**Earned through:** the Aurel hearings, the Glass Steppe expedition, the Unwritten Coast campaign, and the collapse of a region he had tried to govern centrally.

**Cost:** conflicting records and memories enter him. Every major correction can cost a personal memory, a relationship, or a physical scar. The more authoritative he becomes, the more people use his name as a weapon.

### Stage 5: Bridgewright

Adrian can work with independent bridges, protect a distributed network from a hostile permission, and call a shared emergency crossing. He cannot create a bridge from nothing, compel a community, or defeat an army by opening a gate.

**Earned through:** the First Room expedition, the Bridge War, and his decision to obey local evacuation plans rather than impose his own.

**Cost:** he loses his personal return mark and suffers identity bleed. He becomes physically vulnerable when the network fails. His power is relational: the bridge is only as strong as the people willing to keep it open.

This is the **one and only time** Adrian gives up a personal return mark. From this volume onward he has no mark of his own: every time he crosses, in either direction, other witnesses must agree to carry him. Later sacrifices are not repetitions of this one; see Stages 6 and 7.

### Stage 6: First Anchor

Adrian can expose the First Grammar’s single-authority patch and help distribute its permissions among independent seals. He can stop a forced version lock for a time, but cannot replace the device with a new central control system.

**Earned through:** refusing to reset the First Room, losing the Ivenn contest, and accepting that the old system must be dismantled rather than mastered.

**Cost:** permanent loss of solo access, a significant personal memory, a lasting bodily injury, and the knowledge that the bridge may fail without him. He cannot return to Earth alone — and since Stage 5 he could not return alone in any case, because he no longer holds a personal return mark. What he surrenders here is the **recognized seat of first holder**: the standing recognition that lets one name answer for the Grammar’s permissions.

### Stage 7: Concord keeper

There is no higher personal stage. Adrian can act only through a quorum of independent witnesses. His final authority is the ability to facilitate a shared decision, not to overrule it.

**Earned through:** public confession, surrendered admin access, and the final defense built from dozens of local and Earthside choices.

**Cost:** he gives up the title “first licensed player” as a power and accepts ordinary legal status on both sides. The bridge remains open because other people accept duties, not because he remains essential.

## 8. Progression discipline

Growth must be shown through specific scenes:

- Adrian trains his body and pays a practitioner for correction.
- He practices a workway with a local teacher and is refused a shortcut.
- He fails to save someone because he did not ask the right witness.
- He loses a route, a memory, a legal right, or a comfortable relationship.
- He changes the design of a network after listening to a person he previously treated as a variable.
- He gives a power or permission to someone else and loses convenience.

No chapter should solve a major problem with a newly unlocked ability that has not appeared in practice. If Adrian solves a problem early, the solution must depend on observation, relationships, materials, or an established cost.

## 9. System-message rules for prose

- Use no more than one short System panel in an ordinary chapter.
- The panel must state an immediate rule, warning, or choice in plain language.
- A panel never explains the entire world or announces a reward for good behavior.
- Characters may ignore, misunderstand, or argue with a panel.
- The narrator may render a prompt as a pale field, a feeling, or a line of text, but it remains a bounded interface.
- The System may be wrong, particularly when Ivenn’s patch is active. Adrian must verify important permissions with a human witness and physical evidence.

## 10. Rules established in Volume 01 Chapters 11–20

These are additions to the sections above, not replacements. They are on the page and binding from Chapter 11 onward.

1. **A workway displaces a duty, and the displaced duty belongs to somebody who is not in the room.** Marrow states the cost in Chapter 16: a wayfinder must personally walk or bear witness to a route; repeated route work taxes memory and balance and takes the road's name into the place where you keep your name; and the limitation is an **erosion**, not a punishment — nobody comes and tells you about it. Critically, the obligation and the temptation are the same object: a workway's holder cannot refuse to carry a road, which is why a wayfinder is the obvious person to be asked to move a boundary stone, and why the Chapter 7 offer was made to Adrian and not to the anchorwright. **Noor Haddad's question — *who does the job?* — is the standing local test of any proposed workway**, and it means an offer must always be costed against the labour it displaces before it is costed against its holder.

2. **A permission has no state.** The Chapter 7 and Chapter 16 offers both end *it is not yet accepted*, ten days and two refusals apart. There is no record of a refusal anywhere in it. Anything built on the assumption that a permission is a thing that can be accepted, refused, and then recalled is built on a thing that does not exist. Adrian writes this down in Chapter 16 and does not know what it means.

3. **The interface's costs are placed, not stated.** The dangerous construction in this volume is not an outright lie. It is a true-sounding sentence in the grammatical position where a cost would go, arranged so that a man stops looking for one: *the cost has been paid, there is nothing further to pay* (Chapter 10), *the cost of the stone is not part of the workway* (Chapter 16), *a holder who does not come may be replaced at the next sitting* (Chapter 11, which is also stronger than what the ledger says). Read every offer for three things — who is named, who pays, and who can refuse — and check each answer against a **written local record**, never against the field.

4. **A holder may be shown a thing and thereby be made its only record.** The Chapter 15 offer — *a holder may be shown which one the water is using* — is true, useful, and refused, because it would leave the answer in one man with no page, no second pair of eyes, and no argument available afterward. A singleton is a failure mode, not a shortcut. The rule generalises: **any permission whose whole content is knowledge, rather than a working, is a permission to stop being a witness.**

5. **The interface's offers stop.** Adrian stops looking for the field on the **Friday night of day 18 of the flood, in Chapter 19**, and the scene that stops him is a man saying the word *quest* out loud for a thing in a wall. He tells the room not to use the word and writes the date down. The interface has said nothing in Chapters 13, 14, 17, 18, 19 or 20 — including at the moment ninety people ask in public what the Narrow Mark is for. Its silences are information and a writer should use them: the panel is loudest when it wants something, it is absent when a public act is being performed, and, so far, it has never once come out of the main breach. Two cautions for later batches. First, the moment a character stops expecting the interface **must be dramatised, not recalled in a notebook** — a retrospective entry is not the same beat and the state files will be believed. Second, a promise may only be made once: the word *quest* has now been spent, having moved from Adrian's private vocabulary in Chapter 5, to Jonah Pike's mouth in Chapter 19, to a refusal on Adrian's own page in Chapter 20.

6. **A local record is only as good as the people who can read it, and the people who can read it are the people who can act on it.** This is why the Chapter 12 leak was not a leak, and why the only durable line on that market board is a door rather than a reassurance. Where a record has to travel, ask who carries it. Peth Lammay's question in Chapter 13 is the standing test: does the community the document is *about* know the document exists, and in what hand.

7. **A written promise about a public place is stronger than a spoken one, but only if it is a promise.** A cost that can be paid once is a fee. A cost that is a labour, paid daily, by whoever is nearest, with nobody's name at the top, is a promise — and the keeper of a room can tell the difference on sight. This is why Nyla Ferrow's price for opening the ground is a rota and not a sum, and why Vail will strike out any name put at the top of it.

## 11. Rules established in Volume 01 Chapters 21–30

These are additions to the sections above, not replacements, and they are on the page and binding from Chapter 21 onward.

1. **A successful working's residue can reach a site the working could not touch, and the site need not be damaged to be opened.** Chapter 25. The working was performed at the Lock of Salt, a resonant site, with consent, at a cost, by locals, at a known tide, and it did exactly what it was set to do: the pale edge of the main breach stepped nine inches toward the near gate, in the direction of the stone that was not holding anything. Its residue — water, pressure, attention — travelled down under a bank and arrived at an older damaged threshold beneath a chapel floor on the same river, and opened it. **The distance between a working and its residue is not bounded by anything the performers can see.** Any later working whose cost is entered as a season, a salt measure, or a day's labour has to be costed for where the residue lands, and a cost column that names only the field is incomplete.

2. **A breach is defined by missing infrastructure, and the flagstone rule is the local test.** On the twenty-fifth day, nineteen persons were carried out of the hole in the yard and **no person was carried out of the hole from the Lock of Salt.** A hole that opens in a building is a hole in a building. A hole that opens in the load-bearing gate of a tidal lock, in a course of stone that the city repeats a maintenance oath in twice a day, is the main breach. The outer tear of Chapter 5 and the tear of Chapter 25 are the same kind of event and not the same place, and the Chapter 25 tear is a *scar in a place* — the first in the series — while Owen's Chapter 38 mark is a *scar in a person* and the first scar that ends a life.

3. **A return mark is established by a separate, expensive, witnessed act, and the act is a plot event with a cost and a date.** Chapter 27, the twenty-eighth day, at the top of the tide, at the sixth hour. The cost column: the standing labour in the yard doubled for the day, a season of the bottom field entered against the instrument, and the day of four men's time that the fresh inspection of the day before came out of, entered again because it had now been taken twice. The working was performed by Hesper Saye, Tamsin Quill, Hettis Roke and a Saltwake mason in the low room by the salt wharf, in about forty minutes. The mark belongs to the person who goes and to nobody else, and **a person without a mark cannot cross through a controlled threshold, which is why the first crossing out of this world costs a fresh inspection, a broken set of terms, and a boy's decision.** The instrument was used in breach of its own terms, is entered as a breach, and is not a precedent.

4. **The interface cannot be refused out of existence, and it now offers to end the obligation rather than to begin it.** **Three prompts in this batch, in Chapters 24, 27 and 29**, all true, all with the cost in the grammatical position where a cost goes. **Chapter 22 carries no prompt** — its four clauses are the Reed Reach's term sheet, a document read on a raft. The closing construction of the batch is new and it is the most dangerous thing the interface has ever said to Adrian: *a debt may be closed*, and a closed debt is a small one, and a small one may be paid by any person and accepted on behalf of the debtor, and *there is no further cost.* That is not a lie about the wall. It is a lie about the field, and it is an offer of **relief**. A system that offers relief is more dangerous than a system that offers power, because relief is the thing a tired person actually wants. Adrian refused it in public and asked to be **continued as a creditor**, which is the correct answer and the humiliating one.

5. **The channel is two-way, and being two-way is not the same as being controllable.** On the twenty-fifth day the black line in the **chapel stone** — not the floor, not the arch, not the gate — carried twelve words of English out loud in a yard in front of about three hundred and ten people, in a flat reading voice, and Adrian understood them and eight hundred million Veyrans did not. It is the first time the stone has said anything that was not the old form of the maintenance oath, and it is the first time a thing from the other side has been audible in public in this world. **The main breach still does not speak. The gate is measured and never heard from.** The voice's source is still a man standing in the lock join below; the chapel stone is still the channel; and the channel can be closed by nobody, at any cost, by anybody. Later volumes may not make it steerable.

6. **A working with no witness leaves its residue in the person, and a bystander is the commonest case.** Mina Okoye, Chapter 25, in a doorway in the lock-chapel yard an hour after a floor came apart, and it is the volume's whole theory of consent in a form that has nothing to do with magic: **a hold is a working without a witness, and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.** This is the mechanism by which Owen Park's shoulder is destroyed and it is the same mechanism by which the city's own flood work quietly spends its dockers four at a time. Any later scene in which a character acts physically for somebody else in a resonant place is a working, and it should be costed as one whether or not anybody says so.

7. **Stage 1 does not grow in this volume and the terms that govern it have not changed.** Adrian remains a Witness. He still cannot perform a working, open a threshold, or hold a place. In Chapter 25 he was in the lower gallery of the Lock of Salt as the man with a rule and a stub of chalk, was asked in front of eleven men whether he was in the working, and said no. In Chapter 27 his whole part in the first crossing was to read a line and mark it in three places, and Hesper Saye made him say that out loud afterwards **so that it would be in a book before it was a story.** Do not let a later chapter quietly promote him into the working because the story needs a climax. The volume's climax is performed by Tamsin Quill, Ottoline Vask, the dock crew and Hettis Roke, and Adrian is the man who says the number.

## 12. Rules established in Volume 01 Chapters 31–40

These are additions to the sections above, not replacements, and they are on the page and binding from Chapter 31 onward.

1. **A rescue is a working, and a working needs a pattern, and a hole is not a pattern.** Ottoline Vask, in a yard, in front of the witnesses, refusing to read six terms she had agreed to read eight days earlier: *a rescue is a working. That is not a figure of speech, it is the definition and it is the reason the six items exist.* Any later chapter that sends a person anywhere on the strength of a cost, a consent and a mark is re-performing Chapter 27 and must be costed and objected to as one, **and a person with a mark in their hand and a scar in a floor will use what is there.**
2. **A description is not a place.** The sixth item of the Chapter 27 terms named a site by *description* — *a dressed joint, described on the twenty-fourth day in the words of the mason and the anchorwright, entered in this book and not named* — because Vask refused to name the thing on the twenty-fourth and Adrian found her refusal a principle rather than an evasion. The description is **true of two places in Saltwake**, one of them the instrument and one of them the scar, and the second crossing went to the scar, and **the same mason's own rule from the twenty-fourth — a quarter of a thing is not a thing — is the mechanism of the volume's only death.** A later writer must name a site by what is at it and not by what has been written about it.
3. **A breach carries a person and drops them back where the rule broke, and the rule that broke can be a rule about a floor.** Chapter 37. The Chapter 27 opening was a **route**: a pattern, a place, a hand, a witness, a cost, and a person named by themselves who can say no on the day. The Chapter 38 crossing was a **breach**: a scar, two marks on one floor where one person was named, and about four minutes and a half in which nothing happened at the one place in the world where an opening has ever been made lawfully. **A breach does not respect the terms, because terms require a working and there was no working.** A line in a place can be mended with work and money; **a mark in a person cannot, because nobody steps over a hand** — Vask, in a yard, and this is the volume's standing rule about marks and it is now load-bearing for the rest of the series.
