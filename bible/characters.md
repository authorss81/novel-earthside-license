# Character Bible

The cast is organized by desire and pressure, not by combat usefulness. A character can become powerful, but their power must preserve a recognizable private want and a costly flaw.

## 1. Adrian Vale — protagonist

### Basic life

- **Age:** 32 at the opening
- **Sex:** Male
- **Earth work:** Lead systems and onboarding designer at Morrowglass Interactive, working on the canceled historical-fantasy multiplayer game *A Crown of Ash*
- **Physical baseline:** Average build, desk-bound, no combat training, poor stamina, competent with tools and patterns
- **Starting location:** Alone in a night workroom in Earth, then the Lock of Salt in Veyra
- **Romance:** Tamsin Quill, one slow-burn relationship with no instant attraction and no possessive shortcut

### External want

At the beginning, Adrian wants three concrete things: to survive the crossing, find a reliable route back to Earth, and prevent the License from pulling more people into an uncontrolled disaster. The want changes as he learns that “back” is not a place that can be designed for him alone. By the middle volumes he wants a just bridge; by the final volume he wants a bridge that can survive his own mistakes.

### Internal need

Adrian must stop treating people as the movable parts of a system. He has to accept that consent, grief, dependency, and public responsibility are part of every design, not exceptions to be managed away. He must become willing to be changed by the people he is trying to help.

### Central flaw and fear

- **Flaw:** Adrian’s designer’s instinct is to make a system legible before he asks whether it should exist. He names, measures, and optimizes as a defense against panic.
- **Fear:** If he is not in control, he will become a disposable character in someone else’s history. The fear makes him hoard information, delay difficult decisions, and occasionally treat trust as a risk to be managed.
- **Failure pattern:** He can see a system-level solution before he can see the person who will pay for it. He must learn to ask, in public, who bears the cost and who has veto power.
- **He is not incompetent:** He notices patterns, remembers names, reads social rules, and can improvise from limited materials. His growth is judgment, cooperation, and responsibility, not a sudden increase in reflexes.

### Strengths and habits

- Excellent at identifying incentives, hidden loops, and translation gaps.
- Patient with people who are frightened or illiterate in a new system.
- Good at making a complicated plan understandable to a frightened group.
- Privately overworks, eats poorly, and uses humor to make a confession sound like a design note.
- He keeps a paper notebook in both worlds even after the System makes written notes less necessary. The paper grounds him in a culture that values witnessed, physical records.

### Arc stages

These are narrative arc labels, not power stages, and “holder” here means responsibility rather than the technical License holder or Passage holder defined in `power-system.md`.

1. **Observer:** Treats Veyra as a test case and the System as a career problem.
2. **Responsible holder:** Accepts that every opening is a promise with named people attached.
3. **Political designer:** Learns that a fair rule is useless if only he can interpret it.
4. **Public witness:** Gives up the private fiction that he can keep the bridge safe by controlling information.
5. **Relinquishing architect:** Surrenders the recognized seat of first holder and helps create a system that can work without him. His personal return mark is already gone by this point, spent in Volume 16.

### Relationships

- **Tamsin Quill:** His principal peer, romantic partner, and frequent professional opponent. She knows local law and refuses to translate people into categories. He is attracted to her precision and challenged by her refusal to be useful on his terms.
- **Mina Okoye:** His first moral and practical counterweight. She makes him answer medical and emotional consequences rather than narrative elegance.
- **Dante Kuo:** His action partner and a reminder that plans fail when the body carrying them is not invulnerable.
- **Nora Vale:** His younger sister, a mutual-aid organizer on Earth. She is the person who makes the cost of the bridge personal when Adrian begins speaking in the language of systems.
- **Ivenn Marrow:** His dark mirror. Ivenn offers a clean, stable future and makes Adrian’s desire for a solution feel reasonable.

## 2. Tamsin Quill — anchorwright and partner

### Basic life

- **Age:** 29 at the opening
- **Role:** Saltwake anchorwright, roadreader, and keeper of a public bridge register
- **Family:** Daughter of a dock clerk; supports a younger brother who works the river ferries
- **Strength:** She can read Crown Law, water joins, and the history of a route by touch and sound
- **Weakness:** She has little formal power and compensates by controlling information and becoming indispensable

### Want, need, and flaw

- **Want:** Keep the Lock of Salt and the Hearth Marches from being claimed by a Crown, a guild, or a player settlement.
- **Need:** Trust that shared knowledge can remain shared and that her own voice is worth more than her function.
- **Flaw:** She records everything because she fears losing people again. She hoards context and often makes decisions for others before asking them.
- **Fear:** Becoming a useful native translator while the community she is translating for disappears underneath her.

### Relationship with Adrian

Their first scenes are friction: she catches him treating a local ritual as a game mechanic, and he catches her hiding a dangerous join-line behind official language. Their respect grows through repair work, failed translations, and arguments conducted in public rather than through grand declarations. She is not impressed by his status or his Earth knowledge. She becomes his romantic partner only after he surrenders the right to use her as a convenient anchor without her explicit consent.

Their mature relationship is a partnership with separate work, separate loyalties, and a home that is not a reward for victory. They may marry or share a residence late in the story, but the ending does not make marriage the cure for political problems.

## 3. Mina Okoye — first-cohort medic

- **Age:** 29
- **Earth work:** Emergency-room nurse and volunteer first-aid instructor
- **Want:** Return to her family and prove that practical knowledge is more valuable than a spectacular class
- **Need:** Accept that she cannot save everyone and ask others to share the work
- **Flaw:** Triage becomes a way to avoid her own grief and pain
- **Fear:** Being unable to distinguish a patient she can save from a patient she has already failed

Mina treats Veyran injuries, contaminated water, plant medicines, and the consequences of a bad crossing. She does not become a magical doctor simply because she has Earth knowledge; she studies local practice and changes several treatments after observing differences in bodies, infections, and consent. She is the first person to tell Adrian that a System prompt showing a possible route is not the same thing as permission to use it.

Her final public role is co-founder of a two-world medical compact that recognizes both Earthside and Veyran practitioners.

## 4. Dante Kuo — Hearthguard captain

- **Age:** 35
- **Earth work:** Fireground logistics coordinator and warehouse safety trainer
- **Want:** Keep the first cohort alive and prove that a disciplined plan can beat panic
- **Need:** Learn to trust local judgment and accept that command is not the same as being in control
- **Flaw:** He volunteers for every dangerous task and calls self-sacrifice leadership
- **Fear:** Being remembered as the person who ordered others into a disaster

Dante has no magical class at first. His competence is logistics, physical conditioning, drills, and reading frightened people. He becomes captain of a mixed Hearthguard company, but only after learning that Veyran fighters know the terrain, weather, and local rules better than his game-derived assumptions.

Dante’s major conflict with Adrian is over whether a rare permission should be spent on one rescue or used to secure a whole district. He survives the series with lasting injuries and a quieter role in reconstruction.

## 5. Priya Shah — player ambassador and media complication

- **Age:** 24
- **Earth work:** Community college student, livestream moderator, and part-time game tester
- **Want:** Become trusted enough to tell the truth about the crossing and return with a story that gives her family a better life
- **Need:** Learn that attention is not consent and that a private confession can become public harm
- **Flaw:** She performs confidence, shares other people’s business for engagement, and treats public opinion as a resource to exploit
- **Fear:** Being ordinary and unheard after spending years making herself visible

Priya is the first player to make a public record of the arrival. Her broadcasts are useful and dangerous: they can warn families, but they also make Veyran people into spectacle. She later helps found the **Common Record**, a cross-world news and consent archive with strict rules about naming vulnerable people.

## 6. Jonah Pike — the colonial mirror

- **Age:** 42
- **Earth work:** Warehouse supervisor and longtime tabletop game master
- **Want:** Build a safe, recognizable settlement where Earthside players can live without depending on an indifferent Earth
- **Need:** Accept that a settlement is not a community until local people can refuse it, leave it, or share it
- **Flaw:** Territorial thinking. He treats land, people, and loyalty as resources that can be allocated by a competent leader
- **Fear:** Another failed attempt at making a home for people who have already lost one

Jonah is not secretly evil. He is persuasive, decent at administration, and genuinely protects his people. He becomes the leader of the **White Ledger**, a player association that claims land and monopolizes templates. His success makes Adrian’s failure to intervene believable rather than convenient.

Jonah’s late arc strips him of control and forces him to choose between his own settlement’s safety and a larger common settlement. He survives and works as a labor mediator, but the story does not excuse the people he displaced or the harm he enabled.

## 7. Nora Vale — Adrian’s sister and Earth-side witness

- **Age:** 25
- **Earth work:** Public-health logistics coordinator and volunteer with a cross-world families’ mutual-aid group
- **Want:** Prove Adrian is alive, recover the missing, and prevent governments and companies from treating travelers as expendable assets
- **Need:** Turn grief into a strategy that does not make every decision a confrontation
- **Flaw:** Anger makes her choose public exposure before she has built protection for the people it may expose
- **Fear:**Adrian will become so committed to Veyra that he will stop recognizing what his absence cost Earthside families

Nora remains on Earth for most of the story. She is a political and emotional bridge, not a rescue party. Her records and testimony force Adrian to answer for the people behind the interface. In the final volumes she becomes a co-chair of the **Crossworld Families Union** and insists that the new charter include a right to return.

## 8. Ivenn Marrow — the Quiet Choir’s First Reader

- **Age:** 41 at the opening
- **Role:** Historian, Crown Law translator, and custodian of a hidden chapter house
- **Want:** Complete the Single Witness design and prevent another Sundering by making contradictory histories impossible
- **Need:** A realization too late to excuse him: plural disagreement is not the disease; unaccountable power is
- **Flaw:** He judges people by the suffering their choices prevent, not by whether they consented to be judged
- **Fear:** Another generation will vanish because nobody could agree on what really happened

Ivenn is charming, patient, and well-read. He does not read minds or control players directly. He alters the permissions embedded in Anchorstones, chooses attractive trials and rewards, and makes the system’s omissions look like good guidance. He is the final human antagonist and the version of Adrian’s flaw taken to its logical conclusion.

Ivenn’s family died during a smaller local collapse caused by conflicting official records. That history explains his fear without excusing his plan. At the ending he is alive, deprived of system access, and responsible for maintaining public accounts of the Sundering under community supervision. He is not granted a redemption that erases his victims.

## 9. Regent Solenne Dary — the Crown’s practical antagonist

- **Age:** 52
- **Role:** Regent of the Glass Crown
- **Want:** Put the bridge under one protective authority before fear, trade, and player power break the kingdom
- **Need:** Understand that a state cannot become safe by making every disagreement invisible
- **Flaw:** She assumes competent central control is better than messy local consent
- **Fear:** Aurel will be invaded or starved because it failed to control a new force in time

Solenne signs a temporary security accord with Adrian, then attempts to seize the network. She is a serious opponent and later a necessary co-signatory. Her final political survival depends on accepting that a Crown can hold a place in the bridge without owning it.

## 10. Celia Rusk — Earthside administrator

- **Age:** 47
- **Role:** Director of Crossworld Continuity at Northstar Civic, an emergency-management corporation working with governments
- **Want:** Make crossings predictable, protect returning citizens, and prevent a private or military actor from controlling the bridge
- **Need:** Accept that safety built through secrecy and monopolized access becomes another form of captivity
- **Flaw:** She confuses manageability with safety and treats informed consent as a variable that can be delayed during emergencies
- **Fear:** Uncontrolled crossing will expose Earth to disease, collapse, or strategic attack

Celia is not a fantasy monarch. She works through risk assessments, contracts, supply contracts, media strategy, and security detachments. Her methods become the clearest late-volume argument for a central administrator. She is opposed, constrained, and ultimately placed under public oversight rather than killed.

## 11. First-cohort support and consequences

The first cohort is **seven people**: Adrian plus the six who arrive through the outer tear in Chapter 5. “The six” always means the arrivals alone; Adrian is the seventh. Eli Mercer is not one of the seven.

- **Noor Haddad**, 27, a bakery owner and logistics planner from Earth. She becomes the cohort’s quartermaster and later runs the Open Hand exchange. Her practical gift is noticing who has been left out of a distribution.
- **Owen Park**, 36, a security contractor and the first player to treat Veyran locals as obstacles. He alone perceives a relic-shaped light at the arrival and at the chapel stone, which no one else can see even in daylight when the physical bright edge of the damaged join is plainly visible; the story keeps that asymmetry unexplained. He is a bystander at the Chapter 25 tear, reaches for the missing child, and injures a shoulder that local healers never fully set; he carries it for the rest of the volume. He dies during the first volume’s bad threshold collapse. A temporary return mark is established for his rescue attempt; when the route tears, that mark becomes the **first black scar carried in a person’s own return mark** — the first scar that ends a life, and the first that another traveler might have tried to inherit. It is not the first black scar in the story: the outer threshold is already scarred in place by the overloaded join, and a black line has sat on the chapel stone since Chapter 7. His death is permanent and is caused partly by Adrian’s underestimated crossing cost, partly by the injury that was never set, and partly by Owen’s refusal to follow instructions; Adrian carries all three truths.
- **Eli Mercer**, 19, an impulsive beta tester who believes invulnerability is part of the game. He joins a later, smaller crossing in Volume 1 rather than the first cohort, and he is not one of the seven. He is injured, learns the limits of the System, and remains a minor recurring player rather than a replacement hero.
- **Lena Morrow**, 22, an Earthside physics student recruited later as a translator and materials researcher. She is a secondary character, not a second romantic interest.

## 11b. Working cast at the Lock of Salt

These are the local hands that perform the Volume 01 workings. They matter because Adrian never performs one himself: at Stage 1 he can only make a pattern visible enough for other people to work.

- **Tamsin Quill** performs the anchor-side pattern in the Chapter 9 closure and again in the Chapter 10 route working, and is one of the four who sign the Chapter 10 inspection.
- **The Chapter 9 dock crew** — two dock workers who hold the near gate in Chapter 4 and close the outer tear with Tamsin in Chapter 9 — are the other two signers of the Chapter 10 inspection. They are working locals, not named leads; their names stay in the public record even when the prose does not linger on them.
- **Hettis Roke**, 44, a Hearthguard member who signs the Chapter 10 inspection line and can refuse the route on the Hearthguard’s behalf. He is the reason the route is a negotiated instrument rather than Adrian’s private arrangement.
- **Sera Vail**, 50, a Lantern Concord notary who writes the public ledger entry, reads the five parts of a controlled threshold aloud at the Chapter 10 hearing, and presses the Chapter 1 fragment-mark into the maintenance seam. She is the civic face of the crossing: she does not believe in the System and does not have to, because her instrument is the record.

The following working cast are named and on the page in Chapters 1–10. They are not leads; they are hands, and later batches may use them without re-introducing them. Full state is in `state/character-state.md`.

- **Ottoline Vask**, a Lock mason in her thirties, thirty-four years in Saltwake, who comes to any of this on her own account and is the technical authority in the batch on joins, sockets, residues and black lines. She is the local who explains *why* and refuses to soften it.
- **Ovin Sarr**, 19, and **Nell Bray**, the two dockers of the Chapter 9 crew and the Chapter 10 signers. Ovin is the young man Adrian pulls out of a hole in Chapter 2; Bray is the woman in the felt hat who tells him what *skyborn* means. They are working locals whose names stay in the public record.
- **Sesh Brack**, 50s, owner of the dry-goods loft above the river market that the arrivals sleep in. A market woman with a common box, a legacy, and a bound hand. She is the local who enforces Tamsin’s second condition on her own body.
- **Nyla Ferrow**, keeper of the lock chapel, whose mother and whose mother’s mother kept it. She speaks rarely and is right.
- **Renna Ord**, a dock worker in a boat-coat who is the first person to say *skyborn* aloud, and who hands Adrian the vocabulary of it.
- **Brann**, a Hearthguard whose voice in the yard on Tuesday morning is the reason nobody was watching the outside of the lock, and who therefore belongs in the batch’s failure record.
- **The nine households of Nine Elms**, the Reed Reach village whose refusal of receiving is entered in the Chapter 10 ledger at the word of the household and not the word of the assembly, and which may withdraw that refusal on any day it chooses. They are a political actor before they are a character, and no Reed individual is on stage before Chapter 10.

## 12. Relationship milestones

1. **Tamsin distrusts Adrian’s labels** and makes him prove that he will ask before using local knowledge.
2. **Mina forces Adrian to share responsibility** for the first cohort’s medical decisions.
3. **Dante and Adrian disagree over a rescue**, establishing that trust does not mean obedience.
4. **Nora refuses Adrian’s attempt to keep the crossing quiet**, making the family cost visible.
5. **Tamsin refuses to serve as Adrian’s convenient anchor**, forcing a separation before their relationship can mature.
6. **Adrian and Tamsin choose each other after public, independent work**, not after a rescue confession.
7. **The final relationship is reciprocal:** Adrian does not stay in Veyra to protect Tamsin from Earth, and Tamsin does not follow him to Earth to be rescued from Veyra.

## 13. Antagonistic relationships and functions

- **Adrian vs. Owen:** Adrian’s first lesson that a player’s confidence is not a safety system.
- **Adrian vs. Jonah:** Adrian’s compromise about control becomes a rival’s territory.
- **Adrian vs. Solenne:** Adrian’s distributed authority challenges the state’s demand for one safe hand.
- **Adrian vs. Celia:** Adrian’s lived cross-world ethics challenge the Earthside demand for predictable control.
- **Adrian vs. Ivenn:** Two people who understand systems respond differently to the same fear of irreversible history. Adrian learns, gives up, and shares; Ivenn learns enough to become dangerous but refuses to relinquish authorship.

No major antagonist should be defeated by a surprise higher power. Their defeat must use relationships, evidence, choices, and a limitation established earlier.

He explains civic machinery when a reader needs it explained and gets out of the way. He is the man who says *that is the term* rather than *that is a technicality*.
- **Marek Osley**, about 48, a **salt factor** of the tally house, with a clerk's stoop and a waxed tablet he has not put down since dawn. - **Renna Ord**, established Chapter 3, expanded Chapters 11, 14, 18 and 20. She teaches the rota, teaches Dante the difference between a chain of command and a chain of asking, sits on a barrow at three in the morning, and stops a dig by naming a cofferdam. ## 15b. Cast established in Volume 02, Chapters 81–90

Working locals, not leads. Full live state is in `state/character-state.md`.

- **Garret Slee, 61**, of the room over the market, who has kept **the Lantern Concord's bound book of this city's own words for nineteen years**. She wrote the order of a queue at the sixth hour and again at the seventh and has two lists that are not the same list. She said the hole in the Reach's rule out loud in a yard and asked for somebody to write down that a hole and a wharf were in the same yard and that nobody joined them up.
- **A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO KEEPS A PUBLIC REGISTER HAS SAID *TO OPEN NOTHING* THREE TIMES, twice in a room and once in a yard, and will not say it a fourth, and on the fifty-fifth day of the Long Month she said she started to write down who the four people in the delegation are, got two names in, stopped, and tore the page out, and it is in her own pocket and not in the book.**
- **A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A STALL COPIED THE FRONT OF THE COMPACT ONTO THE BACK OF A SLATE ON A MARKET TABLE ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE FORTY-FIFTH DAY OF THE LONG MONTH BECAUSE HE THOUGHT A PAGE OUGHT TO BE IN TWO PLACES, and about four hundred people who read a wall nine miles up the road that week trace back to that table and not to the stall.** He said he would rather be laughed at than be a man who has carried a piece of paper about for nine days, and said a cost with no figure on the end of it is a cost and a price with a figure on the end of it is a promise. **A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road and a woman of about thirty-four in the store on the upper road are five people and are never in one sentence as one person.**
- **A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO WHO DIGS SAID WHAT A DAY OF A NUMBER WOULD COST, which is a hundred and sixty-five days and a person standing on the ground on each one at the same hour and nobody anybody could be argued out of a thing about, because it is a figure with no shape, and then said he can date a bank and cannot date a number.** He has been telling people a channel with no water in it is a hole with a smell in it for fourteen days and says about nine of them have written the number down instead of the sentence.
- **A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR IN THE STORE ON THE UPPER ROAD, WHO IS ON NO LIST IN IT, HUNG A SLATE WITH THE FIFTY-SIXTH DAY OF THE LONG MONTH ON THE CHEST ON THE FORTY-SIXTH SO THAT NOBODY WOULD HAVE TO ASK, and said the date out loud four times to about four different people on the fifty-fifth.** She is not a title, not a rank, not an office and she has not been named.
- **A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO COUNTS WATER ON THE FINGERS OF HER LEFT HAND READ A WHOLE PAGE WITH HER FINGERS ON IT AND THEN SAID THE MAN WHO WROTE IT HAD PUT THE SETTLEMENT'S OWN SENTENCE ON A WALL, and asked that it be entered that she was asked a question in a yard and did not answer it.** *Described and not named in this batch, which is the device working and not a failure: she was named in a room in daylight with a date on it in Chapter 0153 and in four chapters of Volume 01 and two of Volume 02.*
- **A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-NINE WHO KEEPS A SHOP FOUR STREETS DOWN FROM A MARKET SAID THAT ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THIS CITY HAVE ALREADY WORKED OUT WHICH ROAD THE SEVENTH LINE IS ABOUT AND HAVE TOLD EACH OTHER, and that if a person wants to know they can stand at the end of it on a Friday morning in daylight and say so.** *Open, and nobody has asked the man who built the road.*
