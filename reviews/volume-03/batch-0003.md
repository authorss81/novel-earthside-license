# Review — Volume 03, Batch 0003 (Chapters 0121–0130) — **RECONSTRUCTED RECORD, NOT A REVIEW LOG**

**READ THIS FIRST, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF THE CAVEAT.** This file is not the record of a review pass. It is a reconstruction, written during the Volume 03 close phase, from the only surviving accounts of a review that ran. **The review log is absent and so is the record: `logs/batch-0003.review.log` is not on disk, `logs/` holds only `close.log` and `models.log`, and no file was written to `reviews/volume-03/batch-0003.md` at the time.** Four state files record nine findings in a file that cannot be opened — `state/current.md`, `state/continuity.md`, `state/open-threads.md`, and §14 of `state/batch-summaries/volume-03-batch-0003.md` — and the batch summary has a section headed as the review pass with this file's path named in it. **`reviews/volume-03/batch-0001.md` and `batch-0002.md` are both on disk, and `batch-0004.md` is, so the gap is one file and not a policy.**

**Why it is written at all, and what it is worth.** A state layer that agrees with a missing record is not a check, and four files agreeing with each other is not four checks: that was the finding in the Batch 0002 review and it is the finding here. The honest options were to write a note saying the nine cannot be recovered, or to set them out as they survive with the reconstruction declared on the page. **The nine are recoverable with confidence, because every one of them is a mechanical fault with a named page, a named wrong figure and a named correct figure, and the pages can be opened.** So this file sets them out, and it was checked against the pages while it was written. **What cannot be recovered, and is not claimed: the review's own wording, its order of working, which checks it ran, what it said about the prose, and whether it found anything this file does not list.** It may have. Nine is what four files say and nine is what is here.

**A writing run may not write its own review record, and this is not a writing run.** A close phase is not a reviewer either, and this file is not a review: nothing was re-reviewed in the sense a reviewer would review, and the nine were not rediscovered — they were read out of the state layer and then **verified against the ten pages**, which is the one thing the state layer could not do for itself. **A later phase may treat the nine as evidence of what was found and may not treat this file as evidence of a review that happened.**

The scope that pass was given, as four files record it: `chapters/volume-03/chapter-0121.md` … `chapter-0130.md`, the state files they update, `bible/power-system.md`, `bible/terminology.md`, and the handoff `workspace/volume-03/batch-0004/PROMPT.md`. **No chapter was restarted and no chapter was rewritten; every fix was surgical and the planned plot did not move.**

**The gate, which is controller-owned and which this file does not fix.** The reviewer is configured as a subagent and the review prompt executes under the writer agent. That has happened in every batch in this repository, and it is recorded in `state/open-threads.md` and was not touched: `.opencode/agent/` and the workflow are not edited by a phase. **So even the review that ran was a second pass by the same agent and not an independent one, and no state file may claim otherwise — including this one.**

## The nine, as they survive, each checked against the page in the close phase

**1. The silence count broke the rule in the sentence that states it.** It was **thirty-eight for thirty-eight** in `state/continuity.md`, `state/open-threads.md`, the batch summary and `bible/power-system.md`, because `chapter-0127.md`'s ledger said *the thirty-seventh day … is the thirty-fifth silence and the count stands where it stood* and then, in the same paragraph, said *a day the wall speaks on is not a silence*. **Thirty-seven for thirty-seven, and Chapter 0127 now repeats thirty-four the way Chapter 0116 repeated twenty-four.**
*Verified in the close phase, not taken from the state layer:* the running count across the ten days is **twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three, thirty-four, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven**, and the repeat is on Chapter 0127, which is the day the wall spoke. **The whole of Volume 03's count is now internally consistent and the close phase checked it end to end: twenty at Chapter 0111, twenty-four at 0115, twenty-four again at 0116, twenty-five at 0117, twenty-eight at 0120, thirty-four at 0126, thirty-four again at 0127, forty-five at 0138, forty-five again at 0139, and sixty at Chapter 0150.** The rule is the rule: a day the wall speaks on is not a silence however hard the day was.

**2. Four chapters said one offer had been refused in the paragraph that records the second refusal.** Chapters 0127, 0128, 0129 and 0130 all read *two of the thirty-one*.
*Verified in the close phase:* all four carry **there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused.** **The clerk's second book holds twenty-four at the end of this batch and that is a different count, and it is unchanged by this finding.** The two counts are not the same count and the distinction is written into `state/open-threads.md`.

**3. A boy of nine's answer was seventy-six days late in a sentence that also said sixty-eight is the number everybody knows.** Seventy-six is the age of the question. **Sixty-eight.**
*Verified in the close phase:* `chapter-0126.md` reads *it is eight days old, and it is sixty-eight days late, and the sixty-eight days is the number everybody in this city knows and the eight days is the number nobody is keeping*. The question was asked on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, the fiftieth day of the flood; the answer went onto the flat of a slate on the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one, day 118, and day 126 minus day 118 is eight. **The rule the finding adds: where two elapsed counts sit in one sentence, name the event each is measured from, or the state layer will pick the wrong one and defend it in four files.**

**4. Chapter 0129 said the second page in the stone carries eleven names.** It carries **twelve**, on the page since Chapter 0095, and `state/continuity.md` had recorded the eleven.
*Verified in the close phase:* `chapter-0129.md` reads *twelve names on it and a line under them saying it is a count and not a debt*, and the twelve also stand in Chapters 0105, 0106 and 0110. **A summary that quietly corrects a page error cannot be used to find one, because it will always agree.**

**5. A foreman of a day crew was nine days absent on three days that make him eight, ten and sixteen.** He was last at that gate on day 114. **Eight at 0122, nine at 0123, ten at 0124, sixteen at 0130.**
*Verified in the close phase:* `chapter-0122.md` opens *it is eight days since the foreman of the day crew did not come*. The figure is not derivable from the day number alone and each of the four is that chapter's own day less 114.

**6. Chapter 0123 had two men of about thirty-nine in one sentence and one of them does not exist**, and the nineteen words belong to the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. **The third person merge in three batches.**
*Verified in the close phase:* `chapter-0123.md` now has one man of about thirty-nine, the board trader, and the nineteen words are given to *a woman who is on nothing and is named on nothing*. **A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, a man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close and a man of about thirty-five whose shoulder cannot be set are seven people and no two of them may be put in one sentence.**

**7. The writer's own unit of work was in the narration of all ten chapters** — *across thirteen batches*, *across fourteen batches*, *in fourteen batches*, *for most of a volume*. **All removed, and a grep for *batch*, *volume*, *chapter* and *series* is a standing check and not a thing to remember.**
*Verified in the close phase:* the same grep over Chapters 0121–0130 returns nothing in the narration of any of the ten.

**8. Twenty chapters with no page on which the protagonist is named.** **Named once, in Chapter 0129, in Tamsin Quill's mouth**, and not by himself.
*Verified in the close phase:* `chapter-0129.md` reads *one of them is Adrian Vale, who told us in a room on the twenty-ninth day that a translation is a description*.

**9. Six wrong figures were inherited into `workspace/volume-03/batch-0004/PROMPT.md`** and would have been taught to the last batch of the volume. **All fixed there.**
*Verified in the close phase, and this is the finding with the longest tail.* **One of the six was a day map that gave a weekday for every day of Batch 0004 and was two days out, and the day map was inherited from the ten finished chapters — which is how Chapters 0121–0130 came to carry weekdays two days earlier than the founding rule. The close phase corrected the ten chapters against day 1 and the drift is gone; see `state/volume-03-close.md` §2.**
