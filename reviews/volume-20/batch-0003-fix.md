## PART ONE — WHAT WAS FIXED IN THE PROSE

### FINDING 1, THE BLOCKING ONE: TWO CHAPTERS CLOSING ON THE SAME SENTENCE. FIXED IN THE PROSE, NOT IN THE NUMBER.

The review reported that `chapter-0973.md:31` and `chapter-0976.md:33` closed on the same sentence, one word apart, and put
the shared run at 34 tokens over about a hundred and thirty characters. It was correct on both counts. §17.17 says no two
chapters of a batch may close on the same construction.

**Fixed by rewriting the closing paragraph of 0973 and by touching nothing else.** Its old closing was the flap down and the
strap standing up off the leather; 0976's closing is a strap gone pale where a hand closes on it. Two chapters cannot both end
on that strap. The new closing is the **crease along the top of that flap taking a set of its own**, so that the flap lies
against the body of the satchel with a finger's width of air between them at the edge nearest his own side and a finger would
go down into that air with nothing at all holding it open. It is stated nowhere else in that chapter, it is not a bar, a
socket, the strip of ground, a page with a number on it, a name in a column, the space on that board, a wet sleeve, a
question or its answer, a line in the grit or the tin, and it is a change rather than a statement that nothing changed.

### FINDINGS 3, 4, 5 AND 6 ARE ONE ERROR IN TWO CHAPTERS, AND ALL FOUR ARE FIXED.

The review split this into four findings and it is worth keeping them apart on the page and not in the act, because they were
four readings of one drift: **in a row of nine people, a face, a space and a cloth went onto the wrong man.**

- **Finding 3, the duplicated handle in a speech tag.** `chapter-0980.md:21` carried *said the man of about thirty-four who
  keeps a stall two stalls along, two stalls along*. Removed.
- **Finding 5, the two boards conflated.** Canon puts the stallholder's board **face in** against his own trestle with the
  bare back turned out (`outline/volume-20.md` §7.2, and `chapter-0963.md:7` staged it that way), and the nineteen rates and
  the empty two-finger space are the **tradesman's**. 0980 turned the stallholder's board face out *where anybody going past
  could read it*, handed him the middle column and the space in his speech, and closed the previous section by giving the
  space to *the tradesman's own shoulder*. All three are now the plan's: his board is face in with the bare back out, the
  middle column and the space belong to the man two stalls along, and the space is in the open with the dark line where the
  edge of the cloth stands running under it, which is the geometry 0980:17 already sets out.
- **Finding 4, the self-contradicting speech.** He said the chalk was in his own pocket and then that he would have to go
  two stalls along and stand in front of another man's pocket for it. Canon puts that chalk in his own inside breast pocket
  and it does not come out on any day of the volume. **The speech is rewritten around the only difficulty the plan allows:
  he has the chalk and he has the hand, and the board with the space on it is not his and nobody at that trestle has asked
  him to write on it, so a figure of his in there would be about him and not about the day. He does not cross the row.** It
  is a better speech than the one it replaces and it is the same refusal.
- **Finding 6, the cloth on the wrong man.** `chapter-0978.md:7` put *the cloth over his own shoulder* on the man of about
  thirty-four, in a chapter whose thirty-first line has the man of about thirty-nine using that same cloth. Canon gives the
  cloth to the tradesman (0951, 0952, 0957, 0963, 0965, 0967, 0968). **The cloth is back on the tradesman's shoulder and the
  stallholder's board is face in with the bare back turned out**, which fixes the staging in the same line.

### FINDING 11, THE CENSUS SPLIT THREE WAYS IN ONE CHAPTER. TAKEN.

`chapter-0980.md:11`, `:15` and `:31` stated *about four people a day* as a walk-past count, as a get-a-thing count and as a
get-nothing count, which made a standing census behave as a derived count and made the arithmetic of one morning disagree
with itself. **0980 now states the census once and the payload of the paragraph it was in is kept**: that is the whole of
what that board does in that row on a morning, and nobody has ever stood in that row and counted a figure that was not
already there. **Census occurrences across the ten fall from fifteen to twelve and every one of the twelve has the word
*people* inside the next thirty characters**, which is what §14.5 requires, and the twelve now stand in twelve different
forms.

any day of this volume.** Frame ten opens *a hand making a mark*, so that clause is **unavailable** to 977 rather than
merely unreached, and the card governs the day. **The record now cites §20 item 8 as well as the chapter.**
