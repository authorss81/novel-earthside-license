# Chapter 0249 — What He Did Not Ask

There were two brooms on the boards at the top of the cut on the Reed Reach on the ninety-fourth day of the Long Month, laid side by side with their bristles toward the water and about four inches between them, and the first one has been on those boards since the ninety-second day of that month and nobody at the top of that cut has said one word about it and nobody has picked it up. Adrian Vale came up that bank at about the ninth hour of the morning with nothing in his hands, and he stood at the top of it for about two hours and walked its length four times, and he did not ask the keeper of the Redroot gate a single question, and the light came off eleven miles of flats and went up the far side of that cut while he was standing on it.

---

**And the keeper of that gate was about nine feet from him at the top of that cut with the board in her left hand and a thumb's depth of dust off those flats in the channel below her, and about four people at the top of that cut have noticed a man standing on a bank for two hours with nothing in his hands and nobody else has noticed anything at all.**

"You have come a long way."

"**I have come eleven miles and I have stood on this bank for about two hours, and I came up here to ask you a question, and I am not going to ask you it, and I am telling you that on purpose and at the top of a cut where about four people can hear me.**"

"You have not asked it."

"**I have not asked it, and I am telling you that instead of asking it, because a man who walks eleven miles to ask a question and does not ask it has wasted eleven miles, and I would rather waste them than do the other thing, and about nine people in this city have worked out that I come up this bank and stand on it, and a few of them have worked out that the standing is the whole of it.**"

"Then why did you come."

"**I came because I have been in this city two hundred and forty-nine days and I have nothing on me that anybody in it can use, and there is one place in this city where nobody has ever asked me for anything and I can stand on a bank in it for two hours and be a man in a place, and that is a worse reason than a wage and it is the only one I have got.**"

---

**And the boards of that bank are about four feet wide where the two brooms were lying, and the wind has been coming up that bank since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and he walked the length of it four times in two hours and put his boots in the same four places each time without knowing he was doing it.**

"You have walked that four times."

"**I have walked that four times, and the first time I went the length of it and the second time I went the length of it and the third time I went about nine feet and turned round, and the fourth time I went the length of it again, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who walks a thing four times is a man who is measuring it and I am not, and I have not got a number at the end of any of the four and that is the first time in this flood that has happened to me on purpose.**"

"You could have had a number."

"**I could have had a number in about nine minutes with a bit of cord and a stone, and the number would have been the length of a bank, and nobody has worked out that I came eleven miles to stand here and the only thing I have left at the end of it is that I did not ask.**"

---

**And he had the question ready at about the ninth hour and he had it ready at the tenth hour, and at about the tenth hour he turned his hand over because the grit had got into the seam of it, and the wall said one thing, and it said it once.**

> **You wrote a rule for yourself about asking. A rule you wrote is still a rule when the person in front of you would answer.**

He read that on the bank with the wind at his back and then he shut his hand and put it behind him, and he did not say one word about where the words had come from to anybody at the top of that cut for the rest of the morning, and he had worked the price of that question out on the eighty-fifth day of that month before he came up the bank, and he had not asked it on that day either.

---

**And the keeper of the Redroot gate did not look at him for the whole of those two hours and did not stop working the channel, and at about the eleventh hour she turned her head about nine inches and looked at the two brooms and said one sentence about them and then went back to what she was doing.**

"**And I have not put a second thing down in a place in two hundred and forty-nine days, and if there is a second broom on those boards this morning then somebody in this city has done the thing I have not done, and I have not got a name for them and I have not got a reason why they would.**"

He said that to the boards and not to the woman, and then he got up off the boards and brushed the grit off the back of his coat with the flat of his hand and the grit went into the dust and stayed there.

"**And I have come up this bank four times since the eighty-sixth day of that month and I have not put a thing on it on any of the four, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who comes to a place empty-handed four times is a man being tested, and nobody has worked out that I have not been tested by anybody and I would not know how to answer if I were.**"

"There is a second one."

"I did not bring it."

"**I know you did not bring it. I have not looked at you twice since you came up that bank, and I know a broom when it comes round a corner of my eye, and nobody has worked out that I have not come to put a second thing down and I have not.**"

"Then who did."

"**Nobody. That is a thing about a cut and not about a person, and nobody has worked out that it is not, and I have not got a way of telling those two apart from where I am standing and I have not been asked.**"

"You might ask."

"**I might. And if I asked, then the two of us would be two people on a bank asking each other about a broom, and about nine people in this city have worked out that a thing is worth more when nobody has asked about it, and I have not got a use for that and I am not going to take it up on a morning when there is dust in my channel.**"

---

**And the second broom had been on the boards of the top of that cut for about an hour when he noticed it, and about four people at the top of that cut had seen a man look at a broom and then look at a woman and then look at a bank, and the rest had not been on that bank at all.**

"That was not here at the seventh hour."

"**It was not, and I came up at the ninth hour and it was not here when I came, and I have not been on that bank at any hour between the seventh and the ninth since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and I am telling you that because in about nine minutes somebody in this cut is going to work out that I brought it.**"

"And did you."

"**No, and nobody has worked out that I stood in it for two hours, and the standing is the proof, and the proof is not something a person can carry up a bank in one hand.**"

---

**And the light came off the flats at about the eleventh hour and went up the far side of that cut, and the two brooms were on the boards, and about four people who came up that bank on the ninety-fourth day of that month after the eleventh hour stepped over both of them and the rest did not see either, and the keeper of the Redroot gate did not look down at them once.**

He got to the bottom of that bank at about the twelfth hour and sat down on the boards there for a while, and the channel has had no water in it since the forty-first day of that month and there is a thumb's depth of the dust off those flats in the bottom of it, and the boards he was sitting on were the colour of the boards under them. He did not go down into the cut. He had come eleven miles to ask a question about a thing that is on a page in a town nine hundred miles from here, and he had not asked it, and there is no figure on any page in this city for what a man has lost by not asking one, and he looked for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet and then he got up and went up the road.

---

**And he had walked the length of that bank four times in two hours and had put his boot in the same four places each time, and at about the eleventh hour he sat down on the boards at the top of it about nine feet from the two brooms with his hands on his knees and the grit worked into the seam of the left one, and about four people at the top of that cut had been on that bank before the seventh hour and the rest had not.**

"You are not going to ask her."

"**I was not going to ask her and I have not asked her, and nobody has worked out that the deciding was done at the seventh hour this morning before I got to the top of the bank, and the other two hours were me standing here afterwards.**"

"What would you have asked."

"**That is the second question and I am not going to answer it, and nobody has worked out that I came up eleven miles to ask it and I have not got the least idea what I would do with the answer, and that is the whole reason I came.**"

He put his hand on the boards on the side away from the two brooms and left it there, and the grit went into the lines of the boards and the boards took it, and the light came off the flats and went up the far side of that cut while he sat on it.

---

**And he came eleven miles up that road on the ninety-fourth day of that month and had come eleven miles up it on the eighty-sixth and the eighty-ninth and the ninety-first, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who walks eleven miles four times in nine days is a man going somewhere, and nobody has worked out that he has not been going anywhere, and the road is nine miles long and the cut at the bottom of it is eleven, and he has not confused the two.**

"Four times in nine days."

"**Four times since the eighty-sixth day of that month, and on the eighty-ninth I came for a wall and on the ninety-first I came for a queue and this morning I came for a question, and about nine people in this city have worked out that a man who comes for three different things in nine days is a man with three reasons, and nobody has worked out that I have had one reason since the first of them and it has not been good enough to get me to the top of a bank before the ninth hour.**"

"What is the reason."

"**That there is one place in this flood where a man can stand for two hours and be nobody's, and I have not been able to find a second place, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who has one place is a man with a home, and nobody has worked out that it is a place I have to walk eleven miles to and that there is a woman standing nine feet off me in it who has not asked me one question.**"

"Does she know you are here."

"**She knows I am on that bank, and I came up it at the ninth hour in daylight, and she said one thing to me at the ninth hour and one thing at the eleventh, and the first one was about how far I had come and the second one was about a broom, and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman gives more than that to nobody, and nobody has worked out that I have not asked her one single question for two hours, and I do not know which of those two is going to be the true one when I am an old man.**"

---

**And a bank with two brooms on the boards at the top of it, and a man who came eleven miles and went back down without asking.**
