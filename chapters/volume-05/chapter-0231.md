# Chapter 0231 — The Board Came Off The Wall

A board came off the wall of the store on the upper road at about the sixth hour of the morning on the seventy-sixth day of the Long Month and went about nine feet and landed face up on the top of that yard, and the man who washes it was not holding it. The wind had been at that wall since first light and had come up off eleven miles of flats with the fine stuff off the racks in it, and the top of that yard was hard and pale and the board did not go into it. There is a trough under that wall with a wet cloth folded over the rim, and the trough is nine feet from where the board landed, and about four people in that yard had come through the door in the last hour and about nine of them had not come through it at all.

---

**And the last figures written on that board are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month, and this morning they are seven days old, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes it has not written on it since the seventieth day of that month, when he washed it and left the day before standing and wrote nothing, and about four people in this city have worked out what a board nobody writes on is and about nine of them have not.**

"Put it back up."

"**I am going to put it back up. I have put that board on that wall every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I am not going to stop because a wind came up off the flats and took the top bracket off it, and I am not going to write on it this morning either, and about four people in this yard are going to say that is the same thing I did on the seventieth day of that month and about nine of them are going to say it is not, and it is not, and I would like it entered that those two are different and that nobody in this yard has worked out how.**"

"It is the same thing."

"**It is not the same thing, and the difference is the wind. On the seventieth day of that month I washed that board and looked at it and decided not to write on it, and about nine people in that yard decided at the same time not to ask me, and that is two people doing a thing. This morning the board came off the wall at the sixth hour and there is a thing on the ground, and I have not decided anything, and about four people in this city have worked out that a page nobody wrote on and a page that came off a wall are the same object in two different conditions and about nine of them have worked out that the second one is not anybody's decision.**"

He turned the board over on the boards, and the writing on the back of it was the writing from the fiftieth day of that month and it had gone into the grain so far that about four people could read it and about nine could not.

---

**And a man of about forty-two came up that road at about the seventh hour and went to the back of the yard and stood against the wall under the ninth line with his hands in his pockets, and he has had his hands in his pockets since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month at the back of a line of his own making, and there is no queue on a Monday. There has not been one since the seventieth day of that month, and about four people in that yard have noticed that he is standing in a yard with nothing in it and about nine of them had stopped noticing on the forty-ninth day of that month.**

"You are standing under your own line."

"**I am standing under a line and it is mine and I have not said a word about it and about four people in this yard have worked out that a man who stands under a page he wrote on a Monday morning is a man who is waiting for something off it, and about nine of them have worked out that a man who stands under it with his hands in his pockets is a man who has already worked out there is nothing coming off it, and I have not told anybody which of those two it is and I am not going to at the seventh hour on a Monday.**"

"Nothing came off it on the last four of them."

"**Nothing came off it on the last four of them and the top of it has gone pale in about four places this morning and that is five, and about four people in this city have been counting the Tuesdays since the forty-ninth day of that month and about nine of them have been counting the words on the top of it, and I have been doing the first of those two things and not the second and I would like it entered that a man who wrote a page is the worst person in this city to be counting what is coming off it.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came up that road at about the seventh hour with a bar over his shoulder and a bag against his chest and there is a mark on the front of his coat where the bag has sat, and it has been a bag against his chest for fourteen days and the sacking has gone stiff over the shape of what is in it.**

"I have heard your board is off."

"**I have not said anything about my board to anybody and there is nobody between here and this city who would have said anything about it, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man comes up nine miles with a bag against his chest and does not go to the door, and about nine of them have worked out that a man who comes up nine miles with a bag against his chest and comes in anyway is a different man, and I would like both of those out of this yard before I open my mouth about either.**"

"You have come in."

"**I have come in and I have not put the bag down and I am not going to put the bag down, and about four people in this yard have been waiting since the seventy-third day of that month for me to put it down and about nine of them have stopped, and I have had fourteen days of carrying it and a sacking that has learned the shape of it, and a man who has carried a thing long enough that the thing has learned him is not a man carrying a thing any more, he is a man being carried by one.**"

He put his hand flat on the front of the coat where the mark was and about four people in that yard watched his hand go to a mark and about nine of them watched the bag.

---

**And the top line of the posting on that wall has gone pale in patches where the wind has been at it, and the top line is the forty-sixth day of the Long Month, and the ninth and last line is under it in a different hand about nine feet above the back of the last person who would stand under it, and about four people in that yard can read the top line this morning and about nine of them cannot.**

"That is going."

"**That is going. It is chalk on a wall at about nine feet and it has been on that wall for thirty days and the wind has been at it since the sixty-ninth day of that month, and about four people in this city have worked out that a date at the top of a page is the first thing that goes and about nine of them have worked out that the ninth line is under the date and not beside it.**"

"**Then it is about to be a page with no day on it.**"

"**Then it is about to be a page with no day on it, and the eight lines under it are prices in words, and there is not one figure under any of the three of them, and the one figure this city has ever put under one of those prices is on a slate in a woman's hand in a yard nine miles from here, and if the top of that page goes then what is left of that page is nine lines in two hands and a date somebody chose, and the date is the part that is leaving.**"

The woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with her arms wet to the elbow and did not look at either wall, and about four people in that yard watched her not look and about nine of them had not known there was anything to look at.

---

**And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from a market had walked nine miles to be in that yard and had her own wage under her arm where it has been since the fifty-sixth day of that month, and she put her hand on the boards where the board had landed and the boards were the colour of the boards under them and had been since the seventy-fourth day of that month.**

"Your measures."

"**My nine measures were hard on the thirty-ninth day of that month and they are hard now and they are not on the shelf they were on, and I am not going to say where they are, and about four people in this city have worked out that I have moved nine measures on account of a wind and about nine of them have worked out that I have moved them on account of the shelf and both of those are the same movement and I have only got one reason and it is the one about the wind.**"

"Then what is the reason."

"**The reason is that a shelf against an outside wall is a place where a thing dries, and nine measures of salt that have gone hard will go hard faster on a shelf against an outside wall than they will on a shelf that is not, and a woman who has been paying a figure for salt since the twelfth day of that month is a person who knows what nine measures are and I have not got anybody else in this yard who does.**"

She put her hand flat on the boards the way she had put it flat on her own arm, and about four people in that yard saw a woman press a yard and about nine of them saw a woman check a floor.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put the cloth over the rim of the trough and came out of the door and stood about nine feet from the wall and looked at the two things on it, and about four people in this city have worked out that the board and the posting are about a foot apart and have been since the forty-sixth day of that month and about nine of them worked it out on the fifty-sixth and have not said it in a yard since. He said it now, and he said it about the wind and not about the wall.**

"Nobody has said that out loud in a yard."

"**Nobody has said it out loud in a yard and I said it in this yard on the fifty-sixth day of that month and about four people in this city have been carrying it since and about nine of them have had it and did not know they had it, and this morning there is a wind on that wall that would take the two of them at different rates, and a board is washed every morning and a page is not washed at all, and about four people in this city have worked out that in about four days the foot between them is going to be a foot of chalk dust and about nine of them have worked out that the dust is going to be on the same wall either way.**"

"So it does not matter."

"**It matters to about four people in this city and to nobody in four hundred miles, and the only reason it matters is that about nine people read the board and about four read the page, and on the fifty-sixth day of that month about four people in that yard had read the board and not the page, and I have washed that board every morning since and I have not done anything about the other one because I am not the man who put it there.**"

He put his thumb on the wall under the place where the date had been and about four people in that yard watched a man's thumb find a bare patch and about nine of them watched a man find nothing at all.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put it back up on the wall at about the eighth hour and washed the trough out and put the cloth over the rim and stood looking at the two of them, and he said one thing about the difference between the two objects on that wall, and about four people in that yard heard all of it and about nine of them heard the words and not the rest.**

"Which of the two is the day."

"**The board is the day and the posting is the page, and about four people in this city have had that the wrong way round for thirty days and about nine of them have had it the right way round and have never been in this yard at the seventh hour in the morning. A day is a thing that happens once and a page is a thing that stays up, and I have been washing a thing that happens once every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I did not know that was a different kind of thing until this morning when the wind took it off the wall and I could see that it is only a day while it is hanging there.**"

"You will write on it tomorrow."

"**I do not know. I have washed it every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I have never once left a figure standing that was not there, and about four people in that yard have worked out that a man who has never done a thing and then stops doing it is a man who has decided something, and about nine of them have worked out that a man who has stopped because there is nothing to put on it has not decided anything, and I have not been able to tell the two apart from the inside either, and I have been standing at this wall since the sixth hour of the morning.**"

---

**And a thumb put flat on the bare patch of a wall where a date had been, and about a foot of chalk dust on the boards underneath it.**
