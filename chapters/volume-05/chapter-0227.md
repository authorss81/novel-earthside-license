# Chapter 0227 — Four Lengths Of Wood

A length of wood about four feet long came up the bank at the bottom of the Redroot cut at about the seventh hour of the morning on the seventy-second day of the Long Month on a man's shoulder. About nine people at the top of that bank had seen a length of wood come up it four times since the fifty-ninth day of the Long Month, and about four of them had worked out where it had come from and had not said it. The cut had been dry for thirty-one days. The bottom of it was pale and cracked and there is a place about eleven feet in where a rod has been lying flat since the sixty-ninth day of that month

---

**And the man who carried it had carried racks up and down that cut since the second month of this flood and has never once been asked to work one and is not asking, and he put the length of wood down on the boards of the yard at the top of the cut and about four people in that yard looked at it and about nine of them had been waiting for somebody to bring something to that yard that was not a sack.**

"Where did you get that."

"**Off a rack. I will say it in those words and I will not say anything else about it, and about four people in this yard have worked out since the eighth hour of the morning that every length of wood anybody in this city has been holding for the last about nine days came off a rack, and I am the first one to say it out loud and I have said it in a yard and about nine people heard it.**"

"Which rack."

"**I am not going to say which rack. A rack is four lengths of wood and a rope and four people, and about nine of the racks in four hundred miles can be worked and every one of those nine is on somebody's rota, and if I name one rack in this yard then about four people will go and look at it this morning and the man of about fifty-two who digs has got a beam with two wood pans on it that he has been carrying nine miles since the fifty-eighth day of the Long Month, and about four people in this city know that and about nine of them have not worked it out.**"

Somebody in that yard said the beam.

"**The beam. I am not going to say the beam either, and I want it entered that a man brought a length of wood up a bank on the seventy-second day of the Long Month and did not name a rack and did not name a beam, and that about nine people heard both halves of that and about four of them have said since that I made a speech and I did not make a speech, I made a decision, and the decision is that a rack is not mine.**"

---

**And the man of about fifty-two who digs was in that yard at about the ninth hour and he had come up that bank with the beam under his arm and the crack in the far pan was not visible from about nine feet and he had not told anybody about it in three days. He put the beam down on the boards and did not put the length of wood anywhere near it and about four people in that yard saw him not do that.**

"You have heard."

"**I have heard and I came up here to hear it said to my face and it has not been said to my face, and I am going to be annoyed about that for the rest of the day, and about four people in this yard have been waiting three days for me to be told by somebody else and about nine of them knew I already had it.**"

"You already had it."

"**I had it on the fifty-eighth day of that month when I made this out of a length of iron and two lengths of wood and I have not been able to say whose either of them was, and I have said that out loud in about nine rooms since the second month of this flood and nobody has ever picked it up. This morning about four people in this city have picked it up at the same time and none of them has picked it up in front of me.**"

He put his hand into the far pan and the crack took the side of his forefinger about the width of a nail. He took his hand out, looked at it, and then took a length of sackcloth out of his coat, wrapped it round his hand and tied it with a piece of string. About four people in that yard watched him do it and about nine of them were still looking at the length of wood on the boards.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-nine who cannot get a rack above her shoulder was at the top of that yard and she has been at the top of that cut twice since the fifty-ninth day of the Long Month and she said one sentence at about the ninth hour and it was about the nine, and about four people in that yard heard all of it.**

"There are about nine racks and about four hundred and forty people."

"**There are about nine racks that can be worked and about four hundred and forty people on eleven miles of flats, and about nine of those four hundred and forty are on somebody's rota and the rest have never been on one, and the nine racks are on the rota as well, and about four people in this city have added the two nines together and about nine of them have not, and I am not going to add them together in a yard either because I have been in two rooms in this flood where somebody added two numbers together and everybody in the room started believing the sum.**"

"They are the same number."

"**They are the same number and they are not the same thing and I have been trying to say that to about four people since the forty-ninth day of the Long Month and I have never once managed it in fewer than nine sentences, and I have said nine of them at the top of this yard and the ninth one was the true one and I did not say it, and I would like somebody to write down that I did not say it and not why.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything about that and about four people in it understood every word of it and about nine of them heard a woman talk about two numbers. **And she cannot get a rack above her shoulder and she did not say why today either, and about four people in that yard had worked it out on their own inside nine days and about nine of them had not asked her about it once.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-two came up that bank at about the tenth hour and stood at the top of the cut and did not come down, and he has no mark and no workway and no title and no seat, and about four people in that yard knew he had been on that bank five Mondays in a row and about nine of them did not know what he was.**

"You cannot have that length of wood."

"**I did not say I wanted it and I am not going to say I did not want it, and a rack is four lengths of wood and I have never had a rack and I am not going to have one now, and I have about three hundred days of being angry at myself about that and I am not going to spend one of them on a length of wood that came off somebody else's frame in a yard.**"

"You have never asked."

"**I have never asked anybody for a rack in two hundred and twenty-seven days and I am not going to start at the top of a cut on a Thursday morning with about nine people standing in it, and I would like somebody to write that down as a fact about a man and not as a virtue, because I have not been able to work out which of the two it is since the seventh week of this flood.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came up that bank at about the tenth hour with a bar over his shoulder and a bag against his chest, and the bag has had a lump in it since the sixty-sixth day of that month, and he stood at the edge of that yard and looked at the length of wood on the boards for about a minute and about four people in that yard watched him look at it and about nine of them watched the bag.**

"**I am not going to ask for it. I want it entered that I stood here at the tenth hour of the morning on the seventy-second day of the Long Month and looked at a length of wood for about a minute and did not ask for it, and about four people in this yard saw me do that and about nine of them have said since that the man who said a page can be corrected by another page came eleven miles for a length of wood.**"

"Why did you come up the bank."

"**Because a bar does not get into a lump and I have had that lump against my chest for ten days and I have taken it to about nine places and about four of them had a bar and none of the four bars went in, and this is the fifth place and I am not going to a sixth, and I am not going to break it, and I said in a yard on the sixty-sixth day of that month that I am not going to find out by breaking it and I have not.**"

"You could take the wood."

"**I could take a length of wood off a rack in this yard in about four seconds with about four people watching and nobody would stop me, and I am not going to, and I am not going to say why in this yard, because the why is that I said a sentence on the sixty-fifth day of that month about a word that cannot be corrected and the wood would be the first thing I have done about it and I am not going to do it for a lump of salt in a bag.**"

He put the bar down on the boards at the edge of that yard, well away from the length of wood, and about four people in that yard watched him put it down and about nine of them did not, and he picked it up again at about the eleventh hour and went back down the bank with the bag against his chest and did not say anything on the way down.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-nine who cannot get a rack above her shoulder put her hand on the length of wood at about the tenth hour of the morning and lifted one end of it about four inches off the boards and put it down again, and nobody in that yard said a word about it, and about four people in that yard have said since that a woman who cannot lift a rack lifted a length of wood and did not say so, and about nine of them have said that she was about to say so and she was not.**

"That is nothing like a rack."

"**It is nothing like a rack. A rack is four lengths and a rope and four people and I cannot get one above my shoulder, and I have just lifted one end of a length of wood off a board to find out whether I still could, and I still could not, and I have not said that out loud and I am not going to, and about four people in this yard have worked it out from about nine feet and about nine of them have not looked up from the boards.**"

Nobody improved on it. The end of the wood went back down on the boards in the same wet print it had been lying in and about four people in that yard saw that and about nine of them had not, and about four people in this city have said since that a woman who cannot lift a rack lifted a length of wood and did not say so, and about nine of them have said that she was about to say so and she was not.

---

**And the light went off the boards of that yard and a length of wood was still on them, four feet from a beam.**
