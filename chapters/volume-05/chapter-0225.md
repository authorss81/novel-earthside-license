# Chapter 0225 — A Line And No Wage

The queue formed along the wall of the store on the upper road at about the sixth hour of the morning on the seventieth day of the Long Month, and it formed the way it forms, and about a hundred and forty people were in it by the time the sun came over the roof. It is the fourth Tuesday that queue has run on counting from the forty-ninth day of the Long Month, which is the one it formed on, and the fifty-sixth day of that month was the second and the sixty-third was the third, and this is the second one it has run on with salt in that chest instead of a wage. The ground under it had gone hard all the way to the foot of the wall and it had been wet two days before and had not been walked on since.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's own board came out at the seventh hour with the cloth and washed it and did not write anything on it and left the figures of the day before standing, which was a Monday, and about nine people were paid on that Monday and about a hundred and forty were not. The posting is on the same wall about a foot to the left of it and is twenty-four days old. He said one thing in that yard at the seventh hour and about four people heard all of it and about nine of them heard the words and not the rest.**

"I have nothing to write."

"You could leave it blank."

"**I could leave it blank and I am not going to leave it blank, because a blank board is a board that says nothing happened on a day, and about nine people did not happen on that Monday. What happened on that Monday is what is standing on that wall. What is going to happen this morning is a thing that has no figures in it and I am not going to invent a shape for it and put a day at the top of the shape.**"

"Who told you to leave the day before's up."

"**Nobody told me to do anything. I have washed that board every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I have written the day before on it every morning since the seventh week of this flood, and I have never once in two hundred and twenty-five days left a figure standing that was not there, and this morning is the first morning there is nothing to put, and I am not going to treat that as an instruction from anybody including myself.**"

He put the trough back under the board and the water in it had a skin of chalk gone grey across the top of it, and about four people in that yard watched him not write and about nine of them read what was already there.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with the slate face down against her thigh, where it has been since the sixty-fourth day of that month, and about a hundred and forty people were in that yard and nobody asked her what was in the chest, and about four of them had decided before the seventh hour that they were not going to.**

"You are not opening it."

"**I am not opening it, and I am not going to tell anybody what is in it, and nobody has asked me, and about nine people in this yard have been in a line for four hours this morning and about four of them got here on the strength of a day that is a Tuesday and not on the strength of anything I did, and I am not going to be the reason they walked nine miles.**"

"Then it is a wage."

"**Then it is whatever you say it is, and a chest that is opened in a yard in daylight in front of about nine people is a wage, and a chest that is not opened is not a wage, and I am not a person who gets to say which of those two things it is. I asked what the date was in four words at the seventh hour of the evening on the thirtieth day of the Long Month and I was told nothing, and I have never asked anybody to write that down, and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.**"

She went back inside and left the door open behind her, and about four people in that yard have said since that the open door was the only sentence in that exchange and about nine of them have not. The thirty-nine men under the near gate of the Lock of Salt were not in that yard on that morning and are on no line of the page on that wall, and nobody in that yard said so, and about four people in this city have said since that they were going to say so in a yard on the sixty-seventh day of the Long Month.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was in that yard at about the seventh hour and stood under the ninth line and read it without looking up, because he does not need to look up at a page he wrote, and it is in a different hand from the other eight lines and it is nine feet above the back of the last person standing under it. He said two sentences and then did not say anything else for the rest of the morning.**

"**That is the only reason about nine people in this yard are standing here and I wrote it there on purpose and I would write it there again, and it is the reason and it is not the wage, and about four people in this yard have worked that out and about nine of them have worked out the other half of it, which is that if the ninth line comes off that wall then the only thing anybody has ever said out loud about a queue being the only functioning way out of this world is said in about nine rooms by about four people who remember it.**"

"Does it work."

"**I am not going to be the man who says whether that line works on a morning when there is nothing at the front of it, and I have been asked that in a market and I have not answered it, and I am not going to answer it here with a hundred and forty people in the yard. You are asking me to put a figure on a thing I have said four times in four yards that a figure does not go on, and I have said that sentence out loud in about nine rooms and about four of them wrote it down.**"

He put his hands in his sleeves and went to the back of the yard and stood there, and about four people in that yard had been waiting for him to take it off the wall and about nine of them had not.

---

**And a man of forty-two came up that road at about the seventh hour and went to the back of that queue and did not come to the front of it and did not go into the store, and he had his hands in his pockets, which is where he has had them since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month at the back of a line of his own making. About four people in that yard noticed him come in and about nine of them had stopped noticing on the forty-ninth day of that month.**

"You opened it on the fifty-sixth."

"**I opened it on the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month in the middle of that yard in daylight in front of about nine people and there was salt in it, and nobody in that yard asked me what was going to happen. About four of them had decided before the lid was off that they were not going to ask me that in a yard, and I have not written a plan down since and I am not going to this morning. I have come nine miles to stand at the back of a queue with nothing at the front of it, and that is the whole of what I came for, and I would like about nine people in this yard to notice that I did not go to the door.**"

"You could have stayed in this city."

"**I could have stayed in this city and watched a board every morning, and I have watched a board every morning for fourteen days since the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month from four streets away, and a board is the only instrument in this city that has not lied to anybody, and I have not been able to say that on a Tuesday in a yard and I am not going to try.**"

He took his hands out of his pockets and put them back and about four people in that yard had been waiting fourteen days for him to do that, and nobody in that yard asked him a question all morning.

---

**And Adrian Vale came up that road on foot at about the eighth hour of the morning and there was a place at the back of that queue that would have held him, and he did not stand in it, and about nine people in that yard knew who he was before he got to the gate and about four of them had been waiting since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month for somebody to say a name.**

"You said it would not form."

"I did not say it in this yard."

"**I said it at a stall by a wharf on the sixty-fifth day of the Long Month to a woman who did not agree with me, and I said it because I have walked nine miles to a yard twice since the fifty-fourth day of that month and both times there was a reason to stand in it. This morning I have worked out that a queue is not a line of people waiting for a thing. It is a line of people standing where a thing was. I was wrong about that this morning and I have been wrong about it since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month, and nobody has corrected me because nobody knew I had said it.**"

"That is not a thing to be wrong about."

"**It is a thing to be wrong about in a yard at the eighth hour of a morning when there is a hundred and forty people in it, and I have been wrong about about nine things in this city in two hundred and twenty-five days and every one of them was this shape, and this is the first one I have been wrong about in front of people.**"

Nobody in that yard said a word. A man at the front of the queue put his hand flat on the boards in front of him and left it there, and about four people nine feet from Adrian Vale had worked out that he was the man who could have been fetched by a name and about nine of them had not.

---

**And a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom was in that yard with a slate he has carried since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and has not put down, and he put it down. He put it face up on the boards of that yard at about the ninth hour and about four people in that yard watched him do it and about nine of them had been carrying a slate for him since the forty-sixth day of that month and had never been asked to put it down.**

"I am not going to say it."

Nobody in that yard knew what he meant and about four of them did and had not expected to.

"**I am not going to say Adrian Vale's name in this yard today, or on any day this month, and I have written that down with the seventieth day of the Long Month at the bottom of it as the day I wrote it and not as the day it stops, and I wrote it this morning and I have told nobody that I wrote it, and I am telling about four of you now because a decision that is only in a man's head is a thing that does not exist and a decision on a slate is a page, and I know what a page is.**"

"You are putting a decision on a page."

"**I am putting a decision against myself on a page and I could not tell you this morning why that is different, and about nine people in this city have written down a decision in a book since the second day of this flood and not one of them has been on the other side of one. I have a slate under my arm since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month because I did not have anywhere to put it, and this morning I have put it face up on a board in a yard, and that is the whole of what I have done and I am not going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to.**"

He picked the slate up off the boards about a minute later and put it back under his arm, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that they had been waiting for him to say the name since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month and about nine of them had not, and nobody fetched anybody all morning.

---

**And the queue was still in that yard at the tenth hour with nothing at the front of it, and a man at the front had his hand flat on the boards where he had put it, and nobody fetched anybody.**
