# Chapter 0220 — A Page With A Mistake In It

A third man stood in front of the stall by the wharf at about the eighth hour of the morning on the sixty-fifth day of the Long Month with nothing in his hands. The wharf boards were wet and the water under them was moving without any wind behind it. There is no queue on a Thursday, and about nine people nine feet away had worked out before he got to the fourth step that he was not the second person anybody had sent and was not the second person anybody had copied.

---

**And he said the sentence, and it was wrong in one word, and the word was wall. About four people nine feet away heard it and about nine of them did not know that there was a wall in it. The woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall had a slate in her hand that she has carried for nine years and has never written on, and the slate was the size of her forearm, and she did not put it down.**

"There is a woman at this stall whose name is on a wall."

"That is not the sentence."

"**It is the sentence I was given. I have got it from a woman at a fire in a market who got it off a man who got it off a man who was in a room on the forty-fifth day of the Long Month. I have walked four streets. I have not got a copy and I have not got anything in my hands and I have come because somebody has told me there is a wall in this city with a name on it and I have never seen a name on a wall that I could go and look at.**"

"It is not a wall."

"**Then it is not a wall. I do not know what it is and I have not got anything in my hands and I am not going to be the third man to stand in front of this stall and be wrong about a thing I could have asked about at a fire.**"

---

**And she came out from behind the stall, and she has been on a page since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and it is the sixty-fifth day and it is twenty days, and two men had stood in front of that stall on the fifty-third day of that month. About four people in this city have said since that there would be a third, and about nine of them have said that a rumour is not a page and is in no book and no office nine hundred miles away can do anything about it. She asked in advance not to be thanked and has not been thanked.**

"There is no wall."

"Somebody said there was."

"**Somebody said there was, and about four people said it to about four other people between the forty-fifth day of the Long Month and the sixty-third day of that month, and it has got itself a wall out of nothing. There is a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board who said in a yard on the forty-sixth day of that month that he would rather have a page with a mistake in it than a wall of good sentences, and about four people in this city have worked out since that what I have is the first half of that sentence and he did not say the other half and I am not going to say it for him.**"

"I am not going to say your half either."

"**Then somebody will.**"

---

**And she corrected it, in a yard, in daylight, at about nine feet from four people, on the sixty-fifth day of the Long Month, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her. It is four sentences long, and she has not said anything in a yard that anybody could hold her to since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month, and about four people in that yard knew what that was and about nine did not.**

"**There is no wall in this city with my name on it and there is no page anybody can walk to. There is a copy on the back of a slate that a man of about forty-four made on a table in a market on the forty-fifth day of the Long Month because he thought a page ought to be in two places, and it has been in more hands than that since, and a word has been in about nine mouths since, and the word has been in more places than the copy for about eleven days, and the wall is the one thing neither of them has ever been in.**"

"That is not the same as saying there is no wall."

"**It is exactly the same as saying there is no wall, and it took me twelve days to work out how to say it without my name in it, and about nine people in this city have been able to say that there is no wall since the fifty-third day of that month and about four of them have been saying the wrong half of it on purpose, and I am not going to thank anybody for either.**"

Nobody in that yard said a word. A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by a wharf went back behind her stall and came out again with nothing in her hands and stood there, and about four people in that yard were angry and about nine of them were not. About four people in that yard have said since that the price she named on the forty-fourth day of the Long Month was being findable while she is arguing, and this morning she was not arguing about anything except a word with a wall in it. About nine of them have said that the price is the same and about four of them have said that it is worse, and nobody in that yard has said which of the two is right, and the woman who named it did not ask them.

---

**And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall was in that yard at about the ninth hour and had come down the four streets from the market, and he made the copy. About four people in that yard had worked that out and about nine had not, and he said one thing and it was about the copy and not about the woman.**

"**A man at a fire in the market asked me on the sixty-first day of the Long Month to make a third one, for somebody up the road, and I said no, and I said no because the first copy is a page and a third copy would be the second rumour, and about nine people in that market have told me since that I have said no to a page and yes to a page and that is not a distinction.**"

"It is not a distinction."

"**It is a distinction I made up on a Thursday morning and I would like somebody to write down that I made it up and that I have been making things up since the seventh week of this flood and that this one is the first one that has got somebody into a yard.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything was in that yard with a slate under her arm and she wrote the sentence the third man had said down, and then she wrote the woman's four sentences underneath it, and she did not join them up. She left the space between the two the width of two fingers, and about four people in that yard watched her leave it.**

"Two sentences with a wall in one of them."

"**Two sentences, and about four people in this city have added the two of them together and about nine have taken one from the other, and I have done both of those things since the fiftieth day of the Long Month and neither of them is in either book, and I have not joined them up and I am not going to, because the day I join a copy and a word up is the day somebody writes *a rumour is a page* in a book and then a page and a word are the same thing and somebody will act on that.**"

"So they are not the same thing."

"**They are the same shape and they are not the same thing, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board has a sentence about a page with a mistake in it and she has a sentence about a word with a wall in it, and neither of them is about the other, and I have not put them next to each other and I would like somebody to notice that I did not.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came down to that wharf at about the second hour of the afternoon and said the other half of his own sentence. He is a party to the compact the posting is bound to and he refused a line of it on the fifty-first day of the Long Month and could not afterwards tell anybody which line he had refused. About four people in that yard had been waiting since the eighth hour of the morning for him to come down four streets, and he said it in about four sentences and about nine people heard it. About four of them have said since that it is the worst sentence anybody has said in this city since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month, and that it is about him.**

"**I said in a yard on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month that I would rather have a page with a mistake in it than a wall of good sentences, and about four people have been carrying that sentence since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month like a thing I said that was clever. It is not about a page. A page can be taken down and I have watched a woman do it on the sixty-fourth day of the Long Month nine miles up that road, in about four minutes, in a back room, and the page is gone. A word cannot be taken down, and every one of the about nine people in this city who has said that sentence with a wall in it said it to somebody else first, and there is no office nine hundred miles away that can stop a word and there never has been.**"

"So the wall was the right answer."

"**The wall was an accident. A copy is a page and a copy can be corrected by another copy and a wall cannot be corrected by anything at all, and a word with a wall in it cannot be corrected by anything at all, and about nine people in this city have worked that out over about twenty days and not one of them has said it out loud and I have said it out loud in a yard on the sixty-fifth day of the Long Month because a woman refused to say it for me and I have been waiting four streets to do it myself.**"

"And what will you do about it."

"**Nothing. That is the whole of the second half of the sentence and it took me four streets to walk and I have said nothing to anybody since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month about a page of mine and I am not going to start on a Thursday. I cannot un-say a thing in nine mouths. I have been the man who says a cost out loud in four sentences in this city since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and I have never once been able to take one back and I am not going to be able to take this one back either and about four people in this yard are going to say that is an excuse and about nine of them are not going to say anything, and I would like it entered that I came four streets to say nothing.**"

---

**And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon a man of about fifty-two who digs came down to the wharf with a rod he has not offered anybody, and there was dried mud on it in two places and he had not cleaned it off. About nine people in that yard had not seen him in about nine days and about four of them had, and he said four sentences and none of them was about salt.**

"**I have been in a hole nine feet from the one everybody measures and I have got nothing. Not a figure and not a sentence. I have been down there six days with a rod and I have come here because I have not got anything and I would rather stand about nine feet from a person than go back up eleven miles and be alone with a rod.**"

"That is the first thing anybody has said to me today that is not about a page."

"**It is and it is not. I have come down eleven miles to say that I have got nothing, and about four people in this yard have heard that a man with a rod has come down eleven miles to say that he has got nothing, and that is a sentence about me and not about a page, and I have had six days of that.**"

---

**And a stall by a wharf on a Thursday afternoon with nobody in front of it, and a slate not written on in nine years, and about four people nine feet away who had come to look at a woman.**
