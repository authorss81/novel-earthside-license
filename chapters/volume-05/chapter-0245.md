# Chapter 0245 — Back On The Rack

A man of about thirty-two came down the bank of the cut on the Reed Reach on the ninetieth day of the Long Month at about the seventh hour of the morning, and he came back up it at about the tenth hour with nine feet of rope over his shoulder, and he put that rope on the top length of a rack at the edge of the cut, and the rack is not his, and its owner was not there, and has not been down that bank since the seventy-second day of that month. The cut has had no water in it since the forty-first day of that month and there is a thumb's depth of the dust off eleven miles of flats in the bottom of it, and the wind has been coming up that bank since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and the light came off those flats at about the eighth hour and lay along the floor of the cut and showed the rope where it lay.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-nine who cannot get a rack above her shoulder was at the top of that bank when he went down and was still at the top of it when he came up, and she did not ask him a question on either of the two trips, and about four people at the top of that cut have noticed that and nobody else has.**

"You went down for that."

"**I went down for that. It has been in the dust at the bottom of that cut since the eighty-seventh day of that month, and it is not my rope, and I went down that bank for it because I was standing on a bank on a Monday morning with nothing on the bank and nothing in the month, and a rope in a dry cut is the only thing in this city that has been going downhill since the eighty-fifth day of that month.**"

"And now it is going up."

"**And now it is going up, and that is the whole of the day so far, and I am not going to tell you what I would have done with a Monday morning if there had not been a rope in it, because there was a rope in it. This is the eighth Monday in a row I have stood on this bank and I have not been down it once before the fifty-ninth day of that month, and about four of those eight have been about a hole and about four of them have not been about anything.**"

"You have come down it twice."

"**I have come down it twice and both of those were about a thing somebody else had dropped, and I would like it noticed that neither of them was about me, and that on the eighty-third day of that month I came down because there was dust in the bottom of it and I have never seen dust in a cut in this flood, and this morning I came down because there was a rope in the dust.**"

---

**And he had the coil in his two hands at the bottom of that cut and the dust had got into it and it came apart about a foot from the end, and the length of it that came off lay along the floor of the cut in a shape that nothing had made.**

"It is stiff."

"**It is stiff and it is grey with the same dust as the floor, and it has been lying in that dust three days, and a coil that has been in dust is a coil that opens wrong. I got my thumb under it about a foot along and turned the whole of it over once before I took it up, and that is the only technique anybody has.**"

"Try it there."

"**No. If I open it here it comes apart in pieces, and I cannot put a knot in anything that is going up a bank on one arm, and the top length of that rack is about nine feet above where I am standing. I am not throwing nine feet of somebody's rope at a board.**"

He got his fingers under the coil at four points along it and lifted it off the floor of that cut in one piece. It came up heavier than nine feet of rope ought to be and the dust came off it onto his knees and stayed there, and he stood at the foot of the bank for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet before he started up.

---

**And there was a rod lying flat in a crack in the floor of that cut where a man had put it on the sixty-ninth day of that month and had not picked it up since, and he went past it twice and did not touch it, and there was about a thumb's depth of the same dust in the crack around it that is in every other crack in that floor.**

"**And it has been in that crack since the sixty-ninth day of that month and there is about a thumb of the flats in the crack around it now, and about four people in this city have worked out that a rod left flat in a crack is a rod somebody meant to come back for, and nobody has worked out that I have not come back for it in twenty-one days and I have come down that bank twice since the fifty-ninth day of that month.**"

"That is yours."

"**That is mine and I put it there on the sixty-ninth day of that month and I have not picked it up and I am not going to pick it up today. I came down this bank for a rope and not for a rod, and nobody has worked out that I have no use for either of them and that is why I came.**"

"Did you come for the rope."

"**I came down the bank. What I did at the bottom of it is a second thing and it happened at the ninth hour, and the bank is what I came down and the rope is what I came back up with, and I have not got those two the right way round in my own head this morning and I am not going to arrange them for anybody else.**"

---

**And the man who carries racks came to the top of that bank at about the tenth hour and looked at the coil on the top length of his own rack, and it was not the coil that had been on it on the eighty-third day of that month, and he did not touch it, and he did not say one word about it to the man of about thirty-two or to the woman of about thirty-nine or to anybody.**

"That is not the coil I left."

"**It is not the coil you left, and it is nine feet of rope and it has been in the dust at the bottom of that cut since the eighty-seventh day of that month, and I turned it over once on the end and I put the loose end under and I did not cut it and I did not splice it, and I am going to tell you now that I do not know how I tied it.**"

"I did not ask you."

"**No, and I am telling you anyway, and about four people at the top of this cut have worked out that a man who came up a bank with somebody else's rope has been somewhere he was not asked to be, and the rest of that cut has worked out that a rope on a rack is a rope on a rack and there is nothing to work out.**"

---

**And the man of about thirty-two said a thing to nobody at about the tenth hour of that morning while the other two were looking at a coil of rope, and about four people at the top of that cut heard all of it and the rest heard nothing, and it was the only thing he had said all morning that was not a reply.**

"I stand up here on a Monday because it is a Monday."

"**I do not know why that is, and I have been on that bank eight Mondays and I have not got an answer, and nobody has worked out that I have been on that bank eight Mondays and could not tell you what a Monday is for, and there is no water in that cut and there has not been since the forty-first day of that month and I did not come up here for the water.**"

"Will you come on the ninety-seventh day of that month."

"**I will not. I have come down it twice and I have been up it eight Mondays and this morning is the last of the eight, and I have worked that out at about the seventh hour and I have not told anybody, and the reason I have not told anybody is that about nine people in this city have worked out that a man who stands on a bank has stopped, and I have not stopped, I have finished, and those two are about nine inches of difference on a Monday morning and I did not want to say it out loud before the rope was back.**"

She did not answer him and he did not say anything else, and about four people at the top of that cut heard a man say that he was finished and the rest heard a man say that he was going to stop, and both of those were said by one man on a bank at the tenth hour of the ninetieth day of the Long Month.

---

**And the top length of that rack had nine feet of rope on it at about the tenth hour with the tie in a place it was not tied before, and the rack was still at the edge of the cut and still not in it, and the woman of about thirty-nine did not come down that bank again.**

He looked at the coil from about four feet off for a while and then he reached out and took the loose end and pulled it a hand's width tighter and left it, and that was the only thing anybody did to that rack on the ninetieth day of the Long Month, and the light went off the flats at about the eleventh hour and the dust in the bottom of that cut began at the south end of the floor and worked its way up the cut the way it does.

---

**And the man of about thirty-two went up that bank at about the eleventh hour on the ninetieth day of that month with dust on both knees, and the woman of about thirty-nine went along the top of the cut with him for about nine feet and then stopped, and neither of them looked down at the coil on the rack.**

"Your knees."

"**My knees, and the dust in that cut has been in my coat since the seventh hour, and I have not got a way to get it out of a coat except a brush and I have not got a brush, and nobody has worked out that I have not been down a channel since before the fifty-ninth day of that month and I did not want to go down this morning and I went down.**"

"Was there a reason."

"**There was a rope, and I have said that twice this morning, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man gives the same reason twice because the second one is for himself, and the second one is for myself. I came down that bank twice and both of those were for me, and I have not told anybody that until this moment and I have told it to a woman on a bank and not to a man at a gate.**"

She stopped at the place about nine feet along the top of that cut where the bank begins to come down and stood there, and the man of about thirty-two went on, and the two of them did not say one word about nine feet of rope in between them.

---

**And the cut at the bottom of that Reach has eleven miles of flats above it and about four hundred and forty racks on them, and the whole of that salt is dried by people who are paid in it for a buyer nine hundred miles away, and about four people at the top of that cut had worked out in the last four days that a rope on a rack at the edge of a cut is the only thing in eleven miles that nobody has an owner for.**

"You could tell the buyer."

"**There is no route in this world that reaches that buyer, and about four people in this city have worked out that a person who dries salt for a buyer nine hundred miles away is a person who works for a name, and nobody has worked out that the name is not a person and there is nobody at the end of it to be told that a rope went over a bank on the eighty-seventh day of that month.**"

"So it goes in the dust."

"**It goes in the dust, and the dust has been going up that cut since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and about nine people in this city have worked out that a thing in the bottom of a cut is in the bottom of that cut in the spring, and nobody has worked out that I am not going to be here in the spring, and the rope will be under the same thumb's depth of the flats that is in the channel now, and that is where a thing that was put down in a hurry ends up.**"

---

**And a coil of rope on a rack at the edge of a cut, tied the way a person ties one when he has done it once.**
