# Chapter 0221 — It Has Gone Hard

A bar went into a sack in the yard of the store on the upper road at about the eighth hour of the morning on the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month and stopped, and the man who put it in took it out and put it back in the same place and it stopped in the same place. The sun had not come over the roof of the store yet and the mud on the top of that yard had gone the colour of the boards under it. He had brought the bar up nine miles on his shoulder with the sack in a bag against his chest, and there was a mark on the front of his coat where the bag had sat, and the sack was one piece.

---

And he had bought it as a quarter of a sack in a market in this city at the fourth hour of the afternoon on the sixty-second day of the Long Month, and he had counted it out of his own hand into the other one's, and about four people in that market had watched him do it, and this morning it is a lump with a shape to it and no way into it. He is a party to the compact the posting is bound to. He said one thing in that yard and it was not about the sack.

"I paid a figure for this."

"**I know what you paid for it, and about four people in this yard have worked out what you paid for it, and I am not going to say it, and neither are you, because a figure in a yard about a quarter of a sack that will not come apart is a figure somebody will write on a slate tomorrow and I have seen what a slate does in this city since the forty-sixth day of that month.**"

"You have seen what a slate does."

"**I have watched a woman put a slate face down on a chest in the back of that store two days ago and about four people in this city asked her to put it back up, and she said no, and nobody has written a date anywhere since. So I am telling you what I think and I am not telling you what it is worth, and those are two different sentences and I have been careful about them for two days.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything. The bar came out of the sack with a piece of the sack still on it, and the piece was the size of a thumbnail and it came away in his hand and he put it down on the boards and looked at it.

---

And the woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from that market was standing about nine feet from the bar when the piece came off it, and she had walked nine miles with her own wage under her arm and she did not put it down, and about four people in that yard have said since that she could have carried it in a bag and carried it on her arm on purpose.

"That is mine too."

"**I know it is. I have had it under my arm for ten days and it has gone the same way as his and I have not opened it and I am not going to open it in a yard.**"

"You have looked at it."

"**I have put my thumb in the seam of it and I have taken my thumb out again and that is the whole of what I have done with it, and I would like somebody to write down that I have done that four times in ten days and that nobody has asked me why, because if somebody asks me why I am going to have to have an answer and I have not got one that is true.**"

---

And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with a slate in her hand that she was holding face down against her thigh, and she had carried it that way since the sixty-fourth day of that month. She is on no list in that store. She did not come to the sacks.

"That is my salt."

"**It is a quarter of a sack of salt that a man in this yard paid a figure for on the sixty-second day of that month, and it is not a sack, and if you want to know what is in the chest in the back of this store you are going to have to ask me in about nine days, because I am not opening that chest on a Friday.**"

"You are not opening it on any day."

"**I am not opening it on a Friday, and I am not going to tell you about the other days, and about four people in this yard have worked out that I am not going to be asked and about nine of them have worked out that they are not going to ask, and one of those nine is a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything and who is standing about nine feet from me with her hand on that bar so that it does not roll off the boards.**"

The bar had been rolling. It stopped when her hand went on it and it did not move again while she was there, and about four people in that yard watched a slate and a bar instead of a sack and about nine of them watched the sack.

---

And a man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's own board came out of the door with the cloth in his hand and stopped, and the board is on that same wall about a foot to the left of the posting and it was washed at the seventh hour and it carries the figures of the day before, which was a Thursday, and about nine of them were paid on that Thursday and about a hundred and forty were not.

"**I am not going to write anything about that sack. I want that said in this yard at the eighth hour of the morning before anybody asks me to. Whatever happens to a sack in this yard, there is not going to be a figure about it on that board at the seventh hour tomorrow morning, because that board is about a day and there is no day in that sack.**"

"Then what is it."

"**It is a quarter of a sack that a man paid a figure for and it is in a yard, and those are two things and a day is the third thing and the day is not going to be on my wall.**"

He wrung the cloth out over the trough and put the trough back under the board and did not say anything else, and about four people in that yard had been waiting for him to say what a lump of salt weighs and about nine of them had not been waiting for anything.

---

And about four people in that yard went into the back of that store at about the ninth hour and counted the knots on the sacks in the chest, because about four of those sacks had been opened and re-tied on the fifty-sixth day of that month and the knots on those four were not the knots on the others, and that is the only thing anybody has been able to say out loud about a sack in this city in ten days. The woman of about thirty-four in the store stood at the door while they did it and did not stop them and did not help them, and the four of them took about four minutes and got the same answer four times, and the answer was four, and nobody in that back room said a word about it.

"That is what he said on the fifty-sixth."

"**That is what he said on the fifty-sixth, and he said he did not know it until the lid was off, and I have had the lid off twelve times since and I still do not know whether those four were opened before or after, and I am not going to find out from a knot, because a knot is a thing somebody did with a rope and not a thing somebody did with a sack.**"

"Then what is it a thing."

"**It is a thing I can point at. That is all it is and I have got four of them and I am not going to ask anybody in this city to make a second use of it, and about four people in this yard have worked out that I would rather have four sacks I can point at than one sack I can weigh and I have not been able to work out why myself.**"

The sacks in the chest were hard to the touch through the sacking and the salt had come to the stitch line at the bottom of the two nearest the door, and the cold coming off the inside of the chest had a smell off the flats in it that was the same smell it had on the fifty-sixth day of that month and had got into the boards.

---

And the woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood, and she asked one question in that yard and about nine people heard it, and it was the only question anybody asked all morning that was not about the ninth line on the wall nine feet above them.

"Shall I write this down."

"You are going to write it down."

"**I am going to write it down, and I am going to put the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month at the top of it before I put anything else on it, and I am not going to join it to the line on that wall about a quarter of a sack, and I would like somebody in this yard to notice that I have not.**"

"You do that every time."

"**I do that every time because the day I join a lump of salt to a line on a page is the day this city has a column for salt, and there is not one, and about four hundred and forty people dry salt on eleven miles of flats and about nine of them have ever been on a rota and the other four hundred and thirty-one have not. Write the day and the two sentences and leave the rest of the page.**"

---

And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said the rest of it himself, at about the ninth hour, in the middle of that yard, standing about four feet from a bar with a thumbnail of sackcloth on it, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him a second question.

"**A sack you can pour is a sack. A quarter of a sack you can pour is a quarter of a sack, and I have been carrying that thing for four days calling it a quarter of a sack because that is the figure I paid. This morning it is a piece, and a piece does not have a part of it. If I break it I have got a lump and a lot of lumps and I do not know which of them is the quarter. I am not going to find out by breaking it, because the day I break it I have got two figures and neither of them is the one I paid and I cannot put a second figure on a thing in four hundred miles.**"

"You will have to open it eventually."

"**I will have to break it eventually, and break is not open, and that is the whole of what has happened to me this morning, and I have had four days of carrying a figure about with me and I have got a lump.**"

He picked the bar up off the boards and put it over his shoulder and said nothing else, and about four people in that yard had been waiting for him to say what the quarter was worth and about nine of them had not been waiting for anything.

---

And the man of about thirty-two came up that road at about the tenth hour with nothing in his hands and stood at the edge of the yard and did not come into it, and about four people in that yard knew who he was and about nine of them said nothing, and he has no mark and no workway and no title and no seat and he is on no roster of anything including that store's.

"That is not a sack."

"**It is not a sack. I have looked at it from the edge and I can tell you from here that it is not a sack, and I have known that since the eighth hour and I did not say anything until the ninth, and I would like somebody to write down that I waited an hour, because I do not think I know why I waited.**"

"Why did you wait."

"**Because a man who has nothing on any page in this city is a man who can be asked what a thing is, and I did not want to be the first one, and about nine people in this city have watched me not be the first one since the seventh week of this flood and about four of them have stopped noticing.**"

---

And a bar, and a piece of sackcloth the size of a thumbnail, and a lump in a bag against a man's chest, and the mud on the top of that yard gone the colour of the boards under it.
