# Chapter 0238 — Down The Bank

A rod was lying flat in the cracked bottom of a cut on the Reach at about the seventh hour of the morning on the eighty-third day of the Long Month where a man had left it fourteen days before, and a man came up the road at the same hour and went down the bank. The channel has had no water in it since the forty-first day of that month and it is the forty-second day of that, and the bottom is hard all the way across and the wind that had come up off eleven miles of flats since the seventy-fourth day of that month was down in the cut and not at the top of it, and about four people at the top of that cut had been standing there since the sixth hour and about nine of them had not known he was coming.

---

**And he is a man of about thirty-two who has no mark and no workway and no title and no seat and is on no roster of anything, and this is the seventh Monday in a row that he has stood on that bank, and the six Mondays before this one were the forty-first, the forty-eighth, the fifty-fifth, the sixty-second, the sixty-ninth and the seventy-sixth day of the Long Month, and the nearest of the six was seven days back. About four people at the top of that cut had counted them and about nine of them had not, and he has said in a market that he is not going to be a different one because somebody came up eleven miles to ask him in a better light.**

"You went down."

"**I went down. I have stood on this bank about nine times since the fifty-ninth day of that month and I did not come down it once, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who stands on a bank is a man who is looking at something he does not want to be in the same place as, and about nine of them have worked out that a man who comes down a bank is a man who has decided something, and I have not decided anything and I came down it because there is dust in the bottom of it and I have never seen dust in a cut and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who says a thing he has never seen is a man who is looking at something.**"

He went down the side the water used to come in on and about four people at the top of that cut watched a man go into a dry channel and about nine of them stayed where they were.

---

**And at the bottom of that cut there is a rack standing at the edge with the rope coiled on the top length that a man carried down a bank and refused to put in on the seventy-fifth day of that month, and there is a second hole nine feet from the one everybody measures with about a thumb's depth of the flats in the bottom of it and a rod standing in that. There is a rod lying flat in a crack about eleven feet further in, and none of the three things was moved by anybody this morning.**

"**That hole is done.**"

"**I do not know what that hole is and I have not been down here since before the fifty-ninth day of that month, and I can see from down here that it is not what it was on the seventy-eighth day of that month, and about four people at the top of that cut have watched a man look at a hole he has not been in for a month and about nine of them have watched him not go near it, and I have not gone near it and I am not going to.**"

"You came all the way down for that."

"**I came all the way down to be in it, and I have not been in this cut since before the fifty-ninth day of that month because a cut with water in it is a thing you stand at the top of and a cut with nothing in it is a thing you can stand in, and about four people in this city have worked out that and about nine of them have worked out that what I have actually said is that I would come down it if there was nothing in it, and there is nothing in it, and I would like somebody to notice that I have not said a word about why I have been waiting for that.**"

He put his hand flat on the cracked bed of that cut and the bed rang under his palm the way a road does, and about four people at the top of that cut heard it and about nine of them did not.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-nine who cannot get a rack above her shoulder was at the top of that bank at about the ninth hour and had been there since the seventh, and she has a refusal with a date on it that she has not shown anybody, and he has said in a market that he is not going to be the man who walks up nine feet and asks a woman whether a thing she said on a date is still the thing she says on the date, and he did not walk up nine feet, and he went down.**

"You came down instead of up."

"**I came down instead of up and I have not come up this bank to you and I am not going to, and about four people in this city have worked out that coming down a bank is the same as not coming up it and about nine of them have worked out that it is not, and it is not, and the reason I came down is that I have been standing on this bank on seven Mondays and looking at a cut and this morning I wanted to know what the dust was doing in it, and that is a thing about dust and not about you, and I would like that entered in that order.**"

"You have not asked me."

"**I have not asked you and I am not going to ask you and I have not asked you since the fifty-ninth day of that month, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who does not ask is careful and about nine of them have worked out that a man who does not ask is a man who has worked out what the answer would be, and I have not worked out what the answer would be, and I have not asked, and those are three different things and I have not got nine minutes for them.**"

---

**And a man who has carried racks up and down that cut since the second month of this flood was at the top of that bank at about the tenth hour and had come down it to the edge of the bottom and had put his hand on the rack and had not moved it, and a rack is four lengths of wood and a rope and four people, and about nine of the racks that can be worked in this city are on somebody's rota and this one is not. About four people at the top of that cut have known that since the seventy-second day of that month and about nine of them have only known that there is a rack.**

"**That rack is not going in.**"

"**That rack is not going in and it is not going to be going in on a Monday, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who has carried a thing down a bank eight days ago and has not put it in is a man who has changed his mind and about nine of them have worked out that he has not changed his mind and has not had a reason, and both of those have been said in this cut and I have not said either of them and I am not going to.**"

"You do not need a reason."

"**I do need a reason and I have not got one, and I said on the seventy-second day of that month that the rest of it was worth a day of walking and that I was not going to say it in a cut with about nine people in it, and it is eight days since I said that and there are about four people in this cut and I have not said it, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who says a thing is not going to say a thing and then does not say it is a man who wanted to be asked and about nine of them have worked out that he wanted to be asked by one person and there has not been one.**"

He took his hand off the top length of that rack and about four people at the top of that cut watched a man take a hand off four lengths of wood and about nine of them watched the rack stay where it was.

---

**And a man of about fifty-two who digs came up that bank at about the eleventh hour and went down it and stood next to him and neither of them said anything for about as long as it takes to walk back up a bank, and the man of about fifty-two looked at the dust in the second hole and at the rack at the edge and at the rod in the crack and about four people at the top of that cut had been counting the days since that man said four weeks in a market on the forty-seventh day of that month and about nine of them had not.**

"That is eight days."

"**That is eight days that a rack has been at the edge of the bottom of a cut, and I put it there on the seventy-fifth day of that month and did not put it in, and I have not come back down this bank since that day and I came back down it this morning because a man had gone into the bottom of it and I wanted to see what was there.**"

"You came to see the rod in the crack."

"**I came to see what a hole that has filled in with the wind looks like to a man who has not stood at the bottom of this cut since the seventy-fifth day of that month, and about four people in this city have worked out that I did not come down here to look at my own hole and about nine of them have worked out that I did, and I did, and I have not been in this cut since the seventy-fifth day of that month and a man went down it on a Monday and there is dust in it and I have got nothing to say about either of those two things.**"

He went back up the bank and did not say anything else, and about four people at the top of that cut watched a man go up and about nine of them had come down after him.

---

**And the man of about thirty-two was still standing in the bottom of that cut when the light came off the flats and went up the far side, and about four people at the top of that cut had been counting the Mondays since the forty-first day of the Long Month and about nine of them had not, and he had come down a bank on the seventh Monday of them and had not come up it, and the second hole was a thumb's depth of dust and a rod was standing in it and a rod was lying flat in a crack about eleven feet further in and a rack was at the edge with the rope coiled on the top length. Nobody had moved the rack or the rod, and the hole had gone by itself.**

"You have been down there an hour."

"**I have been down there about an hour and I have looked at three things and I have not touched one of them, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who comes down a bank looks and about nine of them have worked out that a man who comes down a bank puts a hand on something, and I have not put a hand on any of the three, and I have not worked out whether that is a decision or a habit and I have had an hour.**"

"The crack one."

"**The one in the crack is a rod a man put down flat on the sixty-ninth day of that month and did not put in a hole, and about four people in this city have worked out that a rod flat in a crack is a man admitting a thing and about nine of them have worked out that it is a rod, and I have been in about nine rooms in this city where a page had a date on a door and I have been in none of them where a man put an instrument down flat on purpose and left it, and I have not got a name for the second of those and I am not going to make one up standing in a dry channel.**"

He went up the bank at about the twelfth hour and about four people at the top of that cut watched a man go up a bank he had gone down and about nine of them had not been at the top of it that morning at all.

---

**And a man standing in the cracked bed of a cut with the light going up the far side of it, and nothing in his hands.**
