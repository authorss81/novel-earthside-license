# Chapter 0222 — A Lump And Not A Ninth

The beam went down onto two pans in the yard of the store on the upper road at about the seventh hour of the morning on the sixty-seventh day of the Long Month. A man of about fifty-two who digs set it on a block of stone he had brought up nine miles on his shoulder behind it, and then a lump of salt came down onto the near pan out of a bag. The pan took it without moving. The wood of both pans had gone the colour of wet ash and there is a crack in the far one that starts at the middle and runs out under the rim, and it has been in that pan since the fifty-ninth day of the Long Month.

---

And the crack was in the pan he had made himself on the fifty-eighth day of that month out of a length of iron and two lengths of wood, and he put his thumb in the crack and showed about nine people in that yard where it went, and nobody in that yard said anything about the crack and about four of them have said since that they did not see it until he put his thumb in it.

"That is my beam."

"**That is your beam and it has a crack in the far pan and it has been there since the fifty-ninth day of the Long Month and I have not told anybody about it at all, and I am telling you this morning because I am going to have to use it and I am not going to use a thing in front of about nine people without saying what is wrong with it first.**"

"You have used it about nine times."

"**I have used it about nine times and it has been right about a ninth of a sack every one of them, and this morning I am putting a thing on it that has not got a ninth in it, and that is the difference and I have known it since the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month and I did not come up the road on the sixty-sixth either because I did not want to have this conversation in a yard without the sack in it.**"

---

And he had not told anybody about the block of stone. It is about the length of his own foot and it came out of the bottom of a cut on the Reach and he has had it in his yard since the second month of this flood, and the beam sat crooked on it on the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month, and about four people in that yard this morning worked out that he had spent about an hour the evening before getting it flat.

"You brought a different thing."

"**I brought the same thing. I brought the same block of stone that I have brought every time, and the beam has sat crooked on it every time, and I have been putting the low place in it with my thumb since the second month of this flood and telling about nine people the beam was wrong, and on the afternoon of the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month a woman who counts water told me the beam was not wrong and the stone was, and I did not want to have that conversation in a yard without a sack in it.**"

"You would rather be wrong."

"**I would rather be wrong in front of about nine people than right in front of none. That is not a virtue and I have not put it in a column and I am not going to.**"

He put his thumb under the low place in the beam and showed them where it was, the same as he does, and about nine people in that yard had seen him do that before and about four of them looked at the block of stone instead of at his thumb.

---

"**A quarter of a sack. That is what is on that pan and I am not going to put a figure on it in four hundred miles and I am going to tell you the same way I told you the last time, which is I am telling you the thing and not the number, so that whoever wants the number has to put a thing in his own hand on it.**"

"You told us the last time you were wrong."

"**I have got that beam wrong by about a ninth since the second month of this flood and everybody within nine miles of this yard has been weighing salt in a bucket since the seventh week of this flood, and I am not going to be the man who puts a figure on a thing and then goes and digs a channel. Move the pan.**"

---

And he moved the pan along the beam four times and got it to sit, the way he does, and then he put both hands flat on the beam and left them there and looked at the lump for long enough that about four people in that yard had worked out that he was not going to be able to do the thing they had come for.

"It balances."

"**It balances. It is a quarter of a sack and it balances, and I can tell you what that is, and I cannot tell you what a part of that is, and I have had a beam since the second month of this flood that can do one of those two things and not the other and I have never needed the other one until a man came up nine miles on Friday with a figure in a bag and no way into it.**"

"You could break it."

"**A man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said in a yard nine miles up the road on the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month that he is not going to find out by breaking it, because the day he breaks it he has got two figures and neither of them is the one he paid. I am not going to be the second man in two days to say a better version of that, so I am going to say this instead. If I break that lump there is no beam in this yard that can tell anybody which piece is his. And a ninth of a sack was a figure a person could put their hand on. This is a figure a person has to break something to get.**"

He took his hands off the beam and wiped them on his coat and the salt came off on the cloth and left the coat white in four places.

---

And Adrian Vale came up that road on foot at about the eighth hour of the morning with nine miles on his boots and did not go into that store, and about nine people in that yard knew who he was before he got to the gate and about four of them had worked out that he was there about the sack and not about the ninth line on the wall nine feet above their heads.

"Give me that."

"**You may take it up if you want it. I am not going to give it to you and I am not going to stop you, and I would like the two of those to be written down separately, because one of them is a thing about me and one of them is a thing about a bar.**"

He picked the lump up off the pan with both arms under it and it went about four inches off the boards. He took it out about nine feet, turned it over, brought it back and put it down on the pan, and the pan took it. His hands stayed on the outside of it afterwards with the prints going white across the palms and about four people in that yard saw the prints and about nine of them saw the sack.

"**You have carried that about four hundred yards in your arms in front of about nine people, and you did not ask anybody for it, and you could not have got it on a rota, and I am the only man in this yard who can be handed a thing and cannot be put on anything, and I would like somebody to write that down as the shape of a thing and not as a favour.**"

"It is not a favour."

"**Then it is the shape of a thing and I have been in this city for two hundred and twenty-two days and it is the first time I have been able to put my hands on it, and I am not going to say anything else about it in this yard.**"

---

And a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road came up that road at about the ninth hour with nothing in her hands and stood at the edge of that yard, and she has come up that road with him twice and has not been in that yard at all before the fifty-eighth day of that month, and about four people in that yard had worked out why she was there and about nine of them had not.

"You have weighed a lump."

"**I have weighed a lump. I have also not broken it and I am not going to, and about four people in this yard are waiting for me to say what a quarter of a sack of salt is worth and I have not got that and I did not have it on the fifty-eighth day of that month either when I said a sack and a ninth and made everybody put a thing in their own hand.**"

"You told us to break it."

"**I told a yard on the sixty-sixth day of the Long Month that I was not going to. A man told me the same morning that break is not open, and I have carried that sentence about for two days and I am putting it down here in front of about nine people and I would like it written down that I said it in that order and not in this one.**"

---

And the woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything was in that yard with a slate under her arm and she wrote the day at the top of it before she wrote anything else, and she wrote the two figures on it as two figures with a finger's width of slate between them and did not join them up, and about four people in that yard watched her do it and nobody asked her to read either of them back.

"**A quarter of a sack, and a beam that can tell you what a thing is. That is all that is on this page and I am not putting the two of them together, and the space between them is the width of a finger because that is how wide my finger is and not because it means anything.**"

"It means something."

"**It means that I have not worked out what it means yet, and a space that has not been worked out is the most honest instrument in this city and about four people in this yard have seen me leave one and about nine of them did not look.**"

---

And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall was in that yard at about the tenth hour and had come up the road with nothing, and he is the man who copied the front of a page onto the back of a slate on a table in a market on the forty-fifth day of that month and put it down on the boards of a stall by a wharf on the fifty-third, and he said one thing about a crack and about four people in that yard heard all of it.

"Whose wood is that."

"**I do not know and I have not known since the second month of this flood, and I have said that out loud in about nine rooms, and this morning I have said it in a yard and about nine people have heard it and about four of them have heard the rest of it, which is that a length of wood like that comes off a rack, and about nine people in this city know what a rack is made of and I am not going to say it for them in front of about four people who have not asked me.**"

"You have said it is wood off a rack."

"**I have said a length of wood like that comes off a rack. That is a fact about wood. Whose rack it came off is a fact about a person and I have never been able to hold the two of them in my mouth at the same time, and I have been carrying that beam about nine miles since the fifty-eighth day of the Long Month and I have not been asked once and I am not going to start asking.**"

The woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road was still at the edge of that yard when he said it and she did not say anything, and about four people in that yard had been waiting for her to, and about nine of them had not.

---

And the beam stayed in the yard of the store on the upper road, and there is a crack in the far pan of it that nobody has looked at since the eighth hour of the morning.
