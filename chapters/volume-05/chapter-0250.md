# Chapter 0250 — An Island Of Boards

The line that had been going round that sack in the yard of the store on the upper road since the eighty-sixth day of the Long Month and the line that a queue had bent round it on the ninety-first day of that month came together at the south end of it at about the ninth hour of the morning on the ninety-fifth day of that month, and after that the sack was standing on ground nobody had walked on. The sack has not moved since the eighty-fifth day of that month and it is not going to be moved by anybody in that yard. The wind has been at that wall since the seventy-fourth day of that month. The board above the door has not been written on since the seventieth day of that month and the figures on it are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month, which is twenty-six days ago, and there is one line left on the wall above that board and it is the ninth.

---

And the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board was at the gate of that yard by the ninth hour, the third time he has come up that road since the eighty-sixth day of that month, and he did not come in past the boards, and nobody has worked out that a man who comes nine miles three times in ten days is a man who cannot stop coming.

"You have come to look at the sack."

"**I have come to look at the ground, and it is the first time since the eighty-sixth day of that month that I have come to look at anything except that sack, and I have got a reason, and the reason is that about nine feet of that yard has not been walked on since that day and everything round it has been walked on four times, and I have been looking at a bag for ten days and I did not know that the bag had an edge to it until this morning.**"

"That is a mark."

"**That is a mark, and it is the only one in that yard that nobody put there, and the mark in the boards under that sack has been in them since the eighty-ninth day of that month, and nobody has worked out that the day it is dated from is the day somebody decided to leave it, and I did not decide to leave it and I have not moved it and I cannot make anybody in that yard agree that it is mine.**"

"It is yours."

"**It is mine the way a coat is a man's when he has not taken it off, and about nine people in this city have worked out that the person who put a thing down is the person who owns it, and a few of them have worked out that the person who put a thing down is the person who gave up the reason for it, and I have not got a third thing to say and I have been saying one of those two since the eighty-fifth day of that month.**"

---

And the store was open on that morning in the ordinary course and there was no queue in that yard at all, and about four people came up that road before the ninth hour and did business and went away again, and the rest came up that road and went round a sack without knowing why they were going round it.

"You have not sold anything today."

"**I have sold a hand's worth of two things to a woman who came up at the seventh hour and did not want to be here, and nobody has worked out that a queue of forty people in a yard on a Tuesday is a store that is not selling, and both of those are the same yard and the same board on the wall.**"

"You did not open the chest."

"**I have not opened the chest since the fifty-sixth day of that month and I am not going to open it for a Saturday, and about nine people in this city have worked out that a chest that is not opened is a chest that is not a wage, and a few of them have worked out that a chest that is not opened is the only thing in that building that is still in the same condition it was in on the fifty-sixth day of that month, and the second of those is the reason and the first of those is the excuse.**"

---

And the man of about twenty-seven washed that board at about the eighth hour and left the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month standing on it, and above that board there was one line on the wall where there had been nine, and he hung the cloth on the rim of the trough and stood about nine feet back and looked at the two of them together for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

"Two pages."

"**Two pages about nine inches apart, and one of them has a day on it that is twenty-six days old and the other one has one line on it and no day at all, and nobody has worked out that a page with one line on it and no day on it is a page that is going to be about one thing, and I have washed that board every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I have not been able to work out which of those two I would rather be looking at.**"

"Which would you."

"**I would rather be looking at the board. That is not a thing I am proud of this morning, and I have said it out loud in a yard in daylight with a wall behind me, and the reason I would rather be looking at the board is that a board with figures on it from the sixty-ninth day of that month is still telling the truth about the sixty-ninth day of that month, and a wall with one line on it is not telling the truth about anything. It is only still there.**"

---

And the woman of about thirty-four in that store was at the back of that yard at about the eighth hour with a pail in each hand and looked at nine feet of ground with a sack standing in it, and she did not go out onto it, and a woman will walk round a sack, and she will walk round nine feet of ground as well.

"**And I have been asked three times in the ten days that ground has been standing there why I will not go out onto it and I have answered none of the three, and a woman who answers none of three is a woman who is building something, and she is not building anything. She is waiting for the man who put that sack down to say one word about it.**"

She said that to the boards of that yard and then she went in, and about four people in that yard heard all of it and the rest of them were at the door.

"**And I have watched that sack since the eighty-fifth day of that month from a doorway, and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who watches a thing from a doorway is a woman who has not decided about it, and nobody has worked out that I have not been watching it, I have been keeping the record of it in my own head where there is no board.**"

"You have not been out there."

"**I have not been out there and I am not going to, because the ground that sack is standing on is the only ground in that yard that has not been used since the eighty-fifth day of that month, and the second I put a boot on it then it is not that any more, and nobody has worked out that a thing nobody has stepped on is a thing nobody has decided about, and I have decided that I am not the one who decides it.**"

"Who has decided it."

"**Nobody. The wind decided it on the eighty-fifth day of that month by leaving that sack where a man put it, and the boots decided it on the eighty-sixth, and the queue decided it on the ninety-first, and none of those three things knew it was deciding anything, and I have watched the whole of it from a doorway in ten days and I have not got a word for what a place is when the only thing standing in it is a thing nobody will move.**"

---

And a beam stood in the middle of that yard with a man's coat held over the pan on it at two corners, and that coat had been on that pan since the ninety-third day of that month, and the wind has been at that wall since the seventy-fourth day of that month and has got under the hem of it and lifted it and let it down, and the coat has not gone off the pan, and the sack was still on its island, and nobody in that yard counted what was under it.

She came back inside at about the tenth hour and put her hands on the edge of the trough and left them there, and the eleven sacks along the wall of that back room had not been counted then or on any day in that month, and the groove in that floor with the crust of salt in the bottom of it was still four inches deep at the end the sacks go down at, and about four people in this city have worked out that a groove in a floor is a record of a habit and nobody has worked out that nobody in that building knows which sack the record is of.

---

And about four people came up that road at about the tenth hour and stopped at the gate of that yard to look at nine feet of ground with a sack standing in the middle of it, and everybody else went on up to the door, and nobody went in past the boards, and the four at that gate had been waiting two days for the man of about thirty-nine to come up and put his arms round that sack and take it inside, and the others had stopped waiting on the ninety-first day of that month.

"They are looking at the ground."

"**They are, and about four people in this city have worked out that a thing nobody will move is a thing a yard gets looked at, and the rest of them have not, and I am not going to be the first one to say what that thing is, and I have not got a word for that sack and I have not looked for one, and the two of those are the whole of what I have done about it in ten days.**"

"You have got nothing to say about it."

"**I have got one thing to say about it and I am going to say it to the boards and not to you, because a man in a yard who says a thing about a sack is a man who has decided what the sack is, and about four people in this city have worked out that a decision about a sack is a decision about a person, and I have not got the standing for that decision and I have not got a page for it either.**"

He said the one thing to the boards of that yard and it was about four words long and it was not a word for a sack, and about four people at that gate heard all of it and the rest heard nothing at all, and the trough with the cloth on its rim was against the wall and the sack was about nine feet off it and neither of them was any nearer than it had been at the seventh hour.

---

And the man of about twenty-seven put the trough back under the wall at about the tenth hour and hung the cloth on it and stood with his back to the wall looking at the two things on that wall above him, and the store was open and there was one person in it and the person was not buying anything.

"You are looking at the wall again."

"**I am looking at the wall and I have decided that I am not going to wash it, and that is the first decision I have made about that wall since the seventy-sixth day of that month and I have made it on the ninety-fifth day of that month at about the tenth hour, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who decides not to do a thing nineteen mornings running is a man who has been deciding all nineteen.**"

"You could write on the board."

"**I could write on the board and I have not written on that board since the seventieth day of that month and I have been asked about it in four yards since the seventy-fifth day of that month, and nobody has worked out that the figures on it are for the sixty-ninth day of that month and they are still the true figures about that day and they will be the true figures about it in four months, and that is the only reason the board is worth more empty than written on.**"

"You will not say what is on the wall."

"**There is one line on that wall and I wash the board about nine inches below it, and nobody has worked out that I can read it and that I have read it every morning since the seventy-sixth day of that month and that I have not said one word about what it says to a person in a yard.**"

"Why not."

"**Because a man who washes a board says what is on a board, and if I say what is on that wall then the man who wrote it is a man whose line has been read by the man who washes the board, and nobody has worked out that I have not done it because I have not been asked, and being not asked is the only thing I have got.**"

---

And nine feet of ground in a yard that had not been walked on, with a sack standing in the middle of it.
