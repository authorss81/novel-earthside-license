# Chapter 0246 — Where The Line Went

A queue formed in the yard of the store on the upper road at about the seventh hour of the morning on the ninety-first day of the Long Month, which is the seventh day that queue has run on, and there was a mark in the boards of that yard that was not in them on the eighty-eighth day of that month. The sack of salt is still where a man put it on the eighty-fifth day of that month, and that mark in the boards is the length of it and the width of it, and the line the queue took went round the north side of it and did not stop. The cold came in off the flats at about the sixth hour and the boards of that yard had gone the colour of the boards under them, and two or three people who came up that road before the seventh hour had looked at the sack and gone on to the door.

---

**And the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came up that road at about the seventh hour and stood at the gate of that yard and looked at six days of wind in the boards under a sack he carried nine miles, and he did not go in and pick it up.**

"Six days."

"**Six days, and I have walked up nine miles twice since the eighty-sixth day of that month to look at it and I have not touched it on either morning, and nobody has worked out that a man who looks at a thing twice is a man who has not worked out what.**"

"You could put it inside."

"**I could put it inside and it would be inside in about nine minutes, and the woman of about thirty-four in that building would have to decide what to do about a bag that came in on its own, and I have worked out since the eighty-fifth day of that month that I would rather it stayed out where a person can see it.**"

"Then what is it for."

"**It is for nothing. I have carried it since the sixty-second day of that month and it has gone hard and a bar will not go into it, and I have said in a yard that the day I break it I have two figures and neither of them is the one I paid, and I have not broken it, and if you ask me what it is for I have to tell you that I do not know and that is the fourth time in ten days I have said that to somebody.**"

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven washed that board at about the seventh hour and left the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month standing on it, and the chest has not been opened since the fifty-sixth day of that month, and nobody wrote anything on anything in that yard between the seventh hour and the eighth hour of that morning.**

"Is there anything today."

"**There is nothing today and there has been nothing on four of the mornings this queue has stood in this yard, and I have not written on that board since the seventieth day of that month, and a board with figures on it from the sixty-ninth day of that month is the only page in that yard that anybody can check, and I have not touched it this morning because touching it is the same as writing on it.**"

"They are the same thing."

"**They are not the same thing, and the reason they are not is that a date on a board is a day and a figure on a board is what a day came out as. I can put a figure back without inventing anything. If I put a date back I am inventing a day, and I have not been able to tell the difference between those two from the inside since the seventy-sixth day of that month, and the man who washes that board is the man everybody in that building tells things to by mistake.**"

---

**And the queue went round the north side of that sack on its way to the back of it, and the south side of it was used by two or three of the people in it, and by the eighth hour there were two lines in the dust of that yard and neither of them was the one anybody had walked before the eighty-fifth day of that month.**

"Do we go round it."

"**That queue is not my queue and the line of it is not mine, and nobody has worked out that a person who keeps a stall said nothing at all this morning and the line went round the sack anyway, and the saying nothing is the reason and the going round is what happened.**"

"You did not say anything."

"**I did not say anything. A sack in a walking line is a thing that bends a line, and everybody who has ever walked anywhere knows that, and a line that has been bent once is a line, and I could have stood in that yard and said four words and made it a decision of a person, and I did not, and that is the reason there are two lines in that dirt and not one.**"

---

**And a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom stood in that queue four feet behind a man who is not on any rota in this city, with a slate under his arm and the wrong side up, and one of those two men knew the other and the other one did not know him, and nobody said a name.**

"I will tell you the truth about the ninety-eighth day of that month, which is that I do not know what day a queue comes, and a man who does not know is not a man anybody can plan a yard around."

He said it to the boards and not to the man, and about four people in that yard heard it, and the man of about forty-two at the back of that line put his hands deeper into his pockets and did not turn round.

"**And he is four feet behind me and he has a slate under his arm and the wrong side up, and about four people in this city have worked out that a decision written down and carried face down is a decision a person is keeping, and nobody has worked out that I would take it off him this morning if he held it out, and I would not say one word about it.**"

"That is the man."

"**That is the man, and he is four feet behind me, and about four people in this city wrote a thing down on a slate on the seventieth day of that month about a name they were not going to say, and that slate has not been in my hand and I am not going to ask him for it, and it is the twenty-first day of that thing, and he has not said it, and he wrote that down for his own.**"

"Does he want you to hear it."

"**He does not want me to hear it and I have not asked him a thing, and I have stood in this yard three times since the eighty-fifth day of that month and there are people in this city who can fetch me by a name I did not choose and not one of them has done it, and there is a man four feet behind me who could have done it this morning at about the seventh hour and did not, and I am not going to say one word about that either, because a man who says a word about a man not fetching him has asked to be fetched.**"

---

**And Adrian Vale stood in the pocket between the bent line and the wall with his hands at his sides, which is the only place in that yard where a person can stand and not be in anything, and nine feet of ground there had not been walked on since the sack came down on the eighty-fifth day of that month.**

"What is that gap for."

"**That gap is not for anything. That gap is about nine feet of a yard that a queue has not got round to, and I am standing in it because I came in at the seventh hour and there was nowhere else to put myself, and nobody has worked out that he is a man who has been in a city for two hundred and forty-six days and has not found one.**"

"You could say that to a person in a room."

"**There is no room in this city that I have been asked into, and I have been in four of them, and in every one of the four there was a date on a door and people in the room and a person at the front of it, and the difference between this yard and those four rooms is that in those four rooms somebody had decided the day.**"

---

**And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and who wrote the ninth line came up that road at about the eighth hour and went to the back of that line and stood behind four people, and there is one line left on the wall of that yard that anybody can read from the ground, and he did not look up at it once in the hour he stood there.**

"You came up for this."

"**I came up for this and I came up for the wall, and nobody has worked out that I would rather stand at the back of it than in the store.**"

"Would you say whether it works."

"**No. That is the fifth time, and I gave the reason in that yard on the eighty-ninth day of that month in daylight, and it has not changed since, and it is not going to change because there is a line in the dirt at my feet.**"

---

**And the man of about forty-two stood at the back of that line with his hands in his pockets, and the line was not the line he had made, and nobody else in that yard knew that.**

"Two lines."

"**Two lines and one sack and neither of the two lines is the one I built, and I have been at the back of a line since the forty-fifth day of that month and I have never been at the back of a line that went round a thing, and the man at the back of a line is a man with nothing to decide, and this morning I am a man watching two lines that are not his go round a bag he did not put there.**"

"Is the queue going to be here on the ninety-eighth day of that month."

"**I do not know and I am not going to guess at it, because the next time that queue stands in this yard it will stand where two lines in the dirt are, and that is a day that nobody chose, and the last four of those mornings nobody chose one either, and the dirt has still got all four of them in it.**"

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out through to the yard at about the eighth hour with the sleeves of a coat wet to the elbow, and she stood at the edge of the boards and looked at the two lines in the dirt and did not go out onto either of them, and she looked at both of those lines and at neither of them, and nobody in that yard had looked at the ground at all.**

"Two lines is worse than one."

"**Two lines is worse than one and one line would have been gone by the second Tuesday, and nobody has worked out that a path is a thing a person can be asked about, because a path does not have a day on it and a queue has one.**"

"You could take the sack inside and have one line."

"**I could take the sack inside and have one line, and a yard that works is a yard that has been fixed, and nobody would work out that a person carried that sack nine miles on the sixty-second day of that month and put it down in the open on the eighty-fifth day of that month on purpose, and I have not been asked whether the open was on purpose and I am not going to be.**"

---

**And the girl of seventeen came through that yard at about the ninth hour of the ninety-first day of that month with a basket on her arm and no slate, and she went round the north side of that sack the way everybody went round the north side of it, and the queue's line had become the ordinary way to cross that yard, and a queue is not a way to cross anything at all, it is a way of standing in one place.**

"You are not in the queue."

"**I am not in the queue and I am going through that yard because the room over that market is four streets from that gate and I have not got a reason that would survive being said out loud, and nobody has worked out that a girl nobody can ask is a girl nobody can be angry at, and I have been a girl nobody can ask since the forty-fifth day of that month and it is the best thing I have ever had.**"

"You could take a share."

"**A woman offered me a share out of her own stall on the sixty-eighth day of that month and I said no, and about nine people in this city have worked out that a girl of seventeen who says no to a share is a girl who has a plan, and I have not got a plan, and I said no because the day I take a share is the day there is a person in four hundred miles who can find out whether I am all right, and there are people in this city who can fetch a man by a name he did not choose, and I am not going to be a second thing somebody can go and look for.**"

She went out through the gate and the two lines in the dirt were four feet apart where she crossed them and about nine feet apart where the sack was, and nobody in that yard marked it.

---

**And two lines in the dust of that yard going round a sack, and the sack standing where it is on ground nobody has walked on.**
