# Chapter 0229 — The Sack That Did Not Come

Nobody came up the bank at the bottom of the Redroot cut on the afternoon of the seventy-fourth day of the Long Month with a sack on a strap, and about four people in the yard of the house at the bottom of that cut had worked out by the fourth hour of the afternoon that he was not coming and about nine of them had worked it out by the second. The strap has worn a groove in the coat of the man who carries sacks. He has carried four of these up that bank and has counted them because that is his trade, and on the sixty-first day of that month he put the fourth one down in that yard and did not go up to the door. He said in that yard that he would not be there for the next one.

---

**And a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps that house came out onto the step at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and stood on it and looked up the bank, and a boy of eight came out behind her and stood on the step and looked up the bank too, and about four people in that yard had been waiting since the second hour of the afternoon for one of the two of them to say something and about nine of them had been waiting since the fourth.**

"He is not coming."

"**He is not coming. He said on the sixty-first day of the Long Month, in this yard, with a sack on his shoulder, that he would not be there for the next one, and about four people in this yard heard him say it and about nine of them did not, and I have not asked him anything since the forty-eighth day of the Long Month and I am not going to ask him anything now.**"

"Somebody will have to ask."

"**No. And I have worked out that nobody is going to have to ask, because about nine people in this yard have been saying since the second hour of this afternoon that they are not going to be the one, and I said it first and I am not going to take it back now that it is nearly dark. I am also not going to be a woman who stands on a step in the evening and asks a man who is not there a question. A question with nobody in it is not a question. It is a hole with a voice coming out of it, and I have been in about nine of those since the seventh week of this flood and I am tired of them.**"

She sat down on the step after that and about four people in that yard saw her do it and about nine of them had never seen her sit on that step, and the boy of eight sat down on the boards at the bottom of it about four minutes later and did not say anything about it.

The step is the only place in that yard with any sun on it and the sun had gone off it about an hour before, and the boards of the yard had a stripe of wet on them where a sack had been standing on the sixty-first day of that month and nothing had been standing on them since. There is a stripe of that wet about the length of a sack and about the width of a sack and it goes four inches darker at one end where a corner of it sat, and about four people in that yard have looked at it and about nine of them have not, and nobody in that yard has said what a mark like that is for.

---

**And a boy of eight went out into that yard at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and untied a length of rope off the wall and tied it again and came back in, and he did it with his teeth in the end of it, and nobody in that yard said one word about it, and about four people in that yard have said since that they have been waiting since the seventh week of this flood for somebody to say something about that rope and that nobody ever has.**

"That rope."

"That rope is about nine years old and it is not tied to anything and about nine people in this city know that and about four of them have said it out loud and nobody has said it in this yard."

"**I am not going to say it in this yard. I have said a great deal in this yard inside nine days and about nine people have carried every word of it eleven miles, and I have decided that this yard is the last place I say anything, and my step is nine feet from that rope and that is the right distance.**"

The woman of about fifty-nine who keeps that house said one sentence to the boy of eight and it was about his hands and not about the sack, and it was the same sentence she said on the sixty-first day of that month, and nobody in that yard improved on it.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four in that house came out onto the step at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, before the light had gone off it, and she is the youngest person in that house and she has been on no rota since the seventh week of this flood, and she said one sentence and it was about the wet stripe on the boards and about nine people at the bottom of that bank heard it.**

"**That stripe is going to be there on Monday. Nobody in this yard is going to scrub it out and I am not going to scrub it out, and if a sack comes up this bank on Monday then there will be two stripes and if it does not then there will be one, and I am not going to be in a yard counting stripes, and about four people in this city would call that a column and about nine of them would call it a Tuesday.**"

"That is a column."

"**It is a stripe of wet on a board and a Tuesday is a thing a man stands in a line for, and you have put the two of them next to each other to see if I will put them next to each other, and I will not, and I have been not putting things next to each other since the second day of this flood and I have got a whole book of it.**"

---

**And about the seventh hour of the evening the woman of about fifty-nine who keeps that house came out onto the step again and said four sentences and about nine people at the bottom of that bank heard all four of them, and the fourth one was about a sack and the first three were about a bank, and nobody in that yard asked her a question about any of it.**

"**A man carried four of these up this bank and he told nobody where they came from, and I have not asked him where they came from once in thirteen days. About nine people in four hundred miles would say that is a strange thing not to ask, and it is not strange. It is a man who is not going to be a second mouth for a thing that comes up a bank. The days went back on the forty-first day of the Long Month, unasked, on a flat, in daylight, and the sack did not stop. I have had four sacks come up that bank inside thirteen days and I have had one day of a sack not coming up that bank, and the difference between those two days is a man with a strap and a groove in a coat.**"

"What are you going to do."

"**Nothing. And I want it entered that I said nothing and I am going to do nothing and I am not going to be the one who asks, and about four people in this yard have said since the second hour of this afternoon that they are not going to be the one either, and about nine of them said it before I did, and that is the first time since the sixty-first day of the Long Month that four people in this yard have agreed on anything and nobody has written it down.**"

Somebody at the bottom of that bank said the obvious thing.

"**Do not. If somebody writes that down then a day when a sack does not come up a bank is a day with a mark against it, and the day before that mark is a day when four men carried four sacks up a bank in the ordinary course, and then somebody in about nine years is going to read a book about how often sacks come up a bank, and I am not going to be in a book about that.**"

"And if it comes tomorrow."

"**Then it comes tomorrow and nobody writes it down, and if it does not come tomorrow then it did not come tomorrow and nobody writes that down either, and about four people in this yard have been working that out since the second hour of this afternoon and about nine of them did not need it worked out. I am not going to be a woman whose step has a column on it. I wrote a slate myself on the forty-eighth day of the Long Month and gave it back the same morning and I have not written one since, and I am not going to start over a sack.**"

A man of about thirty-two was at the bottom of that bank at about the eighth hour and had come eleven miles and did not go up either, and he said nothing at all in that yard, and about four people in it had been waiting for him to say something about the days that went back on the forty-first day of that month and about nine of them had not.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road came up that bank at about the eighth hour of the evening with nothing in her hands and stood at the bottom of it and did not go up, and about four people in that yard knew why she was there and about nine of them had worked out that she had walked eleven miles to stand at the bottom of a bank, and she has not chosen a day to ask in a room and this was not a day she had chosen.**

"They will not tell you where it comes from."

"**I know they will not and I did not come up that bank to be told and I am not going to stand in that yard and be the one who asks, because the woman of that house has decided as far back as the forty-eighth day of the Long Month that nobody in this city is going to count the sacks on that step and I have not got anything that is worth more to her than a stranger with a figure of her own in her mouth.**"

"You could have one."

"**I have had a number in my mouth since the forty-seventh day of the Long Month and I have not spent it in a yard and I am not going to spend it tonight, and about four people in this city have worked out what it is that I am holding and about nine of them have not, and I would like somebody in this yard to notice that I have come eleven miles and asked for nothing and that is not a thing anybody is going to thank me for because I have not done it for them.**"

She went back down the bank in the dark at about the ninth hour and about four people in that yard watched her go and about nine of them had gone inside before she did.

---

**And the keeper of the Redroot gate had been at the top of that cut from first light, and she was still there at about the seventh hour of the morning, and she worked the channel with the board in her left hand and did not put it down once, and she is fifty days into a year of being angry that has not run out, and she said nothing to anybody about it and nobody in that yard has said her name and nobody went up that bank with a message.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening the boards of that yard had gone the colour of the boards under them, and about four people were still standing in it and about nine had gone, and the step was empty.**
