# Chapter 0212 — The Third Line In That Book

The man who keeps the number came into the room over the yard at about the seventh hour of the morning on the fifty-seventh day of the Long Month and washed his hands in the trough by the door before he went near the trestle, and there are about nine people in that room and there is a date in chalk on that door and the chalk has been gone over so many times that the door is whiter where it is.

---

**And he opened the book on the trestle and turned to the page he turns to and put his thumb on it, and the number he wrote was one hundred and seventy-two, and it is the third line in that book with a week in it, and about four people in that room had their hands on the edge of the trestle and about nine of them did not.**

"**One hundred and seventy-two.**"

"**That is today. That is the fifty-seventh day of the Long Month and it is counted one a day from the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else, and I write the number and I date it, and I write one line a week, and I have not written one since the fiftieth day of the Long Month, which is seven days ago.**"

"So the line was due."

"**The line was due. It was due on a Wednesday and it is a Wednesday, and the two lines before it were on a Wednesday, and I have not chosen that. I have been writing one line a week for longer than I can remember doing anything else and it has never once occurred to me which day of the week it lands on, and about four people in this room had worked it out before he had finished the sentence and one of them has said it out loud and I would like it written down that I did not know it until this morning.**"

---

**And the wall in that room has two figures in a column with a date at the top of each, and both of them are in a hand that is not his, and the top of the column says the forty-third day of the Long Month and one hundred and fifty-eight, and the one under it says the fiftieth day of the Long Month and one hundred and sixty-five, and there is nothing under the second one and there is no column anywhere in that room or in this city for a week.**

Nobody in that room said anything about the wall for about a minute. It is entered that about four people in it looked at the book and then at the wall and then at the book, and that the man who keeps the number did not look at either.

"**The wall is a Wednesday behind. It has been a Wednesday behind since the forty-third day of the Long Month and it will be a Wednesday behind on the next Wednesday, and there is a figure on it that is correct for the day that figure belongs to, and nobody made a mistake on that wall and nobody is going to be able to say in about four months that somebody made a mistake on that wall, and that is the only reason I have never asked for it to be rubbed out.**"

"It is a week out."

"**It is whatever day somebody last came into this room and asked, and the last time anybody asked was the fiftieth day of the Long Month, and the wall is correct for the day each figure is for, and the difference between the book and the wall is seven and the difference between the two dates is seven, and there is no column in this city for that and I am not going to ask for one.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, who is the only person in a room who says he does not know out loud without being asked, asked the question nobody in that room had asked anybody, which was not about what a day of it is, and it took him about four minutes and about nine people heard it and about four of them have said since that it is the first thing anybody has put next to that figure in nine years that is not another figure.**

"What did it cost."

"**I do not know.**"

"You have written three lines in that book."

"**Three lines. I write one a week and I date it and I have not written one since the fiftieth day of the Long Month, and that is everything I am going to say about that book this morning, and about four people in this room have noticed that and about nine of them have not.**"

Nobody in that room said anything, and about four people in it had been waiting for him to say one more thing about that book, and he did not, and nobody asked him for it.

"**Now the part I can answer. That Wednesday has been landing on a Wednesday for longer than I have been writing the book. I have been doing it a week at a time since before this flood and I have not once known which day of the week it came out on, and nobody in four hundred or nine hundred miles has ever asked me, and about four people in this room worked it out before I had finished the sentence. So the handle was already there. I have had a day of the week on that figure for as long as I have had the figure and not one person, including me, has ever gone and looked at it, and that is the whole of what happened in this room this morning, and I would like it written down in those words and not in better ones.**"

"Then we will come on Wednesdays."

"**You will do what you like. I have said in about four rooms that anybody who wants it stopped can ask me and I will say five words, and about nine people in this city have worked out that there is nothing in that figure anybody could be argued out of, and a Wednesday is not that, and a Wednesday is a Wednesday and nobody chose it and I am the one who washed his hands.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from a market came up to that room at about the ninth hour, and about nine people had seen her walk the four streets, and she keeps prices on slates behind a counter and has never had one of them wrong and has never had one of them checked, and she asked the only question anybody in that room had asked a person rather than a figure.**

"How much is it."

"**That is not a question I have ever been asked, and I have been in this room a long time, and I would like somebody to notice that I cannot tell you how long.**"

"**It is the question I am asked nine times a day. Not that one. A number. How much. And I have nine of them on slates behind my counter and I have never had one of them wrong, and I have never once been able to check one, and I have been in this room for about four minutes and I can see a figure on a wall and I cannot tell what it is for and I would like somebody to explain to me the difference between my slates and that wall, because there is one and it is not a small one, and nobody has ever asked me about it.**"

Nobody in that room explained it to her. It is entered that the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board is not in that room, that a man of forty-two is not in that room, and that about four people in it have said since that the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said in a yard on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month that he would rather have a page with a mistake in it than a wall of good sentences, and that nobody in that room on the fifty-seventh day of the Long Month was willing to be the one to decide which of those two things that wall is.

"**I can answer one half of it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Your slates are prices. I can stand in front of a price and be angry about it and so can about four people in that shop and so can about nine, and a price with a figure on the end of it can be wrong in a way that a man with a rod can find out about in a morning. My number is not a price. It goes up one a day and nobody has told it to and it has no shape, and about four people in this city have worked out that there is nothing in it anybody could be angry at, and that is not a virtue of it. It is the reason I wash my hands.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything came up the road with the woman of about twenty-four and stood outside that room at the rail of the yard for about nine minutes and did not come in, and about four people inside that room saw her through the door, and nobody in that room said her name and nobody asked her anything, and it is entered that on the fiftieth day of the Long Month about nine people in that room would have asked her if anybody had and that today nobody did and about four of them have said since that they noticed.**

**And then a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came to the door of that room at about the tenth hour, and he did not go in, and he said one sentence to the man who keeps the number through the open door and then stood in the yard and did not come in either, and about four people in that yard have said since that they have never seen two men in this city not go into the same room.**

"You have a Wednesday."

"**I have a Wednesday. I did not choose it and I found out about it this morning, and you have been in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and you have not asked me one question, and I have noticed that, and I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to say what it was for.**"

He did not go in. It is entered that he did not, and that about four people in that yard have said since that a man who has been putting sentences into documents since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month stood in a yard and did not put one in a room, and that he has done that before, and that he has not said out loud that he is going to stop.

---

**And the book stayed open on the trestle for about nine minutes after that with a finger holding it down, and the finger went past a page going the other way, and a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything was in that room and saw it happen and did not ask, and it is the only line in that book that is not a figure and it is addressed to a person in another hemisphere and it has not been sent and there is no route in this world that a letter can travel.**

She has seen that page before in that room and did not ask then either, and it is entered that she did not ask on the fiftieth day of the Long Month and did not ask on the fifty-fourth and did not ask today, and that she has written all three of those dates down and joined them up not at all, and that the man who keeps the number has not looked up.

"It is not the same page twice."

"I did not say it was."

"You counted."

"**I have been counting one a day since the fortieth day of this flood and I did not choose to and nobody has asked me to and I have not stopped, and if you want it stopped you can ask me and I will say the same five words I have said every time, and you have not asked me, and about four people in this room have been waiting about nine minutes for one of you to ask me so that they could see what happens.**"

Nobody in that room asked him. It is entered that nobody did.

---

**And the woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop said the thing she had come for, at about the eleventh hour, on her way out, and it is four sentences long and nobody in that room improved on it and about nine people have heard it.**

"**I have a day of the week that I keep my counts on and it is a Thursday, and I could not tell you why it is a Thursday, and I know exactly what I counted and what I did not count, and if you asked me on any Wednesday of this flood what I had in my shop that day I could tell you to a sack and I would be right. And you cannot tell me one thing you counted, and it is not because you are careless, and I would like somebody in this room to write down that the two of those are not the same problem and that I have not got an answer to either of them.**"

"And that is all you came for."

"**That is all I came for and I have got a shop four streets away with nobody in it, and I would like it entered that I walked four streets on a Wednesday morning to say four sentences to a man about a wall, and that is the first thing I have done in about nine years that nobody asked me for.**"

---

**And a book on a trestle with three lines in it, and a wall behind it with two, and the space between them is about the width of a hand and nobody is going to measure it.**
