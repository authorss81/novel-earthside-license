# Chapter 0215 — What The Ninth Line Is Worth

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put a slate down face down on the boards of his own stall in a market in this city at about the ninth hour of the morning on the sixtieth day of the Long Month, and then turned it over about four minutes later and wrote nothing on it. The boards of that stall had been washed that morning and there was still water standing in the joints of them. About four people in that market asked him why he had done it twice and he told them both times and neither answer was the same.

---

**And the reason he gave the first time was that a slate is a page and a page is a place a person can be found in, and the reason he gave the second time was that a slate face down on the boards of a stall in a market is the ninth line of a document. He said that out loud and then stood there and let about nine people in that market work out what he had just said about himself.**

"I did it twice because I did not know which one it was."

"You have written on that slate before."

"**I have written on that slate about four times since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and every single time somebody told me not to, and that is not a principle, it is a habit, and I have just found out in about four minutes in front of about nine people that a habit and a principle look exactly the same from about four feet away.**"

"Which one is this one."

"**I do not know and I have been trying since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and it is the sixtieth day of that month and the posting has been up on a wall nine miles up the road for fourteen days, and I am going to say it in this market, and I have had it in my head since the fifty-first day of that month and I have not slept on it.**"

Nobody in that market stopped working. Somebody four stalls down began planing a length and the noise of it went on through the whole of what he said, and about nine people were at their boards and about four of them had come and stood. About nine people heard the whole of it, and about four of them have said since that a man who has spent fourteen days being unable to say a thing is a man who has been building a room to say it in and not a man who was stuck.

---

**And what he said was four sentences long and there was no figure in any of them. About nine people in that market heard it, and about four of them have said since that it is the only thing anybody has said in a market in this city in fourteen days that they could not argue with. He has not improved on it.**

"**The ninth line is worth exactly what the queue was worth before I wrote it down. The queue was worth a sack and a place at a fire and a Tuesday, and it is worth a sack and a place at a fire and a Tuesday, and the page has not changed what it is worth by one grain of salt.**"

"So nothing happened."

"**Something happened and it is not about the worth. For fourteen days the only thing anybody in four hundred miles has done about the fact that this queue is the only functioning way out of the world is to write it ninth on a page and read it. It used to be a thing you walked to on a Tuesday. It is now the ninth line of a document, and the document is bound to a compact that two bodies can end and neither of them is me, and about four people in this city have worked out that if the ninth line came off the page then the thing it is worth might come off with it.**"

"Would it."

"**No. And that is the whole of what I have and I have said it in a market and not in a yard because there are no people in this market who cannot leave. And I have put the only thing in this city that works at the bottom of a page on purpose, in a room, in daylight, with a date on the door, and about four people in this market have been waiting fourteen days for me to say that I would take it off the ninth line, and I would, and I am not going to, and I have thought about that since the fifty-first day of the Long Month.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said the one question he had come for, and he asked it in a market in daylight with his own board shut in front of him and about four people heard it. About nine of them had asked it themselves since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month. It is the question that has not been answered and this chapter did not answer it either.**

"Does it work."

"**That is not what I am answering. Whether that line works is a question about a Tuesday and I have been in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and about four of them asked me that and I have not answered it and I am not going to start on a Saturday with about nine people standing in a market. I am telling you what it is worth. Whether it works is a different question and it is not mine and it is not yours and it is not a thing that gets answered in a market by a man who wrote it.**"

"Then whose is it."

"**That is a better question than the first one and I am not answering that either, and about four people in this market will go home and be annoyed with me and about nine of them will be glad and I have stopped trying to work out which is which and that is the other thing I have learned since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month.**"

"You refused a line of it on the fifty-first."

"**I refused a line of it on the fifty-first day of the Long Month and I have not been able to tell anybody since which line I refused, and about four people in that yard noticed at the time that I did not know whose line it was either, and I have not gone back and found out, and I am not going to today.**"

"Somebody in this market could tell you."

"**About four people in this market could tell me and about nine of them would tell me a thing they have worked out themselves and about four hundred people nine miles up that road have read a page with a line of mine on it and cannot tell what it is. I have been the man who says a cost out loud in four sentences since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and the only thing I have got to offer anybody today is that I do not know, and I have said in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month that I do not know, and everybody has stopped finding it interesting, and I would like it noticed that it has not stopped being true.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from that market came in at about the eleventh hour and said one thing, and there was dust on the outside of her sleeves from the four streets again. She has said one thing about a wall in a room over a yard on the fifty-seventh day of the Long Month and about four people in this market have heard both, and she did not improve on the earlier one and she did not repeat it.**

"**A man of about thirty-four has just said in this market that he would take the ninth line off the page and is not going to. I keep a shop four streets from here and I have had about four people in that shop ask me whether the queue is still on the page every day since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month, and every single one of them asked me because they were afraid, and not one of them asked because they wanted to know whether it worked.**"

"That is a fact about fear and not about a line."

"**It is a fact about a line. It is the ninth line's whole worth and he did not say it and I am not going to say it for him either, because he has stood in front of about nine people this morning and not put a page on a thing, and I am not going to be the one who puts a sentence in his mouth in a market. I have said my own thing in a room and I have four streets to walk home and I am going to go and do it.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, who is the only person in a room who says he does not know out loud without being asked, worked out a way to find out what the ninth line is worth in about four minutes in that market. He did it with the hand that closes, moving it over the boards of the stall a span at a time, and it is cheap, and he said it out loud, and about four people in that market said afterwards that they had already worked it out and were not going to do it.**

"**Go and stand in a market on a Tuesday and count how many people come to a stall to ask whether the queue is still on the page. That is the whole of the test. It costs nothing. It takes about four hours. About four people in this city have already told me they are going to do it and not one of them has told me they are not, and about nine of them are going to do it and tell me the number anyway, and I have been the man who says *I do not know* out loud in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and I have never once been able to stop anybody counting something.**"

"You are not going to do it."

"**I am not going to write the number down. That is the whole of my objection and it is not a good one and I have heard it out of my own mouth. If I write it down it is a column, and I have been on one for about two hundred days and I would like to be the first person in this city who counted a thing and handed it to somebody else in a yard and did not keep it. And if four of them do it and tell me, I will hear it, and I will not be able to do anything with it, and that is the correct outcome and I am not going to pretend it is a cost.**"

"Nobody has asked you to do anything."

"**Nobody has asked me to do anything since the sixth week of this flood and I have stopped mentioning it, and about four people in this market have noticed that I have stopped mentioning it and about nine of them have not, and one of those nine is going to be a man who counts on a Tuesday.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything was in that market and had a slate under her arm, and she wrote one sentence down and then stopped writing and did not start again, and she put the chalk down on the boards of the stall rather than back in her pocket. It is entered that about four people in that market saw her do it and about nine of them did not know there was anything to write.**

"What did you write."

"**I wrote *the ninth line is worth a sack, a place at a fire and a Tuesday, and that is the same as what it was worth, and that is not a finding, that is a man in a market agreeing with a page.* And then I put the slate down because about four people in this market have just spent nine minutes listening to a man say a thing, and a sentence that says a man said a thing is the beginning of a page about a man, and I have had enough of those since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month.**"

"And the finding."

"**There is no finding. There is a man who put a queue on a page and has spent fourteen days finding out that he cannot say what it is worth, and a woman who keeps a price on nine slates, and a man who keeps a Wednesday, and a beam with a ninth of a weight on a slate in a woman's hand, and about four people in this market have said since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month that all of those are the same thing, and they are not, and I am not going to join them up on a Saturday.**"

---

**And the slate was face up on the boards of that stall at the fourth hour of the afternoon with nothing on it, and the boards had dried and the water in the joints had gone and the slate had them under it. The man who writes on it when he is told not to did not pick it up again, and about nine people in that market walked past it.**

**And a queue in a yard nine miles up the road with a page with it on at the bottom, and a Saturday with no queue in it anywhere in this city.**
