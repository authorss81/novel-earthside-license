# Chapter 0242 — Over The Edge Of It

A coil of rope on the top length of a rack at the edge of the bottom of the Redroot cut went over the edge at about the seventh hour of the morning on the eighty-seventh day of the Long Month and unrolled itself down the bank in about the time it takes a man to walk out of a yard. The wind that took it has been coming up off eleven miles of flats since the seventy-fourth day of that month. The cut has had no water in it since the forty-first day of that month, and there is a thumb's depth of the dust off those flats in the bottom of it, and a rope unrolling into that dust makes a noise like a boot on boards, and the woman of about thirty-nine who cannot get a rack above her shoulder heard it and came down the bank with a hand out in front of her as though she were feeling for a door that was not there.

---

**And the rack is still at the edge of the bottom of that cut with the top length of it bare where the coil was, and the rack is not in the cut and has not been in the cut, and the man who carries racks was about nine feet up that bank with both hands inside his coat.**

"That rope is mine."

"**It is on your rack and it has been on your rack since before I came, and it went over the edge on its own, and nobody on this bank has lost anything this morning. About four people on that bank this morning have worked out that a thing which goes over an edge of itself is not a thing anybody has lost, and the rest of that bank has worked out that it is a thing somebody will have to go down for.**"

"I am not going down for a rope."

"**You are not going down for a rope and you have not been down that bank since the seventy-second day of that month, and I am not asking you to. There is a woman nine feet below the two of us who is already on her way down, and neither of you is going to help her, because the rope is not nine feet of rope, it is a coil, and a coil takes two hands and an edge to open, and there is no edge on that bank.**"

---

**And she got to the foot of that bank at about the seventh hour and stood in the dust at the bottom of it and looked at nine feet of rope lying along a channel that has not held water since the forty-first day of that month, and the rope was stiff along its whole length and grey with the same dust as the floor of the cut.**

"Do not touch it yet."

"**I have got my hand on it and I am not going to pick it up yet. It has been in that dust since the sixth hour and dust gets into a coil, and if I open it here about nine feet of it comes apart in my hands and goes down that bank in pieces, and I did not come down here to leave nine feet of somebody's rope in pieces.**"

"Will you manage it at all."

"**I will manage it in about four moves on flat ground, and this is not flat ground and I have one arm on the good side this morning, and I have not told anybody that and I am not going to, and nobody has asked me the difference and I would like it kept that way.**"

She crouched where the rope went under a root and got her fingers under it a hand's width at a time, the way you get a finger under a rope you have no reason to hurry. The dust came off the top of the coil onto the back of her hand and stayed there, and she did not wipe it, and the wind came down the cut and lifted the loose end of the rope about a foot and dropped it.

---

**And the man who carries racks came down four steps of that bank at about the eighth hour, which is as far as he has gone on it since the seventy-second day of that month, and he looked at the rope on the ground and then at the top length of his own rack.**

"A coil opens wrong."

"**A coil opens wrong, and it opens wrong in a way that is not in the rope, it is in the dust, and the dust has got between the turns of it and about nine feet of rope that has been in dust is a shorter thing than nine feet of rope when you come to lift it.**"

"You could wet it."

"**I could wet it and there is no water in that cut to wet it with, and that has been the case since the forty-first day of that month, and there is a channel about nine feet from my boot that has not held anything since then, and about four people in this city have worked out that a rack cannot be put in a dry cut and the rest of that city has worked out that nobody has tried, and both of those are the same fact and one of them is the reason and one of them is the excuse.**"

He went back up the four steps and stood where he had been standing and did not say one word about the four steps, and the woman of about thirty-nine at the foot of the bank did not look up at him while he said it or after.

---

**And Adrian Vale came down that bank at about the eighth hour because there was a rope on it and he is a person who goes to a thing, and the rope was not his and the bank was not his and the rack was not his, and he turned his hand over on the way down because the grit had got into the seam of it.**

> **A thing that has gone over an edge of its own accord has not been given up by anybody.**

He read it twice on that bank with the wind at his back, and it did not tell him whose rope it was and it did not tell him what a man is for who is standing on a bank that is not his, and he put his hand down and shut it and did not say one word about it to anybody on that bank for the rest of the morning. He stood about nine feet from the woman of about thirty-nine and looked at the rope and did not offer to take it, and about four people on that bank have noticed that he did not offer.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-nine rolled the coil on its side against the foot of the bank and pushed it four inches at a time until it lay along the channel the way rope lies when nobody has opened it, which is not the way it lay on the top length of that rack.**

"**Nine feet of rope, and it has come apart about a foot from the end, and the foot of it is lying along a channel that has not held water since the forty-first day of that month, and about four people on this bank have worked out that a rope in a dry channel is a rope in a dry channel, and nobody has worked out that it is going to be on a rack again by the tenth hour.**"

"That is the whole of it."

"**That is the whole of it, and it is nine feet of rope lying in a dry channel, and it will be in a dry channel on the eighty-eighth day of that month and the eighty-ninth and the ninetieth, and the rack will be at the top of that bank with a bare top length on it. Those are two objects in two places now, and about four people on that bank have worked out that they used to be one, and the rest of that bank has worked out that a rope is a rope.**"

"Will you carry it up for me."

"**I will not. You have not asked me to and I would have said no, and I have said no to about four things this morning before I got to the bottom of this bank. The reason I am not going to is that rope is the only thing on that top length anybody can use to get a rack into a cut, and if I carry it up there I am carrying a tool up to a bank, and the next person to come down here will be a person with a tool and a reason, and I am not putting those two things next to each other today.**"

---

**And the woman of about thirty-nine said four things at the foot of that bank to nobody in particular between the eighth hour and the ninth hour, and about four people on that bank heard three of them and the rest heard none, and none of the four was a question.**

"A rack is four lengths of wood and a rope and four people."

"**A rack is four lengths of wood and a rope and four people, and about nine of the racks on these flats can be worked, and every one of those nine is on somebody's rota, and this one is at the edge of this cut on nobody's rota, and the two things about it that are wrong are that the wood is off a frame and the rope went over the edge on the eighty-seventh day of that month.**"

"Who put the wood there."

"**A man who carries racks, and he is nine feet up that bank at this moment with his hands in his coat, and he has not been down this bank since the seventy-second day of that month, and I am not going to ask him about the wood, because the day I ask him about the wood is the day he has to answer for two frames and one rope, and he has not got a rope to answer with and that is not his fault.**"

"You could put the rope back on it."

"**I could put the rope back on it and I am not going to, and the reason is that a rack with a rope on it is a rack with a rope on it.**"

---

**And Adrian Vale was still on that bank at about the ninth hour and had not offered to lift the coil, and the woman of about thirty-nine looked up at him from the foot of it for about the length of a breath and then asked him why, in a yard, in daylight, on the eighty-seventh day of that month.**

"You did not offer."

"**I did not offer and I want the reason said out loud, because the reason is that I have been in this flood two hundred and forty-two days and nobody has ever handed me a rope and asked me to carry it up a bank, and the second I do that I am a man who carries things for people who have not asked him, and about nine people in this city would work out that a man who carries things unasked is a man who is available, and availability is the only thing I have got.**"

"You would have carried it."

"**I would have carried it. I am not going to pretend I would not have, and the not pretending is the whole of what I have learned in this flood and it took me two hundred and forty-two days and a rope in the dust at the bottom of a cut.**"

"Would you have said yes if I had asked."

"**If you had asked I would have said yes, and I have said yes four times in this flood to things I should have said no to, and nobody has worked out that a man who would have said yes is a man who has not been asked anything in two hundred and forty-two days and has started to think of a yes as a way of being spoken to.**"

He put his hand in his pocket and left it there for the rest of the hour, and about four people at the top of that cut have noticed that a man who put his hand in his pocket on a bank and left it there is a man who has decided something about his own hands.

---

**And the light came off eleven miles of flats at about the ninth hour and went up the far side of that cut, and the rope lay in the dust at the bottom of it, and nobody came down that bank again that day, and the dust began at about the south end of the coil and worked along it the way dust does on a rope that nobody has moved.**

The man of about thirty-nine went up the bank at about the ninth hour and went along the top of it and did not look down, and the man who carries racks went with her for about nine feet and then stopped and let her go on, and neither of them said a word about the rope on the ground or the bare length on the rack, and the two of them were the only people on that bank who had said nothing about it at all.

---

**And the man of about fifty-two who digs came to the top of that bank at about the ninth hour of that morning and looked at the two people on it and at the channel below and did not go down, and about four people at the top of that cut had been waiting since the seventy-eighth day of that month for him to say what the one thing was, and nobody asked him.**

"You came up."

"**I came up. There is dust in the bottom of that cut and there has been dust in the bottom of that cut since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and I have not been in it since the fifty-ninth day of that month, and nobody has worked out that I have never had a use for dust and I have looked at it every day of it.**"

"Is there a hole."

"**There is a hole nine feet from the one four people measure and it is not a hole, and a rod in it has come up about four inches since the seventy-eighth day of that month, and I put a rod in it on the fifty-ninth day of that month to find out one thing about a sack, and I have not told anybody what the one thing was, and I am not going to put a rod in a third one.**"

"You never have."

"**I said on the seventy-eighth day of that month that I am not going to start a third time for a man who wants a figure, and nobody has worked out that a man who stops measuring and says so out loud has found the end of the thing he was measuring, and that was said in this cut nine days ago and it was not said to me.**"

He looked down the bank at nine feet of rope in the dust and did not ask about the rope, and about four people at the top of that cut noticed that he did not ask.

---

**And a bare top length on a rack at the edge of the cut, with nothing coiled on it and nothing under it either.**
