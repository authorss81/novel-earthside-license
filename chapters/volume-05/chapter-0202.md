# Chapter 0202 — Four Hundred And Forty

About nine people were walking up the four miles of the causeway at about the eighth hour of the morning on the forty-seventh day of the Long Month, and not one of them had a load, and the first of them had been born on the flats and had never stood at the top of a road before Saturday.

It is a Sunday. The queue's day is a Tuesday. The causeway is four miles and four feet wide at the top and it took forty days to build and it has had nothing on it since the morning of the forty-second day of the Long Month, and the ground under it was taken in the seventh week of this flood without asking anybody, and the man who built it is a party to a compact that was made on Friday and put a share on the same posting as every other share.

---

**And about four hundred and forty people dry salt on those flats, and about nine of them are on the settlement's rota in four hands, and the rest have never been on it and are paid in salt and a place at a fire, and this morning about nine of them walked four miles up a road they have worked beside for nine weeks and did not know until Saturday that there was a second way to look at it.**

"**Say the number again.**"

"**Four hundred and forty. And I counted people and not racks, and I have stood at the end of this about nine times in nine weeks and asked, and nobody ever said no, and about four of the figures I have been given are wrong and about four of them are right and I have not found out which.**"

"**You are saying it as though it is a figure somebody can check.**"

"**I am saying it exactly the way I have said it every other time I have said it, which is as a number that about four of us got from four different people and nobody wrote down. That is not the same as a figure on a wall and I know the difference and so do you, and I have known the difference since about the twenty-first day of the Long Month, which is when I first said it in a room, and about nine people in this city have not been asking me a question about it since then.**"

---

**And the man of about fifty-two who digs was at the bottom of the cut at about the ninth hour of the morning, standing in six days of nothing, and he had a rod in his hand and he put the rod down when about nine people came over the bank, and he said the number of days out loud before anybody asked him for it, which is a thing he has done twice now.**

"**Six days.**"

"**Six days since the water stopped. And I am not going to tell you what that becomes, because I have told you the thing it becomes and the thing it becomes is that in about four weeks there is nothing at the bottom of this cut that anybody can put a rack into, and four weeks is not a figure, it is a direction, and I have said the direction twice now and about nine people in this city have written the number down instead.**"

"**Write the direction down.**"

"**I will write the direction down. Six days, and a smell, and a rod that goes in further every time and not much.**"

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road asked the question that the whole of the forty-seventh day of the Long Month turned on, and she asked it in the middle of four miles of causeway with about nine people on it and about four hundred and forty people four miles below them who cannot hear a word of it, and she asked it in the plainest words anybody has used since the seventh week of this flood.**

"**How do I get on it.**"

"**Which it.**"

"**The posting. It went up on a wall at the seventh hour yesterday morning with a price under every line and a date at the top of it and about nine people in front of it, and I read all of it, and there is a line for the water in the channel and there is a line for the store and there is a line for the road and there is a line for the queue, and there is not one line on it with a name of mine against it, and I have read every one of those lines twice.**"

"**Whose household are you.**"

And that stopped the four of them on that causeway for longer than the question had taken to ask, and about nine people behind them kept walking and one of them said he was sorry and was not, and nobody in four hundred miles has an instrument that would answer it either.

"**The labour is shared and posted. That is a sentence in a document that was read out on Friday in a room with a date on the door and about two hundred and forty people heard it. A shared thing gets shared between two bodies. The Redroot Commons and the Reed Commons. And about nine of the people who work these racks are on the settlement's rota because a man in a workroom up the road walked down here about nine weeks ago and asked who wanted a Tuesday.**"

"**And the rest of us.**"

"**And the rest of you have never answered to anything and there is no line on that page for a person who has not answered to anything, and I am not able to write one, and I am not able to put a name against a line without knowing whose household puts it there, and I have said since the twenty-first day of the Long Month that a day of a wage is not a wage, and about nine people in this city have heard me say it and about four of them wrote it down, and none of them has come back to me about the four hundred and thirty-one.**"

---

**And about halfway up, where the bank is high enough to look down into the channel, they stopped for a while without any of them deciding to, and there are four hundred and forty racks standing in that channel with reed mats and stones along the top of them, and a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and a rack that has been worked does not stop drying, and salt does not rot.**

"**Then that is the whole of the work.**"

"**That is the whole of the work and I have said the same sentence four times in four different rooms since the forty-second day of the Long Month and about nine people in this city have written the number down instead of the sentence.**"

"**How many can still be worked.**"

"**About nine. And I have not worked one since Monday morning and I am not going to work one this week, and I have not told anybody that I have not worked one, and I have told about nine people on this causeway the number nine and not the other half, and I would like it entered that I have entered it myself and that nobody made me.**"

He put the rod back in the ground where it had been before about nine people came over the bank, and he did not put the rod back in the same hole, and about four of them noticed that and none of them said anything about it.

---

**And a third of the way up the causeway a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall caught up with them, and he had walked up from the store, and he had been in this city since the second month of this flood, and he said the one thing about the seventh line that anybody said that day, and it is the only line in the eleven that says anything at all about a road, and nobody in the room on Friday knew which road it was, and about four people in this city have worked that out since and not said so out loud.**

"**The road is open to everybody on a posted day.**"

"**That is the seventh line.**"

"**That is the seventh line and it is the only one of the eleven that says a thing about a road and it has been on a wall in this city for a day, and I have read it about four times and I have worked out that it does not say which road, and about nine people in that room on Friday knew that and about nine of them did not, and I was in the room.**"

"**So which road.**"

"**I do not know and I am not going to guess, and here is what I know. There is one road in four hundred miles that anybody in this city has built. It is four miles and it took forty days and about forty people built it who were not from here, and the ground it stands on was taken in the seventh week of this flood without asking, and the man who built it is a party to the compact that the line is in, and he put a share on the same posting as every other share in it in that same room on the Thursday and it is not above any of them.**"

"**Ask him.**"

"**I am not going to ask him. Not today, and not this week, and I would like it entered in about four places that I am not going to ask him, and I am not going to ask him because about four hundred and forty people are standing at the bottom of a road that he took without asking and he has lost the thing that used to run along it, and I am not going to walk up nine miles and put a question about a line to a man on the morning after the week he has just had.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from a market said one thing to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall as about nine people came past them going down, and she has served him for six years and she does not know what the seventh line says, and she said it to about nine people and it went up the causeway and about four hundred and forty people heard it by the fourth hour of the afternoon.**

"**Then somebody is going to work it out and it is going to be worked out in a yard, and you know what a yard is.**"

"**I know what a yard is. My entire life in this flood is a yard.**"

"**And I am telling you that about four people in this city have already worked it out and about four of them have told each other, and I have not told anybody which of them I mean, and if a person wants to know whether a line about a road is about the four miles they have been walking up for nine weeks, they can stand at the end of it on a Friday morning in daylight and say so, and about four people in this city would have to answer, and that is all I am going to say about it and I have said it to you and not to the wall.**"

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road said the thing that about four hundred and forty people have been living inside since the seventh week of this flood and have not been able to say in a room, and she said it in the plainest words she has and she did not improve on it afterwards, and about four of the people on that causeway heard it and about four hundred and thirty-one people four miles below heard nothing.**

"**There is a man nine hundred miles from here who pays all four hundred and forty of us, and he pays in salt and a place at a fire, and there is no route in this world that gets to him, and no page anywhere that tells him anything, and I have been standing at the end of this causeway about nine times in nine weeks working out how to tell him, and I have not worked it out, and the reason I have not worked it out is that there is no way to tell him and I am not going to pretend that wanting to tell him is the same as being able to.**"

"**Then he does not know.**"

"**He does not know. And about four hundred and forty people work eleven miles from a compact that was read out on Friday in a room with a date on the door, and the compact has a line in it that says a person may be found, and the one person in this world who pays those four hundred and forty cannot be found by anybody, and I have worked that out in about nine minutes on a Sunday morning on a road and I would like somebody to write it down who is not me because I am going to say it badly.**"

Nobody wrote it down. About four people in that group have said since that they should have and that they did not have a slate, and about nine of them have said that a person saying a thing out loud in the middle of a road with about nine people on it is the oldest instrument there is and that it worked for four hundred years and works still, and about four of them have also said that it is the reason nobody can find out whether it was said at all.

---

**And about four of them stood on the causeway in the middle of the four miles with the wind off the water, and none of them had a load, and one of them was on no line of the posting at all.**
