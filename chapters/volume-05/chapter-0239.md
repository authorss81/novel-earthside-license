# Chapter 0239 — The Sixth Tuesday

The queue formed along the wall of the store on the upper road at about the sixth hour of the morning on the eighty-fourth day of the Long Month, and it formed the way it forms, and about a hundred and forty people were in it by the time the sun came over the roof. It is the sixth Tuesday that queue has run on counting from the forty-ninth day of that month, which is the one it formed on, and the fifty-sixth, sixty-third, seventieth and seventy-seventh days of that month are the four between that one and this one, and the eighty-fourth is the fourth of the six that has run on with salt in that chest instead of a wage. The ground under it had gone hard and pale and had been walked on twice since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and the top of the posting on that wall is a bare patch of the colour of the wall with the chalk gone off it in about four places.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's own board came out at the seventh hour with the cloth and washed the board and did not write on it and hung it back up, and the figures on it are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and this morning they are fifteen days old, and about four people in that yard have been counting the days on that board since the seventieth day of that month and about nine of them stopped counting on the eighty-first.**

"You have not written since the seventieth."

"**I have not written on that board since the seventieth day of that month, and the figures on it are for the sixty-ninth day of that month, and about four people in this city have worked out that a board carrying a day fifteen days old is a board about the past and about nine of them have worked out that it is still a board, and I have not been able to work out which of those two is a thing I have done and which is a thing that has happened to me.**"

"You could write this morning's."

"**I could write this morning's and about four people in this yard would read it before the eighth hour and about nine of them would not read it at all, and what would be on it is a day about about a hundred and forty people standing in the ordinary course, and there is no figure in that day, and a board with no figure on it is a board a person walks up to for nothing, and about four people in this city would call that the first honest thing on a wall since the seventh week of this flood and about nine of them would call it a waste of a morning.**"

The water in the trough had a skin of chalk gone grey across the top of it and had been washed out of it four times, and about four people in that yard looked at the trough and about nine of them looked at the queue.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything came up that road at about the ninth hour with a slate under her arm, and she has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood, and she has carried that slate since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and has never once been told to put it down, and this morning she put it down on the boards of that yard and asked one question. About four people in that yard heard it, and about nine of them did not, and nobody answered her.**

"Shall I write this down."

Nobody in that yard said anything. It is the third time she has asked that in a yard, on the sixty-sixth day of that month and again on the seventy-third, and this morning is the eighty-fourth, and about four people in this city have worked out that nobody in this city has ever not answered it, and about nine of them have worked out that this morning somebody did not answer it.

She picked the slate up off the boards and put it back under her arm and about four people in that yard watched a woman not get an answer and about nine of them had not noticed that she had asked.

---

**And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that road at about the tenth hour with a piece of chalk in his right hand, and he wrote the nine lines onto the back of a slate in a room over a market in daylight with a date on the door on the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and put a copy on the boards of a stall by a wharf on the fifty-third day of that month, and about four hundred people who read that wall in that week trace back to a table in a market and not to the wharf. He has not put a figure under any of the three prices, and he is not going to this morning.**

"That is what you came up for."

"**I have come up nine miles with a piece of chalk in my hand and about four people in that yard have watched me hold it since the gate and about nine of them have watched me not use it, and I am not going to put the date back at the top of that page, and I would like that said in that yard at the tenth hour of a morning with about a hundred and forty people in it before anybody asks me why.**"

"It is your page."

"**It is my page and the ninth line is my hand and the eight lines under it are the hand of a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom, and the date at the top of it was the forty-sixth day of the Long Month, and it has been on that wall for thirty-eight days and the wind has taken about four words off it, and I have not put it back and I am not going to.**"

"Then what is the page."

"**It is nine lines in two hands with a date gone off the top of it, and about four people in this city have worked out that a page with a date on it is a page about a day and about nine of them have worked out that a page with a day on it is a page anybody can be angry at, and the two of those have been true for thirty-eight days and only one of them is left, and I would like about four people in this yard to notice that I have worked that out standing in front of it and not after.**"

He put the chalk in his pocket and about four people in that yard watched a man put a thing away and about nine of them watched a page stay the way it was.

---

**And the woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from a market had come up that road for a third morning running and had not gone into the yard, and she stood at the gate with her own wage under her arm where it has been since the fifty-sixth day of that month, and about four people in that yard had worked out that she comes up nine miles to stand at a gate and about nine of them had worked out that she comes up nine miles to stand at a gate.**

"You are at the gate again."

"**I am at the gate and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who keeps a shop four streets down from a market has been at a gate for three mornings and about nine of them have worked out that she has, and I have not come in on any of the three and I am not going to this morning, and I have not worked out whether I come up nine miles for a yard or for the road, and I have not got time this morning to work it out.**"

"You could see the page from the gate."

"**I could see the page from the gate and I have seen it from the gate, and the top of it is gone, and about four people in this city have worked out that a shopkeeper is the only person in four hundred miles who can tell you what a thing costs and about nine of them have worked out that a shopkeeper is the only person in four hundred miles who cannot tell you what a thing is worth, and both of those are about a price and neither of them is about a wall with a date missing off it.**"

She put her hand under her own arm where the wage was and about four people in that yard saw a woman hold a thing and about nine of them had gone in.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with her arms wet to the elbow and did not come to the sacks, and there are twelve sacks in that chest and about four of them have a knot on them that is not the knot on the other eight, and about a hundred and forty people were in that yard and nobody asked her what was in it, and about four of them had decided before the seventh hour that they were not going to.**

"You have had it in there since the fifty-sixth day of that month."

"**I have had it in there since the fifty-sixth day of that month and this is the sixth Tuesday that queue has run on and the fourth of them with salt in that chest, and I have not opened it and I am not going to this morning, and a man came up that road with a piece of chalk in his hand to put a date back on a page and I have not got a piece of chalk and I am not going to ask him for one, and about four people in this yard have worked out that those are two different things and about nine of them have worked out that a woman in a yard with a man's page behind her is one thing and not two.**"

"Have you asked him whether the ninth line works."

"**No. I have not asked him and about four people in that yard have been waiting since the sixtieth day of that month for somebody to ask him and about nine of them have stopped, and a man who will not say whether a line works is not a man anybody should be asking in a yard on a Tuesday with a hundred and forty people in it.**"

---

**And a man of about forty-two went up that road at about the eleventh hour and stood at the back of that queue and did not go to the front of it and did not go into the store, and he opened the chest on the fifty-sixth day of that month in the middle of that yard in daylight in front of about nine people and there was salt in it, and about four people in that yard have been waiting since then for him to say one thing he will do instead of the thing he wants said. About nine of them stopped waiting on the eighty-first day of that month.**

"Six Tuesdays."

"**Six Tuesdays counting from the forty-ninth day of that month and two hundred and thirty-eight days of this flood behind me, and the board on that wall is the only instrument in this city that has not lied to anybody and it has not been written on since the seventieth day of that month, and I have watched it from four streets away every morning and I am not going to stand in this yard and tell about nine people that I have watched a board go from the seventieth day of that month to this morning without a figure on it, because that is a thing about a board and a yard is a place where a thing gets said four times and remembered wrong.**"

"You could say it and not mean it."

"**I could say it and not mean it and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who says a thing and does not mean it is a man who has said it and about nine of them have worked out that a man who says a thing and does not mean it is a man who has not said it, and both of those are the same man and I have known that since the forty-sixth day of that month and I have not come up nine miles to be either one of them.**"

He put his hands back in his pockets and about four people in that yard had been waiting for him to take them out and about nine of them had stopped waiting in a yard at the back of a queue on the seventy-first day of that month.

---

**And a bare patch at the top of a page on a wall where a date used to be, and the chalk of it in the trough underneath.**
