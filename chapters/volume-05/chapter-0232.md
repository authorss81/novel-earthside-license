# Chapter 0232 — The Fifth Tuesday

The queue formed along the wall of the store on the upper road at about the sixth hour of the morning on the seventy-seventh day of the Long Month, and it formed the way it forms, and about a hundred and forty people were in it by the time the sun came over the roof. It is the fifth Tuesday that queue has run on counting from the forty-ninth day of the Long Month, which is the one it formed on, and the fifty-sixth day of that month was the second and the sixty-third the third and the seventieth the fourth, and this is the third of those five that has run on with salt in that chest instead of a wage. The ground under it had gone hard and pale where about a hundred and forty people had stood on it on the last Tuesday and had not been walked on since, and the wind that had come up off the flats since the seventy-fourth day of that month was not doing anything at that wall this morning.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's own board came out at the seventh hour with the cloth and washed the board and did not write on it and hung it back up, and the figures on it are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and this morning they are eight days old, and the posting is about a foot to the left of it and the top of the posting has gone pale across about four words.**

"I have nothing to write."

"You had nothing to write on the seventieth day of that month too."

"**I had nothing to write on the seventieth day of that month and I have had nothing to write every morning since, and about four people in this yard have worked out that a man who stops writing is a man who has decided and about nine of them have worked out that a man who has nothing to put on a board has not decided anything, and I have told you which of those I believe and I am telling you again in front of a hundred and forty people because about four people in this city have said that I said it once in a yard and did not say it in a market.**"

"You said it in a yard."

"**I said it in a yard at the seventh hour of a morning with a cloth in my hand. A yard is where a person says a thing about themselves and a market is where a person says a thing about a price, and I have not said a thing about a price to anybody in this city for a long time and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.**"

The water in the trough had a skin of the old chalk across the top of it gone grey, and about four people in that yard looked at the trough and about nine of them looked at the queue, and neither of those two things is what he had been thinking about.

---

**And a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom was in that yard with a slate under his arm that he has carried since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and has not put down, and it has a decision on the front of it that he wrote on the seventieth day of that month, and this morning he came to the edge of that yard and looked at the board and did not put the slate down.**

"You are not going to put it up again."

"**I am not going to put it up again. I put a thing face up on the boards of that yard on the seventieth day of that month with a decision against myself on it and about four people in that yard watched me do it and about nine of them did not know what it was, and I have had seven days to work out what I did and what I have worked out is that a thing anybody can walk up to and look at a second time is a thing about the person who looked and not about the thing, and I am not going to be a man who is looked at.**"

"Who would look twice."

"**About four people in this city would, and about nine of them would not, and the four of them would do it to see whether I had put it there and not to see what was written on it, and I have decided that I would rather be a man whose slate nobody in this city can check.**"

He put the slate under the other arm and about four people in that yard saw a man change which side of himself a thing was on and about nine of them had already gone inside.

---

**And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from a market had walked nine miles to that yard for the second morning running and had her own wage under her arm where it has been since the fifty-sixth day of that month, and she put her back against the wall on the far side of the yard from the board because there is a nail in that wall about the height of a person's shoulder and she did not want the sacking against it.**

"You have come two days running."

"**I have come two days running and I have come nine miles twice and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who keeps a shop four streets down from a market has been walking nine miles twice to stand against a wall, and about nine of them have worked out that I have come about the wind, and I came about the wind on the seventy-sixth day of that month and I have come about the wall today, and the wall is that page and about four people in this yard know it and about nine of them do not.**"

"You could stand by the door."

"**I could stand by the door and the door is where about a hundred and forty people are coming in and out of and I am not going to stand in a doorway on a Tuesday about a page that has lost the top of itself, and I have not been in this yard for the whole of the time that page has been on that wall, and I am not going to start by standing in the way of a queue.**"

She shifted her weight off the nail and about four people in that yard saw a woman move two inches and about nine of them saw a woman stay exactly where she was.

---

**And a man of about forty-two came up that road at about the seventh hour and went to the back of that queue and did not come to the front of it and did not go into the store, and he has had his hands in his pockets since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month at the back of a line of his own making, and about four people in that yard noticed him come in and about nine of them had stopped noticing on the forty-ninth day of that month.**

"Five Tuesdays."

"**Five Tuesdays counting from the forty-ninth day of that month, and the seventy-seventh is the fifth, and I have been in this flood for two hundred and thirty-one days and I have five of those Tuesdays and nothing has come out of that chest since the fifty-sixth day of it, and I have watched that board from four streets away every morning and the board is the only instrument in this city that has not lied to anybody, and I am not going to say anything about that in a yard.**"

"You could say it in a market."

"**I could say it in a market and about nine people in this city have been waiting since the fiftieth day of that month for me to say something in a market, and what I would say in a market is that a board which has not been written on since the seventieth day of that month is more use to about four hundred and forty people on eleven miles of flats than anything I have ever said, and I have not got a figure for that and I am not going to put one on it, and that is why it is a market sentence and not a yard sentence and why I am standing at the back of a line instead of saying it.**"

He took his hands out of his pockets and put them back and about four people in that yard had been waiting on that pair of hands since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and nobody in that yard asked him a question all morning.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that road at about the ninth hour, and he wrote the ninth and last line of that page in his own hand on the forty-fourth day of the Long Month, and he read that line under the ninth every morning that week without looking up, and this morning he stood under the top of the page instead and looked at the bare place where a date had been for about as long as it takes to wash a board.**

"You have not looked at the ninth line."

"**I have not looked at the ninth line this morning and about four people in this yard have worked out that a man who wrote a line has stopped reading his own page and about nine of them have worked out that he is looking at the top of it because the top of it is going, and I have not been asked whether the ninth line works since the sixtieth day of that month and I am not going to be asked it this morning, and I would like about four people in this yard to notice that the man who put the date on that page has not come up the road today and that the man who put the ninth line on it has.**"

"You are the same man."

"**I am the man who put the ninth line on it and the man who put the date on it is a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom and he is not in this yard and he has not been in this yard since the seventieth day of that month, and I am not going to stand in a yard with about a hundred and forty people in it and say the word date and have about nine of them think I have said the word wage.**"

He looked at the ninth line then, from about nine feet under it, without moving his head, and about four people in that yard watched a man read a thing he did not have to read and about nine of them had gone in.

---

**And Adrian Vale came up that road on foot at about the sixth hour of the morning before the queue was a queue and got the trough out from under the wall and carried it out to the edge of that yard and emptied it and brought it back. He did it again about nine times, and about nine people in that yard knew who he was before he got to the gate and about four of them had been waiting since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month for somebody to say a name.**

"You are on no rota in this building."

"**I am on no rota in this building and I am on no rota in any building and that is not a complaint and I am not going to make it one, and about four people in that yard have worked out that a man on no rota carrying a trough is a man who has found the one thing in that yard nobody is on a rota for, and about nine of them have worked out that I would have carried it anyway, and both of those are true and the second one is the one I would like entered.**"

"The trough is not a wage."

"**The trough is not a wage and I have not carried it for a wage and I have carried it because the board is washed every morning at the seventh hour and it cannot be washed in a yard that has nine feet of standing water in it, and that is a thing about a wall and not about a man, and about four people in this city would say that is the first useful work anybody has done in this yard and about nine of them would say it is the first work anybody has done in it that is not about a day, and I have not been able to work out which of those two is a compliment.**"

He put the trough back under the wall at the eighth hour with the cloth over the rim and the board back up above it with eight-day-old figures on it, and about four people in that yard watched a man put a trough back and about nine of them watched him not go into the queue standing nine feet away from him.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with her arms wet to the elbow again and there are twelve sacks in that chest and about four of them have a knot on them that is not the knot on the other eight, and about a hundred and forty people were in that yard and nobody asked her what was in it, and about four of them had decided before the seventh hour that they were not going to.**

"You are not opening it."

"**I am not opening it, and this is the fifth Tuesday that queue has run on and the third of them with salt in that chest, and I have not opened that chest since the fifty-sixth day of that month and I am not going to open it this morning, and about nine people in this yard have got here on the strength of a Tuesday and about four of them have got here on the strength of a line on a wall that has lost the top of itself.**"

"**The ninth line is still there.**"

"**The ninth line is still there and it is the only reason any of them are standing in my yard, and the ninth line is at the bottom of a page and the part of that page that is going is at the top of it, and I have watched the top of it go and I could not tell you which morning it started, and about four people in this city have worked out that a page whose date comes off is a page about work and about nine of them have worked out that it was a page about a day, and I am not going to be the one who says which of those two it is now.**"

---
