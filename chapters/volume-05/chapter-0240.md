# Chapter 0240 — Put Down

A bag of salt came off the front of a man's chest and went down onto the boards of the yard of the store on the upper road at about the eighth hour of the morning on the eighty-fifth day of the Long Month, and about four people in that yard had been waiting since the seventy-third day of that month for him to do that and about nine of them had stopped waiting on the eightieth. The wind had come up off eleven miles of flats since the seventy-fourth day of that month and it was still at that wall, and the boards of that yard were the colour of the boards under them, and the bag stood on them in a shape that was going to be a mark in about four days. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board had carried that bag against his chest for twenty-three days and he put it down and did not open it and did not say what was in it.

---

**And there is a mark on the front of his coat where the bag has sat and it has been there since the sixty-second day of that month, when he counted a quarter of a sack out of his own hand into another one's in a market in this city, and the salt in that bag went hard on the sixty-sixth day of that month and a bar would not go into it, and he said in a yard on that day that the day he breaks it he has got two figures and neither of them is the one he paid. Break is not open.**

"Nobody asked you to put that down."

"**Nobody asked me to put that down and I put it down, and about four people in this yard have been waiting twelve days for me to put a thing on those boards and about nine of them stopped on the eightieth day of that month, and both of those are about a bag of salt and neither of them is about me, and I have worked out since the eighth hour of the morning that a man who puts a thing down in a yard has decided he is carrying it, and I have not decided that.**"

"Then why did you."

"**Because the wind has been at that wall since the seventy-fourth day of that month and that bag has been against my chest for twenty-three days and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who is carrying a thing in front of him cannot be told anything by anybody standing behind him, and about nine of them have worked out that I have been walking about nine miles with a thing against my chest that I could not put down in a yard, and both of those are true and the second one is about a man and not about a bag.**"

The mark on the front of that coat was still on it and the coat was hanging in the same place on him, and about four people in that yard looked at the mark and about nine of them looked at the bag.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that store's board came in with the cloth in his hand at about the ninth hour and looked at a bag of salt standing on the boards and did not pick it up, and the board on that wall has not been written on since the seventieth day of the Long Month and the figures on it are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who washes a board every morning has nothing to say about a mark on the floor of a yard and about nine of them have worked out that he has.**

"**I am not going to write that down.**"

"**I did not say anything about writing it down and you have said it for me, and about four people in this yard have worked out that the man who washes that board is the man everybody in this building tells things to by mistake, and about nine of them have worked out that he is the man nobody tells anything to. I have not written on that board since the seventieth day of the Long Month and I am not going to, and a mark on a floor is not a day and I have said that out loud in a yard on the seventieth day of that month and I am not going to say it again this morning.**"

"You said you had nothing to write."

"**I have had nothing to write since the seventieth day of that month and I have washed that board every morning since and hung it back up, and this morning there is a bag of salt standing on the boards of that yard, and about four people in this city would put that mark on a board and about nine of them would not, and I am the man who has the cloth in his hand this morning and I am not going to be the man who put a bag on a page.**"

He wrung the cloth out over the trough and about four people in that yard watched a man wring a cloth and about nine of them watched a bag stand on boards.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back and looked at a bag of salt standing on the boards of her yard and did not pick it up, and she is on no list in that store, and the chest is behind her with twelve sacks in it and about four of them have a knot on them that is not the knot on the other eight.**

"**That is a wage.**"

"**That is a wage that a man has been carrying against his chest for twenty-three days and that came out of a chest in this building in a yard in front of about nine people on the fifty-sixth day of that month, and about four people in this yard have worked out that a wage is a thing a person is given and about nine of them have worked out that a wage is a thing a person has, and about four hundred and forty people on eleven miles of flats have neither of those two, and I have not got anybody in this yard who is going to say which of the two words is the right one.**"

"He has not opened it."

"**He has not opened it and it has gone hard and a bar will not go into it and he said in this yard on the sixty-sixth day of that month that the day he breaks it he has got two figures and neither of them is the one he paid, and he put it down this morning, and about four people in this yard have been waiting since the sixty-sixth day of that month for one of those two things and about nine of them have been waiting for neither, and I am one of the nine and I would like that written down in a hand before anything else in this yard is.**"

"Written down by whom."

"**By the woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything, who put a slate on the boards of that yard on the eighty-fourth day of that month and asked one question and about four people in that yard heard it and about nine of them did not and nobody answered her, and I have thought about that for a night and I have not got a better instrument and I am not going to ask her for hers.**"

---

**And Adrian Vale came out of the back of that store at about the ninth hour with the trough under his arm and carried it to the edge of that yard and emptied it and brought it back and did that about nine times, and about nine people in that yard knew who he was and about four of them had been waiting since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month for somebody to say a name, and nobody said it, and nobody asked him about a gate and nobody offered him anything.**

"That is the ninth time."

"**That is the ninth time and I have done it nine times on the seventy-seventh day of that month, on the eighty-fourth day of that month and this morning, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who empties a trough nine times is a man who is not on a rota in this building and about nine of them have worked out that he is the only one in this yard who is not on a rota, and I am not on a rota and I have never been on a rota and the trough does not care and that is the whole of what I have learned from it.**"

"You have had a paper in that coat since the thirteenth day of the Long Month."

"**Since the thirteenth day of that month, and it is in the inside of that coat, and it is a share with the post taken out of it, and I have neither said yes nor said no. About four people in this city have worked out that a man carrying a thing without answering it has decided not to answer and about nine of them have worked out that he has not, and I have not, and I have taken that paper out of that coat once, on the seventy-first day of that month, and put it back in about the time it takes to turn a thing over, and I have not taken it out since and I am not going to this morning.**"

---

**And the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board was still at the gate of that yard at about the eleventh hour and the bag was still on the boards four feet from where he had put it down, and nobody in that yard had picked it up and nobody had asked him to pick it up, and about four people in that yard had worked out that two people can stand nine feet from a bag for three hours. About nine of them have worked out that one of them is a woman on no list and one of them is a man who wrote an objection to a compact into the page he is party to.**

"Nobody has touched it."

"**Nobody has touched it and I put it down three hours ago and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who puts a wage down in a yard in front of about nine people has handed it to somebody and about nine of them have worked out that he has not, and I have not, and the only two people in this yard who could pick it up are the two of us standing here and neither of us is going to.**"

"It is going to leave a mark in four days."

"**It is going to leave a mark in those boards, and about four people in this city have worked out that a mark in the shape of a bag is a thing that can be read and about nine of them have worked out that a mark in the shape of a bag is a thing that has been here, and there is a woman in this city who has been on a page since the forty-fifth day of that month and a stripe of wet on four boards at the bottom of a cut that the wind took off, and I have put a bag on the boards of a yard about four feet from a beam and I have not got anything in my mouth about which of those two things this is.**"

About four people in that yard watched two people stand nine feet from a bag and about nine of them went in, and the wind was still at that wall.

---

**And a beam stood in the middle of that yard on a block of stone with one pan on it and a sack of salt sitting in that pan, and it has stood there since the seventy-third day of that month, and the man of about fifty-two who digs made it on the fifty-eighth day of that month and carried it up that road and left it there and has not carried it home, and about four people in this yard had worked out inside three days that every length of wood anybody in this city has been holding came off a rack.**

"That beam is still there."

"**That beam is still there and a sack has been sitting in the one pan on it for twelve days and the wind has been at that wall since the seventy-fourth day of that month and about four people in this city have worked out that a quarter of a sack in an open pan in a yard is a quarter of a sack that is being left out and about nine of them have worked out that it is a quarter of a sack that is being kept, and I am the man who made the pan and I have not touched it and I am not going to.**"

"You could carry it in."

"**I could carry the sack in and I could carry the beam in and I carried that beam up that road from the fifty-eighth day of that month and I left it in this yard on the seventy-third day of that month, and a beam with one pan on it is a thing that can do one of the two things it was made to do, and about four people in this yard have worked out that the one thing it can do is telling you what a thing is, and about nine of them have worked out that the thing it is telling me about is a sack a man has just put down on some boards, and I would like about four people in this yard to notice that I have not been in this yard for twelve days and that everything in it has happened without me in it.**"

---

**And a bag of salt standing on the boards of a yard with the wind still at the wall, and the man who brought it already back on the road.**
