# Chapter 0207 — Sent For

About four people were standing in front of his stall at about the seventh hour of the morning on the fifty-second day of the Long Month, and one of them was holding a slip of paper with a date on it, and he had kept that stall for about nine weeks, and he came out from behind it and the four of them had come about a price and not about him.

It is a Friday. The queue's day is a Tuesday. The posting has been up six days on a wall nine miles up the road past this city and about four hundred people have read it, and the causeway has had nothing on it for ten days, and the store's chest has a week left in it.

---

**And the slip is a copy of a line off the posting with a date cut into the corner of it, and there is a hand against the line, and the hand is his, and he wrote it on the Thursday of last week in a room over a market in about four minutes, and then went back down to his own stall and opened it up for the day.**

"**You put a price under a line with your name on it.**"

"**I put a price under a line and I wrote my own sentences into a page and I have said in about nine rooms in three weeks that a man who says a thing out loud in words cannot be lied to, and I was wrong about one half of that and the half I was wrong about is that a man who says a thing out loud in words can be fetched.**"

"**Who sent you.**"

"**Nobody sent me. That is what I have been standing here working out for about four minutes. There is a page on a wall nine miles up the road with a date at the top of it and a price under a line and a hand against that line, and a slip of it came down the road this morning with a date cut into the corner, and about four people in this city have decided that a slip with a date on it is somebody sending for a person, and I have got no way of finding out whether they are right and I have not been in a room to ask.**"

"**You could ask.**"

"**I could ask in a room in daylight with a date on the door and about nine people in it and I would find out in about four minutes, and that is exactly the instrument that stopped its author in this city about three weeks ago, and I am not going to use it to find out whether I have been sent for. If I have been sent for, then a page can be sent for with, and that is a fact. If I have not been sent for, then a slip is a piece of paper. Either way I find out by walking to it, and I am walking to it.**"

---

**And he walked about four streets and went into a room over a market with a date in chalk on the door, and about nine people were in it, and about two hundred and forty could hear out of the hall and the landing and the market below it, and it is the seventh time about two hundred and forty people have heard a thing said in that room and nobody counted and about four people have said since that it is the seventh and that nobody will be able to say in about four months which seven.**

"**You have a slip with a date on it.**"

"**I have a slip with a date on it and a price on it and my own hand against the line, and I have come four streets to be told what it says, and I would like to say before anybody else speaks that I put that line on the wall on Saturday morning with my hands and I put the store and its queue ninth because I put it there on purpose, and I have not taken it off and I am not going to, and everything that has happened in the last three days as a result of it is mine.**"

Nobody improved on that. It is entered that about four people in that room had been going to say something about the ninth line and did not, and that about nine of them have said since that they were waiting to hear him own it and would have been angry if he had not.

---

**And then about four people in that room asked him the same question in four different ways in about nine minutes, and it was one question, and it is the price under his own line, and he could not answer any of the four, and the finding of the fifty-second day of the Long Month is in what he said when he stopped trying.**

"**A sack, a place at a fire, and a day of the queue.**"

"**That is what is written. Is it right.**"

"**I do not know. I have been asked that four times in nine minutes and I am going to give you the same four sentences to all four of them and I am not going to improve on them for the fifth person.**"

"**You wrote it.**"

"**I wrote the nine sentences into a page on the Thursday of last week in a room over a market, and on the Friday the compact was read out and the posting was bound to it, and on the Saturday I put it on a wall at about the seventh hour of the morning with a bar of chalk, and at about the ninth hour I admitted in a yard that I did not have a number under any of it. A man of about twenty-four from a workroom said on that same Saturday that a page with a date on it and a price under every line and no figure under any price is a list of intentions, and I told him it was the price, and I have been telling myself it was the price for six days, and about four of you have come to find out whether it is.**"

"**It is not. I do not know what a sack is worth to a person who has not got one. I do not know what a place at a fire is worth. I know what a day of the queue is worth because I have stood at the back of it, and I have never been able to put a figure on that either and I have been getting paid in it for nine weeks.**"

---

**And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, who was in that room and who has a column of his own with eleven entries in it and no figure on the end of any of them, said the sentence that about nine people in that room had been waiting six days for without knowing they were waiting for it.**

"**Then it is a cost and not a price, and I said the difference out loud on Saturday morning to about nine people who did not ask me, and about nine people in this city have written it down since and about four of them have written it down wrong.**"

"**A cost is what a person says they are paying. A price is what a person says it is worth. You have written three costs on a wall and called them a price because the document said there had to be a price under every line, and about nine people in four hundred miles are going to read that wall in about four days and work out that the fourth line of a compact nobody can administer is administered in four words by a man with a bar of chalk.**"

"**Then take it off the wall.**"

"**No. I am not going to take it off the wall and I want that entered, because if I take that page down then about four hundred people who have read it have read nothing, and a page that is wrong is worth more this week than a page that is missing, and I have eleven entries in a column that I have been arguing about for a month and I am not going to be the man who takes down a page somebody else put up.**"

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-four who has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood put her hand flat on the first line of a copy of the compact in that room, in daylight, with a date on the door, did not read that line out, read the second name out loud, and then stopped, and about nine people in that room had their hands on the table and did not move them, and about four hundred people in that room could not have told you which name she had not read.**

"**There are four names on the front of that page and there are about nine people in this room whose hands are on the table, and I have been in this city since the second day of this flood and I am the person who writes things down, and I have been telling nine rooms in three weeks that a name on a page is a place a person can be found in, and I have never once been on one, and this morning I have been on one, and I did not put myself there, and I did not know I was going to be on one until Friday of last week.**"

"**And I am not going to read the rest of the names out. Two of them are people in this room and one of them is standing nine feet from me and about nine people in this room know which one, and the fourth is the name at the top of it and I have not read that one out once in seven days and I am not going to start in a hall.**"

"**Then what are you saying.**"

"**I am saying that a page with a name on it is a room. Not a metaphor. I have been in about nine rooms in three weeks and about nine of them had somebody sent for, and I did not notice that the sending was in a document, and neither did they, and about four people in this city have worked it out this morning and about nine of them are sitting in this room.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything was at the back of that room with a plain slate under her arm and her own in her bag, and she had heard the whole of the morning, and she said the thing that about nine people in that room had not thought of, and she said it in about four sentences and then sat down.**

"**There is one of us in this room who is not on the front of that page. It is me. I read it out twice from a plain slate because my own has my name at the top of it, and I have said that reason in about nine rooms this week and I am not going to say it again, and I am not saying it now. I am saying the other thing.**"

"**I am the only one of the nine of us who cannot be fetched, and it is because I was careful. And about four people in this room have spent the morning finding out that being on a page is a thing that happens to you, and about nine of them did not know it had happened, and I have known it since the second week of this flood because I refused to be.**"

"**And I am going to say the part that is going to be the most unpopular thing said in that room since the second month of this flood, and I have thought about it on the road and I have not got a better version. Being unfindable is a thing I did on purpose and it has cost me nothing at all, and about four of you have just found out that being findable is a thing that was done to you and it is going to cost you something you cannot put a figure on, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the two of those are not the same kind of thing and that neither of them is fair.**"

Nobody in that room answered her. It is entered that about four people wanted to and about nine of them have said since that there was nothing to say that was not either a promise or an argument, and that about four of those nine have said that a girl of seventeen being the only person in a room who is safe is not a thing this city should be proud of and is not a thing it can be ashamed of either.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the one thing she came to say, and she said it in about four sentences and it is hers and it has not improved in seven days, and about two hundred and forty people heard it.**

"**Four people and a question have not left for the ground people call the First Landing. Nobody has been asked whether they will go and nobody is going to be asked. The only instruction it has is *to open nothing*, and that is my sentence and I wrote it. This page does not send it, this room does not send it, and nothing that was read out on Friday of last week ends it or touches it, and I would like both halves of that entered.**"

"**Nobody sent you.**"

"**Nobody sent me and nobody fetched me and I came, and that is the whole of the difference between me and the four people in that line, and I have been in this city long enough to know that is a difference somebody is going to want explained and I am not going to explain it.**"

---

**And a slip of paper with a date cut into the corner of it, and three words on it in a hand that is not the hand that cut the date, and the three words are a line number and nothing else.**
