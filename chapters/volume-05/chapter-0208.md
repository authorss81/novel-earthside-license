# Chapter 0208 — In Front Of The Stall

A man was standing in front of the stall by the wharf at about the eighth hour of the morning on the fifty-third day of the Long Month and he was not buying anything and he was not going to, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall had a slate in her hand that she has carried for nine years and has never written on, and she saw him from about nine feet off and she did not put the slate down.

It is a Saturday. The queue's day is a Tuesday. The posting has been up seven days on a wall nine miles up the road past this city, the causeway has had nothing on it for eleven days, and the store's chest has a week left in it, and about nine people were standing about nine feet from a stall by a wharf on a Saturday morning because about four people in this city had worked out in four days what a name on a page is for and this was where they had come to see whether it was true.

---

**And he had the back of a slate. There was no slate on the front of it. On the back, in a hand that had been copying all afternoon and had got the spacing wrong in two places, was the front of a page, and there is a name at the top of it, and about nine people nine feet away had worked that out before he got to the stall.**

"**I copied it on Friday of last week.**"

"**From a table.**"

"**From a table in the market under the room, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, while about two hundred and forty people were still standing under it. I copied the front of it onto the back of a slate because I have been in this city for the second month of this flood and I have been arguing about pages since the seventh week, and a page ought to be in two places. That is all the reason there is. I have not sent it anywhere and I have not shown it to anybody until this morning and I have been standing in front of this stall for about four minutes deciding whether to say that out loud or not, and I have decided to say it, because I would rather be laughed at than be a man who has carried a piece of paper about for nine days.**"

Nobody said anything. About nine people nine feet away had heard all of it.

---

**And she came out from behind the stall, and she has been on a page for eight days, and she asked in advance on the forty-fifth day of the Long Month not to be thanked, and nobody has thanked her, and about four people in that yard have said since that they do not think they could.**

"**You are the first one.**"

"**I am.**"

"**Then you are the first thing that has happened to me since Friday that I did not choose, and I want to be exact about the price before I say anything else, because I named it in a room in my own words and about two hundred and forty people heard me, and I would like to know whether I got it right.**"

"**You got it right.**"

"**I did not get it right. I said that anybody in four hundred miles with a copy could walk to that wharf and stand in front of that stall and wait, and that I would be the one who comes out, and that I would be findable while I am arguing. That was the price. That is what I said and I am not going to unsay it.**"

"**It is the eighth day and there is nothing to argue about. It is the eighth hour of the morning on a Saturday and I have got a slate in my hand that I have not written on in nine years and there is a man in front of my stall and about nine people nine feet away who have come to look at me doing nothing, and that is not the price I named. The price I named was findable while I am arguing. What I have got is findable on a Saturday morning with nothing to argue about, and about nine people in this city have had eight days to work out that the two of those are not the same thing, and I am the one who is standing here finding out.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came down the wharf at about the ninth hour and stood about four feet off and did not come closer than that, and he said the thing nobody had said, and it is in a book in this city in his own words with the date on it.**

"**It did not come from here.**"

"**No.**"

"**I want to say it properly because about four people in this city have decided since Friday that the wharf is where it started. It is not. The wharf is where it turned up. There is a man with the back of a slate who copied a page out on a table in a market at the fourth hour of the afternoon on Friday of last week because a page ought to be in two places, and every single thing that has happened since — a wall nine miles up the road, two figures under a line, a refusal nobody can record, a slip with a date cut into the corner of it and a man walking into a room to be asked what his own price is worth — every one of those came out of that table and not out of this stall, and it took four days, and the man who did it is standing in front of you and he is about forty-four and he keeps a stall and he has not said one word all morning that was clever.**"

"**He said a page ought to be in two places.**"

"**He said a page ought to be in two places and about nine people in this city have been arguing with that sentence since Friday, and about nine of them have won, and about four hundred and forty people are not going to know any of it happened.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four came down to the wharf at about the ninth hour with a slate under her arm and she had walked four streets and had been standing in a market for about an hour, and she did not go and stand next to either of them, and she said the one sentence about the rule of the Reach that anybody has said about it since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and she said it in a yard and not in a room, and it is not a room and nobody asked her a question.**

"**The rule of the Reach says every household affected by a change must be heard. It has been in this city for four hundred years, it has no name on it, and it has a hole in it, and the hole is the shape of a household, and I have not been near that household and neither has anybody in this yard and neither is anybody going to.**"

"**I am saying that in a yard, at the ninth hour of the morning, in daylight, at about nine feet from a woman who has been on a page for eight days, and I am not going to walk about four hundred miles and I am not going to tell anybody what I know, and I would like about four people to hear me say it and about nine of them to know that I said it and I am not going to say it twice.**"

"**And I would like it written down in the same hand as everything else, and I would like somebody to write that a hole and a wharf were in the same yard on the fifty-third day of the Long Month and that nobody joined them up, because the day this city joins them up is the day it has a column.**"

Nobody joined them up. A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall heard her say it and looked at the water with his thumb going along the edge of the boards and then said a thing about a channel with no water in it, and about nine people in that yard have said since that he did that on purpose, and he has entered that he did.

---

**And then the man of about forty-four who keeps a stall said the last thing of the morning and he said it to her and not to about nine people nine feet away, and it took about nine seconds and it is the only thing anybody said in that yard that was about the copy itself.**

"**I am not going to apologise for it and I am not going to be praised for it and I would like both of those entered. I made a second copy of a page because a page ought to be in two places, and the second copy has been in a market, and a bar of chalk, and a yard nine miles up a road, and a slip with a date cut into the corner of it, and it has not been in my own house once and it is not going to be. Put that in the book and do not put me in it with anything else.**"

"**What else.**"

"**I have eleven entries in a column of my own and I have argued about a cost for a month. Do not put me next to that. I have been in two rooms since Friday and both of them were about somebody else's page.**"

---

**And at about the second hour of the afternoon a second man came and stood in front of the stall, and he had nothing in his hands at all, and about nine people nine feet away worked out before he had got to the fourth step that he was not the second person anybody had sent and that he was the first person anybody had told.**

"**I heard about it.**"

"**Where.**"

"**In a market, off a woman who heard it off a man who heard it off a stallholder who was in a room on Friday. I do not know any of them. I have got no slate and no copy and I have not walked up a road or past a gate, I have walked four streets, and I came because somebody told me there was a woman at this stall whose name is on a page now and I have never seen a name on a page that I could go and look at.**"

And she put the slate she has carried for nine years down on the boards of the stall for the first time in the whole of the time anybody in this city has been watching her, and she did not pick it up again for about a minute, and about nine people nine feet away saw her do it and about four of them have said that it is the only thing that happened in that yard all day that they will still be able to describe in about four months.

"**Say the sentence back to me.**"

"**There is a woman at this stall whose name is on a page.**"

"**That is it. That is the whole of it and you have got it exactly right and you have never seen the page, and I have been standing here for about four hours being looked at by a man with the back of a slate, and I want somebody to write down the difference between him and you because it is the only thing I have learned today that I did not already know on Friday.**"

"**A page is a place a person can be found in. I said that in a room, or somebody said it, and it is true and it took eight days and a man with a slate to show me that it is the small half. The other half is that about four people in this city told about four other people in about eight days, and there is no page in that and no office nine hundred miles away that can stop it and no form for ending it, and I do not know which of those two men in front of me is more dangerous to me and I have been thinking about it since about the fourth hour of the morning and I have not got an answer and I am not going to pretend that a person who is findable is a person who can be found only by somebody with a document.**"

She picked the slate up again and did not write on it, and it is the ninth year of not writing on it, and about nine people nine feet away did not see her write on it and about four of them have said that they looked for it.

---

**And a slate with the front of a page on the back of it, lying face up on the boards of a stall by a wharf, and nobody's hand on it.**
