# Chapter 0213 — Two Of The Three

A sack came down onto a beam and two pans in the yard of the store on the upper road at about the eighth hour of the morning on the fifty-eighth day of the Long Month, and the top of that yard was churned to mud and the sun had not come over the roof of the store yet. The beam was made by a man of about fifty-two who digs out of a length of iron and two lengths of wood, and he had brought it up nine miles on his shoulder that morning, and there was a mark on the front of his coat where the sack had sat. About nine people in that yard watched a sack of salt go down on it, and about four of them had come to find out what the three prices on a wall nine feet above them were worth.

---

**And he moved the pan along the beam four times and got it to sit. The beam dipped under the weight and came back, and he put his thumb under the low place in it and showed them where it was. He did not say the number, and about four people in that yard had to ask him for it twice before he said it out loud, and he said it in a mouth, once, and nobody improved on it.**

"**That is a sack and a ninth of a sack, and I have got that beam wrong by about a ninth and I have been getting it wrong since the second month of this flood, and everybody within nine miles of this yard has been weighing salt in a bucket since the seventh week of this flood, and I am telling you the ninth and not the number so that whoever writes it down has to put a thing in their own hand on it.**"

Somebody in that yard wanted it in a hand and said so in one word.

"Write it."

"**I am not going to write it. I am a man who digs. I have got a beam and two pans and I have got a ninth off it and I have not got a weight for it, and I am not going to be the man who puts a figure on a thing in four hundred miles and then goes and digs a channel.**"

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road was in that yard, and she has come up the road with him twice since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and had not been in this yard at all before today. She had the figure from him in a sentence and gave it back in a sentence, and about four people heard both.**

"**One and a ninth, and not one and a half, and the reason he is telling us the ninth is that he would rather have a page with a mistake in it than a whole of good sentences, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said that in this yard on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and did not do it, and I have carried that ninth up nine miles in my head since the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month and I am the one who is saying it out loud today, and neither of those two men is in this yard.**"

"And he is not going to know you said it."

"**He is going to know inside five days, because about nine people in this city say a thing in a yard and then say it somewhere else, and that is how everything that has happened on that wall happened, and I have been on this road since the second month of this flood and I know exactly how far a thing said in this yard travels. It is about nine miles and I have never once known it to take longer than that.**"

---

**And then the man of about fifty-two who digs said the thing that the sack on the beam had been in the yard for, and it took him about four minutes. He said it with his hands still white to the wrist from the salt, and about nine people heard it, and about four of them have said since that it is the first time anybody has put those three words next to each other in daylight. He said himself that it was not a finding.**

"**A sack is a thing I can put on a beam. A place at a fire is a thing a person can be found at, because somebody has to see a person to give them one, and about nine people in this city have worked that out since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and not one of them has written it down. A day of the queue is a thing a person can be found in, for the same reason, and since the seventh week of this flood.**"

"So two of the three are findable."

"**Two of the three are findable and one of them has got a weight on it this morning, and about four people knew that before I said it and about nine of them have been carrying a sack past a fire since the seventh week of this flood without noticing that a person standing at a fire is a person somebody could go and get. I am not saying that is a bad thing. I am saying that a man of about thirty-four wrote three prices under three lines on the forty-fourth day of the Long Month in a room with a date on the door, and two of the three of them are a person showing up somewhere, and nobody has ever put that on a page about anything.**"

"What would you put it on."

"**On a slate, in my own hand, and not on that wall, and I would like it entered that about four people in this yard have been waiting for me to say that I am not going to, and I am not.**"

Nobody in that yard had a piece of chalk in their hand. They had come nine miles and they had their hands in their sleeves and about four of them turned a pocket out to show that, and there was no chalk in it. The posting on that wall is the same page it has been since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month, with a date at the top of it and a price under every line and no figure under any of the prices.

---

**And the man of about thirty-four who wrote nine of the eleven lines of that page came into that yard at about the eleventh hour and read the ninth off a slate that a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road was holding, and he did not touch it. There was mud on the side of one boot and not on the other, and it is entered that he had come nine miles on foot. About four people in that yard watched him not touch it.**

"That is a figure under one of my lines."

"**It is a figure under one of your lines that a man of about fifty-two who digs made out of iron and wood, and it is in her hand, and it is not on your wall. If you put it on your wall then everybody in four hundred miles who has read that page is going to read the other two prices as worth nothing, and I would like you to say out loud that you know that. And then I would like you to say out loud that you are not going to put it up, and I have watched you not put a page on a thing in about nine rooms and I am not asking you for another one.**"

"I have not written it."

"**I know you have not written it. I have been in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and I have not seen you write anything, and that is not why I am saying this. I am saying it because you are the only man in this city who could put a figure on that page and get away with it, and the only reason you would get away with it is that about four people have decided you are honest, and that is a worse thing to be than wrong.**"

He did not say the rest of it to her. He turned about nine feet away from her and put both hands on the beam, and about nine minutes later he said the same thing to nobody at all, which is that a page with one figure on it out of three prices is a page that says the other two are worth nothing, and that he is not entitled to say that about a fire and a queue. About four people in that yard heard him say it and about nine of them wrote nothing down.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything came up that road in the middle of the afternoon and put the ninth on a slate of her own in her own hand and did not put it on the wall. The chalk she had brought was in her fist and she put it in her pocket instead, and about nine people in that yard watched a figure appear in this city that was not on any page, and nobody in that yard told her she should not.**

"Nobody has said that is a price."

"**It is not a price. It is a ninth of a beam that a man made out of iron and wood, and it is in my hand, and the other two of the three have nothing in any hand anywhere in four hundred miles, and I have written the one down because I write things down and I have not written the other two down because I do not have them.**"

"You could leave a gap."

"**I could leave a gap, and the gap would be a shape, and the shape would be a price, and about four people in this yard would read it as somebody's opinion about a fire. I have been reading that page for twelve days and I have not been able to say what one line of it is worth, and I am not going to be wrong about a gap.**"

---

**And about four people in that yard asked whose beam it was before the end of the afternoon, and the man of about fifty-two who digs answered them in four sentences, and it is entered that about nine people in that yard have said since that it is the first true thing anybody has said about where a thing in this city comes from and that nobody acted on it.**

"That is not your beam."

"**The iron came off a gate and the wood came off a rack and I made the pans out of a sheet of iron I found at the bottom of a cut in the seventh week of this flood, and about four of you have now asked me whose it is and I am going to answer all four of you and then I am going to stop answering anybody.**"

"Whose gate."

"**I do not know. I have been on about nine of these flats since the second month of this flood and I have never once been asked by a keeper and I have never once asked one, and there is a woman of about thirty-eight at the top of one of these channels who keeps a gate and who has a boy of eight, and I am not going to stand in a yard nine miles from here and say whose gate it was. The rack is worse. A rack is four lengths of wood and a rope and four people, and about nine of them in this yard have stood next to one today and not one of them could tell you whose.**"

"So it is nobody's."

"**It is in my yard and I will not answer that. It is in my yard, and I do not know where the iron came from, and about four people in this city have said since the fortieth day of this flood that a thing with no name on it is the only kind of thing there is, and I made the beam in the seventh week of this flood and it has been in my yard since the second month of this flood, and there has been a thing on it every day since the forty-second day of the Long Month, and I have not thought about that once until this afternoon.**"

He did not say whose it was again. He wiped his hands on his coat, which put more salt on the coat than it took off, and about four people in that yard asked twice and about nine of them did not ask at all. The beam and the two pans stayed in that yard, and about nine people walked past them on their way to the store and about four of them looked.

---

**And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by a wharf came up the road for the second time since the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month and stood in front of that beam, and the sun had come over the roof of the store by then and the wet had gone off the top of the sack. She has been on a page for thirteen days and she asked in advance not to be thanked, and about four people in that yard looked at her to see whether she would say the thing all of them wanted her to say and she did not.**

"You can be found at a fire now."

"**I can be found at a fire and I can be found in a queue and I have been on a page for thirteen days and men have stood in front of my stall, and about four people in this yard have been waiting since the forty-ninth day of the Long Month for me to say that this beam is the first honest thing anybody has put next to that page, and I am not going to say it, because a man of about fifty-two who digs did not do it for me and I did not ask him to.**"

"You could have said you had."

"**I could have said I had, and then about four people in this city would have had a woman of about thirty-four saying a thing was good, and I had been on nothing for two hundred days when I read that page out on the forty-fifth day of the Long Month, and I have thought very carefully since about what the ninth line of that compact costs, and one of the things it costs is that I am now a person who says when things are good.**"

Nobody in that yard said a word about it. The sack settled on the boards with a sound when she shifted her weight, and about four people in that yard were angry and about nine of them were not. She went down the nine miles on foot and did not stay, and the beam and the two pans stayed in that yard overnight, and about four people have said since that nobody in this city knows whose beam it is in the morning.

---

**And a beam and two pans in a yard nine miles up the road with a ninth of a weight on a slate in a woman's hand, and nothing at all on the other two.**
