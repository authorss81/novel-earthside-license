# Chapter 0244 — The Only Line

On the eighty-ninth day of the Long Month the posting on the wall of the store on the upper road had one line left on it that anybody could read from the ground, and that line was the ninth, and it was the last line, and it was in a different hand from the eight above it, and the eight above it were there and could not be read. The wind has been at that wall since the seventy-fourth day of that month and the fine stuff off eleven miles of flats has been going into the face of that chalk all that time, and the ninth line was the last one written and it was written with a harder piece of chalk than the others and by a hand that leaned on it.

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that store's board was at the foot of that wall at about the eighth hour with the trough and the wet cloth, and he has washed that board every morning since the seventh week of this flood, and he has not written on it since the seventieth day of that month, and the figures on it are the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month, which was twenty days ago.**

"You are late."

"**I am not late. I am at the eighth hour the same as I have been at the eighth hour since the seventh week of this flood. What has happened is that there is a line on that wall I can read from where I am standing, and I have not thrown a bucket of water up a wall in this yard since the seventieth day of that month, because there is nothing on the board to clean and there is one line on the page above it, and I did not know that until this morning.**"

"How long is it going to last."

"**The chalk is not going to last. The chalk on that wall was never going to last, and the wind has had fifteen days at it and the date at the top went in about four places, and what is left of those eight lines is the shadow of eight lines and about four people in this city can still find them from about nine feet off, and the ninth is the one that a person standing in this yard at the eighth hour can read with their hands down.**"

---

**And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and who wrote that ninth line came up that road at about the eighth hour and stood under that wall with his hands behind him, and he had come up here on the eighty-fourth day of that month with a piece of chalk in his hand and had not put the date back and had put the chalk in his pocket instead, and about four people in that yard had been waiting since the eighthieth day of that month for him to say one word about the eight lines that have gone, and the rest had given up waiting on the eighty-eighth.**

"You have not washed the wall."

"**I have not washed the wall and I am not going to, and if I do it will be with a cloth and not a bucket, and the reason is a piece of chalk. Those eight lines went first because they were done with a soft one in a hurry at the ninth foot of a wall, and yours has gone last because you leaned on it, and the wind off those flats has been eating the difference for fifteen days, and this morning there is one line where there were nine.**"

"You still have the chalk."

"**I have still got the chalk and it is in this coat and it has been in this coat since the eighty-fourth day of that month, and nobody has worked out that I have been carrying it to this wall every morning to find out that I am not.**"

He took it out and looked at it and put it back, and the woman of about thirty-four in that store watched him do that from the trough and did not say one word about the chalk.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four in that store came out of the back with her arms wet to the elbow and asked him the thing nobody has asked him since the seventy-fourth day of that month, and she asked it in a yard in daylight with what is left of a date on the wall above it.**

"Does it work."

"**No. That is the fourth time and it is the first time anybody has asked me with nothing at the front of it. I said it three times and the fourth one is going to be the same, and I am going to give you the reason this morning, because the reason has changed since the seventy-fourth and the old one would be a lie.**"

"Give me the new one."

"**On the seventy-fourth I said I would not be the man who says whether a line works on a morning when there is nothing at the front of it, and that is still true and it is not the reason. The reason is that a line is not tested by the man who wrote it. It is tested by the people it was written for, and they have to be able to read it themselves and say so in a yard, and there is one line on that wall and it is the one I put on last, and if I stand under here and say that it works then the only person in four hundred miles who knows whether it works is me.**"

"Then nobody knows."

"**Nobody knows, and I have been in this yard twice on a thing and I have not once been the person who is believed, and I would rather be a man who wrote a line that nobody can check than a man who says his own line works in a yard at the ninth foot of a wall.**"

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven went up four steps of the yard with the cloth in his hand and put his thumb flat on the bare place at the top of that wall where a date had been on the forty-sixth day of that month and had gone in about four places, and he took the thumb away and there was chalk dust on it and about four people in that yard saw a man's thumb find a place where something had been.**

"**There are four things on that ground and one of them is me, and I am the only one of the four who is going to ask about any of them.**"

He put his thumb into the wet patch where the pail had gone over this morning and left a print about the size of a thumb in the grit, and then he wiped it out with the side of his hand and made a second print about the same size, and nobody in that yard said one word about a man putting a mark down and taking it back.

"**And I have washed that board every morning since the seventh week of this flood and I have never once written a date on it that was not the day before, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who only ever writes the day before has been keeping a record, and nobody has worked out that I have not been keeping a record, I have been keeping a day.**"

"You could write the day over the top of that."

"**I could write a day on a wall that has had a date on it and lost it, and about nine people in this city would call that putting it back, and the rest would call that writing a new one, and those are not the same thing and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can tell me which of the two a page is, and I wash a board.**"

"You said the board is the day."

"**I said the board is the day and the posting is the page, and that was on the seventy-sixth day of that month with a board on the ground and a date gone off a wall, and about four people in that yard heard both halves of it and one of them wrote nothing down, and this morning the board is the day and the page is one line, and neither of those is a thing I can wash.**"

---

**And Adrian Vale was at the gate of that yard at about the eighth hour with his hands at his sides, and he had come up nine miles on the eighty-ninth day of that month and there was nothing in that yard for him to carry, and about nine people in that yard knew who he was and nobody said a name and nobody asked him for anything.**

"You are not on anything."

"**I am not on anything, and there is nothing at my feet, and I have come up this road four times in ten days and on three of them there was a trough or a pail or a sack, and this morning there is none of those, and I came anyway, and nobody has worked out that I have admitted it.**"

"You could go up the road."

"**There are nine miles of road between here and a woman who is on this page. The man who wrote that line came up it on the eighty-fourth day of that month with a piece of chalk in his hand and put the chalk in his pocket instead of using it, and I have come up it five times with nothing, and about nine people in this city can fetch me by a name I did not choose and not one of them has done it in ten days, and I have stopped noticing that, and I do not think I am allowed to stop noticing it.**"

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven wrung the cloth out once with both hands over that trough and stopped, and left the wall above the ninth line unwashed, and the water in that trough was cold and there was a bucket standing in it that nobody in that yard lifted.**

He stood with the cloth in his two hands and did not put it on the rim for about the length of a breath, and then he hung it over the rim of the trough where it has hung every morning since the seventh week of this flood, and the two marks the cloth left on his hands were white at the knuckles, and the woman of about thirty-four in that store went back inside. The man of about thirty-four who keeps that stall went down the nine miles with the chalk still in his coat.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four in that store went back through to the back room at about the ninth hour and there were eleven sacks along that wall and a groove in the floor under them with a crust of salt in the bottom of it, and about four of those eleven have a knot on them that is not the knot on the other eight, and somebody in that back room has known about those four knots since the fifty-sixth day of that month and nobody else has.**

"Nobody has counted those."

"**Nobody has counted those and nobody is going to count those, and the reason I am saying it out loud in a room with a door is that there is a woman of about fifty in this city whose whole trade is writing down figures off things and she has not been in this room once in four months, and nobody has worked out that I would rather a crust in a groove stayed in a groove than got a figure on it.**"

"You could weigh one."

"**I could weigh one of the four on that beam in the yard and I have not done it and I am not going to, and nobody has worked out that I put them there so that I could see that I had not opened them.**"

---

**And the man of about twenty-seven did not wash the wall for the rest of the eighty-ninth day of that month, and the cloth stayed on the rim of the trough, and the ninth line stayed where it was at about the ninth foot of that wall, and the bare place at the top of that wall stayed bare above it.**

"You will have to say something about that eventually."

"**I will have to say something about it to somebody, and the somebody will not be me, and nobody has worked out that the reason I washed that board every morning was that a day came past and I had put something on it, and now I am not putting anything on anything and I have not washed a wall.**"

"That is not the same as a decision."

"**It is not a decision and it is not not-a-decision, and those are the two places a person can stand when there is a wall in front of him with one line on it, and about nine people in this city have worked out that standing still is the third place and a few of them in this city have worked out that there are only two places, and I have been in the third one since the seventy-sixth day of that month and I did not know it was a place.**"

---

**And the store on the upper road did the ordinary work of a morning in the middle of all that, and a man came in at the ninth hour for a measure of something and got it, and a woman came in at the tenth hour for the same thing and did not get it, and neither of them looked up at the wall while they were in there.**

"Did you write it on the board."

"**I did not write it on the board and I have not written on that board since the seventieth day of that month, and the figures on it are for the sixty-ninth day of that month and they are still right about that day, and a person who wants to know what a thing cost on the ninety-fifth day of that month is asking a question that nobody in this building has an instrument for, and I am not going to build one out of a board and a cloth in the middle of a morning when there is one line on the wall above me.**"

"Then what will you tell him."

"**I will tell him what I told the last four of them, and the last four of them were told on the seventy-first day of that month and the seventy-fourth and the seventy-sixth and the eighty-first, and about four people in this city have worked out that a shopkeeper who says the same thing four times has stopped thinking and nobody has worked out that I say it the same way four times because I have not got a new one, and both of those are the same man at the same counter.**"

"Would a new one be better."

"**A new one would be better for about four days, and then about four people in this city would have the new one and the old one would be gone, and I would have said two different things about two different days and neither of them would be about a day, and I have got a board on that wall with the truth about the sixty-ninth day of that month on it and I am not throwing that away to be interesting at the counter.**"

---

**And a wet cloth on the rim of a trough going cold, with one line left on the wall at the ninth foot above it.**
