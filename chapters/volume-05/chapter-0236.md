# Chapter 0236 — A Hand Opened Once

A man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close put a wage he had had for twenty-five days down on the boards of a stall by a wharf at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the eighty-first day of the Long Month and put his other hand flat on the boards either side of it, and the wind that had come up off the flats since the seventy-fourth day of that month was coming off the water at that wharf instead and had gone round the corner of the stall and stopped. He carried that wage four streets twice on the sixty-eighth day of that month and he has not opened it, and the knot on it is the knot it came out of the settlement's chest with on the fifty-sixth day of that month. About four people in that market had been waiting since the ninth hour of the morning for him to do a thing with it and about nine of them had not come to that market at all.

---

And the woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall came out from behind it and did not touch the sack, and she has been on a page since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month and it is thirty-six days, and about four people in this market know what that is and about nine of them do not, and she asked in advance not to be thanked and has not been.

"You have not opened it."

"**I have not opened it and I have had it twenty-five days and it came out of a chest in a yard in front of about nine people and I was one of about a hundred and forty it came out to, and about four people in this market have worked out that a wage nobody opens is a wage somebody is still refusing and about nine of them have worked out that a wage nobody opens is a wage somebody has forgotten about, and I have not forgotten about it and I have not refused it and there is a third thing it is and I have not got a word for the third thing.**"

"You could sell it."

"**I could sell it to about nine people in this market in about four minutes and I have not done it and I am not going to, and about four people in this city have worked out that a thing that is not a price does not have to be sold and about nine of them have worked out that a thing that is not a price is not worth anything, and those are not the same and I have been on a page for thirty-six days and I have not said a word in any of this market about a page.**"

She went back behind the stall and left a gap about the width of a person between her and the sack, and about four people in that market saw a woman leave a gap and about nine of them had not known there was one to leave.

---

And a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came along the boards of that market at about the fifth hour of the afternoon with a page in her own pocket with a date on the back of it, and it is not a slate and it is not in a book and it has two names on the front of it and no third. About four people in this market have known that since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and about nine of them have never been in a room she was in.

"You keep a page in a pocket."

"**I keep a page in a pocket and I have carried it since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman with a page in her pocket is a woman who can be held to something, and about nine of them have worked out that a woman with a page in a pocket is a woman who has taken it out of a book, and I have done both of those and I am not going to take it out of that pocket in this market.**"

"You could date it."

"**I could put a date on the back of that page this afternoon and about four people in this market would see a woman with a dated page and about nine of them would not, and there is a woman standing nine feet from me who asked what a date was in four words at the seventh hour of the evening on the thirtieth day of the Long Month and was told nothing, and I am not going to be the second person in this city to hand a date to a person who is not asking me for one.**"

She went on along the boards and about four people in that market watched a woman keep a page in a pocket and about nine of them had gone inside before she got to the end of the stall.

---

And at about the sixth hour of the afternoon his hand opened, and it was not put anywhere and nothing happened to it, and it stayed open for about as long as it takes to take a board off a stack and then it shut again and did not come open a second time that day.

Nobody in that market said a word about it. About four people in that market were looking at his hand and about nine of them were looking at the sack, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall was looking at the boards.

"Twenty-five days."

"**Twenty-five days since the fifty-sixth day of that month and I have not opened it and my hand has not closed on anything properly since before that, and about four people in this market have worked out that a hand which does not close is a hand that cannot hold a sack and about nine of them have worked out that a hand which opens by itself is worse, and I have not got an argument against either of those and I have not been asked for one, and about four people in this market have just watched it happen and I would like it noticed that nobody in this market has said one word about it and that I have not either.**"

"You will want to say something about it."

"**I will want to say something about it in about four days and I will not, and the reason is that a hand that opens once is not a thing anybody in this city has a word for, and about four people in this market are going to write one down and about nine of them are not going to, and if I say something first then the four of them will have mine and I have not had one and I am not going to have one by the sixth hour of a Saturday.**"

He put the hand down on his knee under the boards of the stall where nobody could see it and about four people in that market watched him put it out of sight and about nine of them had stopped looking at it before he did.

---

And a girl of seventeen stood at the rail of that wharf at about the seventh hour of the evening with a plain slate under her arm that has her name at the top of it, and she is the only person in this market who is not on the front of any page in this city, and about four people in that market knew that and about nine of them did not, and nobody asked her anything at all. She read the top of it out under her breath and stopped in the middle of a line and put the slate face down on the rail and left her hand on it.

"That is not on the front of any page."

"**It is not on the front of any page and it is on the rail of a wharf in this city at the seventh hour of the evening and about four people in this market have looked at it and about nine of them have not, and my own has my name at the top of it and that is the only reason I am standing here, and I have not been asked anything by anybody today and I have not wanted to be asked anything today.**"

"You looked at a line and stopped."

"**I looked at a line and stopped because a hand opened on the boards of a stall and I have been standing at this rail since the fourth hour of the afternoon and I have not been asked one question, and a girl of seventeen is not a person anybody comes to with a question, and about four people in this city have worked out that being unfindable cost me nothing and about nine of them have not, and I have worked out that this cannot go on the way it has been going and about four people in this city have worked out the same and about nine of them have not, and I am not going to be the one who writes down the day it stops.**"

She put her hand on the slate and left it there, and about four people in that market watched a hand on a slate and about nine of them had gone inside.

---

And four streets down from that wharf a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop had nine measures on a shelf that is not against an outside wall and had moved them there on the seventy-sixth day of that month and told nobody, and she has said more about the prices on a page nine miles up the road than anybody in this city and she has not said one word in this shop about a hand.

"You were at the wharf."

"**I was at the wharf and I am not going to say what I saw there, and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who keeps a shop four streets down from a wharf is four streets from anything that happens at that wharf and about nine of them have worked out that I go, and I do go, and I have not said one word about a hand and I am not going to and about four people in this city have worked out that I have not said one word and about nine of them have worked out that I have not seen anything.**"

"You have not seen anything."

"**I have not seen anything, and a shopkeeper four streets from a market is the only person in four hundred miles whose whole trade is a thing that happened somewhere else, and about four people in this city have understood that sentence and about nine of them have thought it means I do not care, and I care about it more than anything in this shop and I cannot put it on a shelf without it being a price.**"

"Have you ever said anything to him."

"**I have never said one word to that man about a wage and about four people in this city have worked out that two people in this city are carrying a thing they have not opened and about nine of them have worked out that we are four streets apart and have never once said so, and I have known that since the fifty-sixth day of that month and I have not gone four streets and I am not going to on a Saturday.**"

---

And the man of about thirty-one stayed at that stall until the light went off the boards and did not pick the sack up and did not open it and about four people in that market had watched his hand and about nine of them had not, and the boards of that stall are the colour of the boards under them and the sack standing on them is going to leave a mark in about four days and about four people in this market have worked that out and about nine of them have not.

"You have been there since the fourth hour."

"**I have been here since the fourth hour of the afternoon and I have not picked that up and I have not opened it and I am not going to, and about four people in this market have worked out that a man sitting next to a wage he has not opened is a man refusing something and about nine of them have worked out that he is a man who has run out of hands, and both of those are about the hand and about four of them have seen the hand and about nine of them have not, and I have not shown it to anybody and I am not going to.**"

"Will you take it home."

"**I will take it home and I have not got a house with a floor in it that I would put that on, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who has not got a floor has not got anywhere to put a thing, and about nine of them have worked out that he has got a coat, and I have got a coat and there is a mark on the front of it and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

The light went off the boards of that stall and about four people were still at it and about nine had gone, and the gap about the width of a person was still there.

---

And a hand that opened once on the boards of a stall by a wharf, and a gap about the width of a person left standing behind the sack.
