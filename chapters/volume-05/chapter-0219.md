# Chapter 0219 — Face Down

A woman of about thirty-four in a store on the upper road turned a slate face down on a chest in the back of that store at about the eighth hour of the morning on the sixty-fourth day of the Long Month. The slate made a noise on the lid of the chest that about four people in that back room heard and nobody repeated. The date on it was eight days old, and the store was open, and about nine people had come nine miles in the last four days to read it.

---

**And about four people were in the back of that store when she did it, and she did not ask them to leave, and she put the flat of her hand on the back of the slate and left it there for about as long as it takes to decide a thing. About nine people in that yard have said since that she did it in front of them on purpose, and about four people have said she did it in front of them because she could not do it in front of a person she had not met.**

"Put it back up."

"**No.**"

"It is the only page in this city with a date on it that anybody has read."

"**It is the only page in this city with a date on it that anybody has read, and the date is the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month, and the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month was a Tuesday. About nine people came nine miles to read it, and about four hundred and forty people on eleven miles of flats have not heard of it and are not going to. I put it up on the Saturday morning of the forty-sixth day of the Long Month so that nobody would have to ask me what the date was. I have asked that question myself, in four words, at the seventh hour of the evening on the thirtieth day of that month, and I was told nothing, and that is on about nine slates and none of them is mine.**"

"Then why turn it over."

"**Because it is a page about a day that has gone. There is a sack in that chest instead of a wage and there is a board on that wall with a figure on it about a Tuesday that is a day old and a page on the same wall with the only functioning way out of this world at the bottom of it in your handwriting, and there is no next date. I am not going to write one. A slate with a day in the past on it is a thing about the past, and about nine people walked nine miles in four days to stand in front of a date, and about four of them asked me whether the last Tuesday had happened and I said it had, and that is the whole of what that slate has been for and it is finished.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was in that yard at about the ninth hour and came into the back of the store and did not ask her to turn it back up. He has been in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and has not put a sentence on a page since the fiftieth day of that month, and about four people in that yard have been waiting since the forty-ninth day of the Long Month for him to say something about a slate.**

"You have taken the clock down."

"**I have taken down the only thing in this city that had a date on it that anybody could read, and I want the word you just used written down next to it, because a date is not a clock. A clock is a thing that runs on and a date is a thing that happened. There has not been a clock in this city since the thirty-fourth day of the Long Month and about four people in this yard have been calling a slate a clock for seven days because they wanted one.**"

"Who wanted one."

"**I did. I did not read it at all between the forty-sixth day of that month and the forty-ninth, and I have read it about four times a day since the forty-ninth, and about four people in this city have read it nine miles on foot, and I am one of the four who wanted a date to be a clock. That is what I am able to say about it and I am not going to say anything else in this yard.**"

He did not ask her to turn it back up. He looked at the back of the slate and then at the door of that store and did not say anything. About four people in that yard were waiting for him to and about nine of them were not, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has now been asked to undo a thing in a yard and has said no twice since the fifty-first day of the Long Month, and neither of the two was said in a market and both of them were said to a person who had made the thing herself.

---

**And then Adrian Vale came down that road on foot at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, walked into that yard and stood about nine feet from a slate lying face down on a chest. He had nine miles of road on his boots and he did not sit down. About nine people in that yard knew who he was before he got to the door, and about four of them have said since that they knew because of the way he did not hurry.**

Nobody fetched him. It is entered that nobody did, and that about four people in that yard had been waiting since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month for somebody to say a name and that about nine of them heard nothing, and that a man who can be fetched by about nine people in this city stood about nine feet from a chest for about nine minutes and was not fetched.

"You have a slate face down."

"**I have a slate face down and the date on it is eight days old and it was the only date anybody in this city could read.**"

"There are about nine people who could have said a name."

"**I know how many there are. I have known since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month and I have not said it once in this yard and about four people in this yard have been waiting ten days for one of them to use one and I would like it entered that not one of them did, and that I have not said my own name, and that being findable is a thing a person can be fetched with and being unfetched on a Wednesday afternoon is not the same as being unfindable and I am the only person in this yard who knows which of those two those are and I am not going to explain it to anybody.**"

"You could have gone and stood somewhere else."

"**I could have. There is a room over a market in this city and about two hundred and forty people in it and I have not been in it since the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month, and I came up nine miles on foot to stand in a yard, and the reason I came is that a page went onto a wall nine miles from this city on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and about nine people in this city have been fetched by it and about four hundred and thirty-one people it is about have not been fetched by anything at all, and I wanted to see the other half of it.**"

"And what is it like."

"**It is like a woman turning a slate over.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything. It is entered that about four people in that yard were waiting for him to say something about being findable and that he did not, and that about nine of them have said since that a man who gave his own name out loud in a room on the fifty-fourth day of the Long Month walked nine miles to look at the back of a store and then said one sentence about a slate.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by a wharf was not in that yard and had not come up the road, and about four people in that yard knew that and about nine did not, and it is entered that nobody in that yard sent for her and that about four people in that yard wanted to.**

"You have had a share with the post taken out in that coat for a long time."

"**Since the thirteenth day of the Long Month. It is fifty-one days and I have neither said yes nor said no, and I gave up holding it as a refusal on the seventeenth day of that month in a room and I have not given it up in a yard since, and I am not going to start today because a woman has turned a slate over.**"

"She would have said something about that slate."

"**She would have said the true thing about it and about four of us would have been grateful and about nine of us would have heard the price of the ninth line in a yard for the third time since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month, and I have had it twice and I am not going to have it in a yard nine miles from my own stall.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four who is on no roll of anything was in the back of that store with a slate under her arm and she has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood, and she asked one question at about the ninth hour. It was the only question anybody in that yard asked all day that was about a person and not about a page, and she asked it with the chalk already out.**

"Shall I write it down."

"**No.**"

"And why not."

"**Because I had been on nothing for two hundred days before the forty-fifth day of the Long Month and I asked what the date was, in four words, at the seventh hour of the evening on the thirtieth day of that month, and I was told nothing, and I have never asked anybody to write that down because I did not want it written down, and I have not thought about it once since and I thought about it about four minutes ago when your hand was on that slate.**"

Somebody in that back room said the obvious thing.

"**Somebody in this room is about to say that a woman asking a question in four words is worth more than a slate with a date on it. Do not. I am not a document and I have not been one since the second day of this flood and I am not going to let a good sentence get made out of me this morning.**"

"And I am going to write it down anyway."

"**I know you are. You have never once not written a thing down in this city and I have watched you do it since the second day of this flood and you are going to write it down with the date on it in your own hand and you are not going to join it to anything, and I am not going to stop you, and I would like it entered that a woman of about thirty-four in a store said no and a woman of about twenty-four wrote it down and neither of them said the word refusal.**"

She wrote it down, and she wrote the date at the top of it before she wrote anything else, and it is four lines, and the third line is that she was asked and said no. About four people in that yard have said since that it is the first page anybody in this city has written about a person turning something over rather than about a person putting something up, and about nine of them have said that that is not a distinction, and about four of them have said that it is the only distinction anybody has made since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month.

---

**And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board was in that yard at about the seventh hour of the evening and said nothing at all. He came in after the light had gone off the boards of the yard and stood at the back of it with his hands behind him. About four people in that yard were waiting for him to say something. He said a sentence in a yard on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month about a page with a mistake in it and a wall of good sentences, and about nine people in that yard have said since that he heard about the slate at about the sixth hour, looked at it, and went and stood at the back. He is a party to the compact the posting is bound to, and he asked one question about the ninth line in a market on the sixtieth day of the Long Month and nobody answered him, and he has not said a word about it since.**

---

**And about the seventh hour the slate was still face down on the chest, and the store was still open, and the light had gone off the back room and come back off the yard. About nine people went up that road in the eight days after the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month to read a date that is not there any more, and about four of them asked the woman of about thirty-four what the date was and she said it, because she has not stopped being a person somebody can ask, and that is a different thing and it is not on a slate.**

**And a slate face down on a chest in a store nine miles up the road, and a date on the other side of it, and nobody's hand on the front.**
