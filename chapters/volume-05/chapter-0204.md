# Chapter 0204 — Its Place On It And Not Above It

About a hundred and forty people were in the yard of the store on the upper road at about the sixth hour of the morning on the forty-ninth day of the Long Month, and the queue had formed along the wall in the ordinary course, and the posting was on that wall from about the seventh foot down to the floor, and every one of them had to stand under it to get to the door.

It is a Tuesday. The queue's day is a Tuesday and about nine of those people are owed a wage on this morning, and the store's own board is washed and up beside the door with the day's figures on it, and about nine hundred measures of salt are standing in a yard at the bottom of a cut covered over, and the causeway has had nothing on it for seven days.

---

**And the store's queue is the ninth line of the posting and it is the last line, and the last line is in the hand of a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and about four people in that yard worked out which line it was inside about four minutes and about nine did not work it out at all until a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close read the whole of it out loud with his finger.**

He got to the ninth line and stopped with his finger on it.

"**The store on the upper road is on this posting, and its queue is on this posting, and it takes its place on it and not above it.**"

"**That is mine.**"

"**That is yours. And you wrote it last. There are eight lines above it and the eighth one is the water in the channel and the seventh one is the road and the fourth one is a person who may be found, and the only line in this yard that forty people can get through is the ninth, and you put it at the bottom.**"

"**I put it at the bottom on purpose and I wrote that line on Thursday in a room over a market and I put my hand to it, and I have said since that it is the only line in nine sentences anybody in that room loses something by, and I have now found out what it costs, and I am going to say what it costs in about nine minutes and about four hundred people are going to hear it and about nine of them are going to be angry with me and I am not going to shorten it.**"

---

**And the wage came out of the store at about the sixth hour of the morning in the ordinary course and about nine people were paid and the chest it came out of has one week left in it, and a woman of about thirty-four in that store, who is on no list in it, stood at the door of the store and read the number off the slate that hangs on the chest and said it out loud to about nine people, and about four of them did not know it and about nine of them did.**

"**One week. The last Tuesday that can come out of this chest is the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month, and I have put that date on the slate and hung it on the chest so that nobody has to ask and nobody has to be told a number they did not ask for.**"

"**Who told you to do that.**"

"**Nobody. I have been in that store for about nine weeks and I asked what the date was, in four words, at the seventh hour of the evening on the thirtieth day of the Long Month, and a man of forty-two found out he had been running a settlement on a word for nine weeks, and he has not told me to do this and I would not do it if he did. I am not a title and I am not a rank and I am not an office and I have not been named, and there is a slate on a chest with a date on it and my hand is the hand that put it there.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said what it costs, and it took about nine minutes, and it is in a book in this city in his own words, and a woman of about twenty-four was in the yard and wrote none of it down and has not been asked to and did not offer.**

"**One. I put the only thing in this city that works at the bottom of a page.**"

"**Two. I did it in a room on Thursday and I have told about nine people in five days that I did it on purpose, and I have not told one of them what being on purpose costs, and about four of them worked it out yesterday and about nine of them are working it out now.**"

"**Three. A queue is a line that nobody decided. It is in the order that people turned up in it and the order that people turned up in it is the only honest thing about it, and there is nobody anywhere who can be angry with a person for being ninth in a queue, and about nine people in this yard have been first in that queue for weeks and would have told you that themselves and would have been right.**"

"**Four. A posting is a line that somebody decided. It is in the order it was written, and it was written in the order of the arguments in a compact, and the arguments in a compact are in the order that nine people made them on Thursday afternoon, and about four hundred people in this city think that is a smaller thing than it is.**"

"**Five. And from Saturday morning the only functioning way out of this world is ninth on a page, and I did that, and I am not going to be the one who says that is somebody else's fault.**"

"**Six. And the share I took is on this posting too, and it is not above any of them, and I have read that line about four times today and about nine people in this yard have watched me read it, and I would like it entered that I read it four times and that about four of them waited for me to say something about it and I did not.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything. The queue moved about two feet along the wall.

"**Seven. On Thursday I said that a wage that stops is not a favour that stops and the difference is somebody standing at the end of it. That is still true and there is no one standing at the end of this one, and it has been four days, and about four hundred and thirty-one people on the flats are the ones who will find out first.**"

---

**And a man of forty-two stood at the back of his own queue at about the seventh hour of the morning with his hands in his pockets, and about nine people in that queue had read the posting, and about four of them had read the ninth line and worked out what it was before anybody said it, and he said the thing he has said in about nine rooms and he said it standing, and he did not put a page on anything.**

"**The wage went out at six. It does not matter what line it is on. I have got one week of it in a chest and I have got a share on the same page as about nine hundred other things and I did not write one word of the page and I would like that said in a yard at about the seventh hour of a Tuesday morning because in about four months somebody is going to say I put a queue at the bottom of a document, and I did not, and the man who did is standing about nine feet from me and he has just said seven sentences about it.**"

"**Then you are content.**"

"**I am not content and I am not not content and I am standing at the back of my own queue on a Tuesday morning and about nine people in it are going to be paid today, and one week of that is in a chest, and after that nobody in that store knows, and I have said all of that in about nine rooms since the seventh week of this flood and I have not said it any better this morning than I said it then.**"

He did not ask for the posting to be changed. It is entered that he did not, and that about four people in that yard were waiting for it, and that a man of forty-two who has been in about nine rooms since the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month and put a page on a table in three of them stood in a yard and did not put one on this.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-four, who has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood, stood at the sixth hour of the morning and wrote the order of that queue down on a slate in about four minutes, and then at the seventh hour she wrote it down again, and the two lists are not the same list, and she has entered that she has two.**

"**Thirty-eight names on the first and forty-one on the second, and eleven of the forty-one were not on the first at all, and I have not asked a single one of them a question, and I want that entered. I stood in that yard with a slate at the sixth hour and again at the seventh and I wrote down what I could see and it was not the same yard.**"

"**Because people joined.**"

"**Because people joined, and because about nine of the ones who were there at six went out into the road and came back in at seven, and I do not know why and I did not ask, and the reason I did not ask is that I have been the person in this city who writes things down since the second day of this flood and I am not going to start asking a queue what order it is in.**"

"**Then you have two lists of the same yard.**"

"**I have two lists of the same yard an hour apart and neither of them is the posting, and the posting has a fixed order on it that nine people decided on Thursday, and about four people in this yard have said to me in the last three days that a page is a thing that can be checked, and I have had two lists of one queue in my hand this morning and I could not tell anybody which of the two was the yard.**"

She put both slates in her bag and did not join them and did not read either of them out, and about four people in that yard have said since that she is the only person in this city who could have made one list of it and did not, and about nine of them have said that the reason is the same as the reason she did not write the split down on the forty-third day of the Long Month, and she has entered that the reason is the same.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said a hundred and nineteen days at about the eighth hour of the morning in that yard, in a mouth, once, and nobody improved on it and nobody joined it up to anything, and it is a different debt and it did not change this week.**

"**A hundred and nineteen days is what the thirty-nine men under the near gate of the Lock of Salt have gone unpaid, and the first of March was the sixtieth day of the new month, and they are not in this yard and they are not on the ninth line and they are not on any line of that page, and I am not going to stand here and join that up to anything because joining it up to anything is the only thing anybody has done about it for a hundred and nineteen days.**"

"**They know.**"

"**They know. About four of them have said so this month and about nine of them have not, and I have not been asked which and I am not going to go and ask.**"

---

**And the queue went along the wall for about two hours under a page that had it ninth, and about four people in that yard read the posting and did not read the store's board, and about nine read the board and did not read the posting, and about nine people read both and one of them was a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and she said nothing at all about either of them.**

**And a slate with a date on it hung on a chest in the back of that store, and the date on it is the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month.**
