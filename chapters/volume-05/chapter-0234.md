# Chapter 0234 — Deeper At One End

The floor of the back room of the store on the upper road was swept for the first time since the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month at about the seventh hour of the morning on the seventy-ninth day of that month, and the sweep brought up a groove in the boards that runs the whole length of the wall the eleven sacks are along, and the groove is about the width of a sack and it is four inches deep at the end the sacks go down at and about half that at the other end of the wall. There is a white crust in the bottom of the groove along its whole length, and the boards on either side of it are the colour of the boards under them. Nobody in that building had made the groove and about four people in that room had walked its length more times than they had walked the length of the wall.

---

And the woman of about thirty-four in that store swept it, and she is on no list in that store and nobody in that building asked her to, and there are eleven sacks along that wall and one sack standing in the middle of the floor with the groove going under it, and it has been standing there since the seventy-first day of that month.

"Nobody asked you to sweep it."

"**Nobody asked me to sweep it and I would like that said before anything else about this floor, because about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who is on no list in a building has swept a floor in that building and about nine of them have worked out that it is the fourth thing she has done in that room in three weeks that nobody asked her for, and I am not going to be a person who is given a list of the things she has done for free.**"

"You are paid."

"**I am paid out of a chest that has not been opened since the fifty-sixth day of that month, and about four people in this room have worked out that a woman who is paid in salt and is not opening the chest is a woman who is sweeping her own floor with her own wage under her arm, and about nine of them have worked out that the wage is in her own house, and I have not got anybody to tell which of those two is right and I have not asked.**"

The crust in the groove came up off the boards in a line about the width of a thumb and broke where she swept it, and it went into a heap and it was white, and it was not wet and it was not dry, and about four people in that back room looked at the heap and about nine of them looked at the sack standing in the middle of the floor.

---

And about four of the twelve sacks in that room have a knot on them that is not the knot on the other eight, and the salt in those four has come up to the stitch line at the mouth and crumbles where you put a thumb on it, and the woman of about thirty-four in that store put those four at the bottom on the fifty-sixth day of that month and has told nobody. About four people in this city have worked out that a woman who can see all four of a thing at once does not need a slate, and about nine of them have worked out that she can count them, and she has never counted them in front of anybody.

"**That is not off a sack.**"

"**It is not off a sack and I have looked at it and I have looked at the four with the other knot on them and I have not put a thumb in any of the four, and about four people in this city have worked out that there are four sacks in this room that were opened and tied again on the fifty-sixth day of that month and about nine of them have worked out that there is one sack standing in the middle of a floor with the same salt in it, and those two are not the same question and I am not going to be asked which of the four it came out of.**"

"You do not know which one."

"**I do not know which one and I am not going to find out from a floor, and a floor is the worst instrument in this city for finding that out, and about four people in this room have worked out that a woman who has put four sacks at the bottom of a heap so that she can see all four of them at once is a woman who has an answer to that and about nine of them have worked out that she does not, and I have got both of those and I have not been asked either.**"

The crust in the heap went grey where the light from the door came across it and about four people in that back room watched a heap change and about nine of them had gone to look at the eleven along the wall.

---

And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that store's board stopped in the doorway on his way out with the trough and looked through into the back room at a slate lying face down on a chest, and it is the only page in this city that ever had a date on it that anybody could read, and it went face down on the sixty-fourth day of the Long Month, and nobody has written a replacement. About four people in this city have known that since the sixty-fourth day of that month and about nine of them have stopped bringing nine miles to read it.

"You are looking at that."

"**I am looking at that and I am not going to go in there and turn it over, and about four people in this city have worked out that the only person in this building who could put a date on anything is a woman who is on no list in it, and about nine of them have worked out that I wash a board and could put a date on a board, and I could, and I am not going to, and a board with a day on it is a day late every morning and I have had enough of that this morning.**"

"It has been there for fifteen days."

"**It has been face down on that chest for fifteen days and the only clock in this flood was the quarter and it ran out on the thirty-fourth day of the Long Month, and about four people in this city have worked out that a slate going face down is the last thing this city had that was going to run out on a day, and about nine of them have worked out that it was not going to run out on anything, and I have not got an opinion and I am not going to get one in a doorway.**"

He went out with the trough and about four people in that back room watched a man carry a thing out of a room and about nine of them watched a slate stay face down.

---

And the man of about twenty-seven who washes that store's board came in with the trough under his arm at about the eighth hour and stopped in the doorway, and he has not washed a floor since the fifty-sixth day of that month and he is the only person in that building who washes anything, and the trough is the one that stands under the wall outside.

"You have taken the dirt out of a groove."

"**I have taken nine days of dirt out of a groove and there is a crust in the bottom of it that came up out of the boards, and I have been looking at that groove since about the fourth hour of the morning on the seventy-first day of that month and I have not said anything about it because I did not have a sentence for a groove in a floor, and I have got one now and it is two words long and I am not going to say them in this room.**"

"Say them in here."

"**I am not going to say them in here, and the two words are not about the groove. A groove is not a column and a crust is not a figure and a hole somebody has swept out is a hole somebody has been in, and about four people in this city have worked out that there is one measuring place in this flood and about nine of them have worked out that I am the one who washes the board, and I have not got a sentence that does both of those at once and I am not going to pretend to.**"

He put the trough down in the doorway and about four people in that back room watched a man put a thing down and not pick it up.

---

And Adrian Vale came in at about the ninth hour and the sack in the middle of that floor had a groove under it about four inches deep at one end and about two at the other and he looked at it for a while and did not lift it, and about four people in that room had been waiting since the seventy-first day of that month for him to lift it and about nine of them had stopped.

"You have not lifted it."

"**I have not lifted it and I am not going to lift it and nobody in this room is going to ask me to, because I said on the seventy-first day of that month that I was not going to be asked to lift it by anybody in this building including myself, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who says that out loud has put the whole thing in his own hands, and about nine of them have worked out that what is in his hands is a sack he is not allowed to touch.**"

"It is in a groove."

"**It is in a groove and a groove is not a hole and a sack in the middle of a floor is not a sack on a rota, and there is no line on any page in this city that says a man on nothing may not stand in a room, and I have worked that out this morning while I was looking at a groove, and the working out took me about four minutes and it is the first thing I have worked out in this flood that anybody in this room could have worked out too.**"

He put his hand flat on the boards beside the groove and about four people in that back room watched his hand go down beside the sack and about nine of them watched the sack not go up.

---

And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall came up that road at about the tenth hour with a slate under his arm and stood in the doorway of that back room and looked at the groove and the heap the sweep had made and about four people in that room knew what he had come up that road for and about nine of them did not.

"You could put that against a day of the queue."

"**I could put that against a day of the queue and against a sack and against a place at a fire, and there is no figure at the end of any of those three sentences and there is a figure at the end of every sentence I have written in a column for about nine years, and I have got eleven entries in that column and the one that is paid is the thirteenth day of the new month, and there is no twelfth entry in it and there is not going to be one this morning.**"

"What would it say."

"**It would say a heap of something off a floor, and about four people in this city have worked out that that is one more thing in this flood that nobody has been able to price, and about nine of them have worked out that a cost is what a person says they are paying and a price is what a person says it is worth, and a woman sweeping a floor has not said she is paying anything, so there is nothing to write, and I have come up nine miles to stand in a doorway and not write anything and that is the whole of my day.**"

He put the slate back under his arm and about four people in that back room watched a man turn round in a doorway and about nine of them did not follow him out of it.

---

And a white crust in the bottom of a groove in a floor, broken into a heap about the length of a sack beside it.
