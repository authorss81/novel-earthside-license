# Chapter 0223 — Somebody Kept It

A man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close put a sack of salt down on the boards of a stall by a wharf in this city at about the seventh hour of the morning on the sixty-eighth day of the Long Month, and the boards were wet and the water underneath them was moving without any wind behind it. He carried it the four streets from the room he sleeps in on his good side and he set it down square to the edge of the boards with both hands, and the hand that will not close did the pushing and the hand that works did the lifting.

---

And he has had that sack for twelve days, and it came out of the settlement's chest on the fifty-sixth day of the Long Month in a yard in daylight in front of about nine people, and he was one of about a hundred and forty people it came out to, and he has not opened it. There is no seam undone on it and the knot on it is the knot it came out of the chest with.

"You want it opened."

"**No. I want to know whether it is still a wage and I am not going to find that out by opening it, and I have walked it four streets twice and I have not opened it, and about four people in this city have known for twelve days that I have not opened it and about nine of them have not known at all.**"

"Then what do you want."

"**I want somebody to say one sentence about a sack that has been kept for twelve days and has not been opened, and I am not going to tell you what the sentence has to say, because if I tell you what it has to say then you have said it and I have not, and that is a different thing and I have thought about the difference for twelve days and I have got nowhere.**"

---

And the woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall came out from behind it with a slate in her hand that she has carried for nine years and has never written on, and she did not touch the sack. She has been on a page since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month and it is twenty-three days, and about four people in this city know what that is and about nine of them do not, and she asked in advance not to be thanked and has not been.

"That is yours."

"**That is mine. I took it out of a chest in a yard nine miles up the road on the fifty-sixth day of that month and I have not opened it and I am not going to open it in front of anybody and I would like it entered that I did not come here to have it opened and I came here because I have nobody else to ask.**"

"You have four streets of people."

"**I have four streets of people and about nine people in this city can be fetched by a name and a word in about nine mouths does not stop, and I am not going to stand on a wharf and explain a sack to about nine people who did not walk nine miles for it. I came four streets.**"

She put both hands flat on the boards on either side of the sack and did not put them on the sack, and about four people who were not there would have called that a thing to say and nobody said anything.

---

And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came down those four streets at about the ninth hour with a board under his arm and stopped at about nine feet, and he has not put a sentence on a page since the fiftieth day of the Long Month and the posting on that wall nine miles up the road is twenty-two days old and has not made a wage.

"You have not opened it."

"**I have not opened it, and I have had a page up on a wall for twenty-two days with a price under every line and no figure under any of the prices, and I have not been able to say what any of them is worth, and this morning I have watched a man carry a wage four streets twice and not open it, and that is the only thing anybody in this market has done this week that I could have put a figure on, and I am not going to, and I would like it written down that I stood about nine feet away from it and could have.**"

"You could have put what on it."

"**Nothing. That is the honest answer and I have not got a better one and I have had twenty-two days to get one. A sack you have not opened is the only object in four hundred miles that nobody in this city has agreed what it is, and I have built a page about prices for three weeks and I have not got one line on it that means anything, and I am not going to fix that this morning by a man with a hand.**"

---

And a woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from that market came along the boards at about the ninth hour and looked at the sack and did not touch it either, and she has said more about the prices on that page than anybody in this city and she said four sentences about this and none of them was about the sack.

"My salt did this on the thirty-ninth day of that month."

"Nobody asked you."

"**Nobody asked me and I am going to say it anyway, because I have been keeping nine measures under my counter since the thirty-ninth day of that month and they are the same as his and I have not told a living soul and I am telling two people on a wharf at the ninth hour of a Sunday morning because I have been holding it since Friday.**"

"How long has yours been hard."

"**Since the thirty-ninth day of that month. And I paid a figure for those nine measures on the twelfth day of that month and I have not opened them either, and about nine people in this city cannot read and about nine of them come into my shop, and I have not been able to work out since the fifty-ninth day of the Long Month whether I have been keeping salt or a price.**"

"Which of those is it."

"**That is the question and I have been carrying it about four streets since Friday and I could not put it down until this morning, and I am not going to answer it on a wharf, and I am not going to answer it on a page either, because the answer is that a thing you pay a figure for and a thing you are given a bag of are not the same object and the only difference between them in this city is a knot.**"

He looked at the knot on his own sack and did not say anything. About four people in that market have said since that she told him the whole of it in four sentences and he did not thank her and she did not ask him to, and about nine of them have said that she told him and he went on carrying it the same four streets he had carried it before.

Nobody in that market said anything. A gull went over the wharf and the water under the boards moved on without any wind behind it, and about four people who were standing there had a shop four streets from a market and about nine of them had a stall that sells nothing that anybody in this city can carry away.

---

And the man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close put his sack back down on the boards at about the ninth hour and asked the one question he had come four streets for, and about nine people in that market heard it and about four of them answered.

"How many came out of that chest."

"**I do not know.**"

"You were there."

"**I was there and about a hundred and forty people were paid and I was one of them, and a man of forty-two opened the chest in the middle of a yard in daylight in front of about nine people because he could not hold it in his own hands. About four of the sacks in it had been opened and re-tied and the knots on those four were not the knots on the others, and he said he did not know that until the lid was off. Nobody counted them. Nobody was ever going to count them. A person is given a sack and not a figure of sacks, and about nine people in this market worked that out in about a minute and about four of us have not.**"

"Somebody could have been given two."

"**Somebody could have been given two, and somebody could have been given none and taken a share of somebody else's, and there is no line on that page for either and there is no line on any page in this city for either, and I have come four streets to find out a number and I am not going to get one, and I am not going to go back up that road and say that I came four streets for it.**"

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps that stall said one sentence after that and it was four words long and she has not said anything else about a sack since the forty-fifth day of the Long Month.

"Then you did not come for it."

"**No. I came for it.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen stood at the rail of that wharf at about the ninth hour with a plain slate under her arm that has her name at the top of it, and she is the only person in this market who is not on the front of any page in this city, and about four people in that market knew that and about nine of them did not, and nobody asked her anything at all for about nine minutes.** She read the top of it out under her breath and then the line under it and then she put the slate face down on the rail and left her hand on it, and about four people in that market watched her hand and about nine of them watched the sack.

---

And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked her, at about the tenth hour, whether the sack could be left on her boards until the morning, and she said no in one word and then said the rest of it standing behind her own counter with the slate still in her hand.

"No."

"**No. I am not going to be the place a thing is left. About nine people in this city have left things on other people's boards since the seventh week of this flood and about four of them have been told to take them away by somebody who did not want to be rude about it. I have been on a page for twenty-three days and I am not going to be a place a thing is left as well, because a stall that things are left on is a stall that is a page, and a page is the thing I said I was not going to be.**"

"I was not going to be the one to say that."

"**You were going to be the second one. I would have said yes to you and I have thought about it for about four seconds and I would have been able to do it, and that is worse, and I would like it entered that a woman of about thirty-four said no in one word and then gave four sentences and nobody improved on either.**"

---

And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall was in that market at about the tenth hour and he made a copy of the front of a page onto the back of a slate on a table in that same market on the forty-fifth day of the Long Month because he thought a page ought to be in two places, and he said no to a third one on the sixty-first day of that month, and he came down to the wharf on this Sunday with nothing in his hands for the first time since the forty-fourth day of the Long Month.

"You are not carrying anything."

"**I am not carrying anything. I have carried a slate down these four streets about nine times and I have said no to a third copy and about four people in this market have worked out that a man who says no to a third copy comes down to a wharf with nothing in his hands, and about nine of them have not worked it out, and I would like it entered that I came down here with nothing because I have run out of things to copy and not because of a sack.**"

"You could copy a sack."

"**I could copy a sack. I have thought about it on about four walks since the sixty-first day of the Long Month and I am not going to, and the reason is that a sack is a thing a person is given and a copy of a sack is a thing a person can be given again, and about nine people in this city cannot read and about four of them come into a shop four streets from here, and I am not going to build a second instrument for the same half of the city that already has one.**"

---

And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board was not in that market and had not come down the four streets, and about four people in this city have said since that he was on the upper road at the ninth hour of the morning with a bag against his chest and about nine of them have not worked out where he was.

---

And a sack went off a wet board and up four streets with its knot on, and nobody in four hundred miles knows how many came out of that chest.
