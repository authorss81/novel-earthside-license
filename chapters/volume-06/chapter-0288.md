# Chapter 0288 — A Person Is Sent

A man of about forty-seven was clearing a sill at a door on a lane off that road on the thirty-third day of the Short Month, a Tuesday, at about the ninth hour of the morning, and a carter of about thirty-five had a cart backed up to the end of the lane with a load of kindling in it and could not get any nearer, and the light came in low off the low ground and did not warm the stones of that lane, and the cold was in the sill and out of the wind and in the hands of about four people that morning, and what was in that lane was a chisel, a barrow, a cart that could not get past, and about four feet of drain silt at the near end that nobody had cleared for a week.

---

The man put the chisel down and did not wipe his hands, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who does not wipe his hands before he answers a question has decided that what he has been doing and what he is about to say are the same activity, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is what four days of notice is for, and that about four of them have said that the four days are not a courtesy for the household but a courtesy for the man who comes afterwards, and that about four people in four hundred miles have said since that this is the first time anybody in this flood has worked out that the four days are for the person carrying the question.

"You are the fourth of them that have been up this lane in four days."

"**I am the fourth of them that have been up this lane in four days, and I am the first one that has not come with a paper in his hand, and I have come with a paper in my hand and I am going to leave it in my coat.** The sheet came on the twenty-ninth, which was four days ago, and it has been on my own table since the twenty-ninth, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has had four days with a sheet on a table has had the time the four days are for, and a man who has not has not, and that nobody has ever worked out which of those two is better." He had not sat down and the sill was not finished. "About four people in that lane have said since that a man who says he is going to leave a paper in his coat in a lane where four men have come up it in four days is a man who has worked out that the paper is the reason the four of them came, and about four people in four hundred miles have said that the fourth one to arrive in a lane is never the reason anybody came, and that about four of them have said that the fourth one is the one who has to work that out for himself."

"I have come four times in four days with a sheet and every one of those four times I have handed it over and been answered in a mouth, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out by now that the asking and the answering are both happening in mouths and that the paper is only carrying the question from my hand to yours."

"**Then the asking and the answering are both happening in mouths, and the only thing on nine pieces of paper in four hundred miles is a question, and a question which travels on its own is the only document anybody has got this month, and it is going to stop travelling on the day the last household answers and not before.**"

---

He put the chisel down on the sill and turned it over twice and then he said yes in three words and then he said a day, and the day was the thirty-ninth, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has been asked a question four days in advance and has answered it with a day in it is a man who has used the four days the way the four days were for, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the first two answers came back in a hand and the third came back in a mouth, and that about four of them can say why and about four cannot.

"You have said yes and you have said a day, and I have not asked you which day and you have told me, and I am not going to ask you why."

"The thirty-ninth is a Monday and it is a market day, and I have said no to two things this month because they were not on a market day." He looked at the sill and then at the cart at the end of the lane. "**The thirty-ninth is also the ninth of nine. Your first room was the thirty-first, your second was the thirty-second, and this is the third, and that is nine kitchens on nine days, and a run is a shape, and a question which is asked on the third day of a nine is a question about a day and not about a household.**"

"Then your nine days are a schedule and I have been asked on a schedule."

"Then I have been asked on a schedule, and I have said yes on a schedule, and I have said the thirty-ninth because the thirty-ninth is a market day, and I am not going to be sorry for either." He picked the chisel up and looked at the edge of it and did not use it. "**The sheet in your coat and the sheet that came up the river in a cart are the same kind of thing with a different sentence at the top of them.** That is not the same as saying that they are the same thing, and about four people in four hundred miles are going to say that they are the same thing, and I am telling you now that they are not."

---

"**There are two gates between here and that house and both of them are shut at this hour, and I have never once asked anybody at either of them for anything, and a man who has never asked anybody at two gates is a man who has worked out that asking costs more than the gate.** About four people in four hundred miles have said that this is the only gate anybody has written a book about, and that the book has a keeper in it and no column for him."

"And the gates are shut and the man who keeps them is not going to open them for you."

"The gates are shut and nobody has asked them to be open, and if the man at a gate were to open one for a person with nothing in his hand, that would be the first thing anybody has ever done at that gate for a person, and about four people in that lane have said since that it is a thing that could be done and is not going to be done, and that about four of them have said that a road that people are let through when somebody is turned out of somewhere is not a road anybody keeps."

A round stone with a hole worn through one side of it lay in the sill of that door and the man of about forty-seven turned it over twice while he worked and put it back at the end of the sill where a stone belongs, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has a stone in his own sill has had it there long enough to have worn a hole in it, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is the only tally anybody on that road keeps that is not written down, and that about four of them have said that the days he keeps are in that stone and not in a book, and that about four people in that lane have said since that a man who keeps his days in a stone is a man who cannot be asked to produce them.

The carter of about thirty-five got a hand up onto the top rail of his own cart to lean over his load and left a thumbprint of tar on it where he put it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a carter who has to climb his own cart to see past his own load is a man whose cart is fuller than his cart was built for, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is a difficulty which arrives about four days before anybody thinks of it and does not go away afterwards.

"There is one on that road that nobody has been to, and there are eight that have been."

A place with eight houses in it is a place. **A house at the end of a lane past two gates is only a house, and the whole of this flood turns on the difference between those two words, and nobody has ever sat down and said it out loud.**"

"You are going to go and ask him."

"**I am going to go and ask him, and I am going without a paper, and a man going to a house with nothing at all in his hand cannot be refused, because there is nothing there to refuse.** A page can be refused and a person can only be turned out, and about four of them have said that being turned out is worse, and that this is why a person is the wrong instrument and is also the only one there is."

The carter of about thirty-five went on leaning over his own load and did not turn round, and about four people in that lane have said since that a carter who does not turn round at the word turned out is a man who has been turned out before and has decided that the second one costs less than the argument.

"You will be turned out."

"**I will be turned out, and I will come back with nothing, and a man who comes back with nothing has not wasted the day, because a page that comes back with nothing in it has not been anywhere.** About four people in four hundred miles have said that the difference between a person coming back with nothing and a page coming back with nothing is that one of them has been in a room and the other one has not, and about four of them have said that this is the first instrument anybody in this flood has built that can fail by being in a room."

And about four people in that lane have said since that they would rather have the instrument that can fail.

---

The man of about forty-seven said that he kept the days of a gate at the top of that lane and that there were not many of them and that they were not written anywhere, and that about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has said out loud on a Tuesday that his days are not written anywhere has said the only thing this week that anybody on that road could be held to, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the third place on the Crown's page wants somebody who can produce a figure, and that a man with a stone in his sill cannot produce a figure and does not pretend that he can, and that about four of them have said that this is the whole of why nobody in this city will put his name in that place.

The barrow stood in the middle of that lane with about four feet of silt in it that nobody had cleared since the twenty-third day of that month, and about four people in that lane have said since that a barrow standing in a lane with about nine days of drain in it is the only reason the cart cannot get to that door, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the things which stop a page arriving and the things which stop a person arriving are the same things, and that the only difference is that a person can be turned round at an obstruction and a page cannot, and that about four of them have said that this is why a cart is a good instrument and a barrow is a bad one.

The carter came down that lane at about the eleventh hour and put a thumbprint of tar on the sill of that door as he went past, and the man of about forty-seven did not wipe it off, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who does not wipe a stranger's tar off his own sill has decided that the stranger was in his lane and that is enough, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a sill is a document and that this is the second one this month that has been written on by somebody who was not asked to write on it.

The third of nine kitchens was asked on the thirty-third day of that month, a Tuesday, in daylight, with a date in chalk on a door, and the answer came back in a mouth and not in a hand, and the day in it was the thirty-ninth, which is the ninth of the nine, and nobody in that lane could say what was on the thirty-ninth.

And about four people in that lane have said since that a carter went out of it at about the eleventh hour with nothing in his hand toward a house at the end of a lane past two gates, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a man going to a door with nothing in his hand is the only version of this instrument that can be turned out of, and that about four of them have said that being turned out on the thirty-third is what a household looks like on the thirty-ninth, and that about four people in that lane have said since that nobody has worked out what happens on the thirty-ninth, and that this is the second time this month that the only thing anybody in four hundred miles has agreed about is a day that nobody can do anything about.
