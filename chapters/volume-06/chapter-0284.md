# Chapter 0284 — Two Thirds In The Bed

The drain at the north end of the market out past the last named house was four feet deep in silt on the twenty-ninth day of the Short Month, a Friday, and about two thirds of it had still been in the bed when the light went off the low ground on the twenty-third day of that month, and it was four feet deep on the twenty-third as well, and nobody has stood in the end of it since. The light that morning was white and flat off the low ground and did not warm the wet boards of that lane, the cold was in the wet boards and in the iron of a barrow that had stood out of the silt since the twenty-third day of that month, and the wind was off the reach and had been at the wall of a store nine miles up the road from this city since before anybody in that lane started keeping count of it, and what was under four pairs of hands from the seventh hour of the morning until the light went was a spade, one barrow, a boot, a bar lying on the near side of the road because it was nobody's week, and about four feet of drain that nobody had stood in for six days.

---

The man of about thirty-six came down that lane at about the seventh hour with a barrow and a spade and put the barrow down at the near end of it and emptied a bootful of water out of the barrow before he said anything at all, which is what he does, and a boy of about fifteen who lives at the north end turned the barrow over on its side to get at the silt under it and got his shoulder under the frame and lifted it and about four people in that lane have said since that a boy of fifteen who puts his shoulder under a barrow nobody asked him to lift has decided that the drain is his for about a minute and a half and is not going to be told so.

"There were nine of us on this on the Thursday and there have been four all week, and I am one of the four, and the other three came at about the fifth hour and not before, and two of them have a store to open and one of them has not said why."

"You have not worked out in six days which two." The man of about forty-nine who kept the store at the north end put his thumb on the edge of a wooden bowl that was standing on a board behind him and did not pick the bowl up. "**About four people in this lane have worked out the same thing and about nine of them have got it wrong, and not knowing which two of nine stopped coming is a different thing from knowing which two did, and I have not got either of them and I have got a bowl.**"

"You have not got a bowl, you have got the shape of one." He put the spade into the silt and it went in about nine inches and stopped, and he did not lean on it. "**There is a foot of this that I cannot get a spade into and I have been at it since the seventh hour, and about four people in this lane have worked out that the difference between nine men and four men is not four men. It is the nine feet of width a man can stand in.**"

---

The woman of about fifty-one came down that lane at about the eighth hour with her cart and could not get past the near end of the drain, and stood there for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then knotted the corner of her sacking and went back up the lane the way she had come, and about four people in that lane have said since that the first thing a cart of kindling cannot do in this flood is get past four feet of drain silt, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a woman who knots her sarking at the end of a lane has decided that a load which cannot be delivered is not a load anybody is going to pay for.

"Her cart cannot get through there and it could not get through there on the Friday either, and that is not what has happened this week." The man of about thirty-six took the spade out of the silt and set it down and wiped his boot on the boards. "**What has happened this week is that nine people who had turned up every day since the ninth day of that month stopped, and nobody sent anybody.**" He put the spade back in and it went in the same nine inches. "About four of the nine said they would be here, and about four said a thing that is not a no, and about four said nothing at all and shut a door, and about four people in this lane have worked out since that a man who cannot tell two reasons apart is a man who will spend the next four days being angry at the wrong nine people."

"Then you are not going to spend the next four days being angry at anybody, and I am not going to help you, because about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the reason a fifth which is written on nothing can be a fifth which is written on nothing the next time is that nobody wrote down the first one either."

---

The man of about forty-nine came out of his store with the bowl in his hand and put it on the boards at the near end of the drain and stood looking down into it, and the boy of about fifteen put the barrow down on its wheels and went to the far end of the drain to see whether the silt was deeper there, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who takes a bowl he has not got anything in out onto the boards in front of a drain is a man who wants to be told not to, and that about four of them have said that a bowl is the oldest promise anybody in this flood has and that a bowl with nothing in it is a promise withdrawn in front of witnesses.

"I am not going to put that out of your way and I am not going to take it back in, and both of those are decisions and I have made about four of them this week and not one of them is the decision anybody would call the decision." He turned the bowl a quarter turn on the boards and left it there. "**On the Thursday I put about four hundred coins in that bowl and it was four parts in five of what I had on the last paying day, and I said so out loud in this market in daylight.**" He put his thumb on the rim. "On the Friday the bowl did not come out at all and about eight of the nine did not turn up. Nobody sent anybody. Nobody shut anything."

"Nobody shut anything is the sentence I would like this whole lane to have."

"Nobody shut anything is the sentence, and I have said it in about four kitchens this week and nobody has written it down, and a drain that stops being worked is not a drain that anybody decided to stop working." He left the bowl on the boards and put his thumb on the rim of it again.

"**And here is the part I have not said out loud to anybody, and I am saying it to the drain and to you. The third of the four places on that Crown page wants a person's name in it and there is nobody to put there.**" He looked at the bar on the near side of the road. "A drain wants about nine pairs of hands and there are four. I have set those two things against each other all week and they are the same thing, and neither of them is anybody's fault."

---

"You have worked out that a drain and a page are the same thing and you have not told anybody."

"I have told about four people and three of them were in a kitchen and one of them was a woman with flour to her wrist who said out loud that a bar goes round and that writing it down makes a name on a page, and she said it on the twelfth day of that month and I have not forgotten it." The man of about thirty-six got the spade under the silt and turned it over and it did not come free. "**And I am not going to say that sentence in this lane, because that sentence belongs to a woman in a kitchen and not to me, and about four people in this market have worked out that a thing said once in a room belongs to the room and not to whoever was standing nearest to it.**"

The boy of about fifteen came back from the far end of the drain and said that it was deeper there and had a kind of a shelf in it about four feet down, and about four people in that lane have said since that a boy who reports a shelf in a drain is a boy who has been down into it, and that a shelf in a drain is the same thing as a bar round a village and neither of them is on a page, and that about four of them have said that a man with a spade and a boy with a shelf are about nine people short of what a drain wants and there is no page anywhere that can make up the difference.

---

The woman of about thirty at the stall came down that lane at about the ninth hour with the butt on her head and the yoke over her shoulder and put the butt down at the near end of the drain and did not take the yoke off, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who puts a butt of water down at the head of a drain and keeps the yoke on her shoulder has told everybody in that lane what she thinks of four feet of silt, and that about four of them have said that she has been the loudest person in that market for about a month and that on that Friday she was not loud at all.

"I will carry silt out of here at four buckets a load and I will not carry it out at nine, and I have said that out loud twice this week and nobody has written it down either, and a drain nine people clear in a day is a drain one man clears in about nine."

"You have said it out loud twice and about four people heard you and none of them came on the Sunday, and that is not a thing anybody in this lane can put a name to." The man of about thirty-six lifted a bootful out of the barrow and set it on the boards and did not throw it. "**There is about nine feet of this drain and there are four of us, and the four of us are four of the nine houses on that road, and on the thirty-first day of that month there is going to be somebody standing in nine kitchens with a date on the door, and about four of those nine kitchens belong to people who will be standing in this lane on that morning with a spade in their hands instead.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall had a second barrow standing at the near end of that lane with no wheel on it, and she had not brought it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman with a barrow that cannot be wheeled at the head of a drain has understood something about that drain that about nine men with a spade have not, and that what she has understood is that the silt has to go somewhere and not into a lane, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is the whole of a company that cannot be counted.

---

A sheet went out of the room over that market at about the seventh hour of that morning for a room on the thirty-third day of that month, which is four days off and not three, and about four people in that lane have said since that a piece of paper and four feet of silt went out of the same building up the same road inside about an hour of each other and that neither of them knew about the other, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the two of them are the whole of this week and that one of them is worth something and the other one is worth about four buckets a load.

Nobody filled the bowl in, and nobody filled it out, and nobody turned it out, and the bar stayed on the near side of the road because it was nobody's week, and the boy of about fifteen went home at about the tenth hour because his mother called him once and did not call him twice, and the man of about thirty-six turned the barrow over again and got his shoulder under it on his own, and about four people in that lane have said since that a barrow that goes over on a load that is too heavy is a barrow a man has put too much on because there is nobody in the room to tell him not to, and that about four of them have said that this is the fifth thing this week that has happened for want of one person in a room.

And the light went off that drain at the same time it went off everything else in that market, and the drain was still two thirds in the bed, and about four people in that lane have said since that this is the fifth day in a row that the drain has been unfinished, and that nobody in that lane has ever once been able to say which of the five was the day that decided it.
