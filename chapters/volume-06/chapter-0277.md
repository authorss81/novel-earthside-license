# Chapter 0277 — Nobody Turned Up

The market out past the last named house opened on the twenty-second day of the Short Month, a Friday, and the drain at the north end of it stood with the barrow out of it and the spade in the ground where it had been left on the Thursday, and people who had turned up for that drain on every day since the ninth day of that month did not turn up. The light was off the low ground and white and the cold was in the wet boards and out of the wind, and what was under four hands that morning at the north end of that market was a spade, a barrow, a bootful of silt, and a bar lying on the near side of the road because it was nobody's week. People in that town have said since that a market in which nine men have been clearing a drain for three days and eight of them do not turn up on the fourth is not a market that has run out of work, and that nine people had been turning up for a thing nobody had asked them to turn up for, and that eight of them had not and one had, and that nobody in that market knows which of the two facts came first.

---

The man of about thirty-six came down the lane from the gate at about the seventh hour with the bar on his shoulder and there was nobody at the drain, and he waited, and then he took his coat off, and people up that lane have said since that a man who arrives at a drain at the seventh hour, waits, and then takes his coat off, has not decided anything in the last half hour and has been waiting for four of the nine all the same.

"They will be along."

"They will not be along and I have not told anybody anything and nobody has told me anything, and I have been standing at this drain since the seventh hour and four of the nine were here at this hour on the last three days and they are not here." He emptied a bootful of silt into the barrow before he said anything else. "**The drain is about nine feet long and about four feet deep in silt at the north end and nine people clear it in about a day, and it has been three days getting cleared, and one man and one barrow is about nine days of the same, and that is not a fifth of anything.**"

"Who put the bar on the near side."

"I did. I put it there at the eleventh hour yesterday and it is not my week and I have not asked and I am not going to, and nine houses up that lane know whose week that is and four of them came past this morning, looked at it, and went on." He set the spade down. "**You are going to tell me that is not why they are not here. I know that it is not why. I want it said out loud that I know it is not why and I have not got a better one.**"

---

The man of about twenty-six came into that market at about the ninth hour with a piece of string round his left wrist with a knot in it that nobody has ever been able to undo for him, and he had been at that drain every day since the twelfth day of that month and had not asked for anything once in all that time, and people in that town have said since that a man who turns up at a drain he is not paid for, is not a household, and has a name on a wall with no line under the name, is the only person out past the last named house who has nothing to lose by that drain standing open.

"I have been to four of the nine."

"You have been to four of the nine at the ninth hour of a Friday morning and they are not there, and what did they say to you."

"They said they would be at the drain, and they are not at the drain." He turned the string a quarter turn on his wrist. "**Nobody has said anything to me about the bowl and I have not asked and I am not going to. I have been in four kitchens this week and the bowl was talked about in three of them, and in all three it was the fifth and not the Hearthguard, and a thing that is in three kitchens is not a rumour. It is a Thursday.**"

The drain went on without either of them. It had been going on since the seventh hour and had a fifth of a day's silt out of it, and it was making the noise a drain makes when a man is in it, and about four people in that market have said since that a drain in work makes a room into a place where two people can stand and say four things in a row and be right about all of them, and that about nine people in that town have said the opposite, and have been saying it for about four days, and that about four of those four days are the four this month.

"You read the wall."

"I read the wall at the eighth hour and my name is on the ninth line and there is no line under my name, and about four people in that room said on the twelfth day of that month that there is no line for me, and I have never once asked anybody to make one, and I have been standing in a market at the ninth hour of a Friday and there is nobody at a drain." He put his boot on the lip of the bed. "**A guest can ask to stop being a guest. I asked, in that room, in nine words, and nobody asked me why and nobody is going to. A person cannot ask to stop being a person, and that is the only advantage anybody out here has got, and I have not found a use for it yet.**" He put his boot back on the lip. "Four people in that market have worked out since that the man standing in a drain on a Friday morning with nothing to lose is the reason a drain would eventually get cleared, and nobody has told him so, and four of them have told a barrow."

---

He went out of the market at about the half hour and came back at about the eleven hour, and he had been to four doors and had spoken at three of them, and people in that market have said since that a man who goes to four doors on a Friday morning to find out whether people are coming to a drain is doing a thing a Hearthguard would call a list, and that he had not written anything down and had asked nobody anything except whether they were coming to the drain, and that about four of the nine said no to a man at a door about a drain and about four of the nine had not opened at all.

"They said no."

"**They did not say no. That is the thing I want put down before anybody in this market makes it into something.** Four of them said they would be at the drain, and four of them said a thing that is not a no, and four of them said nothing at all and shut a door, and I have been in four kitchens this week and I know what a no sounds like in this flood, because I have heard a woman read four places out loud in one of them, and none of the four said it to me." He was wet to the knee and he stood the spade back in the ground. "I am going to say the next part and I would like one person in this market to hear it. About four of the nine are not at the drain because they have worked out that a fifth that is written on nothing is a fifth that can be nothing on Thursday, and about four of the nine are not at the drain for about nine reasons that have got nothing to do with the bowl, and I cannot tell the two apart, and people in this town have said that a man who cannot tell two reasons apart is a man who will spend the next four days being angry at the wrong nine people, and I am not going to be that man."

"You have not been angry."

"**I have been angry since about the seventh hour this morning and I have not shown it to anybody, and I have shown it to a drain, and the drain has taken about four feet of it and about nine days of the rest of it.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall came down from the market with a butt on her head and a needle in her hand and had a pail in each hand by the tenth hour, and she put the pails in the bed of that drain and did not say that she had carried them, and people in that market have said since that a woman who puts two pails in a dry drain bed at the tenth hour on a Friday is a woman who has decided that a drain is a thing that can be watered, and that four of the nine who were paid a fifth less the day before would have done the same and four of them are at home.

"You have not asked me why they are not here either."

"**I have not and I am not going to and I have a butt on my head and a pail in each hand, and I am not going to stand in a drain and be the woman who asks that.**" She went along the bed with the water and did not look at either of them. "I got nine houses to run a pail line on the tenth day of that month and I did it on my own, and about nine people have said since that I made the line myself, and I took it back in a kitchen the next day and it was not mine, and I have not said it since and I am not going to say it here. A line that runs itself is a line that runs itself, and a line that runs itself stops, and people in this lane have known that since the Sunday and about nine of us have not said it."

"You are saying it now."

"**I am saying it now, and I have said four other things this week that I have not said before, and I would like it entered that I am not saying it to you and I am saying it to a drain.**" She emptied the first pail into the bed and the silt drank it in about nine inches. "Four of the nine counted that bowl twice on Thursday and four of the nine did not, and I do not know which four counted and I am not going to find out, and I am telling you that a man who has worked out that the fifth he was given on Thursday is written down nowhere can work the same thing out about the next Thursday, and that is all that happened in this market overnight, and nobody did it on purpose and everybody did it."

---

The man of about forty-nine did not come into that market on the Friday, and people in that town have said since that a man who has kept a store at the north end of that market for eleven years and has not opened on a Friday has not been ill and has not been sent for, and has sat in his own shop with the door shut and the bowl on the counter, and worked it out in about an hour, and did not write to anybody and did not send anybody and did not go to four houses, and that a man who can put about four hundred coins on a table for nine people out of his own pocket cannot make it a bigger figure on the Friday by wanting to, because there is no page on which the wanting goes and no day on which it could be said to be owed, and about nine people in that town worked that out at about the same hour he did, and four of them came into the market anyway, and four of them did not leave the house.

The rest of that market went on. The stalls opened and a woman of about thirty-three at a bread board at the south end sold four loaves and did not sell two, and the woman of about thirty at the stall mended a net from the ninth hour to about the third hour of the afternoon without once putting it down, and about four people who have been in that market have said since that the only thing anybody did all day was not do anything, and that about four people in four hundred miles have made that observation about a market on about nine days this flood, and that none of the nine were in this one.

The bar was still on the near side of the road at the second hour of the afternoon, and the man of about thirty-six had been in that bed since the seventh hour and the man of about twenty-six had been in and out of it since the ninth, and people in that town have said since that a drain nine people clear in a day is a drain one man clears in about nine, and that nobody in that market said it on the Friday, and that about nine people in that town could have worked it out inside a minute, and that it was said on the Saturday by a woman at a stall to four people standing in a lane, and that it was the first time anybody out past the last named house had said a figure out loud about a favour, and that nobody in that town turned it into a wage, because a figure about a drain is not a wage and a wage is not a figure about a drain, and that the ninth day of the Crown's page found out more about nine people out past the last named house than the Crown will get in a quarter, and that it found it out by them not turning up, and that nobody in that town on that Friday thought of it in those words, and that a woman at a stall said four things out loud in one morning that she had never said before, and that none of the four was about the Crown.
