# Chapter 0285 — The Yoke Against The Door

A woman of about thirty lay on a bench in a house at the top of a lane of nine houses nine hundred paces past a market town on the thirtieth day of the Short Month, a Saturday, with her right arm across her chest and a needle in her left hand and a net in her lap, and the yoke she had carried since the tenth day of that month stood against the inside of her own door where she had put it that morning and had not picked it up since. The light came in at one window about three feet off the floor and there was nowhere in that house to stand out of it, and the cold was in the boards and in the iron of a butt that stood at the head of the lane, and what was under about three pairs of hands in that house from the seventh hour until the second hour of the afternoon was a needle, a net, a butt, two pails on a yoke, and a channel at the low end of that lane that had a foot in it and a hole in the bed of it that has been in the bed for about three weeks.

---

"I have got the use of the arm back and the shoulder has not come back with it, and I am telling you at the seventh hour on a Saturday morning because if I wait until the second hour of the afternoon I will have been carrying something and it will not be a fact."

"You are not going to be able to carry a butt for about a month and you know that as well as I do, and about four people in this lane have worked out that a woman who tells you the month before she has to say it out loud is a woman who has already worked it out on her own and has decided that being told first is worse." The man of about forty-three of the fourth house had his left hand flat on the boards and was not lifting anything with it. "**I have had one arm back for a fortnight and I can put a thing on a shelf and I cannot lift about four buckets, and we are two people in one lane who cannot do the thing the lane is built out of, and neither of us is going to be the one who says that out loud first.**"

"I have said it out loud first. That is the whole of what I have been doing for seven days and I would like it noted that I got there first and that nobody thanked me for it."

Nobody thanked her for it, and the needle went into the net and came out and the net went on getting bigger by about a finger's width every hour, and a woman mending a net with the wrong arm and saying out loud that she got there first is a woman who has decided that being first at anything is the only thing left to be.

---

The boy of about eleven came in at about the eighth hour with the yoke on his shoulder and two pails on it and stood them down against the outside of that door and came past the bench without saying anything, which was what he had done on every morning since the tenth day of that month, and about four people in that lane have said since that a boy of eleven who comes past a bench with two pails on a yoke and says nothing is a boy who has worked out that a house that cannot carry does not need to be asked to.

"He is doing it because there is nobody in this house who can."

"He is doing it because there has not been anybody in this house who can since the twenty-third day of that month, and a week is long enough for about nine houses to find out that they do not have to ask." She turned the net over and did not look at the door. "**And I am not going to take it back off him. I have thought about it for two days and I have decided not to, and the second fastest way for a thing to happen in a nine-house village is for the person it used to belong to be too tired to take it back.**" She put the needle down flat again. "About four people in that lane have worked out that the fastest way for a thing to happen in a nine-house village is for it to be nobody at all."

"You have not been asked to take it back."

"Nobody has asked me anything about it and I have answered it, and that is the arrangement in this lane and it has been the arrangement for about a fortnight." She put the needle down flat on the boards and left it there. "**The line runs without me and it runs better without me, and a thing that runs better without a person is not a thing that person is going to get back by asking for it.** I have known that since Wednesday and I have not said it out loud until now, and about four people in this house have heard me say it."

---

She got up off the bench at about the tenth hour with the net in her left hand and stood in the middle of that floor, and she got the yoke up off the boards with her left hand and could not get it over her right shoulder, and she put it down again, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who tries a yoke once and puts it down without trying it twice has decided in the space of about a minute and a half what a month of Saturdays is going to be like, and that the minute and a half is the whole of a recovery and that nobody was thanked for it.

"I got it up to the shoulder and I could not get it over, and I am not going to try that again today, and I want it said in this house that I tried it once."

"You tried it once and you came down with it, and that is the whole of what a Saturday is." The man of about forty-three put his own left hand flat on the boards and turned it over and looked at the back of it. "**I have been in this lane about a fortnight with one hand that will lift about four buckets and one that will lift nothing, and I have got exactly the same result as you and about nine days ahead of you, and neither of us has said that out loud.** Two people in that lane who have each lost one arm, he said, are the last two anybody is going to ask about, because there is nobody left in the lane who can carry."

The net went on getting bigger by about a finger's width a row and the boy of about eleven came back up the lane at about the eleventh hour with the yoke and the two pails and stopped outside and did not come in and went on up to the butt, and about four people in that house have said since that a boy who has started rapping at a door before he comes into a house is a boy who has decided that a house with a woman on a bench in it is a house you ask, and that he does not rap at any of the other eight doors and that this is the only one of the nine he raps at.

"He is the only one who raps."

"**He is the only one who raps because this is the only house he has ever wanted anything out of.**" A boy of eleven who has worked out which of nine houses is worth rapping at has worked out something about that lane that about four of the adults in it have not. The needle went into the net and came out, and the two pails standing on the boards outside that door were about an inch apart with their handles turned the same way, which is a thing a person does when she has put a thing down and is not thinking about it."

---

The woman of about forty-six came across that lane at about the ninth hour with the sieve in her hands and stopped outside that door and did not come in, and a woman who has carried a sieve every morning for a fortnight and who stops outside a door and does not come in is a woman who has worked out that a house with a woman on a bench in it is a house you come past and not a house you go into, and this is the fifth time she has done it, and about four people in that lane can name what it was about and about four cannot. The light went off the boards about an hour before it went off the stones at the head of that lane, and the net was on her lap with the needle still in it, and the two pails against the outside of that door were standing where the boy had left them with the handles the same way round, and the yoke was still against the inside of that door, and the channel at the low end had about a foot of silt in it and had had a foot in it since the tenth day of that month.

"I have the sieve for an hour if you want it." She said it through the door and not into the room.

"I have a net and about nine hours and neither of them is any use to you and both of them are mine, and you have not come in for a fortnight and I am not going to ask you why, and about four people in this lane have said since that a woman who does not ask another woman why she has stopped coming has understood that the reason is that the other woman has decided what her coming means."

The man of about forty-three got up off the bench and went and stood at the door where the yoke was and did not pick it up, and a man who goes and stands next to a thing a woman has put down and does not pick it up is a man who has worked out that a yoke in a doorway is not a thing anybody in that house is going to be given, and that this lane is at the stage where a thing gets put down and nobody picks it up and out loud.

"You are not going to be thanked for the two days either."

"I am not going to be thanked and I was not going to be thanked, and I was not going to be given the yoke, and I am not going to be given the yoke on a Sunday either when the boy is off." She picked the needle up and went back into the net. "**There is a hole in the bed of that channel about four feet deep that a man went into on the tenth day of that month and came out of, and it has been in the bed for about three weeks.** The water that comes down the pail line is about five buckets to a house instead of about nine, and none of that is anybody's fault and all of it is mine now, because I am the one who cannot do it."

"You said on the Friday that you would not carry at nine and you have been carrying at four ever since and nobody has said that was a favour."

"**Nobody has said it was a favour and nobody is going to, and a thing nobody has called a favour is not a favour, it is a thing somebody is doing, and that is the whole of the difference and it took a fortnight to find out.**" The boy of about eleven came back up the lane with the yoke on his shoulder and the two pails on it empty and went past that door and down to the butt at the end and filled them and came back and stood them against the wall outside, and about four people in that house have said since that a boy who fills his own pails and stands them against the outside of a door is a boy who has decided that a door is a thing a house can be reached at, and that a house is not a building on that count.

---

The two of them had not spoken about the drain at all that morning, and about four people in that house have said since that a Saturday on which two people in a lane do not mention a drain that four of them stood in on the Friday is a Saturday on which both of them have decided the same thing about it and neither of them has said it, and that the whole difference between a favour and a wage is whether two people in a room are allowed to be angry at each other about it, and that nobody in that lane is.

The net was bigger by about a hand's width by the time the light had gone off the floor, and the yoke was still against the inside of that door and had not been moved, and the butt was standing at the head of the lane with about two buckets in it, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who has spent a whole Saturday in a chair mending a net with the wrong arm and has not once asked anybody to fetch the water is a woman who has got to the end of something, and that the end of it is not a decision and is not a refusal and is not on anybody's page, and that the arm will be all right and the shoulder will not, and that neither of those is going to be written down anywhere by anybody, and that the yoke was still against the inside of that door at the end of the day, and a yoke which has stood against a door through one whole Saturday has stopped being a thing anybody in that house is carrying and has become a thing that house has, and that this is the only way anything in this flood has ever been given away.
