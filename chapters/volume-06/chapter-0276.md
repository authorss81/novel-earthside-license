# Chapter 0276 — Less Of It

The market out past the last named house opened an hour late on the twenty-first day of the Short Month, a Thursday, and the reason it opened late was that a man of about forty-nine who kept a store at the north end of it had to count what he had before he gave any of it away, and he counted for about half an hour and came up with a smaller number than the one he had counted on the last paying day. The light was off the low ground and white and did not warm the boards, and the cold was in the iron of the barrow handles and out of the wind, and what was under about fourteen hands that morning was a barrow, a scale with a stone in the end of the beam, a bar stood on the near side of the road outside, and a wooden bowl with about four hundred coins in it. Nobody in that market was told that a Hearthguard had refused anything, and people in that town already knew, and about four of the nine were standing in the market waiting for the bowl to be put down.

---

The drain at the north end had gone over on the Tuesday with about four feet of silt in it and the barrow had been going in and out of it since, and the market had opened at the sixth hour every day of that week with a wheel track across the boards, and on the Thursday it opened at the seventh hour instead, and about four people in that town have said since that the first cost anybody could see of a Hearthguard's refusal was a market in this one that opened late, and that the reason it opened late had nothing to do with a Hearthguard and everything to do with about four hundred coins in a bowl.

He put the bowl down on the boards and did not take it up again, and about four people who have been in that market since the seventh hour have said since that a man who puts a bowl of coins down and leaves his hands off it has decided the number and is not going to be argued out of it by anybody in this market.

"It is the same number of people as the last paying day."

"**It is the same nine and it is the same of every one of the nine, and I have had a store at the north end of this market for eleven years and I have never once paid a smaller day, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to you that I know why, because I do not know why and I have not asked anybody why and I am not going to.**" He put one thumb on the edge of the bowl and did not move it. "**It is four parts in five of what it was and it is the same nine, and four parts in five is not a mistake, and a man who is a fifth short ought to say so in a market and not go round it.**"

The man of about thirty-six came down the lane from the gate at about the ninth hour with mud to the knee and had not been near his own work first, and people in that market have said since that a man who comes out of a gate with mud to the knee and goes to a wage before his own work has decided that a wage is a thing that happens to people and his own work is a thing that happens to him, and that about nine people in that market have watched him do that four days running this week.

"You are a fifth short."

"I am a fifth short and I will be a fifth short on the next paying day and the one after, and I am a fifth short because about four carts went through that gate last week and about two went through it the week before, and a man with nine sacks and two mules stood in the middle of a thing being explained to him eleven days ago and told me his difficulties and said the road was not one of them, and he was right about that, and he has not been through since and I have not sent anybody to ask him why."

"Nobody shut the road."

"Nobody shut the road. That is the thing I want said in this market and I am not going to say it on anything that gets kept." He got his hand out of the bowl and wiped it on his coat. "A Hearthguard of forty-four stood up in a room over this market yesterday morning with a date in chalk on the door and said no to a road, and he said it on behalf of a body of four men, and he was right. The day before that a man stood in that same room and told about nine people that a body cannot be asked a question. **Neither of those two men shut anything and neither of them is the reason, and about four people in this market have worked that out in a morning and about nine of them have worked it out wrong.**"

---

A cart came through that gate at about the tenth hour going the other way and did not stop, and the man of about thirty-six walked out to the chain and stood by it while it went and came back in, and people in that market have said since that a man who walks out to a gate to watch a cart go through without stopping has just been shown the arithmetic of his own market by a wheel, and that about nine people in that town have been shown it that week and about four of them had not worked it out.

"It did not stop."

"It did not stop and it did not slow, and there were two of those on Tuesday and there was one on Sunday, and about a month ago there were four a day and some of them stopped and had a drink and put something in a bowl." He came back in and stood at the end of the boards with his hands behind him. "**I am not telling you that man is wrong. I am telling you that a bar on a road that nobody is using is a bar that is not keeping anything, and that a bar that is not keeping anything is a bar a person could put a hand on, and about four people in this market have said that sentence out loud this week and about nine of them have not.**"

"And you put one on the near side at the eleventh hour."

"I put one on the near side at the eleventh hour and about nine houses up that lane know whose week that is and it is not mine, and I have not asked, and I am not going to." He shifted his weight. "**About four of us have been saying the same sentence about a rack since the Sunday before last and none of us heard it off anybody, and I have stopped waiting to find out where it came from, because people in this market worked out last week that the sentence was never anybody's. It is just what a rack does.**"

A woman of about thirty at the stall had her needle and a net in her hands and had put a cup of water down at the north end of that market for the man of about forty-nine without saying a word to him, and about four people in that market have said since that a stallholder in a market where nothing is sold for money has a different idea of what a stranger is for, and that he drank about half of it and set the cup down about nine inches from where she had put it, and that about four people in that market have said that the nine inches is a thing that has happened in that market about nine times in the last month and that nobody has ever once asked either of the two about it.

"The season is gone and nobody in this market has said so."

"About four people in this market have worked out that the season is gone and not one of us has said it in a market, and I have said it in my own kitchen twice this week and once in a lane, and I am not going to say it here. **About four of the nine who are getting four parts in five of what they had on the last paying day are standing in this market and I am not going to be the one who says a thing like that in front of a bowl of coins.**" She drew the needle through and put it back without looking at the eye of it. "**A season is a season and it is not a figure of days, and there is nobody in this town going to stand at the end of this week and turn eleven weeks into a number so that they can say what it came to, and about four people in four hundred miles have got that number and about four of the four are in this market and none of us has said it out loud.**"

"You have all of you got it."

"About four of us have and the other five have not, and that is how a number of that kind works. It comes out of one mouth and it goes along a road, and by the time it has been said to nine people it is not a number any more, it is a thing a person is frightened of, and the whole of the difference between those two things is about four hundred yards of market." She put the needle down in the net and did not pick it up again. "**I am not going to be one of the four who says a number out loud in a market so that people in four hundred miles get frightened by it, and I said that in a lane on the fourteenth day of that month and I have not moved it and I am not going to be moved about it by a bowl.**"

The man of about forty-nine counted the bowl again in front of about four people and got the same number the second time, and about four people who have watched him do it have said since that a man who counts a bowl of coins twice in a market because he does not like the answer is a man who is going to pay the same smaller number next week and who would much rather be found out now, and about nine people in that town found out on the Thursday.

---

A woman of about thirty-three who kept a bread board at the south end of that market came across the boards with four loaves under one arm and stopped where the bowl was and asked the man of about forty-nine the question that people in that town had been asking each other since the seventh hour, which was whether the number was right, and he said it was, and she said that four of the nine who had been paid less were eating less and that she had worked it out that morning out of what she had sold on the last paying day and not out of what anybody had told her, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who works out a wage out of her own bread board without being told anything is a woman who has a number nobody gave her, and that her number and the bowl were the same.

The woman of about thirty-three took her four loaves back to the south end and did not sell two of them, and about four people in that market have said since that a bread board in a market is the first instrument anybody out past the last named house has ever had for noticing a wage, and that nobody has ever asked it to do that and it did it on a Thursday with a barrow and a bowl and nothing else.

The man of about thirty-six went out of that market at about the eleventh hour with a bar over his shoulder and stood it on the near side of the road and came back in and sat down on the boards by the drain and did not say where he had been, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts a bar on the near side of a road at the eleventh hour on a Thursday has taken a turn that nobody gave him, and that houses in the second village up that lane know whose week that is and that it is not his, and that about four of the nine had already worked that out and had said nothing to anybody.

The bowl went out at about the second hour of the afternoon. It was the same nine and it was four parts in five of the last one, and nobody said a word about it, and about four people in that market town have said since that a market in which nine people are paid about a fifth less than they were paid the last time and nobody says anything is a market in which about nine people have all decided the same thing privately on about four separate days this month, and that the thing is not anger, and that it is the first thing anybody out past the last named house has felt about the Crown's page, and that nobody has put a name on it anywhere, and that about four of the nine counted it and put it away and about four of the nine counted it twice, and that those are the same act done by nine people, and that nobody in that market said so, and that about four people in that town have worked out since that the fastest way for a thing to be noticed in nine houses is a bowl and a barrow and a Thursday morning, and that about nine of them have already worked that out and about four have said it.
