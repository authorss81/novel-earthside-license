# Chapter 0264 — Everybody Here Already Knows

A cart came down the road past this city on the ninth day of the Short Month with a man of forty-one in a very good coat on the boards of it, and the road out past the last named house is nine miles of gates before it and a market town at the end of it, and the day was a Saturday and the market was on. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures standing on it are those of that sixty-ninth day and are forty days old, and it has stood unwritten thirty-nine. The wind has been at that wall for thirty-five days. A case from nine hundred miles away has been shut on a table in a room over a market for thirteen days now and nobody has carried it up that road and nobody has been asked whether they would.

---

The company in that town is eleven people and a hearth and a bench, and it is a company of that place and keeps that place, and there is no other word for it in use on that road, and about four people in this city have said since that a company in this world is a company that keeps a place and that the place is the whole of the name.

They were clearing a drain at the north end of the market when the cart came in, with eleven of them and two barrows and about nine children who were not part of it and were not kept out of it either, and the light was flat off the low ground, the cold had got into the barrow handles by the fourth hour of the afternoon, and what was under about twenty-two hands that afternoon was a barrow handle, a spade and about four feet of drain silt.

"You have come out on a cart."

"**I have come out on a salt cart, because a cart was going and I am not a person who pays for carts, and I have not been in this town before and nobody in it has been asked to do anything.**" He got down off the boards and put his hand inside his coat and left it there for about as long as it takes to fill a pail. "There is a page in a room over a market that wants the days upon every gate between this city and the gates of the west road, and I have read five sheets of it, and I have not opened one of them in this town and I am not going to."

"You are going to ask us which gates there are."

"I am going to ask who keeps them, and about four people in this town are going to have told me inside a minute, and I would like to have asked first whether I am allowed to ask."

A man of about thirty-six of that town came out of the drain with a spade in one hand and a bootful in the other and put the bootful in the barrow before he answered, and about nine people on that street have said since that a man who empties his boot before he speaks to a stranger has decided what order he is going to do things in.

"We keep them. Everybody keeps them."

"Everybody keeps them in the sense that there is a bar and somebody has it."

"**There is a bar and somebody has it, and it goes round, and whose week it is is not a thing anybody writes down here, and it is not a thing anybody needs written down, because there are about nine houses on this stretch and every one of them knows whose week it is without asking.**" He leaned the spade against the barrow and wiped the back of his hand across his mouth and about four people on that street have said since that a man wiping his mouth with the back of his hand in front of a stranger from down the road is a man who has stopped being careful what he looks like.

"You know it without asking."

He was already walking out of the drain and up onto the boards of the market when he said that, and he walked about nine paces and stopped, and about four people on that street have said since that a man asked a question he already knew the answer to is a man testing a question.

"I know it without asking because I can walk from this drain to the north gate in about four minutes, and if it is my week then the bar is on the near side of the road in a stack under a lean-to, and if it is not my week then the bar is on the far side in the same stack, and that is the whole of how anybody here knows anything about it."

He put the spade down and stood on the head of it, and a woman of about thirty at a stall on the north side of that market put a cup of water down on the end of the drain without saying anything to anybody, and about four people on that street have said since that the stallholder in a market where nothing is sold for money has a different idea of what a stranger is for, and that she put it down for the man in the good coat and he did not drink it.

"**You want it written down. I am telling you what that costs. A bar on one side of a road instead of the other is a duty, and a duty that is written down is a rota, and a rota is a page with a name on it, and there are about nine people in this town who have never once been on a page.**" He picked the spade up off the ground and turned it over and set it down again. "They are not going to be on one, because a man in a good coat came off a salt cart, and I am telling you the cost now so that you can go back and tell somebody that I told you."

"About four of the eleven who are out in that drain have heard every word you have just said."

"About four of the eleven have heard it and about nine of the town have, and there is nobody in this market who is going to go home and ask their neighbour whose week it is, because everybody already knows, and that is the whole of the difference between this market and the room you came out of."

---

The town has no gate of its own and has never had one. Past it there are two villages. The first of the two has a company of its own that is four people. The second of the two has nobody at all, and it is nine hundred paces further on, and it has nine houses and a channel that takes its water from a gate in the road.

The man of about thirty-six walked out to the second village with the man in the good coat and took four of the company's eleven with him and sent the other seven back down the road to the town, and about nine people on that road have said since that he took four and not the eleven and did not say why, and that the four he took were the four whose weeks were not coming round that day.

They went past nine houses and about four of the nine had a door open and about four did not, and the first three doors out of that village were on the same side of the road and the fourth was not, and about nine people who have walked that village have said since that the fourth house is the reason a stranger walking down that road comes back having counted the houses on the wrong side.

The channel is dry. It has been dry for four weeks and the bed of it is cracked into plates about the size of a hand, and the rack that stands in it is standing in a bed with no water in it, and about four people in that village have said since that a rack in a dry channel is the only thing in the world that everybody can see and nobody can do anything about.

"Where does the water come from."

"**It comes through the gate in the road. That gate is on the road and it is not ours, and it is opened when the road opens it, and the road has not opened it since before the weather turned in the first week, and the post the bar works on has been out since the second week and nobody has put it back.**" He crouched and put a hand flat on the plates of the bed and lifted it and the dust on the underside of his hand was the colour of the dust on the road. "We do not have a company. We have nine houses and a bar that goes round and a channel that fills when the road lets it. **I want that in whatever it is you are not opening, because I have never been able to say it to anybody and I have wanted to for about four weeks.**"

"You have wanted to say it."

The man in the good coat had a hand out of his coat by then and was holding the flap of it open, and about four people in that village have said since that a man in a good coat on a dry channel bed is a man who is aware of what he is standing next to, and that the town had already told him what it costs to be told.

"I have wanted to say it to about four people and I have said it to none of them, because the only people I could say it to are the ones who would go and ask the road, and the moment anybody asks the road the road is a thing that gets asked, and I have seen what a thing that gets asked does to people." He stood up and put the heel of his hand against one eye for a second, which is what a person does when the wind off a dry channel bed has got into them. "**You have asked me two questions and both of them have been about who, and not one has been about what, and I have been on this road about a hundred days and no stranger from down there has ever asked me what anything costs.**"

"I am going to ask you what it costs."

He put the spade under his arm and looked at the bed of the channel for about as long as it takes to count nine things, and one of the four of the company that had come out with them began, without being asked, to turn over the plates at the edge of it with the toe of a boot, which is what a person does at a dry channel bed who has been turning over plates at the edge of it for four weeks.

"It costs a post and about nine days of somebody's hands and a channel bed dug out by hand, because there is nothing in this village to put a barrow in and there is a barrow in the town that belongs to eleven people." He nodded at the toe of the boot going over the plates. "**It costs the fourth household their rack for that week, because the fourth household is the one with the rack at the low end, and it costs about four days of a whole household's water carrying by hand, and it costs nothing at all that anybody in four hundred miles can put in a column.**"

"Go on."

"And that is the last of it, and I have said the whole of it, and about four people who have walked this road since the first month have said that it is the first complete answer anybody has given to anything coming out of that market." He shifted the spade to his other hand. "That is why you will not be able to make anybody here sign anything about it. You have got a thing that costs and the thing that costs is not a number of days that a person can be given, and a person who cannot be given it cannot be made to answer for it, and that is not a trick of ours. It is just what a gate is."

---

The man of forty-one in a very good coat stood at that dry channel with his hand inside his coat and did not take it out for about a minute, and about four people on that road who were not of the town have said since that a man in a very good coat out past the last named house is a man nobody can check, and that he said so himself in a room over a market on the second day of the Short Month, and that this is the second week that has cost somebody something.

He did not open one of the five sheets. He asked the man of about thirty-six three more questions and got three answers that were the same as the first three, and then he said that four were enough, and he thanked nobody, and he was gone down the road by about the seventh hour with about four of the eleven walking him as far as the first gate and no further.

About four people in this city have worked out since then that a thing everybody on that road knows and nobody on that road has ever written down cannot be given to a clerk, and that a clerk nine hundred miles away is not going to be given it either, and that the whole of the Crown's difficulty in a market out past the last named house is a difficulty of handwriting.

Nobody out there was asked for a list. Nobody out there has been asked whether a day suited them, and nobody out there has heard of a case on a table, and the market opened on the ninth day of the Short Month and closed at about the ninth hour of the evening with a drain cleared at the north end, a channel dry at the second village, and about nine names in this city that nobody out past the last named house has ever heard.
