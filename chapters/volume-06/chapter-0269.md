# Chapter 0269 — The First Way, On The Ground

A man of forty-one in a very good coat walked the road west on the fourteenth day of the Short Month, a Thursday, with about eleven people out of three villages, and it was the morning after a Crown schedule with four places ruled on it had been read out in four kitchens in a market town, and nobody had asked one of the eleven whether the day suited him. Forty days is how long the wind has been at the wall of the store on the upper road, and it has been there since the two hundred and twenty-ninth day of this flood. The board over that door shows the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month, forty-five days old, and has been left alone for the forty-four days since the day after them. In this city a case from nine hundred miles away has stood shut on a table for eighteen days, counted from the ninety-sixth day of the Long Month, and it has not gone up that road and it has not been opened and nothing has been filled in.

---

The first of the three villages is nine hundred paces past a market town and has four people in a company of its own, and the first gate on the road out of it is a bar and a chain and about nine feet of mud, and the bar was on the near side of the road because it was the man of about thirty-six's week, and about four people in that village have said since that a man of thirty-six who keeps nothing held a gate for a man in a very good coat on the fourteenth day of the Short Month, and that he did it because the man asked him to in a voice that did not ask.

"What am I looking at."

The man in the good coat stood about nine paces back from the chain and did not go nearer to it, and about four people in that village have said since that a man who stands nine paces off a gate is a man who does not want to be counted as being at the gate.

"You are looking at a gate, and a gate is the second of the four places, and the first of the four places is a date, and I am going to tell you what a Crown schedule wants to hold at this gate, and then I am going to ask you to walk nine hundred paces with me and tell you the same thing at the next one."

"Tell me here."

The man of about thirty-six put both hands on the top of the bar and left them there, and about nine people who have watched him do that have said since that a man with his hands on a bar he does not own is a man who has been asked a question about it.

"There are eleven weeks of the days of three villages on the gates of this road, so that the road is kept while a schedule is made up. That is what the first place on that road would hold, and it is in the Crown's own words and I am reading the Crown's words and not mine." He took his hand out of his coat and did not have anything in it. "**It is safe. And I am not going to say that word again on this road, and I said in a room over a yard on the second day of that month that nobody in that room was to use it until it had been read out twice, and I have read it out twice and I cannot find what it is attached to on any of five sheets.**"

"You said it once anyway."

The bar came the rest of the way down onto the mud while the two of them were standing there, which it did not have to do, and about four people in that village have said since that a gate coming down while a page is being explained is the loudest possible way of saying that the page is not the subject of this morning.

"I said it once because I was reading a line and the line has the word in it and I did not translate it, and about four people in this city have worked out that not translating a word is a thing a person can do out loud in front of about nine people and that all nine of them heard it." He put his hand back in his coat. "**About four of the nine of you are going to say that word every day for a month and I am not going to be able to stop it, and it is on a sheet nine hundred miles away and it is not attached to anything.**"

---

Two mules and a man of about forty-two came up that road behind them at about the fourth hour of the afternoon going the other way with about nine sacks of nothing in particular, and he would not have stopped and stopped, and about nine people in that village have said since that a man with two mules who stops in the middle of a thing being explained to about nine other people is the only person on that road who is not part of it, and that he was the only one anybody asked a question he could answer.

"What are you doing with those mules."

He got the near mule's head round without being asked to and stood it, and about four people who have been on that road have said since that a man with nine sacks on two mules who can turn a mule in a lane the width of a gatepost is a man who has been doing that since before anybody was watching.

"I am taking them through. That gate has been shut since the fourth hour of the morning and it does not open for me until whoever keeps it decides, and I have been to the house and the house says the bar is across the road because a man in a good coat is standing about nine paces off it." He did not get the mules any nearer. "**You have brought eleven people down this road to stand at my gate and I have got nine sacks to move and about four hours of light. Those are the whole of my difficulties and not one of them is the road.**"

"You can go through when we have finished at this gate."

The man of about thirty-six had the bar in his two hands by then and had it about a foot off the mud and was holding it there, and about four people in that village have said since that a man holding a bar off the ground does not have to make a decision at all, he only has to put it down when he decides.

"I can go through when the person who has the bar says I can go through, and about four people in this village have worked out that a man who has not been asked whether a day suited him is a man who cannot be told no, and I would like it said that I have not said no, and that I have said nothing at all, and I would like both of those put down if anybody is putting anything down."

Nobody was putting anything down. The mule man went through at about the fifth hour of the afternoon with nine sacks and the bar went back onto the mud behind him, and about four people in three villages have said since that the first time a man with two mules was asked about the road he was asked by mistake, and that he answered it correctly, and that about four of the nine of the people who heard him have not worked out yet that he was the only one in the lane who was asked whether the day suited him.

---

At the second village the word had arrived before the road did. About four people in that village have said since that the word came from the first village on a cart of kindling in the morning and that a woman of about thirty-three said it at a door and did not know where she had had it, and that by the time the man in the good coat got to the second gate the word safe and the word villages had been put next to each other out here by about nine mouths, and that in a room over a yard four hundred miles back down this road those two words are on a wall on two lines and have not been joined up and are not going to be joined up by anybody in this city.

"Nobody in this city joined them."

The second gate is a post and a chain and no bar, and the reason there is no bar on it is not that the second village has nobody keeping it, which is what a stranger assumes on the first morning, and it is that the bar has been standing in the stack under the lean-to at the top of that lane since the post came out at the bottom in the second week, and there has been nothing to hang it on since. About four people in that village have said since that a gate with a chain on it that no person has ever opened is a gate that is only closed in the way a door is closed in a house where everybody is out.

"Nobody in this city joined them and I have not joined them, and they have been joined out here by the whole of that road and a cart of kindling, and about four people have said since that a word on a page with nothing attached to it is not a word that waits for somebody to attach it. It is a word that attaches itself to whoever says it first, and this time that was a man at a gatepost on the fourteenth day of the Short Month." He stood at the post and did not touch the chain. "**And I want it said that this is the second gate I have stood at today and that I have put the same thing at both of them, and that there is no office between this post and the building that signed the first one.**" "**Which means that the same sentence cannot be checked at both ends of it by anybody.**"

"You are the only person in four hundred miles who has been in that building."

The woman of about thirty at the stall had walked out with the rest of them and had a net and a needle in her hands and had been in that lane for two hours, and about four people in that village have said since that a woman with a net at a gatepost is a woman who has decided to be in a thing without being a party to it.

"I am, and I said so in a room over a market on the second day of that month, and about four people in this city have said since that the cost of it is that I am the only person in four hundred miles who cannot be checked by anybody in the room I am standing in, and it has cost this road about a week of being talked about." He took a folded sheet out of the coat and did not open it and put it back. "I said a thing about racks and channels and water in that room on the second day of that month and I am not going to say it again on this road and I am not going to improve on it, and I have got about nine days of three villages in this coat and I have not opened the sheet that says so and I am not going to open it on a gatepost."

---

They walked back up the lane at about the seventh hour of the evening and about eleven people went into nine houses and the light went off the low ground behind them and there was no fourth day in it anywhere.

Three of them were still standing in the lane at about the ninth hour, and about four people in that village have said since that three people arguing in a lane about a word they heard once is the ordinary end of a day like this one, and that not one of the three could say where they had heard it, and that about four of them in the first village had it from a cart of kindling and the other five had it from each other.

A question goes to everybody who will be in the room, in writing, in that person's own words, four days before the room, and there are four of those four and there has never been a fifth. Nobody on that road was asked anything, and nobody was asked whether the day suited them, and a road is not a room, and about nine people in three villages have worked out in about a week that the difference between being told a thing and being asked about it is about four days, and that a road cannot supply four days.

Nobody in four hundred miles will be able to say which of the eleven who stood at a gatepost on the fourteenth day of the Short Month will hold the first way correctly and which of them will not, and about four of them will be wrong by the middle of the next week, and the word safe is in three villages, and the two words on a wall four hundred miles back down that road are still on two lines and have not been joined by anybody in this city, and eleven weeks is a figure in words and is not a number of days, and nobody has converted it.

The man of forty-one in a very good coat went back down that road at about the eighth hour with his coat buttoned and about four of the company walking him as far as the market, and about nine people in that market have said since that a man who has spent a day on that road with about eleven people and has come back with no sheet open is a man who has spent the day and is going to have to say so on Monday, and that nobody in that market has asked him what the first way is and that about four of them are going to.
