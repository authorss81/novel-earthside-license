# Chapter 0270 — The Third Thing

The light off the low ground at the second village was flat and white at about the seventh hour of the morning on the fifteenth day of the Short Month and it did not warm anything, the cold was in the wet clay and in the handle of a barrow and out of the wind, and the wind has been at the wall of the store on the upper road for forty-one days, and what was under about twenty pairs of hands at that channel all day was a spade, a barrow, a length of chain, a bar, and about nine feet of bed that had been dug twice and filled in once. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures on it belong to that sixty-ninth day and are forty-six days old, and it has stood unwritten forty-five. A case from nine hundred miles away has been shut on a table in a room over a market in this city for nineteen days and it has not gone up that road and it has not been opened and nothing has been filled in.

---

The bar and the chain went on the gate in the road at about the tenth hour of the morning on the fourteenth day of the Short Month, which is yesterday, and about four people in three villages have said since that a man who keeps a road put a bar back on a gate he had not opened in four weeks because a man in a very good coat asked him to, and that nobody asked the man whether the day suited him and nobody asked him why it had been out.

"You put it back yesterday."

"**I put it back yesterday because a man in a good coat stood nine paces off it and asked me to, and I have never in nine years done a thing to that gate because a stranger asked me to do it, and I did it, and I have not slept about it.**" The man of about thirty-six had the spade in his hand and did not put it down. "You are the fourth person to come up this road since the water went out and about four of the three before you wanted something, and I would like it known that I am going to open this gate for whoever asks me and that I am not going to ask any of them anything."

The gate was opened at about the eleventh hour and the water came down a foot of it by about the second hour of the afternoon, and the bed of the channel took it and about nine people in that village have said since that a channel that has been dry for four weeks takes water like a person who has not eaten and not like a person who is being given too much of something. About four of the nine houses had a pail in the bed before the water reached the low end, and the ninth house did not have a pail in it, and about four people in that village have said since that the ninth house put its pail in at about the half hour and that the half hour was the whole of its week.

The bar went back onto the chain at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the chain went over the head of the post and the post did not move, and about nine people who were standing about nine paces off it have said since that they watched the post not move and that all nine of them looked at the man of about thirty-six and not at the post, and that he looked at the post.

---

The rack at the low end had not been worked since the water went out of it and it was not lifted and it was not carried, and a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and about four people in that village have said since that about nine people have said that sentence out loud in the last week and that every one of the nine was quoting a person who was about a hundred and forty years dead, and that nobody in that lane can say where the sentence started.

"You have got the water."

"We have got the water for about four days and the bed will fill and the water will stand and the rack at the low end will be a week older on Monday than it was last Monday, and that is the whole of what water has done for a rack in nine days." She put the sieve down on the lip of the channel and did not pick it up again for about a minute. "**I have been doing the sums of this household every morning since Sunday with a sieve in my hands and a man nine days off the use of an arm, and I want to tell you what the sum is, and then I am going to tell you what I have not worked out.**"

The water came over the lip in about four places at about the second hour of the afternoon and about nine people in that village turned out to stand in it, and about four of them have said since that a foot of water in a channel four weeks dry is the most welcome thing that has happened on that road in about a hundred days, and that the barrow went down the bed twice and lost a wheel both times.

"Go on."

The man of about thirty-six got the bar off the chain and stood it against the post and then did not move away from it, and about four people in that village have said since that a man who puts a bar against a post in front of a stranger is a man who wants the stranger to be able to see the post.

"The sum is that a gate that comes out of a road for four weeks and goes back in for a day does not cost the road anything, and the four weeks cost about four weeks, and a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and all three of those were true before anybody in this village heard that word, and all three of them will be true after." She picked the sieve up. "**What I have not worked out is why it was out at all.**"

"Tell him. He is the man of the road."

The man of about thirty-six went back to the post and put his hand on the bent bar and did not lift it, and about four people in that village have said since that a man with his hand on a bent bar is a man who has just been told something about it by a person he could not argue with.

"I am not going to tell him anything, because he is a man with two mules and nine sacks and he is not a man of the road, and about four people in that lane have worked out that I have said that in public and I have not taken it back and I am not going to." She went on down the channel with the sieve and about four people who have watched her do it have said that a woman who says a thing like that and then goes on with a sieve has made a decision and is not going to be argued out of it by a week. "What I have worked out instead is this. The bar on that gate is bent. It has been bent since before I was at this house and it is bent about a finger's width and it does not go down without a man on the end of it lifting. The post the bar works on is out at the bottom, and the bottom of it stands in the water, and the water came to the bottom of it about four weeks ago. And the man who bent it is not the man who has the turn now and has not been for about nine years, and nobody has ever asked him, and he is not on a page, and there is no page."

"There is no page."

"There has been no page for about a hundred and forty years, and that is not a figure I have got from anywhere, that is how long the post has been in the ground, and about four people in this village have worked out that the two facts are the same fact." She turned round at the end of the channel and the water was up to the edge of the lip and going over it in about four places. "**A Crown document has come nine hundred miles about the days on a road, and this road has been kept for a hundred and forty years by a bent bar and a post that is out in the water and a person who is not the last one to have held it, and not one of those three is on any of five sheets, and not one of them is anybody's fault.**"

---

Tamsin Quill and Adrian Vale came up that road at about the second hour of the afternoon and were in that kitchen by about the fourth, and she had a book of Crown Law in a bag at the bottom of it and about four people in that village have said since that a woman who carries a book nine hundred miles and a man who carries nothing is the most common thing that comes up that road and that about four of them have got it wrong and about nine have not.

"There is a second way on the fifth sheet and you have not read it out."

"**There is a second way on the fifth sheet and I have not read it out in three villages in four days, and I am not going to read it out at a kitchen table with the man who asked me for it, and I have said the same thing to about four people since Tuesday.**" Tamsin Quill put the bag on the bench and did not open it. "A second way of keeping a road is a thing you can only read out to people who could be in it. I do not know who is supposed to be in the second way, and I have said that in a room over a market and nobody has found me anybody to put in it."

The bag on the bench had not been opened and the book was at the bottom of it, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that a bag that has not been opened for two hours is a bag that is not going to be opened, and that the person who brought it knows that and is waiting for somebody else to work it out.

"You have read it."

"I have read it four times and it is a page and a half and it does not say a place or a gate or a body, and what it says is that a road may be kept by a reading entered in a register, and that the reading is entered under the name of a person who can be shown to have made it." She had her hands flat on the bag. "**Somebody has to be the name. That is the whole of the second way and it is about four words long and it is the only page in five that has not got a gate on it.**"

"You could enter it."

"I could enter it, because I am the only person in this village that can read a clerk's hand and is not a household, and about four people in that lane know that, and a name on a register that can be looked up is a name in a column, and you knew that when you asked me and you did not say it."

The sieve did not stop moving. The woman of about forty-six had it in both hands and went on working the clay out of about nine buckets of water and did not look up once through the whole of that, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that a woman with a sieve in her hands is a woman who has decided what a kitchen is for.

"You did not say it because I did not give you the chance."

"You did not say it because you wanted it, and I have wanted you to want it for about three days, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a thing you know and do not say is a thing you have chosen, and that is the whole of what has happened this afternoon and I am not going to make it smaller by saying it nicely." She put her hands in her lap. "**I am not going to say that you did it on purpose. You have never once done a thing on purpose in my hearing and you did not do this one on purpose either, and that is worse.**"

That was the whole of the afternoon. The bag went back down to the bottom of the kitchen and the book stayed in it and about four people in that village have said since that a woman who has carried a book nine hundred miles and has not opened it again has decided something, and that nobody in that kitchen has asked her what, and that about nine of the eleven people at the gateposts on the fourteenth day of that month heard nothing about any of it, and that a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and that a road on this stretch has been kept for about a hundred and forty years by a bar and a turn and a person with a bucket, and that the Crown has now written down two ways of keeping it and a gate on it is still out of its socket about nine feet from where a woman is sifting clay out of a pail, and nobody on that road has been asked anything by anybody, and nothing was decided on the fifteenth day of the Short Month.
