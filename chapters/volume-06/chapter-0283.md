# Chapter 0283 — I Know Which Four

A room over a market in this city had a date on the door in chalk on the twenty-eighth day of the Short Month, a Thursday, and a Hettis Roke of forty-four of the Hearthguard of the outer ward, nineteen years in, sat in it with a slate he turned over and turned back, and a woman of about twenty-nine who kept a public register put her second slate on the table face up, which is a thing she does not do. The light came in on the south side and went off the floor by about the middle of the afternoon, and the cold was out of the wind and in the shutters, and what was in that room was a slate with a heading on it and nothing under it, a second slate, a piece of chalk, and a woman of about thirty-seven who was a Hearthguard and had come up that road with him and did not sit down.

---

Nobody had asked whether the twenty-eighth suited him and nobody was going to, and a sheet with the question in his own words on it had gone out to that room four days before, on the twenty-fourth, and he had received it and had not answered it and had not said that he would not answer it, and about four people in this city have said since that a man who has read a question four days ahead and has said nothing at all about it has done the thing the four days are for, which is to arrive having had time to have said no.

"I am going to read out two things out of a book, and then I am going to ask you one, and the two things are the reason for the question and not the question." She put her hand on the second slate and left it there. "**On the one hundred and thirty-seventh day of this flood you were asked about your own eight in a room in daylight with a date on it, on a day you did not choose, and you said no, and the no was entered, and the asking was entered, and the asking is the whole of it.**"

"That is right."

"And on the fiftieth day of this flood, in about two hundred and forty people, you said no to a form and yes to a yard, and the two were entered separately, and then you asked to be asked again on a different day." She did not turn the slate. "**What I am about to do is the first of those two things and not the second, and I want that on the slate, because a man who has asked to be asked again on a day of his own choosing has been asked on somebody else's day ever since, and this is somebody else's day, and the whole of this flood is somebody else's day.**"

The woman of about thirty-seven got up off the bench she had not sat on and went and stood in the doorway with her back to the light, and a woman who has been in a room a quarter of an hour without sitting down has decided that she is about to hear something, and that standing in a doorway is how a Hearthguard says she is not the one being asked and is also the only way of seeing the whole of a room at once.

"Then ask it."

"I am going to ask it, and I am going to name the cost first, because that is what I do, and about four people in this city have said since that a woman who names the cost of a thing out loud before she does it is a woman who has been made to do it, and that this is the third time in about a month that she has done it, and that the first two were hers." She put the chalk on the table and did not pick it up again. "**The cost is that an asking entered twice is a record, and a record is a thing an office can ask a clerk for, and you are the only page anybody in four hundred miles has ever had about eight names, and I do not want to be the reason a building nine hundred miles away learns how to ask for you.**"

"I have been an asking twice and I have been a record twice, and the second of those is this morning and it is not finished."

"Then say the rest of that later and not now, and say the four words, and I will write down that you said them and I will not write down what they were." She squared the second slate to the edge of the table and left her hand on it. "**And I would like it understood in this room that a woman who keeps a register has spent her life being the person a thing gets put into, and that this morning is the first time in about a hundred and eighty-three days that she has been the person who put something in, and that the something is a question, and that a question is not a thing a register is for.**"

---

"You are not going to be brief about it, and I am going to read the question out once, and it is a question you wrote, and it is not mine and it is not this room's." She turned the second slate so that the writing faced him. "**The eight names your great-grandfather entered in the roll of the dead of the outer gate, and the eight stones, and the four names cut on the stones of people they were buried with. Where are they, and what became of them, and who may say so.**"

"It is mine."

"It is yours, and it has been in a book since the one hundred and thirty-seventh day of this flood, and about four people in this city have worked out that a question in a man's own words is the only instrument anybody has ever built here that cannot be answered wrongly on purpose." She put her hand flat on the table on the other side of it. "**Answer it or do not, and if you do not then I will enter that you were asked and I will enter nothing else, and the nothing else will be the only part of it that is mine.**"

The woman of about thirty-seven in the doorway turned her head once towards the shutters and back again, and a woman who turns her head towards a shutter while a thing is being said has counted the light and worked out how long there is, and that a Hearthguard who counts the light in somebody else's asking is a Hearthguard who expects to be the second half of it.

"I know which four."

He had said it at the same speed as the five sentences he had said in a market room on the twentieth day of that month and had not stopped at the end of it, and about four people in that room have said since that four words is not an answer to eight names and a hundred and forty years, and that four words out of a man who has said no five times in one hundred and forty-six days is a man who has decided to give the size of a thing and not the thing, and that the size of a thing is the only part of it that cannot be looked up.

"**That is four words, and I am not going to have a fifth one this morning, and the fifth one would be mine and not yours.** I am going to write down the day, and that you were asked, and that you answered, and I am not going to write down what you said, and about four people in this city have worked out that a woman who writes down that a man answered without writing down what he answered has built the only page in four hundred miles that four people could be shown and not one of them could look up."

The woman of about thirty-seven had a slate in her coat and did not take it out, and it stayed in the coat where it had been since she came up that road, and a woman who has carried a slate for four hundred miles and has not opened it in a room where a man of forty-four is answering a question about his own dead has decided that what is in it is not hers and is not this morning's.

The woman of about thirty-seven said it from the doorway, and nobody turned round, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who counts four words out loud in a room in which a man of forty-four has just spoken has done the thing an instrument is for, which is to be a second pair of ears in a room where an answer is being given in daylight.

"Then it goes into a book as a man knowing which four and not as a man naming them."

"**It does not go into a book as anything except a day, an asking and an answer, and I will not put a man's knowing into a book on the twenty-eighth day of that month, because a man who knows which four is not a man who has told me which four.**" About four people in this city have worked out that this is the whole of the difference between a register and a return."

The slate said the day, and that she had asked, and that he had answered, and it said no more than that, and there was no figure on it and there was no name on it, and about four people in that room have said since that a page which carries the day and the asking and the answer, and carries neither the answer nor a name, is the only page in four hundred miles that four people could be shown and could not be looked up, and that this is the whole of what she is for and that she is not going to be told so again.

"I have four words in four mouths and not one of them is on a page, and I have a question on a page for the second time in a hundred and forty-six days, and one of those two is a thing you did and the other is a thing I did, and I would like this room to know which is which."

"**The Crown's page has four places in it, and they are a date, a gate, a holder and the days, and not one of the four of them will take four words.**" A document with four places in it and four hundred miles behind it has nowhere whatever to put a man who says he knows which four, and about four people in this city have worked out that a form which has been left with an empty place in it is a form that is still waiting for somebody to fill it in."

"And what will you do on the thirty-ninth?"

The woman of about thirty-seven came in out of the doorway and stood at the end of the table and did not look at the slate, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who comes back into a room and stands at the end of a table rather than at the door has decided that whatever is said next is not hers to leave, and that the second half of an asking is never the person who did the first half.

"**Four of them will say yes, and I do not know which four, and I am not going to find out in advance, because a register-keeper who finds out in advance is a person who has turned the asking into a form, and that is the whole of what the Crown's page is and this is not it.** I am going to keep a day on nine answers, and I am not going to keep what the nine answers are, and about four people in this city have said since that a woman who is prepared to lose the answers in order not to have them is the only person in four hundred miles who has understood what a register is for."

There were nine chairs in that room and the ninth one was the only one that was spoken for and nobody had put a name on it, and a chair nobody has put a name on is the oldest instrument in four hundred miles and the only one that has never once been filled in, and that a man will sit in the ninth chair and will not know he is the ninth, and that this is the whole of what a room with a date on the door is for.

---

Nobody in that room put the two rolls on the table, and the slate with the heading on it stayed face up with nothing under it, and the case stayed shut in another city with its seal unbroken, and nobody in that room filled in a date, a gate, a holder or the days.

And about four people in that room have said since that a man of forty-four was asked about his own eight for the second time in a hundred and forty-six days, on a day he did not choose, and that he gave four words and would not give a fifth, and that the four words are in four mouths and on no page.

And about four people in this city have worked out that the only page in four hundred miles about eight names is a book kept on a quay nine hundred miles off, into which a clerk has written the count four times a year since before any of them were born, and that the count in that book has never once been a number that anybody came, and that a man who knows which four of his own eight got a name cut on somebody else's stone has known it for about a hundred and forty years, and that this is the first sentence anybody in four hundred miles has said about it, and that it is four words long.
