# Chapter 0279 — About Four Hundred And Forty

The salt wharf in this city was doing nothing on the twenty-fourth day of the Short Month, a Sunday, and about eleven miles off it the flats were standing a foot deep in the tail of a tide that came up four days and went down three, and about four hundred and forty people were working salt on those flats, and none of them was on any page anybody in this city keeps. The light was off the water and white and the cold was in the wet boards and out of the wind off the open reach, and what was under about six pairs of hands on those boards that morning was a flat of split ash on a handle going over the planks, a line of barrow, a stone with a hole in it for a rope, and people standing in a lane between two stalls doing nothing at all.

---

The girl of seventeen swept the boards of that stall and had a plain slate under her arm, and it was a plain one because her own had her name at the top of it, and about four people in that market have said since that a girl who sweeps the boards of a stall on a Sunday and carries a slate with nothing at the top of it is a person a market has decided to stop asking anything of, and that nobody has said so out loud and about four of the shops on that wharf have quietly stopped offering her anything.

The woman of about thirty-four who kept that stall had a slate of her own in the front of her apron and she did not take it out, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman with a slate in an apron and her hands full of something else is a woman who has decided what the morning is for, and that what the morning was for was not a number.

"You have been counting again."

"I have been counting in my head since the first hour and I have been doing it wrong for about nine years in the ordinary way, and the wrong way is the only way there is." She did not stop what she was doing. "Three people in this market have asked me for it since the second day of that month and I have said no three times, and I am not going to say no a fourth time in a lane. **It is about four hundred and forty.**"

A man of about thirty-eight who had come down the wharf with about nine of his own reasons and no reason at all that anybody could see stopped about nine feet short of the stall when he heard the number, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who stops nine feet short of a stall when a woman says a number has heard the number before and has not expected to hear it on the boards.

"That is what I have been putting in a return."

"That is what you have been putting in a return and people in four hundred miles have worked out that a return is a document with a heading on it and four places and no name in it, and about four of those four have been in a room in this city in about ten days." She put what she was holding down on the boards. "**I have had that number in my head since before this flood and I got it off a woman on a causeway in the first month and I have said it in about four kitchens and on the boards this morning, and there is not one page in four hundred miles that has it on it except yours.**"

"Yours is not a page in four hundred miles. Mine goes nine hundred miles in a quarter."

"**I know where yours goes, and about four people in this city have worked out the same, and not one of the four has been to the building it goes to.**" She looked along the boards. "There is a man in this city who has, and he is about nine feet from that stall, and neither of us has said a word to him, and I would like it entered that I noticed him before I said the number and that I said the number anyway."

---

The girl of seventeen stopped sweeping for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then went on, and about four people in that market have said since that a girl who stops sweeping for the length of a pail being filled and then starts again has decided that a number spoken on boards is not a thing to be caught in a flat of split ash and carried home, and that nobody told her to decide that.

The man of about thirty-eight came the rest of the way to the stall and put both forearms on the boards and did not put a hand inside his coat until he had been like that for about a minute, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts two forearms on a board before he puts a hand inside a coat is a man who has come to say something and has changed his mind about how to start it, and people in that market have watched a man change his mind about how to start something.

He put a hand inside his coat and left it there and took out a flat of wood with notches cut in one edge and about nine of the notches worn smooth, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who takes out a tally he has been carrying since the first month and holds it the way a person holds a thing they have been asked about four times a year for a long time is a man who is the only page.

"It goes four times a year. It has gone four times a year since the winter before the water and it has never once been answered and nobody has ever come, and about four people in this city have worked out that a thing which is sent four times a year and never answered is a thing somebody nine hundred miles off has not decided is a question." He put the flat of wood on the boards. "I count on the flats. I have counted since the first month and I put it in, and I do not know what it is for, and I have never been asked and I have never told anybody. **I have told about nine people in this market this morning by saying nothing, and I would like that entered, because it is the fourth time this year that I have told somebody by saying nothing and about four of the four were not on purpose.**"

"Nobody asked you what it was for."

"Nobody has ever asked me what it was for and people in this market have said since that a man who sends a number four times a year and is never asked what it is for has understood that a page is a question and has not found anybody to ask it, and that the whole of what I have been doing is a question going out of a market in a bag, and that a bag is not a person and a question that is not a question to anybody is a thing that can be used." He picked the flat of wood up. "**You have just said my number out loud on the boards in daylight, and about nine people in four hundred miles have heard it, and I have been putting it on a page for about nine months, and the day it goes into a page in a building I have never seen, it is not nine people who have said it.**"

---

A carter came down the wharf at about the eleventh hour with an empty barrow and asked the man of about thirty-eight a question about a load of salt, and got the ordinary answer, and the man of about thirty-eight gave it without looking at the boards and without looking at the stall, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who answers an ordinary question in the middle of the strangest morning of his life without lifting his eyes is a man who has decided that the morning and the question are two separate things and is going to keep them separate, and that about four people in that city would have said the opposite.

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat had a hand inside a coat that had nothing in it and had been standing about nine feet from that stall for the whole of it, and people in that market have said since that a man in a good coat standing nine feet off a stall with an empty coat is a man who has found out that a number he has heard four times this month is the only correct figure anybody in four hundred miles has, and that he cannot do anything with it at all.

"I have been in the building it goes to."

"You have been in the building it goes to and you have not been asked for the figure and you have not got it, and about four people in this market have said that a man in a good coat has spent four days in four rooms and has not once been asked for a number, and that is not an accident and it is the same reason nobody on a wall over that market is asked for a bar." He took his hand out. "I am telling you what a clerk does with it, because I have watched it done. A schedule wants a date, a gate, a holder and the days, and about four people in this market have worked out that a clerk cannot make any of those four out of a road. **So a clerk will make them out of a return, because a return is a document with a heading and four places and no name in it, and it is the only correct paper anybody in four hundred miles has.**"

"That is not true."

"It is not true and it would be true, and that is the whole of what I have come down to the wharf to say. **I have said it once and I am not going to say it twice, and about four people in this market have worked out that a man who has come nine hundred miles of thinking to say a thing once is a man who is frightened of the second time.**" He did not look at the man of about thirty-eight. "If that sheet is ever filled in from returns, it goes out correct. Four hundred and forty is a correct number. And every household in it is eleven miles off this wharf and on a flat, and none of them is on a gate and none of them has days anybody can be shown, and a schedule that goes out correct about the wrong people is the only kind of correct there is."

---

A woman of about thirty-three at a bread board two stalls down put her hand flat on her own boards and did not come over, and people in that market have said since that a woman at a bread board who puts her hand flat on her own boards and does not come over is a woman who has worked out that a stall is the only thing she has got, and that about four hundred and forty people are not a thing that can be done to from a stall.

"You have said a number on your own boards."

"**I have said it on my own boards, on a Sunday, in daylight, to about nine people, and about four hundred and forty people are a thing I have been carrying since the first month without a slate, and I am not going to put it on one.**" She put the slate back into the front of her apron and put her hand on the string of it. "The reason is nine years old and it is not that I am frightened of a number. It is that a number that has been said out loud is on about nine people's mouths, and a number on a page is on a page, and about four people in this city have worked out that a thing on a page in a market is a thing a clerk can send for."

"You have said it once out loud."

"Once out loud and about nine times in my own head, and about nine of us heard it this morning, and about four of us have it, and nobody in this market has written it down, and people in this market have said that that is the third time this month that a thing has been said on boards instead of on paper, and every time it has gone well, and about nine times in ten it does not go well and nobody has worked out why."

Nobody said anything on those boards for a while, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who says a number and a man who says what a clerk will do with it are the two halves of a thing nobody out there wanted, and that the girl of seventeen swept the boards through the whole of it and was not asked one question, and that about four people in this city have said that a market where nobody asks a girl of seventeen anything is the only instrument this flood has that cannot be entered in a schedule, and that a person who cannot be found is not a qualification and has never once been a solution.

About nine people in that lane knew what the count was by the middle of that morning and there was no page in four hundred miles for any of them but the nine, and about four hundred and forty people were on a flat eleven miles off a wharf, and nine of them were on a rota, and the rota had no name on it that anybody in this city could read, and nobody on those boards said a word about the flats, and nobody on those boards said a word about the nine either, and the girl of seventeen put the flat of split ash against the boards at the end of the lane and went home, and about four people in that market have said since that she was the only person on those wharves all morning who did not know what the number was for.
