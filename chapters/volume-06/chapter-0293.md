# Chapter 0293 — Five

A house on that road had a date in chalk on its door on the thirty-eighth day of the Short Month, a Sunday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, and a sheet of paper with a question on it in a hand that is a girl's had come to that house on the thirty-seventh, which is the day before, and the light came in on the south side of that front and went off the floor by about the middle of the morning, and the cold was in the boards and in the chain that was across the front of a cart, and what was under about four pairs of hands that Sunday was a question, a chain, a hand-cart of salt going past, and a woman of about twenty-two with a pail she had not put down.

---

The chain came off the front of the cart and went up over the driver's shoulder and the cart came through the gap and the chain went back across behind it, and the whole of that took about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and a woman of about twenty-two stood in the front of her own house and watched a cart go past her doorway with a chain in the way of it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a household which is being asked a question by a stranger while a cart is going past its front is a household that has to answer with its hands full, and that this one did not put the pail down.

"You are the eighth and there is one after you and you are the only one of the nine that I have not got right yet, and I am going to say something in this room that I have not said in any of the other eight, and I am going to say it to you and not to the lane."

"**Then say it, and I have had a sheet since yesterday, which is one day, and yesterday is a day, and I am not going to tell you it is four.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven came in behind her and put a sack down against the wall and did not open it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who comes in with a sack and does not open it has decided the sack is not part of the conversation and is going to have to be somebody else's problem, and that he is right about that.

"The sheet went out on the thirty-fourth. That is four days back." He put his hands behind him and stayed by the door, which is what he does in kitchens. "**It came here on the thirty-seventh, and the thirty-seventh is the day before this one, and you have had it about a day, and I am not going to stand in your front and pretend that is the instrument.**"

"You are not going to stand in my front and pretend it, and you have not come in and sat down, and my wife has not put the pail down, and I would like all three of those entered somewhere." The man of about thirty-seven put the sack again. "Entered is the wrong word. I would like them to have happened."

---

Nobody in that front said anything for a while and the cart went up the road and the chain rattled off the driver's shoulder about nine times and the woman of about twenty-two put the pail down at last on the step and not inside, and a pail put on a step and not inside is a pail that is about to be needed again.

"I have been in eight rooms in nine days and I have got the days, and I am going to put them on your step in the order they happened, and you are going to tell me if I have got one wrong, and you are the only people in four hundred miles who can tell me because you are the only people who have heard all of it." He did not look at the sack. "**A room on the thirty-first and its sheet came on the twenty-seventh. A room on the thirty-second and it came on the twenty-eighth. A room on the thirty-third and it came on the twenty-ninth. A room on the thirty-fourth and it came on the thirtieth. Four rooms, four sheets, four days each, and four of them said yes.**"

"And the other four."

"The thirty-fifth nobody can tell me about, and I have stopped asking, and I am telling you I have stopped asking instead of telling you nothing, which is a thing I decided on the road this morning and it is the first decision I have made about this since the thirtieth."

---

The man of about thirty-seven took two steps into the room and then stopped, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who comes two steps into a room and stops has decided that the rest of the room is not his, and that the woman of about twenty-two has not asked him to come the rest of the way and has not told him to go back.

"**A room on the thirty-sixth and the sheet came on the thirty-second, and that room was a place and not a household, and there were two of us in it, and four days cannot be divided, and neither of them had four days.**"

"That is two."

"That is two households, and both of them said yes, and both of them gave a day, and one of the days was not one of the nine and the other one was not either, and neither of them signed, and they are lying face down on a shelf in a house with a door that shuts now." He put his hand flat on the door frame and took it off. "**A room on the thirty-seventh and the sheet came on the thirty-fifth, and that is two days, and a man and a woman in that room gave me two different days and neither of them was the thirty-seventh.**"

The chain came off the front of a second cart and went back across, and the woman of about twenty-two came in off the step with her pail in both hands and set it down by the far wall and stood behind it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who brings a pail into a room in the middle of an argument about a number has decided that the pail is going to be in the room for the rest of the morning whatever the number turns out to be.

"And this one is one day."

"And this one came yesterday. That is one day, and I am the one who put it there, and nobody put it there for me, and I will say that once and then I will not say it again."

---

The woman of about twenty-two said the number and the room did not repeat it and about four people in that lane have said since that a number said once in a kitchen in front of a pail is a number nobody in four hundred miles can take away, and that this is the first number this week that has been said out loud in a room and not entered on anything, and that the woman who said it is twenty-two and has been in this flood since she was nine.

"**It is five.**"

"It is five, and here is what five is, and I am going to say all of it and then I am not going to say any of it again." He stayed by the door. "Four of the nine had four days. Five did not. Two of the five are one household in a place, one had two days, one had one day, and one is a house at the end of a lane past two gates that a man asked on the thirty-third with nothing at all in his hand, six days before the day it was to be asked on, and it answered him, and the answer cannot be filed anywhere, and its own sheet did not arrive until the thirty-fifth, which is four days, and it had already answered before the sheet got there."

"So the fifth had four days."

"**The fifth had a sheet four days before its day and had answered the question six days before that with nothing in a man's hand, and a notice that arrives after the answer is a receipt and not a notice, and that is the whole of what that one is and nobody did anything to it.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven sat down on the step with the sack behind him and did not say the first word of the answer for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who takes that long to say a thing he has already said in his own head is a man who is aware that the room he is saying it in has just heard nine sentences about other rooms and is not going to be handed the ninth one quickly.

"The thirty-eighth is a Sunday and this is the only Sunday I have got, and about four of us are going to be at the trough, and I am not going to be one of them." He said it to the door frame and not to the woman. "**We will give you a Sunday in about a fortnight and I will tell you the day, and you can come and write it down or you can not, and I am not going to hold a day in my mouth for a man I have met once.**"

"You have had the sheet for one day and that is your fortnight, and you are not going to be asked for it and there is nothing on the sheet that says you have to."

"There is nothing on the sheet that says I have to, and about four people in this house worked out that in about four hours yesterday, and one of them was me and one of them was my wife, and we got there by different roads." The woman of about twenty-two picked her pail up off the step. "**We are not going to write on it, and I want it entered that this is a decision and not an oversight, and I want it entered by whoever is keeping the nine days, because a thing that is not written down by accident and a thing that is not written down on purpose are the same thing in about nine months and mine is on purpose.**"

---

The pail went out of the front and the chain came off the front of a second cart and went back across, and the woman of about twenty-two was in the lane before the cart was past, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who goes to a trough with a pail in the middle of being asked a question by a stranger is a woman who has decided that the trough does not know about the nine and that the trough is owed a pail, and that this is the whole of the difference between a household and an office in one sentence and neither of them is wrong.

Nobody in that front put the five on anything, and nobody in that front said the word fault, and about four people in that lane have said since that the room did the whole of that in about nine sentences and that not one of the nine was a complaint, and that the reason none of them was a complaint is that there is nobody in the chain of them to complain to: the notice went out on the morning it went out and the copyist signed none of it and the girl who copied it is on no roll and cannot be fetched and the road was out for a day and nobody shut it.

The man of about thirty-seven put the sack against the wall again and this time it moved a foot and he stopped it with his boot and did not open it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who stops a sack with his boot and does not open it is a man who has put two things in a room this morning that are not the question and is not going to be asked about either of them.

"You have said a number out loud in my front and you have not put it on anything and I do not know what to do with it."

"**Nobody does, and that is the count, and I am not going to give you a reason for it, because I have got nine reasons and every one of them is a road and not a person, and a person in this flood can be asked whether a thing suits them and a road cannot, and that is the first time anybody has been able to say that sentence without making a speech.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven said that the four were not a good number, and said it before anybody had said anything about the four, and the woman of about twenty-two asked him why, and he said that a thing which works four times out of nine in a week is not a thing anybody should be pleased with, and that whoever goes home tonight and hears four will tell their own household that four is what happens, and that it will be true, and that it is not a reason to go on.

And the eighth of the nine kitchens was asked on the thirty-eighth day of that month, a Sunday, in daylight, with a date in chalk on a door and a cart of salt going past the front with a chain across it, and that the sheet went out on the thirty-fourth and came on the thirty-seventh, and that the count was made in a kitchen and not in a room over a market and not by a woman with a register, and that it came to five, and that the fifth of the nine is a day nobody in four hundred miles can account for and that nobody has said whether a household was asked on it, and that nine days and nine households have been the same number for nine days and are not the same number this morning, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that nobody can now say how many households there are, and that nobody sent anybody and nobody was at fault and that the four are not a number anybody should be given tonight.
