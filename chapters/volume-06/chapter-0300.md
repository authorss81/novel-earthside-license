# Chapter 0300 — A Gate That Is Opened On A Day

A gate on a road that comes down from the north side of the Marches was chalked on the forty-fifth day of the Short Month, a Sunday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, and a woman of about fifty was at it with a bar of ash and a wooden hook and her other hand in the front of her coat, and the light came in over the shoulder of the hill and did not reach the gate, and the cold was out of the north and in the iron of the hook, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a bar of ash, a hook, a piece of chalk, a date, and a road that had been shut for six days and was not going to be open for anybody who wanted to go up it.

---

She put the date on the post above the other ones and did not rub any of them out, and there were fifteen days of that month left after it and nobody in four hundred miles had said anything about any of the fifteen, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who chalks a day on a post on a Sunday when nothing is coming up the road is a woman who has decided that the day does not have to be justified by traffic, and that a gate is a thing that exists on a day whether or not anything uses it, and that this is the only sentence anybody has said this month that is about a gate and not about a road.

The bar of ash went into its two brackets and the hook went over its handle, and she stood with her hand flat on the post for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then took it off and looked at the wood where her hand had been, and it was the same wood it had been the day before.

---

A woman of about sixty-five came down that lane at about the ninth hour with a boy of about eight and a creel on her hip, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman going *down* a lane on a Sunday morning with a creel and a child is going to a house at the bottom of it and not to a market, and that she came down the lane past a shut gate because a shut gate is a gate and there is no other way down that lane at all, and that a road nobody is on is the only kind of road anybody can still walk down without asking anybody for anything.

"Is it open?"

"It is open, and there is nothing on the far side of it, and I am not going to say one word to you about the far side of it because I have not been up it since the third week of the first month and I do not talk about roads I have not walked on." She did not take the bar out. "**And you are going down and not up, and I can see that from here, and I have not been asked one question this week by anybody except two men with a barrow and a woman with a case, and I am not going to start on a Sunday.**"

---

The woman of about sixty-five went down the lane and the boy of about eight went after her with the creel until she took it off him, and a child of eight who carries a creel until his mother takes it from him is a child being given something and taking it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that neither of those two things in this flood has ever once gone into a column, and that a woman of sixty-five and a boy of eight walking down a lane past a shut gate are the only two people in this flood being given anything on a Sunday, and that it is not nothing.

Then the lane was empty and stayed empty until the fourth hour, and the keeper of the gate did the gate.

---

The boy of about eight came back up the lane at about the tenth hour on his own with the creel on his hip and not on his shoulder, and he stopped at the near end of the gate and looked up at her for about as long as it takes to fill a pail before he said anything, and about four people in that lane have said since that a child who stops at the end of a lane and looks up at a gatekeeper before speaking is a child who has been raised somewhere that a person at a gate is somebody you wait for rather than somebody you walk past, and that this is a habit about four hundred years old and it is not in a book and does not need to be.

"Is your mother not at the bottom?"

"She is at the bottom and she has sent me up for a thing she has not told me what it is, and I have been up and down this lane four times since the seventh hour and I have not seen you, and I am saying that and not saying that you are slow." He put the creel down. "**She says to say thank you and I am not saying it because you are not the gate, you are the woman at the gate.**"

"Then say thank you to the woman at the gate."

"I am not saying it either and my mother will want to know I did not, and a person who is not thanked and does not say thank you is the same sort of thing from both ends, and a gate is the only object in this flood that nobody has ever managed to put a name on."

---

She worked the hook down and the gate came four inches and she put her hand flat against the post and found the draught and put the bar back in and it stopped, and then she took the bar out again and stood it against the post and opened the gate the whole way, which she had not done since the Friday before that. About four people in that lane have said since that a woman who opens a shut gate the whole way on a Sunday with nothing behind it is a woman who has decided that a gate which is only ever opened four inches is not being kept, it is being held, and that the whole difference between those two things is about nine inches.

She went out through it. She went nine paces out along the middle of the road with the bar still in its brackets behind her, and she stood on the road, and the wind was out of the north and it came off the shoulder of the hill behind her and it did not stop, and a woman who walks nine paces out onto a road she keeps and stands on it in the wind on a Sunday morning is a woman who has gone to look at the road, and nine paces is the whole distance a person can go and still shut the gate behind themselves in about nine seconds.

---

There was nothing on that road to look at. It came down over the hill on the fourth and had not come down it since the second hour of the night of the thirty-ninth, and what was there at nine paces out from the gate at about the ninth hour of a Sunday was a road and a bar across the top of it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that morning that this is the third time in this flood that somebody has gone out of a doorway to look at a thing and come back in, and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever walked a shut road to check that it is shut.

She did not go up the road to the wall. She came back through the gate, and pulled it to and not pushed it, and put the bar of ash back into its two brackets and the hook over its handle, and a woman who shuts a gate by pulling it has found the same fact three times this month in three kinds of thing, and the three of them were a door that shut, a gate that shut, and a form that was filled in, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that pulling and not pushing is the whole of what a household does and the whole of what a document cannot do.

---

She worked the gate to and the thirty feet of ash came against its stop and made the noise it has made every day for nine years, and the gate runs in two brackets that were set in the stone when the second gate went up that road, and a woman who has been closing thirty feet of ash four inches at a time for nine years knows the sound it makes before she has finished pulling it and does not need to look, and a keeper is not a person who watches a gate, a keeper is a person who has learned a noise.

The boy of about eight was still at the end of that lane with the creel on his hip, and he had been there for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and had not asked her anything, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that a child who waits at the end of a lane for a thing that is not going to be given to him is being taught something by the waiting.

The key on the tarred cord fits a lock on the inside of the second gate, which is a gate into a yard and not a gate onto a road, and she took it out of the apron pocket and held it in her palm for a moment and then put it back and did not go through the yard, and it has not been turned since about the second month of this flood, and a key that has not been turned in eight months is not a key anybody has thrown away, it is a key somebody has decided to keep working.

---

A cart came up the lane from the south at about the middle of the morning with a load of boards on it, and the driver stopped at the gate and looked up the road and then down the lane, and a cart that stops at a shut gate and looks up a road it cannot use is a cart that has been sent for the fourth time this week and has not arrived, and the driver said nothing to the woman at the gate because he had worked out before he got there that a person at a gate is not a person to be sent to, and the whole of this flood is contained in a driver not saying a word to a woman at a bar.

At about the second hour a man of about sixty came up the same lane on foot with a handcart and stopped at the gate and asked her, in about the words the man with the barrow had used on the fortieth, whether anybody had been up the road, and she gave him the same answer, and he said he had heard the road was gone and wanted to put a fence across the top of it before somebody else did, and that he had a length of rope in the cart and had never got as far as having a fence.

---

"You told him no and he is going to come back."

"He is going to come back in about nine days with a fence and a length of rope and he is going to be perfectly reasonable about both of them, and I am going to say no again, and that is the whole of what there is to say to a man with a fence, and I have said no to about four men this month and I have not said it unkindly once." She put the hook down. "**And he is not the first person this week to be told no by me about a thing I have not looked at, and the reason I do not have to look at it is that nobody has asked me to, and the whole of this flood is people not asking each other and it turns out to be the only instrument that works.**"

---

The man of about thirty-eight did not come that day and had not been going to, and the arrangement was for the Thursday, and he worked that out over the weekend and told nobody, and turned round on the fortieth, and has not been up that road since the fourth, and a barrow of boards for a house on this side of the hill has been standing against the wall of a gate for five days and nobody has taken it away.

She stood at the post then for a while with her hand in the front of her coat, and nobody was on that lane and nobody came up it, and a woman who stands at a gate post with her hand in her coat and says a thing out loud to nobody is not doing it for the record, and nobody in four hundred miles is going to hear it, and this is the third time in about a month that the truest thing anybody has said in this flood has been said to a post.

"**There is a line in that orchard and I have not looked at it and I am not going to look at it this month, and I do not know what it is, and I am not going to tell you what I think it is, because I do not think anything about it.**"

And there is one man in four hundred miles who knows that she does not know, and he is going to be on a roof in about nine days, and he is not a person anybody writes things down from, and everybody else in four hundred miles is going to go on not knowing too, and that is going to cost them nothing.

---

The rest of that Sunday she did the gate about nine times between the seventh hour and the fourth, and the doing of it is this and there is nothing else about it: the hook down, four inches, a hand flat on the post, the draught found, the bar back in, and the gate stopped. And about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who works a gate shut and opens it four inches and shuts it again nine times on a day when nothing is coming up the road is not holding a road and is not keeping one, and is running a thing with a tolerance in it, and that the whole of her trade on that Sunday was a quarter of an inch in nine years and a hook that does not slip, and that those are the only two measurements taken on that road this month and neither of them was taken on a sheet.

She took the key out of the apron pocket at about the fourth hour and looked at it and put it inside the front of her coat where it had been at the seventh hour, and the cord left a mark on her hand that took about as long to come out as it takes to fill a pail. A key is not a figure and a keeper is not a holder, and a brass thing on a tarred cord in an apron pocket is the only object in this flood that nobody has ever managed to enter anywhere, and it is not on a wall and it is not in a book and it is not in a register with nine days in it, and the month has fifteen days of it left after it and nobody in four hundred miles has said what is going to happen in any of them.

---

And at about the fourth hour she was at the near end of that gate with the bar of ash back in its two brackets and the hook over its handle and the boy of about eight gone down the lane again, and from where she was standing the near end of that orchard was visible past the gap in the low wall, and the straight line in the ground under it was visible from a gate as a line and nothing else, and she did not go up the road to look at it and she did not send for anybody, and the man on the flats about eleven miles the other way, whose whole trade is putting things in holes, was not sent for and would have refused in the time it takes to shut a gate, and nobody measured it and nobody dug it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a straight line in the ground is the only thing anybody in this flood has seen this month that is not a page, a wall, a drain, a gate, a wage or a person, and that it is the first thing in about three hundred days that has no use, and it is about a foot wide and about two inches deep and it is not a path and not a watercourse and not a furrow and not a boundary, and the day she is looking at it on is a day she would not remember, because she has not been counting the days, and there is nothing on that post above the forty-fifth but the forty-fifth, and she had not taken yesterday's down either.
