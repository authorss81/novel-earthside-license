# Chapter 0259 — Nobody Will Carry It West

The case from nine hundred miles away was still shut on a table in the room over a market on the fourth day of the Short Month, and it had been on that table for eight days, and on the fourth day of the Short Month a man was asked to carry it up the road to three villages and have it read to the people in them, and he said no before the sentence was finished. The wind has been at the wall of the store on the upper road for thirty days. The board above that door carries the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month, which are thirty-five days old, and has not been written on since the day after them. A woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road came up that road at about the second hour of the afternoon and sat down against the wall by the step at the back and did not say one word for the whole of that day.

---

The question had been written out and given by hand four days before that room, to every person who would be in it, and nobody who could not be given it was in it, and nobody had asked Hettis Roke whether the day suited him. He is forty-four and of the Hearthguard of the outer ward and nineteen years in and he keeps the ground behind the ordinary wall, and he has said no four times in this city and has never said yes to anything, and about four people in this city have said that a man who has never said yes to anything is a man who has been asked the wrong things four times.

"You have read the question."

"I have read the question and I have carried a case like that one up a road once, and I am going to say no, and I would like it entered that I said no before the question was finished and that I did not want to be asked in a room and that I would have said the same no in a yard."

"You are not going to give a reason."

"**I am going to give a reason and it is nine words long and it is going to be the whole of it. Nobody here carries a Crown case but the Hearthguard.**"

Nobody in that room moved for a while, and the light in it went off the flats and up the far wall of it while they were not moving.

"That is nine words."

"That is nine words and it is true and it is not an argument, and about four people in this room are going to spend the next ten minutes trying to make it into an argument, and I would like it entered that I watched them do it and that I said nothing while they did it."

"You could carry it as a civilian."

"**I could put this coat on a bench and take the case up that road as a man who is not a Hearthguard, and a case carried that way is a case carried by nobody.**"

A woman of about twenty-four turned her slate over and wrote the date on the back of it, and about four people in that room saw her do it and none of them said a word.

"That is worse."

"**It would arrive at three villages as a thing that came from nowhere with no keeper on it, and about four people in those villages would be told a thing by it, and nobody in those villages would be in a position to say no to it, and that is the whole of what a no is for.**"

"You have thought about that before."

"I have thought about that before and I have not done it and I am not going to. The Hearthguard's share of a thing is the only part of it anybody can refuse, and if I take the coat off then nobody in four hundred miles can refuse the rest of it."

The wind came at that wall from the same quarter it had been coming from for a month, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had stopped noticing it and had started listening to it.

"You are not refusing the case. You are refusing the carrying of it."

"They are the same thing and I know they are the same thing."

"Explain the knowing."

"**Nineteen years ago an instrument named me as a witness and said that I sign also for the Hearthguard and may refuse the whole of the Hearthguard's share of it. That is on a page in this city with a date on it and about nine people in four hundred miles have read it. The moment a thing gets split into two words in this city it gets carried by somebody, and about four people in four hundred miles have spent a hundred and forty days finding that out the slow way.**"

The room waited for the rest of it. There was no rest of it. He put his hand flat on the table beside the case and left it there and did not look at the wax.

"You have said no and there is nothing after it."

"There is nothing after it and the room can go home."

"What has happened today, then."

"**A Crown case is not going up that road. About four people in this room have worked out that a case which does not go is a case which is not read, and they are right, and that is the cost of it, and the cost belongs to three villages and not to this room.**"

A man at the back put his weight on one foot and then on the other and did not say anything for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet.

"Whose season is it."

"**I am not going to say that either, and I am not going to say how many people are in them, and I have been asked neither question today and I would like it entered that I was asked neither.**"

"Nobody has asked those villages."

"Nobody has asked those villages anything, and I have not been asked to ask them, and there is a woman of about seventy on that road who has been asked nothing in nine years and refused four times and got a fifth refusal out of a notary on Friday, and I am not going to be the reason she gets a sixth from a case."

---

A woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road wrote something on a slate in that room at about the fifth hour of the afternoon without saying anything, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register read it, and then she entered it on her second slate in a second hand and read that back once and did not improve on it, and about four people in that room heard the whole of it and about nine did not.

"What is on the slate."

"It is a written asking, and it is dated four days ahead, and it says that the case is to be read to the three villages by somebody who is not carrying it."

"He has refused to carry it and he has asked for it to be read."

"He has refused to carry it and he has asked for it to be read, and about four people in this room have been in this city for two hundred and fifty-nine days and nobody has ever asked for that."

Somebody at the door said something to somebody else and neither of them came in, and the room did not turn round.

"Has it been asked for anywhere."

"Nobody in four hundred miles has ever asked a thing to be read by somebody who is not bringing it."

"I did not think that was true."

"**I have wanted to ask it myself about four times since the second month of this flood and I have not, because I have never once been the one carrying anything to anybody, and asking for it on another person's behalf is a thing a person does when they want the credit of it.**"

A chair was moved about four inches along a boards floor by a person who had not been asked to move it, and about four people in that room have said since that they have never found out who moved it.

"That is not a good enough reason."

"It is the reason I have, and it is good enough for a woman of about twenty-nine who has never carried a case up a road in her life, and about four people in this room have noticed that I said it that way and none of them said so."

"Why does he want it."

"He does not want it. He wants the asking entered, and he said so in four words and then he sat down, and I have been in rooms with men who want things for nineteen years and that was not it."

The page on the table was turned so that the writing faced the man who could not read it, and about nine people in that room have said that this happened and that nobody has said out loud who turned it.

"What did you think he wanted."

"I thought he wanted to be asked again on a different day. He asked for that himself once, a long time ago, at a meeting-ground with about two hundred and forty people in it, and he was not asked then whether the day suited him either, and nobody has since."

"And today he was not asked."

"Today he was not asked and he did not ask, and the two of those are not the same thing and about four people in this room have been treating them as the same thing since the ninth hour."

Adrian Vale was at the back of that room on a stool for the whole of it. He read the entry on the second slate when it was read back, and he read it twice, and he said nothing about it, and nobody asked him, and about four people in that room noticed that he read it twice and about nine did not, and it is entered that he read it and it is not entered what he thought of it.

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat was in that room and had his hand inside his coat about nine times in the two hours he was there, and he said almost nothing, and what he said he said with his back to the window.

"You could read it to them yourself."

"I could read it to them myself and then it would be me, and a man in a good coat who has been in a building nine hundred miles from here is not a person three villages will take an instruction from in the second month of a flood, and I have not found that out by argument. I have found it out by watching about four people not take an instruction from me."

"You have never tried."

"I have never tried and I am not going to try inside a hundred days. A man who has not tried a thing is not a man who has decided against it, and about four people in this city have said that to me and they are right, and I have not decided against it and I have not got to it either, and those are two different sentences and this city keeps not hearing both of them."

Somebody wrote a figure on the back of their own hand and then put their hand in a pocket, and about four people in that room have said that they have each done that about nine times in about nine weeks.

"You want to try it."

"I want to be asked to, by somebody who is not going to be disappointed when I do, and nobody has asked me that either."

The woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road wrote the last line of that entry on the second slate at about the seventh hour of the evening, in her own hand, and she did not say one word while she did it, and about four people in that room noticed that she was writing and about nine did not, and nobody remarked on it, and about four people who were in a yard on the ninety-second day of the Long Month, when she put a broom down on some boards and said nothing about what it was for, have said since that a woman who puts a thing down on somebody else's boards and then writes on a second slate in another room has done two things that nobody in either room has joined up, and that she is not going to be asked about either.

---

He had asked, once, to be asked again on a different day, and about four people in this city know that he asked for it himself, and about nine do not know when he asked, and he has not said. He was entitled to ask it again today and he did not ask it, and about four people in that room have worked out that a man who is entitled to ask and does not ask is a man who has already got his answer and is waiting to be asked for it properly, and nobody in that room was going to be the one to ask him, and he went home up the road at about the eighth hour on foot.

Nobody thanked him. He was not thanked and he has said that he did not want to be, and about four people in that room worked out, on the way out, that a man who has kept a gate for nineteen years for a state has never once been asked to keep a gate, only to answer for one, and none of them said it in the room, and one of them has said it in a yard since.

The count of refusals said out loud in this city moved by one on the fourth day of the Short Month, and it was entered that it moved by one, and that the two counts in two books in this city, which have never been made smaller, were not made smaller by it and are not going to be.

A refusal out loud has to cost somebody something specific, and this one cost a season's worth of being told nothing, and about four people in that room have said that a season is a season and not a figure, and that nobody in this city is going to be told how many days it is today, and that the man who said no was right, and that being right is not a discount on the cost.

A man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came up that road at about the sixth hour, having heard nothing, and stood in the doorway of that room for a while with his hat in his hand and did not go in, and about four people inside that room have said since that a man who stands in a doorway holding a hat is a man who has come to find out whether a thing he heard on a road is true, and that he went back down the road without finding out and without asking, and that about four people in this city have worked out that not asking is the third of the three things he has done in a month and that nobody has ever written down which of the three is the trade.

The case did not go up the road that day. It stood on the table in a room over a market with a docket tied twice to the handle of it and a seal of wet stone under the cloth, and it stood there on the next day and the day after that, and a day it does not go is a day of somebody's season, and the chapter does not say how many there are.
