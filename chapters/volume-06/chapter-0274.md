# Chapter 0274 — Nobody To Hand It To

A man with a piece of paper in the front of his coat walked out of a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house on the nineteenth day of the Short Month, a Tuesday, having been in the room over that market since about the seventh hour of the morning with a company of eleven, and he came out of it with the paper still in his coat. The light was off the low ground and white and the cold was out of the wind and in the boards of the market, and what was under about twenty hands in that market that day was a spade, a barrow, four feet of drain silt at the north end, and a net that a woman was mending between one pail and the next. He had come up that road four days earlier with a date in his coat and had not told anybody in this market what the date was for.

---

He had the paper out and turned about nine inches of it so that the person nearest could see the top of it, and people in that market have said since that a man who turns a piece of paper so that the top of it faces somebody has stopped thinking about the paper and started thinking about the person, and that the two of those are about four days apart in this flood.

"It goes to everybody who will be in the room."

"It goes to everybody who will be in the room four days before the room, and it goes in the person's own words, and it is in writing, and that is the whole of it and there is no fifth and I have not said anything to anybody this morning about there being a fifth." He had the paper flat on his palm. "**That is a piece of paper that can be given away. I have been in this market since the seventh hour and I have not been able to give it away once.**"

"You have had that in your coat since the fourteenth day of that month."

"I have had a piece of paper in my coat since the fourteenth day of that month and the four conditions on it are mine, and the question on it is not mine, and it is in the hand of a person who keeps a stall by a wharf back down that road in this city, and has not given it to me and I am not going to ask her for it, and about four people in this market have worked out that a man who will not ask a woman for a piece of paper has a reason and that the reason is not manners."

The woman of about thirty at the stall had a needle and a net in her hands and had not looked up once in about half an hour, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who goes on mending through the whole of a thing being explained to her has decided in advance what the explanation is worth, and that on this morning the explanation was worth about a quarter of a pail.

"You have not read us the question."

"I am not going to read you the question and I am not going to write it and I am not going to tell you what is in it, and about four people in four hundred miles know that the whole of the fourth condition is that nobody can check where it came from, and I have not come here to break the fourth condition in a market with a barrow in it."

---

The man of about thirty-six put his boot down into the silt at the north end of that market and worked it about nine inches and stopped, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts his boot into four feet of drain silt in the middle of a conversation is a man who has decided that the conversation is going to be longer than it was.

"Whose words are they in."

"**That is the whole of it and I have been asked it by about four people in the last week or so and I have not got an answer that I can use.**" He took the paper off his own hand and held it the way a person holds a thing they have not been able to put down. "There is a woman in this city who keeps a stall by a wharf and can read a clerk's hand and is not a household, and a woman in a village about four hundred miles from here who has been reading clerk's hands off wet sheets in four kitchens since the thirteenth day of that month, and a man in a good coat who has been in a building nine hundred miles away and cannot be the author of a question, and that is four people and every one of the four is in a position where a question in their own words is a question in a column."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the only answer I have and it is not an answer, and I have said it out loud in a market because I have said every other thing I have got out loud in a market this week and about four people in this town have worked out that a man who can only say things in a market is a man who has run out of rooms." He looked at the company of eleven at the north end and at the four of them who had come out of the drain and at the seven who had not. "**And I am telling you that I have got four people in four hundred miles who could write it and not one of the four is in this room, and I am not sending anybody to fetch one, and about nine people in this city have said that is a decision and I am telling you it is.**"

He put the paper back in the front of his coat and did not button the coat, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts a paper away and does not button the coat over it is a man who has not finished with it.

---

"You have come out here to ask us to keep a road for eleven weeks."

"I have come out here to ask three villages whether a road can be kept for eleven weeks out of the days they have put on it, and I have come out here with the one instrument in this flood that has ever worked, and about four people in this city have worked out that I have had the instrument for about nine days and I have not used it once and there is a reason and the reason is this morning." The man of about thirty-six had not looked up. "You keep a place. Eleven of you, and a hearth, and a bench, and two barrows, and a bar that goes round and is not written down. That is a body. And my instrument says the question goes in the person's own words to every person who will be in the room, and I have been sitting in that room since the seventh hour trying to give a piece of paper to a body and I cannot do it, because a body does not have any words of its own, and the eleven words it has are eleven people's, and each one of them is a person, and giving a person a question that belongs to a body is giving the question to nobody."

"Give it to me and I will read it out."

"You are a person and you are not a household, and the question is not in your words, and the second condition is that it is in the person's own words, and if I put it in yours this morning then it is a question you wrote and not a question they wrote, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that that is the same act as asking a clerk to write it and it is the ninth line on a wall in a yard." He had his hands empty. "**I cannot ask a body a question. That is the whole of what has happened today and it has taken me about six hours to find out.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall drew the needle through and put it back without looking at the eye of it, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who sews through a refusal of her own without stopping has been asked nothing and has refused nothing and has not been treated as a person in the four days of an instrument, and that about nine people in that town have noticed that and none of them has said it out loud.

"There is a rule."

"There is a rule and it is about four hundred years old and it has no name and it goes from household to household, and every household that a change touches has to be heard, and it is the only thing in this flood that anybody has ever been able to lean on, and about four people in this market know it without being able to say who told them." The man of about thirty-six got the spade and turned it over. "It has never had anything to say about a body. Nine houses in a lane are nine households and the rule goes into nine kitchens. A company that keeps a place is not a household and it is not a person and the rule has no line for it. **The hole in that rule is exactly the shape of a body, and about four hundred years of people have been walking round it because there were bodies out here before there were Crowns asking anybody for anything.**"

"That is the first true thing anybody has said in this market about a company."

"It is the first true thing anybody has said in this market about anything that I have been on this road for, and I have said the cost of it out loud and you can go back and tell somebody I told you, and I told you in a market because a market is the only room on this road that anybody can find me in." He put the spade down and stepped out of the hole. "**So the rule has a hole in it and the hole is a company, and the way round the hole is a person, and there is exactly one person on this stretch of road who keeps it and who I have not asked anything in about a hundred and forty days and I have not sent anybody.**"

---

He was out of the market by about the second hour of the afternoon and the paper was still in his coat, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who has walked out of a market with a piece of paper he has not been able to give away is a man who has understood something about his own instrument in about six hours that four hundred years of a rule have been walking round, and that about nine people in that town have seen him go and none of them has said what he will do with it.

The paper had gone out on the sixteenth day of that month, a Saturday, which was the fourth day before the Wednesday, which is a thing the instrument requires and is the only reason he was in that market at all, and about four people in four hundred miles have said since that a man who has an instrument that requires four days' notice has a date in his life from the day he writes the instrument down, and that he has not been asked once in nine days whether the dates in his life suit him or anybody else. It had gone out in four places, to a company of eleven, to a company of four, to a woman with a stall, and to a Hearthguard of forty-four out of the outer ward, and about four people in that market have said since that four pieces of paper in four places is a page of its own, and that nobody in four hundred miles could produce all four of them without saying that he had them.

He had found out on the nineteenth day, and not before, that three of the four of them could not answer. A company is a body and a body is not a person. A woman with a stall is a person and a household and a household is nine people and nine people are not a body and are not a question. The fourth of the four had been standing in a room reading a piece of paper in somebody's own hand on the Saturday morning, and about four people in that market have said since that a man of forty-four read it standing up in about the length of a pail being filled and did not sit down, and that he asked one question, and that the question was whether the day suited anybody else, and that the answer he was given was that nobody had been asked, and that he said nothing at all to that.

And the fourth of the four had been standing in a room reading a piece of paper in somebody's own hand on the Saturday morning, and about four people in that market have said since that a man of forty-four read it standing up in about the length of a pail being filled and did not sit down, and that he asked one question, and that the question was whether the day suited anybody else, and that the answer he was given was that nobody had been asked, and that he said nothing at all to that.

There was a room over that market with about nine people in it and a date on the door, and it was four days off on the night of the nineteenth day of that month, and a body was not going to be asked in it, and a person was, and about four people in that town have said since that the whole of the difference between those two things took about four hundred years to arrive at and about six hours to find, and that the man who found it was not the man it was about, and that nobody who was going to be in that room had been asked whether the day suited them, and that one of them had not been asked on the Saturday either and had not asked.
