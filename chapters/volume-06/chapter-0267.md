# Chapter 0267 — Eight Names And One Person

A room over a market in a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house had a date in chalk on the door on the twelfth day of the Short Month, and the day was a Tuesday, and the date was the twelfth day of the Short Month in the hand of a woman of about twenty-four who had come up that road on her own errand and had not been sent by anybody. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures on it belong to that sixty-ninth day and are forty-three days old, and it has stood unwritten forty-two. The wind has been at that wall for thirty-eight days. A case from nine hundred miles away has been shut on a table in a room over a market in this city for sixteen days and has not been carried up that road and has not been opened.

---

There are nine of the people the room was for, and they came in in about nine minutes and sat down on about nine benches and about four of them had come from this city and the other five had not, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody in it could tell from the room which of the nine was which, and that the woman of about twenty-four had asked for no such list and had not wanted one.

The woman of about twenty-four had a slate. She turned it over, wrote the date on the back of it, and put the chalk into her apron, and about nine people in that room have said since that a woman who has written a date and put the chalk away has said that the writing is over and the speaking is starting, and that the two of those are not the same act.

"You are going to read it out."

"**I am going to read out the count and the fifth line and I am not going to read out the other ten, and I would like that entered before I start and not after.**" She had the slate flat on the table in front of her and her hands on the edges of it. "The other ten are about the ground and the workway and the labour and the salt and a share, and not one of them is in question in this room today, and about four people in this city have worked out that the way a page gets to be used is by being read out in a room where it was not needed."

"That is eleven lines and you are reading one."

"That is eleven lines and I am reading one, and the one is the fifth, and the fifth says that a guest may ask to stop being a guest." She looked at the bench on the left-hand side of that room. "**The asking is the whole of it. No reason is asked for and it is not asked twice, and about four people in four hundred miles have never seen that instrument and it is the only one anybody has.**"

The man of about thirty-six of that town put a hand flat on the table and did not speak for about as long as it takes to count nine things, and then he said the name of the gate.

"The gate is ours to name and not yours."

"It is a gate and it is not an office and it is not a district, and there is a bar in it and about four of us take the turn, and you are the man who has the turn this week and you have said the name out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door." She wrote it on the slate. "**There is no word on that line except the name of the gate. A gate is a place. A company is a company that keeps a place, and the place is the whole of the name, and that is not a translation and it is not a class and it is not a word anybody has made up.**"

---

Then nine people said their names in that room, one at a time, in the local language, and the woman of about twenty-four wrote each of them on a line under the name of the gate, one name to a line, and the doing of it took about four minutes, and the fourth minute was the one that mattered, and in the fourth minute a man of about twenty-six in that room stopped looking at the slate.

There was nothing else on any of the nine lines. No class, no word, no heading except the name of a gate, and four people in that room looked for a word above the names for about a minute and a half and did not find one, and the not finding it was the finding.

The fifth of the nine was spelled two ways before the fourth hour of the afternoon, and she was asked about it once and gave the other one, and that was the end of it, and a name said out loud in a room in daylight is the name and not the spelling, and a spelling is a thing a clerk does.

"Nobody in this room can be entered under a name they have not said out loud."

The bench on the left had about nine people on it and none of them had moved in about half an hour, and about four people in that room have said since that a bench of nine people waiting to give a name is the quietest thing that happens in a room where a company is being made.

"That is the whole of how it is done and I have not looked for another way and I am not going to, and about nine people in this city have said since that the not looking is the whole of what this method is, and that the method is about nine days old and has been used twice and has worked twice, and that about four people in this room think two is not a number to be proud of." She turned the slate a quarter round on the table so that the nine lines were square to the edge. "**I would rather have a page that two people have said their names out loud on than a page that nine people are written on, and I have said that in a room before and I have been told by about four people that it is not a method and I have not taken it back.**"

"Where is the word for them."

"**There is no word for them and there is not going to be one made in this room.**" The woman of about thirty at the stall had a needle and a net in her hands and had not looked up once in about half an hour. "The word is *guest*. It has been a word for about a hundred and forty years and it is on the fifth line of a page that eleven lines long, and if anybody in this room invents a better one then a clerk nine hundred miles away will take the better one and use it, and about four people in this city have worked that out in the last ten days and none of them liked it."

"And the wage."

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute, and about nine people in that room have said since that a wage was the thing everybody in it had come for and the thing nobody could put anywhere.

"You cannot post a wage on a page like that one."

"You cannot post a wage on a page like that one, and I will tell you exactly why in three pieces, and not one of the three is that the page is bad. **A wage is an amount, a day to pay on, and a name to pay. That page has the name on it. It has not got an amount on it, and it has not got a day on it, and a page with a name and no amount and no day is not a page a wage can be posted on.**"

"And nobody here has ever been paid by a page."

"Nobody here has ever been paid by a page and nobody here expected to be, and that is the whole of what the Crown's schedule is going to cost, and I want it said out loud in this room, because about four people in a city nine hundred miles down that road have spent ten days being frightened of it." The needle went through the net and came out the other side. "A thing a person has never had does not frighten anybody when it is taken away."

Nobody in that room had said anything, and about nine people in it have said since that the sentence went round that room and came back changed, and that the change was that it was not about a wage at all any more and everybody in the room knew it and nobody said so.

"About nine of the people on this bench have been on the ground and at the salt for two hundred and sixty-seven days, and not one of them has ever had a day to be paid on, and about four of them have had nine months of being told by somebody else what their week is worth. **The Crown's page is the first thing in about nine months that has wanted to give any of them a day to be paid on, and it is going to take about nine months to get it to them through four offices that do not exist, and every person on this bench has been waiting for the other half of that sentence for two hundred and sixty-seven days.**"

"Four offices that do not exist."

"**Four offices that do not exist, and a road that keeps, and about nine hundred miles of water in between, and I would like the man from down that road to write those four things on whatever he has and I would like about four of these people to see him do it.**"

---

At about the fourth hour of the afternoon a man of about twenty-six in that room stood up where he had been sitting since about the tenth hour of the morning, and he gave his own name out loud in the local language in a room in daylight, and the woman of about twenty-four wrote it on a line like the other eight, and then he said that he was not going to be a guest any more, and about four people in that room have said since that the asking was nine words long and that nobody asked him for the ninth.

"Have you said it."

"I have said it and nobody has asked me why and nobody is going to, and about four people in this room know that they are not going to and one of them has counted it." He sat down. "**A guest is the only thing in four hundred miles that a person can end by asking, and I want it entered that the ending goes one way and I did not know until I was in the middle of it that it goes one way.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall put the needle down in the net and did not pick it up again for about a minute and a half, and about four people in that room have said since that this was the only sound in the room for a minute and a half and that nobody in it breathed.

"There is no line for you."

"**Then there is no line for me, and I am not going to ask anybody in this room to make one, and about four people in this city have worked out that a word invented in a room in daylight to put a person into a page is a column.**" He did not look at the wall. "I did not ask for a column. I have got a person instead, and that is a worse thing to carry home and a better thing to have said, and I have worked that out sitting down and I am not going to work it out again."

The man of about thirty-six, who was not on that roll and had said the name of the gate out loud in a room, got up and turned the slate round so that the writing on it faced the bench on the left and not the bench on the right, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who is not on a page can still turn a page round, and that he did it for the man who had just spoken and not for the room.

"You are not on it."

"I am not on it and I am the man who said the name of the gate out loud, and those are two facts about this room and only one of them is on the wall." He put his hand flat on the table again. "**About four people in this village have said that a name at the top of a page with nobody's name under it is a company, and that it is the only kind there is out here, and that the man who named the gate is not in it, and that is correct and it is not a fault and I am not going to be asked about it again.**"

The wall that roll went up on had been a wall with about four nail holes in it and nothing else, and about nine people in that town have said since that a page on a wall in a market is the oldest instrument in this book and that nobody in that town has ever been asked what it is for.

The roll went up on the wall of that room at about the sixth hour of the evening with the name of a gate at the top of it and nine lines under it, of which eight were guests and one was not, and about four people in this city have said since that the number of names on it is nine and the number of classes on it is none, and that a company of eight guests and one person can be counted by anybody who stands in front of the wall, and that it cannot be posted a wage, and that the man of about thirty-six who said the name of the gate out loud is not one of the nine, and that the man of about twenty-six who asked to stop being a guest is, and that the bar in that gate is still on the far side of the road because it is not his week.
