# Chapter 0281 — Not A Second Form

A man of about forty-one in a very good coat came into a room over a market in a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house on the twenty-sixth day of the Short Month, a Tuesday, with nothing whatever in the inside of the coat, and a woman of about twenty-four had put a date on the door of that room in chalk at about the seventh hour of the morning because the room was going to be used. The light came in on the north side and did not get past the middle of that floor before about the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was in the shutters and out of the wind off the low ground, and what was on that table for the whole of the day was a slate, a piece of chalk, a sheet of paper with four places ruled on it and nothing written in them, and a woman of about fifty-one who sold kindling coming in and out of that door with a cart she could not leave standing where it was.

---

He sat down and did not put his hands on the table, and the woman of about fifty-one came in behind him with an armful of birch and went out again, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who arrives with an empty coat and puts both hands flat on a table instead of into his pockets is a man who has come to say a thing he has said before and is deciding whether to say it again.

"You have a sheet in front of you with nothing in it."

"That sheet has been shut in a case on a table in another city since the ninety-sixth day of the Long Month, and the four places on it are a date, a gate, a holder and the days, and all four of them are empty, and nobody has opened the case this morning, because nobody is going to open the case this morning." She put her hand flat on the slate and did not turn it over. "**You have come a long way with an empty coat, and I would like to know what you have come to say before you say any of it.**"

"I have come to say one thing, and it is about a building and not about a road, and it is nine sentences, and I have said all nine of them in a room in daylight with a date on it, and the first of them is that when a form goes back to an office with a place in it left empty, the office does not send the form again."

The woman of about fifty-one came back in with a second armful and set it down against the wall and went out again, and about four people in that market town have said since that a woman who sells kindling and is in and out of a room while the important part of a morning is being said has heard most of it, and that nobody has once asked her what she makes of it, and that about four people in this market have worked out that the quickest way for a thing to be got wrong in a market is for everybody in it to be carrying something.

"That is the first one, and you said it in a room in this city about nine days ago, and one of the people in that room was me, and nothing has happened in this market since, and a thing that has been said once and not picked up is not a rule. It is a thing somebody said."

"That is fair, and I have come back to say the second in the same words, because the first has been lying on that table for about nine days. **The office sends a person.**"

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the man of about forty-one turned his right hand over once on the table and put it back flat, and the light had come as far as the near edge of the slate by then and had stopped there.

"A person is not a page."

"**A person is not a page, and that is the whole of the difference, and I have spent nine hundred miles of road working out what to do about it.**" He still had not put his hands anywhere else. "A page can be ignored, and everybody in this flood has proved that about pages about nine times over, and about four of those times it was on purpose. A person cannot be ignored. A person has to be answered, and to be answered a person has to be spoken to, and a person who is spoken to can say no, and that is the only thing in four hundred miles that a Crown is able to send that anybody out here is able to refuse."

"And the third of the four places wants a person in it."

The woman of about fifty-one put her head round the door with the withy over her shoulder and said that the second village had come down to half a load and would take the rest on a Tuesday, and went out again, and about four people in that market town have said since that a woman who interrupts a room with a load of kindling does it four times a day and has never once been asked to stop, and that about four people in this market have worked out that the fastest way to be a person somebody can talk to in this flood is to be carrying something while they are talking.

"**The third of the four places has to have a person in it, and this city will not put anybody there, and nobody has been asked.**" He did not lower his voice and he did not raise it either. "About four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a column nobody will fill is a column somebody else fills, and I have been in the room where that gets decided, and I am not going to tell you what it looks like in there, and I would like that entered."

"Then say the rest of it, because a man who stops in the middle of a sentence has put the whole weight of it on the person he stopped in front of."

"**I stopped on purpose, and I would like it entered that I stopped on purpose, and about four people in this market have noticed that a man who came nine hundred miles to say nine things got as far as the eighth, and that the eighth was the only one of the nine about a person, and that he put his hand inside the coat when he said it.**"

---

The woman of about twenty-four turned the slate over and wrote the day on the back of it and wrote nothing under it, which is a thing she does, and then she put the chalk down on the table and left it lying there, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who puts a date on the back of a slate and nothing under it has decided that what is said in that room does not belong to the room, and that a woman who then puts the chalk down and leaves it has decided something else besides.

"That is entered, and it is the first of three things I am going to say, and the second of them is the thing you did not bring with you, because you have never stood in nine kitchens in a week." She put two fingers on the corner of the slate. "**A page is answered with a page. A person is answered with a person, and the only instrument in four hundred miles that a person can be answered with is a question in their own words, and it has to reach them four days before the day, and the four days are not a courtesy. The four days are the instrument.**"

"That is the instrument this city built on the one hundred and thirty-seventh day of this flood and has used twice since."

"That is the instrument, and it has four parts, and one of the four is a question written in the words of the person it is about." She did not look at him. "**A question written in the words of the person it is about cannot be written by a person standing in this room, and that is the whole of the difficulty, and there is nothing in four hundred miles that gets round it except legs.**"

The woman of about fifty-one put her head round that door with the withy over her shoulder and said the second village would take the rest of a load on a Tuesday, and the man of about forty-one did not turn round, and about four people in that room have said since that a man in a good coat who does not turn round at a door has worked out that the woman at the door is not the woman he is in there with, and that about four people in this market have noticed that he was right.

"Say the third thing."

"**Nine rooms, nine days, four days of notice, and the first of the nine is the thirty-first day of that month.** The first notice goes out tomorrow, and the first of the nine has not been given its words yet, and the other eight of them are on the far side of that road."

---

The man of about forty-one looked at the door, and the cart went past outside, and the woman of about fifty-one shouted at a boy she could not see, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who looks at a door in the middle of a hard morning is a man who has just heard the part of a thing that costs something, and that the cost is always on the far side of a door. The man of about forty-one took the room back, and he spoke for the rest of that afternoon.

"**A person who comes up this road with a column in her hand is going to be offered the days, and there is nothing in that column for her to put them in except the third place, and nobody here is going to be in the third place, so she will walk to the next gate. There are six gates on that stretch, and at the seventh thing she will write, and what she writes will be correct.**"

That is the ninth sentence and he said it at the same speed as the other eight and did not stop at the end of it, and he shut the coat and did not stand up, and the woman of about twenty-four did not fill in any of the four places, and nobody in that room said the word holder out loud, and about four people in that market town have said since that the eighth of those nine sentences was the only one about a person and the ninth was the only one about a page, and that a man who puts them in that order and then stops has told a market which of the two is going to arrive first.

"You have said nine sentences and I have not heard eight of them."

"**I have said nine sentences and eight of them I have said twice and the ninth I have said once, and I am not going to say a tenth, and about four people in this market have worked out that a man who stops talking has usually said the expensive thing last and not the first, and that this morning it was the first.**"

---

The woman of about twenty-four wrote nine things on a slate in a column and turned the slate round so that the writing faced the man it was about, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who turns a slate so a man can read it has done the opposite of what she did a moment ago with the date, and that about four people in this market cannot work out which of the two was the decision and which of the two was the habit. The woman of about twenty-four took the room back, and the man of about forty-one sat with his hands on the table and let her.

"Thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three, and then the sixth, the seventh and the eighth and the ninth of them, and I have left the fourth and the fifth off the end because those two are the ones nobody has words for yet, and the ninth of the nine is the thirty-ninth, and the thirty-ninth is a Monday." She put the chalk down on the slate and left it lying across the last figure. "**A sheet goes out four days before each of those days, and there are nine sheets, and the first one goes out tomorrow, and if one of them does not go out then that day is a day nothing happens in, and nothing will have gone wrong on it, and there will be no way of telling anybody afterwards which kind of day it was.**"

"And I am to walk to four of those houses before tomorrow is out."

The man of about forty-one turned the slate back so that the writing faced the woman again, which is a thing he had not done all day, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who turns a page of somebody else's writing back towards the person who wrote it has stopped arguing with the writing and started arguing with the writer, and that about four people in this market have worked out that this is what it looks like when a man from nine hundred miles off runs out of other places to say a thing.

"**You are to walk to however many of them will let you stand in their kitchen long enough to be asked, and you are going to have to leave the coat on a chair four times in a day, and a coat is what a person is identified by at about nine hundred paces from where the person is.**"

"Then I will go tonight and not tomorrow, because tomorrow is the day the first sheet goes out."

"**Then you will go tonight, and you will not get four of them, and about four people in this market have said since that a man who tries to be a day early with a thing that takes four days ends up doing it three days early, and that a three-day instrument is not a four-day one and does not fail, it only arrives warm.**"

The chalk was still lying across the last figure on that slate, and the woman of about twenty-four did not pick it up, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who leaves a piece of chalk lying across a figure she has just written has decided that the figure is not finished and that she is not the one who will finish it.

---

The cart came back up the lane at about the third hour of the afternoon and stopped outside, and the knot in the corner of the sacking was still there, and about four people in that market have said since that a knot that is still tied is a load that has changed its mind, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out by now that the only document any of them will get in this flood is a bit of string on a cart, and that nobody has ever asked to see it.

The door of that room still had a date on it in chalk at the end of that day, and the case from nine hundred miles away was still shut on a table in another city with its seal unbroken, and nobody filled in any of the four places, and nobody in that market town could say the name of the person who was coming, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that a man came nine hundred miles to tell a market it is about to be asked a question it will have to answer with a person, and went out of the door with the same nine sentences, and that the ordinary end of a day like that one is that everybody goes back to work, and about four of them did.
