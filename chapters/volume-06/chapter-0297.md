# Chapter 0297 — Nine Days On The Water

A raft went along the side of that road on the forty-second day of the Short Month, a Thursday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, and it was three boards and a beam and about nine feet of nothing under it and it was poled by a man of about twenty-six and a woman of about fifty-nine, and the light came off the water and went up the bank and did not come back down, and the cold was out of the north and in the wet end of the pole, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a pole, a rope, a raft, and a bank with a household standing on it.

---

The man of about twenty-six put the pole in the bed and stopped the raft about two feet short of the bank, and about four people on that water have said since that a man who poles a raft to two feet off a bank and stops is a man who has decided he is not going to arrive at a door, and that there is no word in four hundred miles for a raft arriving and that a person standing on a bank watching one arrive is a person being administered to.

"You have come to the wrong house."

"We have not come to the wrong house, we have come to the ninth one, and there are nine places on this water and this is the ninth, and I have got a list of nine in my head and not on a paper, and the reason there is no paper is the whole of what this morning is." He got the pole up out of the bed and let it stand. "**And I am not asking you anything, and I want that said first, because you have had nine rooms in nine days with men in them who came with papers, and this is not that and it is not the second of that.**"

"Then what is it."

"It is a rule about four hundred years old that nobody has a name for, and it says that a household the change touches gets heard, and I am not going to tell you what the change is until you have been heard, and you are going to be heard whether or not anybody wants to hear you."

---

The woman of about fifty-nine sat down on the beam of the raft and put her hands on her knees and said that she had been on about nine of these and that the thing she had never got used to was the water, and that everything else about a raft was ordinary and the water was not, and about four people on that water have said since that a woman who sits down on a raft the moment it stops is a woman who has decided that the raft is going to be there for as long as it takes, and that nobody had told her how long that was, and that a person with nowhere to be is the cheapest thing in this flood and the only one that cannot be posted.

The man of about twenty-six said the change, and it was two sentences, and the first was that no holding company will be sent west of the Marches gate before the season turns, and the second was that a road north of the Marches is shut and nobody shut it, and the bank did not move and the water went on the other side of the raft.

---

"And if I say it to you and I say it wrong, what happens to it?"

"Nothing happens to it at all, and that is the first thing anybody has to understand about a raft. A person who has been asked what happens to what they say is a person who has been handed a document, and the whole of the difference is that a document has somewhere for it to go." He did not get the pole out again. "**You can say it wrong nine times and the tenth time out of ten it goes in the water, and nobody is going to write down that you said it, and that is the only kind of thing a person will tell the truth to.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-six on that bank said that the first of those sentences had been read out in a room over a market in a city nine hundred miles the other way on the day before, and that a man with a very good coat had read it out and that nobody had signed it, and that she had heard it from a man who had been in that room and that the man had said it was correct.

"Then you know the change and I have still got to hear you."

"**You have still got to hear me, and I have still got to say it, and those are two different things and the second one is four hundred years old and the first one is yours.**" She did not come down to the water. "Nobody has been shut out of anything by that sentence. Nobody has been put in anything. A man read it out in a room and it cost somebody a road and it cost somebody else nothing, and I have had about four days to decide what I think of it and I have not decided, and I am going to say that out loud to a raft because that is what a raft is for."

---

The man of about twenty-six did not write anything down, and a man poling a raft with a list of nine in his head and not on a paper has decided that a rule with no name cannot be kept on a paper, because a paper has a heading and a heading is a name, and this is the first time anybody in four hundred miles has noticed that a rule and an instrument are two different kinds of thing, and about four people in this flood have spent a month trying to get one document to do the work of the other, and a man on a raft has just refused it in about nine words.

"That is the whole of it?"

"That is the whole of it and I have said it to eight places and every one of those eight has said something back, and this is the ninth, and one of the nine is going to say nothing at all, and I am not going to tell you which one and I am not going to tell you the days, and about four people on that water have said since that a raft that goes to nine places on nine days and does not write a day down anywhere is the only instrument anybody has this month that a clerk nine hundred miles off cannot ask to have produced, and that this is the second time in about a fortnight that the answer to that has turned out to be somebody refusing to write something down."

---

The man of about twenty-six shifted the pole two inches in the bed and it did not move the raft, and a man who shifts a pole and does not move the raft is a man who has run out of the nine things he can do with his hands while a person is deciding whether to be honest, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is what a rule with no name looks like from inside a boat, which is that there is nothing to do while somebody is thinking.

"You have said it and I have said it and neither of us has signed anything."

"**Neither of us has signed anything and there is nothing to sign, and I would like it understood that there is nothing to sign.** About four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that a person handed something to sign will look at it, and a person handed nothing has only two choices, and the whole of four hundred years of this rule lives in those two."

"Then I am talking and I have told you a thing and I would like to know what you are going to do with it."

"Nothing, and I am going to the next bank, and I am going to have it in my head for about four hours and then it will be gone, and that is not a failure of an instrument. That is what it is for." The woman of about fifty-nine had got the rope round her hand and was not using it. "**A thing you can show somebody is a thing you have to be right about, and being right about a thing you are going to lose in four hours is a different sort of being right.** About four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the second of those is the one anybody has ever actually relied on."

---

A man of about sixty-eight came out onto the bank from behind a stack and listened to about nine of those words and then went back in, and about four people on that water have said since that a man who comes out of a door, hears nine words, and goes back in is a man who has decided that a thing he has heard nine words of is not a thing he is going to have an opinion about before the ninth place, and that this is what a household affected by a change is, and that a household is not obliged to have a view and that being obliged to have a view is a document.

"He has been heard."

"He has been heard and he has not said a word and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that being heard and saying nothing are two different things and that the second of them is the one nobody can enter anywhere, and that a register with nine days in it and nothing under them in a city nine hundred miles the other way is a very large version of the same instrument, and that neither of them has a name and that is not a fault."

---

The man of about twenty-six pushed off and got the pole in again and the raft went on down the side of the bank, and the woman of about fifty-nine got up off the beam and dried her hands on her own skirt and did not look back at the bank, and about four people on that water have said since that a woman who does not look back at a bank she has just left is a woman who has decided that the ninth of nine is finished and that a raft that looks back is a raft that has not finished.

"You said it was not a treaty."

"It is not a treaty and I am not going to call it one, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a thing which holds because nine people came to a raft is not a treaty, and that a treaty is two things with a line between them and there is no line on this water and there is no second side of it, and that the only party to it is nine places and about nine people, and that nobody has signed it and nobody is going to."

"Then what is it."

"**It is a rule four hundred years old that nobody has a name for, and a raft, and nine days, and a household at each one, and that is all of it.** It is not on a page and it is not going to be on a page, and it is not going to be replaced by anything a document can do, and I have poled about nine hundred yards of water this morning to say that to one bank."

---

The raft itself was three boards and a beam and it had been put together at the top of that water on the first day of the second month of this flood by four men who had nothing else to do with a week, and one of the four had cut the boards and the other three had nailed them, and there is no page anywhere in four hundred miles with the word raft on it and a date against it, and about four people on that water have said since that the only instrument in this flood that has cost anybody nothing at all is nine feet of board and a pole and about two people who are not being paid and are not asking, and that a ceiling of nine hundred miles has sent four kinds of thing to this city this month and the cheapest of them by a long way is the one nobody ordered.

The pole had been in the bed and out of it about nine hundred times by then and the woman of about thirty-six on that bank had watched all of it, and she went in after they had gone and shut the door and did not stand at it, and about four people on that water have said since that a woman who shuts a door behind a raft and does not stand at it is a woman who has decided that a raft is not something a person waits at a door for, and that anybody who waits at a door for a document is waiting for a different sort of thing entirely.

And about four people on that water have said since that a raft went to nine places on nine days and that only this one of the nine has a day anybody can name, and that nobody has written the other eight down and that nobody is going to, and that the sentence dated at an office nine hundred miles off for this morning has been read out in a room in a city nine hundred miles the other way and has been carried up a road in a case and has come down to a bank in a raft, and that the two of those are not joined to each other by anybody, and that a road north of the Marches is shut and a road west of it will not be kept till the season turns, and that the rule that got somebody onto that raft is older than both of the roads and has never once had to be written down, and that a man poled it with a list of nine in his head and gave the whole of it away in about nine words and went on to the next bank.
