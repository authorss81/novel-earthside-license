# Chapter 0257 — A Safe Route

A second sheet came out of the inside of a good coat at about the second hour of the afternoon on the second day of the Short Month and went onto a table in the room over a market, and there is a line on that sheet about keeping the road while the schedule is made up, and about four people in that room can read that line and the rest of them cannot, and the man who cannot read it is the one who has to decide whether it is safe. The wind has been at the wall of the store on the upper road for twenty-eight days. The board above that door carries the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month, which are thirty-three days old, and has not been written on since the day after them.

---

The man of forty-one in a very good coat put the sheet down flat and squared it to the edge of the table with two fingers, and he did not read it, and about four people in that room who could read it looked at him not reading it.

"You have brought a second page."

"I have brought a second sheet of the same thing. It is the fourth of five and the fifth is a schedule with the four places ruled on it and nothing written in them, and the fourth is the one with the instructions on it, and the instructions are the part that matters and the instructions are four lines long and about four of us can read them."

"Read them out."

"**I will read them out and I am going to read them out in the order they are written, and I am asking one thing of this room first, and the thing is that nobody in it is to say the word safe until it has been read out twice, because about four people have already got it in their mouths and none of them has read it.**"

The four of them who could read that line did not look at each other while it was being asked for, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had noticed that at the time and had not known what to call it.

"Whose word is it."

"It is nobody's. That is the difficulty. It is on the sheet and it is not attached to anything. It is the only word on that sheet that is not attached to anything, and a word on a page with nothing attached to it does more work than a person does, and I have watched that happen four times in four hundred miles and I am not going to watch it a fifth time in a room with two hundred and forty people outside it."

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board was in that room and had not spoken in it. He had come up the road at about the first hour and had sat down against the wall by the step at the back where the light does not reach, and he had not written anything, and about four people in that room had looked at him about nine times and had not asked him what he was there for. At about the fourth hour of the afternoon he got up, went to the door, stood in it, and looked down the road for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then came back in and sat down again in the same place and did not say one word about having gone, and about four people in that room have said that he went to that door to be looked at rather than to look at anything.

---

He read the four lines out in the local language, and the woman of about twenty-four wrote them down in the order he read them, which is a trade she has had since the second month of this flood and about which she has never once been asked to give an account of herself.

"There are two ways to keep a road open while a schedule is made up."

"There are two ways."

"**One of them is safe.**"

"Say more than that."

The woman of about twenty-four turned her slate over, wrote the date on the back of it, and wrote nothing else under it, and about four people in that room saw her do it and none of them asked her what she had left out.

"**Eleven weeks of the days of three villages on the gates of the road itself, so that the road is kept while the schedule is made. That is the first way and it is the one this sheet recommends, and it is in the Crown's own words and I am reading the Crown's words and not mine, and there is a word at the front of it that this sheet does not define, and I have looked for the definition on four of the five sheets.**"

"There are five sheets."

"There are five sheets and the definition is not on any of them, and about four people in this room have understood that before I said it and about nine have not."

"Whose days."

The wind had got into that room under the door and was moving the edge of the sheet on the table, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had stopped noticing the sheet move and had started noticing where the draught came from.

"**The days of the households of three villages.**"

"Explain the rest of it."

"**A rack stands in a channel. A channel takes its water from a gate. A rack that has not been worked does not come off.**"

"Go on."

A man at the back of that room had a hand round the leg of his chair, and about four people in that room have said since that he put it there about the second hour and did not take it off in the other eight.

"**Eleven weeks of a channel without a household's water is eleven weeks of a salt season that is not recovered in this flood or the next one. That sentence is mine, and I have said it in two rooms, and I am not going to soften it in this one.**"

"It is a hundred and forty years old."

"The mechanism is a hundred and forty years old. The sentence is four days old and it is mine, and about four people in this city have said that I do not have a second sentence."

"You have put your own sentence in the Crown's four lines."

Somebody at the back of that hall said something to somebody else and the somebody else did not answer, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board looked at the door for it and then looked back at the table.

"**I have read the Crown's four lines and I have said one thing of my own about what they do, and the two are separate, and anybody in this room is free to write them on two lines and I would be obliged if somebody would.**"

The woman of about twenty-four did. She wrote the two words *safe* and *villages* on two lines of a wall in a room over a yard, and she did not join them up, and about four people in that room wanted them joined up and about nine did not, and the wall is nine feet from a trough and about four feet from a door and nobody has washed either line since.

About four people in that room were frightened and about four of them were not, and the man of forty-one in a very good coat was the only one of the nine who was not frightened at all. Nobody asked him how he knew what was on the fourth sheet. He has read a second sheet to about nine people in two days and four of them are frightened and it will be in their mouths by the end of the week and in nobody else's, and about four people in this city have said that this is what it costs a man to be the one who has been in the building, and that the cost is that he is now the only person in four hundred miles who cannot be checked by anybody in the room he is standing in.

"Have you read this sheet."

"I have read it."

"You have said that in four words and it is the whole of your answer and I am going to let it stand, because you have been in the building where the first one was signed and I have not, and that is the whole of what either of us has."

"Have you been asked what the second way is."

The man of about thirty-nine came in out of the doorway and sat down in the same place without being asked to, and about four people in that room have said since that he had not been outside the room so much as in the doorway of it.

"I have not been asked the second way and the second way is on the fifth sheet with the four places ruled on it and nothing in them, and the second way is that somebody keeps the road without anybody's days moving, and I have not read that one out because I do not know who is supposed to be in it."

---

About the sixth hour of the afternoon the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register entered that second reading on a second slate in that room, in the order he had read it, and she entered the four lines and then she stopped, and about four people came to look at it and one of them said out loud that there was no date at the head of it.

"The sheet does not have one."

"**The sheet does not have one and I am not going to put one on it.**"

"Explain that."

"**A reading entered under a date is a reading somebody can look up. This one came from nine hundred miles away with a date on a docket and not on the instructions, and I have been the account of documents in this city for a long time, and I am not going to start making them easier to find than they are.**"

The second slate was turned round on that table so that the writing on it faced the man of about thirty-nine, and he read four words of it upside down and did not say that he was doing it.

"You are the only person here who has understood that."

"I am not the only person here who has understood it. I am the only person here who is going to be the one who wrote it down, and those are two different things, and about nine people in this city keep not knowing which of the two they are looking at."

Nobody in that room put a hand on the fifth sheet except to point at a place on it, and about nine people in this city have said since that a sheet nobody touched is worth more than a sheet nine people have touched, and that this is the second time in four days that the better thing and the honest thing have been the same one.

The fifth sheet lay on the table all evening with the four places ruled on it and nothing written in them, and about nine people looked at it and about four of them put a finger in one of the four places and took it out again without marking anything, and it is entered that nobody in that room wrote in any of the four, and that the four places on it are in the same order as the four places on the docket, and that a person who has seen both of them has seen a form with a column in it and nothing else.

"Nobody in this room can say what eleven weeks is."

"Eleven weeks is eleven weeks."

"**Eleven weeks is a figure in words and not a number of days, and I am not going to convert it, and I am not going to give a reason, and about four people in this room have already worked out that I have got a reason.**"

"You have got a reason."

Somebody wrote eleven weeks on the back of their own hand and then put the hand in a pocket, and about four people in that room have said since that they have each done that about nine times in nine weeks and that not one of them has said the number out loud.

"I have got a reason and I have had it since the first day of the Long Month and it is not going in a book. What I have got in a book is that a rack stands in a channel, and a channel takes its water from a gate, and a rack that has not been worked does not come off. That is on a page at this table and it is a hundred and forty years old and it is the only thing about this that anybody in this room can check."

"Who is going to ask about the eleven weeks."

"Nobody in this room. The person who has to ask is a person and not a household, and a household is nine days of work and a person is one, and I have been in a room about four times this flood where a person was asked to do a household's work and the answer was no in about four seconds and it was correct."

"Who is the person."

The man of forty-one in a very good coat put his hand inside his coat, checked something in there, and took his hand out again, and nobody in that room remarked on it.

"**Alys Kern. She keeps a gate on the road west, up past the last of the houses out here, and the stretch of that road with houses on it is called the Hearth Marches, and that is the name on the fifth sheet and it is the only name on any of the five that is the name of a place rather than the name of an office.**"

"Is she in this city."

"She is up the road past this city and she is the person who would have to ask, and nobody has said her name to her, and I have not sent anybody to say it to her, and about four people in this city have assumed that somebody was sent and none of us sent anybody."

---

The room over the yard with the two words on the wall of it is a room that two hundred and forty people cannot hear into, and the two words have been on that wall since about the seventh hour of the evening, and the wall is nine feet from a trough and about four feet from a door, and on the ninety-fourth day of that month a man of about twenty-seven stood about nine feet back from a wall nine inches above a board and looked at two pages together and said he would rather be looking at the board, and about four people who were in this room on the second day of the Short Month have noticed that the two words on the other wall are a different colour from the chalk on that one and have not said so.

At about the ninth hour of the evening that room emptied, and the sheet went back inside the good coat, and the man of forty-one in a very good coat checked the inside of it with his hand before he put it back on the hook by the door, and he did not say to anybody in that room how he knew what was on the fourth sheet, and he was not going to, and the not-saying was the last thing he did in that room and it was a hand going into a coat and coming out again and nothing else, and about four people who were still on the landing have said since that they have watched a man keep a thing in a coat for two days and that neither of them asked him what it was, and that if you had asked him he would have said he had read it.
