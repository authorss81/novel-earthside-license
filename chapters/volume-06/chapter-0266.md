# Chapter 0266 — What A Household Has

A kitchen at the low end of a channel nine hundred paces past a market town had no paper in it on the eleventh day of the Short Month, and the day was a Monday, and there is a room in this world in which nothing has ever been written and that room is a kitchen and this was one of them. The light came in at one window about three feet off the floor and there was nowhere in that kitchen to stand out of it. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures on it belong to that sixty-ninth day and are forty-two days old, and it has stood unwritten forty-one. The wind has been at that wall for thirty-seven days. A case from nine hundred miles away has been shut on a table in a room over a market for fifteen days and it is four hundred miles from this kitchen, and nobody in this kitchen has heard of it.

---

The man of about forty-three got up off the bench at about the fifth hour of the morning to put the pot on and got as far as being upright and then had to sit down on the edge of the bench with his left arm held against his chest in the position it had been in since about two o'clock on Sunday afternoon, and about four people in that village have said since that a man who gets up to do a thing he could have done sitting down is a man who has not worked out yet what he is for this week.

"The fire wants feeding."

"**The fire has wanted feeding since the fifth hour of the morning and it will want feeding at the ninth hour as well, and I can feed it sitting down with my right hand, and I want it entered that I got up because I have got up at the fifth hour of every morning for about nine years and a man does not find out at the fifth hour of a Monday that he is somebody else.**" He said it in the voice a person uses in a room where they are going to be all day.

She put the pot on sitting down. She had been doing a great deal of that kitchen's work sitting down since about the third hour of the afternoon on Sunday and about four people in that house have said since that a household with one pair of hands finds out in about a day that half of what it does is sitting down, and that the half it finds out about is the half it had never counted.

The woman's hands went to a sieve and a board and then to a pot and then back to the sieve, and about nine people who have been in that kitchen since Sunday have said that the sieve was in her hands for about six hours of the Monday and that the sieve was not the point of the day and that a person who works with a sieve for six hours is a person who is not asking herself anything.

---

"You are going to be no use for nine days."

The arm went back against his chest and the sleeve of it had to be turned up over the coat, and about four people in that house have said since that a sleeve turned up over a coat is the whole of what there is to be said about a Monday.

"**I am going to be no use for nine days and I know the figure better than you do, because I counted it in the hole.**" He turned the arm a quarter turn and stopped when it went wrong. "**Nine days is what the man from the town said on Saturday and he said it before any of us started and he has not moved it since, and I have not moved it either, and about four people in this village have worked out that two men who have never spoken have the same number.**"

"He said nine days about a post."

She put the sieve down and picked it up again and did not do anything with it in that stretch, and about nine people in that house have said that the two of them said nothing for about a minute and that the minute was not a quiet anybody enjoyed.

"**He said nine days about a post and about hands and he said it in a lane,** and I have been in a hole in a channel since about the seventh hour yesterday morning and I have thought about very little else, and it is the same nine days." He put the arm back against his chest. "What I cannot do is lift with the left and there is no left in this house this week, and the rack at the low end is a week old and it does not come off, and about nine hundred buckets went into a hole on Sunday, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of this household and I have just said all of it out loud for the first time."

"You have said all of it out loud for the first time."

The light at that window had by then come off the lane and lay across the boards between them, and about four people in that house have said since that two people disagreeing about who ought to be told what are at their easiest in a room with a bar of light across the floor and that neither of them said so.

"I have said it out loud in my own kitchen to my own wife, which is about the nine hundred and ninety-ninth time it has been said in this house, and it is the first time it has been said in a voice." He looked at the window. "**And that is the whole difference, and I want it said back to me by somebody who was not in the hole.**"

"Who am I going to say it to."

Somebody in the lane called to somebody else and the somebody called back and neither of them came to the door, and about four people in that village have said since that a kitchen at the low end of a channel is the only room in nine houses where a person can hear the whole lane and cannot see a hand's width of it.

"You are going to say it to the woman of the fourth house up the lane, and you are going to say it before the middle of the day, and you are going to say it with the nine days in it, because there is a man in this village who has been carrying nine days of water up a pail line since the tenth hour of the morning and who does not know that the two of you have the same number." He turned the arm a quarter turn and stopped when it went wrong. "Four hundred miles of road and about nine months of somebody's season and a page has come up that river wanting the days, and the days are in this kitchen and they have been in this kitchen since before it, and nobody in four hundred miles has asked us for them and I am not going to be the one who starts."

"You have not started."

"I have not started, and I am not going to, and if you want to know what our days are you can ask me a month from now and I will tell you the whole of them, and I am telling you now that the reason is not that it is ours. The reason is that the moment the days of a household are said to a person with a slate, they are a thing that has been said, and a thing that has been said can be written down, and I have been keeping my own counts in my own head for about nine years and nobody has ever wanted them and I have never once had to defend them."

---

She put the sieve down on the board and left it there, which is the only time in the whole of that Monday that her hands were not on something, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who puts a sieve down in the middle of a Monday has decided that the sieve is finished with.

"Then I will tell you what our days are not, and you can take it to whoever wants them, and it is the only thing I am going to say about them."

"Go on."

The boy of about eleven was still against the wall with the yoke on it and did not come in, and about four people in that house have said since that a child standing in a doorway for four hours is the cheapest clock in the world.

"**A household's days are not a schedule.** A schedule is a set of days with a thing held against them, and every day in it is the same as every other day in it, because that is the whole reason for the page it is on." She picked the sieve up again about two minutes later. "Our days are a set of things that happen in a set order, and the order moves with the weather and with how many hands we have and with whose child is awake, and on a day like today our order is different from last Monday and it will be different again on Thursday, and there is no way a person could stand at the end of a week and put a number against Monday and have it be right on Monday."

"That is not a number of days."

The pot on that hearth had been off the boil for the better part of a quarter of an hour, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who lets a pot go cold while she is saying the truest thing of her week is a woman who thinks the pot will wait, and that it will, because it is Monday.

"**That is not a number of days, and that is the whole of what I have.** I can tell you what we do on a good week. I cannot tell you what we do on Monday, and the distance between those two sentences is about four hundred years long and I did not learn it anywhere." She set the sieve against the side of the pot so that it would not fall. "**And I am telling you that on a Monday with no paper in this kitchen, and I am not going to be told that I have said something clever.**"

"You have said something that is going to be on a page in about four months."

"Then it will be on a page with no name on it, because I have not given you one and I have not given you a household, and about four people in this village have said since that the woman from the fourth house up the lane gave nine buckets to this house on Sunday and would not say so twice, and that is what I am doing now."

A boy of about eleven came in at about the eighth hour with two pails on a yoke and stood them down against the wall and did not say that he had carried them, and about four people in that house have said since that a boy who puts two pails down and says nothing about carrying them is a boy who has worked out that the household knows.

---

The woman of about thirty at the stall came down the lane at about the tenth hour with the butt on her head again, and she put it down in the lane outside the fourth house and came in without being asked, and she sat down on the bench at the far end of the kitchen, which is not a thing anybody does in a nine-house village unless they have been coming to that house for about a hundred days.

"I am not here about the arm."

"Everybody who came into this kitchen on Monday is about the arm. The man from the town came at the seventh hour with the barrow and the woman from the fourth house up the lane came at the ninth and I have had four people and about nine children in this kitchen since the fifth hour of the morning, and every one of them came about the arm, and about four of them brought something." She did not look up from the sieve. "**What have you come about.**"

She had her hands on her knees the whole of that and about four people in that kitchen have said since that a woman who arrives with a thing to say and puts her hands on her knees instead of into her lap is a woman who has decided to say it to one person and not to a room.

"I have come about Sunday. I have not said anything to anybody since about the second hour of the afternoon yesterday and I have turned over nine things in my head and I have not said any of them, and I am going to say about two of them to you and not to him, because he was in the hole and I was not, and a person who was in the hole does not need a person who was not in the hole to tell her what happened in it." She put her hands on her knees. "The pail line ran from the eighth hour until about the fourth and it ran itself. About nine of us. It was not organised and it was not written down and it was not mine, and I would like it entered in whatever it is you keep that I am not going to be shown, that on the tenth day of the Short Month nine houses moved about nine hundred buckets of water by hand and not one person in this village was told to and not one person in this village wrote it down."

"You want it entered."

"**I want it to have happened, and I have just found out that those are about four different things** and that about nine people in this village have been treating them as one for a hundred and forty days." She got up and went to the door and put the yoke up on her shoulder with the two empty pails on it. "I am going to carry the pails back up the lane and I am not going to be thanked for that either, and if he cannot lift with the left then one of mine is going to come down this lane every morning and it is not going to be said out loud, and that is all of it."

She went up the lane with the empty pails and about four people in that village have said since that a woman carried an empty yoke up a lane and left a full one standing against a wall, and that nobody in the fourth house said anything about it, and that the fourth house put the full one straight to use.

The kitchen had no paper in it at the seventh hour of the evening. The week's water was coming down a pail line that nobody had ordered. The rack at the low end of that channel was a week old and a hundred and forty years ago somebody said that a rack that has not been worked does not come off, and about four people in four hundred miles have said since that the first household in this book to be asked about its own days was not asked by anybody, and that it said all of them out loud in its own kitchen to its own wife and to a boy with a yoke, and that about four hundred miles away there is a room in which a page is waiting for them.
