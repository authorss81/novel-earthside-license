# Chapter 0278 — One Barrow And Two Men

The light off the low ground on the twenty-third day of the Short Month, a Saturday, was white and flat and did not warm the wet boards of the market out past the last named house, and the cold was in the boards and in the iron of the barrow and out of the wind, which had been at the wall of a store nine miles up the road from this city for forty-nine days out of the two hundred and twenty-ninth day of this flood, and what was under about three pairs of hands at the north end of that market from the seventh hour of the morning until the light went was a spade, one barrow, a boot, and about nine feet of drain that had been silted to a depth of about four feet on the Tuesday and was not a third of the way through on the Sunday. Nobody had been asked to be there and one man had not turned up on the Friday and did not turn up on the Saturday either, and people in that town have said since that a man who does not turn up on one day and does not turn up on the next has made a decision and a man who makes a decision about a drain on a Saturday has usually made it in a lane.

---

He had the barrow out of the drain by about the eighth hour and the first load on it was four times what it should have been, because the silt had gone to the consistence of a loaf where it had been standing since the Tuesday and the spade came up in plates about the size of a hand, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who loads a barrow four times over on the first morning of a drain that nine people are used to is a man who has decided that the barrow is going to be a problem and has not said so.

"That is the wrong end of it."

"That is the end that has been standing since the Tuesday and it is the end that nobody has stood in, and about four of the nine turned up on this drain on the Friday about a week ago because it was there to stand in, and I am not going to be the man who says the Friday was a holiday." He put the spade in and the plate came up and broke against the edge of the barrow and half of it went over the side onto the boards. "**Move up. If you are going to stand there you are going to stand at the end of the barrow and not at the end of the drain, and about four feet of that came out of the boards this morning and about nine days of work went with it, and it will come off the boards and it is not coming back on.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall was at the end of the barrow because she had come down at about the seventh hour with a butt on her head and had not been asked and had not said anything about why, and people in that market have said since that a woman who comes to a drain with a butt at the seventh hour on a Saturday after being told on the Friday that four of the nine are not coming has come for a reason that is her own, and that nobody asked her, and that she did not say.

"I have not been paid this week."

"Nobody has been paid this week and four of the nine have stopped coming and you have come, and I have worked out nine reasons for that and one of them is that you got nine houses to run a pail line on the tenth day of that month and you have not stopped since. **I am not going to be the man who says that out loud to a woman with a butt on her head.**" He went back into the bed. "Get the other end and do not lift it with your back." He went back into the bed. "Get the other end and do not lift it with your back, and about four people in this market have said that is a thing a person says to a woman about nine times a week and about four of the times she does it anyway."

---

They worked the north end for about two hours and it went at the rate of about four feet of drain a day between them, which is about twice what he had said and about a fifth of what nine people had been doing on the Tuesday, and about four people in that market have said since that two people on a Saturday with a barrow that a boy had already knocked a wheel against a ridge is about twice one person and about a fifth of nine, and that the arithmetic in between is the whole of what happened out past the last named house that weekend, and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever put a fraction in a schedule.

"You are going slower than you were on the Thursday."

"I am going slower than I was on the Thursday because on the Thursday I was doing about a fifth of a day and calling it about a fifth, and people in this market have said that a man who has been doing a fifth of a day's work for two days and calling it a fifth is a man who has not counted how much is left." He turned a plate over with the back of the spade. "There is about two thirds of that drain left in this bed and there are about nine days of it in front of me and about four of the nine people who used to be at it are somewhere else on a Friday, and that is not a complaint, it is a drain."

"Do you want me to go and get somebody."

"I do not want you to go and get anybody. **If you go and get anybody I will not be angry and I will not be glad, and about four people in this market have said that a man who cannot be angry and cannot be glad about a thing like that is a man who has already worked out that going and getting somebody is the last thing anybody should do at a drain on a Saturday.**" He put the spade down and stood up in the bed. "They are right, and I worked it out on Friday and not this morning, and I would like that said."

---

The first thing to go was the barrow, and it went at about the eleventh hour with a fourth load on it, and it went on the wet boards at the top of the drain with a wheel against a ridge that had been left there by a barrow on the Friday, and people in that market have said since that a thing which breaks on the second day is a thing which was going to break on the first, and that a barrow belongs to eleven people and a wheel that has been in silt for four days is a wheel that has been in a channel for four days, and that nobody in four hundred miles can put that on a page.

Four feet of wet silt went across nine feet of boards. The woman of about thirty at the stall got a hand under the near end of the frame and did not lift it and said one word, and the man of about thirty-six got the far end and turned it about a quarter turn, and the two of them got the barrow up out of its own mess in about four minutes and about nine feet of silt was on that market's boards and the drain was not a third of the way through.

"That is the end of that."

"That is the end of that barrow for the week and it is not the end of the drain." He put the spade back in. "I have been up this lane about nine days of this flood and I have never had a barrow go and I have watched about four other men have one go, and every one of them went on a load that was too heavy, because a man alone puts more on than nine men put on together. **That is not a thing anybody learns from a book, because there are nine pages about a barrow in four hundred miles and none of them is this one.**"

"Get two loads instead of four."

"Get two loads instead of four and get four of the nine days instead of one, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of this market on a Saturday and I have said it out loud. **About four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the arithmetic of one man and one barrow is worse than the arithmetic of nine men and one barrow, and the only reason it is worse is that nobody tells you, and I am telling you now.**"

The woman of about thirty at the stall went down the lane with the butt after that and came back at about the third hour of the afternoon with a barrow of her own off the top of a house at the end of the market, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who has been in a drain for six hours and then goes and gets a barrow has decided that the second barrow is the only argument left, and that about nine people in that town have watched a woman get a second barrow this month and about four of them have understood what it was for.

"That one has no wheel."

"**That one has no wheel and it will go about nine feet and then it will not, and we are going to use it for the silt at the top and not for the bed, and people in this market have said that a barrow with no wheel is a barrow that four people can lift, and that is the only advantage it has got and we are going to get all of it.**" She set it down and did not take her hands off the handles. "I carried a yoke up a lane in the second week and nobody said thank you and I have never asked, and I am not carrying this for a wage and there is not a wage and I want that said at the north end of this market by a man with a bar."

"You want it said."

"I want it said out loud in a market in daylight and I do not want it on anything, and about four people in that market have said that the only two things a woman can be out there are a favour and a wage and she would like it entered that she has picked one, and about nine people in four hundred miles could not name a third." She did not let go of the handles. "**And I am not going to be thanked for the second barrow either, and if one of the nine comes back on a Monday I am going to hand them the end of it and I am not going to say anything.**"

A carter's boy of about fifteen came through the gate at about the second hour of the afternoon going the other way, stopped for about a minute, put his shoulder against the end of the barrow, got it round the ridge and back onto boards he had not seen, and went on down the lane without saying anything to anybody, and people in that market have said since that a boy of about fifteen who puts his shoulder to a barrow for about a minute and then goes on his way is a boy who has been going up that lane since the first week and knows the ridge, and that about four people in that town could not tell you his name.

The bar went on the near side of the road at about the third hour of the afternoon and the man of about thirty-six put it there himself, and the woman of about thirty at the stall filled the sill that had come out of the boards with the butt and did not sweep the boards and nobody asked her to and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who wets nine feet of boards and does not sweep them has decided that a wet board is a board that a boot can stand on and a dry board is not.

The man of about forty-three of the fourth household, nine hundred paces up the lane, was not at that market on the Saturday and was not expected to be, and people in that lane have said since that a man whose left arm came back on the sixteenth day of that month can lift a sieve and about four buckets and cannot lift a barrow, and that he is at the low end of a channel with a sieve and about four buckets a day, and that about four of the nine houses in that lane have worked out that the fastest way for a thing to happen there is for it to be nobody's business, and that it is the same sentence a woman at a stall has been saying since the tenth day of that month and she has not been asked about it and has not been thanked, and that about four feet of a drain at the north end of that market was cleared on that Saturday by two people and a barrow with a wheel in it and about two thirds of it was still in the bed when the light went off the low ground, and that nobody in that town put a day on it, and that about nine people in four hundred miles could have said how long it would take and about four of them had asked and had been told not to go and get anybody.
