# Chapter 0299 — A Line Under An Orchard

A gate on a road that comes down from the north side of the Marches was open on the forty-fourth day of the Short Month, a Saturday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, and a woman of about fifty was at it with a bar of ash and a wooden hook and her other hand in the front of her coat, and the light came in over the shoulder of the hill and did not reach the gate, and the cold was out of the north and in the iron of the hook, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a bar of ash, a hook, a barrow of boards, a date in chalk on a gate post, and about nine hundred paces of orchard with a straight line in the ground under it that had been there before she kept that gate.

---

She put the bar of ash into its two brackets and took her hand out of her coat and put the key into her apron pocket, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who takes her hand out of her coat in front of a stranger and then puts the key somewhere she can reach it is a woman who has decided that the day is going to be a working day and that a working day does not need a coat.

The man of about thirty-eight came up the lane with his barrow and stopped at the same near end and did not turn round, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has stopped at the same near end of the same gate every morning since the fortieth and has not turned round is a man who has accepted the arrangement and is waiting to see whether it holds, and that he did not ask her for the barrow this time and neither of them mentioned the road being shut.

---

"You are not going up it."

"I have a load of boards for a house on this side of the hill and I have had that load since the fortieth and I am not going up the road for it, and I have stopped explaining myself to this gate and I would like to be allowed to keep not explaining myself to it."

"You are allowed to and nobody has ever asked you to explain yourself to a bar." She had the hook in her hand and had not used it. "**And I am not going up it either and there is a thing about that road I have not said out loud to anybody, and I have had since the Thursday, and I am not going to say it today, and you are going to stand there and not ask me about it, and I would like it entered that a man stood at a gate and did not ask.**"

---

The orchard was planted in the year the second gate went up that road, and about four people in that lane have said since that the man who planted it is dead, and nobody in this flood has said his name out loud, and a gate and an orchard went up at the same time on the same stretch of road by two different arrangements of people, and one of them has a bar of ash in a bracket and the other one has a straight line under it that nobody put there, and a thing a person built and a thing nobody built can stand nine hundred paces apart in the same field, and this flood has no instrument at all that will tell them apart.

---

The man of about thirty-eight looked up at the hook and then at her and then at the barrow, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has looked at a hook and then at a woman and then at a barrow in that order has decided that the thing he wants to know is in the yard behind her and not on the road in front of him, and that he was right about that and that he did not know he was right.

"I am not going to ask you."

"You are not going to ask me and I am going to tell you one thing about it and then I am going to stop, and I want you to notice that I am telling you, and that is different from being asked, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that those two are the only two things anybody has ever had in this flood."

---

There was an orchard about nine hundred paces off that road, behind a low wall with a gap in it, and it had been an orchard before the water and it was an orchard now, and the trees were about nine years old and planted in rows that a person could see, and under them the ground was not level and had never been leveled, and across the middle of it, from the near end of the rows to a stone at the far end that is not a stone anybody put there, there was a straight line in the ground about a foot wide and about two inches deep, and it was not a path and it was not a watercourse and it was not a furrow and it was not a boundary and it did not follow a row of anything, and it was straight, and the ground either side of it was exactly as uneven as the ground a yard off.

"I have been looking at that for about three days."

"You have been looking at it and you have not gone and stood in it."

"**I have not gone and stood in it and I am not going to, and I have not put anything in it, and I have not had anybody else put anything in it.** There is a man on the flats about eleven miles the other way whose whole trade is putting things in holes, and nobody has sent for him and nobody is going to, and I would like you to notice that I have made that decision on my own and out loud and without asking anybody."

---

The bar of ash shifted in its brackets in the wind and went about two fingers and stopped against the post, and a bar of ash in two brackets at the top of a hill in the north is the only thing on that road still doing the job it was made to do, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that this is the second time this flood that a thing nobody has looked at has turned out to be the honest one.

"And why not."

"**Because he would say no in about four seconds and he would be right, and because there is nothing at that gate this month that needs a man to say yes.**" She put the hook down against the post and stood on the step that was there. "And I could not tell you what is under it and neither could he, and a man who is asked to put a rod in something he has not been told the name of is being asked to agree with whoever asked, and the first thing anybody needs in this flood is not to be asked to agree with anybody about a thing nobody has a name for."

The man of about thirty-eight did not say anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who hears a thing he has never heard of named as nothing and does not make a joke about it is a man who has understood, at some distance and about four hours late, that a woman has just told him the shape of the whole flood in about nine sentences and has not used a single word that could be written down.

---

"He is not going to dig it."

"He is not going to dig it and neither is anybody and nobody is going to measure it, and I am not going to, and if somebody comes up here with a rope and a chain and a bar about nine months from now and asks me what is in that orchard, I am going to tell them what I have told you, which is that there is a line in the ground and I do not know what it is."

"You might know what it is in nine months."

"I might, and I would be a different woman on that morning, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that the whole of what is wrong with this flood is everybody waiting to be somebody else in about nine months, and that the ones who are going to be somebody else in nine months are the ones who go and look first." She looked at the hook. "**I have kept this gate for about nine years and I have never once seen a day in it, and if you asked me which day I first saw that line I could not tell you.** It is not that I have forgotten it. It is that I have not been counting the days, and I am the only person in four hundred miles this month who can say that and mean it."

---

She put the hook down against the post and stood on the step that was there and looked at the barrow rather than at the man, and a woman who looks at a barrow instead of at a man when she is about to say something she has not said to anybody is a woman who has decided that the barrow is a safer person.

"You have not told anybody in four days."

"I have told a barrow of boards at a shut gate on a Saturday morning. That is one person and she will not be here on Thursday, and the cheapest place to say a thing nobody can check is in front of somebody who is going somewhere else, and I have not planned that and it has simply turned out to be true about four days running." The key came out of the apron pocket and went back twice. "**And I am not going to be a woman who told a market about a line in the ground, because a thing that gets said in a market gets said again, and a thing that gets said again gets asked about, and I have got about nine years of not being asked about anything and I am not going to spend them on a straight line.**"

---

The man of about thirty-eight put his hands on the handles of the barrow and looked at the orchard for a while and then looked at the gate and then at the bar of ash in its brackets, and a man who has looked at a bar of ash in its brackets and worked out that there is nothing to hold it against has looked at the only thing about that gate that is still doing a job, and that what a bar is for is to be in a bracket with nothing to lean on, and that this is the whole of what a shut road is five days old.

"I will take the boards up on Thursday if you open it."

"**I will open it on Thursday if you come at the seventh hour and not at the eighth, and that is the whole of the arrangement, and it is not written down and it is not a promise, and I am not going to be thanked for it on Thursday.**"

---

The rest of that Saturday she did the gate. She worked the hook down and the gate came four inches and she put a hand out flat against the post and felt for the draught and found it and put the bar back in and it stopped, and she did that about nine times between the seventh hour and the fourth, and the date on the post was the forty-fourth and there was a date above it from the forty-third and a date above that from the forty-second, and nobody had ever taken one down.

About four people in that lane have said since that a woman who opens a shut gate four inches nine times a day is not checking whether the gate is shut, because she knows whether the gate is shut, and that what she is checking is whether the frame has dropped a quarter of an inch in nine years, and that this is the third time in this flood that somebody has turned a piece of the world's ordinary maintenance into the only honest thing in a room, and that on the twenty-third of that month it was a drain, and on the fortieth a bar of ash in a bracket, and that neither of those is going to be in anybody's report, and that the only people who have ever put either of them in a report are about four in a lane who cannot write anything down, and that this is the forty-third week of a flood in which the truest account of this city has been kept by people with no instrument at all.

---

And on the forty-fourth day of that month, a Saturday, a keeper at a shut gate on a road that came down from the north said out loud in daylight to a man with a barrow that there was a straight line in the ground under about nine hundred paces of orchard and that it was not a path and not a watercourse and not a furrow and not a boundary, and that she was not going to look at it and that a barrow of boards is the only place she has said it and a barrow of boards is going up a hill and not staying in one place, and that the man who digs on the flats about eleven miles the other way was not sent for and would have refused in the time it takes to shut a gate and would have been right, and that nobody measured it and nobody dug it and that nobody in four hundred miles has said what it is and that nobody is going to be the first, and that the gatekeeper would not remember the day and did not have to because she had not been counting them.
