# Chapter 0254 — Nobody In The Room Can Say Who Sent It

The room with a door on the ninety-ninth day of the Long Month had a date on the door in chalk and the date was the right one, and the question on the table had been written out and given by hand to every person who would be in that room four days before the room, and nobody who could not be given it was in the room, and nobody asked Hettis Roke whether the day suited him. He is forty-four and of the Hearthguard of the outer ward and nineteen years in, and he keeps the ground behind the ordinary wall, and he came at about the ninth hour with a waxed slate in his hand and had not been in that room before this morning. The light came in at one window about four feet off the floor and there was nowhere in that room to stand out of it.

---

The question on the table was one sentence, and it had been read out by a woman of about twenty-four in the room over a market on the day it was written, and it was: whether the docket from nine hundred miles away is the Crown's. Nine people were in that room and about nine of them had the sentence in their own hand, and the copies were not identical, because four of them had copied it and five had written it out again from somebody else's copy, and about four people in this city have worked out that a question which comes back four times in four hands is not four questions.

Roke turned the waxed slate over and turned it back. It is a thing a man does with a slate when he has had it a long time and the writing on it is his own and nobody has asked him to read it out. Adrian Vale was at the back of that room on a stool with his hands on his knees, and he watched both turns go by, and afterward he entered in his own book that he had watched both turns go by, and he entered that he had not been asked to and that nobody in that room had remarked on the entry, which is the only reason he made it.

"You have turned that over twice."

"I have turned it over twice and I have entered that I turned it over twice, because in about four minutes somebody is going to say that a man who turns a thing over is a man who is thinking, and I was not thinking. I was checking that the other side was still there."

"You are Hettis Roke."

"**I am. Nineteen years in the Hearthguard of the outer ward, and I keep the ground behind the ordinary wall.**"

Nobody in that room moved for a while, and the light in it went off the flats and up the far wall of it while they were not moving.

"And the slate."

"**The four things I have been asked to answer for this morning are on it, and I brought it because a thing I answer for ought to be in my own hand and not in a clerk's.**"

"And you can answer them."

"Can you answer them."

A woman of about twenty-four turned her slate over and wrote the date on the back of it, and about four people in that room saw her do it and none of them said a word.

"**No.**"

"That is quick."

"**It is quick because I have done it before in about four seconds. I would like it entered this morning, before anything else is said in this room, that I cannot say yes and I cannot say no, and that the reason is that I have got nothing to point at.**"

"You have a slate."

The wind came at that wall from the same quarter it had been coming from for a month, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had stopped noticing it and had started listening to it.

"I have a slate with a count on it, and the count is not an answer to any of the four, and I have not dressed it up as one for nineteen years and I am not going to start this morning."

"Who is not in this room."

"**A woman of about thirty-nine at the top of that cut, who cannot get a rack above her shoulder, and a man of about thirty-one in a house by the wharf, who cannot make a hand close.**"

"They are not in this room."

A man at the back put his weight on one foot and then on the other and did not say anything for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet.

"**Neither of them could be given the question in writing in four days and neither of them is here, and you have not asked me why I did not put their names on the paper and I am telling you before you ask. A list of who was not asked is a list.**"

---

A man of forty-one in a very good coat came in at about the ninth hour and sat down at the table without taking anything out of his pockets, and about nine people in that room had never heard the name he said in the next four minutes, and about four of them have since said that they would rather have gone on not having heard it. He had his hand inside his coat once before he said it and once after, and nobody in the room remarked on either.

"Regent Solenne Dary."

Nobody said anything, and the one window put a bar of light across four feet of floor and about four people in that room moved their feet out of it in the same second without saying anything about it, and the room got quieter, not louder.

"I have been in a room at the top of that road, nine hundred miles from this table, and I sat on one side of it and she was on the other, and I am giving it to you here because there is a date on this door and because there is a man in this room who has kept a gate for nineteen years and has never once been in the room where that page was signed. Here is what she said, in nine sentences, and I am not going to improve on it."

"Go on."

"A quarterly return has been in her hand four times a year. The return has a district on it. The district has a population on it."

"And the rest of the page."

Somebody at the door said something to somebody else and neither of them came in, and the room did not turn round.

"The district has no rota on it. The district has no share on it."

"That is three sentences and a quarter."

"It is three sentences and a quarter and I have the rest of them, and you will not like the rest of them any better, and I am going to say them in the same room on the same day in the same order."

"Go on, then."

A chair was moved about four inches along a boards floor by a person who had not been asked to move it, and about four people in that room have said since that they have never found out who moved it.

"There is no name on any roll she can find for that district, and she has had clerks looking for four quarters."

"Looking how."

"Looking how clerks look. And I asked her what a road kept by a body that answers to nothing and cannot be counted is, and she said it is not a road, it is a rumour."

He put his hand inside his coat and took it out again, and about four people in that room have since said he did it twice and that neither time was he thinking about the coat.

"And the last of them."

"And she said that in a bad season a rumour kills people before it has been found out to have been one."

"That is nine."

"That is nine. That is what she said and it is all of it, and I have not improved on it in four days and I am not going to improve on it in this room."

Then Roke put the waxed slate on the table, face up, and did not push it towards anybody, and he said the five figures he had come into that room with, in a voice with nothing in it at all, and they were these.

"**Eight. Every year. Nineteen years. Four times a year. Nobody.**"

"Will you say what the eight is."

"**The eight is what I have written on a roll of the dead of my own gate since the first year I had the gate. The count has been eight every year for nineteen years.**"

"You send the roll."

The page on the table was turned so that the writing faced the man who could not read it, and about nine people in that room have said that this happened and that nobody has said out loud who turned it.

"**Four times a year for nineteen years, to the office this docket came from.**"

"And what came back."

"**A clerk on a quay has written the count in a book every time, and the count in that book has been eight every year, and nobody has ever come.**"

"Nineteen years of a count that comes back the same."

Somebody wrote a figure on the back of their own hand and then put their hand in a pocket, and about four people in that room have said that they have each done that about nine times in about nine weeks.

"**Nineteen years of a count that comes back the same, and a clerk who does the writing is not a villain and is not going to be asked anything about it, and the eight is the same eight every year because the eight is the eight.**"

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register wrote all five of those on a second slate in that room, in his words, in the order he had said them, and she did not join them up. She did not put a line under them and she did not put a figure at the head of them and she did not set the word *nineteen* beside the word *nobody*, and about four people in that room watched her not do the third of those and none of them said anything about it.

A girl of seventeen was in the room. She is on the front of no page in this city and cannot be fetched, and being unfindable is not a qualification, and about four people in that room had worked out in the first two minutes that nobody in it could say where she had come from and had not asked. She had a plain slate under her hand that is not her own — her own has her name at the top of it, and that is the only reason there are two slates in this city and not one — and she was asked to copy the five figures so that a second place would have them, and she copied them in her own hand in the order he had said them, and she did not improve on one of them and she did not put a mark beside any of them either, and about four people in that room have said since that a girl of seventeen who will not put a mark beside a figure is doing something that most of the adults in that room could not do.

"You will not join them up."

"I will not join them up. A count in a column is one thing and a count under a date is another, and if I join those five figures together on a slate in this room then somebody will come along in about four months with a book and find a column with my hand at the bottom of it."

"You are the only person in this room who is not frightened."

"**We will be frightened or we will not, and neither of those changes what is on the table.**"

A page was put down flat on a table and about four hands went towards it and about nine hands stayed where they were, and about four people in that room have said since that the nine hands were the ones that would have to answer for it.

"He is not frightened."

"**He is not frightened, and I have watched him not be frightened for about an hour, and I would like it entered that I would rather he had been.**"

"Why."

"**Because a man who is not frightened in a room like this one has had something in his life that the rest of us have not had, and I have not got a way of asking him for it and I am not going to look for one today.**"

The hour changed and nobody in that room said what it was, and about four people in that room have said since that they all knew.

"Would you ask him."

"I would ask him the day he was given it. I am not going to ask him today and he would answer me today and that would be the wrong answer and I would have to carry it."

Then the man of forty-one in a very good coat put his hand inside his coat before he answered and took it out again, and about four people in that room have said since that he did it twice and that neither of the two was about the coat.

"And the figure on that return."

"**I am not going to give you the figure, and I would like it entered that I was asked for it in this room and did not give it.**"

"That is the second time today."

"**It is. The figure is on a page in a building nine hundred miles from this table and this room is not that building, and about four people in this city would put it on a slate today if I said it, and a figure on a slate is a figure in four months.**"

A woman of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board put his own hand over a thing on the table and left it there, and about four people in that room have said that he has done that four times in nine days and that nobody has ever asked him what was under it.

"And the thing she did not say."

"**She did not say the days on a gate are the Crown's to schedule. Nobody asked her, and that is the whole of what I have to say about her, and I have turned it over for four days and there is nothing else in it.**"

"That is a small thing to have carried nine hundred miles."

"**It is the only one of the nine sentences that was not said by her office.**"

Somebody at the back of that room said a word that about four people heard and about nine did not, and the person who said it did not say it again.

"That is what you have."

"**That is what I have. A state nine hundred miles away cannot put a question to itself, and that is the one thing it has never once been able to do, and about four people in this city worked out that a page cannot do it either, and they worked it out in a month and without a road.**"

---

The room emptied at about the eleventh hour and the five figures were on two slates in two hands and neither hand was Roke's, and the waxed slate went back into his coat with the writing on it the way it was, and about four people in that room have said since that the whole of it was a man showing a room five numbers he had been carrying alone for nineteen years and then not saying one word about the fifth of them.

And a name was in a room in this city in daylight with a date on the door, and five figures were on two slates under it that had not been joined up, and the first sentence of the whole of this is a gatekeeper's, and it is about a roll of the dead of a gate that went to an office four times a year for nineteen years, and a count of eight that was written down every time in a book on a quay nine hundred miles away, and nobody came, and the clerk who wrote it every quarter is not a villain and is not going to be asked anything about it.
