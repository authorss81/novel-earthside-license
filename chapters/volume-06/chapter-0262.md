# Chapter 0262 — Four Hands And Nobody Holding It

A case from nine hundred miles away stood shut on a table in a room over a market on the seventh day of the Short Month, and the day was a Thursday, and it had been on that table for eleven days, and the room spent the whole of that day finding out that it could not say who was holding it. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures standing on it belong to that sixty-ninth day and are thirty-eight days old, and it has stood unwritten thirty-seven days. The wind has been at that wall for thirty-three days. The docket is tied twice to the handle in oiled cloth under a seal the colour of a wet stone, and nobody in that room put a hand on it once in seven hours, and about four people in this city have worked out that this is not care and is not respect.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was in that room and he had come up the road with nothing on him except his coat. The copy of the four lines was not on him and was not in the room, and about four people who know that have said since that a man who leaves a page at home and then comes into a room to talk about who holds pages is a man who has already answered the question in his own head and has come to see whether anybody else agrees with him.

"You have copied the four lines out."

"**I have copied the four lines out and I have copied them for myself and I have copied them for about four people, and I am not going to tell you the number and I am not going to tell you who the four are, and you may put all three of those on a slate if you like.**"

The man of about thirty-four put a piece of chalk on the table, about two inches long and worn down at one end to a stub, and he put it down the way a person puts down something they have been carrying for a long time, and then he did not touch it again for the rest of that day, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who puts a piece of chalk on a table in a room where a page is being argued over has told everybody what he is not going to do with the day.

"You copied them for four people."

"I copied them for the people who could not come to a room, and there are about nine of those on a market day, and one of them is my own sister and I am not going to say which of the nine she is." He put his thumb on a mark he had made in the margin of his slate and left it there, so that the mark stayed and the thumb did not move off it. "**A copy of a page is a page. I said that in a yard on the third day of that month and I have said it since to about nine people, and I am not going to unsay it here because there is a woman in this building who keeps the register.**"

"You have made five documents out of one."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had the piece of chalk in front of him and did not pick it up, and the man of about thirty-nine turned his slate a quarter turn on the table so that the four lines on it faced the window and not the man, and about four people in that room have said since that the two men at that table spent the whole morning with a piece of chalk and a slate in the wrong orientations and neither of them was about handwriting.

"I have made five *copies*. The document is the one in the case and it is on that table and it has not moved an inch since the seventh hour of the morning. If a copy stops being a copy then this room is wrong about the only thing it is certain of, and about nine people in four hundred miles are relying on this room being right about it and none of them know that they are relying on it."

"You cannot tell me how many copies there are."

"I cannot tell you how many copies there are, and I want it entered that the reason is not that I do not know. The reason is that I know, and a number of copies is a number of men." He lifted the thumb off the mark in the margin and the mark stayed where it was without him. "**Ask me for the number and you have a page with a figure on it, and a figure under a date is a column, and about four people in this city worked that out in a room in daylight about nine days ago and did not like where it took them.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall turned the piece of chalk end for end on the table and left it where it was, and neither of the two men at the table said anything about that, and about nine people in that room have said since that the two of them were aware of the chalk for seven hours and that the rest of the room was not.

---

The man of about forty who carries sacks had a sack in that room for most of the day. It had nothing to do with anything on the table; he was paid by the load, and the floor of that room wanted two measures of salt in it, and he had brought them up the road on his back in a sack and was getting them out of it on his knees by the door for about three hours with a flat trowel, because a measure that has gone hard has to come out of a corner as a corner. About four people in that room have said since that a man on his knees with his back to a conversation is the only person in it who cannot be asked to have an opinion, and that about nine people in this city would have given an opinion if they had been standing up.

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came in and stood at the table and did not sit down, and the second slate was under her arm, and she put it down face up, which is not what she usually does.

"**The cost first, and then the thing. The cost is the register's slate, and it is the second one, and I have carried it since the second month of this flood, and what I am going to do with it now is put a number on it.**"

"You are going to put a number on a slate."

She had not sat down and she was not going to, and the man of about forty with the trowel worked the second measure out of the sack behind her and set it on its end against the skirting, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who will not sit down in front of a thing she is about to refuse is doing the only thing she can do with her body while her mouth is working.

"**I am going to put a number on a slate, and the number is going to be how many people in this city hold a copy of a Crown instrument, and I have named the cost before I have said the thing, and I would like the people in this room to notice that I have done it in that order three times now.**" She did not turn the slate over. "And I am not going to do it, and here is the rest of the reason. A Hearthguard of forty-four sat in the room with the door on the ninety-ninth day of the Long Month, said nine words, and then said that a list of who was not asked is a list. He said it about two people he could not give a paper to. He was right, and it took him one sentence and it has taken this room five days to come round to the same place."

"A list of who has a copy is a list."

The register-keeper turned the slate over and left it face up and did not write on it, which is the first thing about that afternoon that about four people in the room noticed, and about nine noticed the hand and not the slate.

"**A list of who has a copy is a list, and a list of who does not have one is a list of a different shape, and the second of the two is the one that has a name in it. That is why the refusal is not the count. It is the other list.**" She put a finger on the face of the slate and did not turn it. "You are not refusing to find out how many. You are refusing to find out how many and write it down, and those are two different things, and about nine people in this city keep not hearing the difference between a thing and a record of a thing, and that difference is the whole of my work."

"You are refusing to find out how many."

"I am refusing to find out how many *and write it down*, and I have not asked, and I would like it entered that I have not asked." The piece of chalk on the table had not moved and the man of about thirty-nine looked at it once and then at her. "It is the first time in nine days that I have refused a question instead of an answer, and the two are not the same thing, and I have been thinking about the difference since the ninth hour and I have not got to the bottom of it."

A page was put down flat on that table and about four hands went towards it and about nine hands stayed where they were, and about four people in that room have said since that the nine hands were the ones that would have to answer for it, and that on the day the case came up the river the four hands and the nine hands were the same four and the same nine.

---

She did not leave the slate face down for long. At about the second hour of the afternoon she turned it over and wrote one line on it in her own hand, and the line was the day, and the four words of the question, and a statement that the room did not answer it. There was no name on it and no figure on it, and she read it back once and did not read it back twice.

"What have you written."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had the piece of chalk an inch from her elbow and neither of them looked at it, and the man of about thirty-nine had gone out of the room at some point in that half hour and had not come back and had not said anything about going, and about four people in the room have said since that he had gone out to decide not to argue and had got the decision over with in the passage.

"**The day, and the question, and that nobody in this room answered it. There is no name on that line, and in about four months a person with a book can look it up and find that a room over a market in this city could not say who was holding a document.**"

"And that is what you wanted."

The light in that room had by then come off the low ground outside and gone along the floor to the skirting, and the second measure of salt stood on its end against the skirting where the man of about forty had put it, and neither of them had moved for an hour, and about four people in that room have said since that the two salt measures in the corner of a room where a document was being discussed for seven hours are the only objects in it that nobody argued about.

"That is what I wanted, and I have not put down what it cost, so I will say it in a mouth. **Five days ago I would not put a date on a reading that came from nine hundred miles away, and the reason was that a reading entered under a date is a reading somebody can look up.**" She put the chalk down beside the slate and not on it, and about four people in that room noticed that she put a piece of chalk beside a slate and did not use it, and that the man of about thirty-four had been putting one on tables for seven hours. "I have put a date on this one. What I have bought with it is that a question with a day on it and no name on it can only be answered by somebody who was in the room."

"You have made yourself findable."

"I have made one sentence findable and I have kept every name out of it, and about four people in this city will tell me those are the same thing, and they are not the same thing, and I am not going to argue it standing up at the second hour of the afternoon on a Thursday."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said nothing in that room at any hour of that day. The piece of chalk stayed on the table until the room emptied and he took it with him, and about four people who were in it have said since that the man who put a page on a wall on purpose, and who has refused five times to say whether that page works, sat in a room for five hours on a Thursday and did not say one word about a copy of that page being in four hands, and that they noticed, and that they did not remark on it, and that they have not worked out yet whether the not remarking was loyalty.

---

The case stood on that table at the sixth hour and it stood on it at the seventh and it stood on it after that, and about four people in this city have said since that a correct page from nine hundred miles away has now been multiplied into four hands by men who could read it, for the sake of speed, and that nobody did it on purpose, and that not one of the four can produce his copy to anybody without saying that he has it, and that saying so is a name on a page.

A Crown instrument cannot be refused by a body, because a body has no door and no reason that has to be given twice. What it can be refused by is a person, one at a time, in a room. **Nobody in this city has refused it, and nobody in this city can find it, and about four people who have sat in that room this week have worked out that a document which cannot be found cannot be withdrawn and cannot be complied with either, and that nobody in that room is going to be the first one to use that sentence out loud.**

The seal of wet stone had not been broken, the docket was still tied twice to the handle, and the schedule was still not filled in. The board over the door of the store on the upper road was still unwritten, and the ninth line on the wall of that store was still where a man of about thirty-four had put it, and the register-keeper's second slate went back under her arm with one line on it and no name on it, and about nine people in four hundred miles will be able to find that line in about four months and will not be able to find anybody.
