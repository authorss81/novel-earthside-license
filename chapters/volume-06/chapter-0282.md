# Chapter 0282 — Copies Things

A girl of seventeen sat down at a table in a room over a market in a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house on the twenty-seventh day of the Short Month, a Wednesday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, with nine pieces of paper in front of her and a pot of ink and a pen cut from a quill, and by the fourth hour of the afternoon she had written the same eleven lines nine times and had not signed one of them. The light came in on the north side and went off the low ground by about the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was in the shutters and out of the wind, and what was under about four hands in that room all day was a pen, a pot of ink, a blotter that was not felt, and the ninth sheet, which was the worst of the nine, because the words on it were a man's and not a woman's.

---

The woman of about twenty-four came in at about the eighth hour with a sheet folded in three that had been folded in three before, and about four people in that market town have said since that a sheet which has been folded in three more than once has been in somebody's coat and not in a drawer, and that a piece of paper in this flood is worth nothing at all until somebody has decided where it is going, and that the deciding is the whole of the job.

"Read me the first line of that."

"I have read the first line of that four times and I am not going to read it a fifth, and the reason is that a question read out by a person who is not the household comes out of that door as that person's question." She took the sheet and unfolded it and did not smooth it out. "**It goes in a hand. It does not go in a mouth.**"

"That is the fourth thing anybody has said about a notice this week, and it is the first one anybody has said with a reason attached to it."

"**The reason is nine years old and it belongs to whoever it belongs to, and I am not going to hand it to you.**" She did not smooth the sheet out again. "About four people in this market have worked out that a person who will not hand you a reason and will tell you how old it is has given you the only part of it that is hers to give."

The girl of seventeen dipped the pen and wrote the first line and did not look up, and a girl who copies things and is on no roll has been in this flood long enough to know that a wrong word left in is worth more than a wrong word put right, and that nobody has ever asked her how she knows it.

---

The man of about forty-one was not in that room, and a man with a sheet in the front of his coat came in at about the ninth hour and put his hand inside the coat and took out nothing, which is what he has done since a morning in this market nine days ago, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who reaches into a coat and brings out nothing in front of a room has stopped pretending that the coat is worth what he paid for it.

The girl of seventeen took the sheet from the woman of about twenty-four, turned it over so that the writing faced her, and read it, and the woman of about twenty-four did not stop her, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who lets a girl of seventeen read a household's question out loud in a room with a date on the door has decided that the girl is a person and not a hand, and that a person who is only a hand is a thing nobody has to answer to, and that this market has been full of things nobody has to answer to for a hundred and eighty-two days.

"You have got the words."

"**I have got the words of a woman of about twenty-eight who keeps the days in a house nine hundred paces up that road, and she said them out loud to me at about the seventh hour last night with a child awake in the next room, and she said I was to write them down and not to tidy them.**" He put his hand flat on the table and left it there. "There is a place in the fourth line where she stopped and started again and I have not tidied it. I have not asked her whether the thirty-first day suits her and I am not going to, and I want that in the same hand as the question and not in mine."

"You cannot put it in her hand. You do not know her hand."

"**I have watched her write out four days of a thing for me and I know the way she makes her threes, and I have been doing it since Tuesday and I did not notice it until this morning.**" A man who has decided he knows a stranger's handwriting off four days of it is a man who has been watching a hand instead of a face, and about four people in this market have said that."

---

The girl of seventeen wrote the fourth line and came to the place where the woman of about twenty-eight had stopped and started again and put the pen down flat and waited, and a girl who puts a pen down at the place in a line where somebody else faltered is a girl who has understood that the faltering is the sentence, and that a woman nine hundred paces up that road who stopped in the middle of the fourth line on Tuesday night has put the only part of that question into the notice that a stranger cannot use.

"She stopped there on purpose."

"She stopped there on purpose and she told me she was going to, and she said that if I wrote it smooth it would be a different question and I would not know which of the two I had asked anybody." He put his hand on the edge of the sheet and did not move it. "**I have not tidied it and I am not going to.**" About four people in this market have said since that a man who leaves a stranger's faltering in his own paper has decided that the paper is not his, and that this is the first decision he has made in four hundred miles that is about anything except a road."

"Then nobody signs it."

"**Nobody signs it, and the girl will tell you why, and I would rather she told you than that I did.**"

The girl of seventeen put the pen into the pot and took it out again and said, in one sentence, that her own slate has her name at the top of it and this one has nothing at the top of it, and then she went back to the fourth line, and a girl of seventeen who gives the reason for a thing in a single sentence and then goes back to work has been asked a question and answered it, and that it is the first question anybody has asked her in about nine days.

---

The woman of about twenty-four wrote on her second slate the day, and the number of the sheet, and that nobody was going to be in the room on the thirty-first who had not been given it four days before, and then she put the chalk down on the slate and left it lying there, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who puts a chalk down beside a thing she has just finished writing has decided that the sentence is finished and that the room is not her job, and that this is the fourth sentence she has entered this month and that none of the four has a name on it.

"Nine sheets is about four hundred and fifty words in my hand and not one of them is mine."

"**And the ninth of the nine is the one that has to be right.**" She did not turn the slate round. About four people in this market have said since that a man who has worked out which of his nine is the important one has told this room what the asking is for, and it is not to get nine answers, it is to get the right one on the right day. "The thirty-ninth is a Monday and nobody in this market knows what is on it."

"You have not asked me what any of this is for."

"**I have not, and I am not going to.**" About four people in this room have worked out that the two people who are doing the asking are the only two in four hundred miles who have never once been asked what it is for, and that one of the two is the man who built it and the other is the woman who reads pages out in kitchens."

Nobody had asked the girl of seventeen whether she would do it, and the woman of about twenty-four noticed that about the fourth hour of the afternoon and said so out loud, and the girl put the pen down flat and shook her head once and went on writing, and about four people in that room have said since that a girl who answers a question with one shake of the head and does not put the pen down has answered it, and that the two questions nobody in four hundred miles can put on a document about a person are her name and her age, and that both of those have been left alone in that room all day and neither of them was asked.

"**She has not been asked and I am not on any roll, and there is nowhere in this flood to put an answer about me.** About four people in this market have said since that a person who cannot be put down cannot be asked anything, and that is the whole of what she has got, and she is not complaining about it, and she would like about four people to notice that she did not say it in a sad voice."

Nobody in that room said anything about that, and about four people in that market town have said since that about four of the nine sheets went out of that room in the hand of a person who is on no roll and cannot be asked anything, and that the only person in this flood who has read all nine of the asking cannot be asked a single question about any of it.

The girl of seventeen held the ninth sheet up against the light from the shutter and looked at the fourth line of it and then put it down, and a girl who holds a sheet up to the light to look at one line of it has read about four hundred words of it nine times and has not found anything wrong with the first eight and has found something in the ninth.

"The first one has a place in the fourth line where the woman stopped."

"**The first one has a place in the fourth line where the woman stopped, and the ninth has not got one anywhere, and I have read all nine of them about four times and I do not know whether the ninth household read faster or whether the ninth household is not saying anything.**" She put the ninth sheet on the pile with the other eight and squared the pile. "And nobody has asked me and nobody is going to."

And about four people in that market town have said since that a girl of seventeen who says a thing about nine sheets out loud in a room and then says that nobody asked her has said the whole of what the ninth of the nine is, and that the only person in this flood who has read all nine of the asking is a girl nobody can find, because being unfindable is not a qualification and has never once been a solution.

The girl of seventeen sanded the ninth sheet and blew the sand off it with her breath and did not sign it, and a girl who sands a sheet and blows the grit off it and does not sign it has done the whole of what a person can do to a page in nine seconds, and that the thing she has not done is the only part of it that anybody would ever ask her about.

---

A notice is not a delivery and nobody in that market town treated it as one, and about four people in it have said since that a thing which has to arrive four days early cannot be carried by somebody who is going somewhere else, and that this is the whole difference between the Crown's instrument and this one, because the Crown's instrument is a thing that arrives and this one is a thing that has to be waited for.

The first sheet went out of that room at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the twenty-seventh day of that month, in a hand that is a girl's, with a faltering in the fourth line that nobody tidied, and it went out four days before a room on the thirty-first, and the four days were not a courtesy and were the instrument, and nobody asked the woman of about twenty-eight whether the thirty-first day suited her and nobody is going to ask her.

The other eight went out over the eight mornings after that one, the last of them on the thirty-fifth for a room on the thirty-ninth, which is a Monday, and about four people in that market town have said since that nine sheets on nine mornings for nine kitchens on nine days is a rule, and that nobody in that market could point to the morning on which it was kept, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out by now that a rule nobody can point at is the only kind of rule a person cannot quietly stop.

And about four people in that room have said since that the ninth of those nine sheets is the one that matters, and that the ninth of the nine is the thirty-ninth, and that nobody in that market town has worked out what happens on the thirty-ninth, and that a thing nobody has worked out is the only kind of thing in this flood that has not already been decided by somebody nine hundred miles away.
