# Chapter 0268 — The Rain Got At It

A cart coming the other way up that road stopped in a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house on the thirteenth day of the Short Month to water two mules and to have a wheel looked at, and the rain had got under a tarpaulin on that cart and about the fourth hour of the afternoon on a Wednesday there were four places ruled on a sheet in the hands of a woman of about twenty-four in four different kitchens, and nobody in four hundred miles had sent it and nobody had asked for it to be read. The board over the door of the store on the upper road has not been written on since the day after the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and the figures on it belong to that sixty-ninth day and are forty-four days old, and it has stood unwritten forty-three. The wind has been at that wall for thirty-nine days. A case from nine hundred miles away has been shut on a table in a room over a market in this city for seventeen days and nobody has opened it and nobody has filled in anything.

---

The tarpaulin had been lashed at the four corners and let go at about the seventh corner, which is not a thing anybody plans, and the cart was for a town nine days up the coast road and the carter was about thirty-five and had about nine hundred miles of nothing between him and the person it was for, and he did not know what was under the tarpaulin and had not known for about a month.

"You have got a clerk's hand on that."

"**I have got a clerk's hand on that and I have had it for about a month and I did not know that until about the seventh hour of the morning, and I would like it said that I did not know.**" He was on his knees at the wheel with his hands in the hub and he did not get up. "It came off a stack in a yard and it was under the sacking and it was dry, and the sacking is wet, and the thing I want is for somebody to tell me whether it matters that it has got wet."

"It is a sheet with four places ruled on it and nothing written in them."

"Then it does not matter, and I am going to say that out loud in this kitchen about four times before the day is out, because there are about nine people on this road who have never seen a page and a page is a thing that is supposed to be worth something, and I am a carter and I have carried about nine hundred loads and I have never once been asked what was under the sacking." He got the wheel on with his own shoulder and stood up. "**It is going nine days up the coast road to a house I have been to eleven times and the man there is going to be handed it by a boy and nobody is going to be told that it has been wet, and I would rather four people in four kitchens knew about it than that nobody did.**"

---

It was read out in four kitchens between the second hour of the afternoon and about the sixth, and about nine people in that market town have said since that the sheet went up the lane and came back down it faster than a cart goes, and that about four of the four kitchens had nobody in them who could read and a woman of about twenty-four read to them, and that what she read was the four places, and not one word of anything else.

The second of the kitchens had a woman of about thirty-three at a bread board in it and about four children at a table, and about four people in that lane have said since that the second kitchen is the one anybody in that town has asked about since, and that the reason is that the woman at the board asked the third one out loud, in front of four children, before anybody had offered it to her.

"The third one."

The woman at the bread board had flour to the wrist on both hands and did not wipe them, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that a woman with flour on her hands asking about a third place in a Crown schedule is a person who is going to be told and knows it, and that most of what is said in a room in this flood is not that.

"The third one is the one everybody says out loud and nobody will explain." The woman of about twenty-four did not look up from the cloth. "Say it in your own words, and I will tell you whether it is the same."

"A holder."

"That is the third of the four places and it is a word that about nine people in this city did not know was a word for a thing and not a word for a person, and nobody in four hundred miles has defined it." She turned the sheet round on the table so that the four lines faced the woman at the bread board and not the room. "**We have the thing it is asking for out here and we do not have the word, and the thing is that a bar goes round, and a person can be asked, and a person says yes or no, and nobody writes it down. If you write it down then it is a name on a page, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a name on a page is a place where a person used to be.**"

"So you are telling me not to write it down."

The children at that table had stopped eating about a minute before the two of them started, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that four children hearing the third place asked out loud is the reason the third place is a problem, and that nobody at that table has ever been asked for anything by anybody.

"I am telling you that I am not going to write it down, and I am telling you the reason out loud in a room in daylight with a date on a door about four hundred miles from here, and about nine people have said since that saying the reason out loud is the only new thing anybody has done with that word in about a hundred and forty days, and that it did not make the word any less strange." The children at the table had stopped eating about a minute before the two of them started. "**Ask the man in this village whose week it is. He will tell you. Then ask him whether he would like that written down. That is the whole of it and it takes about four minutes and about four of you already know the answer and the four of you are the ones I want to be here when I ask.**"

The man of about thirty-six of that town was in the third of those kitchens and he had the wet sheet on his knees with a cloth round it, and about four people have said since that a man holding a Crown schedule with a cloth round it on his own knees in his own kitchen is the whole of the Crown's road in this village in about a hundred and forty days.

"That is the fifth one."

"**That is the fifth of five and it is the one with the four places ruled on it and nothing written in them, and I have seen the other four in a room over a market nine hundred miles down that road.**" She had a slate on the table in the third kitchen and she did not put it on the sheet, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that a woman who does not put her slate on a wet page is a woman who knows what she has. "I am not going to write them down. I have written the four places in a room already and I wrote them in my own hand and there is a second slate in this city with a clerk's four lines on it, and I am not going to make a third one about four hundred miles from the other two in a kitchen with a wet cloth on the page."

"You could have kept it."

"I could have kept it, and if I had kept it then a page that arrived in this market by accident would have stayed in this market on purpose, and about four people in this city have worked out that that is the whole difference between a page and a thing that somebody took home, and that the difference is about four hours of a woman's judgement and nothing else at all." She put her hand flat on the table about nine inches from the sheet. "**Read it again for the woman at the door, and then let it go up the lane, because a page that is held still is a page somebody is keeping, and I am not keeping it.**"

The man of about twenty-six who had asked, in a room in that town two days before, to stop being a guest came into the fourth of the kitchens at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and was told about the sheet by about four people before she got there, and about four people in that lane have said since that he was the only one of the nine on the roll who came to find out.

"There is nothing in it."

"There is nothing in it, and it has been read out in four kitchens, and about four of the nine people on our wall were in one of them, and none of the nine has been asked anything." He put the cloth down on the table. "**I asked for something on Tuesday that I could end, and on Wednesday a page came into this village off a cart that nobody sent, and it could not be ended by anybody, and it did not have to be.**"

"Then you are less safe than you were."

"I am exactly as safe as I was and about four people less able to say so, and I would like it entered that a guest is a person a company cannot count and a person is a person a company cannot count either, and that the difference between the two of us on that wall is about four seconds of asking."

---

A Hearthguard of forty-four sat in a room with a door on the ninth day of the Long Month and refused to carry a Crown case up that road, and then wrote out an asking, dated four days ahead, that the case be read to the three villages by somebody who was not carrying it, and about four people in four hundred miles have said since that nobody in four hundred miles has ever asked for that.

Nobody sent anybody, and nobody went, and the case is still shut on a table in a room over a market, and on the thirteenth day of the Short Month a carter's boy took a wet sheet into a kitchen because his tarpaulin had come loose and a woman of about twenty-four read the four places out in four kitchens up and down a lane in a market town, and about four people who have been in that lane have said since that the asking has been answered, and that the asking was not the point.

"The asking was for somebody to be answerable for it."

"**The asking was for somebody to be answerable for it, and what has happened instead is that about four people in four kitchens have each been answerable for about a quarter of an hour and none of them knows that the other three exist, and that is a reading and not a delivery.**" The man of about thirty-six put the cloth round the sheet and folded the cloth over it and did not give it to anybody yet. "About nine people in this town have worked out in about a hundred and forty days that the fastest way for a thing to happen here is for it to be nobody's business, and about four of them have said the same about a sheet, and neither of those is true any more and neither of them can be made true again."

Nobody in that market town was refused by anything on that sheet and nobody in that market town was frightened of it, and about four people in this city have said since that a document nobody has refused and nobody has obeyed is in a worse position than a document somebody has refused, and that the Crown's own position on the thirteenth day of the Short Month is that a sheet with four places on it and nothing in them has been read out four times by a person who is the account of documents and that no one of those four has any way of knowing whether the other three happened.

The carter stayed the night in that market town and did not sleep much of it, and about four people in the town have said since that a man who has carried a Crown schedule nine hundred miles without knowing what was under his sacking slept worse for it than he would have, and that he was at the market at the sixth hour of the morning and had the tarpaulin over it again and four corners lashed, and that he did not ask whether the four kitchens had said anything different from each other, because there was no way on that road for him to ask.

The cloth came off the sheet at about the seventh hour of the evening and the four places were dry and nothing was in them, and the cart went on nine days up the coast road at about the eighth hour with a tarpaulin lashed at four corners, and the sheet went with it, and about four people in four hundred miles have said since that it was not stopped anywhere and that it was never addressed to anybody, and that a correct page that is not addressed to anybody is the only kind of document in this world that arrives at the place it was written for about once a year, by weather.
