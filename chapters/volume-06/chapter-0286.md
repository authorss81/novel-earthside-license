# Chapter 0286 — A Day In Her Own Hand

A woman of about twenty-eight put a date on the door of her own house in chalk on the thirty-first day of the Short Month, a Sunday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, because a man with a question in somebody else's words had come up that lane twice in four days and had said he would come at the seventh hour on the day the question named, and the light came in at one window about four feet off the floor and got as far as the middle of that kitchen and no further, and the cold was in the boards and out of the wind off the low ground behind the house, and what was in that kitchen all morning was a pot on a fire, a child of about two on the boards with a spoon, a pail by the door, and a sheet of paper with a date on it and eleven lines written on it in a hand that was not his.

---

He came at about the seventh hour and he brought nothing, and about four people on that row have said since that a man who has come to a kitchen four days after leaving a question there arrives with his hands empty, and that a man who arrives with his hands empty in a kitchen is a man who has worked out that he is not one of the people the question is about.

"**You have not brought it.**"

"I have not brought it, because the four conditions say the question goes to everybody who will be in this room, and I am not in this room, and I have read that twice on the road and both times it said the same thing." He put his hands behind him and left them there, and his other hand went to the front of his coat and stopped there without going in. "I am the one who carries it and I am not one of the people it is for, and about four people on this row have worked out that those are two different things, and the man who worked that out did not get it out of the paper."

"**You got it out of the paper and then you walked two days to check that the paper was right.**" She turned the pot with the edge of a board, and the child of about two turned the spoon over on the boards, and neither of them was looked at. "That row is called Long Rigg and nobody has ever put it on anything, and there are nine houses on it, and about nine of them have had a page out of a case that came up a river in a cart going somewhere else."

---

"**I have written it out.**"

"You have written what out." He did not sit down and she did not ask him to.

"The thing that came on Wednesday. **I have written my answer to it out in my own hand, and I wrote it on Thursday and I have not changed a word of it since, and it is in a box on the shelf and I will read it to you and I will not hand it to you.**"

The box was on a shelf above the fire and she did not get up to get it, and about four people on that row have said since that a woman who has decided not to stand up for a box has decided that the box is hers and that the room is hers and that the two of them are on her side of the fire.

"You will read it to me and I will not take it, and then what happens to it."

"Then it goes back in the box and it stays in this kitchen, and that is the part I have been thinking about for two days, and that is why I said I would come to the door." She did not get up. "**A thing in a box in a kitchen is a thing that cannot be entered in a column. A piece of paper is a thing that can be entered in a column.** About four people in this village have worked out that those are two kinds of thing and not one thing in two shapes, and about four of them have worked out that the page that came up the river wants the second kind, and that I am the first person on this row who has understood it."

The child of about two got hold of the end of the bench and pulled it about an inch and let go of it and did it again, and the pot went on wanting looking at, and a kitchen in which the furniture is being moved by a child of about two is a kitchen in which two people are able to talk about a page at all, and that this is an accident and not an instrument and that the instrument is the four days and not the child.

She read it out. It was two lines and a mark at the bottom of them that was not a name, and it said yes, and it said a day, and about four people on that row who have heard it since have said that the day in it is not the thirty-first and is not any of the days that nine kitchens have been set out for, and that a household has been asked a question in its own words and has answered it in a day that nobody put there.

"**I have not signed it.**"

"You have not signed it and you have put a mark at the bottom instead, and a mark is not a name and cannot be looked up, and a document that nobody can enforce is the first document this village has ever wanted." He turned the edge of the box about a quarter turn on the shelf and did not open it. "I have not signed it because my name on a piece of paper is a thing a person can be found by, and about four people in this village have worked that out this month without ever having had it said to them, and I did not learn it off a page, I learned it off a man who came up this lane on Tuesday and would not put his own name to anything."

---

"You have said yes."

"I have said yes and I have said a day, and the day is mine, and I am telling you what it is so that you can put it on something, and you are not going to be able to put it on something, and I have known that since Thursday." She put her hand flat on the box and did not open it again. "**The thirty-first is a Sunday, and on a Sunday I have nine people in this kitchen and about four of them are not mine.** About four people on this row have said out loud this month that a day is not a date. I have not said that sentence to anybody and I am not saying it to you now. **I am saying that on a Sunday this kitchen is not a room with two people in it, and that is why my day is not the thirty-first.**"

"Nobody has asked you whether the thirty-first suited you."

"Nobody asked me whether the thirty-first suited me and I would have said no, and that is the whole of what four days of notice did, because by Friday I had the four days in front of me and I had found out what I would have said." She turned round and looked at the pot and it was not doing anything and she turned back. "**About four people in this village have worked out that a question given four days early is a question a person can say no to, and a question given in a room is a question a person can only be caught by.** About four of them have said that nobody in this village has ever had a question put in writing before this week, and that is the whole of what has happened to nine houses this month, and it is not the page."

---

"What day is it that you have named."

"**It is a day I chose, and it is not one of the nine, and I am not going to say it out loud twice.** About four people in this village have said since that a household which has been asked a question and has answered it in a day that is not on the list has done the one thing nobody in four hundred miles has managed, which is to put something of its own into a form and not into a column."

He did not ask her the day. He stood at the side of that fire with his hands behind him and about four people on that row have said since that a man who has come two days to ask a question and has not asked the one question that would have let him fill a page has come to be told what he has been told, and that the day is in a kitchen and is going to stay in a kitchen, and that a fact which cannot be got out of a room is the only kind of fact there is.

The child of about two had by then got the spoon under the pail by the door and was working at it with both hands, and the pail was half full of nothing that anybody in that kitchen wanted, and she looked at the child and not at the pail, and the woman of about twenty-eight watched that for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and did not stop, and about four people on that row have said since that a woman who watches a child of about two work a spoon under a pail and does not stop is a woman who has decided that the whole of the morning has been worth it and is not going to lose the end of it.

---

The pot wanted looking at and the woman of about twenty-eight looked at it and then at the man, and the man of about thirty-two spoke for the last time in that kitchen, and he spoke for himself, and a man who has said nothing for two hours in somebody else's kitchen and then says four sentences at the end of it has been standing at the back of the room the whole time and not in the front of it.

"You have not asked me a question."

"You have not asked me a question." He looked at the fire and did not look at her. About four people in this village have said since that the man who carries a question into nine kitchens is the only person in nine kitchens who is never asked anything, and that this is not a courtesy and is a thing that happens to the one who is doing the asking. "**And a person nobody asks anything is a person who cannot be entered in a column, and that is the reason nobody is going to ask you.**" I have not come here to be asked and I have not come here to be thanked, and I have come here to stand at the back of a kitchen and be told what a household says."

"**You have said that in the wrong order and in the wrong kitchen.**"

"I have said it in the right kitchen and I have said it to the only one of the nine who has answered me, and about four people on that row have said since that the first of the nine is a woman of about twenty-eight who gave a household a yes and a day and did not give a name, and that the second of the nine is going to be a woman who reads pages for a living, and that she is going to be harder to ask than you are and the reason is that a person who reads pages for a living has spent her life being the person a thing gets put into."

---

The pot was taken off the fire at about the eleventh hour, and the child of about two was given the spoon and a piece of bread, and the box went back on the shelf with two lines in it in a hand that was not his and was not anybody else's, and the date stayed on the door in chalk, and a woman who leaves a date in chalk on her own door for a week has turned the door into a notice and made the notice hers.

And about four people on that row have said since that the first of nine kitchens was asked on the thirty-first day of that month, a Sunday, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, and that the question had gone out four days before, on the twenty-seventh, in a hand that is a girl's, with a faltering in the fourth line that nobody tidied, and that nobody asked the woman of about twenty-eight whether the thirty-first suited her, and that she suited it, and that the first household in four hundred miles to be asked a question by a document answered it in a day that was not on anybody's list, and that the second sheet went out of a room nine hundred paces down that road the next morning for a kitchen on the thirty-second, and that the two of those things happened on two mornings about nine hundred paces apart and neither of them knew the other was happening, and that this is what a household's days are, and have not said so, and that a household's days being the nine days a fortnight ahead of a question that has not been asked yet is the only definition of the thing anybody in four hundred miles has this month, and that nobody is going to write it down because it does not go in a column.
