# Chapter 0289 — The Person Arrives

A woman of about forty-five came up a lane off that road on the thirty-fourth day of the Short Month, a Wednesday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, with a case in her hand and nothing in the case that anybody in that lane could see, and she stood at a door with a date in chalk on it for about four hours before anybody opened it, and the light was white and flat off the low ground and did not warm the stones of that lane, the cold was out of the wind and in the iron of the case handle and in about four pairs of hands that morning, and what was under those hands was a case, a sill, a bar lying on the near side of the road because it was nobody's week, a bootful emptied at the near end of the lane, and a date in chalk on a door that had been put there that morning by a woman of about fifty-three who had not put a name under it.

---

She said her name at about the seventh hour and said the day, and the day was the thirty-fourth of that month and she said she had chosen it, and a woman who stands at a door for four hours and gives her name and a date before anybody has opened it is a woman who has decided that the first two things anybody may know about her are the two things that are on a page, and that this is the whole of the difference between a person and a document and that it is about as much as there is.

"**My name is Ilva Rooke, and I have come from the office at the top of that road, which is nine hundred miles from here, and the thirty-fourth day of the Short Month is the day I have chosen, and I chose it in a building in which I had no reason to choose any day at all.**" She did not strike the door a second time. "I have four places in a book and two of them are a date and a gate, and I have got the date and I have not got the gate, and I have come up this lane to fill in the third one, and the third one wants a person, and I would like it to be somebody who is standing in front of me."

"You have come up a lane to fill in a column and there are two women on this road who are not going to give you a name, and you do not know that, and I have known it since Tuesday."

"**I have known since Tuesday that there are two women on this road who are not going to give me a name, and I have put a date on a door on Wednesday morning anyway.** A date in chalk on a door is the household's and not the document's, and that is the first thing anybody has done this month that a column cannot have, and I have watched a woman do it from the near end of her own lane through a door I was not let in."

---

The man of about thirty-six came down that lane at about the tenth hour from the gate at the top of it, with his bar in one hand and a bootful of water in the other, and he emptied the bootful at the near end of the lane and did not go up to the door, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who comes down a lane where there is a stranger standing at a door and stops at the near end of it has worked out that a door with a stranger at it is a room with a date on it, and that a room with a date on it is not a room anybody has asked him into, and that this is the only gatekeeper anybody has ever heard of who decided not to go up to a door, and that he is a man of about thirty-six with a bar and a barrow and a spade and that none of those three things is a reason anybody can put in a column.

"I keep the gate at the top of this lane and I have come down because there is a stranger at a door, and I am going back up, and I have not come down to be spoken to."

"**I have come to this lane for a person in the third place of a book, and I have not found one yet, and I have been standing at this door since the seventh hour.** About four people in four hundred miles have said that a person who stands at a door for four hours is a person who is prepared to be told something, and no page in four hundred miles can stand at a door and be prepared for anything." She turned the case over in her hand. "I have a piece of paper in my coat with my own name on it and the day I chose written under it in my own hand, and I have not shown it to anybody and I am not going to show it to you, because it is the only piece of paper about me in the world and it is not a page about anybody else."

The woman of about forty-five looked at him for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then looked back at the door, and a woman who has looked at a man with a bar and then looked back at a door has decided that the man is not the third place, and that she will be right about that and that it will not matter at all.

---

The door was opened at about the eleventh hour and the asking went on until about the second hour of the afternoon, and a woman of about fifty-three opened her own door to a stranger at the eighth hour of the morning and then had a question put to her in her own words at the eleventh, and that those two things happening in one lane on one day are the whole of what is happening in this flood, and neither of the two women in that kitchen knew the other one was in the house.

"You have come to a house where a man is asking me a question, and my sheet came on the thirtieth, which was four days before the thirty-fourth, and it has been on that shelf since the thirtieth and I have not shown it to anybody, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who has had four days alone with a question has had the time the four days are for, and that a household which has used the four days is a household that can be said to have answered, and that nobody has ever worked out what a household which has not used them has done instead."

"I have come to a house where a man is asking you a question, and I have been at the door since the seventh hour, and I am not going to be told about it in a doorway, and about four people in four hundred miles have said that the two questions in that kitchen are the only two anybody has and that neither of them is mine." She put the case down on the boards inside the door and did not open it. "**I am going to sit down and I am going to be in this house until the light goes, and I am going to ask the same question four times today.**" About four people in that lane have said since that a person who asks the same question four times is a person who has come a long way and is not going to go home having asked it once."

"That is not a reason to sit in a woman's kitchen."

"**That is not a reason to sit in a woman's kitchen and I am going to sit in it.** About four people in four hundred miles have said that the only reason a person has for standing in a room is that the room is where the person is, and that this is the answer I was sent out here to find and that I have not found it, and I am going to look at it in about four kitchens before the light goes off."

---

The case sat on the boards by the sill for about an hour and was not opened, and about four people in that lane have said since that a case which has come nine hundred miles and has sat on a boards for an hour without being opened is a case that has arrived, and that this is the only thing anybody has ever sent that has arrived, and that a person sitting next to it has also arrived and that this is the difference nobody in a building nine hundred miles off can be shown.

The woman of about forty-five said that the fourth of the four places wanted the days, and that she was not going to ask for those in a kitchen either, and that a person who could produce the days was a person who could be asked to produce them every quarter, and that about four people in that lane have said since that a person who can be produced is a person who is produced, and that this is the reason the fourth place is the one nobody has got.

The woman of about fifty-three said that there were days and that they were not written anywhere and that she was not going to say how many, and about four people in that lane have said since that a household which has been asked for the days twice in one morning by two different people and has answered one of them and not the other has worked out something about a page and about a person that about four people in four hundred miles are still arguing about.

The woman of about fifty-three said yes in one sentence and said that the day was hers and was not going to be on a list, and about four people in that lane have said since that a household which has answered a question in the presence of a stranger from a building nine hundred miles off and has not given the stranger a single word about it has done the fourth of a thing in four days that nobody in four hundred miles knew could be done, and that the stranger heard it.

"You have said yes in front of her."

"I have said yes in front of her because she was in the room, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who says yes in front of a stranger and then says nothing else in front of the stranger has decided that the stranger is a person and not an office, and that a household which decides that about a stranger on the day it is asked has worked out in about an hour what about four offices in four hundred miles have not worked out in a hundred and eighty-nine days." She did not look at the case. "**And I have not given her a name and I am not going to.**" A name given to a person who has come for a column is the whole of what a column is, and the third place on her page is going to be empty, and that the woman who has just said yes has known that since Tuesday."

"Nobody in four hundred miles has said anything of the kind, and I have told her that once already this month and I am not going to tell her twice." The man of about thirty-two said it standing at the side of that fire and not sitting down. The third place on that page will be filled from a return, because a return is the only correct paper anybody in four hundred miles has ever held, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a man who says that out loud in a kitchen on a Wednesday morning has said it about a thing he is not going to be asked about either. "And I have not asked her anything, and she has not asked me anything, and the two people in that kitchen who have not been asked are the two who are asking, and that this is the fourth day running that the only people nobody asks anything are the ones doing the asking, and that this is the cheapest thing in this flood and the only thing a person cannot be entered in."

---

Nobody was entered in a column, and the case stayed shut on the boards of that kitchen until the light went off the stones, and the man of about thirty-six did not come up that lane again, and a Crown office has sent a person up a road to fill in the third of four places and has come back with two of the four and a shut case, and that a schedule cannot be filled in by anybody out here and that the only office which could take it back is nine hundred miles off and the next quarter is not a date anybody in that lane holds, and that a form that goes back empty is the ordinary end of a document and not a defeat, and that a person who has stood at a door for four hours is not ordinary, and that the only thing which came up that lane this morning that was not ordinary was the waiting.

The fourth of nine kitchens was asked on the thirty-fourth day of that month, a Wednesday, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door and a stranger in the kitchen, and the answer came back in a hand, and the hand was not given to the stranger.
