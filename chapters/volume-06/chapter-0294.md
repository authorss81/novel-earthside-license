# Chapter 0294 — The Days Go Back

A house at the end of a lane past two gates on that road had a sheet of paper on its sill on the thirty-ninth day of the Short Month, a Monday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, with a date in chalk on the door above it and the date two days old, because the sheet went out on the thirty-fifth and the thirty-fifth was four days back, and the light came in off the low ground and did not reach the lane past the first gate, and the cold was out of the wind and in the iron of the second gate's bar, and what was under about four pairs of hands on that lane that morning was a bar, a cart wheel that could not get past a barrow on its side, a slate, a lump of chalk, and a sheet of paper with a question on it in a hand that is a girl's.

---

The man of about thirty-six came down the lane with his bar and a bootful and emptied the bootful at the near end of it and did not go up, and the man who carries the question was standing at the second gate with his hands behind him, and the carter of about thirty-five was standing at the first one with nothing in his hands for the second time in six days.

"You are the ninth of nine and I am not going to be in that house when the last one is said and I am telling you that now so that nobody in four hundred miles can say afterwards that I was stood in the doorway."

"You are not going to be in that house and I have not asked you to be, and about four people on this lane have said since that a man who announces where he is not going to be on the morning of the day is a man who has worked out what the day is." The man of about thirty-six did not look at him. "The man who went up this lane on the thirty-third came back down it and said so in a room over a market, and everybody in that room has heard it, and I have heard it, and I am going to be stood at this gate all morning and I will not come up."

---

The sheet was on the sill of that door and the woman of about fifty-four had it face up and had been reading it since the thirty-fifth, and about four people on this lane have said since that a woman who has had a sheet face up on a sill for two days has not been waiting to be asked anything, and that the whole of the asking this week has been standing in front of a door with nothing in its hands, and that the two of those facts are the same fact.

Then a man's voice came out of that door, and it was not raised, and it was the voice of a man of about fifty-eight who had answered a carter on the thirty-third with nothing in a hand and had not been asked a single question about the thirty-ninth, and what he said was four sentences long and every one of them was for the lane and not for the man at the gate.

"**There are days on this lane and they are ours, and they have been ours since before the water, and this morning we are taking them off, and we are not doing it for anybody and we are not saying it to anybody.**"

The lane heard it. The man of about thirty-six heard it and put the bar down against the post and left it there, and the carter of about thirty-five heard it and wiped his thumb on his belt and the tar came off onto the leather again, and about four people on this lane have said since that the whole of what happened on that road this morning was said out of a door at about the seventh hour by a man who had already said the same thing to a stranger six days earlier without a sheet and been believed, and that nobody collected it and nobody wrote it down and nobody was in a position to ask him to.

---

The woman of about fifty-four came out onto the step with the sheet in her hand and put it on the step and came back in and shut the door, and about four people on this lane have said since that a woman who puts a sheet face up on a step and goes back inside without saying anything else is a woman who has decided the sheet is finished, and that a sheet which is finished is a thing nobody can pick up again and use, and that this is the fourth time this week that a sheet has been left face up somewhere by a woman who is not on any roll.

A boy of about ten came up the lane with a plain slate under his arm and a lump of chalk in his fist and stopped at the first gate and put the slate against the bar and began to write the dates on it in a row, and then rubbed them out, and then began again, and about four people on this lane have said since that a boy of ten who cannot count has been sent to count a road and that nobody sent him with a paper, and that he is going to get some of it wrong and that the getting of it wrong is the only account of the thirty-ninth anybody will have, and that nobody is going to correct him.

---

The man who carries the question was still at the second gate at about the ninth hour and the sun came over the low ground and did not get into the lane at all, and about four people on that lane have said since that a man who has stood at a gate for two hours in the cold because he has decided not to be in a room is a man who has finally understood something about the instrument he built, and that what he has understood is that four days of notice is for the person being asked and not for the person asking, and that he had known that since the twenty-eighth of that month and had not known what to do with it until the morning of the thirty-ninth.

The woman of about fifty-four came out onto the step again at about the middle of the morning and stood on it in the one place on that lane where the sun was not, and she did not call anybody and she did not look up the lane, and after a while she said the second thing out of that house, and it was two sentences and it was not louder than the first.

"**Nobody has to do anything on the strength of what was said this morning, and if anybody comes up this lane with a paper about it, the answer is that we said it once and it was not for a paper.**" She went back in and shut the door, and a household which hears its own four sentences repeated at it by a stranger all morning does not get angry about it, and this one did not even answer the repetition, because the first four sentences had been said to a lane and the second two had been said to a paper, and a lane and a paper are not the same size.

"You could go up."

"I could go up and I have not gone up, and I am going to be told what happens in there by somebody who was in there, and I am not going to be told it by myself, and that is the last decision I am going to make about this week and I made it on the thirty-sixth."

---

The man of about thirty-six picked his bar up off the post at about the tenth hour and carried it up the lane to the top gate and did not come down again, and the carter of about thirty-five went out of the lane at the near end and got his cart round the barrow on its side by backing it nine feet and then pulled forward, and the boy of about ten was still at the first gate with the chalk in his fist and about half of the thirty-ninth on his slate and about nine wrong.

Then a woman of about nineteen came up that lane from the road at about the eleventh hour, at a run, and she had been in a house on that road since the tenth hour and had not come up the lane for any other reason, and she was through the second gate and past the barrow and up to the man who carries the question before he had got a hand to the post, and she was not out of breath, and about four people on that lane have said since that a woman of nineteen who comes up a lane at a run and is not out of breath has been in a room and has come out of it and is not stopping, and that she is nineteen and she has been in this flood since she was six, and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever entered her anywhere.

"You were not in the house."

"I was not in the house."

"**The last one said it about the eleventh hour and you were not in the house, and I was, and I have come down to tell you and I am going to tell you the whole of it because nobody else is going to and I have decided that in about four seconds on the stair.**" She put her hands on her knees and did not go down them. "The house at the end of this lane put a sheet face up on its step and went back in and shut its door, and I was not in it, and I am not going to invent what was in it for a man who was not in it either."

"They have had the sheet since the thirty-fifth and they have not written on it, and the man said it out of the door at about the seventh hour and everybody on this lane heard it, and then the woman came back in and shut the door, and then about four other houses on this lane did the same thing in about the next hour, one after another, and nobody said anything to anybody and nobody was asked, and the last of them was not on this lane at all."

---

The man who carries the question did not ask her a question, and the man who has carried a question into nine rooms in nine days and has never been asked one was told the whole of the ninth day by a woman of nineteen who was not asked either, and the only two people in nine rooms who are never asked anything are the one who is asking and the one who has been sent to say that it is finished.

"Tell me the rest of it and then I am going to say one thing back and I am not going to ask you anything."

"There is no rest of it. That is the rest of it." She straightened up. "Nobody has signed anything. Nobody has sent anybody. **Four of them had said yes in writing four days before and the rest had not been asked in time and every one of them said it this morning anyway, and not one of them said it to you, and not one of them is waiting to hear from you.**"

---

The sun came off the low ground about half an hour after that and got as far as the first gate and stopped, and about four people on that lane have said since that a lane with two gates in it is the only place in four hundred miles where a thing can be said that everybody hears and nobody can be shown to have said it, and that the two gates have been shut since the second week of this flood and that nobody has opened either of them all morning, and that a thing said between two shut gates has no room it can be carried into and cannot be entered in anything, and that this is the first instrument anybody in this flood has built that cannot be carried anywhere at all.

The man who carries the question stood at the second gate until the light was off the stones and said, to a boy of about ten with a slate and a lump of chalk, four sentences about a road that had come up that lane from the north on the fourth day and had not come down it in the evening, and about four people on that lane have said since that a man who says four sentences about a road he is not on is a man telling a child something he is not going to be thanked for, and that the child wrote it on the slate in about nine words and got about nine of them wrong.

---

And about four people on that lane have said since that the days went back on the thirty-ninth day of that month, a Monday, in daylight, at about the seventh hour, out of a door, in four sentences, and that nobody in four hundred miles asked any of the nine for it, and that four of them had said yes in their own words four days before and five of them had not been asked in time and all nine said it the same morning, and that the schedule has four places in it and a column of days belongs to a household and there is nobody in this city who can be shown to be holding any of them, and that the road north of the Marches did not open again after that night and nobody shut it, and that a boy of about ten is the only person in four hundred miles with a record of the thirty-ninth and that he is not to be corrected and is not on any roll and has not been asked one question.
