# Chapter 0253 — The Queue Runs And The Ninth Line

About a hundred and twenty people went through one door in the yard of the store on the upper road on the ninety-eighth day of the Long Month, and it is a Tuesday, and the queue is a Tuesday, and the light was the flat white that comes off eleven miles of flats with a wind behind it, and the cold was in the hands and not in the air, and what was under about nine hundred pairs of hands all day was rope, a rail, and the ends of coats. The wind has been at that wall for twenty-four days. The board above that door carries the figures of the sixty-ninth day of that month and has not been written on since the seventieth, and those figures are twenty-nine days old this morning. A sack of salt is standing on nine feet of ground about nine feet off the trough and nobody goes near it.

---

Jonah Pike has run a warehouse on a river in another country for nineteen years and has been wrong about one thing in ten for as long as anybody in this city has been able to check, which is since about the fortieth day of this flood. He came up that road at about the seventh hour with a printed sheet in his hand and gave it to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall before he said a word, and the man of about thirty-four took it, looked at the wage at the top of it, and did not say whether that was right.

"You are early."

"I am at the front of my own queue at the seventh hour, which is where I have been every Tuesday since the forty-ninth day of that month, and today I am at the front of a question instead, and I have been at the back of a queue for two hundred and fifty-three days."

"You do not enjoy it."

"**I do not enjoy it, and I have four seconds to say so and I am spending them, because in about nine minutes there will be a hundred and twenty people in this yard and a man who has said the pleasant thing out loud has bought himself a morning.**"

Nobody in that room moved for a while, and the light in it went off the flats and up the far wall of it while they were not moving.

"You have a sheet."

"I have a sheet with a wage on it and I gave it to you before I said anything, and that is a tell, and everybody who has met me for a year knows it is a tell, and I have never worked out why I do it and I am not going to stand in a yard and work it out on a Tuesday."

The yard filled the way it fills. It came down the road in a line with gaps in it, because a line that has come down the same road for two hundred and fifty-three days has learned where the ground is soft. It bent twice. Once round the trough, and once round nine feet of ground with a sack standing in the middle of it, and neither bend was announced, and nobody in the line had been told to make it, and about four people in that yard have worked out that the second bend is the oldest thing in it and that nobody remembers the queue going round anything else.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his boards out at the back of that yard, as he has on a Tuesday for as long as anybody there can remember, and about a hundred and twenty people went past them.

The work at that door was two people on a rail and a third on a tally-board that has been scrubbed so often it has no face left, and the cold had got into about nine hundred hands by the fourth hour, and by the ninth hour two of the people on that rail were doing the thing with their thumbs where the rail is, which is not a habit but a way of holding on. A woman came up the road at about the eighth hour with a child on her hip and a bag she could not put down, and she stood in the line for the length of about a hundred and twenty people and then went in, and the line closed behind her the way water closes behind a stone, and about four people in that yard have worked out that the line is the only thing in it that behaves the same on every Tuesday and that nobody has ever told it to.

"The board is not written on."

"The board is not written on and I have not washed the wall above it since the seventieth day of that month and I have decided not to, and the first of those has not been improved on and the second one I made this morning at about the seventh hour."

"You wash the board."

"**Every morning since the seventh week of this flood. There is a figure on it from the sixty-ninth day of that month and that figure is twenty-nine days old and it is still the true figure about the sixty-ninth day of that month, and it will be the true figure about it in four months.**"

A woman of about twenty-four turned her slate over and wrote the date on the back of it, and about four people in that room saw her do it and none of them said a word.

"And the wall above it."

He wrung the cloth out once, hung it on the rim of the trough, and stood about nine feet back and looked at the two of them together for about as long as it takes to fill a pail. He did not say one word about the wall, then or at any other hour of that day, and the cloth stayed on the rim of the trough from the eighth hour until about the fourth hour of the afternoon and got a skin of dust on it where it hung.

---

The ninth line is the only line on that wall anybody can read from the ground. It is the store and its queue, and it is in a harder chalk and a different hand from the eight above it, which are there and cannot be read, and there is no date at the top of that wall anywhere, and there has not been a date at the top of that wall for about four weeks and about four people in this yard have stopped looking for it and about nine have not. Jonah Pike read it at about the ninth hour, standing at the back of his own queue, and did not read it out loud, and has not read it out loud since, and about four people in that yard had worked out inside a week that the ninth line was the reason any of them came up that road at all. About nine of them had not said so out loud. One of them said so on the ninety-first day of that month and was not asked again.

"Here is the thing I have come up the road for."

"Say the thing."

"**Whether a store nine miles up a road is on the ground adjoining a crossing. That is the question, and it has to be settled in a room, in daylight, with a date on the door, and I am not going to settle it in a yard, and I have been on this road four times in ten days working on it in my head and I have got nowhere.**"

"You have run a store for nineteen years."

The wind came at that wall from the same quarter it had been coming from for a month, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had stopped noticing it and had started listening to it.

"I have run a store for nineteen years in another country and I am right about nine parts in ten of the things I look at, and here are three things I think about that docket, and two of them are right and the third one I cannot finish."

"Go on."

"**One. It is correct.**"

"That is not three things."

A man at the back put his weight on one foot and then on the other and did not say anything for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet.

"**Two. It does not name this store, and a document that does not name a thing is not a thing that takes it, and that is the only protection anybody in this city has got, and it works by accident.**"

"And the man who wrote that line."

"He put this store on a page on purpose fifty-two days ago and he has not moved it since, and he has refused five times to say whether the line works, and I have not asked him a sixth time and I am not going to ask him in a yard."

"Three."

Somebody at the door said something to somebody else and neither of them came in, and the room did not turn round.

"**The Crown does not do a thing like that for nothing.**"

"That is the one you cannot finish."

"**That is the one I cannot finish, and I have turned it over on this road and on the road back, and I am not going to guess at it in a yard in front of a hundred and twenty people. I would rather be wrong in a room.**"

"Then take it up the road."

A chair was moved about four inches along a boards floor by a person who had not been asked to move it, and about four people in that room have said since that they have never found out who moved it.

"I have taken four things up that road in two hundred and fifty-three days and three of them were salt and one of them was a bell that somebody wanted back, and a road is nine miles long and it goes past nine hundred people who all want to know what is in the cart, and I have been wrong about a road twice in nineteen years and both times it was because I thought the road would keep still while I did the other thing."

"And the copy."

"And the copy. I am going to take the copy up the road to nine people I can name without a page, and about four people in this yard will tell me I have done the right thing and about nine will not say anything, and I have noticed that the nine are the ones I am actually asking."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not answer him. He looked at the ninth line on the wall for a while, and then at the sack on its nine feet of ground, and then at the board with its figures twenty-nine days old, and about four people in that yard watched him do it and none of them said anything. The trestle count was two hundred and thirteen on the two hundred and fifty-third day of this flood, and it is one a day from the fortieth day of this flood and nothing else, and it is on a page eleven miles back down the road and in no mouth in that yard.

---

At about the eleventh hour Jonah Pike made a copy of the four lines of the docket on the back of a sheet he had a week of nothing on. He did it himself, in his own hand, and he got the four places in the order they were in and put nothing else on it, and then he said out loud that he was going to take it up the road to nine people he could name without a page. About four people in that yard said that was a good idea. About nine said nothing.

A woman of about thirty-four in that store, who is on no list in that building and has never been on one, was at the back of the yard with a pail in each hand. She came about nine feet of ground short of the sack and stopped, and about nine people going past her went round her as though she were the sack, which she has noticed and has said out loud once.

"You are taking that up the road."

"**I am taking it up the road to nine people I can name without a page, and it takes me four days, and four of the nine will be in different places on four of the days, and I have never once in nineteen years had a page fail to arrive.**"

"A copy of a page is a page."

"It is a copy of a page, and the page is correct, and a correct page in nine hands is nine times the chance of a correct page in one hand, and I have been doing that since I was twenty-six."

The page on the table was turned so that the writing faced the man who could not read it, and about nine people in that room have said that this happened and that nobody has said out loud who turned it.

"That is four sentences and none of them is about the thing you are carrying."

"No."

"**A copy of a page is a page. A thing that is not on a page is not a document, and it is on no page, and there is no office in four hundred miles that can be given it, and a road is not an office and neither is a yard and neither is a man with nine names.**"

He stood in that yard with the copy in his hand for about as long as it takes to get a hand wet. Then he put it down on the boards of the stall at the back, and left it there, and it was still on those boards when the yard emptied at about the sixth hour of the evening, and about four people coming past the stall on their way out looked at it and went round it, the way a line goes round a thing it has not been told about.

"I have never been at the front of a question in my life."

"No."

"**I have run a store for nineteen years and I have been at the back of my own queue every Tuesday since the forty-ninth day of that month, and I liked it.**"

"You did like it."

Somebody wrote a figure on the back of their own hand and then put their hand in a pocket, and about four people in that room have said that they have each done that about nine times in about nine weeks.

"**I did. And I do not know what to do with the front of a question, and I am not going to find out standing here.**"

"You have not said a word about that line."

"**That line on that wall is the only reason a hundred and twenty people came up that road today, and I have not said a word about it, and neither have you, and I would like it entered that I did not say a word about it.**"

---

And a copy of a correct page lay on the boards of a stall in the yard of the store on the upper road, and the ninth line was nine inches above a board that had not been written on for twenty-eight days, and the man who washed the board said nothing, and about four people in that city have said since that he is the only person in that yard who has read it.
