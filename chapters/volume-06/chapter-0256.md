# Chapter 0256 — The Hundred and First

The room with a door had a date in it at about the ninth hour on the first day of a month that did not exist when the day before it was written, and the date was in a form nobody in that room had written before, and about two hundred and forty people could hear out of the hall and the landing. On the table in that room, at the left-hand end, a docket from nine hundred miles away lay with a date at the head of it in a form that had ended the day before, and both of those dates were correct in the form they were written in. The board above the door of the store on the upper road carries the figures of the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month, which are thirty-two days old, and has not been written on since the day after them, and the wind has been at that wall for twenty-seven days.

---

Sera Vail has been a notary for twenty-six years and has certified about nine hundred things, and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence. She came into that room at about the ninth hour with a slate under her arm and she did not sit down, and a woman of about twenty-four was standing at the table with a chalk in her hand and a page in front of her and she was not writing on it.

"You have stopped."

"**I have stopped. I have written a date at the head of a page every day since the first day of the Long Month and yesterday was the hundredth day of that month.**"

"And today is."

"Today is a hundred and one, and there is no hundred and first day of anything in this world, and there has never been one, because a month is not a thing that goes on for ever. I have run out of month."

Nobody in that room moved for about as long as it takes to say a thing twice, and the hall outside stopped giving the room's voices back, and the two of those happened within about four seconds of each other.

"Then write the flood."

"**Then a person reading that page in four months does not know which of the four hundred days of it it was, and I have not been doing that since the fortieth day of this flood and I am not going to begin on a Friday.**"

"You are a recorder. You write what you are given."

"I write what I am given, and there is a difference between a recorder and a notary that took me nine years to learn. A recorder writes down the day it is. A notary certifies the day a thing was done. This morning I have been asked to certify a day and there is no day to certify, and I have been asked it in a room with a date on the door that I did not write and cannot certify either."

The woman of about twenty-four turned her slate over, wrote the date on the back of it, and then went on holding the chalk in her other hand without using it, and about four people in that room saw her do both and none of them said a word.

"Who should be asked about a name for it."

"Nobody. A month is not a thing a person gets to name, and I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I have never once had to name anything. The person who would most obviously be asked is sitting against that wall and she said no at about the eighth hour, before I was in the room."

---

The woman of about seventy against that wall is from a village of nine households that has refused four times. She had come up the road in a cart and she had not wanted to come and she had come, and she had said no in a room before the notary was in it, and about four people in that building had heard her say it and about nine had not.

"Will you say the reason again, for the room."

"A name given to a month is a name a clerk will use. In about four months a clerk nine hundred miles away is going to write it at the head of a page, and nobody in that building will be able to tell whether it means the month this room means or a month they have heard of from somewhere else."

"That is a good reason."

"It is my reason and it is not good, it is correct, and I have been asked nothing for a long time and I am not going to be asked this. I have said no four times in about nine years and every one of the four was correct and I would like the four of them left where they are."

The cart the woman of about seventy had come up the road in had gone back down it by the tenth hour, and about nine people in that room have said since that they had stopped watching the door for it and had started watching the wall instead.

"You may be asked to hear the name."

"I may be asked to hear the name and I will hear it and I will not like it, and both of those are mine, and I am not going to be asked which of the two I would rather."

Nobody in that room had an answer to that. The hall outside had gone quiet in the way a hall goes quiet when the people in it have decided to listen, which is not the same as being quiet, and about four people in that room have said that the difference was in the landing and not in the room. And about four people in it had already worked out that the woman of about fifty-six who keeps a shop four streets down from the market had come to this room for the same reason and had said the same three words in a market the day before, and the notary had been told about it in that market and had said that she would do it in a room and not in a market, and here she was.

The notary stood at the table and did not sit down and put her slate flat on it, and then she said the cost out loud before she said the name, and it was hers, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the first time in twenty-six years they had heard her give the price of a thing before she did it.

"**A name invented by a notary is a name an office can use. In about four months a clerk nine hundred miles away is going to write it at the head of a page and nobody in that building will be able to tell whether it means what I mean.**"

"That is the reason the woman against the wall gave you."

"**It is the same reason and she gave it better and she gave it first. I am giving it again because I am the one who is going to do it.**"

"Say the rest of the cost."

A man at the back of that room changed his foot and the boards went under it, and the notary went on with the cost as though the boards had not.

"**I have been the only person in this city keeping a count of my own counting since the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else, and I am tired of it.**"

"That is a small cost."

"**It is the only cost there is. The two months before this one ran to sixty-five days and I registered this one on that pattern, because the pattern was the one in front of me, and a form does not stop a month. I am going to pay it in front of you.**"

"You did this before."

Somebody in the hall said one word to the person beside them and the person beside them said it back, and about four people in that room heard it and about nine did not, and none of the three turned round.

"I did this before, on the first day of the Long Month, in a room with a date on the door, and about nine people were in it and about two hundred and forty could hear out of the hall, and nobody refused me that morning, and I have thought about that on about nine days since and I have got to the bottom of it this week. Nobody refused me because there was nothing to refuse. There was no page with a column in it, so there was nothing to put a name into, and a person will refuse a name for a thing that is only a name and will not refuse a name for a thing that is going into a page."

"Then that is what has changed."

"That is exactly what has changed and I would like it entered in those words, because about four people in this room came here to watch a woman name a month and what has actually happened is that a column arrived from nine hundred miles away five days before I did this."

"Name it."

Nobody in that room sat down, and about nine people in the hall had to stand up because the front row stood up, and the notary waited for that to finish before she said it.

"The Short Month. It is sixty days long and it ends on the sixtieth day of it. The hundred and first day of the Long Month and the first day of the Short Month are this morning, and both of those are true, and a page that says one of them is wrong."

---

About four minutes later the wall said one thing, and it said it once, and it is entered that nobody in that room can be told where it came from, and that the woman of about seventy against the wall went the colour of the wall and then did not.

> A name given to a thing so that the thing can go in a column is not the name of the thing.

"Then it is refused, and it is entered that I said no to it in about four seconds. I will tell the room what refusing it has cost, because a refusal that costs nobody anything is not worth the counting. I have just invented a name in a room and I am going to keep the name. That is what it cost."

"It is not much of a cost."

"It is the whole of the cost and I have paid it and I am not going to be told about it by a woman of about twenty-four who has been writing dates on doors since the eighth hour this morning. The count of refusals said out loud in this city is one larger than it was at the ninth hour this morning, and I am not going to join that up with anything, because there are two counts in two books in this city that have never been made smaller and this does not make either of them smaller."

"You will keep the name."

The docket at the other end of that table was turned so that the writing on it faced a man who could not read it, and about four people in that room have said since that it was done for him and that he did not look up.

"I will keep the name. I have put my own name on a month twice in a hundred days now, and the first time was because there was no page at all and the second time is because a page had a column in it, and I have not improved on either and I am not going to."

Then the woman of about seventy against that wall spoke for the second time in that room, and nobody had asked her anything.

"You did not need me to say no. You wanted a person in the room to have said no before you did it, and I have been a person somebody was asked for before, and it is not a comfortable thing to be used for, and I would like it entered that I said that and that I am still here."

"You are still here."

"**I am still here, and I am seventy, and behind me there is a village of nine households that has refused four times.**"

"The fifth is a name."

About four people in that room wrote something on the back of their own hands in that half-minute and about nine wrote nothing, and the woman of about seventy against the wall watched all of it from where she was sitting and did not ask them what they had written.

"**The fifth is a name at the head of a page and not a thing anybody has done. I am not going to take it back to them and tell them it was nothing, and I am not going to tell them it was a good thing either, because I do not know.**"

"Then what will you tell them."

"**That a refusal nobody records is not a refusal, and about four people in that village have been saying that to me for nine years, and this morning they are right and it is the worst morning I have had in nine years.**"

"Then let it be recorded."

The woman of about twenty-four put the page down flat in the middle of that table, and about four hands came towards it and about nine stayed where they were, and the woman of about seventy against the wall looked at the nine and not at the four.

"**Then let it be recorded in a room and not in a book, because a book is what the clerk writes the number in, and about nine households behind me have found out once already what a book with a number in it does.**"

---

The reading back was the last work of that day and it was done for the hall and the landing, and about two hundred and forty people heard it twice, and the two readings were the date, the name and the reason, in that order, and the reason was the woman of about seventy's and not the notary's, and about four people in that hall have said that putting a refusal into a reading back in front of two hundred and forty people is a thing nobody in this city has ever done and that it was the notary's decision and not the old woman's. A girl of seventeen read it from a plain slate and not from her own, and her own has her name at the top of it, and the date, the name and the reason were on the plain slate in a hand that was not hers, and she read them twice in the same words, and the second time she read them more slowly than the first, and nobody in that hall has said since that the second reading was the one that did it.

At about the fifth hour of the afternoon she wrote a date at the head of a page for the first time in a form that had not existed when she was born, and it was the first day of the Short Month, and she wrote it in her own hand, and it is on a page in this city with a date on it, and about four people in the room with the door watched her do it and nobody said anything at all. Nobody stopped her and nobody helped her and nobody told her what to write, and the two hundred and forty in the hall and the landing had heard the form read out twice before she wrote it, and that is the whole of what makes a form, and a man of thirty-two in this city has said that sentence before about something else and it is not improved on by a notary.

And on the same table at the other end the docket from nine hundred miles away lay with its own date at the head of it, and that date is the hundred and first day of the Long Month, and it is in a form that ended the day before, and the two dates on that one table are a month apart and both of them are correct, and about nine people in four hundred miles know which of the two was chosen by a person.
