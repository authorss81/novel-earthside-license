# Chapter 0298 — Nobody Says It

A room over a market in this city had a date in chalk on its door on the forty-third day of the Short Month, a Friday, from about the tenth hour of the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was in it with a piece of chalk in his hand that he had not used, and the light came in on the south side and went off the floor by about the middle of the afternoon, and the cold was in the shutters and in a cart-load of kindling that came and went four times in an hour outside the door, and what was in that room was a second slate, a piece of chalk, a slate the man of about thirty-four had brought with him and had not turned over, and nothing else at all.

---

The woman of about fifty-one came into that room with the corner of her sacking knotted in her hand and went out again and came in a second time and shouted at a boy she could not see through the shutters, and about four people in that market have said since that a room where a woman comes in and out four times with a knotted corner in her hand and shouts at somebody who cannot be heard is a room where two people can talk about a line on a wall nine miles up a road, and that this is the fourth time in a month that a room has been made talkable by somebody else's delivery.

The man of about thirty-four turned the chalk over in his fingers twice and put it down on the table in front of him where it could be seen, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts a piece of chalk down in the middle of a table so that everybody can see that he is not using it is a man who has decided to be visible in a room, and that he has never once done it before this month.

---

"You have written nothing on the wall."

"I have written nothing on the wall and I am not going to and the wall is nine miles up that road and I have not been up it in four days and I am not going on a Friday." He put the chalk down again, the same way. "**I would like to say one thing in this room before anybody else says anything, and it is a short one.** I have said a version of it before and I would like it said again now and then I am going to sit down."

---

He said it, and about four people in that market have said since that it was that he does not want to be thanked for it, and nobody in that room thanked him, and a man who asks in advance not to be thanked and is not thanked has done the only thing in this flood that has been asked for and not given, and that a person who does not want a thing is the only kind of person a page cannot be written about, and that the whole of the ninth line on a wall nine miles up that road is that, and it is still on the wall and it has not been dated and it has not been read out and it is not going to be.

---

The woman of about twenty-four came in at about the middle of the morning and did not sit down and put her own hands flat on the table on either side of the second slate, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who is the account of documents in this city putting her hands on a table on either side of a slate is a woman who has decided that whatever happens to that slate next will happen in front of her, and that she has been on the wrong side of a page for about a month and has not moved.

"I have read a roll of gates in this city and a roll of people in the Marches and they are the same gates and they are not the same thing, and about four people in this room worked that out in about nine days and none of us has ever put them on one table."

"You have not."

"I have not and I am not going to and I have said that out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door on purpose, so that if anybody in four hundred miles ever asks me whether anybody ever put them together, I have got an answer with a date against it and not a memory." She took her hands off the table. "**I have not worked out what the difference is worth and I am not going to, and anybody who tells you I have is talking about a person who has not read them both.**"

---

The woman of about fifty-one came in a second time and put her hand flat on the table beside the chalk for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then took it off and went out again without saying anything, and a woman who puts a hand on a table beside a piece of chalk and takes it off again has said a thing about that chalk that she is not going to say out loud, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that the only conversations anybody has had this year about that line have happened within nine inches of it.

"Then nobody is going to say it and I am not going to make anybody say it and we can get on."

"We can get on, and I want to be exact about what getting on is, because about four people in this city have spent a month being vague about it." She put the chalk where he had put it and did not pick it up. "**The schedule stands and it is correct and it is on a table in that room, and there are four places in it and three of them are empty, and the third of them wants a person.**" She put her thumb on the slate. "The person who is supposed to be in it is a man with a bar of oak shod with iron and a chain over a pulley about four hundred miles up that road, and this city will not name him, and nobody has asked him, and he has never been asked one question in about four hundred days."

What the third place wants is a holder. What the road has got, at that gate and at every gate the Marches keeps, is a keeper, and the two words have never once been in the same line of the same hand, and the woman of about twenty-four read one of the two rolls out loud on the twenty-fifth of that month and did not read the other one at all.

---

The man of about thirty-four said that the sentence about the bar and the chain was not his to correct and that about nine people in that room had worked out the same thing over about a month and that none of them had ever written it down, and that a description is a removal with a column for where the person is, and that he had heard that said out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door about six weeks ago and had not been in the room when it was said and had worked out what it was by himself over about nine days.

"You worked it out by yourself."

"I worked it out by myself and I got here by myself and it took me about nine days and about four hundred miles of road, and I have never once been asked to say it out loud and nobody is going to ask me today." He turned the slate he had brought over, face down, and left it face down. "**And the word in the third place of that page is on a page in this city and on a page in the Marches and neither of them has ever been defined by anybody, and I am not going to define it.**" He did not turn the slate over. "If I did it would be the first definition in four hundred miles, and it would be a word invented in a room, and that is a thing a clerk nine hundred miles off can use."

---

The woman of about fifty-one came in a third time and put a bundle of withy down against the door and went out again, and came in a fourth time at about the third hour and unknotted the corner of her sacking and said that a house up past the last named house had sent for a bundle and would not be having one, and a woman who unknots a corner of her own sacking in a room where nobody is talking to her is a woman putting an ordinary end to an ordinary morning, and that the man of about thirty-four looked at her while she did it and said nothing, and that this is the fourth time in about a month that somebody in that room has gone back to a thing they were doing before the conversation started, and that nobody has ever once told them they could, and that a woman who puts a bundle down against a door in the middle of a conversation about a line on a wall has decided that the bundle is going to be there when the conversation ends and that the conversation is going to end before the bundle is collected, and that the two most durable things anybody owns in this flood are a queue and a bundle of withy.

"The truce holds because a raft is going to go to nine places on nine days and a household is heard at each of them, and nobody has signed it and nobody is going to, and about four people on that water worked out this morning that a thing which holds because nine people came to a raft is not a treaty and it is not going to be improved on into one."

"And what does that leave the bridge."

"It leaves the bridge where it has been since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month, which is a store and a queue on a wall, and the queue is the only instrument in four hundred miles that cannot be entered in a schedule, because a queue is a line nobody decided, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that the whole of what is left after a document is a thing that is not a document." He put the chalk down for the third time and his hand stayed on the table beside it. "**The woman at the top of that road has not been defeated and has not been exposed and has not changed her mind, and her page is still correct, and the next quarter is not a date anybody in this room holds.**"

---

Nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him whether the ninth line works, and every refusal this month has been about a name and not about a line, and that the man of about thirty-four did not give a reason in that room and did not give a second one, and that he gave a reason once and it is on a page and it is still standing, and that a reason which has been given once and has worked is not a thing that gets improved on every time a person is asked, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a man who gives you a new reason every time he is asked is a man negotiating, and that this one has not been negotiating since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month.

"You have not been thanked."

"**I have not been thanked and I asked for that on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and I have asked for it since, and I would like it struck out of any list anybody is keeping of the things I have said.**" His hand was still beside the chalk. "And I would like a piece of chalk put on this table that nobody is going to use, and I would like to go home."

The man of about thirty-four put the chalk down for the fourth time and it did not roll, because that table has a lip on that side, and he looked at it for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and a man who has put a piece of chalk down four times in an afternoon and looked at it four times is a man who has about nine days left of deciding whether to do something with it, and that nobody in that room has any idea what those nine days are for.

"I will be asked again," he said, and nobody in that room asked him again, and a man who says *I will be asked again* in a room where nobody is asking him anything is a man describing a season, and that he has been doing it since the first week of the second month of this flood and that it is the only forecast anybody has made this year that has not been wrong yet.

The man of about thirty-four did not turn the slate over and the woman of about twenty-four read the date on the door from the step on her way out, and neither of them said the other one out loud, and two people in a room who can both read a date on a door and do not say it to each other are two people who have both worked out that a date read out loud in a room gets entered.

And on the forty-third day of that month, a Friday, a schedule with a heading and four places on it was left standing in a room over a market in a city that cannot write on its own wall, and that a bridge is a store and a queue, and that a queue is a line nobody decided, and that the man who put the last line there has refused five times to say whether it works and has not moved it and has not dated it and has not had it read out, and that nobody thanked him, and that a raft went to nine places on nine days without a page on it, and that a road north of the Marches is shut and a road west of it will not be kept till the season turns, and that a correct page from an office nine hundred miles off is still correct.
