# Chapter 0287 — The Price Of No Name

A woman of about twenty-four put a date on the door of her own house in chalk on the thirty-second day of the Short Month, a Monday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, because a sheet had come to that house on the twenty-eighth, which was four days before, in a hand that is a girl's, with a faltering in the fourth line that nobody tidied, and about four people in that market town have said since that a woman who is the account of documents in this city had a question of her own put through her own door four days before anybody thought to ask her whether it suited her, and that nobody thought to ask her because nobody asks her anything.

---

The light in that kitchen came in off a yard and did not get past the second board, and the cold was out of the wind and in the stone, and a man of about sixty-two sat with his back to the fire mending a boot with a needle and a thread of his own, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who mends a boot by a fire while his daughter is asked a question by a stranger is a man who has decided that what is happening in his kitchen is not his business and is going to be about nine minutes long.

"You are four days late asking me whether I want to be asked."

"I am not four days late, and the notice went out on the twenty-eighth, which is four days ago, and the room is today, which is the thirty-second, and nobody asked you whether the thirty-second suited you and nobody is going to." He put the sheet down on the boards with the writing facing her and did not put it down near her. "**A question that has been in a house for four days is the only kind of question that survives a door, and you are the account of documents in this city and you have said that about four times this month without ever once being on the wrong side of it.**"

"**I have been on the wrong side of it since Thursday morning and I have had four days to work out what that costs.**" She did not turn the sheet over. "It costs that a sheet with no name at the top of it is worth nothing to a household that is in trouble. A household that cannot read has to be able to hold a thing up and show it, and a thing with no name on it cannot be shown to anybody, because the first thing a clerk asks is whose it is. **So the price of no name is that when I go to the room over that market with a thing in my hand, nobody in it can do anything for me.**"

---

The man of about sixty-two pulled the thread through the boot and turned it and pulled it through again, and about four people in that house have said since that a man mending a boot has both hands full and a woman answering a question has one, and that neither of them can be in the other's way, and that about four people in this market have worked out that a kitchen in which two people are doing something with their hands is a kitchen in which a question can be asked at all.

"You are the account of documents and you have just said that a document with no name on it cannot be used, and about four people in this market have said since that this is the first time anybody has said the price out loud."

"I have said the price out loud because nobody else is going to, and because about four people in this market have worked out this month that a thing which cannot be used is a thing which cannot be taken, and that this is a good thing to have found out about a page, and that it is also a thing that can kill about four people a winter." She put her thumb on the edge of the sheet and did not move it. "**And I am not going to put a name on it, and I want it entered in this house that I know what that costs and I have decided to pay it, and that nobody in this kitchen is going to argue with me about it, and that includes you.**"

"You are not going to argue with me about it because you have already decided and the sheet is on the table."

"I have decided and the sheet is on the table, and the answer is written, and it is in my hand, and it is in a box on the shelf with a mark at the bottom of it that is not a name, and you are not going to take it and I am not going to give it to you." She put the pot on over the fire without taking her eyes off him. "**And I am going to read it to you once and then it goes back in the box, and about four people in this market have worked out that the first two kitchens have done exactly this and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to end up with a single piece of paper out of any of them.**"

---

"What is in it."

"**Two lines and a day and a mark that is not a name, and the two lines say yes and the day is not one of your nine.** About four people in that market have said since that a household which answers in a day that is not on the list has done the only thing anybody in this flood has done this month that a clerk could not turn into a column."

"That is not an answer, that is a refusal with a yes in the front of it."

"That is not a refusal and I am not going to argue about the word." She read the two lines out and they were what she had said and nothing else was in them, and she put them back in the box. "A hand is not a page. **A page can be carried to a room and laid on a table and kept, and a hand stays in the hand that made it until that person dies, and then it is nobody's.** About four people in this market have worked out that this is the only document in four hundred miles that cannot be entered in a column, and that it also cannot be used to get anybody anything, and that both of those are the same fact and not two facts."

Nobody said anything for a while and the boot went on and the pot went on and the light came as far as the second board and stopped, and about four people in that house have said since that about four people in four hundred miles have now worked out that a document which cannot be enforced and cannot be produced is a document that can only be obeyed, and that nobody in this flood has ever obeyed one.

"Nobody is turned away by this sheet."

"Nobody is turned away by this sheet and nobody is helped by it, and about four people in that market have said since that the first of those is what a household wants and the second of those is what a household is frightened of, and that both of those are true at the same time in a kitchen on the thirty-second day of that month." She put the box back on the shelf. "**And I am the account of documents in this city, and I have spent about nine years making things easy to find, and I have put two lines in a box on a shelf in my own kitchen with no name on them, and about four people in this market have worked out that the woman who reads pages out to other people has just become the only one of them who cannot be found.**"

---

"I have had four days with that sheet on the boards in that kitchen and I have done four things with it and none of them is reading it." She put the pot on the fire and moved it off the hottest part of it. "**I have written an answer, and I have taken it to the room over that market and shown it to a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a register and asked her whether a thing with no name on it can be entered anywhere.** She said no, and she said it in front of about four people, and about four people in that room have worked out that a register-keeper who says no to a piece of paper is a rare thing and that this month it has happened twice."

"And what did you say to her."

"I said that I did not want it entered, and she said that was the first time in about a month anybody had asked her not to enter something rather than asking her to refuse to enter it, and about four people in that market have worked out since that those are two different requests and that the second one is the one everybody makes and the first one is the one nobody makes."

The man of about sixty-two said the one thing he had been going to say for about an hour, without stopping the boot, and it was that a man who cannot read can be shown a thing, and that a man who cannot read and has no name on a thing is holding a piece of paper about a man who cannot read, and that he had had a paper like that in his hand twice in his life and had been told both times that there was nothing in it, and that the second of those two times was in this flood and he did not know it. Nobody asked him a second question and about four people in that house have said since that a man of sixty-two who has said one thing about a piece of paper in about nine minutes and then gone back to a boot has done the whole of what a third person in a room is for, and that about four people in this market have worked out that the third person in that room was not asked anything either, and that about four of them noticed it.

The pot had come to the boil and she did not get up for it, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who does not get up for a pot that is boiling has decided that this conversation is more important than the pot, and that about four people in this market have worked out that a pot is the oldest deadline in this flood and that about four people in four hundred miles have decided this week to let one boil.

The water went off the boil by itself after a while and she said that the pot had made the decision for her, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who lets a pot make a decision for her has worked out that there are about nine decisions in a kitchen a week that nobody has to make and that about four of them are made by fire.

---

The man of about sixty-two bit the thread off the boot and turned it over and looked at what he had done and was not satisfied with it and did it again, and about four people in that house have said since that a man who has been mending the same boot for about nine minutes and has not looked up once has heard every word of it, and that about four people in this market have worked out that about four people in that house know what is going on and are not going to be asked about it, and that this is the third house this month where that has been arranged on purpose.

The second sheet went out of the room over that market at about the seventh hour of that morning for a room on the thirty-fifth, which is four days off, and about four people in that market town have said since that a sheet went out of a room nine hundred paces from a kitchen on the same morning as the kitchen was asked, and that about four of them have said that the distance is nine hundred paces and that the four days are longer than the distance, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is the only arithmetic anybody in that market has run this month that comes out right, and that it will not come out right twice.

And about four people in that house have said since that the second of nine kitchens was asked on the thirty-second day of that month, a Monday, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door and a boot being mended by the fire, and that a household answered it in a hand, and that the hand has a mark at the bottom of it that is not a name, and that about four people in that market town have said since that this is the second time this week that a woman has refused to sign something and that nobody has yet worked out what the two refusals have in common, and that about four of them can name it and about four cannot, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the thing two women have in common is that neither of them has a name on anything, and that about four people in that market have said since that the ninth line on a wall nine miles up the road is the only line left of nine, and that the man who wrote it has refused five times to say whether it works, and that about four people in that market have worked out this month that the two refusals this month were both about a name, and that about four people in four hundred miles cannot tell the difference between a woman who will not write her name at the top of a thing and a woman who will not be asked for it, and that nobody has asked them to.
