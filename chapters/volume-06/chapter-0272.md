# Chapter 0272 — There Is A Fifth

A woman of about twenty-four put a date on a door in chalk in a market in a market town nine hundred paces past the last named house on the seventeenth day of the Short Month, a Sunday, and a man and a woman stood on either side of the table underneath it for about four hours, and nobody in that room put anything on a second piece of paper. The light came in off the low ground and went off it again by about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the cold was out of the wind and in the flags under the hearth, and what was on that table for the whole of the day was a bag, a slate, a piece of chalk, and a folded sheet of paper in a man's coat that came out twice and went back twice. In this city a case from nine hundred miles away has stood shut on a table in a room over a market for twenty-one days, counting from the ninety-sixth day of the Long Month, and nobody has carried it up that road and it has not been opened and nothing has been filled in.

---

The bag went down on the table at about the seventh hour of the morning and the book of Crown Law was at the bottom of it and it did not come out for about two hours, and about four people in that market town have said since that a bag that sits unopened on a table for two hours is a bag somebody has decided not to open and has not said so.

"You have not read it back to me."

"I read it four times on Friday and I have not opened it since and I am not going to open it this morning, and the reason is that if I open it this morning I will look something up, and I am not going to look anything up in a room with a date on the door about what it says to a register." Tamsin Quill had her hands flat on the bag. "**I have come up here to say a price out loud and I have not come up here to find out what a clerk's book says about registers, and those are two different things and one of them is what I have been asked for since Friday.**"

"You have not been asked for anything."

"You asked me on Friday to enter a reading in a register under my own name, and you did not say what it would cost, and I said no to the asking and not to the cost, because I had not been told the cost and I am not allowed to refuse a thing I have not been told the price of. I have had two days to work out the price and I have got it. **I want it said in this room, and I want the man who did that to Friday to be the person who hears it.**"

The man of about thirty-six of that town came in at about the eighth hour with a barrow wheel off and left it in the doorway and went out again, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who brings a wheel into a room and leaves it in the doorway and comes back in twenty minutes has told everybody in the room what he is about and is not.

The woman of about twenty-four put the chalk into her apron and did not stay, and about four people in that market town have said since that a woman who writes a date on a door and then goes out of the room has said that the date is for the room and not for the writing.

"A name on a register."

"**A name on a register is a name in a book. A book is kept. A name in a book can be looked up by anybody who can read and handed to anybody who asks for it, and the third of the four places on that Crown page is a holder, and a holder is the thing that holds the days. There is no office between this table and the place the days are supposed to go.**" She turned the bag a quarter turn on the table and did not open it. "**If I enter that reading, I am not a person who read a clerk's hand. I am the name the road is held in. That is what the second way is and I read it on Friday in about four minutes and I have not been able to put it down since.**"

"That is the cost."

"That is the cost and I have been carrying it about two days and I have not said it out loud to anybody, and about four people in four hundred miles who could have worked it out have not worked it out, and I am not going to be one of the four that works it out alone in a room on a Sunday."

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall nine miles back down that road writes on walls on purpose and was not in that market and did not know what was said in it, and the man who was in the room on the seventeenth day of that month had been walking up that road for six days with the emptiest pockets in this flood, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who has carried nothing up a road for six days cannot be answered with anything except what he has.

He put the folded sheet on the table. It was four lines in his own hand and a date at the top of it, and it had not been on that table before and it was not the question, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who puts four lines on a table is a man showing the instrument and not the asking.

"I am withdrawing the asking."

"You are withdrawing it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on a Sunday and about four people in that market town have worked out that a man who withdraws a thing on a Sunday is a man who has had three days to decide whether withdrawing it is honest or convenient."

"I have had three days to decide and I decided it at about the sixth hour this morning and I have not moved it since, and I want it entered that I decided it before I came up here and not in this room." He did not let go of the paper. "**I am not going to apologise for Friday. I have thought about that for two days and I have decided that an apology is a thing a person says so that the other person has to do something about it, and you are not going to have to do anything about Friday, and I am not going to be forgiven for it and I am not asking.**"

The man of about thirty-six came back in at about the ninth hour with a new pin in the wheel and made a noise with his mouth about the old one and went out again, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who is in a room twice in two hours and is not in the conversation is a man who has come in to find out whether the two of them are all right and has not found out.

He was in the room a third time at about the eleventh hour and he came in without the barrow and stood at the end of the table with his hands on it, and about four people in that market town have said since that a man who puts two hands on a table at the end of it is a man who has been in a lane and has come in to say one thing and is going to say it to both of them.

"You have both been at that table since the seventh hour and neither of you has said the word out loud, and I have been in and out of this room three times and I have not heard it, and about four people in this market have said that there is a word about the road in that room and that it is going to be said on Monday or Tuesday by somebody who is not going to be asked." He had not looked at either of them. "**I am not asking what the word is. I am telling you two things I know. One is that the bar goes round and the second is that about four of us have been saying the same sentence about a rack since Sunday and none of us heard it off anybody. That is all.**"

"Go and do your drain."

"I am going to do my drain and I have said the two things and I am not going to be in this room again until the fourth hour." He took his hands off the table. "**You are both welcome to tell me afterwards that the word was something else. About four people in this market have already worked out that it will be something else.**"

He went out. The door had a gap in the bottom of it about an inch high and the light off the low ground came under it in a band and moved across the flags while he was in the room, and about four people who have been in that room on that Sunday have said since that a man who puts two hands on a table to say two things he already knows is a man who has decided to be in a room without being a party to it, and that about nine people in that market town can do that and that he is the best of them and that nobody in that room thanked him for it.

"I have not asked you to be here."

"Nobody has." Tamsin Quill turned the bag a quarter turn back. "**He came in three times and he said one thing and he did not ask and I am not going to say that he is the only person in this market who has worked out how to be in a room, because you were in this room on Friday and you did not do that.**"

---

"I have not said I did not mean to."

"I know you have not and about four people in this market would have said so on Friday and about nine of them would have said it wrongly, and you have never once done a thing on purpose in my hearing." She did not look up. "**You did not do it on purpose and it is worse, and I said that on Friday and I have not moved it since, and I have had two days to move it and I did not.**"

---

About the second hour of the afternoon the two of them had been quiet for long enough that a person in the doorway could have said a great deal, and a person in the doorway did not say any of it, and about four people in that market town have said since that a market town has about nine people in it who can stand in a doorway for two hours and not say a thing, and that the whole of what was said in that room on the seventeenth day of that month was said between two of them and not once to the room.

"There is a fifth."

"There is not."

"**There is a fifth and I have had two days to work out whether there is and there is, and I am not going to tell you what it is, and I want that entered in this room with the date on the door outside it.** You wrote four conditions in a kitchen on a piece of paper and you told me in a room that there was no fifth and you said no twice to somebody about it, and the four are that a question is in the person's own words, and that it is in writing, and that it goes to everybody who will be in the room, and that it goes four days before the room. **Those four are about the question. Not one of them is about the man who is asking it.**"

The man in the coat had his hand on the folded sheet and did not move it and did not pick it up.

"You have thought about that for two days."

"I have thought about nothing else for two days, and about four people in four hundred miles could tell you the same thing about themselves if they were asked, and nobody is going to ask them, and I am not going to be asked and I have said the whole of it." She took her hands off the bag. "**I am not going to hand it to you. You have the four and the four have worked twice in this city and one of those two times it worked against the man who wrote it, and that is a better instrument than I could make in an afternoon, and I am not going to improve on it for a man who used what I know on Friday and did not say what it cost.**"

She did not cry and she did not raise her voice and the bag went back on the bench at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the book in it, and about four people in that market town have said since that a woman who puts a bag down and does not take it with her has left something in the room and it is not the bag, and that about four people in this city have worked out that what she left in the room was the fifth condition and that it is not going to be said out loud this month.

The chalk on the door outside was not wiped and was not added to, and about four people in that market town have said since that the date on it was a day nobody had chosen, and that a question goes four days before the room, and that the man who wrote those four conditions down was in that room two days earlier than he had any business being, and that not one person in four hundred miles has worked out what a fifth condition would be, and that if anybody in four hundred miles could work it out it is the woman who has just said that it exists and is not going to say it, and that a thing which belongs to a person and is not on a page is the only kind of thing in this flood that a clerk nine hundred miles away cannot ask about and cannot have.

Adrian Vale walked back up that road at about the sixth hour with the folded sheet still in his coat and the bag not on his arm and neither of them said anything to the other about Friday, and about four people who were in that market on that Sunday have said since that a thing that has been paid is not the same as a thing that has been mended, and that this was the first, and that about nine people in that room could tell you which of the two it was and none of them said it was the second.
