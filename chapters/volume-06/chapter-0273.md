# Chapter 0273 — A Heading Off A Sheet

A man of forty-one in a very good coat came down the road to this city on the seventeenth day of the Short Month, on a Sunday night, having walked the whole of that road with a sheet folded in the inside of his coat that he had not opened on a gatepost, and at about the tenth hour of the morning on the eighteenth day of that month, a Monday, a woman of about twenty-nine put a date in chalk on the door of a room over a market and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in with a piece of chalk in his hand and put it on the table and did not use it for six hours. The light in that room was off the low ground and flat and the cold was in the shutters, and what was on the table between the three of them for the whole of the day was a second slate, a piece of chalk that was not used, and one sheet of paper with a heading on it and four places ruled underneath it and nothing written in them.

---

He had his hand inside the coat and left it there for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who checks the inside of a coat before he answers is a man who has found out that the thing he is about to say cannot be said twice.

"You have come down the whole road and you have not opened it."

"I have not opened it and I am going to open it once, and I want it opened in a room with a date on the door, and about four people in this city have said since that is the only condition a man in a good coat is ever going to be able to satisfy." He took his hand out with a folded sheet in it. "**On Thursday I said in a market that I was going to have to say what the first way is, and I am saying it, and what I am going to say first is a word I have never had to say to anybody, and I have not said it out loud in four hundred miles.**"

The register-keeper turned her second slate round and put the chalk beside it and did not pick it up, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who puts a chalk beside a slate and does not pick it up has decided that she is going to write down whatever is said and not decide what it is.

"It is the heading on the fifth sheet."

"**It is the heading on the fifth sheet, and the fifth sheet is the one with the four places ruled on it, and the four places are the date, the gate, the holder and the days, and every one of them is empty, and the heading above the four of them is a word and I am going to say it in the local language and I am going to say it once.**"

He said it. It was two syllables and it had a hard break in the middle of it and about four people in that room heard it and about nine would hear it again over the next month and none of them would be able to say where it had come from, and about four people in that room have said since that a word a man says once in a room and nobody can repeat is a word that has left the building.

"That is a word for a return."

"It is a word for a thing that goes back to the office it came from, and a return is a thing this building sends out four times a year, and about four people in this city have worked out in about nine days that a return is a document that has a heading and four places and no name in it, and that the fifth sheet is a return, and that a return is not a demand." He put the sheet flat on the table with the heading at the top and the four empty places under it. "**Nobody in four hundred miles has a word for what a return is. We have it on the wall here and we call it something else and we have been calling it something else for a hundred and forty days.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four turned his own piece of chalk round on the table and did not pick it up, and about four people in that room have said since that a man turning a piece of chalk end for end is a man deciding something about his hands and not about the page.

"So there is a heading on it now and it is not a demand and it is not a refusal either."

"It is a heading and a heading is the strongest thing on a page, and I have been in a room nine hundred miles from here where a thing with no heading on it was a thing nobody could find again." He took his hand out of his coat and left it on the table. "**A clerk files under a heading. The four places underneath it are four places a clerk fills in. The day that sheet gets a heading in a building nine hundred miles away, the four places under it stop being a request made by somebody and start being a form somebody expects back.**"

The register-keeper wrote the word on her second slate and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who reads a clerk's word back once and stops has decided that the writing is the whole of what she is going to do with it, and that a reading entered in a register under a name is a different thing and she has not confused the two and will not.

"You have written it down and you have not signed it and there is no figure on it."

"I have written a word I cannot check on a slate nobody will be shown, and about four people in this city have said that is what a register is, and I am not going to put anything under it and I am not going to put my name beside it, and I have decided that this morning and I have not moved it." She squared the slate to the edge of the table. "**The Crown sent one page and this city made more than one and nobody in this room is going to say how many, and that was decided in here nine days ago and it has not moved either.**"

The man of about thirty-four pushed the piece of chalk about nine inches towards the middle of the table and left it there, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who pushes chalk away from himself in the middle of an argument has made a decision about a wall and not about a page.

"I am not writing it on a wall."

"Nobody asked you to write it on a wall, and you have said the word wall in a room and there are about four people in it who have watched you write on one about four times, and a name at the top of a page with nobody's name under it is a company, and a heading with nothing under it is somewhere to put things, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that the Crown's instrument is a place to put things and has been one since the day it came up the river." He took his hand off the chalk. "**I have not written anything on a wall in this city since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and I am not going to start on a Monday because a man in a good coat opened a sheet.**"

---

The register-keeper stood up at about the second hour of the afternoon and did not move her chair and came back to it and sat down again, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who stands up in a room and sits down again without moving the chair is a woman who has stood up for a reason she is not going to say out loud, and that the reason was most likely that she had been sitting still for four hours and had four things in her that were about the days on gates.

"The case has been on that table twenty-two days."

"Twenty-two days, counting from the ninety-sixth day of the Long Month, and about four people in this city have worked out that twenty-two is the first figure anybody has said out loud about that case in a week, and about nine have not worked out why." He did not look at the door. "**It has not been opened and nothing has been filled in and the docket is still tied twice to the handle, and a case that is not opened cannot be shown to have been dealt with, and a case that is not dealt with cannot be withdrawn.**"

"And the figures over the door of the store nine miles up the road."

"The figures over that door are the sixty-ninth day of the Long Month and they are forty-nine days old, and a man of about twenty-seven washes that board and has decided not to wash the wall above it, and the board has stood unwritten for the forty-eight days since the day after them, and the wind has been at that wall for forty-four days and it began on the two hundred and twenty-ninth day of this flood." He said the four figures the way a man reads out a room, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who reads four figures out loud in a room has just been the only person in the room with a reason for being there, and that three of the four belong to a building he has not been inside. "**None of those four is on a page in this city and all four are on a wall, and a wall does not answer.**"

The three of them sat with that for a while, and outside the door somebody put a chair down the corridor and did not carry it any further, and about four people in this city have said since that a person who carries a chair as far as a door and then stops has worked out that a room with a date on the door is not a room a chair should be brought into, and that about nine people in that building have made that judgement this month and none of them have written it down.

---

The first way is being kept in three villages out past the last named house, and nobody in four hundred miles has any instrument that can require anybody to keep it, and the man in the good coat said that at about the fourth hour of the afternoon in a room with a date on the door, and about four people in that room have said since that a thing which three villages are doing and which no page can ask anybody to do is the first thing in this flood that a Crown instrument cannot reach into, and that the Crown's own answer to that is written on a sheet with a heading on it, and that the answer is a company.

"A holding company is a company that keeps a place while a schedule is made up, and the place has a name, and the name is the whole of the entry, and the keeper of that place has not been asked a single question by anybody and nobody has been sent to her." He had the sheet still flat on the table and had not touched it. "So the first way is not on any of our pages and the only thing in four hundred miles that could hold it in place for eleven weeks is the one body in this flood with a page of its own, and that page is the one with the heading on it. **I have not said that out loud in four hundred miles and I have said it in here, and in here there are about four people who have heard me.**"

"You have said it in here and there is nobody in here who can do anything about it."

"I know that and I have said it anyway, and about four people in this city have said that a man who says a thing out loud in a room because it needs saying and cannot be acted on is a man who has run out of other places to say it, and I have run out, and that road out past the last named house is all that is left of it." He put the folded sheet back inside his coat. "**I have not said the word. I said it once on a gatepost on the fourteenth day of that month and I am not saying it in this room, and it is in three villages and it is not on a page in this city, and about four people in this city have decided that is the correct position and I have not.**"

The register-keeper did not put a word under the one she had written and turned her second slate face down on the table, which is a thing she does, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who turns a slate face down in front of two people is a woman who has finished with a thing and not with the people, and that nobody in that room took it as being told to leave.

A sheet of Crown paper with a heading on it in a word nobody in four hundred miles can repeat, four figures nobody in this city can act on, and a first way being kept in three villages with no page anywhere that can ask anybody to keep it, and the case on a table twenty-two days and the figures over a door forty-nine days old and a wind forty-four days at a wall, and about four people in this city have worked out since that the hardest of the four is the one with the heading on it, and that about nine people in that room could not have said why, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had put his piece of chalk down at the sixth hour of the morning and had not touched it and had not been asked to and had not offered.
