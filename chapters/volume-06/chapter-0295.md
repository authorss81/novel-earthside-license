# Chapter 0295 — North Of The Marches

A gate on a road that comes down from the north side of the Marches was open on the fortieth day of that month, a Tuesday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, for the first time since Monday night, and a woman of about fifty was standing at it with a bar of ash in one hand and her other hand inside the front of her coat, and the light came in over the shoulder of the hill and did not reach the gate, and the cold was out of the north and in the iron of the hook, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a bar of ash, a wooden hook, a barrow of boards, and a road that had come down over that hill on the fourth day and had not come down it since the second hour of the night of the thirty-ninth.

---

The man of about thirty-eight put his barrow down at the near end of the gate and did not turn it round, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who puts a barrow down at the near end of a gate instead of turning it round has not yet worked out what the gate is for today, and that about four seconds is how long it takes.

"I have been up this road every morning since the first week of the second month and I went up it on the fourth, and I am not going up it this morning and I would like to be told by somebody at this gate that I am not going up it this morning."

"**You are not going up it this morning and I am telling you because you asked me, and you are the first person to ask me anything since the spring.**" She had not taken her hand out of her coat. "And I would like it said that a man with a barrow of boards asked me a question at this gate and got an answer, and that I am not enjoying it as much as I expected to."

---

She put the bar of ash into its two brackets and took her hand out of her coat, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who takes her hand out of her coat in front of a stranger has decided the stranger is somebody to be worked with and not somebody to be hidden from, and that what came out of the coat was a brass key on a tarred cord about a foot long, and that she put it into her apron pocket and not back inside the coat.

The man of about thirty-eight looked at the far side of the road for a while and then turned the barrow round, and the boards went up the hill over the stones and the sound of them went off, and the lane was empty and the gate was not.

---

"Somebody in that city has said the road is shut and about four people in that lane have said it was not shut anybody, and I want to know which of those two things I am standing in the middle of."

"You are standing in the middle of a road whose days went back to nine households on Monday morning, and nobody asked any of the nine." She put her hand flat on the bar of ash. "**And the second place on that page is a gate, and nobody in four hundred miles has ever been able to say which gate is the first one, so there is a form nine hundred miles off that cannot be filled in, and I am told about it about once a week by a man on a cart, and that is the whole of what I have ever been asked about it.**"

---

A woman of about sixty came up the lane from the south at about the ninth hour with a creel on her back and asked the same question the man with the barrow had asked, and got the same answer, and went down the lane, and about four people in that lane have said since that two people in about two hours asking a gatekeeper the same question is not a coincidence and is not a rumour either, and that what it is, is that a road which was there on Monday morning and is not there on Tuesday morning is a thing people find out at a gate and nowhere else.

The woman of about fifty worked the hook down and the gate came three inches on its own weight and she put the bar of ash back in and it stopped, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who puts a bar back in immediately after a gate has moved three inches on its own has decided that the gate is where it is, and that the bar is there so that nobody has to watch it, and that this is the whole of what a gate is and the whole of what a keeper is.

---

"You are going to write your name on that post and I would like to be standing here when you do it."

"**I put a date on that post every morning at about the seventh hour and it is the fortieth day of that month, and it is mine and it is not the document's, and there has been a date in chalk on a door in nine houses on that road this week and not one of them is a date anybody sent me.**" She turned round and looked at the post and there was a date on it in a hard piece of chalk and there was one above it about two inches high that had been there since the spring and had never been rubbed out. "I do not wash it and I do not take it down and if somebody in a building nine hundred miles off sends a clerk to look at a gate and the gate has a date on it, they will find a date on it, and it will be correct."

---

"The winter road is this road."

"The winter road is this road and four others and about nine miles of somebody else's road that I have never been on, and I am not going to tell you what is on those four, because I have not been on them and I am not going to describe a road I have not walked." She took the key out of her apron pocket and turned it twice and put it back and it went in and out about nine times in the time it takes to fill a pail and then stopped. "**And I am not going to say how many households are up that hill, because a man in a very good coat said in a room in that city that nobody in four hundred miles has ever said.** I have believed him about that for a month and I am not going to be the one who breaks it."

She looked at the key in her apron pocket and did not take it out, and a woman who looks at a thing in her own pocket instead of answering a question has decided that the pocket is the more honest half of the answer, and that people carry things because their hands are steadier than their answers.

"You have believed a man in a coat for a month without meeting him."

"I have believed a man in a coat for a month and I have not met him and that is the only sentence I have got about the Crown of anywhere and I am keeping it."

---

Nobody at that gate said the word fault and nobody at that gate said it afterwards either, and about four people in that lane have worked out for themselves that the days on that road are gone because nine households gave them back and not because a road was shut, and that a schedule of a thing nobody is holding is a schedule of a thing nobody can be shown has gone, and that a person is the only instrument anybody has that can be shown to have gone, and that nine of them went on Monday morning in daylight without a form and without a reason and nobody asked them for one.

The woman of about fifty said, to the gate post, out loud, with nobody else in the lane, that she had been asked nothing about Monday and that she would not have wanted to be, and that a keeper who is asked what happened to a road she keeps is being asked to be a person about a road and not a person about a gate, and that the second of those is the only one anybody has ever had out of her, and that she had a key on a string in her pocket the whole of the time and had not needed it once.

---

A cart came up the lane from the south at about the eleventh hour with a load of boards on it for a house that is on this side of the hill, and it came to the gate and stopped, and about four people in that lane have said since that a cart that stops at a gate on a Tuesday is a cart that has come to a shut road and has had to go round it, and that going round it means eleven miles and a market and a day, and that the driver stood in the road for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then went on, and that nobody at that gate said a word to him and he did not expect to be spoken to and said so.

"You did not ask him what he was doing at my gate."

"He was not doing anything at my gate, he was doing eleven miles at it, and there is no question a person asks a driver for doing eleven miles at a gate, and the man who asks is a man who wants a page."

---

"You have been at this gate since the seventh hour and you have not sat down and you have not gone to the house."

"I have got a house nine hundred paces back down that lane and I go to it at the fourth hour and I am here from the seventh to the fourth and that is the arrangement and it was my mother's before it was mine." She said it the way a person says something that has been said to them before. "**There is a bar and a hook and a key and a date on a post and that is the whole of the work, and there is nobody up that road this month to be any of it for.** I am going to keep doing it on Thursday, and nobody is going to thank me for Thursday and I would rather nobody did."

---

The man of about thirty-eight came back down that lane at about the fourth hour with his barrow empty and stopped at the same near end and asked her a second question, and this one was not the same question, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who comes back down a lane in the afternoon to ask a second thing is a man who has spent the day on the other side of a shut gate doing arithmetic about his own winter and has come back with a question he did not have in the morning.

"Does anybody up there know yet."

"Nine of them knew at about the seventh hour this morning and the rest of it is a Tuesday and I have not been up there since the third week of the first month and I am not going to start on a Tuesday because a man with a barrow has come back down." She took the key out and put it straight back. "**And nobody is going to go up there to find out, and if you are thinking of going up there to find out then I would rather you did not, because a road that is shut is the only thing anybody has at that gate at the moment and the first person who walks up it to look at it is the end of it as a road.**"

---

She took the bar out of the brackets at about the fifth hour and stood it against the post and shut the gate the way she shuts it, which is by pulling it to and not by pushing it, and a woman who pulls a gate to and does not push it has worked out that a gate has one hand that works and one that does not, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that this is the same fact as a sheet that goes to a place and not to a house, and that they have worked it out from two completely different things inside about nine days.

The date on the post was the fortieth and there was a date above it from the day before and one above that from the day before that, going back about nine of them, and nobody had ever taken one down, and the bar of ash stood against the post with the hook through its handle so that a person coming up in the dark could find both of them in the same place.

And the road north of the Marches was shut on Monday night by nobody and has not been opened since, and that a woman of about fifty kept it shut on Tuesday by putting a bar of ash into two brackets and by answering three questions out loud, with the answer the same each time, and that she has a brass key on a tarred cord in an apron pocket and that a key is not a figure and a keeper is not a holder, and that the form nine hundred miles off still has four places in it and cannot be told which gate is the first one, and that the season which went out of three villages in the third week of that month is not this road's season and is nobody's figure, and that this is the second loss of the flood and it is permanent and it was paid for in about four hours of daylight on Monday morning by nine people who were not asked and by one keeper who was not asked either and who would have refused if she had been.
