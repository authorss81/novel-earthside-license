# Chapter 0271 — The Sixth Of Nine

A left arm that had been out of a hole in clay since the tenth day of the Short Month came back on the sixth day of the nine the man from the town had said out loud on the Saturday before it, and it came back at about the seventh hour of the morning on the sixteenth day of that month, a Saturday, in a kitchen at the low end of a channel nine hundred paces past a market town, and it did not come back in front of anybody. The light came in at one window about three feet off the floor and there was nowhere in that kitchen to stand out of it, and the cold was in the boards and in the iron of the pot and out of the wind, and what was under about eight pairs of hands in that house from the seventh hour until about the eleventh was a sieve, a pot, a butt, two pails on a yoke, and the week's water at the low end of a channel that had a foot in it and a hole in the bed that had been in the bed for six days. There was no paper in that room. There had not been any for about a hundred and forty days.

---

He got up off the bench and reached for the pot with the left hand and the pot went over about nine inches before he got it right, and the woman at the sieve watched the pot go and did not say anything about it, and people in that village who have been in that kitchen since Friday have said since that a woman who watches a pot go over nine inches and says nothing has been waiting up for it.

"You have got it."

"I have got it and I have not been able to lift with it since the second hour of the afternoon last Sunday, and I am telling you at the seventh hour on a Saturday morning because if I wait until about the eleventh I will have been using it for four hours and then it will not be a fact." He put the pot on and stood there looking at his own left hand. "**It does not do what it did.**"

"It is the same hand."

"**It is the same hand and it is not the same hand, and I can put a thing on a shelf with it and I cannot lift about four buckets with it, and I have not tried the second thing and I am not going to try it in this kitchen where you can see.**"

She went on with the sieve. About four buckets of that household's water had gone into the hole in the bed of that channel on Sunday and had not come out of it, and what came down the pail line on Saturday morning was about five buckets to a house instead of about nine, and the woman of about thirty at the stall had been down the lane with a butt on her head since the sixth hour putting it where nine houses could reach it.

The boy of about eleven came in at about the eighth hour with the yoke on his shoulder and two pails on it, and he stood them down against the wall and came past the bench without saying anything at all, which was what he had done on every morning since Sunday, and about four people in that lane have said since that a boy who says nothing about carrying pails is a boy who has worked out that the house knows.

---

The sieve was in her hands for the whole of the first hour and a half of that Saturday, and about nine people who have been in that kitchen since Sunday have said that the sieve was in her hands for about six hours on Monday and about two hours on Tuesday and about an hour on Wednesday, and that nobody in that house had counted any of it, and that on Saturday she put it down for the first time since the Sunday and then a man picked it up.

He put it down on the lip of the channel side of the boards, at the end, about nine feet from where the pails stood, and not on the table, and then he picked it up again and held it in his left hand and worked it against his knee the way a person works a thing they have not held in six days, and about four people who have watched him do it have said since that a man testing a sieve against his own knee at the eighth hour of a Saturday is a man who is not sure the arm is his.

"Give me that."

"**I have had it for about a minute and a half and I want another minute, and then I am going to hand it back, and I am not going to be thanked for the minute and a half and I am not going to be given the pot.**"

"You are not going to be thanked and I was not going to thank you, and I have been sitting here for six days working out what a kitchen is with one pair of hands and I am not going to be told about a minute and a half."

She put her hands on her knees. About four people in that house have said since that a woman who puts her hands on her knees instead of into her lap has decided that she is going to say something to one person and not to a room, and about nine of the nine houses in that lane could hear the sieve going through the wall and could not hear either of them.

"I have been doing the sums of this house since Sunday with a sieve in my hands and a man who could not lift, and I told you on Monday that I would not tell you what our days are and I have not told you and I am not going to today." She did not look up. "**I am going to tell you what my Saturday was, because you asked me nothing and I have decided to give you one, and it is the sixth day of a nine and it is the only day this week I know the shape of.**"

"That is a schedule."

"That is not a schedule and you have not been in a kitchen long enough to be told things in one, and if anybody in four hundred miles writes down the shape of a Saturday in this house it will be wrong on the next Saturday and wrong in the way that matters, which is that it will be right about the sieve and wrong about who is standing in the room." She took the sieve off him and went back to the pot. "**On Monday we were two people and a sieve. On Saturday we are two people and a sieve and a woman with a butt and a boy, and that is not the same Saturday twice and it will not be the same Saturday next week, and the whole of the Crown's page wants it to be the same Saturday twice.**"

Nobody at the door. The sieve went on. And about four people in that house who have been in it since Sunday have said since that the two of them did not finish that on Saturday and that the not finishing was the first good thing that had happened in six days, and that a household which has said one true thing out loud in a week and then stopped talking is a household that has decided what it is for the week, and that nobody had decided it out loud, and that the woman at the sieve was the only person in that room capable of having decided it.

---

He got up off the bench again at about the ninth hour and went out to the door and stood in it for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, looking down the lane at the water standing in the bed of the channel a foot deep and grey, and about four people who have been in that lane since Sunday have said since that a man who stands in a door looking at water he cannot go and work has a thing on his mind that is not the water, and that about four of the nine houses in that lane have a man in the door on a Saturday and the other five do not.

The channel had a foot in it because the gate on the road had been opened on Friday morning for about a day and a half and had been put back across the chain at the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the bed of it had taken what came down and about four of the nine houses had a pail in it before the water reached the low end, and the ninth house had put its pail in about half an hour after the rest, and about four people in that village have said since that the half hour was the whole of that house's week.

"That gate is the road's and not ours."

"That gate is the road's and it is not ours, and the post it works on is out at the bottom and standing in the water, and the man who put the chain across it on Friday is a man who has kept that turn for nine years and was not asked to put it back and put it back." He had his good hand on the door frame. "**We got a foot of water on Friday and we have a hole in the bed of it, and I do not know which of those two is going to last longer and neither does anybody else on this road.**"

"You are not going to the low end."

"I am going to the low end and I am going to it with a sieve and about four buckets and I have told you that and I am telling you the reason as well and the reason is the hand, and you are not going to argue with the hand because the hand does not have a week on it." He came back in and sat down. "**The rack at the low end has not been worked and nobody is going to work it and it does not come off, and that is about a hundred and forty years old, and it is the only sentence about that channel anybody can check.**"

The sieve had not stopped for the whole of that. It was in her hands and it was in the pot and it was against the side of the pot and it was back in her hands, and about four people in that house have said since that a woman who goes on working through the whole of a thing her husband has said about a channel is a woman who has decided that the thing he said is not going to be argued about in this kitchen this morning, and that about nine people in that lane could hear the sieve and could not hear the sieve's owner and have decided that is on purpose.

"You have said that sentence every day since Sunday."

"I have said it every day since Sunday and I have said it in nine houses by now and about four people have said it to me back and about four people in four hundred miles have said it out loud somewhere that I will never be told about, and that is the only thing I have got to say about a channel and I have not said it to a stranger before today."

---

The woman of about thirty at the stall came down the lane at about the ninth hour with the butt empty on her head and put it down outside that door and came in without being asked, which was what she had done on every morning since Sunday, and she took the yoke off the boy and put it over her own shoulder and went out again with the two pails on it before either of them had said anything.

"She is not going to wait."

"She was not going to wait on Sunday either and I have stopped saying anything about it." The man of about forty-three had his left hand flat on the boards and was looking at it. "I have been six days not lifting, and I have watched about four people carry for me, and I have said thank you to about two of them and I have not meant it once."

"You meant it about nine times, and I counted four of them, and about four people in this lane have said since that a man who says thank you nine times in six days to people he has not thanked in his life is a man who has found out what he is not."

"I have found out what I am not and it is not a man who can lift about four buckets and that is the whole of what Saturday has." He put the hand down. "**I am not going back to that channel. I want it said in this kitchen and not at the end of the day, and it is not a decision and it is not a refusal. It is a fact about a hand and I have had six days to have it.**"

Nobody in that kitchen said anything to that for about as long as it takes to count nine things, and the sieve went on moving, and about four people in that house have said since that a man who says he is not going back and does not say he is refusing anything is a man who has been thinking about the difference for six days and has not found a word for it either.

"You will be at the low end on Monday."

"I will be at the low end on Monday with a sieve and about four buckets a day instead of nine, and the low end is nine hundred paces from the water and the barrow goes to about nine feet from the hole, and about four people in this village have worked out that the difference between four buckets and nine is a hole about four feet deep that a man went into on Sunday and came out of." He looked at the sieve. "**That is what is in my hand this morning. Not the channel.**"

---

Nobody in that house said that the hand was back, and nobody in that lane said it either, and about four people in the market town have said since that a Saturday in that house was the first day in nine that nobody said one word about what had come back, and that the woman at the sieve worked until about the second hour of the afternoon and then set it against the side of the pot so that it would not fall, and that the man of about forty-three went out to the door twice and both times came back in without anything in his hands, and that about four of the nine houses came down the lane for a pail between the ninth hour and the eleventh and went back up it again, and that a pail came down that lane every morning that week from a butt and a woman and a boy and not one person in any of the nine houses had asked for it, and that the man who had said nine days on the Saturday before last had been at the market at the sixth hour of this one and had not come up the lane at all, and that the figure had been eight days old that morning and had not been moved by anybody.

The boy of about eleven came back up the lane at about the second hour of the afternoon with the yoke on his shoulder and the two pails on it empty and went past that door and down to the butt at the end and filled them and came back and stood them against the wall outside, and about four people in that lane have said since that a boy of eleven who fills his own pails at the end has worked out that nobody in the fourth house is going to bring them in, and that he has not asked to, and that about nine mornings of that week went the same way and that on the ninth of them somebody at the market said out loud that the line had been running itself since the Sunday, and that about four people in that lane have worked out since that the fastest way for a thing to happen in a nine-house village is for it to be nobody's business, and that this is the second time this month that a person has said that sentence in that lane and both times she was right and neither time anybody thanked her.
