# Chapter 0296 — Nobody Signed It

A room over a market in this city had a date in chalk on its door on the forty-first day of the Short Month, a Wednesday, from about the tenth hour of the morning, and a man of about forty-one in a very good coat was in it with a sheet of paper in his hand that had come down the road in a case, and the light came in on the south side and went off the floor by about the middle of the afternoon, and the cold was in the shutters and out of the wind, and what was in that room was a second slate, a piece of chalk, a shut case that had been set down against a wall and not opened, and a sheet of paper with four places ruled on it and nothing in them.

---

The man of about forty-one put his hand inside the good coat and found nothing in it and left it there, which is what he does, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who checks a coat that is empty in front of three people is a man who has run out of the one gesture that used to mean something and is doing it anyway, and that nobody in that room has told him to stop.

"You have come down that road with a page and you have not said what is on it."

"**It is dated at an office nine hundred miles away and it is dated for the forty-second of that month, which is tomorrow, and it is correct, and it is not signed by anybody in this city.**" He put the sheet down on the table with the writing facing up. "And there are nine sentences on it that I have read out in this room and about nine people in this room heard me read them out on a day in the second week of that month."

---

He said it, and it was one sentence, and it was that no holding company will be sent west of the Marches gate before the season turns, and the woman of about twenty-four read it twice off the sheet in the local language and did not look up, and about four people in that market have said since that a sentence of that shape can be read out twice in a room without anybody in the room deciding whether it is a favour or a sentence, and that it is neither, and that it is a fact about a road, and that a fact about a road is the only kind of thing anybody in that room has ever been able to do anything with.

"It costs you the winter road."

"It costs the Marches the winter road." He had his hand still inside the coat. "**And it costs the office at the top of that road nothing at all, and both of those are on the same sheet in the same hand, and neither of them is a judgement, and I want that said in this room by somebody who is not me.**"

---

The woman of about twenty-four said that she had been in rooms about four hundred miles of paper in nine years and had never once been in one where the two halves of a sentence were on the same page, and that this one was, and that a person who has spent nine years finding out what a document is for cannot be asked to say whether this one is good and can be asked what it is for, and that it is for a road, and that a road is the only thing on it.

"Then say what it is for, in one sentence, and nobody else in this room will say it."

"**It is for keeping a road open in the winter by a body that answers to nobody and can be counted by nobody, and the office at the top of that road has decided not to send one, and has not been asked to and has not had to be.**"

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register turned the second slate round and did not write anything on it, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who turns a slate round towards a man who has brought her a correct answer and then does not write on it has decided that there is a thing she could write and has decided not to, and that this is the second time in a fortnight she has done it and that the first time was a different kind of nothing and this one is not.

"Say it and I will enter it."

"I have entered the nine days and the askings and no answers, and I have entered a heading with nothing under it, and I entered a day for you on the thirty-fifth and told you what it bought, and this morning I am going to enter this sentence and a date against it and nothing else, and the cost of that is that in a quarter of a year a clerk can read that a sentence was said in a room in this city and cannot read a word of what anybody in this room thought of it."

"That is a good instrument and I have said so before."

"It is the only good instrument anybody has produced in about a month and it is made out of the refusal to write things down, and I would like that entered as well and I am not going to enter it."

---

Ilva Rooke, of about forty-five, had set the case down against the wall at about the tenth hour and had not sat down and had not opened it, and about four people in that market have said since that a woman who has stood by a wall for four hours with a case at her feet has decided that the case is not part of the conversation and that she is, and that a person sent nine hundred miles to fill in a column is at zero in a room where a column is being talked about for the fourth time this month, and that nobody has said so out loud and everybody has worked it out.

"Two of the four places are filled in and I have brought the book back up that road myself and nobody has asked me what is in it, and the third place is empty and it wants a person, and I would like it entered that it is still empty on the forty-first of that month and that I have not gone looking for anybody to put in it."

"Then it is still empty."

"It is still empty and it will be empty on the day the sheet is dated, and I have stopped minding about it, and about four people in that room have worked out that the third place is the only one of the four that anybody in four hundred miles has ever refused on purpose, and that a refusal on purpose is the only kind that stays refused when the person refusing goes back to where she came from." She put her hand flat on the case. "**And nobody has asked me one question about the winter road and nobody has asked me whether I think the sentence is a good one, and I would like both of those entered, because a person who is not asked is a person who is not on a page, and that has been the finding of this whole month and I am not going to be the one who breaks it.**"

---

The man of about forty-one took his hand out of the coat and put it flat on the table on the other side of the sheet from the register-keeper's chalk, and about four people in that market have said since that a man who puts a hand on a table next to a piece of chalk is a man who has noticed that a piece of chalk is a thing a person can be found by, and that he has noticed it about a day late.

"What she is not going to say, and I am going to say it because she would not, is that the woman at the top of that road is not defeated, and she is not exposed, and she has not changed her mind, and the schedule is not withdrawn, and the only office that could take it back is nine hundred miles off and the next quarter is not a date anybody in this city holds." He did not move his hand. "**And she read the same numbers your man read in the spring and she came to a different conclusion, and her conclusion is not a stupid one, and a person nine hundred miles off who comes to a different conclusion from the same page is the only person in this whole thing nobody has refuted.**"

---

"You have been on that road with it for nine days and you have not opened it on a gatepost, and everybody in this city knows you did not open it on a gatepost, and I want to know what the road was like."

"The road was like a road and I went up it with the sheet folded inside this coat and I did not take it out once, and I slept in two villages and ate in four houses, and in four houses out of about nine people asked me what the paper was for and I said nothing, and in one of them a man of about thirty-eight put a barrow down and asked me a question about it and I answered him, and I have been carrying a piece of paper for nine days and the only answer I gave anybody on that road was given to a man with a barrow." He turned the sheet over and did not show anybody the back of it. "**And the office at the top of that road is not wrong about the ground, and that is the part of the sentence that has nothing to do with the Marches, and everybody in this room has been avoiding it for two days.**"

"We have not been avoiding it. We have been putting it in the wrong order."

The woman of about twenty-four moved the sheet a half inch so that the edge of the table was clear under it, and a woman who moves a document a half inch in the middle of a conversation about documents is a woman who has decided that a document with a mark under it is a document somebody has handled, and that handling is the only thing anybody in four hundred miles has been able to do to this one.

"Then put it in the right order, and the right order is that a person who has been in a room nine hundred miles off came back and looked at a road and then wrote down one sentence about it, and that sentence is correct, and it does you no good whatever, and there is no room in this city where a correct sentence can be made to do good or harm."

---

The man of about forty-one turned the sheet over, did not show anybody the back of it, and put it back the way it had been, and a man who turns a page over and does not show the back of it is a man who has decided there is nothing on it worth a room, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out this month that the backs of pages are where a person's own reasons live and that the backs of pages have never once been read out loud anywhere in this flood.

"There is a gate at the west end of that road and a man keeps it and this city will not name him."

"**There is a gate at the west end of that road and a man keeps it and I am not going to name him, and your register has never had him in it, and I have never asked you to put him in it, and that is two of us keeping a thing out of a page for nine days and neither of us has ever said so out loud in the same room.**"

The man of about forty-one put his hand back inside the good coat, and a man who checks an empty coat at the exact point in a conversation where a name was refused is a man who has understood what the coat is for, and the inside of a coat is the only column anybody in this flood has been able to keep a person out of, and it is not a column and it cannot be produced and it is in a man's own hand.

---

The woman of about twenty-four put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the table and said that a sentence of that shape with a date on it and no name under it is the only thing anybody has brought into that room this month that anybody four hundred miles away can act on, and that she had spent nine years making things easy to find, and that this is the first one she has ever seen that is easier to find and worse to have.

"That is not a judgment and you have made one."

"It is a comparison between two documents and I am the account of documents and it is my trade and I am not a party to it." She left her hands on the table. "**And I will say one more thing and then I am going to go and read a page of somebody else's, because there is a woman at the bottom of this market who has been waiting since the twentieth for somebody to come and look at a sheet, and she has been waiting a fortnight, and nobody sent me and I am going on my own.**"

Nobody in that room sent her and about four people in that market have said since that the account of documents in this city has spent nine years being sent and has gone out on her own, and that the two are not the same and that nobody in the room said a word about it.

---

Nobody in that room said the number of the nine out loud and nobody in that room said the word cost, and about four people in that market have said since that a correct page from an office nine hundred miles off has been read out in a room in daylight with a date on the door, and that the two sums on it are a road in the Marches and nothing at all, and that nobody in that room has been able to make those two facts mean the same thing, and that this is the first week of the flood in which that has happened, and that it happened about nine days after the last of the nine households spoke and did not ask anybody.

And about four people in that market have said since that the man who carries the question was in that room for four hours and was not asked one question in it and did not say one word about a gate, and that a woman of about forty-five set a case against a wall and did not open it, and that a second slate has a sentence and a date on it and nothing under the date, and that the heading is still on the slate with nothing under it, and that the schedule stands and is correct and is on a table in this city that nobody in four hundred miles can reach.
