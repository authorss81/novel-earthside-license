# Chapter 0167 — The Meeting-Ground Floats

A meeting-ground is a raft with a roof on it and it has to be poled to where the people are, and on the morning of the sixteenth day of the Long Month it was poled eleven miles up the Reach to hear a settlement's ask about the days on a gate, and it is two hours under way before anybody on it has said a word out loud.

**It is a Thursday. There are about forty people and a boat and a roof and a bench, and it floats, and a body that floats is a body that has to go and find the people, and that is the whole of what this day turns out to be about.**

---

The pole is nine feet and it is not a punting pole and it is not a boat hook and it is used by three people at once, one at each end and one amidships who does nothing except say *no* and *yes* and *hold*. A meeting-ground goes where the people are. It cannot be summoned. It has a roof and a bench and about nine feet of standing room and a rail, and about four hundred years of the Reed Reach have built the rule about it, and the rule is the only reason the Commons is not a council.

The rule is that **every household affected by a change must be heard**, and it is the shortest sentence about four hundred years of that raft, and about four people in this city have spent a hundred and sixty-nine days thinking it was a decoration.

**And on the Thursday morning a woman of about fifty who counts water on the fingers of her left hand got onto it at about the seventh hour with about nine people and a slate, and a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom got onto it with about nine people and a printed sheet, and he was not refused, and he says that he has never been refused, and that he says it as a fact and not as an argument, and that about four people in this city have told him that a thing never refused is not the same as a thing welcomed.**

---

**And the ask was heard in full, in the local language, in daylight, on a raft, by about forty people, and a water-count wrote down who said it and not what it meant, and the finding of the day is that she wrote down eleven lines and about nine people have asked her since what it meant and she has said the same sentence nine times.**

The ask is this, and it is the whole of it, and it is in the settlement's own words and a water-count read it out and did not improve on it.

> **We ask the Reed Commons for the days on the Redroot gate.**
>
> **The days are the whole of the share of that household on that channel, for the rest of the season. The gate itself is not for sale and cannot be sold and we are not asking for the gate. The board, the keeping of it, the name in a speaker's record, all of that stays where it is and is not in question.**
>
> **What is asked for is a quantity of water on given weeks, and we will hold the channel and clear the drain and stand the racks in any weather, and the workway we will offer for it will be chosen by the person who takes it and the whole of its cost will be read out to that person in a room in daylight with a date on it before the taking, and any person in this room may refuse to hear any of it and nobody will ask why.**

**And the man who read it out then said the thing that is the best sentence anybody in that settlement has said, and he said it in about nine seconds, and about four people in that room wrote it down in their own hands and about nine did not have a hand free.**

"**I am not going to be able to improve on any of that in the four days I have got. I want to say the one thing that is not in it. A household is not losing a gate. It is being paid for a duty, and the duty is holding a channel that four hundred and forty racks drain, and a household that has been keeping one channel for nine years by itself is not losing anything it had a right to.**"

---

**And the meeting-ground did not answer it, and not answering is a third thing, and the third thing is what this day is for.**

**It is not a refusal. It is not a grant. It is not a deferral to a date. It is this, and it was said in the local language by about nine speakers, and a water-count wrote it all down, and it is the answer:**

**The household must be asked, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it. The day is not the meeting-ground's to set and must come back to it. A gate is a thing a community grants. A community cannot be asked a question on behalf of a household that has not been in a room.**

The man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom accepted it before the water-count had finished writing.

"That is the correct answer," he said. "**I want the day written down and I want it on a slate and I want somebody in this room to write the words *not the meeting-ground's to set* at the top of it, because I have been in four hundred miles of arguments about communities and every one of them was somebody trying to get a date set by a body that cannot set one.**"

Nobody wrote it at the top. It was written in the middle, and it is on a slate, and the slate is on a raft.

"**Why are you not arguing,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who had walked up the Reach road that morning and had no business there and had come.

"**Because it is correct and correct things do not get argued with, and I have been on a road since yesterday with four people telling me the settlement is a villain and I am not, and the reason I am not is that a body that will not answer a question on behalf of a household that has not been in a room is a body I can bring a settlement to.**"

"And that frightens somebody."

"**It frightens me,**" said the man of about twenty-nine. "**I have been the only person in four rooms this month who can be sent anywhere and be believed and about four people in this city have started to need that, and the day somebody in this room stops being frightened of me is the day I stop being useful and start being a post.**"

---

**And here is the uncomfortable half of the sixteenth day of the Long Month, and it did not take long to say out loud, and it is not about the settlement and it is not about anybody on the raft except two people, and it is on the page because the raft was there.**

Hessa Ruun said it herself, unasked, in her own words, and nobody asked her, and it is entered that she had been carrying it since the third day of the new month.

"**A water-count records who may use a water gate. That record is a list of persons. The Reach has kept one for nine years. About four people in this city are angry about lists and none of them has ever been angry about this one, because this one kept a gate open, and it kept two open, and it is the only list in four hundred miles that has never once been wrong about a person. And I do not know whether that is a good answer. I have stopped waiting to find out.**"

"And if somebody used it to take something," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then it would be the first time in nine years and it would be about a household and not about a stranger, and the Reach would take the gate back and the person would go on the list again in nine weeks, and that is the whole of my protection and I have never tested it on anybody who mattered.**"

---

**And the finding of the raft, which is the finding of the sixteenth day of the Long Month and which nobody on it wanted, is that the Reach's own rule has a hole in it, and the hole is the shape of a household that a change does not reach and that is affected anyway, and there is one such household in four hundred miles and it is not on this raft and nobody in this room is going near it.**

The rule says every household affected by a change must be heard. The rule does not say how anybody finds the households. The rule has been in use for about four hundred years and it has never needed to, because everybody who lived on this water knew everybody who lived on it, and about four people in four hundred miles now do not.

"It is not that the rule is wrong," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall. "**It is that the rule was written by people who found out by walking, and we have stopped being able to walk, and there is a house on this water that the rule does not reach and that would be affected by anything said on this raft, and none of us is going to it, and about four of us have wanted to all day.**"

"You have not named it."

"**I have not named it and I am not going to name it in a room with forty people on it, and the reason is on a page in a stone nine miles up a road where a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow keeps it, and the page says that this city chose not to ask, and she offered the woman whose name is on that page the only thing she had, which was that she would not take it down and would not ask her, and she is looking at it every time she comes up that road for the next forty years.**"

**And it is entered on the raft on the sixteenth day of the Long Month, in about nine hands, that about four people wanted to go and did not go, and that the not-going was correct, and that the rule has a hole in it and the hole is not going to be filled this year by anybody in this city, and that nobody stood outside anything.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything was not on the raft and did not go, and he was asked whether he wanted to be there, in a gateway, at the bottom of the flats, on the Wednesday morning, by a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, and he said no and gave a reason in nine words and the reason is right.**

"**I am the most findable person in this world and I said so in front of two hundred and forty people, and that raft is forty people who will remember a face.**"

"You could have come and stood at the back."

"**I would have been at the back in about a minute and there would have been about nine people at the front looking at the back, and the ask would not have changed and the rule would not have been misread and I would have got exactly what I got on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, which is to be a man in a room who is looked at.**"

---

**And the raft came off the meeting-ground at Verrow at about the ninth hour of the evening, and there are nine words on a slate about the day a household is going to be asked, and the day itself is not among them, and the day is not on the slate, and the reason it is not on the slate is that the meeting-ground does not get to set it, and about four people on that raft worked that out between them and nobody wrote it down.**

**And it was poled eleven miles on the Thursday morning, it is under way, and it is not back, and it will not be back before Saturday night, and the day will be on the slate when it gets here and it is not on it now, and there is no body in four hundred miles that can be hurried by a question except a body that has to go and find the person, and a body that has to go and find the person is a body that floats.**
