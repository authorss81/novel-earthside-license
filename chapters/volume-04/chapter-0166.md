# Chapter 0166 — The Name, Asked For

A room with a door at Redroot has a bench in it facing the door, and about four people noticed the bench inside about four minutes of coming into it, and about four of them had been in a hall over a market for a hundred and seventy days with a step at the back of it and no name on the step, and a bench facing a door is a seat, and a seat is a thing a person can sit in and be angry from, and nobody in that room had anywhere to be angry until about the fifth minute.

**It is the first real room in this stretch of days that is not a room over a market, and it is a room in a house, and a house is not a page, and it took about nine people about four minutes to work that out and about four of them got it wrong on the way and one of the four said so.**

---

The room has a bench, a table, a fire that is out, and a door that shuts. It is eleven feet by nine. The bench faces the door because that is where a bench goes in a house with a door, and that is the whole of the reason, and four people in the room knew it was a reason and about four people in the room did not.

"**A bench facing a door,**" said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, "**and I have been in a hall over a market for a hundred and sixty-nine days where there is a step at the back with no name on it because a bench facing a door is a seat and a seat is a place to be angry at, and about four people in this city have said that sentence to me in about nine rooms. And this bench is in a house, and a house is not a room that anybody is convened into, and I do not know whether that is the answer or whether I have just found a worse problem.**"

"Nobody is convened into anybody's house," said a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house and a boy of eight. "**That is what a house is.**"

**And the woman of about fifty-nine is Bel Dain, and she is the boy of eight's grandmother, and she has held that room since before the flood, and about four people in it have never asked her a question and one of them has tried twice.**

---

**And the asking came at about the eleventh hour of the morning, and it came from the boy of eight's grandmother, and it was not Adrian's asking and Adrian is not in the room.**

The reason she asked is a reason that is on the page and it is the reason this whole run of days turns on, and it is short.

"There is a settlement up the road past this city that wants the days on the gate at the bottom of this channel," she said. "**They will ask. They have not asked yet. They will ask in about a month, because they do everything in a room with a date on it and they have not found out about us yet. And when they do, somebody is going to say we are a commons, and if we are a commons then we are a thing that can be written to.**"

"We are a commons," said a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow.

"**We are a rope and a bell and a board with chalk on it and nine days of a rota, and there is no name for that, and a thing with no name cannot be written to. That is the whole of the argument and I have had it since Sunday and I did not sleep.**"

---

**And the name was refused once, properly, by a person who does not want to be asked, and the refusal was correct, and it is entered, and it is the reason the naming works on the afternoon of the fifteenth day of the Long Month and not before.**

The refusal was nine words and it is in a book in this city in the words of the person who said it and nobody improved on it.

"**A thing with a name is a thing that can be written to, and a name on a body is a column with a body in it.**"

"And who wrote to," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who had walked up the road that morning.

"**About four people in this city have been written to. And one of them is on a desk nine hundred miles away with a question in its eighth column and no name in it, and I would rather be the reason that column is still empty than the reason it is not.**"

"And if you have no name and they come and ask you, what do you say to them."

"**I say no. That is what a person can do and it is the only thing any of us has got.**"

**And about four people in that room agreed with the refusal, and about four did not, and the four who did not said so out loud, and that one of the four was a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom who had come up the road to hear how the settlement would be described, and who said that a body with no name is a body that a stranger can say anything about, and that he was not trying to argue, and that he had come up the road to hear it and not to say it, and that he was going to leave at the second hour of the afternoon.**

---

**And the naming happened anyway, in a room with a door, in daylight, on the fifteenth day of the Long Month, and it is on the page in about nine words and a date in chalk on a door, and it is not a roll, and nobody in that room had an office or wanted one.**

> **On the fifteenth day of the Long Month, in a room with a door at Redroot, in daylight, in about nine people: this rota, this queue, this bell on this post and this shared labour shift are Redroot Commons.**
>
> **It has no roll and no officer and no seat and no standing, and nobody's workway is a term of it, and no party to it may be asked to give one up.**
>
> **And the difference from the word on a wall up the road past this city, which is the same word and a different thing, is this: that wall is a list and this is a rota.**
>
> **And the right of any person in it to refuse any part of it, without a reason, is on the wall, in the words of the man who argued it on the seventh day of the new month, who was right and has not been told he was right.**

---

**And the naming was put to the room before it was put to anybody, and about four people in it said no and one of them said yes, and the room did not vote, and it is entered that there was no vote because a vote is a list of who said what, and a woman of fifty-nine who keeps the house said so, and it is the third time this year that the same sentence has been said in three rooms by three people who have never met.**

"I am not going to count hands," she said. "**A count is a list. A list of who was in a room on a Wednesday is a removal with a column for where the person is, and this city decided in the second month of this flood that it was not going to do that, and I am not going to do it in my own house on my own afternoon.**"

"Then how."

"**I will say it and about four of you will say it after me, and that is a room, and it is the same instrument a compact of eleven lines uses, and I have read about that compact and I am not a notary and I do not want to be one.**"

---

**And the third sentence of that exchange of the seventh day of the new month went on the wall in the settlement's own words, because it is a sentence about a rule with nobody behind it, and it is the only sentence in that room that anybody in this room had read out loud before, and the man it is written by is a man of forty-two who has been right about nine parts in ten since the sixth day of this flood and did not write it, and has not been told he was right, and will not be told by anybody this month.**

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall read it out in the local language, slowly, twice, and it is a right of refusal that cannot be bought out, and about nine people in that room had no idea that a person from another world had argued for it in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people on the thirty-fourth day of the new month, and that four of those people wrote it out in four hands, and that the man who argued it said then that it could not be administered and that he was right about that too.

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything asked what happens to a rule nobody checks, and a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house answered it at once and gave the answer as a story about a floor and a rail and fifty-one years, and the story is on the page four times in this world, and nobody in the room had heard it, and it is the first time in a hundred and sixty-nine days that something has been put in a room that nobody in it knew.**

---

**And then, at about the third hour of the afternoon, a woman of about fifty-nine asked the room the question that makes the naming dangerous, and she asked it herself, and about four people had been carrying it since Sunday and nobody had said it out loud, and nobody resolves it and nobody has to.**

"**A thing that can be asked has a standing,**" she said. "**And a standing cannot be refused. I want that in the room. I have wanted it in the room since Sunday and I have not said it because I did not want to be the woman who says a thing that stops a naming, and I am saying it anyway because I would rather stop the naming at the third hour than at the third month.**"

"Nobody is asking Redroot Commons anything," said a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow.

"**Somebody will. Not soon. In about a month, and they will be polite, and they will read a cost out first, and the cost will be true, and it will be the ninth sentence in a row of sentences that are all true, and then they will ask.**"

"Then it is a body that can be asked," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, "**and you have just talked us into it and out of it in one afternoon.**"

"**I have done both, and I am not going to resolve it, and I would like it written on the wall in these words: a commons that can be asked is a danger, and a commons that cannot be asked is a secret, and this city has spent a hundred and sixty-nine days finding out that it cannot have either without paying somebody for it. Somebody is going to pay. I do not know who. I have a boy of eight and a gate and nine days of weather and I am not going to be the one who finds out by being asked.**"

**And a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom, who had said he was leaving at the second hour and did not, said one sentence on the way to the door, and it is on the page because it is the settlement's own view of what it has made and nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody improved on him either.**

"**I have been in this room for five hours and I have been trying to work out whether your name helps you or hurts you, and I have got to the end of it, and I am going to say the end of it and then I am going to go, and the end of it is this. A thing with no name can be walked over. A thing with a name has to be argued with. Both of those are true and I do not know which one you want, and I have been sent to find out and I am going to report that I did not.**"

---

**And a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything was eleven miles away at about the second hour of the afternoon, at a gate, in daylight, when a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board came down the road from Redroot on foot, alone, and told him, in nine seconds, in daylight, at a gate.**

**It is entered that he was not asked whether the day suited him, and that nobody thought of asking him, and that the reason nobody thought of asking him is that he is not a party to it, and that this is the first time in a hundred and sixty-nine days that not being in a room has been a protection rather than a cost, and that a woman of twenty-four entered that sentence in her own words and did not improve on it and called it the finding of the fifteenth day of the Long Month.**

He said one word.

"Good," he said.

**And the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said, afterwards, on the road, the thing that about four people in this city understood and that is the reason this day is on the page at all: *you have just said the only good word anybody in this city has said about a body that is not theirs, and you are not in it, and it is better than anything you have made in this city, and I am not going to say that to your face and I have said it on a road.**

---

**And the last thing on the page is a date in chalk on a door at Redroot, and the same door has no name on it and nothing written on it but the date, and from about nine feet away a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, who has been on nothing for a hundred and seventy days and who had never been to Redroot in her life, read the date off the door and did not go any closer.**

"Good," she said.

She waited, to see whether anybody else was going to say it. Nobody was.

"**Good,**" she said again, to nobody, and went down towards the flats.
