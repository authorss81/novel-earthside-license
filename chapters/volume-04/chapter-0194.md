# Chapter 0194 — A Date In Chalk On A Rail

A woman of about thirty-eight with a board in her left hand said what she had come to say on the afternoon of the forty-first day of the Long Month in daylight on a flat with about nine people standing about nine feet from her, and it took about four minutes, and nobody improved on one word of it, and about two hundred and forty people who could hear nothing heard nothing.

It is a Monday. The store on the upper road is open and about a hundred and forty people are on a rota in four hands, and the queue's day is tomorrow, and nine of the hundred and forty walked up the road past this city today and are standing on a low place at the bottom of a long cut.

---

"**I gave the days on this gate on the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month, in a kitchen, in daylight, with a date on the door, to a man from a store up the road past this city, and I gave all of them, nine days of a week, every one, for the rest of the season, and I chose the day and I told nobody I was going to.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I have said every day since then that I was not going to be asked about it again, and about four of you believed me and about four of you came out here this morning anyway, and that is not a fault and I am not going to make it one. Nobody has asked me. A rule has got me, and that is a different thing, and the difference is the whole of what I have.**"

"You do not have to do this."

"**I know. I said the same thing four minutes ago.**"

"**Then say it, because a rule is four hundred years old and it has no name on it and about four people in this city have been telling each other for two days that nobody can be told what to do by a thing with no name, and if you stand there and do it because a rule made you then the rule is a person and the rule is not a person.**"

"**The rule is not a person and I am not a person because of a rule. I am here because this is the bottom of my cut and I have a boy and a house and nine days of a week on a rail behind me, and I said them out loud on the twenty-fourth in a kitchen with a door on it, and I am saying them out loud now with no door on it, and neither of those is a rule. If a rule had made me do it I would have gone and sat down.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house at the bottom of that cut said four sentences, and they are the four sentences that nobody in four hundred miles has a right to and everybody on that flat needed, and she did not raise her voice and she did not ask anybody for anything.**

"**I am fifty-nine. I keep that house. There is a woman of thirty-eight in it and a boy of eight in it and I have been in that house for thirty-one years and I am the one who has to live in it for the next year, and nobody on this flat has said that out loud yet and I have just said it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I am not saying it to be thanked and I am not saying it to be talked out of. She said a year and a year is a year and I am the one who has to be in the room at the end of it, and I have not asked her for anything and I am not going to, and I would like it entered that I said the word *room* and I have not said the word *kitchen* once this week.**"

"You are saying she is angry at you."

"**I am saying that I have not asked her about it in seventeen days and I have asked her about something else every day for seventeen days, and there is a difference and about four people in this city have taught it to me and about nine of them did it in a yard.**"

"**That is the cost of it, then, and it is yours.**"

"**The cost of it is hers and mine and the boy's, and about nine of the three of us did not know that this morning and about one of us has not known it since the twenty-fourth, and I am not going to say which one and I am not going to be asked.**"

---

**And then she said the rest of it, and it is the whole of the forty-first day of the Long Month, and she has not improved on it and is not going to, and about four people on that flat have entered it in their own hands and about nine will.**

"**I am giving the days back. All of them. Nine days of a week, every one, for the rest of the season, and not the first one and not four of them, and there is no date on this that says when it starts because it starts now and it goes on to the end of the season the way the first one did.**"

"That is the whole of it."

"**That is the whole of it and nobody is giving me anything and nobody has given me anything and I am not going to be given anything, and I would like that entered, because about nine people on this flat have been standing here since the eighth hour of this morning waiting for a thing to arrive with my name on it and nothing is arriving with my name on it.**"

"What is arriving."

"**Nothing. There is no second page. There is no cost and there is no wage and there is no workway and there is no sack and there is no bed and there is no place at the front of a queue, and there is a woman of about thirty-eight standing on a flat with a board in her hand, and she has lost the days and she still has the gate, and the gate has been in that cut for nine years and it is going to be in that cut in four months, and I am not going to say anything else about that because about nine people in this city have been saying it for me all morning and I did not ask them to.**"

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his hand flat on the deck of the raft and left it there.

"And what you have lost."

"**I have lost nine days of a week and a wage on a Tuesday and a ration in a sack and a dry bed and a place at the front of a queue that works, and I took the workway on the seventh day of the Long Month and I have had the rest of it for seventeen days, counted from the twenty-fourth, and I have said that I am going to be angry about it for a year, and I am seventeen days into that year, and I am not going to be argued out of the rest of it by anybody in this city including anybody standing on a bank.**"

Nobody was on a bank as far as she could see.

---

**And then a woman of about fifty who counts water on the fingers of her left hand said nine words, and they were the shortest thing anybody said on that flat, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked her afterwards whether she had meant to say them and she said she had meant to say them and she was not going to say them again.**

"**I enter the days back against the household.**"

"**In your book.**"

"**In my book. I have kept it nine years and every name in it is alive, and I told a man on a wet Friday in the third day of the Long Month that I would rather he was angry about it than grateful about it, and he wrote it on the back of his own hand in pencil and did not wash it off, and I have not thought about that once in seventeen days and I have thought about this morning.**"

"That is a list."

"**It is a list. It is the only list in four hundred miles of people that has never once been wrong about a person, and I am the only person in this city who keeps one, and I have said both of those things out loud before, and I am not going to say them again today because today is not my day and I am not going to stand on a flat and be the person with the list.**"

---

**And about the eighth hour of the afternoon the days went back into the rota on the board at the bottom of that cut, in a fifth hand, and the board went up on the rail with the date on it under the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth, and about four people stood and read it and about nine people did not know it had gone up.**

The rota is a page and it is copied in four hands on the upper road. This one was not copied. There was nothing to copy it into: a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road asked, out loud, whether the four copies on the upper road would be brought up to date, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said no, and gave the reason, and it is nine words.

"**Those four copies say nine days a week for a store up the road. If I alter them, a person nine miles away is working a rota that is not the one on this board, and about four hundred and forty people work off those copies.**"

"So the copies stay wrong."

"**The copies stay right, for that store, for the week they were made in, and they are a page with a date on it and a date is the only thing that makes a page true, and I have learned that in four months from a woman who counts water on her fingers and I am not going to unlearn it on a Monday because it is inconvenient.**"

And a man of about fifty-two who digs said the other half of it and it is seven words.

"**Then nobody outside this cut knows until somebody walks down that bank.**"

And nobody on the flats in front of that cut knew it that evening, and they found out on the Tuesday morning, from about nine people walking past them with barrows.

---

**And a man of about thirty-two was on the bank above that cut at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and had been there since the second hour, and he did not come down, and about nine people on that flat did not know he was there until about the sixth hour, and a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything saw him and did not go up and has not said since that she saw him and did not go up and is not going to.**

**And nobody had gone to get him, and nobody had asked him whether the day suited him, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the whole of that in nine sentences on the deck of the raft at about the seventh hour and it is in a book in this city in her own words.**

"**He walked eleven miles to be able to see nine feet of water and he is the only man in four hundred miles that a room believes, and he cannot be sent to a gate, and both of those are the same fact and he has known it since the thirty-sixth of the Long Month and has said it out loud in front of about two hundred and forty people, and I am not going to say it again, and the next time is the one I would like to stop having to say.**"

"**So we do without him.**"

"**We did without him. And I want it entered that about nine people on this flat would have been glad to have him standing on it, and that the gladness is the reason the rule has a hole in it and not the reason the rule is a rule, and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be thanked in a room, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who is going to fetch a man for a day that nobody asked him to have.**"

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the evening the keeper of the Redroot gate put the board on the bench by the door and took the chalk out of her coat and put a date on the rail, and it is the forty-first day of the Long Month, and it is a fifth hand, and the twenty-third is still under it and the twenty-fourth is under that, and about four people on that flat stood and watched her do it and about nine people did not know until the morning.**

The salt did not stop drying. Salt does not stop drying.

**A date in chalk on a rail, in a fifth hand, and nothing under it but the two dates before it.**
