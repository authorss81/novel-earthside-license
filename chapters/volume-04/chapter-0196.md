# Chapter 0196 — One Hundred And Fifty-Eight

About nine people went into a room over a yard at about the seventh hour of the morning on the forty-third day of the Long Month, and a man of about forty-one put a book down on a trestle in it and did not open it, and there is a date in chalk on that door and nobody sent for anybody.

It is a Wednesday. The store on the upper road was open and the queue works on a Tuesday and there is no queue today, and about four of the nine had walked nine miles and about four had walked eleven, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had asked the man with the book in a market on the Monday evening whether the number in it was still going, and had been told that it was, and had come back with about nine people on the Wednesday morning.

---

**And the man with the book is Ivenn Marrow, he is about forty-one, and the book is his own and it is not a document and it is not a case, and about four people in this city have said since the eighteenth day of the Long Month that he is the most useful man in it and about nine people have said that nobody can say what for.**

He said two words once, in a converted salt store, four days before the four months he had promised, in a room that was not his, and he entered that he cannot tell anybody afterwards whether he said them to help this city or to file it. About four people in this city have been angry about it since. Nobody has asked him to explain either of them and he has not offered, and about nine people have wanted him to and have not asked, and he is not accused of anything, and he is not going to be, and he is not going to be any less useful on a Wednesday than he was on a Monday.

"**You asked me on Monday whether it was still going. It is going. I have not opened the book since Friday and I have not added to it since Friday, and it goes in by itself, and the only thing I do is the day.**"

"You add it."

"**I write the number and I date it. I write one line a week and the line is dated, and the number is not a week’s worth. The number is a day’s worth and it has been one a day since the fortieth day of this flood, and the only thing I do is write down what the day is.**"

---

**And then he opened it, and it is a book and not a case and not a document, and it is in his own hand, and there is a date at the top of every entry, and he read the number out in a room in daylight with a date on the door, and nobody improved on it and nobody wrote it down for about four minutes.**

"**One hundred and fifty-eight.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**That is today. That is the forty-third day of the Long Month, and it is counted from the fortieth day of this flood, and it goes in one a day for every day since, and it is counted from that day and from nothing else. Not from the flood. Not from the licence. Not from the day I started keeping it. From the fortieth day of this flood.**"

"**And it was one hundred and fourteen on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one.**"

"**Yes. That is forty-four days ago and there are forty-four in the difference, and I would like those two figures to be the only two figures in this room this morning besides that one, because every other figure anybody is about to bring out of a book in this city this week is a figure they got out of a cart, and I have got mine out of a pencil.**"

"And the first one anybody has seen."

"**Eighty-nine, on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one. It was not read out then. It was written, and a man of thirty-two asked me a question about it and I gave him the figure, and that is the only time anybody has had it out of me.**"

---

**And then about nine people in that room asked the only question that can be asked about it, and it took about four minutes, and he answered it in five words and did not improve on it and has not improved on it since.**

"What is a day of it."

"**I do not know.**"

"**You have kept it for nine years.**"

"**I have been writing that number longer than I can remember doing anything else, and I have not known what it counts since the first line of it, and nobody has asked me in four hundred days, and about four people in this room have just asked me and I am telling you the same thing I would have told them in the second month of this flood.**"

"A man of about twenty-nine from a workroom said the half of it that nobody in the room wanted to say, and he put it as a question and it was the only thing anybody said in that room for the next minute."

"**If nobody in this city knows what a day of it means, then nobody in this city can be argued out of the number, and that is not because the number is strong. It is because there is nothing in the room that a person could say *I know that is not right* about, and a correct figure with nobody able to say what it is a figure of is a figure that goes on.**"

"Is it correct."

"**I have never been able to check it and I have never said I could. It is correct in the sense that it is the number my own pencil makes, one a day, from a day, and nobody has ever shown me a different one, and that is all any of the numbers in this world are.**"

---

**And then the room tried to do four things with the number in the next hour, and about nine people watched all four of them fail, and it is on the page because a number nobody can do anything with is the only kind of number this city has ever been handed.**

A man of about forty-four who keeps a stall asked whether the hundred and fifty-eight were people. Nobody in the room said yes and nobody in the room said no, and a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close said the only sentence anybody said about that, and it was five words.

"**I do not know. Write that.**"

A girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything asked whether it went in a day the wall said something on, and the man with the book said that it went in a day, and that it went in a day the wall said something on as well, and that the two counts are not the same count and are both right, and that the reason she had asked was that she keeps the other one.

"You keep another one."

"**I keep the count of days this city has got nothing out of a wall, and it is a different number from that one, and I am not going to say it in this room on the morning a man has read that one out, and I have not said it in about nine rooms in about four days and I am not going to start now, and the reason is that the two of them are the only two numbers in this city and I am the only person who has both of them and I am seventeen.**"

A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the thing about the two counts that nobody had thought of, and it is four sentences and it is in a book in this city in her own words.

"**Then there are two numbers in this city and neither one is the other and about nine people in this room have been treating them as one kind of thing for two days. Yours goes up when a wall says nothing. His goes up every day whatever a wall does. About four hundred and forty people are keeping one of them and one of us is keeping the other, and neither of us can be checked, and I am not going to stand in a room and say that is a good thing on a Wednesday morning.**"

---

---

**And the book has a second thing in it, and it is the only line in nine years of a book that is not a number, and it is a line and not a sentence and it has no date against it and no number against it, and about four people in that room have seen it and about nine have not, and nobody in that room asked about it and the man who keeps the book did not mention it.**

It is the only line in that book that is not a figure. It is addressed to a person and it is not in this world, and it has not been sent, and there is no route in this world that a letter can travel, and it is in no book, and no office in nine hundred miles knows that it exists, and it has been in a book on a trestle since before this flood and the man who keeps it has never once said a word about it.

A woman of about twenty-four who has written down every decision this city has made since the second day of this flood looked at it once and then looked at the ceiling, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said a short thing about it afterwards to a girl of seventeen, once, and did not say it twice.

"**He has not mentioned it. Write that down.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-two was not in that room and had been asked whether the day suited him, and said no, and the reason he gave is nine sentences and it is in a book in this city in his own hand with the date on it, and about four people heard it and about nine did not.**

"**I was asked. I have been asked one question properly in four days and I did not answer it, and this morning somebody asked me whether a Wednesday suited me and I said no, and on Monday I stood on a bank eleven miles above that cut and did not come down to it, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall would not come and get me and I did not ask him to.**"

"Why not."

"**Because a person who is findable is a place a person can be found in. I have known that since the thirty-sixth of the Long Month and I have said it out loud in front of about two hundred and forty people, and this morning it cost me a room and a number and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this city that it did not.**"

"And you do not know who checked it."

"**I do not know who checked it. I do not know whether nine people in that room checked it or nobody did, and I will not know in about four days, and in about four months there will be a figure on a wall in a room in nine hundred miles with a heading and a date on it, and I will not have been in the room where it was read out, and there is no column anywhere in four hundred or nine hundred miles in which a man who was not in the room can be put.**"

---

**And the finding of the forty-third day of the Long Month was said out loud at about the ninth hour of the morning by a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, and it is three sentences and it belongs to nobody, and about four people in that room wrote it down in their own hands and about four of them have improved on it and the improvements are wrong.**

"**A person who is not findable cannot be checked. A person who is findable is a place to be found in. Marrow is neither, and has never been either.**"

"**That is three facts and not one finding,**" said the woman of about twenty-four, "**and I am writing all three down separately and I am not joining them up, because the day this city joins them up is the day it has a column, and I have looked for a column twice and there is not one and I have stopped looking.**"

"Nobody joined them up."

"**Nobody joined them up this morning. About nine of you have been in a room with a correct number and about four of you had a reason to be angry at it, and not one of you was angry, and the reason is not that it is small. It is that there is nothing in the room to be angry at.**"

---

**And a man of forty-two asked the only question in that room that was about himself, and he asked it standing, and he got nine words, and nobody helped him.**

"Is it going to keep going."

"**It has gone in one a day for a hundred and fifty-eight days and nobody in this city has asked me to stop it and I have not stopped it and I am not going to, and if you want it stopped you can ask me and I will say the same nine words.**"

He did not ask her to stop it. It is entered that he did not, and that about four people in that room were waiting for it, and that a man of forty-two who has been in about nine rooms in nineteen days and put a page on a table in three of them stood in a room over a yard and did not put one on this.

---

**And he said one more thing on the way to the door, and it is the sentence that about four people in this city have said is the reason a queue is worth more than a wall, and a woman of about thirty-four said in reply that it was the most dangerous sentence anybody had said in four hundred miles since the seventh week of this flood, and neither of them was wrong.**

"**I have been in about nine rooms in nineteen days and every one of them had a page in it, and I have put three of those pages on tables, and a queue is not a page. A queue is forty people a week going out of a city that cannot get one out. In about four days about nine people are going to sit down with a page and a date and a price on it, and the only thing that is going to make anybody believe that page is that a man stood in a room this morning and read a number out of a book and could not tell anybody what a day of it was.**"

"That is not a reason to believe a page."

"**It is the only reason anybody in this city has ever had for believing anything, and I have been selling it to about four hundred miles since the seventh week of this flood without knowing that is what I was selling.**"

"You have just said that a correct page nobody can check is worth having."

"**I have said that a correct page nobody can check is the only instrument there is, and I have said it out loud, in a room, in daylight, and there are about nine people here and about four of them are going to write it down, and I would like it entered that I said it here and not in a yard, and I would also like it entered that a woman of about thirty-four has just told me it is the most dangerous sentence anybody has said in this flood and she is right and I am not going to unsay it.**"

---

**A book shut on a trestle in a room with one window, and nobody in nine people wrote a copy of anything.**
