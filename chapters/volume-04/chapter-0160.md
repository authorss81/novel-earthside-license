# Chapter 0160 — Ninety-one Acres of a Hundred and Forty

A man from the store on the upper road was standing in a gateway at the bottom field on the ninth day of the Long Month with a figure written on the end of a line in his own hand, and he did not know that a woman of forty-four had been owed a figure and a decision for eighty-four days, and nobody says whose field it is until about the fourth hour of the afternoon.

**The season of the bottom field has been entered in this city eleven times and there is no figure on the end of any of the eleven, and the cost column has those eleven in it and no twelfth, and it is the ninth day of the Long Month and it is a Thursday.**

---

He got the shape from a stall-keeper and not from anybody in the store, and about four people have worked out how, and it is on the page because it is the least important thing in that room and the most useful.

It is a figure at the end of a line, and the hand, and the date. The payer's name goes on it. **The name of the person being paid does not go on it**, and a woman of twenty-nine from the store asked him why not, and he said that a wage paid out of somebody's own pocket is a favour and a favour is a thing that stops, and a wage paid out of a store by people who owe it is not a favour, and stopping is a different act, and he said that he had learned the difference from a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall in this city in a market, and that neither of them had thought of it as a lesson.

"You will not get that from a person from where I come from," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who was in the gateway on the Thursday and did not know what he was going to be told.

"Then I will not," said the man from the store.

---

**And the gateway is at the bottom of a hundred and forty acres, and ninety-one of them is the bottom field, and the settlement has held all hundred and forty since the seventh week of this flood without asking, and the man from the store came to pay a season and not to talk about the other forty-nine, and he said so when he was asked and he was not ashamed of it and a woman of twenty-four entered that as the third thing that settlement has said out loud unprompted.**

The bottom field is the field a woman of forty-four of Corrow farms, and it is the field the season of which has been entered eleven times, and the eleven entries are the Reed Reach's standing charge and Adrian Vale's whole debt and the cost column all at once, and it has been entered in a book since the third day of this flood, which is a hundred and sixty-one days ago, by about nine different hands, and not one of the eleven has a figure on the end of it.

Elsa Skell was standing in the gateway when he said her name.

---

**And here is what happened, and none of it is the finding and all of it is.**

She did not want the money. She said so before he had finished the sentence, in the local language, in daylight, with a date on a gate beside her, and she gave one reason and the reason was correct: that a season paid by a stranger is a season that somebody else decides the terms of, and she had been deciding the terms of it for a hundred and sixty-four days by refusing to fill in a figure.

"It is correct," said a woman of twenty-four.

"It is correct and it is also me being proud, and I have been being proud about it since the eightieth day of this flood and I would like somebody to say so, and about four people in this gateway have not said so and one of them has a slate."

Then she took it.

**She took it while he was still holding the chalk out and there was no speech and there was no thanks and at the end of it the figure was hers and the season was paid and it was paid on a day by people who owed it.**

"Do not write down that I took it," she said.

"I will not," said a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything.

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything wrote it down anyway, in a narrow column, in her own hand, and said so before he had got the end of the sentence, and was not sorry, and the reason she gave is the reason and it is in her words and nobody improved on it.**

"**Because if nobody writes it down then in about four months somebody is going to say that a woman of forty-four of Corrow was paid a season by a store on the upper road, and there is nothing anywhere that says it, and the next person who comes up that road with a figure is going to find out whether it works. That is not me describing her. That is me refusing to be the reason a payment is the only one nobody can find.**"

"And that is a column with a person in it," said a man of thirty-two.

"**It is a column with a person in it and it is going in a book that is on no roll of anything and does not go down a river, and I have thought about that for nine minutes, and I am still going to do it, and I would like it entered that I thought about it.**"

---

**And now the second thing, and it is the reason a page on the back of a stone nine miles up a road is going to be wrong about this woman for the rest of the time anybody in four hundred miles is alive.**

There is a stone at a fork nine miles up a road with four pages on it, and the second page says that a woman of forty-four of Corrow is owed a figure and a decision. **The page is wrong.** It has been wrong since it was put there, and a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow who keeps that stone has been asked twice to take it down and has not and is not going to, and the reason is in a document that took four years and a barrow to get to and it is that a stone that has had a page taken down is a stone that will be corrected, and a stone that can be corrected will be corrected by whoever comes next.

So the page stands and it is wrong, and today a woman was handed a figure by strangers who did not know the page existed.

**Which means Elsa Skell is now owed two figures. One from a page nobody in nine hundred miles can take down, and one from a stranger with a hand and a date, and both of them are correct, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles or nine hundred for taking one of them off the other, and nobody in this gateway pretended otherwise, and the gateway ended without anybody raising it, and a woman of twenty-four entered that she raised it in a book on the Friday and that the man named in it did not.**

"Two correct figures about the same field and no instrument for it," she said. "**That is a wall. It is not a wall anybody can be angry at, because a wall nobody can be angry at is the best kind there is, and I have spent a hundred and sixty-four days being angry at things and I have got tired and I am not going to start again on a Friday.**"

---

**And at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, in a gateway, in daylight, a man with no mark, on no roster, outside every office in the world, said out loud the thing this is built on, and it took about four minutes, and about nine people were in the gateway and about two hundred and forty could not hear it and it did not matter, and the whole of it is in a book in this city in his own hand with the date on it.**

"**I said on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, in a room over a market, in front of about two hundred and forty people, that the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that held that hearing is in about five weeks. The hearing was on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one. The interval is thirty-five days and it is the quarter and the quarter runs out on the two hundred and eighty-ninth day of this flood, which is a Monday. I have had that number in my hand since the sixty-fourth and I have done nothing with it in thirty-five days, which is one day for every day there is, because the interval is one day for every day there is.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am going to do nothing with it for another twenty-five days on purpose. Twenty-five days from today is the two hundred and eighty-ninth day. That is the day the quarter runs out. The delay is the length of the quarter exactly, and I have not arrived at that by arithmetic, I arrived at it on Tuesday night and then I waited two days to say it.**"

---

**And he entered it, in his own hand, with the date on it, in about nine sentences, and the ninth one is the finding and it is the first decision he has made since he came out of the wall that he cannot improve on.**

> **A refusal is a page. A decision not to do something is also a page, and I have put a refusal on a page in this city for a hundred and sixty-four days and I have never once put a delay on one.**
>
> **The delay is the only thing I have done since the sixty-fourth day and it is going to cost somebody a gate.**
>
> **I cannot improve on it because it is not a shape. It is a date.**
>
> **I am writing it down in my own hand and I am not asking anybody to countersign it and it goes nowhere.**
>
> **I have no mark, no roster, no seat, no wage and no office, and there is no office in four hundred miles I could put this in front of, and a man with no mark is the most findable person in this world and I said so in front of two hundred and forty people and I am not going to un-say it today.**
>
> **I am entering it in a book on a table in a room, in my own hand, with the date on it, and nobody has asked me to, and it is the first page I have written in this city that is not a refusal and about four people in this city understand what that costs and I am one of them.**

---

**And on the Friday morning a woman of twenty-four put the question to him in a gateway without any preamble at all, and it is the first sentence anybody in this city has written down about him that is not a refusal.**

"Is waiting a plan or a place to stand."

"The second one," he said. "**And the difference is the whole of the next twenty-five days, and it is not going to improve, and on the day it does I will not be the man who says so, because on that day there will be a family standing in a room without a date on it and I will not be the man who says the arithmetic was the plan.**"

She wrote it down. **And she wrote it in a book on no roll of anything that does not go down a river, and it is in his own words and not hers, and it is the second page anybody in this city has put on the back of a thing, and the first one is wrong, and this one is in a woman's hand in a book on a table in a room and can be taken off it in about four seconds by anybody who wants to.**

**And the last thing on the ninth day of the Long Month is a figure on the end of a line in a gateway, with the payer's name on it and the paid woman's name off it, and a second figure that nobody in four hundred miles can take off the first, and a man of thirty-two who has entered in his own hand that he is going to do nothing about it for twenty-five days, and the twenty-fifth day is the day the quarter runs out, and the second figure is hers and she is not going to be told about either of them by anybody in this city.**
