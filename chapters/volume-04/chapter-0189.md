# Chapter 0189 — A Question With No Answer In It

A man of about thirty-two walked nine miles up the upper road on the thirty-eighth day of the Long Month with a single page in his own hand and gave it to about nine people before about the eleventh hour of the morning, and about nine people in four hundred miles received a question written in his own words four days before a meeting-ground was going to be poled to where they were, and not one of the nine could answer it, and that is the instrument and it took four days to build and it has now been used three times.

**It is a Friday. The store on the upper road is open and about a hundred and forty people are paid on a Tuesday and about nine of them are standing in a workroom at about the eleventh hour of the morning looking at a page, and a date in chalk is on the door of that workroom, and the date is the thirty-eighth day of the Long Month.**

---

The instrument is this and it is in a book in this city and it was built in four days in the second month of this flood and has been used on a Hearthguard of forty-four on a day he did not choose, and on a woman of about thirty-six in a gateway three days ago, and now on a body that floats:

**The question in the person's own words. Given in writing to everybody who will be in the room four days beforehand. Anybody who cannot be given it is not going to be in the room. Nobody asks the person whether the day suits them.**

And the question is this, and it is four sentences long, and it is in a hand that is not a clerk's, and a girl of seventeen has it on her own slate and has read it once and has not read it back.

> **On the fortieth day of the Long Month, a meeting-ground at Verrow will be on the flats at Redroot, and this question is put to it, in daylight, in the local language, with a date on the door, and it is this.**
>
> **Whether the Reed Commons will withdraw the days on the Redroot gate from the store up the road past this city, for the rest of the season.**
>
> **The person asking it is not a party to it and holds no office, no roster, no mark, no seat, no wage and no land right, and is barred from every roster at his own request in his own hand, and can be sent into any room in four hundred miles and be believed, and can be sent to no gate at all.**
>
> **And he will not answer it, and he will be at the back of it, and about four people already know that and about nine people in this city do not.**

---

**And about nine people received that page before about the eleventh hour of the morning, and every one of them had it in their own hand by the sixth hour of the evening, and the instrument says that anybody who cannot be given it is not going to be in the room, and exactly one person could not be given it, and about four people in this city have said that this is the first time the instrument has been used on a body and that it broke on the body and did not break on a person.**

The one who could not be given it is about sixty-one years old and has a barrow and keeps a stone nine miles up a road with four pages on it, and the fourth page has a date in chalk on it and the second page is wrong and is going to stay wrong on purpose, and nobody in this city has ever taken any of the four down.

"I have not been given it and I am not going to be in the room."

"You are a household affected by it."

"**I am a household that keeps a page for a woman whose name is on that page, and I offered her the only thing I had, which was that I would not take it down and would not ask her and would have to look at it every time I came up this road for the next forty years. And that offer was made in the second month of this flood and it is a standing offer and it is not a page and it is not in any instrument anybody has built, and if a man of thirty-two has built an instrument that says everybody affected must be in the room then he has built it about me and not for me, and I am not going to be in the room and I am not going to be angry about it, and I have written the day on the back of the fourth page in chalk and it is the thirty-eighth day of the Long Month and nobody has to read it.**"

**And a man of about thirty-two wrote her refusal into his own book with the date on it, in his own hand, and did not improve on it, and about four people in this city have said that a man who has spent a hundred and ninety-three days building an instrument has been refused by it in about nine seconds by a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow, and that he wrote it down.**

---

---

**And a man of about forty-two read it four times at about the eleventh hour of the morning and put it down and said the thing that is the reason a man of about forty-two is a serious problem for four hundred miles and is not a defeat and is not going to be one.**

"I cannot answer that."

"You are not a party to it."

"**I am the party it is about. That store up the road past this city is the thing being asked about and I am the man at the front of its queue, and I could answer it before I had finished reading it and I have not, and I am not going to, and I would like it entered that a man of forty-two was asked a question on the thirty-eighth day of the Long Month and could answer it in four seconds and did not, and that the reason is that the question does not want my answer.**"

"Then what do you do with it."

"**I look at it. That is the only thing I can do and it is not a courtesy, it is the shape of the thing: a question may not be answered and may not be refused, and the only party that may do either of those things is the one it is put to, and the one it is put to is a raft with a roof on it that has to be poled to where the people are. So I look at it. And about nine people in this workroom have noticed that I have said I will not answer and have said nothing else, and about four of them have noticed that I have said I will not answer and have also not said whether I will be there.**"

"Are you going to be there."

"**I am going to be in a yard on the fortieth day of the Long Month at about the eighth hour of the morning with about a hundred and forty people's day on it and a store shut behind me, and I am not going to be on a raft, and I would like both halves of that entered and I would like the entering of it to happen whether I asked for it or not.**"

---

**And then a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom did the thing he has done twice in a hundred and ninety-three days, which is to say the thing out loud that nobody in the room had thought of, and it took him about four minutes, and about nine people have written it down since in their own hands and about four have improved on it and the improvements are wrong.**

"You have written that you can be sent into any room and be believed, and that you can be sent to no gate at all, and both of those are the same fact written twice, and about four people in this workroom have read it as two facts and it is one."

"It is two facts."

"**It is one fact. A thing that is findable is a thing that can be sent and a thing that can be sent is a thing that cannot go, and the reason is not a rule and it is not an office and it is not a punishment, and it is that there is nobody to send. You can be sent to a room because a room has about nine people in it who want something. You cannot be sent to a gate because a gate is a piece of water in a cut bank and it does not want anything, and the person it belongs to is a woman of thirty-eight who has said in advance that she does not want you there.**"

"That is the most useful thing anybody in this store has said to a man in a hundred and ninety-three days," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, "**and it is a thing about a rope.**"

"**It is a thing about a rope. And I would like it entered that I have been the only person in that workroom who can be sent anywhere and be believed for about a hundred days, and that the day somebody in that room stops being frightened of me is the day I stop being useful and start being a post, and I said that on the sixteenth day of the Long Month on a raft and about four people wrote it down and about nine did not, and I am saying it again because the man of thirty-two has just written the other side of it on a page and the two halves are the same thing.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the one thing that had to be said about a raft in a room in a hundred and ninety-three days, and she said it in four sentences and it is hers and it is in a book in this city and nobody improved on it and it is not the same sentence as the one she said in the second month of this flood, though it is about the same raft.**

"**A delegation of four people and a question has not left for the ground people call the First Landing, and nobody has been asked whether they will go, and I am not going to say why, because the reason is that a person who is asked is a person on a list, and a delegation is a number with legs and you can send a number and you cannot send a page.**

**And a meeting-ground is a number with legs. It is a raft with a roof on it and nine feet of standing room and a rail, and it cannot be summoned, and in about four days it is going to be poled to where about nine people are standing, and it will carry one page, and one page is not a delegation, and about four people in this workroom have just worked out that this is the first instrument in four hundred miles that this city has built which is better than a delegation and I did not build it and neither did he.**"

"You did not build it," said a man of about thirty-two.

"**I wrote a compact of eleven lines in the second month of this flood and the fifth line of it is a person who may stop being a guest, and that is the only line anybody can act on, and it is on a chalk wall nine miles back down that road in a hall. A question is not that and it is not like it and it is better, and I am not going to be the one to say that out loud twice in a year.**"

---

**And a share with the post taken out has been in a man's coat for about twenty-five days and nobody in that workroom mentioned it and about four people in that workroom wanted to, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked him about it in a yard at about the sixth hour of the evening and got four sentences and about nine people heard them and nobody can be written to for having it.**

"You have not answered it."

"**I have not answered it. It is a page with about eleven lines on it and no date and no place and nothing in it anybody could be obliged to do and no way to revoke it, and you gave me four days on the thirteenth day of the Long Month and there have been twenty-five days since then and I have not said yes and I have not said no, and I have carried it about every day, and I am not going to tell you what I have been doing with it because a share with no date on it is a share that goes on for as long as nobody objects, and nobody is going to be annoyed, because you pay.**"

"You are not going to keep refusing it as a way of not having it."

"**No. I have given that up on the seventeenth day of the Long Month and I did not tell anybody and about four people in this city have worked out that I gave it up. I am not going to hold a page in my coat for a hundred and ninety-three days to be the only man in it, and I am not going to give it up in a yard, and it is going to sit in a coat and about four people are going to know it is there and about four hundred people are not, and that is the whole of the price of that page and I have paid it and I am not going to be thanked for it because I said in advance I would not be.**"

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said one thing in a yard about that, and it is nine words, and it is the last thing anybody said in this chapter about a man of thirty-two.**

"**Then stop being the only one.**"

---

**And the last thing on the page is a plain slate with four sentences on it in a girl's own hand, and it is not her own, and she has not read it back and is not going to, and her own book has her name at the top of it and has had since the second month of this flood.**
