# Chapter 0153 — Somebody Is Told

A man of thirty-two was standing at a gate in the wrong weather with a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, and the woman said the word out loud, once, at the gate, before anybody had asked her anything, and then said she was not going to say it again that day and did not know why she had said it.

**It is the second day of the Long Month and it is a Thursday and about four people are at a gate at the bottom end of a long channel, and the word is Redroot, and it is the first time in a hundred and fifty-seven days that a place anybody in this world can reach has been said out loud by somebody who did not have to.**

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had a printed sheet in her coat with the word on it in the fourth line and she had read it on a step over a market yesterday and she had said it out loud once, standing up, with the rain coming, before her mouth was ready.

"I have got no reason," she said. "**I have been not on anything for nine years and it turns out that is the first thing anybody in four hundred miles has ever wanted.**"

"A man of forty-two put it in a fourth line on purpose," said a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything.

"**A man of forty-two put it in a fourth line on purpose and I read it off a piece of paper in a room with nine people in it and I have carried it eleven miles in my coat and it came out of my mouth before I knew I was going to say it. That is the whole of how a place becomes findable. It is not a conspiracy. It is a woman who is off every list she has ever been on.**"

---

**The place is a low place at the bottom end of a long channel and the red root grows in the cut bank where the water has been eating since the water came, and it grows there because the bank is the only thing on the Reach that is not salt, and it comes up in March and it is not a plant anybody eats and it is the reason a place nine miles from a town has a name at all.**

There are racks on the flats at the bottom, in rows, and they belong to about nine households, and the rows are old and the wood is silver and the salt on them is white and hard and it is the best salt anybody in this world has seen and about four of the racks have a settlement's mark on the corner of the base, cut, not written, and the mark is a bar and a line and nothing else.

"That is not our ground," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**No, and I am not going to say whose it is, because I have said one name today and I have said it once over and I did not come to say two.**"

---

**And the woman who was waiting at the gate had been there since the fifth hour of the morning and had watched two people walk out onto a bank that is not a road, and she did not turn round when they came up.**

She was about fifty. She had a board under her arm that was not a page and a stick of chalk in her hand, and she counted water on the fingers of her left hand while she was standing there, which she has done for nine years, and she has said one thing about a fourth line out loud in four rooms since the eightieth day of this flood and has not been asked about it once.

"**I am Hessa Ruun, I am the water-count of the Reed Reach, and I am the closest thing this wetland has to a notary and nothing like one, and I do not speak for the meeting-ground and I have not come to be asked anything.**"

"You came eleven miles."

"**I came eleven miles because there is no room down here with a door on it and I have been in nine of them this year.**"

"**Then say the thing you have come to say,**" said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, "**and say it in front of four people and not two, because I would like a witness who is not standing next to you.**"

"**No.**"

---

And the no was short, and it was not about the man of thirty-two, and it is entered that a woman of twenty-four who was not there was told about it afterwards and entered it in a book in the local language, and the entry is nine words long and it is in her own hand.

"**Naming a place that is not on a list is how a place becomes findable.**" She put the chalk down on the gate board and did not pick it up. "**And a man with no mark and on no roster is the most findable person in this world, and he said so himself in front of about two hundred and forty people, and I read about it in a register that a clerk of the fourth desk copied out, and I do not believe him, and I am using it.**"

"You are using a thing he said against him."

"**I am using the truest thing anybody in this world has said about himself in a hundred and fifty-five days, and I would do it again, and I would like him to hear me say that I am using it and not that I have taken his side.**"

"He wants more than four people in the room."

"**He can have four people and a board. He cannot have nine people and a page, and he cannot have a woman of twenty-four with a book, and he cannot have the meeting-ground, and the reason is not that I do not trust him. The reason is that I am not the one who has to live on the Reach afterwards.**"

---

**And then she said it, and it was the second time, and it was to four people, in daylight, at a gate, with a date on it, and the date went on the gate board in chalk and not on a page, and that was on purpose and she said so before she did it.**

> **The second day of the Long Month. At the Redroot gate, at the bottom end of the channel.**
>
> **Said here, once, on a board, because a board is a thing and a page is a place a person can be found in. Said to four people. Nobody asked.**
>
> **Redroot.**

The chalk went on and it did not come off in the rain for about nine hours and a boy of eight took a cloth to it at the seventh hour because he thought boards should be wiped, and a woman of about thirty-four told him not to and did not explain and he did not ask her again.

**And it is entered that the man of thirty-two was not asked anything and asked nothing and was told, and that he noticed he was not asked, and that a woman of twenty-four entered that she noticed him notice it and did not improve on it.**

"You did not ask me a question," he said.

"**No.**"

"**I have gone eleven miles and I have been told a name and I have not been asked one question and I want it on the record that I noticed, because in a hundred and fifty-seven days the only thing anybody has wanted off me is the reading.**"

"You can have the reading of this," said Hessa Ruun. "**It is that a place got said. That is all that happened and you were in it.**"

---

**And then the argument of the day started, and it was not an argument anybody had chosen, and it is the whole of the day.**

"Nine households," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who had walked up with them and had said nothing for an hour. "**About nine households on it and the Reach's salt racks on the flats at the bottom and a gate, and it is not on the list.**"

"**It is not on the list,**" said Hessa Ruun.

"The list is the shared gates of the Reach and the hamlets on them, and it is on a page that a notary read out in a hall in the first month of this flood, and it says *Four Elms, Long Water, Silt Row, Corrow, Black Sluice, Nine Elms and the two gatehouses*, and Redroot is not on it, and I have known that list since the tenth day of this flood, which is a hundred and forty-seven days ago, and I have never once asked why."

"**Then nobody in this city can say why, including you, and it is a Thursday, and you have asked four people, and one of them is the water-count.**"

"**Can you say why.**"

"**No.**"

The rain came across the flat in a sheet and went under a rack and came out the other side.

"**And that is the finding and it is four sentences and none of them is a reason. A list is a list. The list was drawn by somebody for a purpose and the purpose was the receiving of one named living traveller per opening into the Reach, and a list drawn for a purpose has a thing in it that is not a village's decision. Redroot's gate is a shared gate of the Reach and it has been for longer than the licence, and it is on no list, and both of those are true at once and about nine people have been living on it for longer than anybody there can date.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the water-count put her hand flat on the gate board over the chalk and left it there, and did not wipe it, and did not add to it.

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked her the other thing, which was the thing she has been carrying since the eightieth day of this flood and has not been asked about once.**

"The fourth channel," he said. "**At Nine Elms. You have said something about a fourth line four times this year and nobody has ever asked you which line or whose.**"

"**It is the fourth line of a term sheet, it is a woman's, and it is in force, and I read out a refusal of a page on a Thursday morning in a converted salt store in about forty people and it took about nineteen minutes, and the refusal is not about him and it was not about his person.**"

"What was it about."

"**It was about a description.**" She took her hand off the board and put it on her own left hand and counted along her fingers, which she was doing the whole time and which nobody in four hundred miles has ever asked her about. "**A description is the only protection anybody in this world has ever been offered by that office, and we spent nine years learning it, and we are not going to be told at the eleventh hour that a description can be a kindness.**"

"Then the fourth line stands."

"**The fourth line stands and it took nine days and it is ours and we are not giving it up for a page.**"

---

"Then what do we do," said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**Nothing. That is what I came to say and it took me eleven miles and four sentences and I have said it.**"

"You have not said what it is for."

"**It is for a man with no mark to be standing at a gate instead of at a hearing, on a day when there is nothing to decide. In about three weeks there will be something to decide and he will not be in the room, and I am not the Reach and I do not know what happens then, and I have not come to arrange anything, and a woman who counts water on her fingers has been asked eleven times this year to be more than she is and I have said no eleven times and I am saying it a twelfth time at a gate.**"

---

**And a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything did not go and stand in a field. He stood at a gate for about another hour in weather that was not fit for it, and he did not say the name again, and he did not write it down, and a girl of seventeen who was not there and who is on no roll of anything was told about it and wrote down the date and the word and nothing else.**

**And the finding of the second day of the Long Month is that a water-count who records who may use a water gate has spent a day writing a place on a board instead, on purpose, in chalk, in the rain, at a gate, to four people, and nobody asked her to and nobody will be able to take it off, and she said out loud why, and the reason is that a board is a thing and a page is a place a person can be found in, and that a man is not a place.**
