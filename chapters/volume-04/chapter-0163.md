# Chapter 0163 — To Open Nothing

A woman of twenty-nine who keeps a public register was walking down a hill on the twelfth day of the Long Month with a page on her for the second time this month, and about nine people were in a room over a market, and a settlement on the upper road had begun asking, in a store, in front of about nine people, which road goes four hundred miles inland.

**It is a Sunday. The delegation has not left. It is four people and a question and a clause, and the clause is *to open nothing*, and the clause belongs to the woman walking down the hill, and this day is the day she says so out loud in a room in daylight with a date on it.**

---

The room had the table back in it and nothing on the walls and a date in chalk on a door that now carried two dates, the second of them from the first day of the Long Month and the first of them from the sixty-fifth day of the month that ended on the hundred and fifty-fifth day of the flood, and nobody had washed either.

"Nine miles of nothing," said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, who had come down the road that morning with a question of his own. "**They asked me at the store. Not me personally. One of the men in the workroom asked the man at the front of the queue, and the man at the front of the queue passed it on, and it came to me at about the second hour, and it is a real question and it is a fair one.**"

"Answer it," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I have answered it. It is a road. It goes up out of the flats, over the shoulder at the two-mile mark, along the top for about six miles and then down into dry country for a hundred and thirty. It is a road. It is not a delegation and it cannot be sent, and about nine people on that road in the first two weeks of this flood have all told me it goes to a place people call the First Landing, and every one of them said it the way you say a thing you have heard and not the way you say a thing you have been.**"

"Nobody has asked whether they will go," said a woman of twenty-four.

"Nobody has asked whether they will go," said the woman of twenty-nine, "**and I have had a page in my coat since the middle of last month about a thing nobody has agreed to, and I am the one who wrote the clause, and I would like the room to understand that those two facts have been sitting in the same pocket since the thirty-third day of the month after this one.**"

---

**And before she stood up, a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board asked the question nobody had asked her, and it was not about the clause, and it is the reason the day is not only about a clause.**

"They are asking about the road at the store because they want to know if there is a market at the end of it," he said. "**That is what a man in a workroom asked. Not whether a road exists. Whether there is somewhere to sell to. And that is not a curiosity, that is a man on my side of the water doing arithmetic about a second store.**"

"And when they find out."

"**Then there will be a cart road, and there will be a settlement on it, and in about four months there will be a queue at the end of it nine miles from here, and in about eight months there will be a woman at the front of that queue who knows nine hundred names without a page.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And that is the thing I have been unable to say in a room for four days and I am saying it now because I am a trader and because I would rather be the one who said it. A settlement with a second market is not an argument. It is a fact that arrives. It is not a thing anybody in this room can refuse, because it will not be a request, and by the time anybody could call it a thing it will be a road with carts on it.**"

"That's tomorrow's problem."

"That is next year's problem and it is arriving in nine days and I have said so in a room and I would like it entered that I said it and that nobody improved on it."

---

**And then she stood up, and nobody asked her to, and there was no line that let anybody choose, and it is entered that she said the whole of the following before she sat down again and that she did it in the local language and that she asked for it to be written in the local language and not in the one anybody in the room came from.**

> **Four people and a question are to go four hundred miles inland to the ground that people call the First Landing, to say what is standing on it, to say whether there is a seam in the ground, and if there is a seam, how long it is and what is in it, to open nothing, to come back, and to say it in a room in daylight in the local language with a date on it.**
>
> **The words *to open nothing* are mine. I put them in on the thirty-third day of the month after this one. Nobody else put them in and nobody has taken them out and I would like it entered that I have been asked three times this year to drop them and I have not.**
>
> **And I would like it entered why, in my own words and not improved on. In about four months somebody is going to say that a delegation was sent to open something. And I did not write that. I wrote the opposite and I wrote it in a room with about nine people in it, and if I had left the clause out then the account of it would be a page with a place cut out of it, and a page with a place cut out of it is a page somebody can put a name in.**

**And then, before anybody thanked her, she said the other half of why she wrote the clause, and it is about a sheet of paper she has carried for seven years and has never put in a book.**

"I have a sheet in this coat that has been in it for seven years," she said, "**and I have carried it out of every book on purpose, and I said out loud on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one that keeping it out of a book is the single most consequential thing I have done in seven years. And I am carrying it down a hill today for the second time this month and I have carried it up that hill about four hundred times, and every one of those four hundred times is a thing I did that the clause in that slate does not say and I am not going to tell you what it is, and I am telling you that I am carrying it.**"

Somebody said *thank you*.

"**Do not thank me for that. I have known what a page that ends a document is since the forty-ninth day of the month after this one, and I have said so in front of about nine people, and a person who knows that is a column. That is the whole of what the clause has cost me. It has cost me the only thing I have ever had that nobody could use, which is not being the person who is asked.**"

---

**And nobody thanked her after that, and she asked for it not to be thanked, and it is entered that she asked, and it is entered that about four people in that room understood the request and about nine did not, and that the four did not act on it for about a day and a half and that this is the third time this year she has had to ask for something that is not a form.**

"And it is the cost," said a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, "**that you are now the only person in this city who has written a thing that four people are going to be governed by. And nobody has asked you whether you want that.**"

"Nobody has asked anybody anything. **That is not a thing you can fix from inside a clause, and I have spent a hundred and sixty-seven days finding that out, and you are the last person in this city I would expect to have to explain it to.**"

---

**And the second resistance of the twelfth day of the Long Month was physical and dull and real and nobody improved on it either: the delegation has not left, and the only instruction it has is a clause, and a clause is not an instruction to anybody's legs.**

"It has not moved in forty-four days, since the thirty-third day of the month after this one," said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board. "**And I have asked about it four times in three weeks and every time somebody has told me what it is for and nobody has told me when it goes.**"

"Because there are four of them and they are not named and the naming is a list," said a woman of twenty-four. "**A delegation is a number with legs. You can send a number and you cannot send a page, and if you write the four names down then it is a list, and a list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is.**"

"So it goes in fours with nobody's name on it."

"**It goes in fours with nobody's name on it and about four people in this city have now said that sentence in three different rooms this year and it has never once got anybody to a gate.**"

---

**And a man of thirty-two who holds nothing was in that room from the tenth hour of the morning to about the second hour of the afternoon and said nothing at all, and a notary of fifty entered that she noticed, and entered the reason without being asked, and the reason is hers and it is about him and not about herself.**

"**He is not being patient. He is standing at the back of a room while somebody else is carrying a cost, and he has been the person who carried one for a hundred and sixty-seven days, and this is the first time in a hundred and sixty-seven days he has watched somebody else do it and he does not have a thing to do with his hands and I have watched him not have a thing to do with his hands.**"

"He is on no roster," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**He is on no roster and he is also the only man in this room who cannot be asked to be in the delegation, and he has known that since the day it was drafted, and he came anyway, and he sat down at the back and he was there for four hours.**"

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening there is a slate on a table in that room and it has a date on it and a route on it and four spaces on it for four names, and the four spaces are empty and have been empty for forty-four days.**

A girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything looked at it for a long time and did not write anything in it and said so out loud, to nobody, at the ninth hour of the evening on the twelfth day of the Long Month.

"**I am not going to be the one who fills them in.**"

"Somebody has to."

"**Somebody does, and it should not be me, and I have thought about it for about nine days and here is the whole of the thinking: the second I put a name in one of those four spaces, that space has got a person in it, and the other three spaces have got the same person missing in them, and a page with a person in it and three people not in it is a page about a person. I have read one of those. It was about me and it was read out badly in a room and nobody corrected it.**"

"**Then what do you suggest.**"

"**That somebody who is not me asks four people, one at a time, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, whether they will go, and that they are allowed to say no and that nobody writes their no down anywhere. And I know that is a list. I have worked that out. I have worked it out about nine times in the last nine days and I have not got a better idea and I am not going to stand here and pretend that not having a better idea is a virtue.**"

**And a woman of twenty-four wrote that down and did not read it back, and it is the only entry in a book in this city on the twelfth day of the Long Month that nobody improved on, and the four spaces on the slate were still empty at the eleventh hour of the evening, and the route was on it, and the date was on it, and the clause was on it in a woman's own words, and nothing in this city has moved.**
