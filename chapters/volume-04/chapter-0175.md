# Chapter 0175 — The Days on the Redroot Gate

At about the ninth hour of the morning on the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month a woman of about thirty-four who is on no roll of anything wrote a date on a door in chalk, in a kitchen, at Redroot, and about nine people went in after her, and that is the whole of the room and there was no second part of it.

**It is a Friday. There is no hall, no landing and no market under that kitchen, and about two hundred and forty people who can hear out of a room over a market eleven miles away in this city cannot hear one word of what was said in it, and about four people who had spent a hundred and seventy-nine days building an instrument in that room over that market noticed this at about the tenth hour and did not remark on it, and it is entered that they noticed.**

---

The date went on the door in chalk on the Friday morning before anybody arrived, and it is the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month, and the same door had a date on it on the Monday and a date on the Tuesday and a fifth date on a rail on the Thursday, and the woman of twenty-four who put the Monday one on asked the household on the Monday whether she might stand in their yard and was not refused, and the woman of thirty-eight said then that it could be washed off in a week.

**It is not going to be washed off in a week and both of them know it and neither of them said so on the Monday, and on the Friday morning there is a date on that door in a hand that is neither of theirs, and the hand is about thirty-eight years old, and the woman of twenty-four asked for it to be entered that she did not put it there.**

---

**And here is who was in the room, and it is nine people, and the reason it is nine and not forty is the rule of the Reach, which is that every household affected by a change must be heard, and which has been in use on that water for about four hundred years, and which nobody in that room was in a position to improve on.**

**One.** The keeper of the Redroot gate, about thirty-eight, with the board in her left hand, who has kept the channel at the bottom of that cut for nine years for nothing and who took a workway from a store up the road past this city on the seventh day of the Long Month after the whole of its cost had been read out to her in daylight with a date on it, and who said yes in nine sentences and put the board down and did not pick it up again.

**Two.** Bel Dain, about fifty-nine, who keeps the house and the boy of eight, and who was asked in this room once this year and that was by mistake.

**Three.** A man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom, who read the cost out on the seventh day of the Long Month, who read the fourth line wrong on purpose once to see who would stop him, who could not tell on Tuesday which room he had left a sentence out in, and who has entered that in a book of his own that goes nowhere.

**Four.** A man of about forty-two, warehouse supervisor in Leeds until the sixth day of this flood, who put a printed sheet over before he said a word on the hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood and who has been right about eight sentences out of nine since.

**Five to nine, and nobody in that room could have told you which of them was which without being told, which is the only way a room of nine people in a kitchen stays a room and does not become a roll:** a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who invented the shape of a wage page and who has a slate he writes on when he is told not to; a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, who has been on nothing for a hundred and seventy-nine days and who has a slate she has never written on and who said so first before anybody asked her anything; a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, who does not name a gate, a household or a person when he reports, because a description is a removal with a column for where the person is; a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, who is the only person in a room who says *I do not know* out loud without being asked; and a woman of about thirty-four from a fourth household on that road, who took a workway on Tuesday in a kitchen with the full cost read out first, and whose reason was three sentences long and all three of them were about a roof and a wage and a Tuesday.

**And a boy of eight was not in that room, and was not asked, and was told on the Monday that he would be told the rest of it on the Thursday night, and was told the rest of it on the Thursday night by the woman who keeps the gate, and about nine people in this city have said since that the whole of what happened in that kitchen would have been done differently with a child in it, and that the child was not, and that this was not an accident.**

---

**And the cost was read out first, before the asking, in full, in the words of the person offering it, and not one word of it was improved on by anybody in that room including the person hearing it, and that order is the whole of why anything happened in that kitchen on the Friday morning.**

> **A workway is a permission and not a trade. It is a groundkeeper's. It reinforces a claimed place, organises the defence of it, and resists being moved off it without choosing to be.**
>
> **It must be defended.**
>
> **And the place it is held on, and the people living on it, are the first of the two.**
>
> **And abandoning a ground that has been claimed is a matter of the holder's convenience, and not of anybody else's convenience, and not of the weather, and not of a season.**
>
> **And a person who is moved off ground they have been on will be moved off it, and the weather will be the reason, and the reason will be true.**
>
> **There is no second page. There is no other clause. You may refuse any part of it at any time and you will not be asked why and you will not be asked twice, and that sentence is on our wall and it is the only thing on it that came from outside this store.**

"And what is asked for," said the man of about twenty-nine, "**is the days. The whole of the share of this household on this channel, for the rest of the season. Nine days of a week, all of them, every one.**

**The gate is not for sale and cannot be sold and nobody in my store is going to write the word *sold* on anything this month, and if anybody in this world thinks a gate can be sold I would like to be told how and I will put it on a board.**

**The board stays. The keeping of it stays. The name in a speaker's record stays. None of that is in question and none of it is traded.**

**What is offered is a wage, on a day, out of a store. A dry bed. A place at the front of a queue that works, on a Tuesday. And the workway you have already got, which does not change and which you already said yes to on the seventh day of the Long Month, and I am not offering it to you again this morning because you have it.**"

---

**And then four people asked her things, and the order of them is the order they happened, and none of the four was a question designed to be answered.**

The first was a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and it was arithmetic, and it is the correct question.

"What is a Tuesday's wage in a measure of salt."

"**A measure is a hundredweight and about nine measures a week goes out of four hundred and forty racks, and a wage is not a measure of salt. A wage is a day and a share and a ration. I can put a figure on the end of the line for the share. The ration does not have a figure, it has a sack, and the sack is the same size every week and I have been arguing with about nine people about the size of it for about nine days.**"

"And is the wage for the same work you are doing now."

"**No. It is more work. That is the whole of it and I would rather somebody said that than did not.**"

The second was a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, and it is in his own words and it is nine words.

"**I do not know whether you want to know this.**"

"I know you do not. Go on."

"**The sentence about a person being moved off ground. Was that in it on the seventh day.**"

"Yes," said the man of about twenty-nine. "**It was. It is one sentence longer than it was on the seventh day of the Long Month and I have not been able to find the room where I took it out, and I put that in a book of my own on the Tuesday and I have not shown it to anybody, and I am saying it now because she is going to ask.**"

"And what is the sentence you left out."

"**That a person who is moved off ground they have been on will be moved off it, and the weather will be the reason, and the reason will be true.**"

"**Read it out. In this room. In that order.**"

And he read it out, in that order, in that kitchen, in daylight, with a date on the door, and about nine people heard it, and the man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close wrote it down on the back of his own hand in the morning and has washed it off and it is on the page instead.

The third was Bel Dain, about fifty-nine, who cannot read, and she asked it in her own words and it took her about four minutes and she did not hurry it.

"A thing with a name is a thing that can be written to, and a name on a body is a column with a body in it. I refused the naming of a commons in this house on the fifteenth day of the Long Month and I was right, and I have held a wall in this house for nine years with a thing on it. **Is this a name.**"

"**It is a date on a door and a quantity of water and a wage, and none of those three is a name and none of them can be written to, and I have looked at all three of them in front of about nine people.**"

"**And in four months.**"

"**In four months somebody can put your name in a column about this and I cannot stop it and neither can he, and I would like it said in this room before anybody says yes rather than after.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

The fourth question was asked by the woman who keeps the gate, of one person, and it is the reason this chapter is on the page.

"You did not read it out the way you read it out on the seventh day of the Long Month."

"**No.**"

"**Say why. Not to me. Say it in the room.**"

"**Because I have read that cost out in five rooms since the seventh week and it has been shorter every time, and on the Tuesday a woman asked me whether it had got shorter and I could not tell her which room, and there is no column anywhere in four hundred miles or nine hundred for a man who notices that about himself.**"

"**Write that down and put the book away, because I have not got time for it. The weather went off in the night and I stood the racks yesterday with nine people and none of them was from your store and none of them was sent, and I am not going to say that to you as a way of not saying yes.**"

"**Then say yes.**"

"**I am going to, and not before my household is asked.**"

---

**And then the asking was made, and it was made once, and it was made by a person, and it is in the settlement's own words, and it has been on a raft eleven miles up the Reach for a week and nobody in that kitchen improved on it.**

"**We ask for the days. Nine days of a week, all of them, every one, for the rest of the season.**"

**And the keeper of the Redroot gate held the board in her left hand and did not put it down, and that is nine years old, and about four people in that room noticed it and about four of them had noticed it before and had been in a kitchen at the bottom of a channel in the sixth week of this flood.**

**And she said yes, and she said it in one sentence, and it is the whole of what she said, and nobody in that room asked her a second question and nobody read anything back.**

"**Yes. The days. All of them, for the rest of the season.**"

**And nobody counted hands, because a count is a list, and a list of who said what in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is, and a woman of about fifty-nine in that room said one sentence about it on the fifteenth day of the Long Month and it was in a book in this city in her own words and it has not improved since, and no hand went up and no hand stayed down.**

"**There is no vote in this room,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, into about nine seconds of nobody speaking, and she was not asking. "**I want that entered. Nine people are in this kitchen and one person said yes and nobody voted and if anybody writes down what happened in here this morning, the line will say one, and one is a count, and I would rather it said a person.**"

"It said a person," said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board. "**Write down that a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a gate said yes to nine days of a week for the rest of the season in a kitchen with a date on the door, and that there was no vote, and that is a page nobody can check, and it is the right shape.**"

---

**And then she said the other thing, and it is the last thing she said in that room on that morning, and it is entered in a book in this city in her own words, and it has not been softened by anybody and may not be.**

She put the board down on the bench by the door, which she has not done since the seventh day of the Long Month, and she did not pick it up again.

"**I chose the day. On the Sunday, on my own ground, and I did not tell anybody I was going to, and the meeting-ground did not choose it and could not have and said so, and a man of thirty-two was eleven miles away and had entered in his own hand that he was going to do nothing about it for twenty-five days and did not break his entry to be in this kitchen, and I told him at a door that I did not want him in it.**

**I have been angry about this since Sunday and I am going to be angry about it for a year, and on the forty-eighth day of the Long Month, which is a month from today, I am going to be angry about it, and I am not going to be talked out of it in this room, and I am not going to be talked out of it by myself either, and if any of you write this down afterwards then write down the year and not the day.**

**That is the whole of what I have. There is not going to be a second part of it and there is not going to be a reason.**"

Nobody in that kitchen improved on that, and nobody has improved on it since, and a woman of twenty-four was not in the room and wrote down afterwards, in her own hand, that the finding of the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month is that a person may say a thing that costs her a year and may not be given a reason for it, and that this is the fourth time in a hundred and seventy-nine days that this city has found an instrument it did not have, and that the instrument is a sentence with nothing after it.

---

**And two things did not happen in that kitchen and both of them are on the page because not happening is the only part of a room anybody can verify.**

A boy of eight was at the bottom of a channel for the whole of that morning and was not asked anything and was not in the room, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board stood outside the door for about an hour and did not stand near the boy, and about four people in this city have said that not standing near him was the hardest thing anybody did on the Reach that morning.

**And the rule of the Reach has a hole in it and the hole is the shape of a household that a change does not reach, and it has had that hole for about four hundred years, and nobody in that kitchen filled it on the Friday morning, and one person asked for it to be entered that it was not filled and that nobody had gone near it, and it is on the page because a rule that gets better when it is used is not a rule that anybody should be using.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening, eleven miles away, at the near gate of the Lock of Salt, thirty-nine men of forty turned up for the ninety-fourth morning since the first of March, and about nine of them looked up when a girl of about seventeen came down the road with a slate under her arm and stopped about nine feet from a man of about thirty-two and would not come nearer.**

She had been in a yard at Redroot from the ninth hour to the eleventh hour on the Friday morning and had not gone in, and then she had walked eleven miles, and she had started at the sixth hour of the morning because a man had given her three questions in writing on the Monday, four days before, and had said in the same note that he was not going to ask her whether the day suited her, and that she was not to answer any of them if she did not want to.

He had not asked her on the Wednesday whether the day suited her, and he did not ask her on the Thursday, and he did not ask her on the Friday.

"**You are supposed to ask me whether the day suited me,**" she said, "**and you are not going to, and I have thought about that for three days and I have got to the end of it and I am going to answer all three.**"

"**All three.**"

"**Did she say she would say it again.**"

"**No.**"

"**Did she say she was glad.**"

"**No.**"

"**What did she say.**"

And he wrote it down, in his own hand, on the back of his own arm, in a hand that is not a clerk's, and a girl of seventeen with a slate under her arm did not read it back and has not read it back and is not going to. He wrote the last of it on his own arm in pencil at the near gate of the Lock of Salt while thirty-nine men of forty stood about nine feet away and nobody said one word, and those men have gone ninety-four days without a wage and have not been told by anybody that a man with no mark and on no roster is the most findable person in this world.
