# Chapter 0152 — The Form Ran Out

A woman of twenty-four put a date at the top of a page on a Wednesday in a month nobody had a name for, and stopped, and could not go on, and the reason she could not go on was that there was no sixty-sixth.

**The chalk on the door of that room says the sixty-fifth day of the month after this one and it is yesterday's date and it cannot be taken off, because a chalk date is not a page and there is no form in four hundred miles for taking a date off a door, and about four people tried.**

---

The page was for the figures under the trestle board, which were going up in the hall for a second day because a man of forty-two had asked for a second day, and it needed a date, and the date had to be a day and a month, and the month ended at midnight.

"The sixty-fifth is yesterday," said a woman of twenty-four.

"Then it is the first," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It is the first of what.**"

Nobody in the room had an answer, and it is entered that about nine people were in that room between the ninth hour of the morning and the sixth hour of the evening and that the first hour of the day was spent on this and that the first hour is the one that counts, because a date at the top of a page is not a decoration on a page.

**And a notary of fifty who had been in the room for the first hour saying nothing came off the wall she was leaning on and asked for the page, and read the top of it, and put it face down on the boards.**

"Give me that," she said. "**I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I am the notary of about nine hundred things and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence, and I have never in twenty-six years been unable to write a date, and I would like it entered that this is the first time and that I am fifty.**"

---

**And here is what the finding of the first hour was, and it is the whole of the reason the rest of the day happened the way it did.**

The form was not anybody's fault. **The Nth day of this flood** ran from the first day to the fortieth. **The Nth day of the new month** ran sixty-five days and ended on the ninetieth. **The Nth day of the month after this one** ran sixty-five days and ended last night, on the hundred and fifty-fifth day of the flood. Both of the two month forms in this book are sixty-five days long and the flood has already turned the calendar over twice, and **nobody in four hundred miles has had to name a month since the first of it, because the first of it had a name already on it before any of them were born.**

"A compact between two named communities cannot be dated in a month that has no name," said a notary of fifty. "**And this city is going to try to do that in about a month, and it is going to be asked for a date, and I am the person who writes the dates.**"

"Can you not write a number?" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I can write a number. A number is not a date. A number is a day counted from something, and if the something is not written down anywhere then in about four months a clerk picks it up and it is the wrong month and there is nothing on the page to be angry at.**"

"**Then it is a chair with a number on it,**" said a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, who had said almost nothing for a hundred and fifty-five days and had said that sentence once, in a yard, on a day written in a month form nobody in this room uses any more, and it had never been copied and never been improved on.

"It is exactly that," said a notary of fifty, "**and I would like it noticed that you did not have to be asked and that you have not said a word in this room for six hours.**"

---

**And here is the thing nobody in that room had to be taught, and it did not take long to say out loud, and it is the reason the form ran out on a schedule and not by accident.**

The first month of the flood had a name before any of them were born, and everybody used it, and nobody in four hundred miles could tell you who had given it to whom. **On the first day of the new month a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had stood in a market and worked out, out loud, with three other people, that the fortieth day of the flood was the last day anybody could call a day a way that meant anything, and that the next one would have to be the first day of something, and that four of them were going to have to say the word new at the same time or not at all.** They said it at the same time. It was not a notary and it was not a vote and it was in no book, and the day it happened is on about nine people's memories and in nobody's hand.

**The new month ran sixty-five days. Nobody decided that either. It ran out because that is how long it ran, and the reason it ran sixty-five days is that the water went down over sixty-five days and came back, and the man who worked that out was a water-count, and a notary entered it in a book, and nobody in four hundred miles has ever asked her why, and nobody has ever asked her why sixty-five..**

"Then the third one will run sixty-five days as well," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**The third one will run however long it runs,**" said a notary of fifty, "**and I am not going to decide it today, and if I write a number on a door in about four months I will be inventing a season and not writing a date, and there is a difference and it is the whole of my trade.**"

---

**And they asked the person they would most obviously have asked, and she came, and she refused, and the refusal was correct, and it is entered, and it did not stop anything.**

She was a small dry woman of about seventy from the village of nine households that has refused four times, and she was asked in a room and not in a hall, by a person, in daylight, with a date on the door behind her, which is the shape this city uses for asking, and she said no before the question was finished.

"No," she said.

"You do not have to give a reason."

"**I am going to give a reason and you are going to write it down and it is going to be worse for you than for me.**" She did not sit down. "**I have put a date in chalk on nine doors in this last month and I did not write my name under one of them, and that is the only thing I have done in forty-four days that I am proud of. And I refused four times and the fourth time I refused as a person and not as a village, and there is nothing on any of the four except a date, and if you put my name on this page then in about four months there is a woman on a page about a month, and the month is the first thing anybody will look for, and I have spent a year making sure that I cannot be found.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And you are going to do it anyway,**" she said, "**and you are right to, and I am not going to stand in this room and stop you, and I would like it entered that I understood that when I said no, and I would like it entered that I am the one who said it, and not the nine, and that the nine were not asked.**"

Both were entered, and the woman of seventy went out and down the hill, and a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything wrote the refusal out in her own hand and read it back to the room once, and then looked at it for a while and did not read it again.

---

**And then a notary of fifty said the cost out loud before she said the name, and it took nine sentences, and she did not write them down and did not need to, because she is the only person in this city who keeps a count of the days and she had the date of the day she was going to need before anybody came up the hill, and a girl of seventeen wrote the number nine at the top of her slate and not the number of the sentences.**

"**One. A name invented by a notary is a name an office can use. Two. In about four months a clerk nine hundred miles away is going to write *the Long Month* at the top of a page and nobody in that building will be able to tell whether it means the month I mean. Three. And he will not ask me, because I am not in his district and I am a woman who certifies things for money. Four. I have been the only person in this city keeping a count of my own since the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else. Five. I said that base out loud in a room on the eighty-seventh day of this flood and nobody has asked me since and I am tired of being the only person here who keeps one. Six. A compact between two named communities cannot be dated in a month that has no name. Seven. The alternative is a page with a blank at the top of it and a page with a blank at the top of it is a chair. Eight. And I have certified about nine hundred things in twenty-six years and not one of them was mine. Nine. And I am going to do it anyway and I would like that entered before I do it and not after.**"

Somebody said *Sera* and she did not answer.

**And then she said the name, and the name is three words, and it is not hers.**

> **The third month is the Long Month.**
>
> **It begins today. Its sixty-fifth and last day is the two hundred and twentieth day of the flood, and that is a figure and not a name, and the name is *the Long Month*, and it goes on a page as *the Nth day of the Long Month*, and the Nth is a count from today and from nothing else.**
>
> **It is the first of the month after this one. It is not the flood. It is not a season. It is a name this room made today because the form ran out, and a notary of fifty made it, and I would like the room to hold on to that for about four months and then ask me whether I was right.**

---

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything wrote the date at the top of a page for the first time in the new form, and read it back, and read it back again, and both times it came out the same, which is a thing a person can say and is not an improvement.**

"The first day of the Long Month," she said.

"The first day of the Long Month," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**The first day of the Long Month,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and then he said the other thing, and it is seven words, and the girl of seventeen did not count them, because nobody counts them any more.

"**A dated thing can be found again.**"

---

**And the rest of the day was ordinary, and a notary of fifty wrote four other dates in the new form before the sixth hour of the evening and got them right four times out of four, and then did the thing she had not done in twenty-six years, which was to certify a thing she had made the name for, and she did it in about four minutes and it took nine people standing in a room and it is the first entry in this city in a hundred and fifty-six days that a person put at the top of it a thing that was hers and not a sentence, and about four people in that room understood what that was and the rest of them went home.**

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf stood by the window with a slate under her arm, and a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything looked at it for a while and did not ask her about it, and that is the whole of what happened between the two of them at the window.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening a girl of seventeen read the whole of the day's entry back in the hall for the people at the back, from a slate and not from her own page, because her own has her name at the top of it, and the date at the top of the slate was in a form that had not existed at the ninth hour of the morning, and about two hundred and forty people in a market underneath heard a month get named.**

**And they tried to take the date off the door for about half an hour, and about four people tried, and it is on the page because it is the argument.**

There is a shutter and a bar and a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall who would have got a corner of it with the bar before anybody could stop him. A woman of twenty-four said no because a door is not a page and a date on a door is the cheapest protection anybody in this city has ever had, and a man of thirty-two said no because a date that comes off is a date that can be put back wrong, and a girl of seventeen said no because the room was going to be about a calendar for four hours and the calendar should not be the door. And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall pointed out that the chalk said yesterday whichever way you looked at it, and that a room which cannot say what day it is has a problem that is older than this one, and that the room would be over before anybody could find a replacement, and that this was the correct answer and not a compromise. **Nobody washed it off and nobody covered it and the door carried two dates for the whole day, and the second one was in the same chalk by the same hand at the ninth hour of the evening, and about two hundred and forty people in a market underneath came up the stair to look at it.**

---

**And on the morrow a woman of about fifty who counts water on the fingers of her left hand came up the Reach road and would not come to the room, and left word at a stall, and the word she left was nine words long and it was this. Nobody comes to my house. Come to the gate. And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at it and said that this city had been asking her the wrong question since the eightieth day of this flood, and that he was not going to be the one who went on doing it, and that she was right.**
