# Chapter 0192 — Nine Feet Of Standing Room

The raft came off the cut at first light on the fortieth day of the Long Month and took most of the morning to get up to the low place where the red root grows, and about nine people were standing on that flat by the time it arrived, and not one of them had been asked to be there.

It is a Sunday. The store on the upper road shut at first light and did not open and did not say why, and about a hundred and forty people had a day, and about nine of them walked eleven miles on it.

---

**And about nine miles up the road past this city, at about the sixth hour of the same morning, about a hundred and forty people were standing about in a yard on a day with nothing in it, and the rota did not go up, and the board was washed and blank and hanging on the wall, and about nine of them put on their coats at about the seventh hour and went out of the gate on the Reach road and not one of them was carrying anything.**

A man of about forty-two was in that yard. He had been in it since the sixth hour. He did not stop anybody and he did not send anybody and he did not say a word to about nine people about where they were going, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that they had been on that road eleven miles each and that he had watched all of them go and had not said one word to any of them, and that about four of those nine asked him why afterwards and he gave the same answer four times.

"**Because a store that asks a person where they are going on its shut day is a store that has begun to keep a register of them, and I do not keep a register of them, and I have been at the front of that queue since the seventh week of this flood and I have got about nine hundred names in my head and I have never written one of them down, and that is the only thing I have got.**"

"You could have written nine today."

"**I could have written nine today and in about four months there would be a page with nine names on it and a date on it, and about four of those nine would be on it for walking to a flat, and a person who is on a page for walking to a flat is a person who is on a page.**"

He washed the board again at about the ninth hour, because it had started to run in the sun, and put it back up with nothing on it.

---

**And the raft is a raft with a roof on it, nine feet of standing room and a rail, and it cannot be summoned, and about four hundred people in this world have learned that in the same way and by the same method, which is by waiting for it to go somewhere else.**

It was two men with poles and a woman with a line, and it went the four miles of causeway and then eleven miles of Reach road's cousin, the bank, and every hour of it was poling, and twice it had to be held while the pole went round something on the bottom that nobody could see and could feel through the deck.

Nobody sent for it. Nobody could.

"**I have been on this raft six times since the sixth week of this flood,**" said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, "**and I have been poled to a place five times and I have been poled to a date once, and I would like that difference entered somewhere, because it is the whole of what happened this morning.**"

"What is the difference."

"**A place can be argued with. A date cannot. There is nobody at a date.**"

---

**And the poling took four hours and most of it was against something, and about nine people on the raft and about nine people on the bank and nobody was in a hurry, because a raft cannot be hurried and everybody in four hundred miles who has ever tried has found that out on a morning with a pole in their hands.**

The two men with poles were from Verrow and about nine people in this city have seen them at a bank in four months and about four of them know where Verrow is, and neither of them came the whole way. The woman with the line came the whole way because a raft with a roof on it cannot be walked across eleven miles of road and the line is the only thing that holds it if it gets into a cut.

At about the seventh hour it stuck. It stuck in a place where the channel narrows and there is about two feet of slack on the port side, and it was got off in about twenty minutes by a man of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road and a woman of about fifty-two who digs, and nobody in either of them said anything about it that was not about the rope, and the reason the man of about thirty-four gave afterwards, to about four people, was that the water is lower this week than it was in the second month of this flood and that the same place was about nine inches deeper then.

---

**And the four sentences were read out on that flat in the local language at about the ninth hour of the morning by a water-count of about fifty who had them on the flat of her left hand, and the chalk on a board at the bottom of the cut was about two feet away and was not used.**

> **On the fortieth day of the Long Month, a meeting-ground at Verrow will be on the flats at Redroot, and this question is put to it, in daylight, in the local language, with a date on the door, and it is this.**
>
> **Whether the Reed Commons will withdraw the days on the Redroot gate from the store up the road past this city, for the rest of the season.**
>
> **The person asking it is not a party to it and holds no office, no roster, no mark, no seat, no wage and no land right, and can be sent into any room in four hundred miles and be believed, and can be sent to no gate at all.**
>
> **And he will not answer it, and he will be at the back of it, and about four people already know that and about nine people in this city do not.**

It took about two minutes and there was no hall and no landing and no market under that flat, and about nine people heard it, and about four hundred and forty people were on the flats in front of it with their hands on racks and heard nothing at all.

**And the third sentence of it says there is a date on the door, and there is, in chalk, on the door of the house at the bottom of that cut, put there on Saturday morning by a hand about thirty-eight years old, and about nine people on that flat had walked past it a dozen times that morning without reading it, and nobody improved the question, and nobody has changed one word of it since it was written on a workroom table on the thirty-eighth, because the instrument says a question given in writing four days beforehand cannot be changed afterwards, and the first thing it did when it was built was stop its author.**

**And the four sentences went off a wet hand at about the eleventh hour, which is the only copy there has ever been, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf was standing nine feet away when it went and did not copy it and said why in four words, and nobody argued with her.**

"**I do not copy things I can hear.**"

"And the man who wrote them."

"**He is not here and I did not expect him to be, and I have been carrying them for a day and a night and I have not said them to myself once, and I am not going to start today.**"

---

**And a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the Redroot gate was standing at the mouth of the cut with a board in her left hand when the raft came round the bend, and she had counted it coming, and she said nothing to anybody about it and she has said since that she was not going to.**

The boy of eight was not on the flat. Nobody had sent him anywhere and nobody had told him to be somewhere and a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps a house at that place had given him a day off in advance on purpose, without being asked, and had asked for it to be entered that she had done it in advance.

**And a man of about thirty-two was not on the flat and was eleven miles away in a room over a market, and nobody asked him whether the day suited him, and the reason nobody asked is that the day is not a thing he is party to, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said that out loud at about the second hour of the afternoon and about four people heard it and one of them wrote it down.**

"**He is not here because he cannot be sent to a gate, and he is not answering it because he is not in the room, and both of those are the same thing, and the whole of the thirty-eighth and the thirty-ninth of the Long Month was him finding that out in public and this morning is the first morning it has cost anybody anything.**"

"You think it cost him."

"**I think nine people are standing on a flat waiting for a body to do something and he is the only man in four hundred miles anybody would have believed if he had stood up there, and he is not here, and I am not going to be the one who goes up eleven miles and fetches him, and the reason I am not is that a raft cannot be turned round.**"

---

**And the raft said one thing, and it said it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and it is short, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board wrote it on the back of his own hand and did not improve on it and has not improved on it since.**

"**I cannot answer that.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Not because I will not. Because I hold no days on any gate. I am a raft with a roof on me and nine feet of standing room and a rail, and I have been on this water since before the licence and I have never once held a day, and there is no column anywhere in four hundred miles in which a raft could be given days and I have looked for one in every language anybody on this flat can read.**"

"Then what do you do with it."

"**The only thing I have ever done with a question I cannot answer. I was poled here on the sixteenth day of the Long Month with a settlement's question about the same gate and I did not answer that one either, and I said then that the day must come back to me, and the day came back as the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month, which was a Friday that a woman chose on her own ground and told nobody about.**"

"And this one."

"**This one I will hold until the rule has been done. The rule is that every household affected by a change must be heard. It is four hundred years old. It has no name on it, and I am not going to give it one, and nobody built it who is standing on this flat. And it does not say who is affected. I am not a body that can find out who is affected, and I am not going to guess, and I have been sitting on this water since the ninth hour of the morning and I have not asked one person on this flat whether they are the household, and I am not going to.**"

"**Then you are going to be here all night.**"

"**I am going to be here all night and I am going to be here in the morning, and it is the fortieth day of the Long Month and it is a Sunday and about nine of you came out on it, and there is no other date in four hundred miles this month that I could have been poled to, and nobody chose that one either, and a man of forty-two worked it out in a yard on the thirtieth and a man of about thirty-nine wrote it down in a workroom on the thirty-eighth and a woman of about thirty-four said it out loud in front of about two hundred and forty people on the thirty-ninth, and it is on three pages and it is the only instrument I have ever had.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty who counts water on the fingers of her left hand, sitting on the deck of a raft tied at the bottom of that cut with her left hand flat on her knee, and nobody on it but her.**
