# Chapter 0174 — Standing the Racks

The wind came round onto the flats at about the eleventh hour of the morning on the twenty-third day of the Long Month and went from nothing to a thing you had to lean on inside a quarter of an hour, and about four hundred and forty racks of salt that had been standing in the sun for nine days had to be covered before the fourth hour of the afternoon, and there were about nine people on that flat who were not on the rota and were not allowed to be.

**It is a Thursday. The day before. The board at the bottom of the channel has a rail on it and the rail has a date on it now, chalked in a fifth hand at about the seventh hour of the morning, and it is the twenty-third, and it is tomorrow's date underneath it.**

---

The rota went up on the board at about the seventh hour in the morning because the rota goes up on a Thursday, and it has gone up on a Thursday for nine days and it is copied by hand in four copies and about nine people who have never been to the upper road are keeping it, and it goes off the board when the rain is bad enough.

It went up in a hand that is not any of the four. It went up in a hand that is about thirty-eight years old and it took about four minutes and it did not go off in the rain, and the woman who put it there stood afterwards with the chalk still in her hand and looked at it, and about four people on that flat have said that what she was looking at was not the chalk.

"You have put the twenty-third on it," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**I have put the twenty-third on it because tomorrow is the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fourth is on nine hands and on a board and in four copies and I would like it to have a day before it.**"

"There is a rail. The rail is for dates."

"**The rail is for dates that get washed off. That is what a rail is for.**"

---

**And then the weather did the thing it had been going to do since Wednesday, and about four hundred and forty racks took about five hours to cover, and the covering is a job and not a thought, and here is the whole of it.**

Each rack is eleven feet off the ground and about nine measures go on it and the salt comes off the shelf in slabs the size of a floorboard and it has to be levered and not lifted, and the cover is a reed mat that has to go on the windward side first and be weighted along the top with stones, because a mat that lifts takes a rack of salt into the channel, and the channel is the thing that carries the water off the flats and a channel with a hundred and forty measures of salt in the bottom of it is a channel that has to be dug out by hand in a wet month.

**And the promise that has been made about the racks for nine days is a promise about a person, and not about the weather, and the person is nine miles up a road.**

*We will hold the channel and clear the drain and stand the racks in any weather.*

"**Nobody from that store is on this flat,**" said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, at about the first hour of the afternoon, with his coat on backwards and one hand on a mat. "**There are a hundred and forty of them and about nine of them know what a rack is, and about nine of them are on the upper road this afternoon, and none of them is here, and the promise was about weather and it is not the weather that is missing.**"

"**Nobody sent for them,**" said a woman of about fifty-two who digs.

"**Nobody sent for them because there is nothing to send with, and because it is the fourth hour of the afternoon and the wind is on the mat, and because a settlement of a hundred and forty people nine miles up a road is a settlement of a hundred and forty people and not an amount of rope. I am not saying the store has broken a word. I am saying a word about a store is not a pair of hands on a flat.**"

"That is the finding of the week," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, and nobody improved on her, and the finding of the week is in nine words and it belongs to nobody: **a promise made in a room is about a room.**

---

**And the racks were covered, and it took about nine people and about five hours and about nine people who were not allowed to be on the rota were on the racks, and a rule from a store that is not on this flat cost that flat about an hour and a half of labour on the day before the day a household gave away its days, and nobody resented it, and it is entered that nobody resented it.**

And the boy of eight was on that flat all day, and he had been told about the day off that morning and had not known what to do with a day off, and he spent it on the windward side of about nine racks with a stone in each hand, because he is the fastest person on that flat at carrying a stone and has been since the sixth day of the Long Month, and about nine adults discovered that fact on the Thursday and nobody had arranged it.

He was not improving on anybody. He was faster than a woman of about forty-four who cannot get a rack above her shoulder and he knew it and said so once, to nobody, and then carried another stone.

---

**And eleven miles away, in the city, on the same Thursday, a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall found a man of about thirty-two in a gateway and said one thing to him and it took about four minutes and neither of them improved on it.**

"You have not been asked anything in four days."

"**I have not been asked anything in four days, and the day before that I was asked nothing either, and the day before that I was told a thing at a gate, and a man of forty-two gave me a name on the Saturday and asked me for nothing with it, and the count of things that have happened to me in a hundred and seventy-eight days that I was not asked about is the whole of the last three volumes and I have stopped finding it remarkable.**"

"Then you are going to be asked on Friday."

"**I am not going to be asked on Friday. Nobody is going to ask me on Friday, because a man who is not a party to a thing is not asked about the thing, and I said that out loud at a door on Sunday and I did not change my mind on Monday and I have not changed it in the four days since. And the twenty-fourth is the fifteenth day of twenty-five that I entered in my own hand with a date on it, and there are ten of them after it, and I am going to keep it.**"

"There are eleven days of the quarter left," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall. "**That is not a figure I have made up. The hearing was on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one and the office that held it cannot be reached and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of it is the thirty-fourth day of the Long Month, which is eleven days from today, and on that day you will have nothing to put in front of it, because there is no road that reaches it and nothing can be sent and nothing could be prepared, and you have known all four of those things since the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And you are going to be right about all of it on Friday, in a gateway, eleven miles from a kitchen, and you are going to be right about it on Saturday, and about four people in this city are going to tell you afterwards that you were right.**"

"**I know.**"

"**And being right about it is going to be the worst thing that has ever happened to you and there is going to be nobody in the world to say so, and I am saying it now on a Thursday, in a gateway, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and you are not going to be able to use it, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like it entered that I said it in advance and that I am not going to say it again.**"

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, in a kitchen, in daylight by a lamp, a woman of about thirty-eight put a board down on a bench by a door and sat down on the bench and told a boy of eight what she had told him on Monday she would tell him on Thursday night, and she did it herself, and nobody else was in the room, and it is entered that she asked for it to be entered that she did it herself.**

He had the rope. He had had it all day and he had it in his lap on the bench and he had eleven knots in nine feet of it and about four of them were the sort that comes undone if somebody is in a hurry and he had done them again anyway.

"**The water's going to somebody else,**" she said.

He did not say anything and he did the knot.

"**All of it. From tomorrow. For the rest of the season. And there is a room over in this house tomorrow morning at about the ninth hour and about nine people are going to be in it, and I am going to say yes.**"

He did the knot again, and it came undone, and he did it again.

"Who."

"**Nine people from a store up the road past this city. And about four of them are not going to be from that store. And I have not asked one of them whether today suited them and neither have I asked you.**"

"Does Dad know."

And that is the whole of the telling, and it is nine sentences, and it is what it is, and nobody in this book is going to be able to do better with it or worse.

"**No. And I am not going to tell him, because he is on the water two days a week and there is a man of fifty-two who can say it for me on the day and I have asked him and he has said yes, and that is the arrangement and I made it on Monday and I did not tell you that either.**"

"You told me Monday."

"**I told you Monday that there were going to be people in the room on Friday. I did not tell you what I was going to say, and I have thought about that for two days and I would do it again, because a boy of eight can carry a day that has not happened and he cannot carry one that has.**"

He put the rope down on the bench, which he had not done all day.

"Can I ask you something."

"**You can ask me anything. I will answer about nine things out of ten and I have told you in advance which ten.**"

"Will the bell still be there."

"**The bell will be there. The rope will be there. The board will be there, and there is a rail on it, and there is a date on the rail for tomorrow, and the gate will be there on Monday morning when I go down and open it, and it will be there in four months, and the whole of what is different is whose turn it is and not whether it is there.**"

He thought about that for a while, on a bench, in a kitchen, at about the ninth hour of the evening on the day before.

"**Then can I have the rope,**" he said, "**because if it is only whose turn it is then I do not need to be frightened of a rope.**"

"**You have the rope.**"

And that is the last of the twenty-third day of the Long Month, and there were eleven days of the quarter left in it, and nobody improved on a child of eight, and on a bench by a door there is a gate board with two dates on the rail and a handprint on the back of it and about nine feet of rope.
