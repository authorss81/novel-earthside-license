# Chapter 0198 — A Person May Be Found

Nine sentences went into a page on the morning of the forty-fourth day of the Long Month in a room over a market with a date in chalk on the door, and the page is a posting, and it has a date at the top of it and a price under every line, and about nine people were in the room and the hall outside it was empty.

It is a Thursday. The store on the upper road was open and the queue had run on the Tuesday and had worked, and about nine hundred measures of salt were standing in a yard at the bottom of a cut, covered, and the day before this nine of the hundred and forty had walked eleven miles and stood on a flat.

---

**And the nine sentences are these, and they are in a man of about thirty-four's own hand and he read them out in about four minutes and nobody improved on one of them, and the woman of about twenty-four wrote none of them down and has not been asked to and did not offer.**

> **The ground stays with the households.**
>
> **No land right of any kind is transferred, and no workway is a term of anything, and no party to this may be required to surrender one.**
>
> **The labour is shared and posted, and this is the posting, and it has a date on it and a price under every line.**
>
> **Nobody may be bought out of a refusal, and the refusal does not need a reason and is not asked for twice.**
>
> **The salt is a common and it is not a product, and the road is open to everybody on a posted day.**
>
> **The store on the upper road is on this posting, and its queue is on this posting, and it takes its place on it and not above it.**

"**Say the last line again and say it slower.**"

"**The store on the upper road is on this posting, and its queue is on this posting, and it takes its place on it and not above it.**"

"**Who wrote that line.**"

"**I did, and I will put my hand to it, and I am not going to pretend I did not write it because it is the only line in nine sentences that anybody in this room is going to lose something by.**"

---

**And the fourth sentence is the third sentence of a thing called the Open Hand and it cannot be administered, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said that in about nine minutes in the second month of this flood, and he is right, and his objection is in the posting in his own words with the date on it, and nobody took it out and he did not take it out.**

"**I said in the second month of this flood that a right of refusal that cannot be bought out is a rule nobody is going to check, and I said it in about nine minutes, and about four people wrote it down and about nine of them wrote it down as though I had said it was impossible, and I did not say that and I am not going to say it now.**"

"So the sentence does nothing."

"**The sentence does the only thing a sentence can do. It is on a page with a date on it and it cannot be bought out, and a person who is bought out has been bought out of a thing that was never enforceable, and the reason it is worth having is not that it works. The reason it is worth having is that it is the shape of a refusal, and a shape of a refusal is the only thing a person can hold up in about four years.**"

"Put it in."

"**Put it in my words and put the date on it and put my name to it, and I want it said in this room that I am putting an objection into a document that I am also a party to, and I have been refused by name four times in four years and I have never once been in a room where a thing I wrote went into a page that did not have my name on it, and I am not going to be careful about it today.**"

---

**And a man of forty-two took a share, and it was a share on posted days on the same posting as every other share in that page, and he said nine sentences about it and eight of them are the best arguments anybody in this city has heard since the seventh week of this flood and the ninth one is a word.**

"**One. I lose the export. That started on Monday night and it is not stopping, and I did not cause it, and I am not going to stand in a room and be praised for it.

**Two. I keep the queue. The queue is on this posting and it takes its place on it and not above it, and that is the sentence I have been living on since the seventh week of this flood and I am giving it up on a Thursday morning in a room with a door.

**Three. I have about a hundred and forty people who are paid on a Tuesday and I have two weeks of it in the store and after that I do not know, and I said that to about nine people on Monday and about nine of them wrote it down.

**Four. This posting has a price under every line and I have never in my life put a price under a line before and I have found out in about four minutes that I do not know how to write one of them out.

**Five. A right of refusal that cannot be bought out is in there, and it is a man of about thirty-nine's sentence, and he says it cannot be administered, and he is right, and it is in the posting anyway.

**Six. I have been arguing against a commons since the seventh week of this flood and I have been right about most of it and I have never once been asked to be in a room where one of my own sentences went into a page.

**Seven. I am not going to tell you what I think of it. I have been wrong about eight things in nineteen days and I have been wrong about them out loud, and a man who is wrong out loud is worth more than a man who is right in a yard.

**Eight. And the ninth thing is a word and it is not mine, and I have known it since the sixth week of this flood and I have never heard anybody else use it, and I am going to say it and I am not going to say it twice.

**Nine. It is *order*.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds, and Renna Ord, who is fifty-eight and cannot read and who reads a join by standing on it twice a day, was standing at the back of it with her hands at her sides, and she said nothing, and about four people in that room knew that the word was hers, and about nine of them have known since a converted salt store in the eighteenth day of the Long Month, and neither she nor he has ever heard of the other and this is the second room they have been in together.

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the price before she said yes, and she has been on nothing for a hundred and ninety-nine days and she said so first, and it is hers and about nine people in this room have said that nobody in four hundred miles gets that right more than twice.**

"**I have been on nothing for a hundred and ninety-nine days and I am going to be on a page before this morning is over, and I have been on one once in nine years and it was a slate and it cost somebody else the price of it and not me, and I am going to say the price now and then I am going to say yes, and if I say the price after the yes then it is not a price, it is a complaint.**"

"**Say the price.**"

"**I have kept a stall by a wharf shut for nine years and I have kept myself off every page in this city for nine years, and the reason is not modesty and it is not fear. The reason is that a page is a place a person can be found in, and I did not want to be found while I was arguing. That is the whole of it. I have argued in about nine markets in this city for nine years and not one person in this city knows what I look like while I am doing it, and about four people know that I am somewhere in it.**"

"And the price."

"**The price is that from today there is a date and a stall and a name on a page, and anybody in four hundred miles who has a copy of that page can walk to that wharf and stand in front of that stall and wait, and I will be there, and I will be the one who comes out, and I will be findable while I am arguing, which is the one thing I have paid nine years not to be.**"

"That is a real cost."

"**It is the whole of the cost and it is not a hardship. On the thirty-seventh day of the Long Month I said in that room that being findable is the price of being real and not the penalty for it, and I have been on the other side of that sentence for nine years and I am not going to argue with it today, and I would like it entered that I am not arguing with it today and I have not enjoyed it.**"

"Then say yes."

"**I am going to say one more thing first and then I am going to say yes, and I have been not going to be thanked for nine years and I am asking in advance, on this page, in my own words, for nobody in this room to thank me, including the four of you who are about to, and the reason is that I have been thanked twice in nine years and both times it was in a market and both times I did not know who had and I have been working it out since.**"

Nobody thanked her.

"**I said no for nine years. I am saying yes this morning. I would like it entered that I said no for nine years, and that this is the yes, and that the two are the same person, and that I am not a different woman today, and that anybody who wants to make me a different woman for the sake of a page can go and look for her because there is not one.**"

"Yes."

"**And I have been asked to take the ninth line and I am taking it, and the ninth line is a person, and a person may be found, and a commons that cannot be found cannot be a party to anything and cannot be answered for, and I have been the person in this city that nothing could be written to since the second month of this flood, and I would like it entered that I have been enjoying that.**"

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the four sentences she has been carrying, and the only instruction on them is hers, and this compact is not it and does not touch it.**

"**Four people and a question have not left for the ground people call the First Landing, and nobody has been asked whether they will go, and nobody is going to be asked, and the only instruction it has is *to open nothing*, and that is my sentence and I wrote it. This is not that document and it does not end it and it does not touch it, and I would like both halves of that entered.**"

"A page with eleven lines on it."

"**A page with eleven lines on it, a date on it, and a name on the front of it, and about nine people in a room, and about two hundred and forty who can hear out of a hall and a landing and a market below it tomorrow, and I have read one out in this room before and this one is not that one and I will say why tomorrow and not today.**"

---

**A hand on a door in chalk, held there by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall for about four minutes after everybody else had gone down the stairs.**
