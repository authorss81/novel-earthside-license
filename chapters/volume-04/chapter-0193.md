# Chapter 0193 — It Will Not Name A Household

About nine people were standing on a low place at the bottom of a long cut on the morning of the forty-first day of the Long Month, and a raft with a roof on it was tied at the edge of that place with a line through a ring, and behind them about four hundred and forty people were on the flats with their hands on racks, and the rule that nobody standing there could name was going to have to be done in the open.

It is a Monday. The store on the upper road opened at the sixth hour and the queue's day is tomorrow, and a woman of about thirty-four in that store asked a man of about forty-two a question on Saturday evening about why the shut was on a door, and he had not answered it and has not answered it, and the store is open and about a hundred and forty people are on a rota that is a page in four hands, and yesterday's line was not copied because there was nobody in that yard to read it, and the line for today went up this morning in four hands and was read by about four people.

---

**And about nine people got to that flat in four hours and two of them walked the eleven miles, and the girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything came up the Reach road behind a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and neither of them said anything for nine miles and about four miles of that was in the rain.**

She had a slate under her arm and it was not her own. She had asked him, on the Sunday, whether she was going to be needed, and he had said that a meeting-ground is a raft and she would be needed if she could read his own question out on a flat, and she had said that the question was in the local language and she could, and that the woman carrying it was the better person to ask, and that if nobody asked her then she would not ask.

**And the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had not asked her, and that is on the page because about four people in this city have spent nineteen days waiting for somebody to ask a girl of seventeen something and nobody has, and the reason nobody has is that the instrument this city built says nobody asks the person whether the day suits them, and the instrument was built about a man of thirty-two and it is now being used on everybody in the room including people who were not in it when it was written.**

They came onto the flat at about the seventh hour and the raft was not there yet. A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his hand flat on a rack frame and looked at the cut for a while and said one thing that about nine people have written down since.

"**It comes round that bend at about the ninth hour. It comes round that bend at the ninth hour on every day it has been on this water, and there is no way to be early for it and no way to make it late, and that is the whole of what a body that floats is: a thing that turns up.**"

---

**And the rule was said out loud at about the eighth hour of the morning by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and it took him four sentences, and a woman of about fifty who counts water on the fingers of her left hand then said the half of it that is hers, and nobody improved on either.**

"**Every household affected by a change must be heard. That is the rule. It is the whole of it, it is about four hundred years old, and it is the only thing in this morning that anybody can act on.**"

"**And it has no name on it,**" said the water-count, "**and I am not going to give it one, and I am not going to say who built it, and I have said both of those things four times in nine years to about four different people and about nine of them wrote it down and about four of them came back and asked me who built it, and I have never told one of them, and I am not going to start on a Monday on a flat.**"

"**Then somebody is going to ask which household.**"

"**Yes, and nobody standing here is going to answer it, and I want to say why before anybody tries, and it is because the rule has no column in it. It says every household affected must be heard. It does not say which one is affected, and it does not say who works that out, and about four hundred years of people have not put that in because as long as there is a place where the people are, the people work it out between them, and about nine of you are here.**"

---

**And then the raft said the thing that about four people on that flat had not thought of, and it said it at about the ninth hour, and it is the only thing a body that floats can do with a question it cannot answer, and about nine people in this city have said since that the whole of what this city has built came down to a raft refusing to guess.**

"**I will not name a household.**"

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf put her head on one side.

"**You are a meeting-ground of the Reed Reach. You have been on this water since before the licence. You have a roof and nine feet of standing room and you have heard every household on this water say things it did not want to say. Name it.**"

"**No.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because I do not know. And I am not going to find out by asking nine people one at a time in front of the other eight, and I am not going to find out by working round the houses until one of them stops talking, and if I do either of those then in about four months there will be a thing on this water that is a household with a mark on it, and I have been on this water for four hundred years and I have never left a mark on anything.**"

And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board said the four sentences that made it a rule instead of a preference, and he did not name a gate, a household or a person while he said them, and about four people on that flat wrote it down and about nine will write it down since.

"**He is right and he is right for my reason and not for his. I have been refused by name four times in four years and I do not name a gate, a household or a person when I report, because a description is a removal with a column for where the person is. That is my rule and it has kept four of my own people out of a book.**"

"And a raft."

"**And a raft. A raft that names a household is a body doing the thing I have spent four years refusing to do, and it is doing it in the open, on a flat, in daylight, with about nine people standing there, and about four hundred and forty people behind them with their hands on racks who will hear about it by the fourth hour of the afternoon and be told one name.**"

"**Then who says it.**"

"**Nobody says it. The rule says every household affected must be heard, and a household that has been affected knows it, and if nine people are standing on a flat then the thing that happens is that about nine people look at about nine other people.**"

---

**And the four sentences were on the flat of her left hand from the ninth hour until about the eleventh, and then they were not, and that is the only copy there has ever been and it is on the page.**

She read them four times. A man of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road asked her twice to say the third one again and she said it again both times and did not hurry either. At about the tenth hour a woman of about fifty-two who digs asked whether a thing put on a hand could be got wet, and the water-count said it could, and that it had been wet since the ninth hour of the evening before, and that in about an hour it would stop being legible, and that a sentence which is in a wet hand is a thing that four people in four hundred miles can be arguing about for about a year.

"And I am one of the four."

"**You are one of the four, and you are not going to be asked what you remember of it in about four months by anybody in an office, and I am not going to be asked either, and the reason we will not be asked is that there is no form in four hundred or nine hundred miles for asking a person what was said to them by a raft.**"

"Then it is only in us."

"**Then it is in about nine people and one hand, and about four days from now a person is going to be able to say that the question on that flat was not the question the man wrote, and there will be no way anybody in this city can check it, and I have been trying to build a thing in this city since the seventh week of this flood that could be checked and the only thing I have got is nine people remembering it.**"

---

**And about nine people did, for about two hours, and nobody convened them and nobody called them to it and nobody wrote a name down, and it is on the page because it is the only time in four hundred years anybody in this city has watched a rule do its own work.**

It is a hard thing to watch. It is not a vote and there was no count and nothing was minuted and no hand went up. A woman of about fifty-two who digs looked at the water and then looked at the ground. A man of about thirty-four from a fourth household on the road sat down on a rack frame and then stood up again. A woman of about forty-four who cannot get a rack above her shoulder looked at nine feet of somebody else's rail and then at the man of about thirty-nine, and the man of about thirty-nine looked at his own boots.

And a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close said the only question anybody asked in those two hours, and it was four words and it was out loud without being asked for.

"Is it me then."

"**I do not know,**" said the man of about thirty-nine. "**And I have said *I do not know* four times in four years and I have never once been asked to, and this is the first time I have wanted to say it and cannot.**"

---

**And at about the eleventh hour a woman of about thirty-eight came out of the gateway with a board in her left hand, and she had not been on that flat since the eighth hour of the morning, and about nine people saw her come and about nine of them stopped what they were doing, and nobody went to get her and nobody spoke to her.**

She put the board down on the deck of the raft. She did not pick it up.

"**I am not going to be asked.**"

Nobody asked her.

"**I want that entered, and I want it entered by somebody who is not me, and I want it entered that I have not said the thing yet, and that I am going to say it because a rule is going to make me, and that a rule making me is not anybody asking me, and that is the whole of the difference and I have been living inside it for seventeen days.**"

"Then you do not have to say it."

"**I do not have to say it and I am going to say it, and if the four of you go home now it is done and nobody will ever ask me again in my life, and about four hundred and forty people behind me are going to be told by the fourth hour of the afternoon that the salt road is shut or that it is not, and not one of them is going to be told which.**"

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall sat down on the rail.

"**Then say it. And I am going to sit here and not say one word, and if I say one word you are to take the board off the deck and I will not argue.**"

**A board face up on a deck, and nobody within reach of it.**
