# Chapter 0169 — The Witness

At about the ninth hour of the morning on the eighteenth day of the Long Month a pale field came up in front of a man of thirty-two and was gone before it had finished arriving and said one short thing, and he said no before it had finished going away, and a person in the room named the cost out loud, and that day repeats the number before it and the count of silences does not move on it, and nobody improved on the rule.

**It is a Saturday. It is the eighteenth day of the Long Month and the count of this city's silences is seventy-eight, and it was seventy-eight yesterday, and a day the wall says something on is not a silence however hard the day was, and that rule has not moved since the fourth day of the month after this one and it is not going to move today.**

---

The field is not a light. It is in front of him and it is not in front of anybody else and it is gone, and what it says is in plain language and it is short and it is the language of obligations and not the language of anything else.

> **ANY CORRECT FIGURE MAY BE CHECKED BY A PERSON.**
>
> **The person who checks it answers for the check and not for the figure.**
>
> **Nothing is owed. Nothing is given. It can be done in a room in daylight.**

"No," he said.

**And the woman of twenty-four named the cost out loud, in the room, in daylight, without taking a breath, and about nine people were in it and about four of them heard the field and about five did not, and the cost is named here and it is hers.**

"**It costs whoever stands in the room when they find one that is wrong. It does not end when the figure is right. It ends when the person who found it has finished being answerable, and there is no form in four hundred miles or nine hundred for finishing, and I have looked, and I would be the one keeping the column if anybody were.**"

"That is the price of a correct page," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**That is the price of a correct page and the price is a name and the name is on the inside of it where only the person who is paying it can feel it. That is the whole of what has happened this morning and I would like it entered and I would like it not improved on.**"

**And it was entered. And the count is seventy-eight this morning and it was seventy-eight yesterday, and there have been thirty-three things the wall has said in this city and four of them have been refused out loud, and a man of thirty-two has refused the thirty-third before it had finished going away and the refusal cost somebody something specific and it is on this page, and that is the rule and the rule did not move today and nobody in four hundred miles is going to be able to say that the fourth refusal did not count because the day was hard.**

---

**And then the rest of the day takes eleven hours, and what is found is at the end of it, and what is found is a name.**

A man of forty-two had asked for four days on the Saturday before this and had been given them and had used them to write a page with no date on it, and a man of thirty-two had taken four days over it and had not said yes and had not said no, and the four days ran out on Wednesday, and on Wednesday evening the man of forty-two wrote to a stall and asked whether the man who holds nothing would be in a room on the Saturday, and the man who holds nothing said yes, and the stall said yes, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall wrote both answers in a book of his own and entered that a man of forty-two had been in four rooms in four days and that three of them were stores.

**The room is the same room. One shutter open, one off. The table back in it. Nothing on the walls. Two dates in chalk on a door. About nine people. A hall, a landing and a market under them, and about two hundred and forty people can hear out of all three on a Saturday, and this is the fourth time this room has had about two hundred and forty people behind it, and the fifth thing anybody has been asked to be in it for is not a form.**

---

**And at about the second hour of the afternoon a man of forty-two said nine sentences, and eight of them are the best arguments anybody in this city has heard in a hundred and seventy-three days, and the ninth one is a name, and it is the reason this day exists.**

"One. There is a man in this city who can be sent into any room and be believed, and he has no mark, no roster, no seat, no wage, no office, no title and no land right, and there is no column anywhere in four hundred miles or nine hundred for him and there never will be."

"Two. **That is not a gift anybody in this room has given him. It is a thing that has happened to him, twice, in public, and he said so himself on a Monday in front of about two hundred and forty people, and he said it was not a safety.**"

"Three. Every settlement in this world that is not this one has a person it sends, and that person is on a roll, and the roll has a name at the top of it, and the roll is the thing that is found, and my store does not have one and cannot have one, because I have been on a board that goes down a river four times a year and I have seen what happens."

"Four. So I have been running an experiment for a hundred days without telling anybody, which is that you can run a settlement out of a queue, and I can, and I have, and the whole of it is one man at the front of a line who learned nine hundred names and never wrote one of them down."

"Five. And in four days' time that man is going to be somewhere and I do not know where and I have not asked, and a settlement of a hundred and forty people is going to be somewhere in nine days, and I have never once in a hundred and seventy-three days sent anybody anywhere and I am not going to start by sending a man who cannot say no to a wage."

"Six. So I am not sending anybody. I am asking for a word and the word is a thing a person can be called in a room and not a post."

"Seven. It is not a post. It is not a roll, it is not a wage, it is not a title, it is not a class, and it is not on any page of mine, and it goes nowhere, and there is no form for it and no form against it, and if you take it you cannot give it up and if you refuse it you cannot be asked why."

"Eight. **And I have thought for two days about whether I am doing this to a man instead of doing it for a settlement, and I have decided I do not know, and I am saying that in front of about two hundred and forty people instead of deciding it in a yard.**"

"Nine. **The word is *the witness*, and it is yours, and I am not going to say it twice.**"

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked the only question in that anybody asked all day which mattered, and it is four words long.**

"To whom do you answer."

**And the man of forty-two did not have an answer to that either, and said so, and the room did not fill the silence for him, and it is entered that a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read and who reads a join by standing on it twice a day said one word out loud at that point and it was not the word anybody was waiting for, and the word was *order*, and a man of forty-two heard it and did not know where he had met it, and the room went on and it is on the page because it is on the page, and nobody in that room had any idea that a woman of fifty-eight had said that word in a yard in a town nine hundred miles from here on the tenth day of the new month, and that a man from another world had arrived at it from the other end in the sixth week of this flood and told a hall of about two hundred and forty people that a list of people with no shift attached to it is an order, and that neither of them has ever heard of the other, and that this is the first time the two of them have been in a room.**

---

**And the four hours between the second hour of the afternoon and the sixth hour of the evening are on the page because about nine people came into that room in them and this is what they did with a name they had not asked for.**

A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf came at about the third hour and asked whether the word was in a book, and was told it was not, and said that was the only reason she had come, and went and stood outside.

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came at about the fourth hour and said that he had been thinking about it since the second hour and that he could not get past one thing, which was that a name you cannot be given up is a name you cannot hand on, and that in about four years, when he is not here, somebody will ask who passed it to him, and that he will have to say a man of forty-two, and that a man of forty-two will then be a person who is in a chain in four hundred miles, and that the man of forty-two knows this and offered it anyway, and that the man of forty-four who keeps a stall said the whole of the answer in about nine words: **he is not in a chain, he is in a queue, and the queue is where people are, and you have all been angry about chains for a hundred and seventy-three days and nobody has been angry about a queue once and that is what I would look at.**

A girl of seventeen on no roll of anything came at about the fifth hour with a slate and stood in the hall and did not come up, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went out and asked her whether she wanted to come up, and she said no, and gave a reason that is in a book in this city in her own words and is not improved on: **I want to be in the room when it is said and I do not want to be in the room when it is written down, and those are two different rooms and I have only ever been good at one of them.**

And a man of thirty-two stood in the middle of that room for four hours with a name, and did not use it once, and it is entered that he did not use it once, and that he had two occasions in those four hours in which using it would have moved a thing, and that a woman of twenty-four saw both of them, and that she has not written down what they were, and that she has been asked twice and has declined twice, and that the second decline was on the eighteenth day of the Long Month and was the correct one.

---

**And Adrian Vale did not refuse it, and it was not restraint and it was not agreement, and it was plainly the third thing, and a woman of twenty-four entered that in these words: *a third thing is not a category anybody in this city has a column for, and the absence of the column is the whole of the finding, and I have looked for a column twice and there is not one and I have stopped looking.***

"**Say that again in a month,**" he said, in about nine seconds.

And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall said it in the hall, where about two hundred and forty people could hear him, and did not stop:

"**He said those words to that man in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and then he said *I will not*.**"

"I know," said Adrian Vale. "**I said it to him and I did not mean it, and this time I do not know what I mean, and that is the difference, and it is not a better answer.**"

---

**And at about the sixth hour of the evening somebody in a market said the word about him to a person who had not met him, and it was not said unkindly, and it was said the way a word is said when a person has just heard it twice and has not been told what it means.**

Somebody in a market said *the witness* about a man of thirty-two, to a woman of about thirty-four, who did not know him.

"And who is that."

"That is the man who reads things."

"He does not read things."

"He is called it," said the man who was about forty-four and kept a stall, and he did not explain it further, and the woman did not ask again.

**And a man of thirty-two heard about that the following morning from a girl of seventeen with a slate, and the finding of the eighteenth day of the Long Month is a single sentence and it belongs to nobody: being findable is not the penalty for being real. It is the price of it. And a man who is about to be the price of something is going to be asked for it.**

---

**And the last thing on the eighteenth day of the Long Month is a slate in the hand of a girl of seventeen, with a date on it, and a name on it that is nobody's, and she has not read it back and is not going to.**

"You are not reading it back," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**No. A name on a page is a place a person can be found in. I have watched one work tonight in a market, from about nine feet away, and it took no time at all, and nobody in it did anything wrong. I am not going to be the one who writes it down.**"

"And that is the first thing you have not written down this year."

"**It is the first thing I have not written down because I have wanted to, and I want it entered that I wanted to, and I am not going to.**"
