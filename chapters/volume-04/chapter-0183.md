# Chapter 0183 — What a Warden Is For

Water came up the low end of a causeway on the thirty-second day of the Long Month at about the sixth hour of the morning and took the bottom four hundred yards of it, and about nine people from a store up the road past this city and about nine people from the flats cleared it in about six hours, and nobody sent for anybody, and the store paid the nine people from the flats, and about four people on that flat have said that the paying is the thing and not the clearing.

**It is a Saturday. The weather has been turned for six days and the flats are holding about nine hundred measures of salt under reed mats, and the causeway is four miles long and four feet wide at the top and it took forty days to build, and about four hundred yards of it is lower than the channel it crosses, and everybody who built it knew that.**

---

The water did not come from the Reach. It came from about two days of rain on the low ground at the bottom end of a long channel where the red root grows in the cut bank, which is ground a man of about forty-two took in the seventh week of this flood without asking anybody, and which carries four hundred and forty racks of salt and about nine households and the bottom of a causeway, and about four people in this city have said that the first thing to be said about the thirty-second day of the Long Month is that the settlement's own road flooded first, and that nobody has worked out yet whether that is a punishment or a coincidence, and that a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has said out loud that a thing which punishes the person who built it is not a punishment.

"It took the bottom four hundred yards and about nine measures off the top of a rack."

"Nine measures."

"**Nine measures in the water and about nine hundred and nine on the page. And I want it said in both places and I want the page and the water to be the same number, because if they are different then the page is a thing somebody chose and the water is a thing that happened, and in about four months somebody is going to want the second one.**"

---

**And the clearing of it is a job and not a thought, and it took six hours, and it went wrong twice, and the wrongness of it is the reason this chapter is on the page and not a better one.**

First: about nine people from the store put nine barrows on the causeway at the top end and carried nothing, because the top end is four feet above the low end and the low end is under water, and a barrow cannot be carried into water, and about nine people had to stand at the low end in it and pull, which is the worst work on a causeway and about four people have said that the settlement has never once asked anybody to do it and did not know until the Saturday morning that it was going to have to.

Second, and this is the one that matters: at about the first hour of the afternoon about nine people from the flats came down off the channel without being asked and started pulling, and nobody from the store had asked them, and nobody from the flats had offered, and about nine people in two groups stood about nine feet apart and pulled on the same rope for about two hours and did not discuss it.

**And a man of about fifty-two who digs stood at the low end and said where the silt goes, and it is the same sentence he said on the fourteenth day of the Long Month about a mound, and this time there was no mound, because there is no bank at the bottom of a causeway. It goes in the water. And about nine people stopped, and the man of about fifty-two said a second sentence, and it is in the ordinary course of a day of work and it is nine words.**

"**It goes in the channel and the channel takes it to the racks and the racks have got four hundred and forty of them and nine days' work on them, and if a hundred and forty measures of silt goes down that channel this week then the racks stop draining and the salt on them is gone.**"

Then a man of about forty-two said, in about four seconds, that he would pay for the channel to be dug out, and a woman of about thirty-four in that store said no, and the argument lasted about nine minutes and it was about who owns the bottom four hundred yards of a road, and nobody in that argument threatened anybody and about four people have said that a man of forty-two losing an argument about ground in nine minutes is the second time in nine weeks and both times he lost it correctly.

---

**And then the thing that happened is the reason a Warden exists, and it is on the page, and it is not a scene, and it took about four minutes and it is the fourth line of a cost read out in full on the seventh day of the Long Month and in daylight with a date on it.**

A woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the gate at the bottom of that channel came out of the gateway with a bar in her hand and put herself at the low end of the causeway, and nobody had asked her to, and nobody had thought of asking her, and about four people in this city have said that the whole of what a groundkeeper's workway is for arrived at a gate on a Saturday morning without anybody organising it.

The fourth line, in the words of the person who offered it, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, on the seventh day of the Long Month:

> **And abandoning a ground that has been claimed is a matter of the holder's convenience, and not of anybody else's convenience, and not of the weather, and not of a season.**

"**I have not been sent,**" she said. "**Nobody has sent me. I am here because I took a workway on the seventh day of the Long Month out of a store up the road past this city, and the fourth line of it is that I do not get to walk off ground that has been claimed because the weather has turned, and this is the first week of the twenty-five that I have had a reason to use that sentence and I have not thought about it once and it has used itself.**"

"You are standing on the Reach's ground."

"**I am standing on ground that was taken in the seventh week without asking, and I have known that since the sixteenth day of the Long Month when a man of about thirty-nine walked a causeway and said nine sentences about it, and the reason I am standing on it is that nobody has a second workway and I have the one that says I do not get to walk off. And if I walk off it, then the ground is not held, and then a hundred and forty people are standing in a store nine miles up a road with a road they cannot get out on. And the ground being held is not a favour to me. It is the whole of the road. I have not been thanked and I am not going to be.**"

---

**And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came down the flats on foot and watched about nine people from a store pull a rope with about nine people from the flats, and then he said the only thing he said all day, and it is on the page because it is the finding of the thirty-second day of the Long Month and nobody improved on it.**

"They are being paid."

"**They are being paid out of a store, by the man at the front of the rope, out loud, in front of about nine people, and there is a figure on the end of the line and the payer's name on it and the paid man's off it, and it is not a favour and it is not going to stop because the payer's name is on it. That is a wage.**"

"For pulling a rope on a Saturday."

"**For pulling a rope on a Saturday that somebody else built on ground somebody else took. And about four people on this flat have worked out since the eighth hour this morning that the thing which has not happened in a hundred and eighty-seven days is a wage going the other way, and it went the other way today, and nobody decided it, and nobody has written it down, and I would like it entered that I have not written it down either and I have a slate.**"

---

**And the six hours themselves are on the page because the work is the finding and everything anybody said that day is a thing said about the work.**

Pulling a rope in about a foot of water at the bottom of a causeway is about nine people doing a thing that cannot be done well, and it got done the way things get done in a hundred and eighty-seven days in this world, which is that about four people who have watched it get good at it and about five who have not. The salt silt at the low end is not silt. It is fine and it is heavy and it goes down a boot in about a second and a half and comes up in a cake, and about nine people had no boots and about nine of them had boots they did not want to ruin and about four people ruined a pair and about nine people have not been repaid for two pairs and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf has written down that she will pay for them out of her own hand and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has told her twice that a favour is a thing that stops.

"**Then write it as a wage.**"

"**I cannot. I do not know what these people are paid and I am not going to invent a number and hand it to a person in a yard, and a number I have made up out of my own feeling is a number that a stranger can check, and a stranger nine hundred miles away would check it and it would be correct and I would be on a page.**"

"So it is a favour."

"**Then it is a favour and it stops, and it is stopping in about four days, and I have said so in front of about nine people, and the two pairs of boots are going to be a favour.**"

And a man of about twenty-nine from the settlement's workroom wrote both of those things down in a book of his own that goes nowhere, and the finding of the thirty-second day of the Long Month has four words in it that nobody in that yard said, and the four words are: **and after that it stopped.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening the causeway was clear enough to walk a barrow down and not enough to take a load up, and the store did not send anybody to look at it, and the store did not tell the flats it was clear, and about four people on the flats went up and looked at it themselves at about the eighth hour, in the dark, with a lamp, and about nine measures of salt had gone into the water and about nine hundred and nine measures were on the page and both numbers were right.**

The next load goes up on the thirty-fifth day of the Long Month, which is a Tuesday, and the queue at the store works on a Tuesday, and about nine people in that store have not stopped working and about nine people on those flats have not stopped working either, and a man of about forty-two has entered in a book of his own that goes nowhere that the thirty-second day of the Long Month cost the store about nine measures of salt and about a day and a half of two grounds' worth of labour, and that he will not be writing the other half of that sentence down.

---

---

**And a man of about thirty-two was eleven miles away in this city on the thirty-second day of the Long Month and did not know any of it until about the ninth hour of the evening, and about four people have said that the thing he did not predict was not the water and was not the causeway, and that it is not going to be possible to write in about four months that nobody in this city could have predicted it, and that this is the second time in nine weeks that the correct thing to do was to be wrong on a page.**

**And the last thing on the page is a bar leaning against a gateway at the bottom of a channel, and a rope going into about a foot of water, and about nine people from a store on one end of it and about nine people from the flats on the other, and a figure on the end of a line in a hand that is not a clerk's.**
