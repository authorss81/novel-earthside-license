# Chapter 0177 — Four Legs of Unequal Length

A man of about thirty-two walked eleven miles on the twenty-sixth day of the Long Month with nothing in his hands, and a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a gate let him into a kitchen, and about four hours later a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house asked him a question that he could not answer, and he stayed until the light went, and no document was read out in that room and none was produced.

**It is a Sunday. The kitchen is one room with a fire in it and a bench by the door and a table with four legs of unequal length and a barrow against a wall, and the fire is out because it is midday and because nobody in that house has made a fire since the second month of this flood except on the Thursday night.**

---

He put his hand on the door and opened it, because he had been in a kitchen at the bottom of that channel once, in the sixth week of this flood, and she had not invited him and had not refused him either, and about four people in this city have said that those are two different things and that he had not noticed the difference at the time and had noticed it by the sixth week of the Long Month.

She was at the bench with the board flat on her knees and a knife, and she looked up, and she did not put the board down.

"You have not come about the water."

"**No.**"

"Then what have you come about, and I am asking it plainly and I am not going to be unkind about it, because it has been two days and I have not been unkind to anybody and I am not going to start on a Sunday to be unkind to the last person who comes through that door."

"**I have not got a reason. I have walked eleven miles and I have got nothing with me and I have been walking since the sixth hour and I would like to be in a room and I do not have a reason for it and that is the whole of what I have brought.**"

"**Then sit down, and do not touch the board, and do not ask me whether I am glad.**"

---

**And here is what happened in that kitchen on the twenty-sixth day of the Long Month, and it took about four hours, and it is on the page because it is the only day in a hundred and eighty-one on which a man of thirty-two was in a room with the person whose household gave something away and did not do one single thing about it, and everybody in the room including him understood that by the third hour.**

The first hour was about the boy. The boy of eight was on the floor with about nine feet of rope across his knees and a knife he was not supposed to have, and he was doing the fourth kind of knot, which is the one that is not for tying anything up, and he showed it to a man of about thirty-two at about the seventh hour of the morning without being asked and without explaining it, and the man of thirty-two looked at it for about a minute and said *that is a good one* and nothing else, and about four people in this city have said that a man of thirty-two looked at a knot for a minute and did not make a speech about it, and that it is the only thing he has done in four volumes that anybody has wanted to keep.

The second hour was about the racks. Nine hundred measures, four hundred and forty racks, reed mats and stones along the top, and a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board had got a false roof over about ninety of them out of the store's own lumber and had done it on the Friday night with nine people and had told nobody, and she found out about it on the Saturday and had not said anything, and about four people have said that a man of about thirty-nine walked four miles of causeway and put a roof on ninety racks in a night in weather and told nobody, and that telling nobody was the correct decision and that she is the only person who was told.

The third hour was about the day she chose. She had chosen it and she had not told anybody she was going to and the man of thirty-nine had worked out on the Sunday morning seven days ago that the meeting-ground could not set a day, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had worked out that it had to be somebody who had not been asked about anything, and about four people in this city have now worked out that it was a woman of about thirty-eight on her own ground with a board under her arm.

"You told me on Sunday," he said, "**that I would be somewhere on the fifth day and that you did not want me there, and I have thought about that for about four days and I have got to the end of it and I am going to say the end of it and then I am not going to say it again.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**You did not tell me so that I would stay away. You told me so that you would not have to decide it in a room in front of nine tired people. And you decided it on a road, alone, and you told me afterwards, and that is a thing I have never had done to me and I do not know what to do with it and I have had four days.**"

"**Then do not do anything with it.**"

"**I am going to try.**"

---

**And then, at about the second hour of the afternoon, he got it wrong, and the getting of it wrong is on the page, and it is in the book in his own words, and it is the one thing anybody has said in that kitchen in four hours that anybody improved on afterwards.**

He said he was sorry.

"**I am sorry about it.**"

And she put the knife down on the bench, which she had not done all day, and she looked at him, and about four seconds went by, and about four people in this city have said that those four seconds are the only time in a hundred and eighty-one days that a man of thirty-two has been in a room and not known what to do with his hands.

"**No,**" she said.

"I said it out loud and you did not ask me to and—"

"**No. You are not. Not to me and not in this house and not in front of him,**" and she nodded at the boy of eight, who had not looked up, "**and I will tell you why, and then you can go and be sorry to about nine other people today, because there are about nine of them and every one of them is a person who is owed something and I have watched about four of them watch you say that to me and I have watched what it did to their faces.**"

"I understand."

"**You do not. If you understood it you would not have said it. You have spent a hundred and eighty days giving the only thing you have got away to people, and it is a good thing, and it works, and about four people in this city have told you that it is why anybody listens to you. And I have got nothing to be given.**"

"You have a wage."

"**I have a wage and a workway and nine days of a week and a boy and a board and a gate, and the wage is a day and the workway is a permission and the nine days are the thing I gave away, and there is not one thing in this kitchen that you can give away. You have come eleven miles to be useful at something and there is no use in it, and I have been four days not angry about anything and I am going to be angry again in about a minute and I am telling you now so that you are standing up when it happens.**"

He stood up. He did not say anything else. He did not improve on it and about four people in this city have said that a man of thirty-two standing up when a woman of about thirty-eight told him to is the whole of what the hundred and eighty days bought him.

---

**And then she was not angry for about nine minutes, and the nine minutes are on the page because she said the thing in them, and it is the only time in four hundred miles that anybody has said it about a gate in this flood, and it is said in a mouth, and it may not be said again and may not be said in a page by anybody who was not in that kitchen.**

He was standing by the door with his hat in his hands and she was at the bench with the board and the knife.

"You are going to go and do something about it," she said. "**I know what you are. You are going to go and find the thing you can put in front of a body that is nine hundred miles away and you are going to be right about all four of the reasons there is not one, and you are going to write them down, and it is going to be a good page, and it is not going to come back down this channel in my lifetime.**"

"Yes."

"**Then say one thing to me before you go, and say it properly, because you have not said one thing properly in four hours.**"

"**A gate is not owned. It is not a thing anybody can sell and it is not a thing anybody can take off a household. What a household has is the days on it, and a board, and a keeping of it, and a name in a speaker's record. The gate itself stays where it is. Nobody can take the days. A household has to give them, and my giving them did not take the gate anywhere, and it is going to be there in four months, and the whole of what is different is whose turn it is.**"

"**That is nine years of mine,**" she said, "**and you have had it for about a week, and I am not going to thank you for it, and I am not going to argue with it either, because I have been saying it to about nine people a week since the third day of the Long Month and you are the ninth.**"

---

**And at about the fifth hour of the afternoon a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the house came in from the yard with a bucket and asked him one question, and it is the reason this chapter is on the page and not a better one, and she asked it in four sentences and she did not hurry it and she was not asked.**

"You have not been in a room that wanted you since the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one. That is twenty-seven days. My arithmetic is a day and a day plus one and it is wrong every time and I have had it wrong for nine years about other things and I am telling you it is twenty-seven days and you can look."

"That is correct."

"**I did not say it to be correct. I said it because you have been sitting in my kitchen for four hours with your hat in your hands and about four people in this house have watched you not do one thing, and I am fifty-nine and I keep this house and I have a boy of eight and I am going to say the thing and I am not going to be sorry for it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**You came eleven miles to be in a room and there is not one thing for you to do in it, and that is the correct outcome and I would like it entered that I said so, and I would like it entered that the reason I am saying it is that a woman of thirty-eight has been two days without being asked one question by anybody, including you, and that is a thing she wanted for about nine years until Friday, and that it stopped being a thing she wanted on Friday and nobody has noticed except me and I have been noticing since about the eighth hour this morning.**"

And the woman who keeps the gate said nothing at all to that, and about four people in this city have said that not saying anything was the correct answer and that any answer would have improved on it.

---

**And he left at about the sixth hour of the evening and walked about four hundred yards up off the flats and sat down on the mound of silt that has been there since the fourteenth day of the Long Month, and the last thing on the page is that he wrote nothing down, and about nine people on that flat have said over two days that a man of about thirty-two came eleven miles, sat in a kitchen for four hours, was told to stand up, and wrote nothing down, and that there is no page anywhere in four hundred miles that says that happened, and that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf heard about it from a boy of eight at about the seventh hour of the evening and wrote it in a book of her own that goes nowhere and that she has not read back.**
