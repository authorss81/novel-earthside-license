# Chapter 0712 — A Wharf Yard And A Stack Of Boards And A Board Lying Face Up On The Top Of That Stack With Two Lines Of Grey Grit Across The Figure On It, And A Man Who Could Not Get His Own Hand Under The Near Edge Of It And Took It Off The Far Side Instead

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were under the near end of a length of board and he got the far end of it up onto the top of that stack and set it down square with the boards under it, and then he put his own thumb flat on the near edge of the board that was lying face up on the top of that stack where the wood of it is bare.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats, and the water had come up to the third course of stone of that yard in the night and had gone off it again by that hour and left the boards in that stack wet through at the bottom and drying at the top.

That board lies face up on the top of that stack on every day of this flood with the sun on the face of it and two lines of grey grit run across the figure on it, and there is a clean piece at the near edge of that face where the wood is bare and nothing on it at all. There is no third line of grit on that face and there has not been one. Nobody in this city has ever been shown where the figure on that board came from and nobody in this city is going to be shown, and that board is not turned face down again and cannot be.

He wanted that grit off that face. He put the flat of his own thumb on the bare wood at the near edge of it and rubbed his own thumb along the line of grit and it did not come off, and it did not come off the second time he tried it either, and the grit is grit and not a thing that a thumb takes off a board, and he got his own hand off it and looked at his own thumb and there was nothing on it that had been there before.

---

About four people a day walk past the front of that wharf yard in the ordinary course and not one of them has ever stopped at that stack. That trough stands at that wharf end of that row of flats with the rag lying on the stone lip of the step where it lives, and the bucket is down in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard, and the rag is on that stone lip and is not inside that trough, and nobody at that wharf has ever asked where that bucket went or what it was doing in between.

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came into that yard at about the seventh hour with his own barrow empty and his own knife in his belt and went to the top of that stack to take the board off it that he had come for. He put his own right hand flat on the near edge of that stack to get his fingers under the board that was lying face up on the top of it, and his own hand would not go flat under the near edge of it, because the near edge of that board was up off the boards under it by the length of board that Adrian Vale had set down on that stack at the fourth hour.

He got his own hand out from under it and went round to the far side of that stack and put his own hand in at the far side and worked that board loose and took it off and set it down on the flags at the side of that yard, and he did not turn it over when he set it down and he did not put his own hand on the face of it at any hour of that day.

"**Somebody has got the near edge of that board up off the stack, and I could not get my hand under it so I have taken it off the far side, and I do not know whose hands did that and I am not going to stand here and work out whose they were, and I have a barrow to get back to and the far end of that stack is lower than the near end of it this morning.**"

Nobody in that yard answered him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody told him that a length of board had been set down on that stack at the fourth hour, and nobody at that wharf is going to tell him, and Adrian Vale was down at the low side of that yard out of the wind with his own two hands pushed up his own sleeves, and he did not hear one word of what that man said and was not told any of it.

---

That length of board was still on that stack at the tenth hour with the near end of the face-up board standing up off the boards under it by the thickness of it, and the two lines of grey grit were across the figure on that face where they were at the fourth hour, and there was no third of them and there is not going to be one.
