# Chapter 0721 — A Trestle And A Board Standing Face In With Its Bare Back Out Into That Market Row, Three Sets Of Figures On A Board At The Far End Of It And A Box Of Chalk On A Step With A Lid That Does Not Shut Flat, And About Four People A Day Going Past That Row And Nobody Keeping One Of Them

The man of about thirty-four had the flat of his own right hand on the top of his own trestle and his own left hand hooked over the front edge of his own board, and the bare back of that board stood out into that market row behind his own shoulder, and anybody coming along that row could have put an arm out and touched the back of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and sixth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, at that end of that market, where the thing a person walks along is the line of trestles and boards and not a piece of ground, and where the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building is a different thing altogether and gets no part of that morning, and one word is standing for both of them in this city and everybody standing on either of them knows which one he is standing on.

At the far end of that market row there is one board on the outside wall with three sets of figures on it, and the figures are this city's own, and not one of the three carries a line of words under it. It is not blank. It is stale, and the chalk on it has gone into the wood in places and come out of it in others, and nobody has rubbed anything out of it in this flood and nobody is going to. At the foot of that wall there is a step, and on that step there is a box of chalk with a lid on it, and the lid of that box does not shut flat, and the chalk in that box is not the piece in the man of about thirty-four's own inside breast pocket, and the two of them are not the same chalk and are not kept in the same place.

About four people a day go past that market row in the ordinary course of a day. And of those, about four people get a thing off the thing they are walking past — a hand flat on a board, half a word, the wet smell off a barrow wheel — and about four people do not. Nobody in this city has ever kept that number, it is not the same twice, and there is no surface in this city on which a number like that would be put down.

---

The man of about thirty-four turned his own head and looked along that market row from the near end of it to the far end of it, and he did that three times, and on the third time he put the flat of his own hand on the top of his own trestle and moved that hand along the wood the way a man moves a hand when he is going along a line of things with his own finger. He had got as far as the trestle two along when he found out that nobody standing behind him would have been able to tell afterwards whether he was counting or whether he was doing it out of habit. He took his own hand off the wood and put it back flat where it had been.

At about the fifth hour he said it out loud, and he said it to the middle of that market row and not to anybody in it.

"**Nobody in this row is going to keep a number of who goes past it, and the reason is that whoever kept it would have to stand in it and do nothing else, and I have stood in it three times this morning and I could not tell myself whether I was counting.**"

Nobody in that market row put a hand on that man while he was standing there saying it, and nobody thanked him for it, and nobody improved on one word of it, and the figures on that board at the far end of that row went on being three sets of figures with nothing under any of them.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came along that market row at about the seventh hour with that board under his own arm and the near end of it past his own shoulder, and he came to the front of that stall and stopped for a moment with his own two hands on the wood of that board, and he did not say one word about the bare back of the other board being out in that row.

"**My near end is on the back of yours and I can get round it, and I am telling you it is there because you stood in that market row this morning and said a thing out loud and nobody has said one word to you about that back board, and I have just come along here with a thing in my hands.**"

The man of about thirty-four did not answer him. He got both his own hands under that board and lifted the bare back of it in and set it down square against the inside of his own goods, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not come out of that pocket at any hour of that day.

After that, a man came along that market row at about the eighth hour with a barrow in his own two hands and got his own shoulder round the front of that stall and his own wheel out onto the flags and back in again. He did it in one movement without stopping and without looking at anybody, and the wet on the flags where that wheel went out and came back is the only thing about that front of that stall that anybody can point to at the end of that day.

About four people a day went on past that market row in the ordinary course while all of that was going on, and about four people got a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four people did not, and not one of them was asked one word by anybody at any hour of that morning, and nobody stopped at the front of that stall or at the far end of that market row or at the foot of that wall.

---

The bare back of that board stood in behind his own goods for the rest of that day, and a man with a barrow turned his own shoulder to get past that front at the eighth hour and again at the ninth hour and again at the tenth hour, and the man of about thirty-four stood at his own trestle at the tenth hour with his own two hands flat on the wood of his own boards and did not put the back of it out again.
