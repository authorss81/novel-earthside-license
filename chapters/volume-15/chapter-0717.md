# Chapter 0717 — The Man Of About Twenty-Seven At The Foot Of A Wall He Washed Once In The Whole Of This Flood And Has Not Washed Since, His Own Bucket And His Own Rag And Two Pieces Of Chalk In One Market That Have Never Been In One Hand

The man of about twenty-seven had his own right hand on the handle of his own bucket and his own left hand on the stone lip of the step in front of him where his own rag was lying, and he lifted the rag off that lip, looked at the underside of it, and put it back down on that same lip in the same place it had been lying in, and he did not put that rag to anything at any hour of that day.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, at that end of that market, and that market row is the line of stalls and trestles a person walks along to get to the front of one, and the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building is not that and is a different thing, and nobody in that market ever says which of the two a word like that one stands for.

The wall above the top edge of the board on the outside wall of that store was washed once in the whole of this flood and once only, and he washed it, and it was not a day that is one of the fifty days this book runs. It has not been washed since and he has not washed it since, and nobody has ever asked him in that market why he washed it once and stopped, and he has never said, and he is not going to be asked this morning either.

There are two pieces of chalk in this city and they are not the same chalk. One of them is in a heap at the foot of that store's wall beside a box that stands on a step whose lid does not shut, and nobody has put a hand in that heap in this flood that anybody in this city knows of. The other one is in an inside breast pocket against a man's own chest two stalls along, and it is not out of that pocket, and the two of them have never been in one hand at any hour of this flood and were not in one hand at any hour of that day.

---

He carried his own bucket to the low place at the side of that market and back again twice, and emptied a little water out of it each time and the water went off the flags and into the channel at the side, and he stood at the foot of that wall at about the seventh hour and put his own two hands into his own sleeves and looked up at it from the bottom of it.

"**I washed this wall once and I have not washed it since and I am not going to wash it this morning, and I have got a bucket and a rag and neither of them is any use to that wall, and I have not been asked about it by anybody in this market and I am not asking anybody about it.**"

Nobody in that market row stopped. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him why he washed that wall once and then left it, and he did not say, and the rag was still on the stone lip of that step where he had put it back, and that store's chalk was still in its heap at the foot of that wall with the lid of that box standing open above it and no hand in it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at his own front two stalls along with his own two hands in his own sleeves and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, and he was not thanked for that and he was not asked about it, and nobody in that market has ever asked either of those two men a question about the other one and this morning is not the morning that one is going to be asked.

---

At about the ninth hour the man two stalls along came out from behind his own boards and stopped at the foot of that wall and looked up at it for a while with his own two hands down at his sides. He did not put a hand on that wall and he did not say one word about it and nobody said one word to him about it, and then he went on down that market row to his own front and stood at his own trestle with the board standing face in against it and the bare back of it turned out into that market row.
