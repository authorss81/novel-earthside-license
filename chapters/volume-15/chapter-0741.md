# Chapter 0741 — A Stool Standing At The Side Of A Bench And A Man Who Put The Flat Of His Own Hand On The Top Of It And Turned It About And Sat Down On It And Was Not Told Whose Stool It Was, And A Morning On Which The Wall Along The Far Side Of That Room Said Nothing At Any Hour Of It

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up that stair with that board under his own arm. He put the flat of his own right hand on the top of a stool standing at the side of that bench. He turned it about on the floor of that room with that hand and looked at where it stood after he had turned it, and then he sat down on it.

That morning was the four hundred and twenty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and it was about the fourth hour on it, and about nine people were in that room over that market over the course of it. The wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day.

That stool is the woman of about fifty-two's own. She carried it out of that room on a day that is not one of this flood and set it down at the side of that bench where anybody could sit on it, and nobody has ever thanked her for carrying it and nobody in this city has ever asked her about it, and a man sat down on it this morning and did not know whose it was and nobody in that room told him.

There is a strip of ground at the foot of that stair at the back of that building, and it runs from the bottom step of that stair to wherever the paving gives out. About four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day, and not one of them is ever asked anything about it and not one of them is going to be.

The outside of that door carries a date in chalk, and the wood under that date is bare about as wide as a hand. Nothing whatever is standing on it. That wood means nothing at all, and no name is under that date and none is going to be.

The front door of that building is a door of its own at the front of it, and about four people a day walk on the ground at the foot of that stair at the back of it without going in at it, and one person in that room came in at the front of that building that morning and went up that stair.

---

At about the fifth hour that man of about thirty-nine said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him.

"**I do not know whose stool that is and I am not going to ask anybody in here whose stool that is, and I have sat down on it, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it this morning.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of that and nobody in that room said one word about it after he had said it. He sat on that stool with that board under his own arm and his own left hand on the top edge of it and he did not say anything else at any hour of that morning.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the front of that building at about the seventh hour and went up that stair, and he stood at the near end of that bench and said one thing out loud there, and it was not about that stool and it was not about that door at the back of that building.

"**About nine people have come up that stair this morning and not one of them has come up it with anything in his hands, and I have come up it with nothing in my own hands for the whole of this flood, and there is no reason anybody in this city could be shown for either of those two things.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody argued with him about it and nobody said one word back. That piece of chalk stayed in that pocket against his own chest at every hour of that day and came out of it at no hour of it. He put both his own hands flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and left them there.

---

At about the eighth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up that stair and came into that room and saw that man of about thirty-nine sitting on her own stool with that board under his own arm, and she did not say one word about it, and she went and stood at the far side of that room with her own two hands down at the sides of her own dress, and nobody in that room asked her one word about that stool and nobody thanked her for it and nobody is going to.

At about the tenth hour that man of about thirty-nine got up off that stool, and the flat of his own right hand had left nothing on the top of it where it had been all morning, and he got his own two hands under that board and lifted it off the floor where it had been standing on end against the leg of that bench and put it under his own arm and went down that stair and out into that market. The woman of about fifty-two was still at the far side of that room with her own two hands down at the sides of her own dress, and she did not come near that bench again at any hour of that day.
