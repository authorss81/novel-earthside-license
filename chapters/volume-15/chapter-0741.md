# Chapter 0741 — The Stool Nobody Named

The man of about thirty-nine came up the stair at the back with a board under his arm, crossed to the far end of the bench, and put his right palm down on the top of a stool that stood beside it. He turned the stool about on the floor of that room with that one hand, looked at where it stood after he had turned it, and sat down on it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. Nine people came into the room over the course of the morning. The wall along the far side of it said nothing at any hour of that day, and it has nothing on it, and nobody in this city has ever put anything on it.

The stool is the woman of about fifty-two's own. She carried it out of her room on a day that is not one of this flood and set it down at the side of that bench where anybody could sit on it. Nobody has thanked her for carrying it. Nobody in this city has ever asked her about it, and a man sat down on it this morning without knowing whose it was, and nobody in the room told him.

A strip of ground runs from the bottom step of that stair to wherever the paving gives out. About four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day, and not one of them is ever asked anything about it, and not one of them is going to be. The front of that building has a door of its own, and about four people a day walk past the back of it without going in at the front, and one person came in at the front that morning and went up the stair.

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair there is a date in chalk, and under that date the wood is bare about as wide as a hand. Nothing whatever is standing on it. That wood means nothing at all, and no name is under the date and none is going to be.

---

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-nine said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor.

"**I do not know whose stool that is and I am not going to ask anybody in here whose stool that is, and I have sat down on it, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it this morning.**"

He got no answer and no thanks. He sat there with the board under his arm and his left hand along the top edge of it and said nothing else.

At about the seventh hour the stallholder of about thirty-four came in at the front of the building and went up the stair and stood at the near end of the bench with his hands pushed up his own sleeves.

"**About nine people have come up the stair this morning and not one of them has come up it carrying anything, and I have come up it carrying nothing myself for the whole of this flood, and there is no reason anybody in this city could be shown for either of those two things.**"

Nobody thanked him for that, and nobody took it up. His piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket against his chest and stayed there all morning and did not come out. He set both hands on the near end of the bench and left them there and looked at the wood instead of at anybody.

---

At about the eighth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up the stair and into the room and saw that man of about thirty-nine sitting on her own stool with his board under his arm. She did not say one word about it. She went and stood at the far side of the room with her hands hanging at the sides of her dress and stayed where she was, and nobody asked her about that stool and nobody thanked her for it.

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine got up off the stool. His palm had left nothing on the top of it after a whole morning of sitting. He got both hands under the board, lifted it off the floor where it had been standing on end against the leg of the bench, put it under his arm, and went down the stair and out into that market.

The stool stayed where the weight of a man had turned it, which was not where she had put it. She was still at the far side of the room with her hands at the sides of her dress, and she did not come near the bench again before the light went.
