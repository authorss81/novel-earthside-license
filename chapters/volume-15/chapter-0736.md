# Chapter 0736 — A Room Over A Market On A Tuesday Morning With About Nine People In It And A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Who Says Out Loud At The Fifth Hour In Her Own Words In Front Of All Of Them What It Has Cost The Man Of About Thirty-Four To Be The Man Who Did That, And Nobody In That Room Thanks Her For Saying It And Nothing In That Room Is Done About It At Any Hour Of That Day

The woman of about fifty-two had her right hand on the back of a chair at the side of that bench and her left hand flat on the wood of that bench, and she kept that hand there through the whole of that fifth hour without moving it and without taking it off.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and nothing has ever been put on the wall along the far side of that room and there is nothing on it at any hour of that day.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book had it open on the wood of that bench in front of her with the flats of both hands on the wood on either side of it. The middle column of that page holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, with a day entered against each of the five and none of the five struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and that word is not in her hand and it went there in a hand that is not hers on a day that is not one of this flood, and nobody in this city has ever been told what it is for. Behind that book on the same shelf stands a second slate with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under that day, and it has not been picked up and it has not been turned over and nothing has been written on it at any hour of this flood, and no hand but her own has gone near that shelf at any hour of that day.

Her own stool is still standing against the far wall of that room where she carried it out and set it down on a day that is not one of the fifty days anybody has kept. Nobody has ever thanked her for carrying it out of that room and nobody in this city has ever asked her about it and nobody in that room is going to ask her about it at any hour of that day.

---

At about the fifth hour she said it out loud in her own words, in that room, in front of about nine people, and she said what it had cost the man of about thirty-four to be the man who did that, and she was not saying one word about what it had cost her.

"**You took it off everybody who goes past that door and you took it off everybody who comes up this stair, and you did it in one morning without waiting to find out what it was, and then you came back in here on the mornings after that and did the same thing again without deciding to, and every morning you come back in here you do it again before you sit down, and not one of us is going to tell you to stop, and I am not going to tell you to stop either.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for saying that. Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with her about it and nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put both hands flat on his knees and said nothing at all about any of it.

At about the sixth hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book said one thing out loud at the near end of that bench and it was not about the stool and it was not about her hands.

"**I have been in this room every morning of this flood and I have not been asked one question by anybody in it, and I did not know until about a minute ago that that was a thing anybody had done on purpose.**"

Nobody thanked her for that either and nobody improved on one word of it, and the woman of about fifty-two said nothing back to her about it, and nothing that was said at that bench that morning was done about at any hour of that day.

---

At about the ninth hour that woman of about fifty-two went across that floor to the far wall and took her stool off it with both hands. She set it down at the side of that bench where anybody could sit on it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked across at it and did not sit down on it, and neither did anybody else at any hour of that day, and it was still standing there at the tenth hour where she had put it.
