# Chapter 0710 — A Chair Drawn Out From Under The Shelf Behind That Bench And Put Back Again With Nothing On That Shelf Touched And No Hand But The Keeper's Near It, And A Man In That Room Who Says Out Loud That He Did Not Know What Was Behind It

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both his own hands on the back of a chair and he drew that chair out from under the shelf behind that bench and carried it out into the middle of the floor of that room and stood it down, and that shelf did not move on the wall and nothing on that shelf shifted on it.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in that room over that market, and that room was empty of everybody but two people at that hour and about nine people were in it by the seventh hour. Nobody has ever put anything on the wall along the far side of that room and nothing is on it now, and no page in this city says that either.

On that shelf there is that book. It carries five figures down the middle of one page, with a day entered against each of the five. None of the five is struck through and no sixth has ever been entered. There is one word near the head of the column on the right of that page, and it is not in her hand. Behind that book, on that same shelf, stands the second slate. A day is cut across the head of it and there is nothing whatever under that day. It is not on a shelf of its own, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and nothing was written on it at any hour of that day.

The chair came out from under that shelf at the fourth hour and stood in the middle of the floor of that room from that hour until about the sixth, and the book did not shift on that shelf and the second slate did not shift on that shelf and nothing on that shelf was touched by anybody at any hour of that day except her own two hands flat on the wood of it at the near end while she stood at that bench, and no hand but her own went near that shelf.

He got down on one knee at the back of that shelf and put his own right hand in past the leg of it and felt along the wall behind it. His own fingers went along that wall about the length of two of them and came off the plaster at the end of them and did not come to the other leg of that shelf, and there was nothing back in the way behind that shelf that his own hand could get at, and he took that hand out from under it. He got up off that knee and put his own two hands on the back of that chair where it stood in the middle of the floor of that room, and he stood there looking at the wall above that shelf for a while, and there is nothing on that wall and nothing has ever been put on it by anybody in this flood.

He had wanted to know what was behind the leg of that shelf for longer than he was going to say out loud in that room, and he could not get his own hand in past that leg while that chair was standing under it, which is the whole of why he moved it, and he would not have the shelf off that wall in any case because the shelf is not his and nobody in that room has ever asked him to have it off. He did not know then what he would do with it if he did get his own hand in there, and he has not decided that since, and nobody in that room is going to ask him.

---

At about the fifth hour he named what it had cost him out loud in his own mouth in that room, and about nine people in that room heard every word of it and nobody thanked him for it.

"**I have moved a chair in this room to get at the back of that shelf, and I did not know what was behind that shelf before I moved it and I do not know now, and that is what it has cost me this morning, and I am putting that chair back where it stood.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what was on that shelf. Nobody in that room said one word about what is on that shelf. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book was the only person in that room who knew and she was not asked and she did not say, and about four of the people in that room noticed that there was a shelf behind that bench at all and about four of them did not notice it at all, and not one of them asked anybody about it.

He put that chair back under that shelf at about the sixth hour and pushed it in against that wall with the heel of his own hand, and the shelf did not move on that wall and nothing on that shelf moved on it when the chair went under it. He went and sat down at the near end of that bench, and he put his own two hands flat on the wood of it and said nothing else at any hour of that day.

About nine people went on doing in that room on that morning what about nine people go on doing in that room, and not one of them said the word shelf out loud after about the sixth hour, and at the far end of that bench the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board sat with his own right hand along the top edge of his own board and did not look towards that shelf at all and nobody asked him to.

At the tenth hour about nine people got up off what they were sitting on and went out of that room by the front door of that building and down into that market row. The chair was standing where it had stood at the fourth hour under that shelf against that wall, and nothing on that shelf had been touched at any hour of that day except by the hands of the one person in this city who keeps that book, and the only thing that had happened in that room was that a man had said out loud in front of about nine people that he did not know what was behind that shelf, and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room is going to.
