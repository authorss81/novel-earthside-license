# Chapter 0707 — About Four People Go Past That Door Every Morning And They Are Not The Same Four Twice, And Nobody In This City Has Ever Kept That Number, And A Man With A Bucket Says That Out Loud In A Room

The man of about twenty-seven had both his own hands on the handle of his own bucket and he set that bucket down on the floor of that room at the foot of that bench and stood up straight and put his own two hands up the sleeves of his own coat, and there was water in that bucket and he had carried it up that stair with nothing over the top of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-second day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

About four people a day go past the outside of that door at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of a day, and about four of them get a thing off the thing they are walking past, and about four of them do not. Nobody in this city has ever kept that number, and it is not the same twice, and the ground at the foot of that stair is the only thing this city has ever had for finding out that anybody was ever in that room, and it works by nothing but feet, and it is not a place anybody goes to in order to be counted and it is not going to become one.

---

At about the fifth hour he said the whole of that out loud, and he said what it was first and he said it in that room and not in that market row, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**I have walked past that door at the back of this building every morning of this flood, and I have never once seen the same four go past twice, and nobody is keeping that number, and a number kept would come out different every morning, and it would be a number of people and not a number of this room.**"

About four people in that room said out loud that it was obvious and about four of them said nothing at all. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him how he knew they were not the same four twice, and he did not say, and the four of them who said it was obvious did not say it a second time.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood of it, and he turned about on that bench so that he was facing the middle of that room.

"**I have watched that from the front of my own stall two stalls along and I have not said one word about it in the whole of this flood, and if a number were kept it would be kept by somebody who is not in this room, and nobody has offered.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody argued with him about it and nobody said he was right, and about four of the people in that room did not hear one word of it because they were not near enough to that end of that bench.

The man of about twenty-seven named what it had cost him before he did anything else, and he named it in his own mouth and in that room and in front of about nine people, and nobody thanked him for naming it.

"**I have said that in here this morning, and nobody is going to thank me for it, and nobody outside this room is ever going to know that I said it, and that is what it has cost me, and I am going back down to my own board.**"

---

While that room was saying all of that, about four people went past the outside of that door in the ordinary course. About four of them got a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four of them did not. Not one of them was asked anything by anybody at any hour of that morning. Not one of them stopped at that door, and nobody in that room was looking at the ground at the foot of it while any of it was happening.

He set that bucket down on the flags at the top of the stone by the step where it stands, and he turned it over on its side and let the last of the water run out of it across those flags, and then he set it down again with its mouth up. He put his own two hands flat on the top of that stone and stood there for a while, and he did not look back at the back of that building, and he did not say one word to anybody about what he had said in that room, and nobody in this city is going to thank him for it, and that is a thing this city has never done and is not going to start this morning.
