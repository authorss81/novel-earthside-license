# Chapter 0727 — A Room Over A Market On A Sunday Morning With About Nine People In It And A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine At The Near End Of A Bench Who Says Out Loud Before She Says Anything Else What It Has Cost Her, And Nobody In That Room Thanks Her For Saying It, And A Book That Goes Back On To A Shelf In Front Of A Second Slate That Is Not Touched

The woman of about twenty-nine had the flat of her own two hands on the wood of that bench at the near end of it, one of them on each side of an open book, and her own left thumb was holding a leaf down against the wood and her own right hand was not on anything at all.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at that hour and has nothing on it and never has, and no hand has gone near that wall at any hour of this flood.

That book has three columns. The middle one holds five figures with a day entered against each of the five, in the order they were made in, and none of the five is struck. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and that word is not in her hand and was not put there by her at any hour of this flood. The second slate stands on the shelf behind that book, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever underneath, and it has not been picked up and it has not been turned over and nothing has been written on it, and it is not on a shelf of its own, and no hand but her own has gone near that shelf at any hour of that day.

About nine people in that room had heard a thing said out loud in it at the fifth hour on the day before and not one of them has seen one word of it on a page, and about nine people in that room are in it this morning, and the woman who keeps that book is one of them and is not on anybody's page about it.

---

At about the fifth hour she said it, and she said it before she said anything else, and she said it out loud in her own mouth in that room in front of about nine people.

"**I am in here. Nobody is going to ask me.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for saying it. Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with her about it and nobody said she was right, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood of it and said nothing, and nobody in that room asked him one word about the day before and he was not going to be asked.

The cost of that thing is not hers alone and she has never said so. About four people a day walk on the ground at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of a day, and they are not in that room, and they were not in that room at the fifth hour on the day before either, and of that about four took something off the thing they were walking past that morning and about four did not, and not one of them knows and not one of them is going to.

---

She sat at the near end of that bench with her own two hands on that book for the rest of that morning and she did not open a second leaf of it and she did not write in it. The woman of about fifty-two came into that room at about the seventh hour and stood at the far end of that bench for a while and did not come near the near end of it, and she said one word to nobody and nobody said one word to her, and her own stool was where she had left it against that far wall and she did not move it and did not look at it.

About four people a day went past the outside of that door at the back of that building in the ordinary course of that morning while all of that was going on in that room, and not one of them stopped and not one of them was asked anything and about four of them were carrying something off the thing they were walking past and about four of them were carrying nothing at all.

At about the ninth hour she got her own two hands under that book and lifted it off the wood of that bench and carried it the length of that room to the shelf at the back, and she set it down on that shelf square with the edge of it, in front of that second slate, and the second slate was standing behind it with its day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and her own hands came away from it and she did not touch that slate and no other hand in that room came near that shelf at any hour of that day.

---

She shut that book on that one word at the tenth hour with about nine people still in that room and carried it the length of that floor and set it down on that shelf in front of that second slate, and the one word near the head of the column on the right of that page went back on to that shelf in a book, and it went there in a hand that is not hers.
