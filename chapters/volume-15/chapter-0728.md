# Chapter 0728 — A Room Over A Market On A Monday Morning With About Four People Asking A Man Of About Thirty-Four One Question Each In The Ordinary Course Of That Morning And Not The Room At All, And What He Says He Is Going To Do About It And What It Has Cost Him To Say So, And Nobody In That Room Thanks Him For Either

The man of about thirty-four had the flat of his own two hands on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and his own right thumb was against a knot in that wood and had been pressed into it hard enough to whiten the nail, and about nine people were in that room and four of them were near enough to that end of that bench to have taken all of anything said there.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it, and nothing has ever been put on that wall, and nobody is going to.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at that bench with the flat of her own two hands on the wood on either side of that book, and the second slate stood on the shelf behind that book with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and no hand but hers had gone near that shelf at any hour of that day.

---

The man of about twenty-seven came to that bench first, at about the fifth hour, and stood at the near end of it with his own two hands down at his sides.

"**Did you mean the people who go in at the front of this building as well as the people who go past that door at the back?**"

"**I did not. I meant the people who go past the door at the back and nothing else, and I have not thought about the front of this building at all.**"

About four people in that room were not near enough to that end of that bench to have heard either half of it, and nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it.

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came to that bench at about the sixth hour and put his own two hands flat on the top edge of it and leaned on them.

"**I go in and out at the front of that building most days and I have been trying to work out all morning what I am supposed to do about it, and I cannot work it out, so I am asking you and not the room.**"

"**You are not going to have to do anything about it. There is nothing in what I said that reaches the front of that building, and I know that is what you want told to you and it is what I have got.**"

He put his own hands flat on the wood of that bench again and said nothing else, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf took his own hands off that wood and went to the far end of that bench and stood there.

---

The woman of about fifty-two came to that bench at about the seventh hour and stood at the side of it where there was room for one person and put her own two hands on the back of a chair.

"**Then what is going to happen to the people who come in at the front, and are they going to be asked, and are we all going to find out on the same morning or is somebody going to be told first?**"

He took his own two hands off the wood of that bench and put them flat on his own knees.

"**They will all know that I asked.**"

"**That is not an answer to what I asked.**"

"**I am going to ask one person in this room the thing I have just said nobody outside it may be asked, and I am not telling this room which one of you it is going to be, and you will find out on the morning I ask, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

Nobody improved on the first of those. Nobody thanked him for either of them and nobody argued with him about either of them and nobody in that room said he was right and nobody in that room said he was wrong, and nothing at any hour of that day settled which of those two it was.

---

There was no vote. There were no four days. The room found out what it was being asked on the morning it was asked, and the four days that would have been the cooling are not available to this room, and have not been at any hour of this flood.

The ground at the foot of that stair, which runs from the bottom step to wherever the paving gives out, is a strip of ground that about four people a day walk over in the ordinary course of a day, and of those about four are after a thing off the thing they are walking past and about four are not after anything at all, and the thing that had been said at the fifth hour on the day before had put every one of those about four people out of reach of any question at all, and not one of them was in that room and not one of them knew and not one of them was ever going to be asked what was said in that room or whether anything was.

The man of about thirty-one who carried things came to that bench at about the ninth hour with his own two hands empty and asked the same question in the same words and got no answer at all, and the man of about thirty-four kept his own two hands flat on his own knees and looked at the floor of that room, and the man of about thirty-one went back down that stair without saying one word about it.

---

Nobody in that room thanked him for the seven words and nobody improved on one word of them, and about nine people were in that room at the tenth hour and not one of them knew which of the nine of them it was going to be, and he was not going to tell any of them, and the four days that would have been the cooling were not available to him either.
