# Chapter 0737 — A Wharf Yard And A Barrow And A Board Carried The Length Of That Yard And Set Down On The Top Of A Stack Of Boards, And A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Wanted The Board That Was Lying Face Up On That Stack And Could Not Get His Own Hand Under The Near Edge Of It And Took It Off The Far Side Instead, And No Figure Printed On Any Of It

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were under the near end of a length of board and he got the far end of it up off the flags of that yard and carried it the length of that yard against his own chest and set it down on the top of that stack square with the boards under it, and then he put the flat of his own hand on the top board of that stack and left it there.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats, and the water had come up into that yard in the night and gone off it again and left the boards at the bottom of that stack wet through and the ones at the top of it drying.

There is one barrow of that man of about thirty-eight's own in that yard and his knife is in his belt. A board lies face up on the top of that stack with the sun on the face of it and two lines of grey grit run across it, and there is a clean piece at the near edge of that face where the wood is bare and nothing on it at all, and there is no third line of grit on that face and there has not been one. Nobody in this city has ever been shown where what is on that face came from and nobody in this city is going to be shown, and that board is not turned face down again at any hour and cannot be, and not one figure off that board is printed here and not one figure off the other side of that board is printed here either.

Three barrows went onto a road that is shut out past the last named house and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on that road and no figure anywhere in this city says how far along that road anybody has got. The handcart with the hazel mallet with its head bound in cord and the coil of rope in it is not one of those three and it is not on that road. No barrow in this city has anything on it on any day of this flood.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that wharf came into that yard at about the seventh hour with his barrow empty and his knife in his belt and went to the top of that stack to take off the board that was lying face up on the top of it, and he put the flat of his own right hand on the near edge of that stack to get his fingers under that board, and that hand would not go flat under the near edge of it, because the near edge of that board was standing up off the boards under it by the length of the board Adrian Vale had set down on that stack at the fourth hour.

He got that hand out from under it and went round to the far side of that stack. He put it in at the far side and worked that board loose and took it off. He set it down on the flags at the side of that yard with the face of it up, and he did not turn it over when he set it down.

"**Somebody has put a board on the top of this stack this morning and I could not get my hand under the near edge of that one to take it off, so I have taken it off the far side, and I am not going to stand here and work out whose hands laid it there, and I have got a barrow to get back to and the far end of that stack is lower than the near end of it is this morning.**"

Nobody in that yard answered him. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him that a length of board had been set down on that stack at the fourth hour, and nobody at that wharf is going to tell him, and Adrian Vale was at the low side of that yard out of the wind with both hands pushed up his own sleeves and did not hear one word of what that man said and was not told any of it.

---

That man of about thirty-eight came back into that yard at about the ninth hour with his own barrow and went in at the near end of that stack, and Adrian Vale went in and worked at the far end of it, and those two men worked those two ends of that stack for about an hour with the middle of it between them without one word between them about who had laid any of the boards on it or where any of them had come from, and at the tenth hour they were still at their own two ends of it with boards between them and not one word had been said at any hour of that day.
