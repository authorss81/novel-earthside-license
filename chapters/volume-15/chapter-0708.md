# Chapter 0708 — The Man Of About Thirty-Nine Carrying A Board Under His Own Arm Into That Market Row With Nineteen Rates On It In Three Columns And A Bare Space About Two Fingers Wide At The Foot Of The Middle Of Those Three, And Nobody In That Row Asks Him About It

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own right hand flat along the top edge of that board where it stood against the leg of the bench, and he took that hand off it and put his own left hand flat on the near end of it instead, and the face of that board was to that room and there were nineteen rates on it in three columns and the foot of the middle one of those three was bare for about two fingers wide with nothing at all in it.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-third day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and he has carried that board up that stair and down it again every day of this flood and has never once left it standing anywhere but on that bench and on a trestle in that market row. That row is the row of stalls and boards a person walks along to get to a front, and it is not the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building, and the word is the same for both of them.

He set that board down on the high side of that row at about the fourth hour and stood it against the leg of a trestle there with the face of it to the road, and he put his own two hands flat along the top edge of it and took them off again. He took that board up twice more that morning and set it down in three different places along that row, and every one of those three times the nineteen rates on it were still in three columns and the middle one of them still had nothing at the foot of it for about two fingers wide. Every one of those three times he put his own hand flat over that bare space and took it away again, and he did not put anything in it and he did not rub anything out.

Nineteen rates on that board and not a twentieth, and the space at the foot of the middle of those three columns has had nothing in it for a long while, and nobody in that market has ever asked him about it, and nobody in that room has ever asked him about it, and he has never said one word about it to anybody in this city. He put his own hand flat over that bare space once this morning and took his own hand away again, and the nineteen rates on that board did not move when he took his hand off it and he did not put anything in the space and he did not put anything under it.

---

The man who keeps the stall two stalls along came along that row at about the seventh hour with his own two hands up the sleeves of his own coat and stopped at the near end of that board and stood there. He did not ask. He did not say anything at all for a while. He stood there and looked along the length of that board from the near end of it to the far end of it, and his own eyes came to the foot of the middle of those three columns and stopped there, and he kept them on it for a long while, and he did not say one word about what he was looking at, and the man who owns that board did not say one word about it either.

Nobody in that market row asked him about it. Nobody in that market row has ever asked him about it. The two of them have been at the same end of that market for as long as that board has been at that end of it, and neither one of them has ever asked the other one anything, and that morning one of them looked at a bare space at the foot of a column and the other one knew he was looking at it and neither of them said a word about it.

"**I have been at this end of this row since before the water came and I have watched it fill up and empty out and fill up again, and I could not tell you this morning which of those two things a person is meant to do with the face of a board, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

"**I have not asked you about the foot of that middle one and I am not going to, and I have not asked you about anything else on that board in as long as I have been two stalls along, and I am telling you that rather than leaving you to work it out.**"

Nobody thanked either of them for saying either of those things and nobody improved on one word of either of them and nobody argued with the man who owns that board about a space at the foot of one of his own three columns, and about four people went past that end of that market in the ordinary course of that afternoon and about four of them did not stop, and not one of the two of them was asked anything else for the rest of that day.

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine put his own right hand flat over the bare foot of the middle of those three columns and left it there while the light came off the flags at that end of that market, and the space was under that hand and it is on nobody's page and it is in nobody's memory in that row and it has been asked about by nobody in that row or by anybody else in this city, and the man two stalls along had gone away down that row with his own two hands in his own sleeves and had not asked him.
