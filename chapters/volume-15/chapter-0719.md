# Chapter 0719 — A Channel Of Wet Silt Along The Bottom Of That Second Row Of Flats Standing Where It Was Not Standing On The Day Before, A Length Of Cord Laid Along The New Line Of It, And No Figure For It Anywhere In This City

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were down in the silt at the near end of that channel and he got his own fingers under the edge of the stuff and lifted it and let it go again, twice, to see whether it would hold the shape of his own hand, and it would not hold the shape of it and it closed up over his own fingers and took them back under.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fourth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, along the bottom of that second row of eleven miles of flats, and the water had come up that row in the night and gone off it again and left a channel of wet silt in the low place along the stones where no channel had been standing on the day before.

That channel used to run from the low place at the far end of that row to the low place at the near end of it, along the foot of those sills, and it does not do that any more. It now starts about where the middle of that row of flats is and it runs to the near end of it, and the silt in it stands higher at the near end than the silt at the middle of it, and nobody has measured that and nobody in this city is going to.

A man has walked that row of flats on foot on four mornings in this flood and counted one kind of thing on it, and all four of those counts have been put down where he keeps them, and the four of them do not agree with one another and are not going to be got to agree. Nobody in this city has those four figures and he has not given them to anybody and this flood has not made a fifth morning of it and is not going to.

---

Adrian Vale went back along that row of flats to the near end of it. He put the flat of his own hand on the stones there and felt along them towards the low place. The stones were under the silt at that near end and came out bare at the middle of the row. He took a length of cord out of his own coat and put the near end of it down into the silt at the near end of that channel. He walked it up along the new line of it to the middle of that row and weighed both ends of it down with two loose boards off that stack at the wharf end. He did not take a figure of anything.

A man came along that row of flats at about the eighth hour for a thing of his own that he could not fetch from the near end of it, and he came to where that cord was lying and took hold of it and followed it, because a cord lying along a line in the silt is a thing a man follows in weather, and he followed it to the wrong end of it, and the thing he had come for was not at that end.

He stood at the middle of that row of flats with his own hand on that cord for a while and then let the cord go and stepped off the stones on the other side of that channel and went along that row the other way and got what he had come for at the near end of it, and he did not say one word to anybody about the cord and he did not know whose hands had laid it and nobody on that row of flats was going to tell him.

Adrian Vale was at the far end of that row with his own two hands in his own pockets and had not heard one word of that and was not told, and the cord was still lying along the new line of that channel at the tenth hour with the two loose boards on it where he had left them.

---

That length of cord was lying along the new line of that channel from the near end of it to the low place at the middle of that row of flats where the silt stands highest, and it is still lying there, and there is no figure anywhere in this city for how high that silt stands at the near end of it.
