# Chapter 0748 — A Room Over A Market On A Sunday Morning With About Nine People In It And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Keeps A Stall Going Down That Stair And Out Of The Door At The Foot Of It And A Man Of About Thirty-Nine Who Trades On A Board Following Him Out And One Question Asked At The Bottom Of That Stair Where Nobody In That Room Could Hear It

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both his own hands flat on the front edge of that bench at the near end of it and he took them off it and put them flat on his own knees where he was sitting, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with that board under his own arm and both his own hands flat on the wood of it in front of him.

That was a Sunday in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and it was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month. The wall along the far side of that room said nothing whatever at any hour of that day.

That board under that man's arm carries nineteen rates and he has traded them in four markets for about nine years, and there is one name that has not been on that board for about the whole of that time and nobody in this city has ever asked him one word about it and he has never said one word about it to anybody. At the foot of the stair at the back of that building there is a strip of ground, and it begins at the bottom step of that stair and ends where the paving gives out. About four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day.

---

At about the sixth hour that man of about thirty-four got up off that bench and went out of that room and down that stair and out of the door at the foot of it, and that man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came down after him with that board under his own arm and stood at the bottom of that stair where about four people a day walk on the ground at the foot of it, and the man of about thirty-four asked him one question and he did not ask it to that room.

"**What is a person for, when the only thing anybody is going to use them for is to have gone past.**"

He did not answer it. Nobody in that room heard it and nobody in that market heard it and neither of those two men said one word after it at any hour of that day. He was thanked by nobody for not answering it and nobody argued with him about not answering it.

At about the seventh hour that man of about thirty-four came back up that stair and into that room and sat down at the near end of that bench and said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor and nobody answered him.

"**There is a front two stalls along and about four people came past the front of it this morning without stopping at it, and I have not asked one of them where they were going and I am not going to ask one of them where they were going this morning either, and that is the whole of what I have got to say in here.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it, and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong. His own piece of chalk was inside that pocket against his own chest at every hour of that day and it was not out of it at any hour of it.

---

Nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board one question at any hour of that day. Nobody in that room asked the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book one question at any hour of that day either, and she was at the near end of that bench with both hands flat on the wood on either side of an open book and she said nothing at all about anything said in that room that day, and the question of that morning was not put to her and was not about her.

At about the tenth hour the woman of about fifty-two came into that room and went to that stool standing at the side of that bench and sat down on it and said one word to nobody, and nobody in that room said one word to her about it and nobody asked her one word about where she had been sitting that morning, and she put her own two hands down on her own knees and stayed where she was until the light had gone off that room.
