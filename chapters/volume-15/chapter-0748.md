# Chapter 0748 — What A Person Is For

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both his own hands on the front edge of that bench at the near end of it, and he took them off it and put them on his own knees where he was sitting, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of the bench with that board under his own arm and both his own hands on the wood of it in front of him.

That was a Sunday in that room over that market, and nine people came into it over the course of the morning, and it was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month. The wall along the far side of the room said nothing whatever at any hour of that day.

That board under that man's arm carries nineteen rates and he has traded them in four markets for about nine years, and there is one name that has not been on the board for about the whole of that time. Nobody in this city has ever asked him a word about it and he has never said a word about it to anybody. At the foot of the stair at the back of that building there is a strip of ground, and it begins at the bottom step of that stair and ends where the paving gives out, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-four got up off the bench and went out of the room and down the stair and out of the door at the foot of it, and the man of about thirty-nine came down after him with the board under his own arm and stood at the bottom of the stair where about four people a day walk on the ground at the foot of it. The man of about thirty-four asked him one question and he did not ask it to the room.

"**What is a person for, when the only thing anybody is going to use them for is to have gone past.**"

He did not answer it. Nobody in that room heard it and nobody in that market heard it, and neither of those two men said one word after it at any hour of that day. He was thanked by nobody for not answering it and nobody argued with him about not answering it.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four was back up that stair and into that room, sitting down at the near end of the bench and saying one thing out loud to the middle of the floor, and nobody answered him.

"**There is a front two stalls along and about four people came past the front of it this morning without stopping at it, and I have not asked one of them where they were going and I am not going to ask one of them where they were going this morning either, and that is the whole of what I have got to say in here.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong. His own piece of chalk was inside that pocket against his own chest at every hour of the day and it was not out of it at any hour of it.

---

Nobody in the room asked the man of about thirty-nine one question at any hour of the day. Nobody asked the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book one question at any hour of the day either, and she was at the near end of the bench with both her own hands on the wood on either side of an open book, and she said nothing at all about anything said in the room the day. The question of that morning was not put to her and was not about her.

At about the tenth hour the woman of about fifty-two came into the room and went to that stool standing at the side of the bench and sat down on it and said one word to nobody, and nobody in the room said one word back to her about it, and nobody asked her a word about where she had been sitting the morning. She put her hands down on her own knees and stayed where she was until the light had gone off the room.
