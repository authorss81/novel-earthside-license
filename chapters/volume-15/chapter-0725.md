# Chapter 0725 — A Room Over A Market In Daylight With A Date In Chalk On The Outside Of The Door At The Foot Of The Stair And About Nine People In It, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Decides Out Loud That The People Who Go Past That Door Are Not To Be Asked What Is Said In That Room, And It Is Written Nowhere

The man of about thirty-four had both his own hands flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and the fingers of his own right hand were along the front edge of the wood and had gone over it once and stopped, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her own two hands on the wood of that same bench on either side of an open book.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

There is one door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and the morning light has not come as far as it yet.

The outside of that door carries a date in chalk and the day on it this morning is the four hundred and tenth day of that month in full. The wood under that chalk is bare about as wide as a hand and has been bare the whole of this flood, and nothing whatever is on it, and it has not been widened or narrowed or cleaned or painted or covered, and that date carries no name and no name is going under it.

What the man of about thirty-four was talking about, and what about four people in that room understood him to be talking about, is the ground at the foot of that stair: a strip of it from the step to wherever the paving stops, with about four people a day walking on it in the ordinary course of a day, and about four of them getting a thing off it and about four of them going over it with their hands empty. Nobody in this city has ever kept that number and it is not the same twice.

---

At about the fifth hour he said the cost of it first, and he said it in his own mouth and out loud, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**They are not in here and nobody will ever know.**"

Nobody in that room took that up and nobody said one word back to him about it.

Then he said what it was, and he said that out loud too, and to the middle of the floor of that room and not to anybody in it. The row at the foot of that stair was not a row of stalls, and not a row of flats, and not a row of flags, and not a row of benches, and not a row of ordinary houses, and he got all five of them out at the same speed without stopping between one and the next.

"**A row cannot answer a question and asking it is the only thing that makes it able to. So the people who go past that door are not to be asked what is said in this room, and I am saying that here with about nine of you standing in it.**"

There was no vote. The four days that would have been the cooling are not available, and have not been available to this room for the eighth volume running, and the whole of that thing was decided in the morning it was put.

Nobody improved on one word of what he had said and nobody thanked him for it and nobody argued with him about it, and about four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what it was about, and about four of them said nothing at all. The woman who keeps that book was at the near end of that bench, and the wood on either side of that open page was under her own two hands flat the whole of that morning, and she was not asked one word about any of it and she did not say one.

---

The decision was written nowhere. No notice went out of that building that morning, and no notice went out of that market, and no notice went out of this city, and there is not going to be an eighth one. The oldest instrument there is for a thing of that kind — a thing that arrives and is read out, and the person who reads it out says out loud what it is for before anybody asks — was not used at any hour of that day, and about nine people in that room found out what they were being asked on the morning they were being asked, and there was no morning before it.

About four people walked past the outside of that door on that morning while the room was finding out what it had been asked. Nobody was keeping anything, so there is no figure anywhere for how many of them there were, and there never has been one. Nobody in that room was looking at the ground at the foot of that stair at any hour of that day, and the wood under that date on the outside of that door was not touched by anybody at any hour of it, and nothing was put under that date, and that bare piece of board has meant nothing whatever on every morning of this flood.

---

The man of about thirty-four did not go down that stair at the tenth hour, and he was still at the near end of that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood of it, and about halfway through that morning he had picked his own stool up off the floor at the far side of that room, carried it the length of the floor and sat down on it at the near end of that bench, and nobody in that room said one word to him while he was doing that. The whole of what had changed in that room between the fourth hour and the tenth hour was that a room had said out loud that it was not going to do a thing, and that there had not been going to be a notice about it, and there is not going to be an eighth one, and the morning light had come the length of the floor of that room and was standing at the foot of the one door at the back of it, where at the fourth hour it had not been at all.
