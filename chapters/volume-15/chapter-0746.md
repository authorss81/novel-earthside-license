# Chapter 0746 — A Handcart With A Hazel Mallet With Its Head Bound In Cord In It And A Coil Of Rope That Has Been Used On The Other Side Of It Brought Up Onto The Top Of A Shut Road Out Past The Last Named House Where A Fence Of Sixteen Willow Posts And Eleven Withies Runs Along The Top Of It, And Nobody Going Along That Fence With A Thing To Measure It And No Post In It Moved

Both of the man of about sixty's own hands were on the shafts of his own handcart where the shafts came down off the wheel of it, and he got the near wheel of that cart up onto the stones of that road and set it down on the far side of the near end of that fence with the shafts down, and the hazel mallet with its head bound in cord was lying along the side of that cart under a twist of sacking and the coil of rope was on the other side of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, out past the last named house, where the road is shut and has been shut the whole of this flood, and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing out on it.

That fence along the top of that road is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on to them with the same cord, and there is nothing else in it. Nothing has been added to it and nothing taken off it, and no post in it has been moved since the man of about sixty put it up, and nobody in this city has walked that fence with a thing to measure it and nobody in this city is going to. That coil of rope in that handcart has been used and it is not new, and it is not the length of new rope that is over the back of a chair at the last named house, and nobody lifted that one off that chair at any hour of this flood.

Four of them went out of this city into that party and three of them went down that road with a barrow apiece, and the fourth of those four said no. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing out on that road and nobody in this city is going to be told which way those three went or what they are doing, and there is no barrow in this city with anything on it and there never has been.

---

At about the sixth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up that shut road on foot because there is no other way at that end of that row of houses to get to the far end of it, and she stopped at the near end of that fence and put both her own hands flat on the top of the near post of it and looked along the top of it as far as she could see, and she did not ask the man who put it up one word about why he had put it up.

"**That cord on those withies is one cord and not eleven of them, and I have not counted anything and I am not going to count anything this morning, and there is nobody in this city who has ever been asked to say where that fence stops, so nobody in this city knows, and I am including the man standing at this end of it.**"

Nobody on that road agreed with her about that and nobody on that road argued with her about it and nobody thanked her for saying it. That man of about sixty put both his own hands on the top of the near post of that fence where she had put hers and pushed at it once and let go of it, and he did not say that she was right and he did not say that she was wrong.

At about the eighth hour he got his own two hands back on the shafts of that handcart and did not go along that fence, and he did not take a withy out of his own coat and put one on, and there is a length of new rope over the back of a chair at the last named house and he has not been asked about that either and he is not going to be.

---

At about the tenth hour he took the flat of his own hand off the top of that near post and got both his own hands back on the shafts of that handcart and turned that cart about on the stones of that road and walked it back off the stones onto the verge above that near end of that fence and stood it there with the shafts down and the wheel against a post that is not part of that fence.

At about the tenth hour he got that handcart off that verge with both his own hands on the shafts of it and walked it down off the top of that shut road in front of him, and the coil of rope was in that cart where it had been all morning and that rope has been used, and not one of the sixteen willow posts along the top of that road had been moved that day and not one of the eleven withies bound on to them with the same cord had been taken off.
