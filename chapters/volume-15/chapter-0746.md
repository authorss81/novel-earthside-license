# Chapter 0746 — One Cord And Not Eleven

Both of the man of about sixty's hands were on the shafts of his own handcart where the shafts came down off the wheel of it, and he got the near wheel of that cart up onto the stones of that road and set it down on the far side of the near end of that fence with the shafts down. The hazel mallet with its head bound in cord was lying along the side of that cart under a twist of sacking and the coil of rope was on the other side of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, out past the last named house, on the shut road, and not one person standing out on it can be shown in this city.

That fence along the top of the road is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound onto them with the same cord, and there is nothing else in it. Nothing has been added to it and nothing taken off it, and no post in it has been moved since the man of about sixty put it up, and nobody in this city has walked the fence with a thing to measure it and nobody in this city is going to. That coil of rope in that handcart has been used and it is not new, and it is not the length of new rope that is over the back of a chair at the last named house, and nobody lifted that one off that chair at any hour of this flood.

There were four of them, and three of them went down that road with a barrow apiece, and the fourth said no. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing out on the road and nobody in this city is going to be told which way those three went or what they are doing, and there is no barrow in this city with anything on it and there never has been.

---

At about the sixth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up that shut road on foot, because there is no other way at that end of that row of houses to get to the far end of it, and she stopped at the near end of the fence and set both hands on the top of the near post of it and looked along the top of it as far as she could see.

"**That cord on those withies is one cord and not eleven of them, and I have not counted anything and I am not going to count anything this morning, and there is nobody in this city who has ever been asked to say where the fence stops, so nobody in this city knows, and I am including the man standing at this end of it.**"

Nobody up on the road with her agreed with her about that and nobody argued with her about it. Nobody thanked her for saying it out loud. That man of about sixty put both hands on the top of the near post of the fence where she had put hers and pushed at it once and let go of it, and he did not say that she was right and he did not say that she was wrong.

At about the eighth hour he took both hands back to the shafts of that handcart, and he did not go along the fence, and he did not take a withy out of his own coat and put one on. There is a length of new rope over the back of a chair at the last named house and he has not been asked about that either and he is not going to be.

---

At about the tenth hour he took his hand off the top of that near post and turned that cart about on the stones of the road and walked it back off the stones onto the verge above the near end of the fence, and stood it there with the shafts down and the wheel against a post that is not part of the fence.

Then he got that handcart off that verge with both hands on the shafts of it and walked it down off the top of the shut road in front of him, with the coil of rope in it where it had been all morning and that rope used. Not one of the sixteen willow posts along the top of the road had been moved that day, and not one of the eleven withies bound onto them with the same cord had been taken off.
