# Chapter 0709 — Four Hundred Yards Of Flags Dry At The Fourth Hour, And The Man Who Washes That Board Was On That Row Before Adrian Was And Did Not Use Them

Adrian Vale had both hands on the rim of that trough and he pulled it along the wet flags at that end of that market for the length of one board and set it down on the high side of them. The bucket was in his own right hand all the way along. The rag was over his own shoulder, and it did not go inside that trough at any hour of that day.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and there are four hundred yards of flags at that end of that market and that is a walk and not a measure of anything, and the light had come off them and the water had gone out of the joints of them and the stones of them were dry underfoot in a way they had not been at any hour of the whole of this flood.

He carried that bucket from the low place at the near end of those flats up to the far end of them more times than he counted before the light came good, and every one of those times he filled it out of the standing water in the joints of the stones and walked it up the high side of those flags and put it over the edge at the wall. The last of that water was out of the joints of those flags before the light came off them. He set that trough down where he set it and turned it about so that the long way of it ran along those flags and not across them, and he set that bucket down beside it and put his own two hands on the rim of the trough and left them there for a while.

He wanted those flags dry by the fourth hour and they were. He had been carrying water from the low place at the near end of eleven miles of flats up to that end of that market in a full bucket at a time since before the light had come good, and had put it over the edge of those flags at the wall, and the last of it had gone down before the light came off them.

He set that trough down on the high side of those flags in the middle of the way a person walks to a front, and he set that bucket down on the flags beside it, and he put that rag down on the stone lip of the step at that end of that market and not inside that trough.

---

The man of about twenty-seven came along those flags at about the fourth hour with his own empty bucket hanging in his own right hand, and he was on those flags before Adrian Vale was, and he came to the place where the water has stood in the joints of them every morning of this flood, and there was nothing standing in them, and he stopped.

He stood there and looked at those flags for a while with his own two hands down at his own sides. He did not take his own bucket off his own hand and put it into that trough, and he did not pick that rag up off the stone lip of that step, and he did not put a hand on any part of that end of that market at all. Then he went on down that row with the bucket hanging empty in his own right hand and the rag lying where it was on that stone lip, and he did not look at that trough again and he did not say one word about it to anybody.

The trough and that bucket were standing out in the middle of the way a person walks to a front at that end of that market, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had to go round both of them to get to his own stall two stalls along, and he did not move them and he did not say anything about them until about the sixth hour.

"**There is a trough and a bucket standing out in the middle of the way a person walks at this end of this row, and I have been at this front since before the water came, and a person goes round them or a person does not get to his own front, and I am not going to stand here and say whose they are.**"

Nobody in that market row answered him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him whose they were. Adrian Vale was up that row of flags at the far end of them with his own two hands in his own pockets, out of earshot and behind the noise of that market, and none of it reached him and none of it was carried to him and he never knew any of it had been said.

Those flags were dry at the fourth hour and they were dry at the tenth hour, and about four people a day walk that market in the ordinary course and not one of them counted those flags or asked whose hands had put the water out of the joints of them, and the man who washes that board went on down that row with his own empty bucket in his own right hand and the rag still lying on the stone lip of the step at that end of that market, and he had not used them, and nobody in that market row thanked anybody for anything about it.
