# Chapter 0713 — A Woman Who Keeps A Public Register Trying To Put The Ground At The Foot Of That Stair Into A Book That Has No Place In It For A Thing That Is Not A Figure, And A Column On The Left Of One Page That Has Never Had Anything Put In It

The woman of about twenty-nine had both of her own hands on the spine of that book and she took it down off the shelf behind that bench and turned it round on its spine until the front of it faced her, and the second slate behind it on that same shelf did not shift and nothing on that shelf moved and no hand but her own went near it at any hour of that day.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

That book has three columns. The middle one holds five figures down one page with a day entered against each of the five, and none of the five is struck through, and there is no sixth figure on that page and there has never been one. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it, and that word is not in her hand, and nothing anywhere else on that page carries anything at all. The column on the left of that page has never had anything put in it on any page of that book, and nobody has ever asked her what that column is for, and she has not told them.

The second slate stands on that same shelf behind that book with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It is behind that book and not on a shelf of its own. It was not picked up and it was not turned over and nothing was written on it at any hour of that day.

---

She carried that book the two steps to that bench and set it down on the wood of it and it fell open where it falls open every morning of this flood. She went down those three columns with her own right forefinger on the wood at the side of that page and not on the paper, and she stopped at the column on the left of it and stood there for a while with her own hand on the bench.

The ground at the foot of that stair runs from the step to wherever the paving stops and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day. She wanted to put that on that page. She wanted to put it in the column on the left of that page where there was room for it and nothing in the way, and there was no way to put it there, because every place in that book is a place for a figure and a day entered against it, and a strip of ground with people walking on it is not a figure, and a thing that is not a figure is the thing itself.

She shut that book on the heel of her own hand and opened it again at the same page and looked at the column on the left of it for a while longer and did not put anything in it, and she did not write one word on that page at any hour of that day, and there is no column in that book for a thing that is not a number and a thing that is not a number is a strip of ground at the foot of a stair.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood of it and he leaned over that open page and read down the five figures in the middle of it with a day against each of them and did not say one word about the column on the right of that page or about the column on the left of it.

"**You have been looking at the left of that page a long while this morning and there is nothing in it, and I am not going to ask you what you were trying to put there, and if you want to tell me you can and if you do not I am not going to say one word about it again.**"

She said it to him and not to the middle of that floor, and half of that room could have taken every word of it and half of that room could not, and nobody in the half that could have said one word back to her, and nobody thanked her, and nobody argued with her about it, and nobody in that room said she was right and nobody in that room said she was wrong.

She put that book back on that shelf in front of that second slate with her own two hands and her own hands came off the wood of that shelf, and the column on the left of that page was as bare at the tenth hour as it was at the fourth hour, and no hand but her own had been on that book at any hour of that day, and nobody in that room is going to say one word to her about the left of that page.
