# Chapter 0750 — A Morning In Which A Board On The Outside Wall At One End Of That Market Carries Three Sets Of Figures Of This City's Own And Is Washed Once At The Fourth Hour And Not Twice, And A Wall Above The Top Edge Of That Board That Is Not Washed, And A Trough And A Bucket And A Rag On A Stone Lip And Not In That Trough, And A Book On A Shelf With Five Figures In The Middle Of One Column And One Word Near The Head Of A Page In The Column Beside It And A Second Slate Standing Behind That Book, And A Board At A Wharf Lying Face Up Under Two Lines Of Grey Grit, And A Length Of New Rope Over The Back Of A Chair, And A Fence Of Sixteen Willow Posts And Eleven Withies Along The Top Of A Shut Road, And A Door At The Foot Of That Stair With A Date In Chalk On It And A Bare Piece Of Board About As Wide As A Hand Under That Date, And A Strip Of Ground At The Foot Of That Stair With About Four People A Day's Feet On It

Both of the man of about twenty-seven's own hands were on a wet rag against the face of the board on the outside wall at the far end of that market, and he ran that rag down the face of that board from the top edge of it to the bottom edge of it in one stroke and then he took both his own hands off it and stepped back off the stone and looked at it, and it was washed once and not twice.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in this city, and that board is on its two nails above a step at that end of that market and it carries three sets of figures of this city's own, none of them rubbed out, and not one of the three of them carries a line of words under it, and it is stale and it is not blank, and not one figure on it is printed here or anywhere else.

The trough stands on the flags at the top of the stone by that step and the bucket is down in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard and the rag is on the stone lip of that step and is not inside that trough. There is one of each of those three things in this city and there has been one of each of them for the whole of this flood, and there is a box of chalk on that step with a lid lying down on the rim of it, and that lid does not shut and it did not shut on the day before either. The wall above the top edge of that board is a different thing from that board and was washed once in the whole of this flood and by no second person on any day of it, and that rag stopped at the bottom edge of that board and did not go up the wall above it at any hour of that day.

---

That man of about twenty-seven stood at the bottom of that step with his own two hands hanging at the sides of his own coat and said one thing out loud to the face of that board.

"**I washed that board once this morning and I am not going to wash it a second time this morning, and there is a wall above the top edge of it that I washed once in the whole of this flood and stopped, and nobody in this market has ever asked me why I washed that one once and stopped and nobody is going to.**"

Nobody in that market row thanked him and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with him about it, and the piece of chalk in the man of about thirty-four's own inside breast pocket was against his own chest two stalls along and did not come out of it at any hour of that day.

Two stalls along that man of about thirty-four kept a stall with his own board standing face in against his own trestle and the bare back of it turned out into the row of trestles and boards behind his shoulder, and he said one thing out loud to the middle of that row and nobody in it stopped for it.

"**I have been at that front every morning of this flood and I have not taken that piece of chalk out of that pocket on one of them, and there is nobody in this market row who has ever asked me what I would do with it if I did, and I am not going to start this morning.**"

---

The room over that market is up that stair at the back of the store's end of that row, and along the wall at the far side of it there is a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder with a sheet standing on it, and that sheet is a heading and one place ruled under that heading with a figure in that one place, and that figure is not printed here, and that sheet has never been returned and has never been withdrawn and has not been written in at any hour of this flood and has not been read out at any hour of this flood. Behind that bench there is a second shelf and the register stands on it in front of the second slate, on that same shelf and not on a shelf of its own. Five figures stand down the middle of that page with a day entered against each of them, and none of the five is struck and no sixth has been entered there and none has been taken out of it. Near the head of the column on the right of that page there is one word and nothing anywhere else on that page, and that word is not in the keeper's hand. That second slate stands behind that book on that same shelf and is not on a shelf of its own, and there is a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It has not been picked up and it has not been turned over and nothing has been written on it at any hour of this flood.

Eleven miles of flats go out from that end of that market in both directions, and at the near end of them a salt wharf has a board lying face up on a stack of boards with two lines of grey grit run across it and a clean piece at the near edge where the wood is bare, and there is no third line of grit on that face and there has not been one, and no figure on that face is printed here and no figure on the other side of that board is printed here either. That board is not turned face down again at any hour and cannot be. The man of about thirty-eight who works at that wharf has one barrow of his own standing empty in that yard and his knife is in his belt, and he came into that yard at about the seventh hour and stood at the near end of that stack and did not go up to that board and did not wipe it and did not turn it over.

---

Out past the last named house a fence stands along the top of a shut road, sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound on to them with the same cord, and it has not been measured and no post in it has moved and nobody in this city has gone along it with a thing to measure it. Four of them went out of this city into that party and three of them went down that road with a barrow apiece and the fourth of those four said no, and there is nothing on any of those three barrows and there never has been, and there is no figure anywhere in this city for how far along that road anybody has got. In a house out past that last named house a length of new rope lies over the back of a chair where it has lain every morning of this flood. Nobody has ever used it and nobody has cut it and nobody has taken it up and no hand has ever lifted it off that chair. The man it belongs to was standing in his own doorway at about the ninth hour with his own two hands on his own knees, and nobody asked him where that fence is and nobody told him and he is not going to be.

---

The outside of the door at the foot of that stair carries a date in chalk, and under that date the wood of that door has been bare the whole of this flood, about as wide as a hand, and nothing whatever is on it and nothing has ever been put on it, and there is no name under that date and there is nothing anywhere in this city that says a room over that market ever decided anything at all about that wood, because that wood does not mean anything at all.

And a strip of ground at the foot of that stair has about four people a day's feet on it and nothing on it and nothing said about it.
