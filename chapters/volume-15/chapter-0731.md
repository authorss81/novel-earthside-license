# Chapter 0731 — A Rag On A Stone Lip And A Board On Two Nails Wiped Once And Not Twice With The Flat Of A Wet Rag, A Box Of Chalk On A Step Whose Lid Does Not Shut And Did Not Shut On The Day Before, And A Man Two Stalls Along Going Away Down That Market Row With A Mark On The Flat Of His Own Palm That He Has Not Washed Off And Does Not Know How It Got There

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on that rag where it lay on the stone lip of the step, and he lifted it off that lip, squeezed the water out of it over the trough with both hands and then let the ends of it hang, and put it back down on that same lip in the same place it had been lying in.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and sixteenth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats, and the water had come up that yard in the night and gone off it again and left the stone of that step black underneath.

That trough stands at the wharf end of that second row of flats and that bucket is down in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard, and that rag lies on the stone lip of the step and is not inside that trough. There is one of each of those three things in this city and there has been one of each of them for the whole of this flood. A board on two nails stands on a post above that step with its face out to the weather, and it is not the board on the outside wall at the far end of that market and it is not the wall above the top edge of that board, and it carries nothing at all on its face and nobody has ever put anything on it.

The man of about twenty-seven washed the wall above the top edge of the board on the outside wall at the far end of that market once in the whole of this flood, and once only, and it was not a day that is one of the fifty days anybody has kept since, and it has not been washed since and nobody has washed it since and nobody in this market has ever asked him why he washed it once and stopped. There is a step at the foot of that wall and on that step there is a box of chalk with a lid on it, and the lid of that box does not shut flat, and it did not shut flat on the day before either. There is nobody in this city who has told him it does not shut and nobody in this city has told him it does.

Adrian Vale took that rag off that stone lip again and wiped the face of that board on two nails from the top of it to the bottom of it, once, with the flat of the wet rag, and put his own hand flat on the wood of it and left it there for a moment and then took his hand off, and he did not go over that face a second time with that rag at any hour of that day. The chalk dust came off it onto the flat of his own hand and he put his own hand out over the trough and the water took it.

There are two pieces of chalk in this city and they are not the same chalk. One of them is in that heap in that box on that step and the lid of that box stands open above it. The other one is in an inside breast pocket against a man's own chest two stalls along, and it is not out of that pocket and it does not come out of it at any hour of this day, and the two of them are not in one hand at any hour of this day and were never in one hand at any hour of this flood.

The man of about twenty-seven came along that yard at about the seventh hour with his own bucket in his own right hand and stopped at the step and put his own left hand on the stone lip on either side of that rag and looked at the face of that board on two nails.

"**That board was standing there with the weather on it and I have washed that wall over there once and stopped and I am not going to say a word to anybody about that board, and there is a rag on that lip that I put there and I have put it back twice.**"

Nobody in that yard answered him. Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him about the wall and he did not say one word more about it, and he took his own bucket down to the low place in the stone and let the water out of it and came back and put it down again.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came along that row of trestles and boards to get to the front of his own stall and found that box of chalk in the way of his own path at that step, because that market row is a line of stalls and a man walks along it on one line and that box stands out on the other, and he put the flat of his own right hand on the lower edge of the lid of that box to lean past it and pushed on it once and it gave under his own hand and the lid came up off the step and he got his own shoulder past it and went on with the flat of that hand open in front of him.

He got down that row of trestles and boards to his own front and he had a mark on the flat of that hand. It was not a figure and it was not a word and it was not a number, and it was the white of that chalk out of that box where his own hand had been on the lower edge of that lid, and it was on the flat of his own palm and down the side of it and across the ball of his own thumb, and it was not any shape anybody had put there on purpose.

He looked at the flat of his own hand for a while at the front of his own stall and then he put it down against the front of his own board and left it there, and the mark came off on the board and he turned his own hand over and looked at it again and there was less of it than there had been, and he did not wash that hand at any hour of that day and there is nobody in that market row who saw him do any of it.

---

He went on down that row of trestles and boards with the flat of his own hand open in front of him and the mark still on it, and that box of chalk was standing on that step behind him with its lid standing open above the heap in it, and he has not washed that hand off and nobody in that row knows where it came from.
