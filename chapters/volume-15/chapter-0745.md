# Chapter 0745 — A Morning In Which Nothing Was Decided And Nobody Was Sent For Anything And One Woman Came Round The Front Of That Building To The Ground At The Foot Of That Stair At The Back Of It And Went Up That Stair And Sat Down At The Near End Of That Bench And Opened That Book, And Nobody In That Room Was Told And Nobody In That Room Asked Her How She Came In

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came round the outside of that building at the front of it and along the side of it and up onto the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of it, and then she came up that stair and put both her own hands flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and sat down and opened that book.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of it.

The ground at the foot of that stair is a strip of it, running from the bottom step of that stair to wherever the paving gives out, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day. About four people went past the outside of that door that day and about four of them were her. Nobody in that room was told that and nobody in that room asked her how she came in and nobody in that room asked her one question about that ground or about that door or about that wall. She is thanked by nobody.

The outside of that door at the foot of that stair carries a date in chalk. Below that date the wood of it is bare about as wide as a hand, and nothing whatever is standing on it, and it means nothing at all. It has not been widened and it has not been narrowed and it has not been cleaned and it has not been painted and it has not been covered, and no name is under that date and none is going to be.

That book has three columns on that page. Five figures stand in the middle of them, one under another, with a day entered against each and none of the five struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it. Beside those five there is one word near the head of that column and nothing anywhere else on that page, and it is not in her hand and no mouth in this city defines it. Behind that bench there is a shelf, and a second slate is standing on it with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It has never been picked up and it has never been turned over and nothing has ever been written on it, and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of that day.

---

Nothing in that room was decided on that morning. Nothing was written down anywhere in this city and nothing was read out of anything, and no document was defeated and nobody was rescued and nobody was defeated, and no route was opened and no permission was granted and no boundary was settled and nobody was sent for anything. There is no eighth notice in this flood and there has not been one. A stool stands at the side of that bench where anybody could sit on it and nobody sat down on it at any hour of that morning.

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor. Nobody in that room asked him one question at any hour of that day.

"**I have carried that board under my own arm up that stair four mornings running and about nine people have walked past me on that stair without saying one word to me about what I am carrying, and I am not going to ask any of them about it either.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with him about it, and that board carries nineteen rates and he has traded them in four markets for about nine years and nobody in that room said one word to him about any of that.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the front of that building and went up that stair and put both his own hands flat on the wood of that bench and said one thing out loud that was not about that ground and was not about that door.

"**I have got a front two stalls along and there is nobody at that front this morning who can say where I have gone, and I have told nobody at that front where I have gone, and I am going to be back at it before the light goes and I am not expecting to be asked about that by anybody.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and his own piece of chalk was against his own chest inside that pocket at every hour of that day and was not out of it at any hour of it.

---

At about the tenth hour he got up off that bench and went down that stair and out at the front of that building and along that market to his own front two stalls along, where his own board stands face in against his own trestle and the bare back of it is turned out into the row of trestles and boards behind his shoulder. He is the one person in this city that a room could have sent out to find out what one woman did with her own feet that morning, and he was not sent, and he put his own two hands flat on the face of that board and left them there.
