# Chapter 0745 — She Came Round The Front

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came round the outside of that building at the front of it and along the side of it and up onto the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of it, and then she came up that stair and set both hands on the near end of that bench and sat down and opened that book.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in that room over that market, and nine people came into that room over the course of that morning. Nothing is on the wall along the far side of that room and nothing has ever been on it.

The ground at the foot of the stair is a strip of it, running from the bottom step to wherever the paving gives out, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day. About four people went past the outside of that door that day, and about four of them were her. Nobody in the room was told that. Nobody asked her how she came in, and nobody asked her one word about that ground or about the door or about that wall. She is thanked by nobody.

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair there is a date in chalk, and below that date the wood is bare about as wide as a hand with nothing whatever standing on it. That wood means nothing at all and it has not been widened, narrowed, cleaned, painted or covered, and no name is under that date and none is going to be.

That book has three columns on that page. Five figures stand in the middle of them, one under another, with a day entered against each and none of the five struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it. Beside them there is one word near the head of the column on the right of the page and nothing anywhere else on the page, and it is not in her hand and no mouth in this city defines it. Behind the bench there is a shelf, and a second slate is standing on it with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under the day. It has never been picked up and it has never been turned over and nothing has ever been written on it, and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of the day.

---

Nothing in the room was decided on that morning. Nothing was written down anywhere in this city and nothing was read out of anything. No document was defeated. Nobody was rescued and nobody was defeated. No route was opened, no permission was granted, no boundary was settled, and nobody was sent for anything. There is no eighth notice in this flood and there has not been one. A stool stands at the side of the bench where anybody could sit on it, and nobody sat down on it at any hour of the morning.

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor, and nobody asked him one question about it at any hour of the day.

"**I have carried that board up the stair four mornings running and about nine people have walked past me on it without saying one word to me about what I am carrying, and I am not going to ask any of them about it either.**"

Nobody thanked him for that. Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with it, and the board carries nineteen rates and he has traded them in four markets for about nine years, and nobody in the room said one word to him about any of that.

At about the seventh hour the stallkeeper of about thirty-four came in the way he comes in, went up, and set both hands on the wood of the bench and said one thing out loud that was not about the ground and was not about the door.

"**I have got a front two stalls along and there is nobody at that front this morning who can say where I have gone, and I have told nobody at the front where I have gone, and I am going to be back at it before the light goes and I am not expecting to be asked about that by anybody.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either, and nobody improved on one word of it. He was told he was not right and he was told he was not wrong, and his own piece of chalk was against his chest inside his pocket at every hour of the day and was not out of it at any hour of it.

---

At about the tenth hour he got up off the bench and went down the stair and out at the front of the building and along the market to his own front two stalls along, where his own board stands face in against his own trestle and the bare back of it is turned out into the row of trestles and boards behind his shoulder. He is the one person in this city a room could have sent out to find out what one woman did with her own feet the morning, and he was not sent, and he set both hands on the face of the board and left them there.

She was still at the near end of the bench with the book open in front of her when the light went off the one window of the room, and about four people went past the outside of the door at the back of the building in the whole of the day, and not one of them knew anything about it.
