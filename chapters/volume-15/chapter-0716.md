# Chapter 0716 — A Man In A Room Saying Out Loud That A Person Going Past A Door In The Ordinary Course Is Doing The Thing They Are Doing And Not The Thing A Room Upstairs Is Doing, And That The Only Instrument This City Has Ever Had For It Was Never Once Pointed At That Room

The man of about thirty-four had both of his own hands flat on the wood of that bench on the side of it away from the woman sitting at that end, and he pushed the heel of his own right hand along the wood once and stopped and left it where it was, and the light off the one window of that room had not come as far as the floor of it yet.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and first day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in that room at the back of the store's end of that market, and about nine people were in it, four of them near enough to the near end of that bench to have taken all of anything said at that end and four of them not, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

About four people a day walk past the outside of that door at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of a day. About four of them get a thing off the thing they are walking past and about four of them do not. The ground at the foot of that stair runs from the step to wherever the paving stops and nothing has ever been done to it, and it is the only thing this city has ever had for finding out that anybody was ever in that room, and it works by nothing but feet, and about four people a day walk on it and not one of them has ever been asked what they were doing when they walked past that door.

He took his own two hands off the wood of that bench at about the sixth hour. He put the flat of them along the top edge of it and pushed that edge along the grain a finger's breadth and then drew them back off it. The wood took the mark of his own hands and gave it back inside a minute. Nobody in that room was watching him do that and nobody said one word about it at any hour of the rest of that day.

---

At about the fifth hour he said it out loud, and he said what it was first and he said it in that room and not in that market row, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**Every one of the about four people a day that walks on that ground is doing the thing they came out to do this morning and not one of them is doing a thing I have anything to do with, and they are not standing there for me, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can turn the first of those into the second of those, and the only instrument this city has ever had has been measuring that market and not this room.**"

About four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what it was about and about four of them said nothing at all. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him how he knew that the ground had been measuring the market and not the room, and he did not say, and the four of them who said they did not know it did not say it a second time, and nobody argued with him about any of it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her own two hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page and did not say one word, and nobody asked her a question about that ground at the foot of that stair at any hour of that day, and nobody has ever asked her one.

He named what it had cost him before he did anything else, and he named it in his own mouth and in that room and in front of about nine people, and nobody thanked him for naming it.

"**I have stood at the front of my own stall two stalls along and watched the back of that building every morning of this flood and I have made a thing of those feet the whole time, and I have not asked one of them one question and I never will now, and that is what it has cost me, and about four people a day go on walking over that ground and not one of them was ever mine to ask.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and about four people went past the outside of that door in the ordinary course while that room was saying all of that, and about four of them got a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four of them did not, and not one of them was asked anything by anybody at any hour of that morning.

---

His own two hands stayed flat on the wood of that bench and he said nothing else at any hour of the rest of that day, and what he had said in that room was that the only instrument this city has ever had for finding out that anybody was ever in that room had never once been pointed at that room, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody improved on one word of it, and the woman who keeps that book was in that room and was not asked and did not say, and no page and no board and no stone anywhere in this city has one word of any of it on it and nobody in this city is going to write it down.
