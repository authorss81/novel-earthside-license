# Chapter 0711 — The Front Of That Building And The Door At The Foot Of That Stair, Two Ways Into One Place And About Four People A Day Who Do Not Use One Of Them, And A Figure That Came Out Right And Told Nobody Anything

The right hand of the man of about thirty-four was on the latch of the front door of that building and his own left hand was up the sleeve of his own coat, and he turned that latch and pushed the front of that door in at the shoulder of it and let it come back against the frame and stood on the step with his own hand still on it.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and that building stands at the back of the store's end of that market with a door of its own at the front of it and a stair at the back of it going up to a room over that market, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

There are two ways into that building and there is no third. The front door of it, and the door at the foot of that stair, and both of them come out on the same floor at the bottom of that stair. About four people a day walk past the outside of that second door in the ordinary course of a day, and about four people a day do not use the front of that building at all, and neither of those numbers is the same twice and nobody in this city has ever kept either of them.

The outside of that second door carries a date in chalk and the day on it this morning is the three hundred and ninety-sixth day of that month in full, and the wood under that chalk is bare about as wide as a hand and has been bare the whole of this flood. Nothing whatever is on it. It is not widened and it is not narrowed and it is not cleaned and it is not painted and it is not covered, and no part of this city says what that bare wood means, because it does not mean anything at all.

---

At about the fifth hour he said the whole of that out loud in that room, and he said it to the middle of the floor of it and not to anybody in it, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**I have got a front door and a back door on that building and I have counted them twice this morning and it is two both times, and about four people a day walk past the back of it and about four people a day do not come in at the front of it, and I have been trying all morning to work out what a figure of two is for, and it has not told me one single thing about anybody.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody asked him how he had counted the doors, and he had counted them on his own two hands in the ordinary course of that morning and did not say so, and nobody in that room said out loud that they did not know what it was about and nobody said they did know either, and the thing he had said went no further than the walls of that room and is on no page anywhere in this city.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with her own two hands flat on the wood of it, and she did not say one word about the two doors, and nobody asked her why she comes in at the front of that building and nobody has ever asked her that, and this morning was not the morning.

He went down that stair and out at the front of that building and along that market row to his own front two stalls along and stood at his own trestle with his own two hands in his own sleeves, and the board standing face in against that trestle had the bare back of it turned out into that market row, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not come out of it at any hour of that day.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came along that market at about the seventh hour with his own barrow empty and his own knife in his belt and turned in at the back of that building instead of going on up that market row, and went in at the door at the foot of that stair and stood on the floor at the bottom of it for a while with his own hand on the frame of the door, and nobody came up that stair behind him and nobody went down it in front of him.

He came out of that door again at about the same hour and went back up that market row the way he had come, and the barrow with him was still empty, and there is not a barrow in this city with anything on it.

"**I have been in at that door and out at it again and I did not go up the stair, and there is nobody in this market that has any business knowing that I did it, and I am saying it out loud because I have got a barrow to get back to and the water is still coming down between these buildings.**"

Nobody in that market row stopped. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him which of the two doors he had gone in at, and he did not say, and about four people a day walk past the back of that building and not one of them has ever been asked anything by anybody at any hour of any day.

---

The figure was two at the fifth hour and it was still two at the tenth hour, and it had told nobody in this city one single thing, and the man who went in at the back of that building at the seventh hour while that room was saying all of that and came out at it again is not going to be asked by anybody in that room which of the two he used, and there is nothing in this city that could tell them.
