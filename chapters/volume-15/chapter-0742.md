# Chapter 0742 — A Satchel With A Flap Down On The Floor Behind A Bench In A Room Over A Market And A Man Who Carries Things For A Living In At The Front Of That Building At About The Fifth Hour And Sat Down At The Far End Of That Bench And Did Not Open It And Did Not Say Why, And A Sheet On A Shelf At The Height Of A Person's Shoulder That Is Not Written In And Is Not Read Out

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put the flat of his own left hand on the flap of that satchel where it lay on the floor behind that bench, and he kept that hand there for a while without lifting the flap and without turning it over, and then he took his hand off it and sat down at the far end of that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood in front of him.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day.

There is one thing in that satchel and he knows what that one thing is and nobody in this city has ever been told what it is and he has never once opened anything he has carried in this flood. The flap of it is down and it has been down every hour of that flood and it is not on the bench and it is not in one hand with anything else and nobody in that room has ever asked him what is in it.

A shelf along the wall at the far side of that room stands at the height of a person's shoulder and a sheet is on it and that sheet is the only thing on that shelf. It is a sheet with a heading and one place ruled under that heading and a figure in that one place, and the figure is not printed here. It has stood on that shelf since a day that is not one of this flood. It has never been returned to anybody and it has never been withdrawn, and no hand has ever written in it and no mouth has ever read it out in this city, and nothing in that room has ever been laid beside it and nothing has ever been put in one hand with it and nothing has ever been compared out loud with it in a mouth.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of about fifty-two stood with both hands on the back of a chair at the side of that bench and asked that room, and not anybody in it, whether a sheet could be carried out of that building in a person's own hand. Nobody answered her at that hour.

"**Can a sheet be carried out of this building in a person's own hand, and nobody has ever told me and nobody is going to, and I am not asking you what a sheet is for, I am asking whether a person may walk out of here with one.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for asking it and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody answered her at any hour of that day. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench and said nothing at any hour of it and did not look down at the floor behind that bench.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the front of that building and went up that stair and answered a question that had not been put to him, and nobody thanked him for answering it.

"**Nobody in this room is going to find out what leaves this building today or on any other day, and I am the one who said that in here on a morning that is not one of these fifty, and I am telling you that now as a fact and not as a favour, and there is a page on that shelf at the height of my own shoulder that nobody in this city has ever seen filled in and nobody in this room is going to ask me about it.**"

Nobody improved on one word of that either and nobody argued with him about it. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong. That piece of chalk was in that pocket against his own chest at every hour of that day and it came out of it at no hour of it. He put both his own hands flat on the wood of that bench and left them there and did not go to that shelf.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair with his own bucket in his own hand and went to that shelf and put the flat of his own hand on the front edge of it and looked at that sheet on it and did not touch it, and he went back down that stair with his own bucket in his own hand and nobody in that room said one word to him about what he had looked at.

At about the tenth hour that man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living got his own two hands under that satchel and lifted it off the floor behind that bench and put the strap of it over his own shoulder and went down that stair with the flap of it down. One thing is in there and he knows which thing it is, and he has not said a word to anybody in this city about which thing it is at any hour of this flood, and he is not going to today, and nobody in this city has ever been told, and he did not open it at any hour of that day.
