# Chapter 0742 — Can A Sheet Be Carried Out In A Hand

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put the palm of his left hand on the flap of a satchel lying on the floor behind that bench and kept it there a while without lifting the flap and without turning it over, and then he took the hand off it and sat down at the far end of the bench with both hands on the wood in front of him.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and nine people came into it over that morning. The wall along the far side of the room said nothing at any hour of that day.

There is one thing in that satchel and he knows what it is and nobody in this city has ever been told, and in the whole of this flood he has not opened anything he has carried. The flap has been down every hour of it. The satchel is not on the bench and is not in one hand with anything else, and nobody in the room has ever asked him what is in it.

Along the wall at the far side of the room there is a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder, and it carries one sheet and nothing else. The sheet has a heading and one place ruled under the heading with a figure in that one place, and the figure is not printed here. It has stood on that shelf since a day that is not one of this flood. It has never been handed back to anybody and never withdrawn, and no hand has ever written in it and no mouth has ever read it out in this city, and nothing in the room has ever been laid beside it.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of about fifty-two stood with both hands on the back of a chair at the side of the bench and asked the room, and not anybody in it, a question.

"**Can a sheet be carried out of this building in a person's own hand. Nobody has ever told me and nobody is going to, and I am not asking what a sheet is for. I am asking whether a person may walk out of here with one.**"

Nobody thanked her for asking it, nobody improved on one word of it, and she got no answer at any hour of the day. The man of about thirty-one at the far end of the bench said nothing and did not look down at the floor behind the bench.

At about the seventh hour the stallkeeper of about thirty-four came in at the front of that building, went up past nine people, and answered a question that had not been put to him.

"**Nobody in this room is going to find out what leaves this building today or on any other day. I am the one who said that in here on a morning that is not one of these fifty, and I am telling you now as a fact and not as a favour, and there is a page on the shelf at the height of my own shoulder that nobody in this city has ever seen filled in.**"

Nobody argued with him about that, and nobody in that room told him he was right or told him he was wrong. The chalk stayed in his inside breast pocket against his chest and did not come out. He set both hands flat on the wood of the bench and left them there and did not go over to the shelf.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair with a bucket in his own hand and went to the shelf and set the edge of his hand on the front of it and looked at the sheet standing on it and did not touch it. Then he went back down the stair with the bucket in his hand, and nobody in the room said one word to him about what he had looked at.

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-one got both hands under the satchel and lifted it off the floor behind the bench and put the strap of it over his shoulder and went down the stair with the flap of it down. One thing is in there and he knows which thing it is, and he has not said a word to anybody in this city about which thing it is at any hour of this flood, and he did not open it.
