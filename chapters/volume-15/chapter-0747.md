# Chapter 0747 — What It Has Cost Her

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with that book open in front of her and the flats of both her own hands down on the wood on either side of it, one on each side of the middle column of that page. She came down the front edge of that wood with her own right thumb once and stopped there and did not go any further down it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room over that market, and over the course of that morning nine people came into it. The wall along the far side of that room has nothing on it and said nothing at any hour of that day.

That book has three columns on the page, and five figures stand in the middle of them in the order they were made in, with a day against each of the five and none of the five struck and no sixth entered and none taken out. One word stands near the head of the column on the right of the page and nothing anywhere else stands on the page, and it is not in her hand and it went there in a hand that is not hers on a day that is not one of this flood, and no mouth in this city defines it. Behind the bench there is a shelf, and it carries a second slate that is not on a shelf of its own. A day is cut across the head of that slate and nothing whatever stands under the day, and nobody has picked it up and nobody has turned it over and nothing has been written on it at any hour of this flood, and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of the day.

At the foot of that stair is a strip of ground, and it begins at the bottom step of the stair and ends where the paving gives out. About four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day, and not one of them is in the room and not one of them has ever been asked anything about it.

---

At about the fifth hour she said it out loud in her own mouth in the room in front of nine people, and she said what it had cost her before she said anything else.

"**Nobody in this city has ever asked me one word about that word in that column, and I have carried the book the length of the room and set it down square in front of the slate every morning of this flood, and I have not asked one of you what it is for, and it is not my hand that put it there, and I have not written one word in the book about it at any hour of any morning of this flood.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for having said it out loud, nobody improved on one word of it, and nobody argued with her about it. Nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and about four people a day went past the outside of that door at the back of that building in the ordinary course of that morning, and not one of them was asked anything and not one of them knows.

About the seventh hour the front door of that building was pushed open and the stallholder of about thirty-four came up that stair and stood at the near end of the bench and said one thing out loud, and it was not an answer to her.

"**I have heard what she said and I am not going to answer it and I am not going to tell her that she is right and I am not going to tell her that she is wrong, and I am going to sit down at the near end of this bench.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on one word of it, and she did not look up off the page and she did not say one word back to him about any of it, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket was against his own chest and did not come out at any hour of the day.

---

At about the ninth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up the stair and came into the room and saw that stool standing at the side of the bench where anybody could sit on it, and she did not move it and she did not say one word about anything anybody in the room had said.

At about the tenth hour the woman of about twenty-nine got both her own hands under the book and shut it on that one word, and carried it across that floor and set it down square with the edge of it on the shelf in front of that second slate. It went there in front of a slate with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under the day, and her own two hands came away from it and went down the sides of her own dress, and nothing but those two hands of hers went near that shelf at any hour of that day, and nobody in the room did one single thing about what she had said at the fifth hour.
