# Chapter 0747 — A Room Over A Market On A Saturday Morning With About Nine People In It And The Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Keeps A Public Register Saying Out Loud At The Fifth Hour In Her Own Words In Front Of All Of Them What It Has Cost Her, And Nobody In That Room Thanking Her For Saying It And Nothing In That Room Being Done About It At Any Hour Of That Day

That woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with that book open in front of her, and she had the flats of both her own hands down on the wood of it, one on each side of the middle column of that page. She came down the front edge of that wood with her own right thumb once and stopped there and did not go any further down it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of it.

That book has three columns on that page, and five figures stand in the middle of them in the order they were made in, with a day against each of the five and none of the five struck and no sixth entered and none taken out. One word stands near the head of the column on the right of that page and nothing anywhere else stands on that page, and it is not in her hand and it went there in a hand that is not hers on a day that is not one of this flood, and no mouth in this city defines it. Behind that bench there is a shelf, and it carries a second slate that is not on a shelf of its own. A day is cut across the head of that slate and nothing whatever stands under that day. Nobody has picked it up and nobody has turned it over and nothing has been written on it at any hour of this flood, and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of that day.

What is at the foot of that stair is a strip of ground, and it begins at the bottom step of that stair and ends where the paving gives out. About four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day, and not one of them is in that room and not one of them has ever been asked anything about it.

---

At about the fifth hour she said it out loud in her own mouth in that room in front of about nine people and she said what it had cost her and she said it before she said anything else.

"**Nobody in this city has ever asked me one word about that word in that column, and I have carried that book the length of that room and set it down square in front of that slate every morning of this flood, and I have not asked one of you what it is for, and it is not my hand that put it there, and I have not written one word in that book about it at any hour of any morning of this flood.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for having said it out loud. Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with her about it and nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and about four people a day went past the outside of that door at the back of that building in the ordinary course of that morning and not one of them was asked anything and not one of them knows.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the front of that building and went up that stair and stood at the near end of that bench and said one thing out loud, and it was not an answer to her.

"**I have heard what she said and I am not going to answer it and I am not going to tell her that she is right and I am not going to tell her that she is wrong, and I am going to sit down at the near end of that bench and put my own two hands flat on the wood of it.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on one word of it, and she did not look up off that page and she did not say one word back to him about any of it, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket was against his own chest and did not come out at any hour of that day.

---

At about the ninth hour the woman of about fifty-two came up that stair and came into that room and saw that stool standing at the side of that bench where anybody could sit on it and she did not move it and she did not say one word about anything anybody in that room had said.

At about the tenth hour that woman of about twenty-nine got both her own hands under that book and shut it on that one word, and carried it across that floor and set it down square with the edge of it on that shelf in front of that second slate, and it went there in front of a slate with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under that day, and her own two hands came away from it and went down the sides of her own dress, and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of that day, and nobody in that room did one single thing about what she had said at the fifth hour.
