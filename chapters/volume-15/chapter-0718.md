# Chapter 0718 — Eleven Miles Of Flats In Weather, A Plank Laid Across The Wet Sill Of A Second Row Of Flats And About Four People Who Went Up That Row With What They Were Carrying And Not One Of Whom Came Down It

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were under the near end of that plank and he got the far end of it across and let it lie along the wet sill of that second row of flats, and then he put the flat of his own hand in the middle of it and put his own weight there to see whether it would take a person, and it took his own weight and it took more than his own weight after that.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and third day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in weather, on that second row of eleven miles of flats, and the light had not come good along the top of that row, and there was standing water in the shadow at the low place and nothing on that ground dried from one hour to the next.

That second row is a row of flats with sills along the bottom of it and water coming out of the joints, and it is not the market row at one end of that market and it is not the strip of ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, and one word does duty for all three of them in this city and the people standing on them know which one they are standing on.

The trough stands at the wharf end of that row of flats with the rag lying on the stone lip of the step there, and the bucket is in the water at the low place at the side, and the rag is on that stone lip and is not inside that trough. About four people a day walk past the front of that wharf yard and not one of them has ever stopped at that stack of boards.

---

A man came along that second row of flats at about the seventh hour with a bundle on his back and went up over the top of that plank instead of going round by the low side, and his own boot went through under the far end of it once and the water came in over the top of that boot and stood in it, and he went on up that row with what he was carrying. He felt along the top of that plank with his own hand before he put his own weight on it and found that it did not move, and he did not look back down that row after he had gone over it, and there was nobody standing on that row to say one word to him about whose two hands had laid it there. Another came up that row at about the eighth hour with a bucket in each hand and went up over the top of it and did not come down that row.

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that second row of flats at about the ninth hour with his own barrow empty and his own knife in his belt, and he went over the top of that plank instead of going round by the low side, and he put his own hand flat on the top of it to see whether it moved, and it did not move.

"**I have gone up over the top of that board and I am not coming back down this row of flats on the far side of it, and I have got an empty barrow and a knife and a yard of wharf to be at this evening, and I am not going to stand here and work out whose two hands laid that board down.**"

Nobody on that row of flats answered him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody told him anything about that plank, and nobody on that row of flats is going to tell him, and Adrian Vale was at the far end of that row with his own two hands in his own pockets and did not hear one word of it.

About four people went up that second row of flats that morning with what they were carrying and not one of them came down it, and nobody in this city has been told which way they went and nobody was keeping it, and it is not on a page anywhere.

---

That plank was still down across that wet sill at the tenth hour with its near end on the near stone and its far end on the far one, and the far end of that second row of flats was standing empty in the weather with nobody on it and nobody coming back along it, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf went up over the top of that plank at about the ninth hour and was not coming back down that row that day.
