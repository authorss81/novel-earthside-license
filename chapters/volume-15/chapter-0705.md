# Chapter 0705 — Seven Notices That Stand In Two Finished Volumes And No Eighth Of Them, And A Room That Spends About An Hour Working Out That It Has No Instrument For Asking Anybody Anything In Advance Of Anything

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both his own hands flat on the near edge of that bench and he turned them over and put them palm down on the wood of it and spread them out flat, and about nine people were in that room and four of them were near enough to the far end of that bench to have taken all of anything said at that end and four of them were not.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room over that market, and the light off the one window came in and lay on the floor of it and came up the leg of that bench, and there is nothing whatever on the wall along the far side of that room, and no hand has put anything there at any hour of any day of this flood.

There are seven notices in this flood and every one of them is a sheet with a day on it and a thing to be done on that day, and all seven of them stand in two volumes of this city and the last of them is the last one anybody in this city has written. There is not an eighth of them and there is not going to be an eighth of them and no notice has gone out of this city since that one. There are four days that used to stand between a thing being said and a thing being done, and they are named in that room as an instrument that has been spent, and nobody in this city has traced them and nobody is going to, and they are not available this morning. And that room has no sheet of any shape in it and no form and no place to put a day on and no person to read one out to anybody.

---

At about the fifth hour he said the whole of it out loud, and he named what it was first and he named the cost of it before anybody asked him for either, and about nine people in that room heard every word of it.

"**I have been at this bench about an hour working out how to tell one man in this city one thing before it happens, and there is no way to tell him, and the only thing this city has is a person saying it out loud.**"

About four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what he was talking about and about four of them said nothing at all. Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody told him he was right.

At the far end of that bench the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board standing against the leg of it and his own right hand along the top edge of that board, and he turned about on that bench so that he was facing into that room instead of facing the wall.

"**Those seven notices are sheets with a day on them, and I read one out once in my life and said what it was for before anybody asked, and there is no eighth of them, and this room is not writing one.**"

Nobody argued with that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody in that room said he was right, and the man of about thirty-four put the flat of his own right hand on that bench and got up off it.

"**I am going down that stair and out at that front door to say one thing out loud to one man in that row, and none of you is going to hear what he says back.**"

---

That room went on for that hour with about nine people in it and four of them near enough to that end of that bench to have taken all of anything said there and four of them not. A man at the far end of that bench turned a corner of nothing at all over with the tips of two fingers of his own and put his own hand down again. A woman near the one window put her own two hands up the sleeves of her own coat and took them out again and put them back, and she did it twice. Nobody wrote anything down at any hour of that hour, and the man of about thirty-four did not write anything down either, and nobody at that bench asked him to.

---

He went down that stair and out at the front of that building and up that market row to the front of the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board. That man was standing at his own boards with his own two hands on the top of them and his own bucket standing on the flags at his own feet, and he turned about when he heard the two of them coming along that row and he did not take his own hands off his own boards.

"**I have come out here to say one thing to you in the open because there is no other way to say it, and I am going to say it once and you are not going to repeat it.**"

"**Stand at my own front and say it out loud, and I will hear you, and there is no paper of it in this city, and you knew that when you came out of that door.**"

He came back up that stair at about the seventh hour and sat down at the near end of that bench again and did not say one word about what that man had said back to him in that row, and nobody in that room asked him, and about four of the people in that room did not know he had been out of that front door at all.

That room had spent about an hour working out the shape of a thing that is not there. It has no sheet of any shape and no form and no place to write a day on and no person to read one out and no four days to stand between saying a thing and doing it. Not one of the five of those was written down this morning, and none of them is going to be, and the seven notices that exist do not help, and nobody in that room said any of it out loud twice.

The front door of that building has a step under it, and that step sticks when it is wet, and the weather was wet, and nobody in that room was told and nobody in that room is going to be.
