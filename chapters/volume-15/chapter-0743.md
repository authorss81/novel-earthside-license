# Chapter 0743 — A Bar In The Sockets On The Far Side Of The Door At The Foot Of That Stair And A Length Of Board On A Man's Own Shoulder That Cannot Be Carried Past It Into That Room, And Two Hands Put On That Bar And Worked At It And Did Not Take That Door Off Its Own Two Hinges, And Nobody In This City Asked Him To

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on that bar where it stands in the sockets on the far side of that door, and he got his own fingers up under it and lifted it in those sockets and let it come down again, and he did it that three times, and the bar came down into the same two sockets it came down into before he came to it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, at the back of that building. Four hundred yards of flags lie at one end of that market and there is a board on the outside wall at that end of it carrying three sets of figures of this city's own, none of them rubbed out and not one of the three carrying a line of words under it, and not one figure on that board is printed here and that board is not the length of board on that man's shoulder.

On the outside of that door there is a date in chalk and under that date a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand with nothing whatever standing on it. That wood means nothing at all. It is not widened and it is not narrowed and it is not cleaned and it is not painted and it is not covered, and no name is under that date and no name is going to be.

There is a bar in the sockets on the far side of that door and that door is on its own two hinges, and there is no way of getting that bar out of those sockets from the side a person stands on when they come up that stair without taking that door off those two hinges, and nobody in this city has asked anybody to take that door off its hinges.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that road with a length of board on his own shoulder and his own barrow standing empty in that yard behind him and his own knife in his belt, and he got that board off his shoulder at the bottom of that stair and turned it on its end and brought it up to that door and put the near end of it through the gap that gap is, and it stopped there.

"**There is a bar in that door on the far side of it and I have got a length of board on my shoulder and I cannot get it past that bar and I am not going to stand here in the road working out whose hands are on it. I can take it round to the front of this building or I can leave it against this wall, and I have got a barrow to get back to and there is water coming down between these buildings.**"

Nobody in that road answered him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him what that bar was for, and Adrian Vale was at the low side of that yard out of the wind with both his own hands pushed up his own sleeves and did not hear one word of what that man said and was not told any of it.

At about the seventh hour the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair and went to that door and put his own hand flat on the face of it on the near side and looked at the edge of it where it came into that frame, and he said one thing out loud to the wood in front of him.

"**I can feel that this door is shut on something on the other side of it and I have got nothing in my hands and I am not going to say a word to anybody about what that is.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on one word of it, and he put his own two hands into his own sleeves and went back down that stair.

---

At about the tenth hour that man of about thirty-eight came back to that door and took his own two hands off that length of board where he had stood it against the outside of that wall, and turned it on its end and shouldered it again and went off up that market with it on his own shoulder and his own barrow standing empty behind him.

That bar was in the sockets on the far side of that door at the tenth hour and that door was on its own two hinges and nothing had gone past that door all morning, and Adrian Vale did not take it off its hinges at any hour of that day and nobody in this city asked him to and nobody thanked him for not doing it.
