# Chapter 0702 — The Man Of About Thirty-Four At The Front Of His Own Stall Two Stalls Along, And A Piece Of Chalk In His Own Inside Breast Pocket That Came Out Once And Went Back Unused

The flat of the man of about thirty-four's own right hand was on the top of his own trestle at the front of his own stall and the other one was up the sleeve of his own coat, and the board standing face in against that trestle had the bare back of it turned out into that market row, and it stood that way at the fourth hour of that morning and it has stood that way every hour of this flood.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, at that end of that market, and that row is the row of stalls and trestles and boards a person walks along to get to a front, and it is not the strip of ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building, and the two of them are not one thing, and the word is the same for both of them, and anybody in this city who has heard of one of them has heard of the other and neither of them is a thing you can tell from the word.

The piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket went into that pocket on the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, at a table nobody is going to use. It came out of that pocket once, on the six hundred and thirty-eighth day of this flood, and it went back into that pocket the same afternoon without his having put a mark on anything with it, and it has been in that pocket at every hour of this flood since then, and it is in that pocket at the fourth hour of this morning, and it does not come out of it on this day of this flood either.

The other piece of chalk in this city is a different chalk. It lies in a heap at the foot of that store's wall at that end of that market beside a box on a step whose lid does not shut, and nobody has ever put a hand in that heap in this flood that anybody in this city knows of. The two of those chalks are not the same chalk, and they have never been in one hand at any hour of this flood, and they were not in one hand at any hour of this day, and the man at the front of that stall two stalls along did not know that the other one was there and was not told.

---

The woman at the next trestle along came out from behind her own boards and put both her own hands under the frame of a barrow that stood with its nose in under the trestle behind her, and it would not come, and she tried it twice and it did not come the second time any further than the first.

"**Will you hold the edge of this a minute while I get it out from under the trestle, and then I will not trouble you again this morning.**"

He took his own right hand off the top of his own trestle and put it flat on the frame of that barrow where she pointed, and he held it while she got down on her own knee and pulled at the far end of it and got it clear of the leg of the trestle and swung the back of it out into the open. The water came out of the frame of it in a short run down the flags in front of that trestle. He took his hand off it and put it back on the top of his own trestle.

About four people went past that end of that market in the ordinary course of that morning and about four of them did not stop, and not one of them looked at the two of them at that trestle, and the piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket the whole of it and did not move.

"**That piece of chalk has been in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it came out once, on the six hundred and thirty-eighth, and it went back the same afternoon unused, and nobody in this market row has ever asked me about it.**"

She got her own two hands off the back of that barrow and wiped them down the front of her own apron and looked at the front of his stall for a while before she said the next thing, and she said it to the front of that stall and not to him.

"**You have said that to me and not to that market, and I am not going to carry it up that market, and I have not asked you about it either and I am not going to.**"

Nobody in that row asked him why. Nobody in that row said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody improved on one word of it, and about four people going past that end of that market caught every word of the whole of it and about four of them did not catch one, and nobody stopped, and the man at the front of that stall two stalls along had not been thanked for saying any of it and had not been argued with about any of it.

At about the ninth hour he put both his own hands into his sleeves with the piece of chalk under the left of them where it lives, and he said one more thing out loud to the front of his own stall, and it was the first thing he has said out loud to anybody in the whole of this flood.

"**I do not know what that piece of chalk is for, and I have had it against my own chest since before the water came, and that is the first time I have said that out loud to anybody at all.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either and nobody said it was true and nobody said it was not true, and about four people going past that end of that market heard all of it and about four did not, and the woman at the next trestle along went on with her own boards and did not look up.

She got that barrow out from under that trestle and set the back of it down in that row and wheeled it up the flags away from that front, and he stood at his own trestle with both his own hands in his own sleeves and the board still standing face in against it with the bare back of it turned out into that row, and the piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket where it has been for the whole of this flood, and it stayed there, and nobody in that market asked him about it.
