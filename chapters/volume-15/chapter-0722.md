# Chapter 0722 — A Handcart And A Hazel Mallet With Its Head Bound In Cord And A Fence Of Sixteen Willow Posts Along The Top Of A Shut Road Out Past The Last Named House, And A Man Who Put It Up And Is Not Going To Be Asked Why

The man of about sixty had both his own hands on the shafts of his own handcart where the shafts came down off the wheel of it, and the hazel mallet with its head bound in cord was lying along the side of that cart under a twist of sacking, and a coil of rope was on the other side of it, and neither of those two things is a withy.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventh day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, out past the last named house, where the road is shut and has been shut the whole of this flood, and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on that road.

Along the top of that road there is a fence of sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on to them with the same cord, and that is the whole of what is in it. It has not been added to and it has not been taken from, and no post in it has been moved since the man of about sixty put it up, and nobody in this city has gone along it with a thing to measure it.

Four of them went into that party and three of them are on that road, and the fourth of those four said no. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing out there and nobody in this city is going to be told which way those three went or what they are doing, and there is no figure anywhere in this city for how far along that road anybody has got. Three barrows went onto that road, and no barrow in this city has anything on it at any hour of any day, and the handcart with the mallet and the coil of rope in it is not one of those three and is not on that road.

---

He got that cart up onto the top of that road at about the fifth hour and left it standing on the near side of that fence with the shafts down and the wheel against a post that is not part of the fence, and he went along the near end of that fence with his own two hands at his sides and looked at the withies on it from underneath. He put his own two fingers on the first withy at the near end and worked it a little in the wind and let it go and it went back the way it had been, and he did that to the second one as well, and then he went back to the cart and stood at the near end of that fence.

At about the sixth hour he said it out loud, and there was nobody within earshot of him at that point and he said it to the top of that road anyway.

"**I came out here to look at that fence and I have looked at it, and I am going to take this cart back down that road the way I brought it up, and I am not going to tell anybody what I came out here for because nobody has asked me and there is nobody here to ask.**"

Nobody on that top of that road answered him, because there was nobody on it. Nobody has ever asked him what that fence is for and nobody in this city is going to, and he put his own right hand into the pocket of his own coat where he keeps a spare withy and his own hand came out of that pocket with nothing in it, and he did not say why it had gone in there.

---

Farther back, at the last named house before that road, the man of about fifty-seven was standing in his own doorway with his own two hands on his own knees. He had been standing there at the fourth hour and he was standing there at the sixth hour and he did not go out on that road at any hour of that day, and the length of new rope was over the back of his own chair inside that doorway where anybody standing outside could see it, and no hand of his own and no hand of anybody else's went near it at any hour of that day.

Nobody said one word to that man about where that fence was or who had put it up or what it was for, and he said one word back to nobody, and the man of about sixty came back down that road at about the eighth hour with his own two hands on the shafts of that handcart and passed the front of that doorway without stopping at it.

At the top of that road, before he came down, he had put the flat of his own hand on the top of the near post. That post turned under his own hand about as far as a post will, and he took his own hand off it and did not put it on the second one, and he did not go along that fence to find out how many of them do that, and he did not take a withy out of his own coat and put one on.

---

He took the flat of his own hand off the top of that near post and did not put it on the second one, and he had not gone along that fence at all, and the top of that near post stood there bare and grey in one place where the flat of his own hand had been on it, and there are sixteen of those posts along the top of that shut road and eleven withies, and he got his own two hands back on the shafts of that handcart and took it down.
