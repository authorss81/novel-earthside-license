# Chapter 0715 — The Store's Board On The Outside Wall At One End Of That Market And The Wall Above The Top Edge Of It, Two Surfaces Put Side By Side In Words By About Nine People In A Room And Found Not To Agree About Anything

The man of about twenty-seven had both of his own hands on the handle of his own bucket and he set that bucket down on the flags at the foot of that store's wall and stood up straight and put his own two hands into the sleeves of his own coat, and his own rag was not in them and was lying on the stone lip of the step at that end of that market where it lives.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundredth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and that market row is the line of stalls and trestles and boards a person walks along to get to the front of one, and the strip of ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building is a different thing altogether, and one word does duty for both of them in this city and nobody standing in that market ever says which of the two is meant.

There is one board on the outside wall at that end of that market and it carries three sets of figures of this city's own and none of the three is rubbed out on any day of this flood. It is not blank. It is stale, and the figures on it are the figures it was carrying before the water came and nobody has taken any of them off it and nobody has put any other ones on it. None of those three sets of figures carries a line of words under it, and no figure on that board is printed here, and this city has never printed one of them.

The wall above the top edge of that board was washed once in the whole of this flood, on a day that is not one of the fifty days this book runs, and the man who washed it is the man standing in front of it with a bucket at his feet. No second person has put a hand on that wall on any of those days and no second person is going to, and the water that came off it that one time has dried and gone and nothing has been on that wall since.

---

About nine people were in that room over that market and about nine people were at one end of that market below it, and about nine people in that room put those two surfaces side by side in words, and they did not agree about anything at all.

"**That board has got three lots of figures on it and every one of them says how many of something this city did, and the wall above the top of that board says nothing and has nothing on it and has been washed once and once only, and the two of them are not talking about the same thing and they are not talking about different things either, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about them.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody in that room asked him which of those two surfaces he had put a hand on and he did not say, and about four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what any of it was about and about four said nothing at all.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with the board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and the bare back of it turned out into that market row, and his own board is not the store's board and the board under the arm of the man of about thirty-nine who trades on rates is not the store's board and the board lying face up on the stack of boards at the salt wharf is not the store's board, and a room that puts two of those in one sentence joins them in nothing at all.

"**I have got a board of my own standing against my own trestle and the bare back of it out in that market row, and I have never once put a figure on it or taken one off it, and I have never worked out what I would put on it if I ever did, and I am not going to be asked about it in here this morning.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody argued with him about it and nobody said he was right, and about four people in that room did not hear one word of it because they were not near enough to that end of that bench.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about twenty-seven picked his own bucket up off the flags at the foot of that wall by the handle of it and carried it to the low place at the side of that market and emptied the last of the water in it out across the flags, and the wall above the top edge of that board was not washed by him and was not washed by anybody, and his own rag was still lying on the stone lip of that step and he did not touch it and did not use it.

The man of about twenty-seven is the only person who has put a hand on either of those two surfaces in the whole of this flood, and he put one of them there once and did not put a hand on the other one at all, and nobody in that market row has ever asked him about it and nobody in that market row is going to.
