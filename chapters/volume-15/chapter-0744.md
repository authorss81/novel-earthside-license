# Chapter 0744 — A Length Of New Rope Over The Back Of A Chair At The Far End Of A Bench In A Room Of A Man Who Is Standing In His Own Doorway With His Own Two Hands On His Own Knees And Nobody Asks Him Anything About It, And A Fence Of Sixteen Willow Posts And Eleven Withies Along The Top Of A Shut Road Out Past The Last Named House That Nobody Has Gone Along With A Thing To Measure It

The man of about fifty-seven was standing in his own doorway with the flat of his own two hands on his own knees. Behind him in that room of his own there is a length of new rope over the back of a chair that stands at the far end of a bench. He has not taken it up at any hour of this flood and nobody in this city has ever asked him what it is for.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and twenty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, out past the last named house, where the road is shut and has been shut the whole of this flood and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on it.

That rope is new and it has been over the back of that chair every morning of this flood and no hand of his own and no hand of anybody else's has gone near it at any hour of any of them. It has not been cut and it has not been taken up and it has not been lifted off that chair and it is not going to be at any hour of this day either.

Along the top of that shut road there is a fence, and it is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on to them with the same cord, and that is the whole of what is in it. Nothing has been added to it and nothing taken from it, no post in it has moved, and nobody in this city has gone along that fence with a thing to measure it and nobody in this city is going to.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about sixty came up that shut road with his own handcart behind him and the hazel mallet with its head bound in cord lying along the side of that cart under a twist of sacking and a coil of rope on the other side of it, and that coil of rope has been used and it is not the length of new rope behind that doorway. He went on up that road past that doorway and did not stop at it and did not say one word to the man standing in it.

At about the seventh hour the woman of about fifty-two came up that shut road on foot and stopped in front of that doorway and put her own two hands down at the sides of her own dress and looked past his shoulder at the back of that chair, and she asked him no question at all at any hour of that day.

"**That rope behind you is new. Nobody in this city has ever asked you what it is for and I am not going to ask you what it is for either, and I am only saying that it is new, and I do not know what that makes it and you do not have to tell me.**"

Nobody on that road agreed with her about that and nobody on that road argued with her about it and nobody thanked her for saying it. The man of about fifty-seven looked at her and then looked at his own two hands on his own knees and did not say that it was new and did not say that it was not new, and he did not ask her one question about where she had come up that road from.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about sixty came back down that shut road with his own two hands on the shafts of that handcart and passed the front of that doorway and said nothing to the man in it and he said nothing back.

At about the tenth hour the light had gone off the top of that shut road and the woman of about fifty-two was still standing in front of that doorway with her own two hands at the sides of her own dress, and there was nobody else on that road after that and nobody came up it at any hour of the rest of that day. The man of about fifty-seven was standing in his own doorway at the tenth hour with the flat of his own two hands on his own knees where they had been at the fourth hour, and he had said nothing at any hour of that day and nobody had asked him anything at any hour of it.
