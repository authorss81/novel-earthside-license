# Chapter 0706 — A Plank Laid Across A Wet Sill Of A Second Row Of Flats That Was Where It Was Put At The Fourth Hour, And A Man Who Went Over The Top Of It And Came Back Over It And Did Not Go Over It A Second Time

Adrian Vale had both hands under the near end of that plank and he got the far end of it across and let it lie along the wet sill of the second row of flats, and then he put the flat of his own hand on the middle of it and put his own weight there to see whether it would take a person.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-first day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in weather, on that second row of eleven miles of flats, and the light had not come good along the top of that row of flats, and there was standing water in the shadow at the low place and nothing on that ground dried from one hour to the next.

He wanted that plank to be where he put it at the fourth hour, and it was. It was there at the seventh hour and it was there at the ninth hour and it was there at the tenth hour, and neither end of it had slid off the stone, and the water came through under the far end of it and ran along the top of that sill in two streams and went off the low side of the stone into the channel.

The trough stood at the wharf end of that row of flats with the rag on the stone lip of the step where it lives, and the bucket was down at the near end of that sill under the low place in the stone where the water out of the joints ran, and he had been carrying water from that trough to that bucket all morning, a full bucket at a time, and emptying it into the channel at the side of that row of flats.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came down that second row of flats at about the ninth hour with his own barrow empty and his own knife in his belt and got as far as that wet sill and stopped on the near side of it. He looked at the water standing on the far side of it for a while. Then he went over the top of that plank on his own two feet instead of going round by the low side, and he stood on the far side of it and put his own hand flat on the top of it to see whether it moved, and it did not move. His own boot went through under the far end of it once and the water came in over the top of that boot and stood in it, and he turned about and came back over the top of that plank the same way and stood on the near side of it again with his own hand on the top of it, and he did not go over it a second time and nobody asked him to.

"**I went over the top of that board instead of going round by the low side, and I did not know whose two hands laid it there, and I am not going to stand here and work out whose they were, and I have got a barrow to get back to and the water has got into my boot.**"

Nobody answered him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody told him anything about that plank, and nobody on that row of flats is going to tell him. Adrian Vale was at the far end of that row of flats with his own two hands in his own pockets. He did not hear one word of that. Nobody told him one word of it, and he did not know that any of it had been said.

He came back along that row of flats to that trough at the wharf end and put that bucket down into the trough and let go of it, and it stood there in the water with a little of the top of it out, and he put his own two hands on the rim of it and looked at the standing water in the stone at the low place, and then he took that bucket by the handle and carried it down to his own yard and set it down by the door of it, and he did not touch the rag on the stone lip and he did not use it.

---

The plank was where he had put it at the fourth hour, with the near end of it on the near stone and the far end of it on the far stone and the water coming through under that far end in two streams along the top of that sill. The man who had gone over the top of it was not standing on that sill again, and nobody on that row of flats was waiting at either end of it for anybody, and nobody had ever been told whose two hands laid that plank there or was going to be.
