# Chapter 0733 — A Room Over A Market On A Saturday Morning With About Nine People In It And A Man Of About Thirty-Four At The Near End Of A Bench Who Says Out Loud At The Fifth Hour In About Eight Words In His Own Mouth What It Is To Have Ruled Out The Only People Who Could Have Answered, And Nobody In That Room Thanks Him For Saying It

The man of about thirty-four had the flat of his own two hands on the wood of that bench at the near end of it, one of them flat and the other with its thumb along the front edge of the wood, and he was at that end of that bench at the fourth hour and at the fifth hour and at every hour between them.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and nothing has ever been put on the wall along the far side of that room and nothing is on it now.

The ground at the foot of that stair is the strip of ground this is about, running from the bottom step to wherever the paving gives out, and it is not a row of stalls and not a row of flats and not a row of flags and not a row of benches and not a row of ordinary houses, and it is not a market row either, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day and not one of them is ever asked anything and not one of them is ever going to be.

He said the cost of it first and he said it in his own mouth and out loud and about nine people in that room heard every word of it.

"**I ruled out whoever could have answered it.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for those eight words. Nobody improved on one word of them and nobody argued with him about them and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book was not in that room at any hour of that day and did not hear a word of them.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair and put both hands flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it, on the far side of where those two hands were, and stood there.

"**I have been up that stair on ordinary mornings for the whole of this flood and I have never once been told what any of you decided in this room, and I did not think about that until about a minute ago, and I am not going to make anything of it in front of anybody.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either and nobody improved on one word of it, and the man of about thirty-four took his two hands off the wood of that bench and put them flat on his own knees, and he did not answer him and he did not say that he had not meant what the man of about twenty-seven had said. He got up off that bench and stood at the near end of it with his hands in his sleeves for about the length of a minute. He sat back down on it at the same end of it where he had been sitting all morning, and the wood at that end of that bench was warm under him when he sat down on it the second time.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up that stair with both his hands empty and went to the far end of that bench and stood at it and listened for a while and said nothing at all, and nobody said one word to him about the eight words, and he went back down that stair without asking one thing.

The man of about thirty-four did not put his hands back flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it at the tenth hour, which is where he has put them at the tenth hour on every morning of this flood, and he put both of them into his own sleeves instead and left them there.
