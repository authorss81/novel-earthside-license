# Chapter 0726 — The Second Of The Two Middle Days Of A Fifty Day Run, And The Ground At The Foot Of That Stair Carrying The Same About Four People It Was Carrying The Day Before The Decision Was Taken In It, And The One Place On That Ground That Went Soft At The Edge And Was Not Measured By Anybody

The man of about thirty-four had both his own hands flat on the stone of the top step at the back of that building and then took them off that stone and put them down flat on the paving at the foot of it, and the paving under his own two hands was cold and there was a skin of water on the low place of it about a step to one side of where he had put them.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, at the back of that building, and the light had not come over that roof yet and the whole of that ground was in the shade of the wall above it.

A run of fifty days has two middle days in it and this is the second of them, and neither of the two of them is the middle, and a person who says which of the two of them is the middle is telling this city something that is not so. There is no instrument in this city that can tell one of them from the other, and there is no reason in this city why anybody should want to.

The ground at the foot of that stair, from the step to wherever the paving stops, is a strip of ground and not a place anybody goes to in order to be on it, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day and about four of them get a thing off the thing they are walking past and about four of them do not. On that morning it was carrying the same about four people it had been carrying the day before, and the day before that it had been carrying the same about four people again, and there is no figure anywhere in this city for that, and nobody in this city is keeping one.

---

He went back up that stair at about the fifth hour and stood in that room over that market for a while with about nine people in it, and nobody said one word to him at any hour of that morning, and he said one word to nobody. That was the ordinary course of that room and it was the ordinary course of that morning and nothing anybody in that room owns would have told anybody in it what had been said in it the day before, and that room did not want to be told and did not ask.

The light came over that roof at about the seventh hour and came down the wall above that ground and crossed it from the step outwards, and about four people walked across it in the ordinary course while it was doing that, and about four of them got a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four of them did not, and not one of them looked down at the ground they were walking on, and not one of them knew that a room had said something about that ground at the fifth hour on the day before, and not one of them was going to.

Nobody came along that ground and stopped on it at any hour of that day. A woman went past it at about the sixth hour with the front of her own skirt held up in one hand so as not to put it in the wet on the low place, and a man went past it at about the eighth hour with a barrow and got his own wheel over that wet without slowing down for it. The two of them came in off that market row side by side and went on out of it again a little way further along, and neither of them turned a head towards that stair.

---

The one place on that ground that was different on that morning from the morning before was the place where the man of about thirty-four had put his own two hands flat at the fourth hour. The edge of the paving there had gone soft under the water and had not gone hard again by the tenth hour, and the flat of his own right hand went into it a finger's breadth further than it had gone into it the day before, and he noticed that and took his own hand out of it and wiped it on the side of his own coat.

He did not measure it and he did not tell anybody about it and there is no figure anywhere in this city for how soft that edge of that paving is or how much softer it was on that morning than on the morning before.

---

The place at the foot of that stair where he had put his own hand flat at the fourth hour had gone soft at the edge of it by the tenth hour, and there is no figure anywhere in this city for how soft that edge is or how much softer it was than the morning before, and he wiped his own hand on the side of his own coat and put it flat on the stone of the top step again.
