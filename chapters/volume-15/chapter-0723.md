# Chapter 0723 — A Book With Three Columns Open On A Bench In A Room Over A Market, Five Figures Down The Middle Of One Page And A Day Against Each Of The Five And None Struck, And One Word Near The Head Of The Column On The Right Of That Page In A Hand That Is Not The Hand Of The Woman Who Keeps It, And A Man Who Came Up That Stair To Be Shown That Middle Column And Went Down It Again With His Own Two Hands Empty

The woman of about twenty-nine had the flat of her own two hands on the wood of that bench on either side of an open book, one of them on each side of the middle column of the page in front of her, and the fingers of her own right hand were against the edge of the leaf and were not moving.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in that room over that market at the back of the store's end of that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day, and there is nothing on that wall, and nobody in this flood has ever put anything on it.

That book has three columns. The middle one holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, and a day is entered against each of the five, and none of the five is struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and that word is not in her hand. It went in on a day that is not one of this flood, and nobody in this city has been told what a word in that column is for, and nobody in this city is going to be told it in that room. Behind that book on the same shelf stands a second slate. A day is cut across the head of it and nothing whatever stands under that day. It has not been picked up, it has not been turned over, nothing has been written on it, and it is not on a shelf of its own. No hand but her own has gone near that shelf at any hour of that day.

There are two hands in that book. One of them is hers and is on that page now. The other one put that word in, and it is not hers, and it is not the same hand, and the two of them have both been in that book and only one of them is standing in that room.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that stair at about the seventh hour with his own two hands empty and came to the far end of that bench and stood there and looked down at the open page. He did not put a hand on it and he did not lean on it. He stood there long enough for the light to come off the one window of that room and reach the wood of that bench in front of the book, and he read that middle column from the top of it to the foot of it, and then he read it again from the top of it to the foot of it, and he did not ask anybody in that room one word about it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own two hands flat on that same wood at the near end of that bench and did not take them off it.

"**I have looked at that middle column this morning and there is nothing on it about anybody standing in this room, and there is nothing on it about the man who came up that stair to look at it, and if I had stood at the foot of that stair at the fourth hour every morning of this flood I would not be on that page, and there is nothing in that book that a person is on.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it, and about four people in that room did not hear one word of it because they were not near enough to that end of that bench, and the woman who keeps that book put her own two hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page and said nothing.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf said one thing himself, at about the eighth hour, and he said it to the middle of that floor and not to anybody on that bench.

"**I came up that stair to look at that page and I have looked at it, and there is nothing on it that is about my yard, and I am not going to ask anybody in here to go and find out whether there is, because I would be told the answer in front of all of you.**"

Nobody in that room said one word about that, and nobody thanked him for it, and nobody argued with him about it, and nobody in that room was asked one question about that middle column at any hour of that day.

There are two hands in that book and the second of them is not hers and is not anybody in that room, and the only person in this city who could put a name to it is the person at the near end of that bench, and she was not asked about it at any hour of that morning and she is not going to be asked about it at any hour of this flood, and the thing itself is the only other thing that could say and a book does not say.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf went down that stair at about the ninth hour and out at the back of that building with his own two hands empty, and he had come up that stair for a figure and he had got a page, and nobody in that room asked him one word about it, and that middle column is the only column of that book that anybody in this city has ever been shown.
