# Chapter 0729 — A Tuesday Morning In Which About Nine People Find Out One At A Time That There Is Going To Be A Question Asked Of One Of Them In That Room And Not One Of Them Knows Whether It Is Going To Be Asked Of Them, Carried Up That Stair On Two Feet And By Word Of Mouth And By No Paper Of Any Kind, And About Four People Walk On The Ground At The Foot Of That Stair While It Happens

The man of about thirty-four had both his own hands flat on the sill of the one window of that room with his own two thumbs hooked over the edge of it, and he was looking down through that glass at the ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building, and the glass was still grey with the morning behind it and there was nothing on that ground at that hour that a person could have named.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in that room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day, has nothing on it, and has had nothing put on it by anybody in this flood.

Here is the whole of the instrument this city has for it. A man said a thing out loud at the front of his own stall two stalls along at about the fourth hour. A woman standing at a trestle along heard it and said it, in her own words, in her own mouth, to a man at a trestle two along. That man carried it up the stair at the back of that building and said it in that room, badly, and got the sense of it and very little else. There is no paper involved. Nothing was written down and nothing was sent and nobody was told to tell anybody, and the four days that would have been the cooling are not available to this room, and there is nothing in this city that would put them there.

---

The man of about thirty-four came off that sill at about the fifth hour and told the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, in two words, standing up, and he did not ask him anything and the man of about thirty-nine did not ask him anything either, and both of them went on standing where they were.

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf found out, and he found out from a woman at the far end of that bench who told him one sentence and then sat down, and he put his own two hands flat on the wood of that bench and worked out for a while whether it was going to be him and then did not say that out loud to anybody.

At about the seventh hour the woman of about fifty-two found out from the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf, who told her the one sentence he had been given and did not add anything to it, and she stood at the back of that chair with her own two hands on it and did not say whether it was going to be her.

Nobody in that room asked anybody anything at any hour of that morning, and that includes the man of about thirty-four, who was asked nothing by anybody, and the finding out went on at the ordinary rate of a morning in which nobody is going to be told anything before the hour it is said.

---

About four people walked on the ground at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of that morning while the whole of it was being said in the room over their heads, and about four of them were getting a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four of them were not, and not one of them knew that there was going to be a question asked of one person in that room, and not one of them was ever going to know that there was.

The man of about twenty-seven came up that stair at about the ninth hour with his own two hands in his own sleeves and nothing in them, and the man of about thirty-four told him the same two words, and the man of about twenty-seven stood at the far end of that bench and put his own two hands flat on the wood of it and did not ask one word and did not say one word, and then he went back down that stair.

---

About nine people in that room had that thing in their heads at the tenth hour and not one of them had been told one word of it before the fourth hour, and the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair and went down it again and he was the last of them, and nobody in that room said one word to him.
