# Chapter 0714 — The Bench Beside A Satchel With A Flap Down Wiped With The Flat Of A Sleeve Where Nothing Had Been On It, And A Man Who Watched That Arm Go Past It And Said Nothing At The Time And Said Nothing Afterwards Either

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were flat on the wood of that bench on the side of it away from the man sitting at the far end of it, and he took them off and turned the flat of his own right sleeve over onto the wood and rubbed it along the length of the bench and back again, and there was nothing on that wood and there had been nothing on that wood all morning.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and ninety-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room at the back of the store's end of that market, and about nine people were in it. Nothing has ever been put on the wall along the far side of that room and nothing is on it now and no page in this city says otherwise.

Behind that bench there is a satchel with a flap, and it belongs to the man sitting at the far end of that bench and it is the man's own and the flap of it is down and it has been down every hour of this flood. He has never once opened anything he has carried in this flood and he is not going to open this on this day.

That shelf behind that bench carries the register in front of it and the second slate behind that book, on that same shelf, and it carries nothing else. The middle column of that register holds five figures with a day entered against each of the five and none of the five is struck and no sixth has ever been entered, and the column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it in a hand that is not the keeper's. Nobody but the keeper's own hand went near that shelf at any hour of that day and the second slate was not picked up and was not turned over and nothing was written on it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in at the front of that building at about the fifth hour and went up that stair and sat down at the far end of that bench, and he put his own two hands flat on the wood of it where Adrian Vale had been, and the wood was dry under his own hands and dry under the sleeve of his own coat, and there was nothing on that bench to wipe and nobody in that room was going to be told about a man wiping a bench that had nothing on it.

"**That part of that bench is dry and I did not know it was dry, and I am not going to ask anybody in here why it is dry, and I have sat down on it and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody in that room asked him why he came in at the front of that building and nobody has ever asked him, and the man at the near end of that bench took his own two hands off the wood and put them in his own lap and looked at the middle of that floor.

---

The man at the far end of that bench had the satchel on the floor behind that bench and had his own right hand on his own knee, and he had been watching the whole of the flat of that sleeve go along that wood and back again from the time it started, and he had not said one word while it was happening. Adrian Vale got the satchel shifted with his own forearm on the way past it, past the flap and not touching it, and the flap came up about as far as it always lies and went back down where it was.

"**He has had his arm along that bench twice this morning and once past that flap and he has not touched it, and I am not going to say one word to him about that and I am not going to say one word to anybody else in this room about that either, and that is all I have got to say this morning.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody thanked him for not saying anything else. Nobody in that room asked Adrian Vale what was in that satchel and nobody has ever asked him, and he is thanked by nobody in that room for not opening it, and the flap of it was down at the tenth hour exactly where it had lain at the fourth hour, and the bench beside it was dry where it had been wiped, and the man at the far end of that bench had said one word about it and was not going to say a second.
