# Chapter 0701 — A Room Over A Market At The Fourth Hour With About Nine People In It, And A Man Who Says Out Loud That Nobody In This City Has Ever Asked One Of The About Four What They Were Doing When They Walked Past That Door

The flat of the man of about thirty-four's own two hands was on the near edge of that bench on the side of it away from the woman sitting at that end, and he took them off it and put them up the sleeves of his own coat and left them there, and the light off the one window of that room had not come as far as the floor of it yet.

It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and eighty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in that room at the back of the store's end of that market, and about nine people were in it, four of them near enough to the far end of that bench to have taken all of anything said at that end and four of them not. The wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day and has nothing on it and never has had anything put on it by anybody in this flood.

The outside of that door at the foot of that stair carries a date in chalk, and the day on it this morning is the three hundred and eighty-sixth day of that month in full, and the wood under that chalk is bare about as wide as a hand and has been bare the whole of this flood. Nothing whatever is on it. Nothing has ever been put on it. It is not widened and it is not narrowed and it is not cleaned and it is not painted and it is not covered. Nothing on that door says a room over that market ever decided anything at all about that wood. Nothing anywhere else in this city says so either. There is no person in this city that anybody could be shown who learned anything in that room.

That book stands on the shelf behind that bench with five figures down the middle of one page and a day entered against each of the five and none of the five struck through and no sixth entered. Near the head of the column on the right of that page there is one word and it is not in her hand. The second slate stands on the same shelf behind that book with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it has not been picked up and it has not been turned over and nothing has been written on it, and it is not on a shelf of its own.

About four people a day go past the outside of that door in the ordinary course of a day. About four of them get a thing off the thing they are walking past — the flap of a sack, a tie, half a word out of somebody's mouth — and about four of them do not. Nobody in this city has ever kept the number and it is not the same twice. The ground at the foot of that stair runs from the step to wherever the paving stops and nothing has ever been done to it, and it is the only thing this city has ever had for finding out that anybody was ever in that room, and it works by nothing but feet.

---

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-four said it out loud in that room, and he said it to the middle of the floor of it and not to anybody in it.

"**I have stood at the front of my own stall two stalls along with about four people a day going past the back of that building, and not one of them has ever been asked what they were doing when they walked past that door, and neither have I, and I am saying so this morning because it has not been said in this room.**"

About four people in that room said out loud that they did not know it and about four of them said nothing at all. Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him how he knew it, and he did not say, and the four of them who said they did not know it did not say it twice.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her own two hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page and said what it had cost her, and she said it first and out loud and in her own mouth, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**I have been at this bench every morning of this flood and not one person in this city has ever asked me one question about anything, and I am saying that out loud this morning as well, and nobody in this room is going to thank me for it.**"

Nobody thanked her for that either and nobody argued with her about it and nobody said she was right, and the man of about thirty-four put his own two hands back flat on the near edge of that bench when she had got to the end of it and did not say one word about what she had said. She did not write one word of it down. There was nothing on that shelf to write it on and no hand but her own went near that shelf at any hour of that morning.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with that board standing against the leg of it and his own right hand flat along the top edge of it, and he had carried that board up that stair and down it again every day of this flood and not one person in that room had ever asked him a thing about it.

"**There is a rate on that board I have had wrong and have not put right, and I am telling this room that before anybody in here finds it out off the board, and that is the whole of what I came up that stair for this morning.**"

Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody thanked him for it, and that rate was still wrong on that board at the tenth hour, and that board went down that stair and along that market row with it wrong on it and nothing said about it in that row either. Nobody asked him about it on the way down and he did not say.

About four people went past the outside of that door in the ordinary course while that room was saying all of that, and about four of them got a thing off the thing they were walking past and about four of them did not, and not one of them was asked anything by anybody at any hour of that morning, and not one of them went up that stair, and nobody in that room was looking at the ground at the foot of it while it happened.

The light came off the one window at about the ninth hour and lay across the wood of that bench in a rectangle with a straight edge along the top of it. The man of about thirty-four kept his own two hands flat on that wood and the woman of about twenty-nine kept hers flat on the open page on either side of that book, and neither of them said one word for the rest of that morning, and about four people went past that door at the back of that building in the ordinary course and did not stop. What had been said in that room at the fifth hour and at the seventh was said in daylight in front of about nine people, and there is not a piece of wood or a stone or a page anywhere in this city that has one word of it on it.
