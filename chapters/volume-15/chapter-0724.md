# Chapter 0724 — A Door At The Foot Of A Stair With A Date In Chalk On The Outside Of It And A Bare Piece Of Board About As Wide As A Hand Below That Date, And A Room Over A Market Finding Out At The Fourth Hour That A Decision Is Not A Thing And Does Not Stay Decided By Itself

The man of about thirty-four had both his own hands flat on the outside of that door, one of them on the bare wood below the chalk and the other on the wood of the frame beside the latch, and he was not wiping it and he was not writing on it and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not come out at any hour of that day.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, at the back of that building, and the outside of that door carries a date in chalk and the day on it this morning is the four hundred and ninth day of that month in full. The wood under that chalk is bare about as wide as a hand. Nothing whatever is on it. It is not widened and it is not narrowed and it is not cleaned and it is not painted and it is not covered, and no name has ever been put under that date by anybody, and nothing in this city says what that bare wood means, because it does not mean anything at all.

He took his own two hands off that door and went up that stair into the room over that market, and about nine people were in it, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day, and nothing has ever been put on that wall by anybody, and there is nothing on it to read.

---

At about the fifth hour he said it out loud, and he said it to the middle of the floor of that room and not to anybody on that bench.

"**A decision is not a thing and it does not stay decided by itself, and somebody in here has to decide the same thing again every morning or it comes apart, and nobody in this city has ever had to do that, and we have been doing it the whole of this flood without once noticing that we were doing it.**"

Nobody in that room took that up. Nobody thanked him for it and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody said out loud that they did not know what it was about, and about four people in that room said nothing at all, and the room went on with the morning it had been having.

Nobody asked him what the thing was that had to be decided again, and he did not say, and the thing is this. Nothing anybody in this city owns carries a thing somebody said in a room into a person who was not in that room when it was said, and nothing that anybody has is going to. So a room that has said something out loud on one morning has to say it out loud again on the next one, out of the same mouths, in the same words, to the same people, and the doing of that is the whole of the finding and nobody in this city has ever had to do it before this flood.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up that stair at about the seventh hour with that board under his own arm and stopped at the far end of that bench and stood there with his own right hand flat along the top edge of that board. Nobody in that room asked him one word about anything, and he said one word to nobody, and he carried that board down that stair and out at the front of that building and along that market row a little while later, and the thing that had been said at the fifth hour went with him and he did not know it had.

About four people a day went past the outside of that door in the ordinary course of that morning, and the ground at the foot of that stair, from the step to wherever the paving stops, is the only thing this city has ever had for finding out that anybody was ever in that room, and it works by nothing but feet, and about four people a day walk on it and nobody in that room was watching it at any hour of that day.

---

The thing that room over that market has to decide again every morning is not a decision anybody ever took, and that is the finding: it is that about nine people come into that room at the fourth hour, and the room has to go on being the place they come into, and it has to be said out loud by somebody in it each time, and about nine people in that room were going to go on being in it at the fourth hour, and nobody in this city has ever had to do that.
