# Chapter 0749 — A Room Over A Market On A Monday Morning That Nobody Came Up That Stair To And About Four People A Day Went Past The Outside Of The Door At The Foot Of It, And The Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Keeps A Public Register Sitting At The Near End Of That Bench With That Book Open In Front Of Her And Carrying It The Length Of That Room Onto That Shelf And Then Carrying It Back Again

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both her own hands flat on the wood of that bench on either side of that open book, one of them on each side of the middle column of that page, and she came down the front edge of that wood with the flat of her own right thumb and stopped at the head of that page and went back up it and did not turn a leaf over.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and nobody came up that stair at any hour of that morning and nobody went down it either, and about four people went past the outside of the door at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of that day without going up it.

That book has three columns on that page. The middle one holds five figures with a day entered against each of the five and none of the five struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it, and she did not write one word in it at any hour of that morning. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and that word is not in her hand and she did not look at it more than once this morning. Behind that bench there is a shelf and a second slate stands on it with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under that day, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and nothing was written on it at any hour of that day, and it is not on a shelf of its own, and her own two hands were the only hands that went near that shelf at any hour of that morning.

The ground at the foot of that stair is a strip of ground running from the bottom step of it to wherever the paving gives out, and it is not cleared and it is not paved over and it is not widened and it is not narrowed, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day and not one of them knows anything about that room.

---

At about the eighth hour she said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor and there was nobody in that room to hear it.

"**Nobody has come up that stair this morning and I have got that book open in front of me and I have not written one word in it, and there is nobody in this room for me to say that to, so I am saying it to the floor.**"

Nobody improved on one word of that because there was nobody there to improve on it, and nothing was said in answer to it, and she put her own two hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page again and went on with that morning.

---

At about the ninth hour she got both her own hands under that book and lifted it off the wood of that bench and carried it the length of that room and set it down square with the edge of it on that shelf in front of that second slate, and stood there with both her own hands flat on the wood of that shelf for a while.

Then she took that book off that shelf again, carried it the length of that room, and put it back on that bench at the near end of it, and sat down in front of it with both her own hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page, and nothing whatever has been written in that book at any hour of that day.
