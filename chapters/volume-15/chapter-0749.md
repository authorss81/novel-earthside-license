# Chapter 0749 — Nobody Came Up That Stair

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both her own hands on the wood of that bench on either side of that open book, one on each side of the middle column of that page, and she came down the front edge of that wood with the flat of her own right thumb and stopped at the head of the page and went back up it and did not turn a leaf over.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market. Nobody came up the stair at any hour of that morning and nobody went down it either, and about four people went past the outside of the door at the foot of the stair in the ordinary course of that day without going up it.

That book has three columns on the page. The middle one holds five figures with a day entered against each of the five and none of the five struck, and no sixth has gone into it and none has been taken out of it, and she did not write one word in it at any hour of the morning. The column on the right of the page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on the page, and that word is not in her hand and she did not look at it more than once this morning. Behind the bench there is a shelf, and a second slate stands on it with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under the day, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and nothing was written on it at any hour of the day, and it is not on a shelf of its own, and her own two hands were the only hands that went near that shelf at any hour of the morning.

The ground at the foot of the stair is a strip of ground running from the bottom step of it to wherever the paving gives out, and it is not cleared and it is not paved over and it is not widened and it is not narrowed, and about four people a day walk on it in the ordinary course of a day and not one of them knows anything about the room.

---

At about the eighth hour she said one thing out loud to the middle of that floor, and there was nobody in the room to hear it.

"**Nobody has come up the stair this morning and I have got this book open in front of me and I have not written one word in it, and there is nobody in this room for me to say that to, so I am saying it to the floor.**"

Nothing was said in answer to it, and she set her hands on the wood on either side of that open page again and went on with the morning.

At about the ninth hour she got both her own hands under the book and lifted it off the wood of the bench and carried it the length of the room and set it down square with the edge of it on the shelf in front of that second slate, and stood there with both her own hands on the wood of the shelf for a while.

Then she took the book off the shelf again and carried it the length of the room and put it back on the bench at the near end of it and sat down in front of it with both her own hands on the wood on either side of that open page, and nothing whatever has been written in the book at any hour of the day. About four people went past the outside of that door while she was carrying it, and not one of them stopped, and not one of them knew that the only other thing the room had been used for the morning was the carrying of a book to a shelf and back.
