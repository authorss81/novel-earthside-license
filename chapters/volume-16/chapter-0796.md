# Chapter 0796 — Eleven Miles And A Wet Sill

The man of about thirty-eight set both hands on the rim of the trough in the yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats and leaned his weight on it to test the seating. Rain drove across the yard in sheets and the flags ran with water.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-first day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The flats lay drowned from the gate to the sky, with standing water over the wheel lines. The stack of boards at the back stood dark, the length on the top of it squared and holding.

He worked the yard through the weather. He emptied the trough with his bucket, reset it level on its bed, and filled it again from the low place in the stone at the side of the yard. The bucket lay on its side in the water where it belongs when he was done. The rag he spread on the stone lip with the damp side up, and the wind tried to take it and failed.

---

At about the seventh hour he took up a short plank from beside the stack and carried it through the rain to the room over the market. He climbed that stair with water running off his sleeves and found the one window shut with wet standing along the wood of the sill inside.

He laid the plank across the wet sill, one end on the stone and one on the wood, to carry the drip clear of the wall. He pressed it down with both hands and held it while the water found the slope and ran. The keeper moved her book a foot along the bench to keep it out of the run. The man of about thirty-four lifted his elbows off the wood until the run passed.

**"It will do till the rain eases," the man of about thirty-eight said.**

**"It carries it clear," the man of about twenty-seven said from the window. "Leave it."**

He left it where it lay and went back down into the weather. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had done right. The plank stayed across the sill all morning with water running down the middle of it into the trough below.

---

By about the tenth hour the rain had thinned over the flats to a blowing mist. The yard held its water in sheets, the trough stood level and full, and the plank lay where hands had put it with the wood dark along its underside.
