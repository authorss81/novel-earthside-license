# Chapter 0756 — Eight In One Mouth And Nine In Another

Both of the woman of about fifty-two's hands were on the wood of that bench, one on each side of a space about as long as a forearm, and she put them there at the fourth hour and she took them off at the fifth hour and she put them back at the sixth hour.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over a market, and about nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and the strip of ground at the foot of the stair at the back of that building runs from the bottom step to wherever the paving gives out.

"**There are about nine of us in this room and there have been about nine of us in it every morning of this flood, and I have never once seen anybody write it down.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the far end of that bench and he took his own two hands off his knees.

"**There are eight of us in this room this morning, and I have said that figure in here on more mornings than I have said any other, and the difference between your figure and mine is one person who has not come up that stair yet and I have not got anything that says which person it is.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of that and nobody asked him how he knew there were eight, and nobody asked the woman of about fifty-two where her figure had come from, and about four of the people in that room said out loud that they did not know and about four of them said nothing at all. He had that piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket against his own chest, and it stayed there through every hour of that day and did not come out of it once.

---

At about the sixth hour somebody lifted that bar out of its sockets on the far side of that door and put it down on the flags inside the room at the bottom of that stair, and the door came open against the wall of the stair and stayed there with its own weight.

Nobody in that room heard the bar come out of its sockets and nobody saw a hand on it and nobody in that room asked who had opened the door at the back of that building. The date in chalk is on the outside of that door and below that date the wood of it is bare about as wide as a hand with nothing whatever standing on it, and that bare piece of that door means nothing at all and has not been widened, narrowed, cleaned, painted or covered on any day of this flood.

A door on two hinges carries its own weight on the side it swings to, and that door swung into the room, and it stood with the inside face of it against the wall of that stair from the sixth hour until somebody shut it again, and the light off the flats came through the opening in a long rectangle that lay across the flags and up onto the bottom two steps and stayed there. The man of about thirty-four put his own two hands on the flat of the bar where it lay on the flags at about the eighth hour and turned it over and looked at the two sockets on the far side of that door and turned it back over again and left it lying there, and nobody in that room asked him why he had turned it over. About four people went past the outside of that door in the ordinary course of that day and about four of them were after something off the thing they were walking past, and not one of them went up, and the rectangle of light on those flags did not turn round when any of them crossed it.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that stair with his own barrow and stopped at the bottom step and looked at that bar lying on the flags and then went up with the barrow to the top of it and came back down again empty, and nobody in that room said one word to him about either and he said nothing to them.

At about the tenth hour the door at the foot of that stair was still open against the wall of the stair and the bar was still lying on the flags inside the room at the bottom of it where it had been put down at the sixth hour, and about four people had gone past the outside of it, and there was nothing on that bar and nothing on that door and nothing on that ground to say how many of them there had been in the room above it at any hour of that day.
