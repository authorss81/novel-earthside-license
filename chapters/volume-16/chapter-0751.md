# Chapter 0751 — The Trough Filled Before The Fourth Hour

Both of the man of about thirty-one's hands were on the rim of that trough, one at each end of the near edge of it, and he pushed it sideways along the flags with the flats of them until the near edge of it stopped a finger's width short of the stone lip of that step, and then he took his hands off it and let it stand where it was.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats. There is one trough at the top of that step and there has been one for the whole of this flood, and the bucket is down in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard, and the rag is on the stone lip of that step and is not inside the trough. A box of chalk stands on that step with the lid of it lying down on the rim of the box, and the lid does not shut. The store's board is on the outside wall at the far end of that market, on two nails above a step of its own, and it carries three sets of figures of this city's own and none of the three has been rubbed out and not one of the three carries a line of words set under it. The wall above the top edge of that board was washed once in the whole of this flood and by no second person on any day of it.

There was nobody in that yard at the fourth hour. The flap of that man's satchel was down against his own hip while he did it and he put the water in with the rim of the trough itself and not with anything else, and the water came up over the inside of it and stood a hand's breadth short of the edge, and he put his own two hands flat on the stone on either side of it and looked at that level for about the length of a minute and then went up the eleven miles of flats with that flap down.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-eight at that wharf came into that yard with his barrow empty and his knife in his belt and put both hands on the near rim of that trough and bent over it. He took his own right hand off the rim and put it flat into the water and turned it over and looked at it and dried it on his own thigh.

"**Somebody has filled this trough this morning and it was not full at the light yesterday and I have not asked anybody about it and I am not going to start now, and the water in it is cold and clean and I am going to use it.**"

Nobody in that yard answered him and nobody thanked him. He filled his own bucket out of it and carried it to the low place in the stone and let the water out of it and came back and filled it again, and the level went down a thumb's breadth and stayed there.

---

Over that market there is a room, and it is up a stair at the back of the store's end of that market, and at about the fourth hour on that same morning about nine people were in it. There is a long bench in there with a near end and a far end, and a chair at the far side of the room, and a table with a cloth laid flat on it, and a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder along the wall at the far side of the room carrying one sheet with a heading and one ruled place under that heading, and behind that bench a second shelf carrying a book with three columns on the page in front of it and a slate standing behind that book on that same shelf with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever standing under that day.

The woman of about fifty-two had the flats of her own two hands on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and said the number out loud in the ordinary course of that morning, and she said it the way she said it most mornings.

"**There are about nine of us in here this morning and there were about nine of us here yesterday, and that is what I have got and it is the only thing I have ever had to say about this room.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of it. Nobody argued with her about it and nobody asked her where she had got it from, because nobody in that room has ever asked her where she had got anything from, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along put both his own hands flat on his own knees where he was standing at the far end of that bench and did not say that it was right and did not say that it was wrong.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up that stair at about the fifth hour with that board under his own arm and got to the near end of that bench and put the flat of his own hand on the wood of it, and about nine people went past him on that stair in the ordinary course of the rest of that morning and not one of them said one word to him about what he was carrying.

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-one came back down the eleven miles of flats and came into that yard with both his own hands empty and put his own two hands on the near rim of that trough again and looked at the level of the water in it, which had gone down a thumb's breadth and had been left by a man who would not say that he had used it.

He took his hands off the rim and stood there, and the water stood where the man of about thirty-eight had left it, and there was nothing on that trough and nothing on the flags round it and nothing anywhere in that yard that said any of it had happened.
