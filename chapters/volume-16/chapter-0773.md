# Chapter 0773 — The Coil Came Back Over The Mallet

Adrian Vale got his fingers into the bight of that coil and lifted it off the bed of the handcart. It came up heavier than it looked, the way a thing does that has been used and put down wet.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and fifty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The wind was still on the far end of that road and not in that yard.

The handcart stood in the yard out past the last named house with its shafts down in the gravel. A hazel mallet lay on the bed of it with its head bound in cord where the head had split. The coil of rope that had been used lay under that mallet. The whole of it was one coil, and a loose end hung off it that had been cut off somebody else's coil at some time.

Along the top of the shut road above that yard there was a fence of sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound onto them. Nobody had ever measured it and nobody was going to.

He wanted that coil of rope out past that house and standing by the top of that road.

---

He carried it in both arms to the far side of the yard and set it down on the gravel at the foot of the shut road. That was the place where the road comes up out of the grass and turns along the top of the fence.

It was a good place for it. It was out of the way of the house and out of the way of the cart, and the man of about sixty would not have to move a thing out of his own bed to get at the mallet. It was a rope the size of a man's two hands. Anybody coming along that road in the dark would have had it at his feet in a moment.

He stood there with his hands still shaped round it and understood, standing there, that a rope put down by the top of a shut road is not a rope left for somebody. It is a rope put there for whoever turns up next with something to get over a fence with, and the fence along that road has eleven withies bound onto it, and every one of them is the right height for a man who has decided to go over it.

He had carried that coil twenty feet. He was not going to carry it the other twenty.

---

At about the sixth hour the side door of that house came open, and the man of about sixty came out into the yard with a hobnail in his hand and no coat on.

He saw the coil of rope on the gravel at the foot of that road from the step. He came down the yard to it and took hold of it in both hands, the way a man takes hold of a thing he has been looking for and has not said anything about.

He lifted it off the gravel and turned it over once. There was a wet print of the grit in the middle of the coil where it had been lying against the ground. He looked at that print for about as long as it takes to decide a thing is not worth asking anybody about.

Then he carried it back up the yard and set it down on the bed of that handcart over the mallet. He put the loose end of it on the top, so that it would not go over the side. He said nothing whatever.

Not one word of it went into either of them. Neither of the two men said anything at all. Inside that house there was a length of new rope lying over the back of a chair where it had lain through the whole of this flood, and no hand in this city had ever lifted it.

---

The man of about sixty did not take that handcart out past the last named house that morning.

He had gone down that road with it on a run of mornings of this flood, up to the top of the fence and back, and had come in and stood it in the yard with the shafts down in the gravel. On this morning he got the cob out from under one of the shafts at about the seventh hour, and then he put his foot back on it.

He got a hoe out of the shed instead and went along the side of that yard. He was the only man on that road that day working at a distance of a foot from his own gate instead of a mile from it. Nobody asked him why, and nobody came by that road to see it.

At about the tenth hour he stood the handcart back in its place with the coil of rope on the bed of it and the mallet under that coil, and he went in. The shafts were down in the gravel in the marks he had made the morning before and in no others.

The side of that yard where he had been working with the hoe all morning was the only part of it that had been done anything to that day. It was done to about the depth of a spade, in a straight line, from the gate to the wall, and the gravel turned up in it was a different colour from the gravel the rest of the way, and it was going to be a line on that yard for the rest of that flood.
