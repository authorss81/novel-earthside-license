# Chapter 0797 — Up The Stair And Down Again

The woman of about twenty-nine took the book up in both hands from the shelf behind the bench and carried it the length of the room to the near end. Her thumbs held the cover shut against her dress while she walked.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-second day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. Light came thin through the one window and lay short of the bench. Below, the door at the stair foot showed its chalk day outward, with the bar home in its sockets within.

She laid the book open on the wood and put her hands on either side of the page. The five strokes down the middle column stood each with a day against it, none struck, nothing added after. People came up and took their places along the bench and the wall.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-four came up from the market with rain on his shoulders and sat down in his hollow. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the far end with her hands at the sides of her dress. The carrier stood by the wall with his satchel under his arm, the flap down.

A tread sounded on the stair. The man of about thirty-eight came up with his barrow handles in his hands, stopped two steps below the top when he saw the room full, and looked at the floor where his corner used to be. He stood there the length of a held breath, with rainwater running off his sleeves onto the steps. Then he turned round and went back down without setting foot on the boards, his barrow knocking once against the wall as he turned.

Nobody called after him. The keeper smoothed the page. The man of about thirty-four looked at the middle of the floor and said nothing. The woman of about fifty-two put one hand out toward the stairwell and brought it back to her dress.

At about the eighth hour the room talked again of small things, of cord and canvas and the drip off the roof. The carrier shifted his satchel higher.

**"That canvas wants tightening before rain," the man of about thirty-four said.**

**"It held through the night," the carrier said. "It will hold till evening."** The book lay where hands had carried it, open at the near end, with the light moving slowly toward it.

---

At about the tenth hour the keeper shut the book and bore it back the length of the room in both hands to the shelf behind the bench, setting it down ahead of the dark shape only her hands ever approach. The stair stood empty below, with a wet mark halfway up where a man had turned.
