# Chapter 0754 — The Front Two Stalls Along

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on the near edge of that board where it stood on its end against the bare back of the stallholder's own board, and he set it down square on the flags of that market row and put the flat of his own right hand flat against the wood of the back of that other board to hold it while he took his hands away.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in the middle of that market row where the thing a person walks along is the line of trestles and boards.

The man of about thirty-four keeps a front two stalls along. His own board stands face in against his own trestle with the bare back of it turned out into that row behind his own shoulder, and anybody coming along that row could put an arm out and touch it. He wanted Adrian Vale's board up out of the flags and into his own hands, because a board standing upright against the back of another man's board is a thing four hundred yards of flags long of people can see, and nobody had looked at it.

Nobody in that row looked at it.

---

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-four came in at the front of that row and got behind his own trestle and put both his own hands on the face of his own board and pulled it a finger's width off the trestle and let it back again, and the bare back of it came away off Adrian Vale's board by that finger's width and went back against it, and he said nothing about it and did not turn round.

"**There is a board standing up against the back of mine and I have got my hands on my own and I am not going to say whose it is and I am not going to move it, and there is a piece of chalk in this pocket that has not been out of it on one morning of this flood.**"

Nobody in that row answered him. About four people went past that front in the ordinary course of that morning, and not one of them said a word to him about the front he was standing at or about the board he had behind him.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came along that row of trestles and boards with his own board under his own arm and nineteen rates in three columns on the face of it, and there is a space about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two where a name is not, and he has traded those rates in four markets for about nine years and has never said one word to anybody about that space.

He stopped at the front two stalls along. He did not go up to the end of the row and he did not go along it either, and he stood there with both his own hands on the face of his own board for about the length of a minute, and behind him the man of about thirty-four said out loud, into the middle of that row, how many of them were in the room over that market.

"**There are about nine of us in that room this morning and there were about nine of us there yesterday.**"

The man of about thirty-nine heard every word of that. He did not turn round and he did not go up that stair with his board under his arm at the end of that morning as he has gone up it on every morning of this flood, and at about the tenth hour he was still at the front of his own board two stalls along with the flap of nothing in his hands, and nobody in that row asked him where he had gone and nobody told him he had done anything.

At the tenth hour Adrian Vale picked his own board up off the flags where he had left it and stood it against the bare back of the other board again where it had stood all morning, and the man of about thirty-four came in and looked at it and said nothing about it at any hour of that day.
