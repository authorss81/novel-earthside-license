# Chapter 0767 — Nobody Asked Either Of Them Anything

Both of the man of about thirty-four's hands were on his own knees where he was standing at the far end of that bench, and he took them off his knees and put the flats of them flat on the wood of the bench and left them there through the whole of the fifth hour.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in that room over that market. Nine of its own people came into it over the course of that morning, and the room had been saying how many of its own people were in it on the ordinary course of every morning of this flood, and no person in four hundred miles has ever put a question to anybody about where that came from.

"**There are about nine of us in here this morning. I have given that figure into this room on more mornings of this flood than I have said anything else and I have never written it down and nobody in this room has ever asked me or anybody else where it came from, and I am saying that this morning out loud and I am not saying it to anybody in particular.**"

Nobody in that room asked him. Not one of them improved on a word of it, and none of them told him he had it right and none of them told him he had it wrong. The piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket stayed against his own chest from the fourth hour of that day to the tenth hour of it and he did not take it out.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about twenty-seven came up out of the yard below with a bucket hanging out of his own hand, and he stopped short at the near end of that bench and set the bucket on the flags at the foot of it.

"**Somebody has put a date in chalk on the outside of that door at the foot of this stair and there is no name on it, and I have carried water past that door every morning of this flood, and I do not know who put it there and I am not going to find out by asking anybody in this room.**"

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book put one of her own hands flat on the wood of that bench without looking up off that page and said out loud that she did not know either, and the man of about twenty-seven stood there with his own two hands on his own knees for about the length of a minute and said that he did not know and that nobody in this room ever had.

Nobody in the room said one word to either of them about either of those two answers. Nobody in that room said one word to either of them about the man of about thirty-four either, and the woman of about fifty-two put her own two hands at the sides of her own dress and looked at the middle of that floor and said nothing at all about any of it.

---

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board put that board off his own arm and laid it flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it with the face of it turned up, and he kept his own right hand flat on the face of it and left it there for about the length of a minute.

About nine people were in the room at that hour. Nineteen rates were lying in the open on the bench in three columns with the part of it where no name is standing up in the light in front of every one of them, and there is a space about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two and nobody in that room had ever asked him about it and nobody in the room said one word about what was on that bench at any hour of the day. He took his own hand off it, put that board back under his own arm and went down the stair with it, and not one of the people in that room said a word about what had been lying face up on that bench a minute before.
