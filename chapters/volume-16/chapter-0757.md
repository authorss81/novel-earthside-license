# Chapter 0757 — Two Mornings He Cannot Put In An Order

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on the near rim of that trough and he leaned over the near edge of it and looked at the level of the water in it, and it was a hand's breadth short of the edge and it had been filled before he got there.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-second day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats. The bucket lies in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard. The rag is on the stone lip of that step and not inside the trough. The chalk is in a box on the step and the lid of it is lying down on the rim, because it does not shut.

He wanted it full before the fourth hour, and he came at the fourth hour and it was full, and he did not take his own hands off that rim and ask one person in that city who had filled it.

Nobody in that yard was there to ask.

---

He took his own hands off the rim and went up the eleven miles of flats and came to that stair and went up it, and the door at the foot of that stair stood open against the wall of the stair with the bar lying on the flags inside the room at the bottom of it, and he went past the bar and went up.

About nine people were in that room over the course of that morning and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board standing against the leg of it and the flats of his own two hands on the wood.

"**I have come up this stair on every morning of this flood and I could not tell you this morning which of them was the first, and I have gone at it this morning for about an hour and I have not got two of them in an order that means anything, and I am not going to get any of you to do it for me.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for that and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody argued with him about it and nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong. Nobody in that room asked him a second question about it at any hour of that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book did not look up off that page, and the woman of about fifty-two put the flats of her own two hands on that wood about a forearm apart from each other and said nothing at all.

---

He came up the eleven miles at about the fourth hour and went back down it at about the tenth hour, and two lengths of board were leaning against the outside wall at the foot of that stair the whole of that time, one of them his and one of them not. At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight came up that stair with his own barrow and had to turn that barrow on its side to get past the two of them and got one end of it down on the flags and took it up again and went on up, and neither man said one word to the other about it at any hour of that day and neither of them was told anything about it by anybody.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight came into that yard with his barrow and drew the water out of that trough into the low place in the stone and put the trough back down on the flags a hand's breadth further from the step than it had stood, and Adrian Vale was not in that yard at the tenth hour and was not told any of it.

The trough stood on the flags where that man had left it and the stone at the low place was wet, and about four people a day walk past that yard in the ordinary course and not one of them has ever stopped at it, and the man who filled it that morning at the fourth hour had said nothing about it and no hand in that yard went near it after the man of about thirty-eight had finished with it.
