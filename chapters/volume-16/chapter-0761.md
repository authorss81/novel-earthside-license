# Chapter 0761 — A Chair Turned Round At The Far Side

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on the two ends of the seat of that chair where it stood at the far side of that room with its back toward the middle of the floor, and he got his fingers under the wood and turned it about on the flags a half-turn and walked it back the width of his own two hands until the back of it stood against the wall along the far side of the room.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room over a market. About nine people came into it over that morning and not one of them put how many of them out loud, and not one of them noticed that they had not, and it is written nowhere that any of them noticed.

He wanted the chair because a man sitting in it where it had stood since the flood came would have his own back to that door at the top of the stair, and that was the one thing he had not been able to get round in a fortnight of coming up that stair. He got it round. He stood the back of that chair against that wall and put his own hands on the arms of it and sat down in it.

From there he could see the near end of that bench and the flags and about half of that floor and no more. The one window of the room is high in the wall and its sill was above his own head where he was sitting, and the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder on that wall was behind him and he could not see it without turning about, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the fifth hour and Adrian Vale saw him come up it and could not have told you afterwards from which end of the bench the man had walked in at.

He took his own hands off the arms of the chair and stood up off it and left it standing there against the wall.

---

The man of about thirty-nine came up that stair with that board under his own arm. Nineteen rates are cut into the face of the board in three columns and it has gone on that man's own arm through four markets for about nine years, and at the foot of the column that stands between the other two there is a bare place about the width of two fingers with nothing cut in it, and nothing has ever been cut in that bare place, and nobody in this city has ever asked him about it.

He got to the far end of that bench, and there was that chair standing against the far wall where it had not stood since the flood came, and he put the flat of his own right hand on the face of that board and looked at the chair for about the length of a minute and then he turned his own board about under his arm and sat down in it.

"**There is a chair by that wall that has not been by the wall since this flood came and I have come up the stair on every morning of it, and I am sitting in it, and I am not going to ask anybody which of them put it there.**"

Nobody in that room answered him. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book had it open on the wood of the bench at the near end of it in front of her and did not look up off that page, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own two hands on his own knees where he was standing and did not say that it was right and did not say that it was wrong, and the chalk that man carries stayed inside his own coat against his chest from the fourth hour to the tenth hour and his own hand never went near that pocket.

Adrian Vale stood at the near end of that bench with his own two hands at the sides of his own coat and his own back to the door at the top of the stair, which is where everybody in the room stands, and nobody in that room said one word to him about where he was standing or about the chair.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about twenty-seven came up that stair with his own bucket in his own hand and stopped at the far end of the bench, and the man of about thirty-nine was in that chair at the far end of it with the board against the leg of the bench and his own boots in the gap between that wall and that bench, and the man of about twenty-seven stood there until the man of about thirty-nine put his own two feet out into the room and the man of about twenty-seven came the rest of the way along and put his own bucket down on the flags.

Nobody in the room said one word to either of them about that, and the man of about twenty-seven went back down the stair at about the eighth hour with his own bucket in his own hand and said nothing at any hour of that day.

At about the ninth hour Adrian Vale went down that stair and out onto the flags at the foot of it and stood there with his own two hands empty and looked at the ground for about the length of a minute and came back up. Nobody in that room said one word to him about going down and coming up, and nobody in the room asked him one word about anything at any hour of the day.

---

At about the tenth hour the chair was standing against the wall along the far side of that room with the man of about thirty-nine's own hands flat on the face of his own board where it stood against the leg of the bench, and the light had gone off the flags some while before and there was a man's boots in the gap between the wall and that bench, and nobody in the room had said one word to either of the two men about any part of it.
