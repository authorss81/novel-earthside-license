# Chapter 0770 — Two People Could Not Say What It Was

Both of the man of about thirty-four's hands were flat on the wood of that bench at the far end of it and he took them off it at the fifth hour and put them back on it at the sixth hour, and he had the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket against his own chest the whole of that morning and it did not come out of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over that market, and about nine people came into it over the morning and were standing in it at one time or another between the fourth hour and the tenth hour.

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up that stair with that board under his own arm and turned it about and stood the edge of it against the leg of the bench at the near end of it and gave that figure out loud into the middle of that floor by himself, the way he goes at it on most mornings of this flood.

"**There are about nine of us in here this morning and that is what there are in here this morning.**"

Not one of them took a single one of those words back up or put another one beside it, and not one of them asked him where the figure came from, and not one of them said a word to him about it.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four turned round on that bench and said one thing out loud into that room without turning to anybody in it.

"**The man who brings that barrow up this stair has been coming up it on the mornings of this flood, and I have been leaving him a length of this bench at the near end of it since the beginning of it, and I cannot tell you this morning since which morning he began, and I have been at it for about an hour and I have not got it.**"

About four people in the room said out loud that they did not know, in four different words and none of them the same as any of the other three, and about four people in the rest of that room said nothing at all about it and went on with what they had in their own hands, and the two of them at the near end of that bench were the two of them who had said the most about it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book said that she did not know and went on looking at that page. The man of about thirty-eight came up the stair at about the eighth hour with his own barrow and stood it along that wall and nobody in the room said one word to him about anything, and he went back down that stair.

---

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-four stood at the near end of that bench and put it to the woman of about twenty-nine out loud, in the ordinary way, that not one of them in that room could say what that figure had been at the fourth hour of that morning, and she said that she could not.

She put her own hand down again on the wood of that bench on the other side of that open page and he put his own flat on the wood on the other side of that from her, and they stood there at the near end of it with about a forearm of wood between them, and neither of them could have said what the figure had been at the fourth hour.
