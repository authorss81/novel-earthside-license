# Chapter 0764 — She Said The Cost Before Anything Else

The woman of about fifty-two put the flats of her own two hands down on the wood at the far end of that bench a forearm apart from each other, and she left them lying there, and that was the first thing she did that morning.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. She was up in the room over that market with the daylight standing on that floor, and the outside face of the door at the foot of that stair carries a day in chalk and nothing else, and about nine people came into that room over the course of the morning. There is a stool at the side of the bench that she carried out of the room herself and left where anybody could sit on it, and nobody has sat on it on any morning of this flood, and she has not sat on it herself on any morning of this flood.

She spoke before she had said anything else that day, and she said what it had cost her before she said what it was.

"**It has cost me the only thing I have got, and I am the one person in this room who knows who is not in it. I have never once said that out loud in here and nobody in this room has ever asked me how I know it, and there is no mouth in four hundred miles that is going to ask me either, and that is the whole of what I have that is mine, and I have paid for it every morning of this flood by not having it out loud.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for it. Not one of them improved on a word of it, and nobody argued with her about it, and she was not asked how she knows it, because no one in the room has ever put that question to her about anything at all. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench at that hour with that board against the leg of it and he heard every word of what she said and he did not say one word back about any part of it. The woman of about twenty-nine had her own book open on the wood of the bench in front of her with her own two hands flat on the wood on either side of the page, and she did not look up off it.

---

Nothing in that room was done about it at any hour of the day.

At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-nine put the flats of his own two hands on the wood of that bench and gave that figure out loud into the middle of that floor the way he gives it out on most mornings of this flood, and about nine of them were in the room at that hour and not one of them looked at the woman of about fifty-two while he said it.

"**About nine of us in here this morning, and about nine of us in here every morning of this flood, and it has come out of this room in my mouth more often than it has come out of anybody else's in it, and that is all I am going to say this morning and I am saying it before I say anything else.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of that. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood up off the wall where he had been standing and went out of the room and down the stair and came back up it at about the sixth hour with the woman of about twenty-nine's water jug in his own two hands and set it down on the table with the cloth laid flat on it, and nobody in that room said one word to him about having gone down that stair for it.

---

At about the seventh hour the woman of about fifty-two took her own two hands off the wood of the bench at the far end of it and put them at the sides of her own dress, and she went and stood in front of that stool at the side of that bench and looked at it and then she sat down on it.

The man of about thirty-four moved along the bench about a forearm's length without being asked and without saying one word about having to. Nobody else in the room moved at all. She sat on that stool with her own hands at the sides of her own dress and her own back against the wall behind that stool and she looked at the middle of that floor, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf came up the stair at about the eighth hour with his own barrow and went round the two of them and said nothing to either of them about anything.

At about the ninth hour the light came over the roof above that one window and came across that floor and reached that bench and stopped a hand's width short of the woman of about twenty-nine's page, and the woman of about fifty-two was sitting at the side of the bench with her back to that wall when it came and she did not turn her own head towards it.

---

At about the tenth hour that bench at the far end of it stood empty with nothing on the wood of it and that woman was still sitting on that stool at the side of it with her own two hands at the sides of her own dress, and about nine people had gone in and out of that room over the course of that morning, and not one of them had said one word to her about what it had cost her or about the sitting down, and the light had gone off that floor by then and she had not stood up off that stool.
