# Chapter 0763 — The Satchel Stood Two Hours On That Shelf

He got his fingers under the strap and lifted the satchel off the wood of the bench and stood up holding it in front of him, and the weight of it went down his arms and settled.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and the market under that room was going about its business below and had not noticed anything that was happening over its head.

The man of about thirty-one had come up that stair at the fourth hour, set the satchel down on the bench at the near end of it, and gone back down, and he had not come up again since. There was one thing in that satchel and its owner knew what that thing was. Adrian had the flap against the side of it under his thumb and he did not put a finger on it and did not turn it up.

A bench in that room was where a thing a person was carrying got put down and taken up again all day long. A shelf at the height of a person's shoulder was not that. That was the whole of his reasoning and he had arrived at it in about the time it took to lift the strap off the wood.

He turned about and looked at that shelf.

There was a sheet lying on it with a heading on it and one ruled place under that heading. Nothing had ever been written in that place and nothing had ever been read out of it. Nobody in this city had ever been asked what it was for. He stood in front of that shelf with a man's satchel in his hands and about a foot of clear wood on the near side of that sheet.

He put it down clear of the sheet. He set it square with the strap hanging over the front edge of the shelf so that it could not go forward off anything, and he put the flats of his hands flat on the wood on either side of it and left them there.

Then he took his hands off, which was the last thing he wanted to do.

He went out onto the flags and along the row and back up the stair. He came in behind the man of about thirty-eight and stood against the far wall with his hands empty, and watched the room for a while without being in it.

---

Nobody asked him what was in that satchel. The man of about thirty-eight was not asked about it either. The woman of about twenty-nine had the register open on the wood at the near end of the bench and did not look up at that wall at any hour of that morning. The man of about thirty-four went on with his stall two stalls along and the chalk in his inside breast pocket and came in once and went out again.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-eight came up with his barrow and his knife in his belt. He stood the barrow along the wall on the near side of that room the way he stood it, with the handles into the corner, and then he came back to the far end of the bench to put his hand flat on the wood of it the way he put his hand flat on things.

The bench was where the register was. The shelf at the far side was where a stranger's satchel was with its owner's strap hanging over the front edge. He stood in the middle of that floor for about the length of ten seconds with his arms down at the sides of his coat, and then he took himself back down that stair with his barrow. He came up again at the ninth hour without it and stood it along the wall with the handles into the corner anyway.

Nobody asked him why he had gone down and come back up. The woman of about twenty-nine turned a page over and did not look up at that wall.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one came up the stair.

He came the length of that room without hesitating. He did not stop at the bench, he did not look along it, and he did not put a hand out in front of him the way a man does when he is not sure of a thing. He went the whole length of that floor to the wall at the far side and stopped in front of that shelf. He put his right hand flat down on the strap of that satchel, on the wood of it, over the place where Adrian's right hand had been lying on it two hours before.

Adrian took his hand off it and put it at the side of his coat.

The man of about thirty-one stood there with his hand on that strap for the length of a minute. He lifted the satchel off the shelf, settled it under his arm with the flap down against its own side, and went back down that stair.

Neither of them said one word to the other about any part of it. Nobody in that room said a word to either of them about it, and nobody told either of them they had done anything. The woman of about twenty-nine turned a page over in that register at about the same hour and did not look along the bench.

---

At the tenth hour that shelf stood empty along the wall at the far side of the room and the sheet and its heading and its one ruled place were lying on it where they had always lain.

In two hours that shelf had become a place in that room.

It had been put there by a man who had never used it and had put nothing on it. It had no arrangement, no chalk, no rule and nothing written on it. And the man of about thirty-one had come the whole length of that floor at the ninth hour without one look anywhere else, and had put his hand down in the exact place where the strap of his own satchel had been lying.

He had taken two hours off it and gone back down that stair with the flap against its own side, and he had not once looked to see whether anybody was standing at the other end of the room.
