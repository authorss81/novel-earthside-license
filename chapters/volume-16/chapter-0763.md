# Chapter 0763 — The Satchel Stood Two Hours On That Shelf

Both of Adrian Vale's hands were on the strap of that satchel where it lay on the wood of that bench at the near end of it with the flap down against its own side, and he got his fingers under the strap and lifted it off that wood and stood up holding it in front of him.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room over a market. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had come up that stair at the fourth hour and set the satchel down on the wood of the bench and gone back down it and had not come up again for five hours. There is one thing in it and its owner knows what that thing is, and about nine people were in there through that whole morning, and nobody put how many of them into the air of the room at any hour of it.

He wanted that satchel set on the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder along the wall at the far side of that room and left there, because a bench in that room is where a thing a person is carrying gets put down and taken up again all day long, and a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder is not.

He looked at that shelf at the fourth hour and did not put it on it, because the shelf carries a sheet with a heading on it and one ruled place under that heading, and nothing has ever been written in that place and nothing has ever been read out of it and nobody in this city has ever asked him what it is for, and he was not going to set a man's satchel down against it.

---

He went up the eleven miles of flats with the satchel in his own two hands and the flap of it still down and came back down them at about the seventh hour, and he stood in front of that shelf and put it down on the wood of it and set it square with the strap hanging over the front edge of it so that it could not go over, and he put the flats of his own two hands flat on the wood of the shelf on either side of it.

He stood there.

No one in that room asked him what was in that satchel. The man of about thirty-one was not asked about it either, and Adrian Vale was not asked one word about what he was doing with it. The flap of it was down against its own side at every hour of that morning and he did not put a finger on it and he did not turn it up. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book had the book open on the wood of that bench at the near end of it and did not look up at the wall at any hour of that day, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood at the far end of the bench with his own two hands on his own knees and looked at the middle of that floor.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one came up the stair and came the length of the room and stopped in front of that shelf and put his own right hand flat on the strap of the satchel, on the wood of it, over Adrian Vale's own right hand where that hand was still on it.

Adrian Vale took his own hand off it. The man of about thirty-one stood there with his own hand on that strap for about the length of a minute and then he lifted that satchel off the shelf and set it under his own arm and went back down that stair with the flap down against its own side.

Neither of them said one word to the other about any part of it, and nobody in that room said one word to either of them about it either, and nobody in the room told either of them they had done anything.

---

At about the tenth hour that shelf at the height of a person's shoulder stood empty along the wall at the far side of that room with the sheet and its heading and its one ruled place still lying on it, and the strap of that satchel had hung over the front edge of that wood for the length of two hours, and it had not been anywhere near that page at any hour of that morning.
