# Chapter 0789 — The Board On Two Nails

The man of about twenty-seven came down to the store's end of the market with his bucket in one hand and his rag in the other. The board on the outside wall hung on two nails above the step, and he set the bucket on the flags and looked up at it.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The trough stood on the flags at the top of the step, half full from the night. The stone lip of the step held last night's damp, and the rag in his hand was still wrung from the morning's first work.

Three sets of chalk strokes stood on the face of the board, each in its own place, none with a line of words beneath it. He knew them as a man knows a thing he passes every day without reading. The wall above the top edge of the board showed weather stains in streaks down the plaster.

---

He worked in the ordinary course. He took the rag and wiped the face of the board from end to end, lifting the dust off the chalk without touching the strokes themselves. The wood came clean around them. He rinsed the rag in the bucket and wrung it, then wiped the frame and the two nails where they entered the wood.

At about the sixth hour the carrier came past with his satchel under his arm and stopped to watch. About four people a day go by that step between the fourth hour and the tenth, and none of them stops for long.

**"You do that board more often than the wall," the carrier said.**

**"The wall is not mine to wash," the man of about twenty-seven said. "The board is."**

He did not reach above the top edge at any hour of that morning. The streaks on the plaster stayed as they were, from the roof drip down to the frame. He had washed that stretch once in the whole of this flood and had not gone back to it, and no other hand in this city had gone near it either.

At about the seventh hour he emptied the bucket into the trough and set the bucket down in the low place in the stone at the side of the yard, on its side in the water, where it belongs. He spread the rag on the stone lip of the step with the damp side up. The box of chalk on the step stood with its lid ajar, for the lid does not shut, and he nudged it a finger's width from the drip and left it.

---

At about the ninth hour the light came round the corner of the building and struck the board face on. The chalk strokes stood out white against the wiped wood. Above them the unwashed plaster showed dark at the edges where the water runs.

The man of about twenty-seven stood back on the flags with his hands empty and looked at the morning's work. He picked up the bucket, hung the rag over its rim, and went up toward the room with water for the table.

By the tenth hour the step stood clear, the trough level, the board clean on its two nails. The wall above its top edge kept its streaks untouched, from one side of the frame to the other, and the drip from the roof found the same path it had found all flood.
