# Chapter 0793 — A Barrow Handle In Wet Weather

Adrian Vale took hold of the near handle of a barrow standing in the yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats and tried to lift it clear of the puddle under its wheel. Water ran off the handles and over his wrists, and the wood slipped in his grip.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventy-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. Wind came down the flats and drove rain across the trough, and the low place in the stone at the side of the yard stood brimful. The stack of boards at the back of the yard shone dark, with a length of board lying face up on the top of it.

He wanted that barrow stood back in that yard before the man who works it came in with his own, so that the yard would hold both and the morning could start level. He got his shoulder under the rim and walked it toward the gate, the wheel cutting a line through the standing water.

---

At about the fifth hour the rain thickened. He had got the barrow in through the gate and set it where there was room by the wall when the wheel sank in soft ground and the rainwater standing in the bottom shifted. He set it down, worked his hands further along the handles for a better hold, and shoved it the last arm's length. His coat hung heavy and his boots filled by slow degrees.

The man of about thirty-eight came into the yard at about the seventh hour with his own barrow, his knife in his belt and his sleeves rolled to the elbow. He stopped at the gate when he saw a second barrow standing in his yard with rainwater to the brim of it. He said nothing at first. He walked round the strange barrow once, took it by both handles without asking, and tipped the wet out into the flags to make room to stand his own beside it.

Water slopped over the sides and went down the front of Adrian's coat and over his boots where he stood too near. The man of about thirty-eight stood his own barrow down a hand's breadth further off the wall than he used to, wiped his hands on his sides, and went to his work at the stack without another look.

**"Leave it," he said, not unkindly. "It lives here."**

Adrian stepped back off the flags. He wiped his palms on his coat, where the grit had marked them. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had done right or wrong.

---

By about the ninth hour the yard had emptied of rain for a while and the trough sent a thin sheet over its rim. The man of about thirty-eight had dragged the strange barrow out onto the paving to clear his working room, and worked on round his own, and Adrian went down the paving past it with nothing in his hands. The strange handles kept two pale prints where his grip had dried them, and the wind took even those before the tenth hour.
