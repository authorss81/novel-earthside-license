# Chapter 0791 — The Morning Neither Mouth Finished

The woman of about twenty-nine laid both hands on the wood either side of the open book and held the leaves flat while the light came over the roof. The man of about thirty-nine came up behind her with his board under his arm and stood it on its edge against the near leg.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. The stair-foot door showed its chalk day outward and the bar sat home in the inner sockets. Cold lay in the middle of that floor and the bench hollows held shadow.

People came up in ones and twos and took their places. The man of about thirty-four settled into his hollow with his elbows out. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the far end with her hands at the sides of her dress. The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket by the window and stayed beside it with his rag over his shoulder.

---

At about the fifth hour the keeper turned a leaf and smoothed it with her thumb. The middle column carried five ink strokes with a day set against each in her own hand, none struck through and nothing entered after them. The high wood between the hollows kept the page level.

The man of about thirty-four drew breath as if to speak into the middle of the floor, the way he had spoken on other mornings at that hour. He looked at the keeper's hands and then at the light, and let the breath out without shaping it. He folded his arms instead and leaned back.

At about the sixth hour the woman of about fifty-two shifted half a pace toward the bench, as if she too had a thing to put into the air at that hour. She put one hand out toward the wood and then brought it back to her dress. She looked down at her own feet and said nothing.

The man of about twenty-seven cleared his throat by the window and spoke of the paving past the gate drying. The carrier answered him about canvas.

**"The flags will dry by midday," the man of about twenty-seven said.**

**"They dry from the gate end first," the carrier said.** The talk went on in small pieces and covered the quiet where the other talk had stood.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-nine carried his board the length of the room and laid it flat at the far end, squared with the end of the bench. He stood beside it with his hands in his coat. People walked past without stopping. The pale gap at the foot of the middle column of rates showed for a moment and he did not cover it.

At about the tenth hour he took the board up face-in to his side and went down. The room emptied behind him. The keeper shut the book and bore it in both hands to the long shelf behind the bench, setting it down ahead of the dark shape only her hands ever approach.

The far end kept no warmth after the stair went quiet, and the table jug stood with its water skimmed by dust.
