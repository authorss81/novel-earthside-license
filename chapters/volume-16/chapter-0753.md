# Chapter 0753 — The Trough Filled Higher Than He Found It

Both of the man of about thirty-one's hands were under the near rim of that trough and he turned it, and the water went out over the stone at the side of that yard and spread and stood in a shape that was not there at the fourth hour, and he set the trough back down on the flags and filled it again from the butt until the level stood higher than it had stood at any hour of the day before.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and thirty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, at that salt wharf at the near end of eleven miles of flats. The flap of his satchel was down against his own hip while he did it. There is a bucket in this city and it is in the water at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard. The rag is on the stone lip and is not in the trough. The chalk is in a box on that step and the lid of that box is lying down on its rim, because that lid does not shut.

Nobody in that yard saw him fill it and nobody in that yard saw him empty it either.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-eight came into that yard with his barrow and stood at the near end of that trough and looked at the level of the water in it, and then he bent over and put his own right hand flat into the water and drew it out again and dried it on his own thigh, and the water came over the rim of the trough and ran down the outside of it and went out across the flags.

"**It is higher than it was this morning and the stone at the side of this yard is wet where it was not wet yesterday, and somebody has been at this trough twice and I have not asked anybody about either time and I am not going to now.**"

He filled his own bucket and carried it to the low place and let it out and came back and filled it again, and at about the ninth hour that man of about thirty-one came into that yard with both his own hands empty and stood at the low side of it out of the wind and looked at the stone and at the level and neither of them said one word to the other at any hour of that day.

---

Over that market, in that room up the stair at the back of the store's end of that market, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put both his own hands flat on the wood of that bench at the far end of it and gave that number out loud into that room the way he gives it out on most mornings of this flood.

"**There are about nine of us in this room this morning. I have put that number into the air in here on more mornings of this flood than I have said anything else, and I have never once written it down, and I have never asked anybody where the first of them came from.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of that. The piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket was against his own chest and did not come out of it at any hour of that day. Nobody asked him one question about it and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book had it open on the wood of that bench in front of her and did not look up off that page and was not asked anything about the number he had said.

On the shelf behind that bench there stands a second slate, and it stands behind the register and not on a shelf of its own, and a day has been cut across the head of it and nothing whatever has ever been put under that day. It has never been lifted off that shelf, never turned over, and never written on, at any hour of this flood, and the woman who keeps the register is the only hand that has gone near that shelf.

---

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-eight came into that yard once more, and that trough was standing a hand's breadth back from where it had stood at the fourth hour, and the stone at the low place in the wall was drying at the edges and wet in the middle, and there was nothing on the ground of that yard to say that any of it had been done there twice.
