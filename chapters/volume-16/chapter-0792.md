# Chapter 0792 — What Saturday Left On Sunday

The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket down on the flags by the window and laid his rag across the rim to dry. The woman of about twenty-nine opened the book at the near end of the bench and put her thumbs on the lower corners of the page.

The fourth hour had come on the four hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. Light lay thin across the middle of the floor. Below, the door at the stair foot carried its chalk day on the outer face, and the bar rested inside its sockets.

The man of about thirty-nine climbed with his board under his arm and set it upright by the near leg. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The woman of about fifty-two held her place at the far end, her hands hanging by her dress. The carrier stopped short of the bench with his satchel under his arm and went to the wall.

---

At about the sixth hour the keeper turned a leaf though the one before it was not finished. The five strokes down the middle column stood each with a day against it, none struck, nothing added after. She kept her hands on the wood all the same.

The room talked of ordinary things. The man of about thirty-four spoke of boards at the store's end wanting a wipe before rain. The man of about twenty-seven answered about the trough taking water over its rim in the night.

**"It took water twice in the night," the man of about twenty-seven said.**

**"It will take it again before dark," the man of about thirty-four said.** Nobody raised a voice. Nobody put anything to the bench at large.

What had been settled on the Saturday stood in the way they did not settle it. At about the seventh hour a silence came where a morning sound had been, and two people shifted at once and then were still. The woman of about fifty-two looked toward the far end and away. The man of about thirty-nine kept his hand on the face of his board and said nothing whatever to anybody.

The carrier shifted his satchel higher under his arm. The keeper smoothed the page. The talk started again further down the bench, about cord prices and a loose trestle pin, and went on without touching the middle of the floor.

---

At about the ninth hour the board went down the room and lay flat at the far end for the hour, face up and squared. At about the tenth the man took it up again and went down that stair with it against his side. The keeper closed the book and laid it on the back shelf ahead of the shaded form no hands but hers ever near.

Rain had got into the stairwell by then, and the bottom step shone where feet had gone down it.
