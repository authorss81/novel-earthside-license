# Chapter 0759 — One Withy And A Piece Of Rope

Both of the man of about sixty's hands were on the near rim of his own handcart and he lifted that rim up off the flags and looked under the cart and put it down again and stood with his own two hands on the handles of it.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, out past the last named house in that city. There is one handcart in this city and a hazel mallet lies along its side with its head bound in cord, and the coil of rope that has been used is not on the cart where it has lain every morning of this flood.

The fence stands along the top of that shut road and it is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound onto them with the same cord. Nobody in this city has ever measured it and no post in it has moved and nobody has gone along it with a thing to measure it, and the man of about sixty put it up and he was not sent and nobody is going to ask him why.

---

He went down to the ground and walked about forty paces along the top of that road with his own two hands empty and stood and looked at the fence and then walked back past the handcart and along the road again and stood in another place and looked at it.

At about the seventh hour he found it. A length of it about as long as his own forearm was hanging on the near end of that fence and one of the eleven withies was bound on to a post with it instead of with the cord, and the knot was not one he had tied, and the cut end of it was clean and had been cut off something and not off a coil.

"**I put this fence up and I bound every one of those withies on with the same cord and this morning one of them is on with a piece of a rope off somebody's cart and I am not going to take it off, and I am not going to be asked about it by anybody, and I am not going to say whose rope it is.**"

Nobody was out along that road at the seventh hour and nobody heard him say that and nobody in that house out past the last named house was told any of it, and the man of about fifty-seven stood in his own doorway with his own two hands on his own knees and looked at him go past and looked down at them.

He cut the piece off that hanging length where the clean end of it was and put it in his own inside pocket, and then he took his own two hands off that withy and put the cord it had been bound with back on where the other piece of it had been, and he tied the eleven withies on that fence back in the order and with the knot he keeps them in. He took his own right thumb and forefinger round each of those knots in turn and drew them tight, and one of them had been tight already and gave a little under his own thumb before it stopped, and he left that one where it had got to. He worked from the near end of that fence to the far end of it and he did not measure it and he did not move a post, and at the far end of it he stopped and stood and looked at the top of the shut road beyond the last of those posts, which nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-eight came up that stair at the back of the store's end of that market with his own barrow and his own knife in his belt and stopped at the far end of that bench and nobody asked him what he had brought up it with and he said nothing.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book had it open on the wood of that bench in front of her, and she put one thing out loud into the middle of that floor in the ordinary way of that morning.

"**There are about nine of us in this room and I have been keeping that figure in my own head since the beginning of this flood and I have never once said it out loud in here, and I have said it now, and nobody has asked me one word about it.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for it, and nobody improved on a word of it, and nobody asked her where that figure had come from, and nobody in that room took a side on whether it was right or whether it was wrong. The five figures standing down the middle of the column on that open page were not struck, and none was taken out of them, and no sixth went into them at any hour of that day.

---

The man of about sixty put his own two hands back on the handles of his own handcart at about the tenth hour and stood at the top of that road and looked along that fence for about the length of a minute, and one of the eleven withies on it was bound on with a piece of a rope that was not his, and the coil of his own rope was still on the cart where he had found the other end of it hanging, and nobody had asked him and nobody was going to.
