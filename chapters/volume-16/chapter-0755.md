# Chapter 0755 — He Did Not Go Along The Fence

Both of the man of about fifty-seven's hands were on his own knees where he was standing in his own doorway, and he took them off his knees and put the flats of them on the top rail of his own door and stood there that way and looked out along that shut road.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, out past the last named house in that city, in the doorway of his own house, and there is nobody else in that house and there has not been on any morning of this flood.

Over the back of a chair in that room lies a length of new rope. It came into this city with him and it has lain on that chair every morning of this flood and it is as good as the day it arrived, and no hand of his and no hand of anybody else's has gone near it, and nobody in this city has ever asked him where that fence is and nobody has ever told him.

---

He stood at that door from the fourth hour to about the seventh hour with his hands on his knees again, and at about the sixth hour he went into the yard behind that house and came back in with a length of cord in his own two hands and a length of iron with a hole in the end of it, and he worked at the gate of that yard for about an hour with the gate flat on the ground on the flags in front of the step.

He got the hole in the end of the iron a little larger with a stone and he got the cord through it and he pulled the cord up until the gate came a hand's width off the ground at its near end. He set the gate down on its near end and stood over it and looked at the cord and the hole and then took the whole of it back off and coiled the cord and hung the iron on a peg inside the door and shut the yard gate on its own hinge with nothing new on it, and that gate had hung crooked since the flood came and it hung crooked at the tenth hour of that day as it had hung at the fourth.

At about the seventh hour he went out onto the step of his own house and pulled the door to behind him and went down to the ground and turned left along the top of that road, and he walked about forty paces. He stopped twice on the way and stood still both times, and the second time he stopped he put his own two hands on his knees again and looked at the ground in front of his own boots for about the length of a minute, and then he stood up straight and went on, and he turned back before he came to that fence.

Along the top of that shut road there is a fence, and it is made of sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound onto them out of one cord. Nobody has measured that fence, no post of it has been moved since it was put up, and nobody has walked it with anything in his hands to do either of those things. And there is no person in this city anybody could send to look at the road past the far end of it.

---

He came back along that road at about the ninth hour with his own two hands empty and went up onto the step of his own house and opened his own door and went in and shut it behind him and stood in the room with the rope on the chair.

He did not take the rope up. He did not touch the chair. He put his own two hands on the back of that chair for about the length of a minute and left them there, and then he went and got the length of cord and the length of iron off the peg inside the door and carried them out onto the step and set them down on the flags there, and then he took them up again and hung them on the peg, and the rope lay on that chair where it had lain at the fourth hour while he was doing both of those things with his hands three feet away from it.

He had gone out along the top of that road that morning for about forty paces and had turned back before the fence, and the gate of his own yard hung crooked as it had hung at the fourth hour, and he had said one word to nobody about any of it, and there was nobody in that house to hear whether he said it or not.
