# Chapter 0798 — What A Morning Is For

The woman of about twenty-nine set the book open on the wood at the near end of the bench and laid her hands on either side of the page. The man of about thirty-nine came up with his board under his arm and stood it against the near leg, his right hand resting on the face.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-third day of the Bare Month, a Monday. Daylight filled the middle of that floor. Down at the stair foot the door carried its chalk day on the outer face, and the bar sat in its sockets within.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The carrier stood by the wall with his satchel under his arm. The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket by the window. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the far end with her hands at the sides of her dress.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-nine moved down the bench to the near end beside the keeper's page, to make room for feet going past. The woman of about fifty-two came along the bench after him, step by step, until she stood close at his shoulder where nobody else along that bench could hear what passed between them.

She waited until he looked up off the board. Her voice stayed low, for him and not for the room.

**"What is a morning for, when the only thing anybody is going to use it for is to have been in a room in it?"**

He looked at her for a long moment with his hand still on the face of his board. He did not answer. He took his hand off the board and put it in his coat, and turned his eyes back to the middle of the floor. Nobody along that bench had heard a word of it. Nobody thanked him for not answering and nobody asked him why.

The keeper turned a leaf. The five strokes down the middle column stood each with a day against it. Talk went on further down the bench about the rain on the flats, and neither of the two at the near end joined it.

---

At about the tenth hour he took his board up face-in to his side and went down that stair. The keeper shut the book and laid it on the back shelf ahead of the shaded form. The near end held the print of two pairs of boots turned toward one another, drying side by side.
