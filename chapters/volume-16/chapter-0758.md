# Chapter 0758 — The Book Carried Twice

Both of the woman of about twenty-nine's hands were under the open book on the wood of that bench at the near end of it, and she lifted it off that wood and carried it the length of that room and set it down square with the front edge of it on the shelf behind that bench, and it stood there with the second slate behind it on that same shelf.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in that room over a market. About nine people came into that room over the course of that morning and not one of them said out loud how many of them there were, and that has not been done on every morning of this flood before and it was not noticed by anybody except a woman who keeps a register.

That book is ruled in three columns. Down the middle of the page there are five figures standing one under another in the order they were made in, and a day is set against each of the five, and not one of the five is struck through; nothing has ever been entered after them and nothing has ever been taken out. In the column on the right of that page there is one word, near the head of it, and the rest of that page is empty. That word is not in her hand. It went there in a hand that is not hers on a day that is not one of this flood, and no mouth in this city has ever been told what a word in that column is for.

---

She stood in front of that shelf with both her own hands on the wood of it for about the length of a minute and then she took the book up off that shelf in her own two hands and carried it the length of that room again and set it down on that bench at the near end of it, and she had not put it in the same place it had been in at the fourth hour.

"**The light comes over the roof above that window at about the ninth hour and it comes across this floor to that bench and it stands on that page until the middle of the afternoon, and there is a day written against each of those five figures in a hand that is not mine on one of them, and I am not having the sun on it.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for that and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody asked her whose hand it is that is not hers, because nobody in that room has ever asked her that and nobody in this city has.

The man of about twenty-seven came up that stair at about the sixth hour with his own bucket in his own hand and stopped at the far end of that bench and looked at that shelf for a while and did not say one word at any hour of that day, and nobody in that room said one word to him about it either, and he carried his own bucket back down that stair again at about the seventh hour.

---

At about the ninth hour the light came over the roof above the one window of that room and came across that floor and reached the near end of that bench and stopped a hand's width short of the book, because the book had been moved and the far end of that bench had been moved into instead, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was not in that room at the ninth hour and had not been in it at any hour of that day.

She put both her own hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page and went on with the morning. Behind her on that same shelf a slate stands with a day cut into the head of it and nothing whatever under the day at all. Nobody has picked it up, nobody has turned it over, and nothing has ever been written on it, and the only hand that has gone near that shelf in this flood is hers.
