# Chapter 0769 — Nothing Was Said To Him All Day

Both of the man of about thirty-nine's hands were flat on the face of his own board where it stood on its edge against the leg of that bench at the far end of it, and he did not take them off it at any hour of that morning.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and fifty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in that room up the stair at the back of that market. Nine of its own people came and went in there over the morning and not one of them said one word to him at any hour of it. He carried that board up that stair at the fourth hour the way he carries it up the stair, and he put it where he puts it, and he sat down at the far end of the bench with his own boots in the gap between that bench and the wall along the far side.

That register lay open on the wood of the bench at the near end of it from the fourth hour to the tenth hour and the light came over the roof above that one window at about the ninth hour and came across that floor and reached that bench and stood on the face of the board for about the length of a minute before it went off it.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of about fifty-two stood with her own hands at the sides of her own dress and put that figure out into the middle of that floor the way she has always gone at it, and about nine of them stood in the room at that hour.

"**Nine of us, most mornings, every morning of this flood, and it has gone out of my mouth on more of them than any other thing I have ever said in this room.**"

Nobody in that room improved on one word of that. Not one of them looked along the bench at the far end of it while she said it, and nothing was said to the man of about thirty-nine about it or about anything else, and he said one word back to nobody about any part of it.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-eight carried a length of board up out of the yard below and stood it on its edge against the wall on the near side of the room, on the spot where he had stood his own barrow on the Sunday before. He went back down again and nobody in that room asked him one word about what he had brought it up there for or where it was going.

---

At about the ninth hour he shifted that board out from under the light that was standing on it with the flats of his own boot, about a foot along that bench toward the near end of it, and he put his own right hand flat on the face of it where it stood and left it there.

The woman of about twenty-nine turned a page over in that register at about the same hour and did not look along the bench, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went out of the room and came back into it and went out of it again and never said one word to anybody about where he had been.

At about the tenth hour the board was standing against that bench a foot further along than it had been at the ninth hour, where the light had been standing on it, and the flats of that man's own two hands were on the face of it, and not one word had been said to him at any hour of that day.
