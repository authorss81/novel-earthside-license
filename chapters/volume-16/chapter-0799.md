# Chapter 0799 — One Thing Done Without Saying

The man of about twenty-seven took up his bucket from the low place in the stone at the side of the yard and set it on the flags to test the handle. Dawn stood grey over the market and the trough held last night's rain to the brim.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. The store's end of the market lay empty with wet canvas sagging. On the step stood the box of chalk with its lid ajar, the way it always stands, for the lid does not shut.

He worked in the ordinary course. He lifted the bucket and poured a steady stream along the stone lip of the step, washing the night's grit down into the flags. He wrung his rag into the water and wiped the face of the board on two nails above the step, lifting the dust off round the chalk strokes without touching them. He rinsed the rag, spread it on the lip with the damp side up, and set the bucket back down in the low place on its side.

---

Upstairs the room filled without the old morning sound. The woman of about twenty-nine opened the book at the near end of the bench and put her hands to the wood. The man of about thirty-nine stood his board against the near leg. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the far end. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. Nobody put anything into the middle of the floor at the fifth hour, and the talk that came forward was of the weather and the mending of a trestle leg.

**"That leg will hold another week," the man of about thirty-four said.**

**"It held last week," the man of about twenty-seven said. "A week is enough."**

At about the seventh hour the keeper turned a leaf. The five strokes down the middle column stood each with a day against it, none struck, nothing added after. The carrier shifted his satchel under his arm. The man of about thirty-nine kept his hand on his board and said nothing to anybody, and nobody said one word to him.

What was done that morning was done at the step below, where water ran clear off clean wood and a lip held its damp for the next man. Nobody above knew a hand had been on the wood below, and nobody below asked anything of anybody above.

---

By about the tenth hour the market step stood clear with the trough level beside it. The rag lay where hands had spread it, heavy with clean water, and the chalk box sat a finger's width out of the drip with its lid rocking in the wind.
