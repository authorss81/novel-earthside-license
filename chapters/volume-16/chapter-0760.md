# Chapter 0760 — The Only Thing That Room Says

Both of the woman of about fifty-two's hands were flat on the wood of that bench at the far end of it, a forearm apart from each other, and she kept them there through the whole of the fifth hour and did not take them off.

It was about the fourth hour on the four hundred and forty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in that room over a market. About nine people were in it over the course of that morning, and the bar that had been lying on the flags inside the room at the bottom of that stair since the sixth hour of the Monday before was back in its sockets on the far side of that door, and the door was shut, and nobody in that room had put it back and nobody in that room knew who had.

"**That figure is the only thing this room has ever said about itself. Nine of us, most mornings, in somebody's mouth, going out of this room in about nine mouths an hour and coming back in about nine. It has never been on a page. It has never been on any of the seven sheets that have gone up that road. It has never been read out of anything and it has never been put down anywhere, and I have been one of the mouths it goes out of every morning of this flood and I am saying that out loud this morning because I have not got anybody to say it to instead of me.**"

No one in that room thanked her for it. Nobody improved on one word of what she said, and nobody argued with her about it, and nobody in that room said that she had it right and nobody said that she had it wrong. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book was at the near end of that bench with her own hands flat on the wood on either side of that open page and did not look up off it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf brought his barrow up that stair at about the seventh hour and took it down again at about the eighth, and nobody in that room said a word to him about either of those two things. Then he came up that stair at about the eighth hour with that board under his own arm and set the flat of his own right hand on the wood of that bench at the near end of it, and he stood there while about nine people went past him on that stair and none of them said anything to him about what he was carrying.

"**I have been the mouth that figure goes out of more often than anybody in this room has, and I have said it in here on more mornings of this flood than I have said anything else, and it has never once been written down anywhere I have ever been, and I am saying this morning that I have noticed that, and I am not saying what I am going to do about it.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what he was going to do about it. Nobody thanked him for saying that he had noticed and nobody improved on one word of it, and the piece of chalk in the man of about thirty-four's own inside breast pocket was against his own chest and did not come out of it at any hour of that day.

---

At about the tenth hour the woman of about fifty-two took her own two hands off the wood of that bench and put them down at the sides of her own dress, where she keeps them, and that bench stood empty at the far end of it.

The register was on that bench at the near end of it with its three columns open and the five figures standing down the middle of them and a day against each, none struck, no sixth entered and none taken out, and behind that book on the same shelf the second slate stood with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. And that figure was in about nine mouths and on no page at all, and there was nothing in that room that would carry one word of what any of them had said out loud about it up the eleven miles of flats or anywhere else.
