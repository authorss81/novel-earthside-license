# Chapter 0786 — The Door At The Foot Of The Stair

Adrian Vale came up the paving to the foot of that stair before the fourth hour and put the flat of his right hand on the outer face of the door. The wood was cold, and the day in chalk stood above his hand with clean wood beneath it.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventy-first day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The market had not filled. The stair above him was dark, and the bar sat in its sockets on the far side of the door, holding it shut from within.

He wanted that door standing open through the morning, so that anybody going past on the paving could see up the stair into the room. He pushed it with his palm, and it gave a finger's width and stopped hard against the bar. He put his shoulder to it, gently, and the bar held.

---

He looked about for something to hold it with. A loose stone lay by the bottom step, about the size of his fist. He set it against the door's edge and pushed the door to it. The door stayed where the bar let it stay, which was barely open, with a slit of dark stair showing.

At about the fifth hour the man of about twenty-seven came down the paving with his bucket full and his rag over his shoulder, bound for the yard at the near end of the flats. He found the door standing off its frame by a finger's width with a stone at its foot, and a stranger with his hand still on the wood.

He stopped on the step rather than go round. He shifted the bucket to his other hand to get past on the narrow side, and the water in it slopped over the rim and went down the front of his coat and over his boot. He set the bucket down hard on the paving and shook his foot.

**"You will have that stone under somebody's ankle," he said, not unkindly.**

Adrian took his hand off the door. The man of about twenty-seven kicked the stone clear with the side of his boot, and the door swung to on its own weight. From inside, at the top of the stair, came the sound of the bar being lifted and set back, as someone made sure of it for the morning.

**"It shuts from the inside," the man of about twenty-seven said. "It has shut that way all flood."**

Adrian nodded and stepped back off the step. He wiped his palm on his coat, where the dust of the door had marked it. The man of about twenty-seven picked his bucket up again and went on toward the yard, leaving a trail of wet prints across the paving.

---

By the sixth hour the door stood shut as it had stood, with the bar in its sockets on the far side. People went past it all morning without looking up, the way they always go past. The man of about thirty-nine came down the paving with his board under his arm, tried the door with two fingers out of habit, found it shut, and went up the stair behind the keeper.

Upstairs the morning went on with the door shut below it. The book lay open at the near end of the bench, and the board went from the near leg to the far end and back under an arm, and nobody above knew a hand had been on the wood below.

At about the tenth hour the man of about twenty-seven came back past with his bucket empty and his boot still dark from the morning's wet. The stone lay where his kick had sent it, up against the wall, out of the tread of feet. The chalk day stood above the shut door, and the clean wood beneath it held no mark of a palm.
