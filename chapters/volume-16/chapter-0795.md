# Chapter 0795 — Hands On The Back Of The Stool

The woman of about twenty-nine set the book open on the wood at the near end of the bench and laid her hands on either side of the page. The man of about thirty-four came in from the market and took his hollow without speaking.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Friday. Light stood in the middle of that floor and had not reached the bench wood. Down below, the stair-foot door bore its chalk day outward, with bare timber under the marks, and the bar sat in its sockets within.

The man of about thirty-one came up with nothing in his hands and stood at the far end. The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket by the window. The woman of about fifty-two waited near the side of the bench, her hands at the sides of her dress. The corner where a barrow used to stand against the wall had been bare for days.

---

The morning went on in the ordinary way. At about the sixth hour the keeper turned a leaf. The five ink strokes down the middle column stood each with a day against it, none struck, nothing added after. Talk went along the bench about the weather on the flats and a trestle that wanted mending, and died and started again.

At about the seventh hour the woman of about fifty-two crossed to the stool at the side of the bench and put both her own hands on the back of it. She stood there a while with her weight on the wood, looking at the far end where nobody sat.

**"The man of about thirty-nine is not in this room," she said to the middle of the floor. "I do not know since which morning he has not been in it."**

Nobody asked her how she knew. The keeper kept her eyes on the page. The man of about thirty-four took his hands off the bench and put them in his coat.

After a while the man of about thirty-four said he did not know, in his own words. The carrier said he did not know, in his. The man of about twenty-seven said he did not know, and the keeper said she did not know, each in different words, which was about four of the people along that bench. About four more of them said nothing at all. Nothing in that room was lifted or moved or answered before the light went.

---

At about the tenth hour the room emptied in the ordinary way. The keeper shut the book and carried it the length of the room to the shelf behind the bench. The stool stood where hands had held it, turned a finger's width on its legs by the pressure.
