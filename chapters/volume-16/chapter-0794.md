# Chapter 0794 — A Board Squared On A Stack

The man of about thirty-eight laid both hands on the sides of a length of board and worked it up the side of the stack at the back of the yard. His barrow stood off the wall where he leaves it, and the trough spilled a thin sheet across the flags.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and seventy-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The flats stretched grey beyond the gate and the wind flattened the puddles. The board on the top of the stack lay face up with two lines of grey grit across it and a clean piece at the near edge where the wood showed bare.

He got the length up onto the top and shoved it square with the stack, the ends flush and the sides true. He stepped back down and walked round it twice to look at the seating. He did not fetch a rule and did not send for one. He sighted along the near edge with one eye shut and nudged the far corner with his thumb until the shadow under it ran even.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about twenty-seven came through with his bucket and stopped to watch. The carrier passed on the paving with his satchel under his arm and wished them the morning, and both men answered him together.

**"That sits well," the man of about twenty-seven said.**

**"It sits where I put it," the man of about thirty-eight said, and left it at that.**

The man of about twenty-seven filled his bucket at the low place in the stone and went on toward the market with it swinging. The man of about thirty-eight ran his thumb along the clean piece at the near edge and then took his hand away without wiping anything. The two lines of grit stayed where they were. The board stayed where he had set it, square on the top, with nobody having measured it at any hour.

At about the seventh hour he took up his barrow by both handles and worked it out through the gate. The yard stood empty behind him with the water running off the flags in sheets.

---

By about the tenth hour the weather had cleared off the flats and the stack stood drying in bands. His knife stayed in his belt untouched all morning with rain beading along the hilt, and the length lay true at both ends above it.
