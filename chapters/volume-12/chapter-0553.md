# Chapter 0553 — A Count Of Papers That Two Mouths Did Not Agree On

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her own right hand flat on the table under the one window at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and thirty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, on the near side of the sheet that came up that road in a satchel with a flap yesterday, and she left it there, and then she took it off again and put it on the other side. The door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room has a date in chalk on it and has not had a name under a date since the water came. Nine people were in that room by the seventh hour and every one of them came up that stair on their own two feet.

---

There are six documents in this flood that have come down that road or gone up it, and three of them have four places ruled under a heading and three of them have a single place ruled under a heading with a figure in it. One of the three of one place was read out in this room a long time ago and has never been returned and has never been withdrawn and is standing on the shelf behind that bench at the height of a person's shoulder. One of the three of one place went out of this city eight days ago, measured from the day it went out, in a satchel with a flap, and it is not in this city and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back. The third of them is on the table under the one window with its one place already filled.

The one on that table and the one on that shelf are in this room together this morning. Neither of them is laid beside the other, neither of them is in one hand, and neither of them is compared out loud in a mouth in this room, and about four people in this room have noticed that there are two of them and about four have not, and not one of the nine has said so.

---

She counted them out loud. She put her own finger on the edge of the sheet on the table without lifting it, and then she walked round the end of the bench and put her own hand flat on the edge of the one on the shelf without lifting that one either, and she came back to the head of that table and said the number.

"That is two."

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"There is a book on that shelf as well and a book is a paper and you have counted a paper off that shelf and not counted the one in your own two hands this morning."

"**I have counted two sheets and I have not counted that book and that book is not one of the six and it is not one of the three of them, and I have said the number I have got and I am not going to say it a second time this morning because I have said it once.**"

Nobody in that room improved on her. Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody in that room said that he was right, and nobody in that room told that man at the step that a book is not a sheet, and about four people in that room have worked out that there are two sheets of that one shape in this room and about four have not, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went back to the head of that table and put her own hand down on the wood and said the same number again a little while later to a woman who had come in at the back of the room, and it was the same number, and the man near the step did not say his number a second time and did not withdraw it, and nobody in that room at any hour of that morning said which of the two numbers that were said out loud in it was the one anybody should be counting.

---

The register stands on that shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and its cover shut and it was not opened at any hour of that morning. Five figures stand in the column of that book that holds figures, one under another in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them in her own hand, and that column is full from the head of it to the foot of it. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of the page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom.

Behind that register on the same shelf a second slate stands on its edge with its face in to the wall, and a day is cut across the head of it, and under that day the slate is bare. It has been bare every morning of this flood. It was not picked up and not turned over and not written on at any hour of this morning, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and her own hand did not go near that slate either.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench with his hands on his knees and the satchel with the flap on the floor under it and the flap open and nothing in it. Nobody in that room asked him a second question about the day before and he did not offer a second thing, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the window with his own board under his arm and the two fingers of bare board along the bottom of the middle column of it not filled, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not come away from it.

The light came off that table before the ninth hour and the sheet on it was still face up on the wood with its one place showing, and the shelf behind that bench was still holding what it holds, and behind that register on that shelf the second slate stood on its edge with its face in to the wall and a day cut across the head of it, and the register beside it was not open and had not been opened, and nobody in that room could say which of the two numbers that were said out loud in it that morning was the right one and nobody in that room said which one was.
