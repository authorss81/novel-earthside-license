# Chapter 0565 — Four Hundred Yards Of Flags And Nobody Counting Them

The man of about twenty-seven came out of the store at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and fiftieth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, with his own bucket in his own right hand and his own rag over the same arm, and he went along the flags at that end of that market from the near gate for about two hundred yards, and he did not go any further than that, and the two hundred yards he went over were his own and everybody in that row has known for a year that they are his own and nobody in that row has ever said so to him.

The flags of that row run about four hundred yards from the near gate to the channel, and the walk is the walk and the four hundred yards is a walk and not a figure about a place, and there is a gutter down the middle of them that takes the water off that row into the channel. The board is on its two nails above the trough against the wall at the far end of the row and the rag is on the stone lip of that step, and the wall above the top edge of that board is not washed and no second person has put a hand on it at any hour.

---

Adrian Vale had the trough at about the seventh hour with the bucket in it and the rag still on the stone lip of the step behind him, and he went up that row from the channel end at the pace of a man carrying a thing that is about as heavy as a small dog, and the two hundred yards the man of about twenty-seven had been over were already done and he did not do them again, and about four people at that end of that market were in that row in the ordinary course and about four were not.

He put the bucket into the gutter at the gutter's edge and pushed it along the bottom of it with the rim of it against the silt until it was full, and he carried that bucket up onto the flags and tipped it into the trough and did not put it over the lip. He did it nine times along that row, and the water going into that trough was about the colour of the water coming out of the gutter, and the grit came out of the joints of those flags in front of the trough as it went.

The man of about twenty-seven was at the far end of that row with his own back to the wall and his own bucket in his own hand when Adrian Vale came up to him, and a woman at the near end of that row said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"You have been going up and down that row since the light came off the wall and that water has not moved an inch."

"**There is no wind coming off the flats today and these stones are not going to be dry by the fourth hour, and the fourth hour is a long way off yet.**"

Nobody acted on that. Nobody in that row improved on him and nobody in that row thanked him and nobody in that row said that he was right, and nobody in that row asked him a question, and the man of about twenty-seven picked his own bucket up and carried it to the channel at the end of that row and back again, and did not use that trough and did not ask to use it.

---

At about the eighth hour Adrian Vale came to the near gate of that row with that trough and set it down on the flags where the four hundred yards came to an end, because the channel is beyond that gate and carrying a full trough through a gate on the flags is a thing a man can do in about as long as it takes a man to walk from that gate to the channel and back. He set it down and he went to the channel with the bucket and he came back about as long as it takes a man to walk from the channel to the near gate and back, and the trough stood on the low flags at that gate the whole time he was gone. The gutter comes down that row to that gate and the trough stood across the gutter and the low flags at that end of the row are the ones the water stands on, and the water came along that row behind that trough and went up against the stones and stood in a flat sheet about as deep as a thumb across the front of three stalls. A man came out of the middle one of them at about the eighth hour and carried two crates and a basket of stuff off the flags onto his own trestle in about as long as it takes a man to walk from the near gate to the end of that row and back, and he was wet to the knee doing it, and he did not know why the water was there and nobody in that row told him.

---

Nobody in that row said a word to Adrian Vale about any of it. He was at the channel end of that row with the bucket in his own hand when it happened and he did not know about it, and nobody in that row said that he was right.

The man of about twenty-seven went along his own two hundred yards again at about the ninth hour and washed his own board at the fourth hour of that morning and the three sets of figures on it came out of the wet as clean as they went in, and the box of chalk on that step stood with its lid open and about a thumb's length of the store's chalk in it and neither that chalk nor the piece in another man's own inside breast pocket was used at any hour of that day.

Nobody counted anything on that row on that day. The water at that near gate went down on its own by the time the light came off the flags, and the stock of those three stalls went back onto the flags in the order it had come off, and the man in the middle of those three put his own crates down on the same flat stone he had lifted them off and stood looking at that gutter for a while without going down to it.
