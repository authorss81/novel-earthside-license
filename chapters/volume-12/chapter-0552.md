# Chapter 0552 — A Sheet On A Table With One Place Already Filled

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and thirty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, with the satchel with the flap under his arm and the flap down, and he put the satchel on the table under the one window with the strap still round it and took his own hands off it and stood back from the table with his arms hanging. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room by that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything.

---

Nobody in that room opened the satchel. The man who had brought it did not open it either, and nobody in that room asked him what was in it, and he did not say. The strap came off and the flap came up and out of it came a single sheet folded twice with a bone, and he put that sheet on the wood under the one window and turned it about once with his own two hands so that the top of it was away from the window, and the bone went down on the table beside it, and the satchel went under the bench with the flap hanging open.

The sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there is one place and there has never been a second one, and the one place is not empty. A figure of this city is in it, in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that room, and the figure is right, and there is nothing else on that sheet anywhere. The heading at the top of it is a heading. It is not a sentence and it does not say what the figure under it is a figure of, and there is no place on that sheet for the person who made it and there never was one.

Nobody in that room can check that figure. About nine people were standing round that table within the hour and every one of them looked at that one place, and about four of them had already been in this city on the morning that a figure of this city was made on it by a person on foot, and about four had not, and not one of the nine said one word out loud about checking it.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the satchel under the bench.

"You have not opened one in nine years and you have not opened this one."

"**I know what is in this one and I did not open it on the road and I am not going to open it in this room, and the two things I have just said are the whole of what I know about it and one of them is more than anybody else in this city is going to get out of anybody.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put both hands flat on the top of the bench in front of him and left them there for the rest of that morning. Nobody in that room asked him where the sheet came from and nobody in that room asked him who had sent it and he did not say, and about four people in that room had worked out that it came up that road on the quarterly round and about four had not.

---

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Then what is it for."

Nobody answered her. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came to the head of that table and put the flat of her own hand down on the wood on the near side of that sheet, palm on the wood, and left it there, and she looked at the heading at the top of it and then at the one place under the heading and then at the heading again, and she did not say a word about it in that room, and about four people in that room had worked out by the time the light had moved across that table that this room is going to be asked something about that figure before that figure is a week old, and about four had not.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and nineteen rates stand on that board in three columns with the rows going across, and along the bottom of the middle column of it there is bare board about as wide as two fingers and nothing whatever in it. Two stalls along and one row down from that window, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall is at his own trestle with his own board standing face in against it and the bare back of it out in the row, and his own hand is flat over his own inside breast pocket. The box of chalk on the step below that window has its lid standing open and about a thumb's length showing in it, and that chalk is the store's and not his, and neither of those two chalks was used at any hour of that morning.

He went down that stair at the eleventh hour with the satchel empty and the flap of it hanging open, and the sheet stayed on that table under the one window, and nobody in that room put a hand on it after the light went off the flags.
