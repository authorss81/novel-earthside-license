# Chapter 0584 — One Figure Carried Out Of A Book By A Hand That Did Not Make It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register uncapped a pen over the table under the one window at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and sixty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and put the flat of her own left hand on a clean sheet of paper and wrote on it with her own right hand. The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever gone under it. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before the pen was uncapped, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of the bench with his own hands on the wood of it and was not asked anything by anybody in that room at any hour of that morning.

---

What she wrote was one figure, and she wrote it out of the head of the column of the book on the shelf behind that bench, which is the figure that went out of this city in a satchel with a flap on the five hundred and forty-fifth day of this flood, and she wrote it in one figure and wrote it large and wrote it on the middle of that clean sheet, and the figure she wrote is written out on no page in four hundred miles. The hand that wrote it is not the hand that made it, and the person who made it is not in that room and has not been in that room since before this flood, and she said so out loud herself before she began.

"**I am copying this and I did not make it, and the person who made it is not in this room and is not going to be thanked for it by anybody here, and I am copying it so that a room can see what one of ours looks like in a single person's hand.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and a man at the step said the thing that is said, to the sheet.

"So we can check it against the one that came down that road."

"**We cannot check it against anything, because there is nothing in this city to check it with, and I did not copy it to be checked, I copied it because a figure on a page in a book is a figure that belongs to a book and a figure on a sheet is a figure that belongs to a sheet.**"

---

What the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said to that sheet was what he says to a page, and the whole of it took him about four seconds.

"**Nobody in four hundred miles is going to see whose hand that is, and the two of those facts are on the same piece of paper and neither of them is.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the pen down flat beside that sheet and did not answer him and did not look at him.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was short.

"**It is a figure of this city and it is right and it was not made by anybody in that office nine hundred miles off, and I am not going to be asked about it and I am not going to say anything else about it this morning.**"

Nobody in that room asked him. Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room argued with him, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had said two things out loud in a room in this flood and both of them had been about a figure of this city and neither of them had been about where that figure came from.

---

About four people in that room had worked out by that hour that a figure of this city has to be a figure of whatever a person stands in the middle of when they are counting it, and about four had worked out that it has to be a figure of what a person can get into a book afterwards, and about four had worked out that it has to be a figure of what somebody up that road is going to be handed, and about four had not worked out any of the three and were still at the table looking at the sheet. Not one of the nine said which of those four was right and not one of the four who had worked it out said it out loud, and the four are four different answers and no two of them are wrong.

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and every one of them was a sheet somebody in this city sat down and wrote, and this city has not written one since, and there is no eighth coming, and the clean sheet on that table that morning is not one of the seven and is not going to be an eighth and is not going anywhere at all. The sheet with the heading on it and the one place ruled under it was lying on the same table from the morning it came up that stair, and it was not laid beside the clean sheet, it was not in the same hand as the clean sheet, and nobody in that room said out loud one word about how the two of them were the same kind of document or a different kind of document.

The store's chalk in its box on the step below that window was not used that morning, because a copy is made in ink, and the piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man at the far end of that bench was not used either, and the box stood open all morning with its lid up. On the outside wall at the end of that market the store's board was on its two nails carrying three sets of figures of which none had been rubbed out and not one of the three was carrying a line of words under it, and about four people at that end of that market walked to the end of that row in the ordinary course and read one of them and went on.

---

The back of that clean sheet is blank from the top of it to the bottom and it stayed blank all morning, and nothing has ever been written on the back of a piece of paper in this city in this flood and no hand in that room turned it over to look, and the one figure that was carried out of a book that morning is on the face of it and not on the other, and a person four hundred miles off will get the face of it and will never know the back of it was there.
