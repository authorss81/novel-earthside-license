# Chapter 0580 — What Goes In The One Place And Has Not Been Written

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of both her own hands on the near edge of that sheet on the table under the one window at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and sixty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and then she took them off and put them flat on the wood on both sides of it, and that sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever under that figure. That morning's date was on the door in chalk at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it and there never has been one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was there for a reason of their own.

That one place is still showing the figure of this city that came down that road twenty-eight days ago, measured from the morning it came up, and the figure in it is right and it is written out on no page in four hundred miles, and it is not the figure that is going into it, and nothing has been put in that one place at any hour of this flood by anybody in this city.

---

A man by that step said the thing that is said, of that sheet.

"Then it is a form with a place in it that has been answered by somebody and is waiting to be answered again."

"**It is a form with one place under one heading, and this room has agreed what goes in that one place, and there is no pencil in this room at the ninth hour and there is nobody in this room going to fetch one.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of that bench with his own knife not in his hand for once and was not asked anything by anybody in that room at any hour of that morning.

---

She lifted that sheet off that table by the far edge of it and held it up off the wood with both her hands so that the people at the far end of that bench could see the back of it, and the back of it is blank from the top of it to the bottom and there is nothing written on it and nothing folded into it, and she turned it over once and set it down on that wood in the same place with the heading at the top and the one place under the heading facing up, and she put her own hand flat on the wood beside it and left it there.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to that sheet, and it took him about four seconds to say it.

"**Whatever goes in that place goes out of this city with that sheet, and whoever reads it four hundred miles off will read it as being this city's answer to its own figure and he will have no way on that paper of telling that it is not.**"

Not one of the nine in that room told him that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on him and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his own arm and looked at the back of that sheet while it was up and then at the man of about thirty-four and then at nothing at all.

---

The register came off the shelf behind that bench and was opened at the head of the column of it that holds figures and was shut again and went back on that shelf with its spine to the wall. That column has five figures in it, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of the five has been struck and no sixth figure was put in that column that day and none was taken out of it, and the column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom. On that shelf beside that book stands a second slate, up on its edge with its face to the wall, and a day is cut across the head of it with nothing at all underneath, and nobody in that room took hold of it and nobody turned it and nothing was ever put on it, and the keeper's was the one hand that came near where it stood.

Through that one window the store's board stands on the outside wall at the end of that market, and it carries three sets of figures, of which none has been rubbed out and none has a line of words under it.

That sheet is not going anywhere this week. It goes back up that road on the round and this room is not going to write a sheet telling anybody anything, and there is no eighth sheet in this flood, and the lid of the box on the step below that window stands open all morning, and the store's chalk in it was not used that day and the piece in another man's inside breast pocket was not used either, and nothing was written under anything on any surface in this city on that day.

The heading at the top of that sheet is a heading and not a sentence, and not one word of it says what the figure in the one place under it is a figure of, and not one word of it is going to be altered in this city before that sheet goes back up that road.
