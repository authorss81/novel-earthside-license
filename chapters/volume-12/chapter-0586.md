# Chapter 0586 — The Second Row Crossed

Adrian Vale took the plank off the trestle at the top of that bank at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and seventy-first day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and walked down into the channel with it across his own two hands, and the water in that channel was up to his own chest before he was in the middle of it and the end of a rope that is not new and has been used a long time went round the near end of that plank in his own right hand and came loose at his own wrist and he took it off and let it go down into the water and did not have it again. He got the near end of that plank up onto the wet sill of the second row of those flats and lay out along the sill of that row and put the far end of it down onto the wet sill of the row beyond, and it lay there from about half past seven in the morning and it was not taken up at any hour of that day.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf came along that second row at about the tenth hour in the ordinary course and stopped on the near side of that plank and put his own boot on it once and took it off again. He said the thing that is said, to the plank.

"**There is no length of a row and no width of a channel that a plank does not cross if the far end of it is put down on something and not held.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on that and nobody on that ground thanked him for saying it and nobody on that ground said that he was right, and nobody on that ground asked him who had put that plank down, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf went along that row and said nothing else about it that day. He has a tally-board, a knife and a barrow of his own and there is a figure of this city on the side of that board that the sun gets to now, and that figure is written out on no page in four hundred miles, and nobody on that ground asked him where it came from and nobody in this city is going to.

---

A man with a handcart and a coil of rope in the cart came down that row from the high side at about the middle of the morning and put the near wheel of that cart at the near sill and then could not get it past the plank, because the plank lay along the wet sill and not across the width of the row, and he stood there with a hand on the handle of it and then he turned that cart about and took it back up that row the way he had come, about as far as a man goes to fetch a thing he has left behind and back again with it. He was gone a good while. Nobody on that ground told him why the cart could not go on, and nobody on that ground said a word to Adrian Vale about it, and Adrian Vale was on the high side of that channel at that hour with the water off him and his own coat open at the front and he did not know that a man with a handcart had gone back up that row on account of anything he had done.

The plank lay across that second row and the channel was about the width of two men lying down end to end and nobody in this city has taken a measure of that channel and nobody has written one down, and the wet sill on the far side of it is a sill and not a step, and there is a difference between those two things that a person feels through the sole of a boot and not in any other way.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came out along that row at about the fourth hour of the afternoon for a reason of her own that she did not give to anybody and that nobody in this city has asked her for, and she was carrying nothing at all, and she went across that plank from the near sill to the far one without stopping on it and without looking down at it, and her own right boot was on the near end of it and her own left boot was on the far end of it and the middle of that plank was under nobody's feet at all while she was on it. She did not know whose hands had laid it there and she did not say anything about it to anybody and she was not thanked for crossing it by anybody, because nobody knows she crossed it.

---

That end of rope went down into that channel about half past seven in the morning and it is still down there at the end of that day, and it was not new when he put his own hand on it, and nobody in this city has asked him where that rope came from or who put it down there, and those two rows are the same two rows that were apart from each other before this morning, and the whole of what has changed between them is a plank and a rope.
