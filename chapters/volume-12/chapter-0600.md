# Chapter 0600 — Five Surfaces And Not One Of Them Settled

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had his own shoulder under the near edge of the board on the wall at one end of that market and his own right hand in the bucket standing on the flags at the foot of the step at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and eighty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and he lifted that board off its two nails and set it down on the flags with its face up and ran the water off that bucket down the whole length of it from the near end to the far one and did it again from the same end, which is two passes, and the rag was on the stone lip of that step and the trough was against that wall with the water in it up to two hands and it did not go over its lip. That board carries three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and the stone above the top edge of that board went on up in the colour it keeps, and no second person put a hand on that stone on that morning or on any morning of this flood, and about four people came past the end of that row before the light came good and about four of them read one set of those figures on their way past and about four read none of them and nobody in that row said one word to the man who washes that board about the stone above the top of it.

---

The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of the room over that market and there is no name under it, and about four people were in that room at about the ninth hour and none of them was sent for anything. The register stands on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall. Five figures stand in the column it keeps them in, one under another, in the order they were made in, a day written in beside each, and not one of the five struck. The column on the right-hand side of that page goes the whole height of that page and has nothing whatever in it, top to bottom. A second slate is on the same shelf, up on its edge, its written face to the plaster, a day cut across the head of it and nothing under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that day and no hand but the keeper's went near it. Over the back of the chair at the far end of that bench there is a length of new rope, and it is as good as the day it was brought into this city, and no hand in that room picked it up or cut it or took it up or lifted it off that chair, and nobody in that room asked the man it belongs to one single thing about it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that shelf, and it was short and it was the last thing said out loud in that room that day.

"**Five figures in that book this morning and five yesterday and nobody has ever asked this room for a sixth, and the column on the right of that page is empty and nobody has told me what it is for, and I am leaving it empty.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and she put the flat of her own hand flat on the spine of that book and took it off again and did not open it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to the one window, where the board on the wall at the end of that market was going back up on its two nails, and it was short.

"**Three figures on that board and not one of them has anything under it, and I have chalk in this pocket that would put something under any of them, and I am not going to.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room said that he was right, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked out of that one window at that board and did not say another word about it that day, and that piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket at the end of that day and it has not come out of that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood.

---

At the near end of eleven miles of flats there is a yard with a stack of boards in it and a tally-board face up on the top of that stack with a figure of this city's own on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and there is a line of grey grit across the face of it, and about four people walk past the open end of that yard every day in this city and not one of them has ever stopped at it, and that figure is written out on no page in four hundred miles. The count that was made on foot out along those flats was made on four mornings, on the five hundred and eleventh, fifteenth, seventeenth and twenty-first day of this flood, and all four of those were entered in the book in a room over a market and the four of them do not agree and were not permitted to agree, and there is no fifth morning of it in this city and no fifth figure and nowhere for a fifth one to be put. The line of words that went under a figure on a sheet on the round is not in this city and no chapter of this flood has printed it and nothing in this flood is going to bring it back.

Out past the last named house the fence stands along the top of the shut road, sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord, and nobody in this city has measured that fence and nobody in this city has moved a post in it and nobody in this city is going to.

---

The three barrows went down that road on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood out of a party of four, and a party of four is four, and that road is four hundred miles long, and about four people in this city can be shown as far as the corner of it and about four cannot, and there is nobody in this city who can be shown one person standing on it, and the figure that left this city on the round is on that road and is not on any of those three barrows and never was.
