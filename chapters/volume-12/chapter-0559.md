# Chapter 0559 — A Chair Out From Under A Shelf And Put Back

A chair was pulled out from under the shelf behind the bench at about the sixth hour on the two hundred and forty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and it went across the boards of that room on four legs and stopped against the wall under that shelf, and it stayed there for about as long as it takes a man to look at a thing from the floor. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About four people were in that room at that hour and the rest of it was empty and there was nothing to be done in it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair at about the sixth hour with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and stopped about where the light off the one window falls short of the bench. He had not come up that stair to be asked anything. He came up it and looked at the shelf behind that bench and then he went round the end of that bench and got his own two hands on the back of that chair and pulled it out from under that shelf and turned it round and set it down against the wall under that shelf with its seat to the wall.

"**There is a thing on the bottom of that shelf that somebody has not looked at in a year and there is no way to see it without taking a chair and going down on the floor, and that is all I have come up here for and I am not going to say what the thing is.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on him and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him what the thing was, and about four people in that room had worked out that he had come up that stair for something and about four had not, and he was not the person the register is kept by and he was not asked what he wanted and he did not say.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the middle of that room.

"There is a chair standing against that wall in the middle of this room and it was not there when I came up that stair."

Nobody in that room answered her. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not in that room at that hour and nobody in that room moved that chair for her, and that chair stood against that wall until the seventh hour and it went back under that shelf the way it stands.

---

The shelf is at the height of a person's shoulder and it is about the depth of a forearm. On it stands the register with its spine to the wall and its cover shut, and behind that register stands a second slate on its edge with its face in to the wall, and a day is cut across the head of it, and under that day the slate is bare. Nothing on that shelf was picked up at any hour of that morning. Nothing on that shelf was turned over and nothing on that shelf was written on, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and her own hand did not go near that slate either, and she came into that room at about the eighth hour and stood at the head of the table and did not go to the back of it.

The column of that book that holds figures still held five figures, one under another in the order they were made in, with the day beside each of them, and it was not opened at any hour of that morning. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of the page and there is nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom, and the man who came up that stair did not put a hand on that book and did not look at that page and did not say one word about that column and is not going to.

---

He went down on one knee on the boards in front of that shelf at about the seventh hour and put his own face about level with the bottom of it and looked along the underside of that shelf from the left-hand end of it to the right-hand end of it and then back again, and the shelf has a bracket at each end of it and a nail in the middle of the front edge of it and dust on the top of it, and there is nothing on the underside of it but the two brackets and the nail. He stayed on that knee for about as long as it takes a man to walk from that shelf to the door at the foot of the stair and back, and he did not reach up and he did not put anything under that shelf and he did not take anything off it.

Then he got up off that knee, took that chair in his own two hands, carried it across the boards and put it back under that shelf with its back to the bench the way it stands, and he went down that stair and out along the flags of that row and nobody in that row asked him what he had gone up for.

There is a rectangle on the boards of that room under that shelf where that chair stood and it is a shade lighter than the boards around it, and it will be the same colour as the rest of them in about a week, and nothing was taken off that shelf to make room for the chair and nothing was put on it.
