# Chapter 0563 — One Ruled Place And Another Ruled Place

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her own right hand flat on the wood of the table under the one window at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and then she took it off and put it flat on the spine of the register on the shelf behind that bench, and then she took that off as well and left both hands on the wood. That stair at the back of that room has a date chalked on the door at the foot of it, and no name has ever gone under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at the eighth hour and every one of them came up that stair on their own two feet.

The sheet that came up that road in a satchel with a flap is on that table with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place is not empty. There is one place on that sheet and there has never been a second one. A figure of this city is in it in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that room, and that figure is right, and there is no page in four hundred miles that has it written out on it, and there is nothing else on that sheet anywhere and nothing under that figure and nothing beside it and no place on that sheet for the person who made it.

---

Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall. The column of that book that holds figures has five figures in it, one under another in the order they were made in with the day written beside each of them, and the newest of the five is at the head of that column and the oldest of the five is at the foot of it, and four of those five were entered on one morning, the five hundred and thirtieth day of this flood, into a column that held one figure before that morning, and none of the five has been struck. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page and there is nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom.

She took her own two hands off the wood, opened that book on the bench, and put the flat of her own left hand on the head of that column and her own right hand at the foot of it, and shut it again, and it was shut in about as long as it takes a man to walk from the window to the back of that room and back. Standing on the shelf where that book stands, on its edge with its face in to the wall, is a second slate with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day; it was not moved, not turned over and not written on at any hour of that morning, and the only hand in that room that went near that shelf was the keeper's, and she did not touch that slate.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair at about the eighth hour with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and looked at that sheet on the table under the one window for about as long as it takes a man to read a line off a board at arm's length, and then he looked at the back of that bench where that book stands, and then he said the thing that is said, to the middle of that room.

"**A single ruled place under a heading, and a single ruled place in a column, are the same thing ruled twice on two sheets of paper, and this room has had both of them for a month and has not said so.**"

That sentence was not agreed with by anybody. Nobody improved on it, nobody thanked the man who said it, nobody put a second question to him about it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was in that room and was not asked what she made of it and did not say. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his arm and looked at that sheet once and then at the back of that bench and said nothing at all.

A woman at the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Then put one in the other and we will have the same thing on one sheet."

"**Nobody in this city is going to put a figure of its own into somebody else's place on a form that has to go back up that road, and the two of them are the same shape and the two of them are not the same thing, and you have asked me to do the one thing I have never done and you have not asked me to do it twice.**"

Nobody in that room answered her. Nobody in that room told that man at the window that the two are not the same thing, and about four people in that room had worked out by that hour that the two are the same shape and about four had not, and not one of the nine said so out loud.

---

There are six documents in this flood, three of them with four places ruled under a heading and three of them with a single place ruled under a heading with a figure in it. One of the three of one place was read out in that room a long time ago and has never been returned and has never been withdrawn. A second of those three left this city in a satchel with a flap eighteen days ago, that count measured from the morning it went out, and no instrument in this flood can bring a figure back. The third of them is on the table under that one window with a figure of this city in its one place that this city did not put there. No two of the six touched at any hour of that morning, and none of the six was ever held by two hands together, and nobody compared any two of them aloud in that room.

The near edge of that sheet on the table and the near edge of the page of that book, lying open with the head of that column showing, are about a hand's width apart on that wood, and the same width is ruled on both of them, and there is nothing at all in the gap between them and nobody in that room put anything there.
