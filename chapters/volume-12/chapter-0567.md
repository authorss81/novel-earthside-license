# Chapter 0567 — Four And Three And Nobody Told Which One To Count

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the first of the sheets off the shelf behind that bench at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and set it on the table under the one window, and a man at the far end of that bench took the sheet with four places ruled under a heading off its nail by the door of that room and set it down at the other end of that table, and there was already a sheet on that table with one place ruled under a heading and a figure of this city in it. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at the ninth hour and nobody in this city sent anybody a sheet to tell them to come.

---

The three of them were not laid beside one another at any hour of that morning. None of the three was ever in one hand with another of the three, and none of the three was compared out loud in a mouth in that room. There are six documents in this flood. Four of them have come up that road and two of them have gone up it, and the last of the four to come up that road came up it fifteen days ago, measured from the day it came up it, in a satchel with a flap, and it is on that table with its one place already filled. One of the three of one place went out of this city in a satchel with a flap and it is not in this city and it is not coming back and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back, and it is not on that table and it is not in that room.

A man at the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"There is one of those on that shelf and one of those on that table and one of those by the door and that is three of them and there were four of them."

"**Four sheets have come up that road in this flood and one of them has not been in this room and is not going to be counted this morning, and three of those four are in this room now and a person can count what is in front of that person and get three and be right about what is in front of that person.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on him and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him which of the two numbers was the one anybody should be counting, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the other number out loud herself about as long as it takes a man to walk from that table to the back of that bench and back, and it was the other one, and not one of the nine in that room said which of the two was the one anybody should be counting.

---

Seven sheets have gone out of this city in this flood. All seven of them went out in the two volumes before this one and every one of them was a sheet somebody in this city wrote, and this city has not written one since and this volume writes none. Nobody in that room wrote anything that morning and nobody in that room said that a sheet was going out of this city that week, and the room over that market is not going to have a sheet put up on its door telling anybody anything, and about four people in that room had worked that out and about four had not.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he said nothing whatever about the count. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket, and the piece of chalk in that pocket has been in it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and it was not out of that pocket at any hour of that morning. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench with his own hands on his own knees and nobody in that room asked him a single question about anything he had ever carried.

---

The three sheets went back to the two places they came off in the order they came off in, and the register went back on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and behind that register the second slate stood on its edge with its face in to the wall with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move and it was not turned over and it was not written on, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf all morning.

From inside that room, through the one window, the store's board is on its two nails on the outside wall at the end of that market with three sets of figures on it and none of the three of them rubbed out and not one of the three of them carrying a line of words under it, and about four people at that end of that market walk to the end of that row every morning and read one of them and go on. Neither of the two numbers said out loud in that room was about any of the three.
