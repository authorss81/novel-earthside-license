# Chapter 0589 — The Line Read Out Once And Not Argued With

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf put his own right hand flat on the near edge of that sheet at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and lifted it off the wood of that table and held it up off the table by that edge at the height of that bench so that the people at the far end of it could read the one place under the heading and the line of words under the figure in it, and then he read that line out once, in his own mouth, in about nine words, at about the ninth hour, in a room in daylight with a date in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and about nine people in it. The figure in that one place is written out on no page in four hundred miles and what is under it is not written out anywhere either.

Nobody in that room asked him to read it again. Nobody in that room said one word against it and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room said that it was a good line or a bad one, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, who wrote those words in her own hand the day before, did not look up while he was reading them and did not say anything about them afterwards.

About four people in that room have said out loud, on days that are not this one, that they mean four different things by the ordinary word for the thing itself, and not one of those four is wrong and not one of the nine in that room can say which of the four of them is the one that person four hundred miles off will hear in those words, and the man who read that line out has not asked any of those four what they meant and about four of them have not said.

---

Then he put that sheet back down on the same table with the writing side up and put his own two hands flat on the wood on either side of it and named what it had cost him, out loud, in about nine words.

"**Nobody can check one word of what I have just said.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the price he named on the two hundred and sixtieth day of that month and the price he named this morning are not the same price and he is not going to be asked about either of them.

He said one more thing, and it was short.

"**I am not on that paper and I was in this room when it was written.**"

Nobody in that room said anything back to him about that, and nobody in that room said that he was right, and about four people at that end of the market below went past the end of that row in the ordinary course and not one of them came up that stair.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to that sheet, and it was short.

"**Every figure in this city is bare now except that one, and the one that is not bare cannot be read without the words and the words are not a record of what it counts.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not say another word about that sheet that day.

The register is on that shelf behind that bench, with its spine turned to the wall, and it is the only book in this city. The column of it that holds figures holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of those five has been struck and nothing was added to that column on that morning and nothing was taken out of it. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom, and nobody in that room has ever asked her what that column is for. On that shelf beside that book a second slate is standing edge up with its written face to the plaster and a day cut into the head of it and nothing under that day whatever, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and nobody in that room went near it, and the keeper's hand went to the book and not to that.

Over the back of the chair at the far end of that bench there is a length of new rope, and it is as good as the day it was brought in, and no hand in that room picked it up or cut it or took it up or lifted it off that chair at any hour of that morning, and nobody in that room asked the man it belongs to one single thing about it, and nobody in this city has ever asked him anything.

---

Nobody said a word about what he had read out, and that sheet is on the table under that window with the figure in the one place and the line of words under that figure, and it is not going to that road for four more days, and for four more days those words are in this city and about nine people have heard them, and about four hundred miles off there is nobody who has, and there is going to be a man in a satchel with a flap carrying them up that road with them and he is not going to open it.
