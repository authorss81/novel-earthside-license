# Chapter 0592 — A Line Of Grit Across The Figure On The Top Of That Stack

Adrian Vale had the flat of his left hand on the near edge of the tally-board on the top of that stack and his right thumb laid along the chalk line where a figure of this city stood. The sun had been on the top of that stack since it came out of the shade. At about the seventh hour on the two hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday, a man at the far end of the stack had both hands on a length of cord that was coming off a cleat and being drawn taut along the length of those boards. They had been cut to one length and their ends were all in a line, and there was about a hand's width of grit in the joints of them.

The man who owns that stack is the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf. He was at the foot of it with a knife in his right hand and the handle turned away from him.

Between the seventh hour and the eleventh hour about four people walked past the open end of that yard in the ordinary course, and not one of them came into it. Every one of them could have read the number on the top face of that board without stopping. The figure of this city's own has been on that side of the board since before the seventh week of this flood, and nobody in this city has ever been shown the working of it and nobody in this city is going to be.

---

The cord came across the top of that stack at about the height of a man's knee and went over the near edge of it and across the face of that tally-board. Adrian Vale kept his hand on that board's edge and his thumb on that chalk line, and did not know the cord was over it until a man at the far end of the stack said the word for stop.

The grit that works out of the joints of those boards when they are wet and then dry has been coming off them onto whatever is laid across them since the day they were cut. That cord was down on the top face of that tally-board for the time it took to mark off one length against it.

The man at the far end of the stack pulled it up off that face and shook it once and coiled it and threw the coil over the post of the trestle. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf turned his knife over in his hand, put the flat of that hand down on the top board of that stack, and took it off again. There is a line of grey grit across the figure on that board that was not on it at the seventh hour.

Nobody in that yard said a word about it at the time.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the open end of his own yard, and it was short.

"**Nobody has ever stopped at that figure and a line of grit across it is not going to make anybody stop at it, and I have never once asked anybody to.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said that he was right. He went back to the foot of that stack and looked at the face of that board for a while, and did not put a hand on it, and did not turn it over.

---

A man with a satchel with a flap across his body came to the open end of that yard about the ninth hour and stopped there and looked in at the top of that stack. The man from the wharf looked back at him, and neither of them said anything to the other about anything. Then the man with the satchel said the thing that is said, to the yard, and it was short.

"**A thing in a satchel with a flap is shut and there is no more to it than that, and the man carrying it knows what is in it and nobody else does, and that has been the arrangement for every day of this flood.**"

Nobody in that yard thanked him for it and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him what was in it. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living went on up the row with the strap of that satchel across his chest, and did not open it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf is a man of about thirty-eight, and the man of about thirty-eight who came up a lane with a barrow of boards is a different man, and nothing in this city has ever put the two of them in one yard. The barrow standing in that yard has nothing in it, and nothing ever goes on a barrow in this city. A figure of this city's own was counted on foot out along eleven miles of flats on four mornings: the five hundred and eleventh, fifteenth, seventeenth and twenty-first day of this flood. There is not a fifth one in this city and there is not going to be one. Nobody in that yard knew how many of those four there were and nobody in that yard asked.

That figure on the top face of that board is a shade harder to read than it was at the seventh hour, and it will be a shade harder to read than it is now at any hour after this one. There has not been rain on that yard in this flood, and rain is what would take the line off. It was the length of a cord, and about as long again as it took to mark off one board. Adrian Vale had his hand on the near edge of that stack the whole of the time and his thumb on the chalk line the whole of the time, and nobody in that yard knew he was standing there. About four people will walk past the open end of that yard tomorrow and about four will walk past the day after, and not one of the four is going to know that the number was ever easier to read than it is now.
