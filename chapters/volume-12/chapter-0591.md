# Chapter 0591 — The Third Bill Named In A Room And Not One Of Them In It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register wound the sleeve of her coat twice round her right hand. She put the flat of it against the outside face of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, and she took the day that was on that door off it in one long smear, from the top of the door down to about the height of her own knee. It was about the eighth hour on the two hundred and seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf sat at the near end of the bench with both hands flat on the wood of it and was not asked anything by anybody in that room. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of it with his board under his arm and the face of it turned in against his ribs.

She put the flat of her hand on the wood under where the smear had been and wrote the day of that morning in chalk, small, in the ordinary way. No name has ever gone under a date on that door and there is none under this one.

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to a man at the far end of it, and it was short.

"Whose is it going out as."

"**Nobody four hundred miles off is going to know a person made that, and she made it on foot, and she is not on the paper and she is not going to be.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody argued with it. The person that cost is about was not in that room and is not in it this morning. She is on no roll in this city and she has no name on any page in four hundred miles, and nothing done in that room today is going to put one there.

The man who said it has a board under his arm carrying nineteen rates and one name that is not on it. He said nothing about that name and nobody asked him about it, and nobody in this city is going to.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that closed sheet on the table under the one window, and it was short.

"**I wrote the words under that figure and I did not count that figure, and that is the whole of what that sheet is and it is the whole of what I did to it.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. The man from the wharf looked down at the backs of his own hands on the wood of the table for a moment, and said nothing at all about any of it, and nobody asked him anything about any of it.

---

A stool stood against the wall at the end of that bench with the dust of the wall on the top of it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in off the stair at about the half hour after the ninth, went along the front of the bench, and carried that stool to the end of it with both hands and set it down square with the front of it. Then he stepped back and put his hands down at his sides.

The man from the wharf looked at that stool. Then he sat down on it at the end of the bench and put his hands on his knees. Nobody in that room said a word about the stool, and nobody asked the man who had carried it why he had done it, and nobody thanked him for it. His piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket. It did not come out of that pocket at any hour of that morning and it is in there now.

---

The register is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine turned to the wall. The column of it that holds figures is full from the head of that page to the foot of it, five figures put there one under another in the order they were made in with the day of each written in beside it, and not one of the five struck out. Nothing was added to that page this morning and nothing was taken off it. The column on the right of that page is the same width as the other one and is empty the whole way down, and it has been since before this flood.

A second slate stands on the same shelf, up on its edge, its written face turned to the plaster, with a day cut across the head of it and nothing at all underneath that day. It did not shift at any hour of that morning and nobody in that room went near that shelf. The keeper's hand went to the book and not to the slate.

Seven sheets have gone out of this city in this flood and this city has not written one since, and there is not going to be an eighth. The sheet on that table under the one window is the sixth document to have come up that road or gone out of it, and it is not one of the seven and it is not going to become one. About four people in that room had worked that out by the ninth hour. About four had not. Nobody in that room said which of the two groups was right.

---

By the half hour after the tenth there were two dates on the outside of that door, and only one of them was legible. The first was the day before, taken off in a single smear from the top of the door down to about the height of a knee. Under the smear was the day of that morning, gone on in chalk, small, in the ordinary way. There is no name under either of them, because no name has ever gone under a date on that door.

The third bill of this flood was named out loud in that room at about the ninth hour, and the person it is about was not in the room, and naming it did not put her on a page. The door at the foot of that stair now carries two dates where it carried one at the eighth hour: the day before, gone off in a single smear from the top of the door down to about the height of a knee, and under the smear the day of that morning in chalk, small, in the ordinary way, with no name under either of them. The book on the shelf behind that bench keeps the day beside each of five figures and does not take any of them off, and the door is the only record in this city that is taken off a surface every morning and put back on it thinner. About four people in this city have thought about which of those two records is worth more to a person reading it in about a year. About four have not thought about it at all, and no hand in this city is going to come along and find out.

The one thing in that room that moved this morning was a stool, carried from a wall to the end of that bench by a man nobody in that room has ever asked about anything, and he set it down square and stepped back from it and put his hands at his sides.
