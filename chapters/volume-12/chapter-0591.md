# Chapter 0591 — The Third Bill Named In A Room And Not One Of Them In It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the sleeve of her own coat wound twice round her own right hand and the flat of that hand against the outside face of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and she took the day that was on that door off it in one long smear from the top of the door down to about the height of her own knee, and then she put the flat of her own hand flat on the wood under where it had been and wrote the day of that morning in chalk, small, in the ordinary way, and there is no name under either of those two days and there never has been one under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them was sent for anything, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned in against his own ribs, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands flat on the wood of it and was not asked anything by anybody in that room.

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to a man at the far end of it, and it was short.

"Whose is it going out as."

"**Nobody four hundred miles off is going to know a person made that, and she made it on foot, and she is not on the paper and she is not going to be.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and nobody in that room turned round to look at anybody else when he said it, and the person that cost is about was not in that room and is not in that room this morning and is on no roll in this city and has no name on any page in four hundred miles and nothing that was done in that room on that morning will put one there. He has a board under his own arm with nineteen rates on it and one name that is not on it and he did not say one word about that name this morning and nobody in that room asked him about that name this morning and nobody in this city is going to.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that closed sheet on the table under the one window, and it was short.

"**I wrote the words under that figure and I did not count that figure, and that is the whole of what that sheet is and it is the whole of what I did to it.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf looked at the back of his own hands on the wood of that table for a moment and said nothing at all about any of it and was not asked anything about any of it.

---

A stool stood against the wall at the end of that bench with the dust of the wall on the top of it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came in off that stair at about the half hour after the ninth, went along the front of that bench, took that stool up off the floor with both his own hands, set it down at the end of the bench square with the front of it, and stepped back from it and put his own hands down at his sides. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf looked at that stool for a moment and then sat down on it at the end of that bench and put his own two hands on his own knees. Nobody in that room said a word about the stool. Nobody in that room thanked the man of about thirty-four for it and nobody in that room asked him why he had done it, and his own piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket and it did not come out of that pocket at any hour of that morning and it is in that pocket now.

---

The register is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine turned to the wall, and the column of it that holds figures is full from the head of that page to the foot of it, five figures put there one under another in the order they were made in with the day of each written in beside it, and not one of the five struck out. Nothing was added to that page this morning and nothing was taken off it. The column on the right-hand side of that page is the same width as the other one and is empty the whole way down, from the top of the page to the bottom, and it has been since before this flood. A second slate stands on the same shelf, up on its edge, its written face turned to the plaster, with a day cut across the head of it and nothing at all underneath that day. It did not shift at any hour of that morning, nobody in that room went near that shelf, and the keeper's hand went to the book and not to the slate.

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and this city has not written one since and there is not going to be an eighth, and the sheet on that table under the one window is the sixth document to have come up or gone out of this room in this flood and it is not one of the seven and it is not going to become one, and about four people in that room at that hour had worked that out and about four had not, and nobody in that room said which of the two groups was right.

---

The date on the outside of that door is the only record in this city that is taken off a surface every morning and put back on it thinner. The book on the shelf behind that bench keeps the day beside each of five figures and does not take any of them off, and the keeper of that book does not wipe those five days out and is not going to, and about four people in this city have thought about which of the two records is worth more to a person reading it in about a year and about four have not thought about it at all, and no hand in this city is going to come along and find out.
