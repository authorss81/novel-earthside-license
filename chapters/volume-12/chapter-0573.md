# Chapter 0573 — Seven And No Eighth

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and fifty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, with his own board under his own arm, and he took it off from under that arm and stood it against that bench with the face of it turned in to the wall, which is the way he puts it down when he is not selling anything. That date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it and there never has been one, and below that date the door is bare and has been bare under it since the door was put up. The stair at the back of that room is nine steps and about nine people went up and down it between the ninth hour and the eleventh and every one of them was in that room before the light came off the far wall.

The sheet that came up that road twenty-one days ago, measured from the day it came up, in a satchel with a flap, was on the table under the one window with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place was not empty, and the figure in that one place is right and it is not written out on any page in four hundred miles and nobody in that room wrote it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that table, and then she sat down.

"**We have to find out what a figure of this city has to be a figure of before anybody puts one in that place, and there is nothing in this room that can find that out, and there is nothing outside this room either.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and a man near the middle of that bench said the thing that is said, to the sheet.

"Could a person be sent to ask."

"**A person cannot be sent to ask, because the way a person finds out what is wanted in this city is to be handed a sheet about it, and seven sheets have gone out of this city in this flood and every one of the seven was written by somebody in this city, and this city has not written one since, and there is no eighth coming.**"

Nobody in that room acted on that either. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living sat at the far end of that bench with his own hands on his own knees, and not one person in that room put a question to him about a single thing he has ever carried up a road.

---

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and they went out one at a time and every one of them was a sheet somebody in this city sat down and wrote. A woman at the step counted them off on the fingers of her own hand without looking at anything and got the same number twice, and then a man at the far end of that bench counted them off on his own fingers and got the other number, and neither of the two of them said out loud which of the two numbers was the one anybody should be counting, and not one of the nine in that room settled it.

Six documents have been made and carried in this flood. Three of them carry four places under a heading and three of them carry one place under a heading with a figure already in it, and one of those three left this city in a satchel with a flap and is not here and is not coming back, and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back. Each of the three that came up that road came up it on its own, and no two of the six have ever touched, and no two of the six have ever been in one hand together, and nobody in that room set one of them beside another and said out loud what the other one was.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of her own hand on the table under the one window, on the wood, about a hand's width from the near edge of that sheet, and left it there while she said it.

"**Twenty-one days ago that sheet came up this stair and nobody in this city was told it was coming, and the four days that would have been the cooling are not a thing anybody in this city can arrange, and we are going to be standing here finding out what we are being asked on the morning we are asked it, the way this room has done every time it has been handed something to do.**"

Not one of the nine in that room agreed with her out loud.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and he looked through the one window at the bare door under the date for a while, and he looked at the box on the step below that window with its lid standing open and the store's own chalk showing in it, and he put his own hand deeper into that pocket while he was looking and he took nothing out, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not say one word about the box or about the door or about the round.

Behind that bench the register is on its shelf with its spine to the wall, and the column of it that holds figures holds five of them, and the column beside that one is empty for the whole height of that page from its head to its foot. Standing on that shelf next to it is a second slate, up on its edge with its face to the wall, and a day has been cut across the head of it with nothing under it, and it did not move all morning and the only hand in that room that came near where it stands is the keeper's.

Nobody wrote on that door that day. There is no eighth sheet in this flood and that room over that market is not going to have one put up on it, and about four people in this city had worked that out before the light came off that window and about four had not.
