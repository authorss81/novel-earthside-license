# Chapter 0557 — A Lid That Shut And Nobody In That Market Noticed

The store's board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and forty-second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and went down into the trough against that wall. The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board laid it flat across the mouth of that trough, turned it over once so that the wet side of it was up to the air, and went at the face of it with the rag from the bottom left-hand corner across and up, and every one of the three sets of figures came out of the wet as clean as it went in. He carried it up the two steps of that step and put it on its two nails again. The rag went onto the stone lip and the bucket went down into the trough. The wall above the top edge of that board was not washed. The box of chalk is on that step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of chalk showing inside it, and the lid of that box has not shut since the water came. About four people at that end of that market were in that row in the ordinary course.

---

Adrian Vale was on the flags in front of that step at about the sixth hour with his own two hands and nothing else. The grit comes out of the joints of those flags in front of that step and goes along that row and there is more of it in front of that box than there is anywhere else in that row, because the box is the one thing on that step and everything the row carries goes past it. He went along the front of those flags on his knees from the near corner of the step to the far one, taking the grit out of the joints with the two first fingers of his own right hand and putting it into the hollow of the flags a foot to the left of where it came from, and he did it three times along the width of that step and he did not hurry it.

A woman with a stall two along had a board of her own up on edge against her own trestle at that hour, and the foot of it was on a flat stone, and the standing water in front of that stall had been coming up over that stone and under the corner of that board since the water came, and about the ninth hour the water goes down that row in one sheet. While the grit was in the joints in front of that board that water had nowhere to go and stood in a flat sheet about as deep as a thumb across the whole front of that stall. By the seventh hour it was running past that stall in a narrow line along the gutter and the stone under that board's foot was dry, and she had about half an hour of that morning that she had not expected to have, and nobody in that row said anything to anybody about where it had gone.

"Somebody has put the water off my front and I do not know who has done it and I am not going to stand here and work out who it was."

Nobody in that row answered her. She got up off that knee about as long as before, put her own hand flat on the top of her own board and left it there, and went on with that stall, and Adrian Vale was on the flags at the far end of that row by then with his own two hands in the joints of the stones and he did not look over at that stall and was not asked and did not say.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at his own trestle two stalls along with his own board standing face in against it and the bare back of it out in the row. He came out from behind that trestle at about the seventh hour to get a length of string off a nail and Adrian Vale was on the flags in front of the step behind him with his back to him, and neither of them said a word to the other at any hour of that morning.

"**That box on that step has not shut since the water came and it has shut, and I have not touched it and neither has that man on the flags, and I have been looking at that step for about as long as it takes a man to walk to the end of this row and back.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right. Nobody in that row improved on him and nobody in that row thanked him and nobody in that row asked him how a lid shuts, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went back behind his own trestle and tied his string on and untied it again.

---

The piece of chalk that a man writes a figure of this city with in his own hand has been in the inside breast pocket of that man's own coat since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and it was not out of that pocket at any hour of that morning and it is not coming out of it. The chalk in the box on that step is the store's chalk and it is a different thing and neither of the two was used at any hour of that morning. No figure was put on that board at any hour of that morning, no mouth in that row said which of the three sets of figures on it is the one anybody should be reading, and the stone above the top edge of that board was not washed and no second person put a hand on it.

Nobody in that market knew that box had shut. The box is the store's and the store was shut behind its own door at that hour, and the grit comes down those flags at the ninth hour in whatever the wind has off the flats, and it will get under that lip again and stand that lid open before there is anybody in that row to see it do it.
