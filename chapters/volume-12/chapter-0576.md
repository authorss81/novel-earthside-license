# Chapter 0576 — The Second Of The Two And Neither Of Them Is The Middle

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf put the flat of his own right hand on the bench at the near end of that room at about the tenth hour on the two hundred and sixty-first day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and left it there while he looked at the shelf behind that bench, and then he took it off the bench and put it flat on the front edge of the bench instead, and he did not say anything about what he was looking at. That date was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and there is no name under it, and below that date the door is bare. About four people were in that room at that hour and the rest of it was empty and the light came in at the one window and not at the door.

A run of days of the kind this city is having has two days in the middle of it, and this is the second of those two, and neither of them is the middle of anything and no day in such a run is the middle of anything, and the other of the two was yesterday and about nine people were in that room at that hour and nothing was decided in it that anybody in it has said one word about since.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took that register off that shelf and put it down on that bench flat and on its cover and with the spine of it toward the window, and she put her own palm flat on the middle of that cover and left it there, and then she lifted it and put the book back on that shelf with its spine to the wall. That book was not opened at any hour of that morning.

The column of that book that holds figures has five figures in it, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of the five has been struck, and the column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom. Beside that book on that same shelf there is a second slate, up on its edge with its face turned in to the wall, and a day has been cut across the head of it and nothing whatever has ever been put under that day, and nobody lifted it off that shelf and nobody turned it over and nobody put anything on it, and the only hand in that room that went near where it stood all morning was the keeper's.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood at that window with his own board tucked under his own arm and its face turned out in to that room, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf was at the near end of the bench, and the two of them had been in that room together the day before, and not one word about the day before was said out loud in that room between the tenth hour and the half hour after it.

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to the bench.

"Two men in a room and neither of them looking at the other one."

"**They looked at each other the whole of yesterday and there is nothing left to look at, and I am not going to be the one who walks round it, and you are not going to be either.**"

Not one of the four in that room agreed with him. Nobody in that room built on what he had said, nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody in that room put a second question to him about it, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the window, and it was short and he said it once.

"**I have said what I came to say and it is not going to be said better this morning than it was said yesterday, and I am not going to be asked anything about it, and I am going back down those steps now.**"

Nobody in that room argued with him. Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room asked him where that figure came from or how long it had been on that board or what it was a figure of, and he went down that stair and nobody in that room watched him go.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair about a quarter of an hour after that man had gone down it and stood at the far end of that bench with his own hands in his own pockets and his own board two stalls along in that row standing face in against his own trestle with the bare back of it out in the row, and nobody in that room asked him anything. The board that the man of about thirty-nine trades on was still at that window with nineteen rates on the face of it in three columns with the rows going across and a space about two fingers wide at the foot of the middle column of it with nothing whatever in that space.

The register stood on that shelf with its spine to the wall and the second slate stood on the same shelf edge up and face to the wall with its day across the head of it and nothing under it, and there was a clean strip in the dust on that shelf between the two of them where a cloth had gone across it the day before and had not been put back, and nobody in that room put anything on that shelf and nobody in that room said one word about the space on the face of a board that is under another man's arm at the other end of that bench.
