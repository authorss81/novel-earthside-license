# Chapter 0599 — A Column Like That Is Where A Word Goes And The Room Does Not Do It

Adrian Vale had his own left hand flat on the front edge of the shelf behind that bench at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eighty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register lifted the register off that shelf with both of her own hands and set it down on the table under the one window, and he put the flat of his own right hand on the spine of that book where it lay and turned it so that the head of the column that holds figures came round to the top of that page. There is a date in chalk on the outside of that door for that morning, and there is no name under it, and there never has been one under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them was sent for anything, and about four of those nine came to that table in the ordinary course and looked at the open page and none of them said one word about the half of that page on the right-hand side of it.

The second slate was standing on that shelf edge up with its written face to the plaster and a day cut into the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and the ledge his own hand was on is the ledge the foot of that slate stands on, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on at any hour of that morning, and no hand but the keeper's went near that slate, and his own hand was on the ledge and on the spine of that book and went no nearer to that slate than a hand's width.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to the column on the right-hand side of that open page, and he took his own hand off his own inside breast pocket to say it.

"Somebody ought to put something in that."

"**A column like that is where a word goes, and nobody in this room has ever been told what it is for, and I am not the one who keeps it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room said that he was right, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it from that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not say a second thing about that page that morning.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that page, and it was short.

"**It has been empty since before this flood and nobody has asked me about it and nobody is going to, and I have not put anything in it this morning and I am not going to.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and about four people at that table had already worked out that a man had just said the first thing anybody has said out loud about that column in this flood and about four had not noticed it happen.

---

A person who does not know what a column is for should not write in it. That is the whole of the reason nobody in that room put a word in the column on the right of that page at about the ninth hour on that morning, and it is not an oversight and it is not the room being careful, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall is not thanked for saying it and is not argued with and is not asked whether the room should have done it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register is not asked either. About four people at that table had the days of those five figures by heart and read them off the page anyway, one after another, in the order they were made in, and about four of them could not have said which of the five was the oldest and did not ask. The column of that book that holds figures holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of those five has been struck and nothing was added to that column that morning and nothing was taken out of it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not look at the page while any of that was being said.

The sheet with a heading and one place ruled under it went out of this city on the round and is not in that room and is not in this city and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back with the words still under it, and from the day after it went there has been no surface in this city with a line of words under a figure on it at all, and nobody in that room said a word about that either.

---

That book went back on that shelf at about the eleventh hour with its spine to the wall, and the column on the right of that page is empty from the top of that page to the bottom and it is empty because nobody in that room has ever been told what it is for, and a man said out loud at the ninth hour that a column like that is where a word goes, and the room did not do it, and that is a decision and not an oversight, and there is no instrument in this city that will keep a person from writing in a column nobody has explained to them, and there is one thing standing in the way of it this morning and there will be the same one thing standing in the way of it tomorrow.
