# Chapter 0579 — A Board The Length Of That Yard

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf got his own right hand under the near end of that board at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and sixty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and the man at the far end of it got both his hands under the other end of it, and they carried it up that yard from the stack to the trestle at the far end of it in about forty goes with a third of the yard put out of line at every step, and neither of them let go of it until it was flat on that trestle and squared against the rail of it. The wind came along that yard off the low side of the flats and it was behind them going up and in their faces coming down, and the salt that had come up the road in that board and the grit that had worked into its joints since came out of the top of it onto their own hands as they went.

That yard is not as long as it is at the near end of it and nobody in this city has taken a measure of that yard. About four people walked past the open end of that yard between the seventh hour and the eleventh hour in the ordinary course and not one of them came into it.

---

His own tally-board is on the top of that stack with the face of it up, where it was put yesterday morning by his own hand, and it is not going to be put back face down. There is a figure of this city on it, and it is a figure he made himself, and it is a figure nobody in this city can be shown the working of, and it has been lying face down on that stack since before the seventh week of this flood with the sun on the other side of it, and it is face up now and there is a number on it in the open.

About four people walked past the open end of that yard all morning. Every one of them could have read that number without stopping and not one of them did, and not one of them looked up at the top of that stack, and nobody in this city has ever said one word to that man about the other side of that board.

---

They carried it back again at about the tenth hour with the wind in their faces the whole way and put it on the top of that stack and squared the near end of it against the end of the boards underneath it, and they took their own hands off it and stood back from it, and the man of about thirty-eight at that wharf stood at the foot of that stack with a knife in his own right hand and the handle of it turned away from him and said the thing that is said, to the yard, and it was short.

"**There is no length of that yard that a man carrying a board cannot get across twice before the light goes off the flats, and anybody who says otherwise has not carried one along it.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody in that yard thanked him for saying it and nobody in that yard asked him where the figure on the other side of his own board came from. The man at the far end of that board did not say one word about any of it. He went and took hold of a barrow that was standing in that yard with nothing in it, and he wheeled it out to the far end of that yard with the handles in both his hands, and he brought it back again with nothing in it the whole time, and he set it down where it had been standing.

---

A barrow stood in that yard the whole of that morning with nothing on it and nothing ever goes on a barrow in this city, and the three that went down the four hundred miles of that other road in this flood went down it one apiece on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood out of a party of four of whom one said no before anybody was asked, and a party of four is four, and nobody in that yard was asked about the three and nobody in that yard said one word about them.

Not one surface in this city carried a line of words under a figure at any hour of that day. The figure on the top face of that board is a number and nothing is under it and nothing is going to be put under it, and the two figures on the outside wall at the end of that market are numbers with nothing under them, and the five in the column of a book in a room over that market are numbers with nothing under them, and the figure in the one place on a sheet lying on a table in that room is a number with nothing under it, and the man who made the one on the top of that stack is standing at the foot of it with a knife in his hand and is not on anything it is on.

At about the eleventh hour the light came off the flats and went off that yard from the near end of it toward the channel, and the top of that stack was the last thing in that yard to lose the light on it, and the figure on it was the only thing on that stack that a person standing at the open end of that yard could have read.
