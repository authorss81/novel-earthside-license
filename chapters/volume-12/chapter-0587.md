# Chapter 0587 — A Board Off A Wall And Back On To It

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put the flat of his own left hand under the near edge of it and his own right hand round the bucket standing on the flags at the foot of that wall. He lifted that board off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and seventy-second day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and set it down on the stone with its face up and the grit coming off it in a grey line where it fell. He washed it from one end to the other and washed it again from the same end, and the water off it went down across the flags and into the gutter at the near end of that row. That board carries three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and the stone above the top edge of it was not washed at any hour of that morning, and no second person put a hand on that stone at any hour of that day.

He washed it the second time from the same end and the water was not as dirty as it had been the first time and he did not hurry that either, and where it came off the bottom edge of that board it ran down the face of the stone in one line, stopped about a foot above the flags, and started again lower down, and there is a run of it dried on that stone above another run and another above that, and the top one is nearly gone and the bottom one is sharp. That board has come off those two nails and been washed and gone back up on them on a great many mornings of this flood, and nobody in that row has counted them and nobody in this city is going to.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was two stalls along with his own board standing face in against his own trestle and the bare back of it out in the row, and he said the thing that is said, to the wall, and it took him about four seconds to say it.

"**Three sets of figures on that board and not one of the three of them rubbed out, and that has been the state of it every morning of this flood and it is going to be the state of it this afternoon.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody in that row thanked him for it and nobody in that row said that he was right, and nobody in that row said out loud which of those three sets of figures is the one anybody should be reading, and about four people at that end of that market had read one of them every morning for the whole of this flood and about four had not stopped at that board at all.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board said the thing that is said, to the stone lip of that step, and it was short.

"**It has been washed and it is going back up and there is nothing in that row this morning that anybody in that row has to decide.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that either, and nobody in that row asked him a second question about it, and nobody in that row has ever asked him anything, and the lid of the box on that step stood open all morning with about a thumb's length of the store's own chalk showing in it and that chalk was not used, and the piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man two stalls along was not out of that pocket either.

---

He carried that board back up onto its two nails at about the half hour after the fifth and put his own hand flat on the top of it to settle it, and the wall above the top edge of that board said nothing at any hour of that day, and nothing was sent to anybody from that row that day and nothing was entered in any book in this city that day and nobody in this city heard anything about that row.

About four people went past the end of that row before the light came good and about four went past it again before the light came off the flags, and every one of the four could have read one set of figures on that board without stopping and not one of them did stop.

Between the sixth hour and the ninth hour the grit line on that stone dried in the sun and went white at the edges first, and the water that had gone down across those flags stopped going down in the middle of that row and left a darker band across some of the stones that nobody walked round. The bucket stood where he had put it down at the foot of that wall with about a hand's depth of water in it, and the rag was on the stone lip of that step where it goes every morning and had not been moved off it that morning at all, and about four people came along that row between those two hours, and every one of the four looked at the wet stone and not one of the four looked at that board, and not one of the four said one word to the man who washed it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along did not look up off his own board at any hour of that stretch of the morning.

---

The board went back up on its two nails about as crooked as it came off them, and the nail at the head of it has gone the width of a thumbnail further into that wall than the nail at the foot of it has, which is not how those two nails went in and which happened while that board was off the wall, and nobody in that row took the two of them to see and nobody in that row is going to, and that board will come off those nails again about as crooked as it went on.
