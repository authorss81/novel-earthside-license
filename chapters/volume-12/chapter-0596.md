# Chapter 0596 — One Pass And Not Two

The trough was against that wall with the water in it up to about two hands, and the bucket stood on the flags at the foot of the step with the water in it a hand's width down from the top. The box on that step had the store's own chalk in it, with a thumb's length of it showing and the lid of that box lying down on the rim of it. Adrian Vale had the rag in his right hand and the flat of his left hand on that step, at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and eighty-first day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday.

That board carries three sets of figures. None of the three has been rubbed out and nothing has been written under any of them. The stone above the board's top edge goes on up in the colour it keeps. About four people came past the end of that row before the light came good, and not one of them stopped at it.

He took that board off its two nails and set it down on the flags with its face up, and ran the water off that bucket down the length of it from the near end to the far one with the flat of his hand. Then he wrung that rag out over the trough and put the rag on the stone lip of that step. He did not turn that board over. He did not do it again. The second pass that has gone down that board every morning of this flood did not go down it this morning.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came to the foot of that step with the empty bucket in his right hand. He looked at that board lying on the flags with one pass of water on it and the grit still in the grooves along the lower edge of it. He stood there and looked at it for about as long as it takes a man to count a thing twice and say something about it, and he did not say anything about it. He put the bucket down at the foot of that step, took that board up off the flags with his shoulder under the near edge of it, and set it back up on its two nails with the figures on it the way up they had been.

Two stalls along, a man said the thing that is said from behind his own trestle, where his board stands face in with the bare back of it out in the row. He put the flat of his right hand on the near edge of that board on the wall, to lean past it, and took that hand away, and looked at the flat of it.

"**There is grit in the lower edge of that board and there was not on it yesterday, and nobody in this row is going to tell me where it came from.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody said that he was right. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went back behind his own trestle with his hand still open in front of him and did not say a second thing about that board that morning. His piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket and it was not in his hand at any hour of it.

A man at the step said the thing that is said, to that board on the wall, and it was short.

"That is one wash."

"**There is one pass on that board this morning and there has been two every morning before it, and nobody in this row has asked me which of the two was the right one.**"

No mouth in that row improved on what he said, and none of them thanked him for it, and none of them said that he was right, and nobody in that row asked him anything else about that board. The man of about twenty-seven picked up the bucket and carried it the length of the row to the channel, and came back with it full.

---

Nobody in that row knew that the second pass did not happen because a man nobody in that row has ever spoken to did not make it. About four people who came past the end of that row between the fourth hour and the ninth hour read one set of those figures on their way past, and about four read none of them. Not one of the four looked at the lower edge of that board where the grit is. Not one of the four is going to. The store's own chalk was in that box all morning with its lid lying down on the rim of it, and it was not used. The piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man two stalls along was not out of that pocket at any hour of that day. The line of words that went out of this city is not in this city and is not coming back, and nothing in this flood brings it back.

He set his right hand flat against the near edge of that board at about the ninth hour to lean past it, and took it away again, and stood for a moment looking at the flat of it. Then he put both of his hands into his pockets and left them there for the rest of that morning. Nobody in that row told him anything at all, and nobody in that row said one word to him.

At the fourth hour tomorrow the man of about twenty-seven will take that board off its two nails, and he will find one pass on it where there have been two, and he will not ask anybody about it, and he will run the water down it twice. The grit will still be in the grooves along the lower edge, because it came out of a stack of wet boards somewhere four hundred yards from here and there is nothing in this row that lifts grit out of a groove. At the end of that day there was a mark on the right hand of a man nobody in that row had ever spoken to. He has not washed it off and he is not going to, and he does not know how it got there.
