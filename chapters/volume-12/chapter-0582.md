# Chapter 0582 — The Stone Above The Top Of That Board

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put the flat of his own left hand on the near edge of it and his own right hand down into the water in the bucket standing on the flags at the foot of that wall at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and sixty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and the water in that bucket was a hand's width down from the top of it and the cold of it went up his own arm past the elbow. There is one trough against the wall at that end of that market with the stone lip of the step above it, and the rag is on that stone lip, and the lid of the box on that step stands open with about a thumb's length of the store's own chalk showing in it. That board carries three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and about four people at that end of that market went past the end of that row before the light came good and not one of them stopped at it.

He put his own shoulder under the near edge of that board and lifted it off its two nails and set it down on the flags with its face up, and the grit that had worked into the wood of it came off it onto the stone in a grey line, and he turned the bucket over onto the top of it and ran the water down from one end of it to the other with the flat of his own hand and did it again from the same end, and then he put the board back up on its two nails with the figures on it the way up they had been, and none of the water on that stone had touched the stone above the top edge of that board at any hour of that morning.

---

A man at the step said the thing that is said, to the stone above that board.

"You washed that stone once and you have not touched it since."

"**I washed that stone once and I have not touched it since and nobody in this row is going to ask me about it.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody in that row thanked him for it and nobody in that row said that he was right, and nobody in that row asked him why he washed it that one time, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board went on down the row with the bucket in his own right hand and did not say another word at that hour.

---

He carried that bucket the length of the row to the channel, set it down on the bank, put his own hands in it up to the wrist, wrung the rag out in it and hung it over the rim of the trough, and refilled the bucket from the channel with the heel of his own boot holding it steady, and carried it back the length of the row, and set it down on the flags at the foot of that wall. The rag was on the stone lip of that step and it had been folded in two and wrung, and he did not fold it and he did not wring it, and nobody in that row said a word about that and nobody in that row said a word to him about it, and he put his own hand flat on the rag and turned it over where it lay and went back to the far end of that row with the bucket in his own hand.

A man two stalls along said the thing that is said, from behind his own trestle, where his own board stands face in with the bare back of it out in the row.

"Your rag is not how you left it."

"**It is not, and I have looked at it and I have not asked anybody about it either, and neither are you.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody in that row thanked him and nobody in that row said that he was right, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it from behind his own trestle and did not say a second thing that morning, and his own piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket and it was not in his own hand at any hour of it.

---

The washing of that board stops at the top edge of it every morning and has stopped there every morning of this flood, and the stone above that edge keeps the colour it has kept since the store was shut, and the place where one stops and the other begins is not a line anybody made and is not a line anybody has measured and has not moved by so much as the width of a thumb since the day the second nail of that board went into that wall. About four people at that end of that market went past the end of that row again before the light came off the flags and about four of them read one set of those figures on their way past and about four read none of them, and nobody in that row put a hand on the stone above the top of that board, and nobody in that row has said one word to the man who washes it.
