# Chapter 0583 — The Cost Named Before Anything Was Done

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both her own hands flat on the wood of the table under the one window, on either side of that sheet, at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and sixty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and she kept them there. The date on the door at the foot of that stair was in chalk and there is no name under it and there never has been one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one sheet of anything had been sent to anybody to ask them to come, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that stair at about the half hour after the ninth with his own knife not in his hand and nothing in either of his hands at all.

That sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place is not empty, and the figure in it is right and it is written out on no page in four hundred miles and it is not the figure that is going into that place, and a line of words is going under that figure on a day that is not this one, and it is not written yet and it is not on anything.

---

She named it before anybody did anything at all and she named it out loud.

"**Every figure in this city is one line of words short of being believed.**"

Then she said the other half of it out loud as well, that once that is done nobody in this city is going to take the words off any of the other figures again, and she said both of those in about a quarter of a minute and she put her own hands flat on the wood on both sides of that sheet again while she was saying them. Nobody in that room thanked her for naming it and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and the person it is about was standing at the near end of that bench with his own hands on his own knife and he heard all of it.

A man near the middle of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"That is a price and not a reason."

"**It is a price and I am not going to give a room a reason this morning, and I gave the price first on purpose, and you may write that down if there is anything in this room to write it on.**"

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the far wall, and it was short.

"**I know where that figure came from and I am not going to say, and nobody in this room is going to ask me.**"

Nobody in that room asked him. Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it, and nobody in that room said one word about where a figure of this city comes from or how long a person has had one, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and looked at the face of the sheet and then at the back of the bench and then at nothing at all.

---

A working slate was standing on the flags below the step of that room with a list of four things on it in a hand that was not the keeper's, and Adrian Vale carried it up that stair and stood it on its long edge against the front of that bench with the face of it turned out into the room, and nobody in that room said a word to him about it. He put the flat of his own left hand on the top of that slate and kept it there, and then he went down those steps again and came back up with a lump of the store's own chalk in his own right hand, the lid of that box standing open on the step with about a thumb's length showing in it, and he turned that lump over twice in his own hand and put it back in that box and put the lid of the box where it had been, which is standing open, and he came up that stair with the slate still standing on that bench and his own two hands empty.

That slate was in the way of the front edge of that bench from the ninth hour, and the register could not be opened on that bench with a slate standing against it, so the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the book out from behind that bench and opened it on the table under that one window instead, with the light of that window across the page from the side, and about four people in that room read the five figures in that column in that light and about four turned the page towards themselves to read it and nobody in that room said a word to Adrian Vale about any of it, and he was at the far end of that bench with his own back to the table and he did not know the book had been moved off that bench.

---

Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall and its column that holds figures holds five of them, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and nothing has been struck out of any of those five and nothing was added to that column that morning and nothing was taken out of it, and the column on the right of that page is the full height of that page with nothing whatever in it. On that shelf beside that book a second slate stands edge up with its written face against the plaster, and a day is cut across the head of it and nothing at all is under that day, and in that morning it stayed where it was standing, and nobody in that room lifted it off that shelf and nobody turned it over and nobody put anything on it, and the only hand that came anywhere near it was the keeper's, and the only hand in that room that came near the working slate standing against that bench belongs to a man nobody in that room has spoken to.

There is a line of words going under a figure on a sheet on a table in that room, and there is not one going under a figure on the slate standing against the front of that bench in a hand anybody at that end of that market could read, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of one man's morning and he did not know he had it and nobody in that room told him.
