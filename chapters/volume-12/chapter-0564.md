# Chapter 0564 — Nineteen One Way And A Different Number The Other

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came down the row at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and forty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned in against him and his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket on the other side, and he stopped at the wall at the end of that row, took that board out from under his arm, turned it about once, and set it down against the foot of that wall on its long edge with the face of it out into the row.

The store's board is on its two nails above the trough against that wall, with three sets of figures on it, and none of the three of them is rubbed out, and not one of the three of them carries a line of words under it. The trough is on its own spot and the rag is on the stone lip of the step and the box of chalk is on that step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of the store's chalk showing in it. The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at his own trough at that hour with his own two hands in the water and his own back to the row, and nobody in that row said a word to him about anything. About four people at that end of that market were in that row in the ordinary course.

---

That board has nineteen rates on it in three columns with the rows going across, and the rows are not even, and there are more of them in one column than in another. He counted them across with the first finger of his own right hand going along each row from the left of that board to the right of it and then along the next one down, and his own lips did not move while he did it, and he got nineteen. He did it again the same way and he got nineteen, and he put his own hand flat on the top rail of that board and looked down the length of that row at about four people who were walking past it and about four who were not.

Then he turned that board about on the ground with the toe of his own boot, so that the rows ran away from him instead of across, and he counted down the three columns with the same finger and he got a different number. He stood there with his own finger on the last line of that board for about as long as it takes a man to walk from the end of that row to the gate of it and back, and he did not say either number out loud.

---

A man at the near end of that row said the thing that is said, to the wall.

"You have been stood at that board long enough for two people to read it behind you."

"**It is nineteen down the rows and it is not nineteen down the columns, and I have known that for nine years and I have never said it to anybody, and you can read it off it yourself and there is nothing on it that stops you.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right. Nobody in that row improved on him and nobody in that row thanked him and nobody in that row asked him which of the two numbers he counted, and about four people in that row noticed that there is a space at the foot of the middle column of that board about as wide as two fingers and about four did not, and nobody in that row has ever asked him what was in it. There was a name in that space in his own hand and he took it off himself about nine years ago and has not said so to anybody since.

He picked that board up off the flags with the face of it turned in, put it back under his own arm, and went along that row and out of it, and the space at the foot of the middle column was on the underside of that board and not in the row for about as long as it takes a man to walk to the end of that row and back.

---

A woman with a stall two along came back along that row at about the eighth hour and stopped in front of where that board had been standing and turned it round on the ground with the toe of her own boot so that the rows ran away from her, and she counted down the three columns with her own forefinger, and she got the same different number that he had got, and she did not know that he had got it and she did not say it out loud and she went back to her own stall.

A fourth person came along that row at about the ninth hour and read that board from the far end of it with his own head the other way up, and about four people in that row saw him do that and about four did not, and there was no number on that board for anybody to write down.
