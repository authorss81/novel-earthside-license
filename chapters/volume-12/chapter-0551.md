# Chapter 0551 — A Second Pass Before The Light Came Off The Flags

Adrian Vale put the flat of his left hand under the bottom rail of the store's board at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and thirty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and got his right hand under the same rail, and lifted it off its two nails, and the nails came out of the stone with a small dry sound and stayed where they were. The rag was on the stone lip of the step where it goes every morning, and the bucket was standing inside the trough, and the trough was against that wall on its own spot with about a hand's depth of grey water in it that the fourth hour had left there. He did not read anything on the face of that board. He tied a loop of string round the top rail of it, took the weight of it on that loop, carried it down the two steps of the step, and laid it flat across the mouth of that trough with the wet side up.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the near end of that row with his back to the wall and a coil of rope over his own shoulder, and he turned round when the board came off the nails and he came back up that row and stood at the step and looked down at the face of that board and did not say anything for about as long as it takes a man to walk to the end of that row and back.

"I washed that at the fourth hour and I have washed it every morning since the seventh week and there is nothing on it that a rag will take."

"**Then that is what it is, and I have not come to take it off and I have not read it, and I have washed it once more and that is the whole of what I have done with it, and a board that has been washed twice in one morning is still a board with three things on it and not two.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him a second question about the three of them, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board took the coil of rope off his own shoulder, put it down on the flags inside the step, and picked his bucket up out of the trough and carried it to the channel at the near end of that row and back again, twice, and did not watch him do it.

---

The left-hand set of figures on that board is the account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and it came down that road four hundred miles in an office's own hand. The other two were put on in chalk in this city, the first of them on a day this city knows and the second of them on a later one, and the two of them do not agree with one another. There is nothing under any of the three. There has never been a line of words under any of the three on that board, and about four people a day walk to the end of that row and read one of them and go on, and about four walk to the end of that row and read one of them and stop there, and not one of the two groups has ever said one word to the other group about which one it read.

He worked the rag from the bottom left-hand corner of that board across and up to the top right-hand one and then did the upper half the same way, and the water came off the stone grey and went over the lip of that trough and along the flags of that row toward the channel at the near end, and the water going down that row this time was about twice what the fourth hour puts down it, and the row was fuller at that hour than it is at the fourth hour.

---

A woman with a stall two along had her own board propped on the flags in front of her at that hour with the foot of it resting on a flat stone. The water came off the lip of that trough and went along that row and got under the corner of that board and lifted it about the width of two fingers off the stone, and she came out from behind her own board and got down on one knee in the wet and put her own hand flat on the top of it and held it there while the water went past it, and she was there about as long as it takes a man to walk to the near gate of that row and back. She got up off that knee with her own hand wet to the wrist. She did not say one word to anybody about it and she did not look over at the step at any hour of that morning afterwards, and nobody in that row said a word to him about it either, and nobody in that row said that he was right.

---

The stone above the top edge of that board is not the colour it was the morning before the water came, and it has been washed once, and that was one morning, and no second person has put a hand on it since. He did not put a hand on it. He put the rag down on the stone lip of the step, took the loop of string off the top rail of that board with his right hand, carried it up the two steps on the loop, and put it back on its two nails with the three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and untied the string off it and put the string in his own pocket.

The box of chalk is on that step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of chalk showing in it, and that chalk is the store's and not anybody's, and it was not used at any hour of that morning. Two stalls along a man has his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and it was there all morning and it is not coming out of it.

At the ninth hour the shutters came off that store and a man set them flat against the wall on the far side of that board, and the three sets of figures on that board had been dry for about an hour by then, and the rag was on the stone lip of the step, and not one person in that row had looked at that board between the fourth hour and the ninth hour.
