# Chapter 0558 — Eleven Miles Of Flats And Nothing Taken Off Them

The trough went out along eleven miles of flats at about the fifth hour on the two hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, carried by two men at the handles, with the bucket and the rag inside it, and it came back up the same road at about the eighth hour with the bucket holding about a third of what it had gone out with and the rag stiff. The weather on that ground was a wind coming along the flats from the low side and no rain, and the wind had the silt out of the channel in the middle of those flats and the silt was lying on the stones on both sides of the channel in long low banks. There is no line in the ground along eleven miles of flats and there is no post on them and there has never been a mark on that ground that a person could stand in front of.

They set that trough down at the channel at about the sixth hour and took the bucket out of it and put it into the channel and pushed it along the bottom of it with the rim of it against the silt until it was full, and they carried that bucket up the bank and tipped it into the trough and did it three times, and the third of those times it went over the stone lip of that trough and about a hand's width of it went over the lip and down the side of it onto the stones. They put the rag into the channel and wrung it out with both of them at the same time and it came out about half what colour it went in, and they put the rag over the rim of the trough and left it there, and one of the two men at the handles stood at the bank of that channel for about as long as it takes a man to look at a bank of silt and then picked the trough up and they carried it on.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was out on that ground at about the sixth hour with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned into the weather. He did not trade. Nobody came up that eleven miles to be shown what was on it and he did not put it down and he did not hold it out and he stood with it under his arm at the near end of the channel for about as long as it takes a man to walk from the low side of that channel to the high side and back, and then he said one thing about the wind.

"**This wind has the banks on the stones and the channel is carrying about what it was carrying on Thursday and no more, and if it is going to carry more it will do it after the ninth hour and there is nothing anybody can do about it before then.**"

Nobody acted on that. The two men at the handles of the trough picked the trough up where they had set it down and carried it another two miles, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came along behind them with the board under his arm, and nobody asked him how he knew what the channel was carrying and he did not say and he is not going to.

A man at the handles of the trough said the thing that is said, to the low side of those flats.

"None of that stone will be dry by the ninth hour and the wind is not going to change before it gets there."

Nobody acted on that either. The two men at the handles carried that trough the last of that ground at the pace they had been carrying it at since the fifth hour, and the rag inside it did not come out any whiter than it went in, and there was nothing in this city for them to do about the wind except carry a trough along eleven miles of flats and bring it back up the same road.

---

A person was on that ground at about the same hour with a slate under an arm and a tin in two hands, going along the stones on the high side of the channel, and she did not come near the trough and the trough did not come near her. Nobody on that ground spoke to her and nobody on that ground asked her anything at any hour of that day, and she spoke to nobody, and nobody on that ground said a word to her about where she had been or about why she was going along there, and nobody on that ground thanked her for anything and she did not do anything in front of anybody that wanted thanking for.

---

There have been four mornings in this flood on which a figure of one kind of thing was made on foot across eleven miles of flats and there have not been a fifth. The four of them are the five hundred and eleventh day of this flood, the five hundred and fifteenth, the five hundred and seventeenth and the five hundred and twenty-first, and on each of those four mornings there was a person out on that ground with a slate under an arm and a tin in two hands, and the four figures they came out with do not agree with one another and are not permitted to agree. They are entered in a column of a book in a room over a market at one end of that road and nobody in this city has a fifth one and nobody in this city is going out on those flats to get one.

Eleven miles of flats went back to being eleven miles of flats at about the eighth hour, and there is no line in the ground along them and there is no post on them, and nobody walked that ground that day with anything to count.
