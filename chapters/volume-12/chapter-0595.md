# Chapter 0595 — The Round Goes Up That Road And Nobody Opens It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her own right hand on the folded sheet that was lying on the table under the one window of the room over a market at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and when the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the stair at the back of that room and put his own two hands out flat on the wood of that table on either side of it, she lifted that sheet off the table and set it into the satchel with a flap that was hanging open against his own hip, and let the flap of it down over the top, and the strap of it went round twice and she did not do the second turn herself. The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before that sheet was picked up off that table, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf was sitting at the near end of that bench with his own two hands on his own knees, and nobody in that room asked him one thing at any hour of that morning.

Nobody in that room opened that sheet. Nobody in that room looked at the face of it after it went into that satchel and nobody in that room asked the man who was carrying it what was in it, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living did not open it and was not going to and is not going to.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said the thing that is said, to the stair at the back of that room, and it was short.

"**Nobody has asked me what is in it and nobody is going to, and I know what is in it, and that is the whole of what I know about it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room argued with him about it, and he put the strap of that satchel over his own head and took the weight of it on his own right shoulder and went down that stair, and about four people at that end of the market below saw a man go past the end of that row with a satchel on him and none of them said a word to him and none of them came up that stair afterwards.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was short.

"**It is the first thing this flood has sent out of this city with words on it, and he is not on it and neither am I, and every other number in this city is bare now.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the one window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and looked at the face of that sheet going out of the room and said nothing whatever about it.

---

That sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there is a figure of this city's own in that one place under that heading, a line of words set under the figure, and neither the figure nor the words is written out on any page in four hundred miles and no hand in this city is going to take those words off it where it lies. It is folded along the two folds that came up that road in it and the bone is still lying on that table where it was set down, and nobody in that room picked the bone up and nobody wiped it and nobody turned it over.

The register is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and the column that holds figures has five in it with the day beside each and none of the five struck, and nothing was entered in that book that morning and nothing taken out of it, and the column on the right-hand side of that page is empty from its head to its foot and was empty when that morning began. A second slate stands on the same shelf on its edge with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and the only hand in that room that came near that shelf was the keeper's, and the keeper's hand went to the book and not to the slate.

At the near end of eleven miles of flats there is a yard with a stack of boards in it, and on the top of that stack a tally-board is lying face up, and the figure of this city's own is on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and that figure is written out on no page in four hundred miles and nobody in this city has ever been shown the working of it and nobody in this city is going to be. The man whose figure it is stood in that room for the whole of that morning and he is not on the sheet that went out of it, and being in that room did not help and nothing that happened in that room was ever going to help with that, and about four hundred miles off there is a man in an office who is going to be handed that page and is not going to be handed a word about who counted the number on it.

---

That round goes up four hundred miles of road out of this city on the ordinary round, and it is not coming back, and it is not being withdrawn, and nothing in this flood has an instrument for taking a line of words off a sheet and leaving a bare figure under a heading again.

The wood on that table under that one window is a shade paler in a rectangle about the size of that sheet than the wood round it, and it was not paler at the ninth hour, and nobody in that room rubbed at it and nobody in that room will, and that table will be one colour again in the ordinary course and there is no surface in this city that is going to be holding the shape of it.
