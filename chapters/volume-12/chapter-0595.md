# Chapter 0595 — The Round Goes Up That Road And Nobody Opens It

At about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her right hand on the folded sheet that was lying on the table under the one window of the room over a market. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the stair at the back of that room and put his two hands out flat on the wood of that table, on either side of it. She lifted that sheet off the table and set it into the satchel with a flap that was hanging open against his hip, and let the flap of it down over the top. The strap went round twice. She did not do the second turn herself. The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it.

About nine people were in that room at that hour, and every one of them was in that room before that sheet was picked up off that table. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was sitting at the near end of that bench with his hands on his knees, and nobody in that room asked him one thing at any hour of that morning.

Nobody in that room opened that sheet. Nobody looked at the face of it after it went into that satchel, and nobody asked the man who was carrying it what was in it. He did not open it and he was not going to and he is not going to.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said the thing that is said, to the stair at the back of that room, and it was short.

"**Nobody has asked me what is in it and nobody is going to, and I know what is in it, and that is the whole of what I know about it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody argued with him about it. He put the strap of that satchel over his head and took the weight of it on his right shoulder and went down that stair. About four people at that end of the market below saw a man go past the end of that row with a satchel on him, and none of them said a word to him, and none of them came up that stair afterwards.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was short.

"**It is the first thing this flood has sent out of this city with words on it, and he is not on it and neither am I, and every other number in this city is bare now.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the one window with his hand flat over his inside breast pocket, and he looked at the face of that sheet going out of the room, and said nothing whatever about it.

---

That sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading. There is a figure of this city's own in that one place and a line of words set under the figure. Neither the figure nor the words is written out on any page in four hundred miles, and no hand in this city is going to take those words off it where it lies. It is folded along the two folds that came up that road in it, and the bone is still lying on that table where it was set down. Nobody in that room picked the bone up. Nobody wiped it. Nobody turned it over.

The register is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall. The column that holds figures has five in it with the day beside each and none of the five struck, and nothing was entered in that book that morning and nothing taken out of it. The column on the right of that page is empty from its head to its foot and was empty when that morning began. A second slate stands on the same shelf on its edge, with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It did not move at any hour of that morning. The only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's, and that hand went to the book and not to the slate.

At the near end of eleven miles of flats there is a yard with a stack of boards in it, and on the top of that stack a tally-board is lying face up, and the figure of this city's own is on the side of it that the sun gets to now. That figure is written out on no page in four hundred miles. Nobody in this city has ever been shown the working of it and nobody in this city is going to be. The man whose figure it is stood in that room for the whole of that morning, and he is not on the sheet that went out of it, and being in that room did not help, and nothing that happened in that room was ever going to help with that. About four hundred miles off there is a man in an office who is going to be handed that page, and he is not going to be handed a word about who counted the number on it.

That round goes up four hundred miles of road out of this city on the ordinary round. It is not coming back and it is not being withdrawn, and nothing in this flood has an instrument for taking a line of words off a sheet and leaving a bare figure under a heading again.

---

That sheet is four hundred miles further off than it was at the ninth hour, and the shape of it on that table is the last of it inside these walls. The wood of that table is a shade paler in a rectangle about the size of that sheet than the wood round it, and it was not paler at the ninth hour, and nobody in that room rubbed at it and nobody in that room is going to. That table will be one colour again in the ordinary course, and there is no surface in this city going to be holding the shape of what was on it.
