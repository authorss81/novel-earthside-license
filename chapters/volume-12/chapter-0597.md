# Chapter 0597 — The Satchel Carried The Length Of A Wet Road

The sun was not on the top edge of the board on that wall yet, and the stone above the top edge of it had not been washed on any day of this flood but one. The trough was against that wall and the water in it was up to two hands. A rag lay on the stone lip of the step where it had been put down an hour before, and a man of about twenty-seven stood behind with a bucket in his hand. The board on that wall carries three sets of figures, of which none has been rubbed out, and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it.

At about the fifth hour on the two hundred and eighty-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came along that row. The strap of the satchel with a flap was in his right hand and his left hand was on the step at the foot of that wall. He went up the row with the satchel on him and did not open it at any point of that morning, and nobody on that ground said a word to him about it.

---

The yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats has a stack of boards in it and a barrow standing at the open end of it with nothing on it. A handcart with a coil of rope in it stood against the wall of that yard, with a hazel mallet in the cart and its head bound in cord. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf was at the foot of that stack with a knife in his right hand and the handle of it turned away from him. A tally-board stands on the top of that stack, face up, and the figure of this city's own is on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and there is a line of grey grit across the face of it that was not on it before. About four people a day walk past the open end of that yard, and not one of them has ever stopped at it.

At the open end of his own yard the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, and it was short.

"**That figure has been on the top face of that board all flood and nobody has ever come into that yard to read it, and nobody is going to.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said that he was right. Nobody in that yard asked him where that figure came from. The man who carries things for a living went past the open end of that yard and did not stop in it and did not look at the top of that stack, and nobody in that yard said one word to the man of about thirty-eight about the satchel.

---

At the near gate there is standing water across the row about the depth of a man's boot. The man of about thirty-one went into it with the satchel held up off his hip in his left hand, so that the bottom of it was above the water. He went through it in three goes, one for the near side of the row and two for the standing part. He did not put that satchel down on either side of that gate and he did not open it, and nobody came along that row in the ordinary course and asked him to set it down.

A man of about sixty with that handcart came up to that gate from the other side about the seventh hour and stopped at it and looked at the water across the row. He is the man who put a fence up out past the last named house in this flood, and he was not sent, and nobody has asked him about that fence since he put it up. He looked at the water and at the man going through it, and said nothing at all, and took the handcart back the way he had come.

Nobody in this city has ever asked that man of about sixty a question about a fence, and nobody has ever asked him where a thing came from. He put a coil of rope in that cart himself, and the head of that mallet is bound in cord, and that cord is not new.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said the thing that is said, to the road at the far side of that gate, and it was short.

"**It is shut and it is going up that road, and there is nobody in this city I could tell what is in it, and there was never going to be.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him a second thing about it. He went on up that road with the strap of that satchel across his chest, and about four people were on that ground at that hour, and not one of them knew what that satchel was.

---

That sheet went out of this city in that satchel two days ago, measured from the morning it went out, on the round. It has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, a figure of this city's own standing in that one place and a line of words set under it, and not one word of either is written out on any page in four hundred miles. There is no barrow in this city that has anything on it on any day of this flood, and the three that went down that other road in this flood went down it one apiece, out of a party of four, and are not on this road at all.

By the tenth hour the water at that near gate was a good deal lower than it had been at the seventh, and there was a line of boot prints in the wet of the road on the far side of it, going up. About four people came down that road after him and about four of them put a foot in one of those prints and about four did not. That satchel is further up that road now than anything else this flood has put on it has ever been, and nobody in this city has taken a measure of how far along it is, and nobody in this city is going to.
