# Chapter 0597 — The Satchel Carried The Length Of A Wet Road

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the strap of the satchel with a flap in his own right hand and his own left hand on the step at the foot of that wall at about the fifth hour on the two hundred and eighty-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and behind him the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had the bucket in his own hand and the rag on the stone lip of that step where it had been put down an hour before, and the trough was against that wall and the water in it was up to two hands. The board on that wall carries three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and the sun was not on the top edge of it yet and the stone above the top edge of it had not been washed on any day of this flood but one.

That man went up that row with that satchel on him and did not open it at any point of that morning and nobody on that ground said a word to him about it.

---

The yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats has a stack of boards in it and a barrow standing at the open end of it with nothing on it, and a handcart with a coil of rope in it stood against the wall of that yard with a hazel mallet in the cart with its head bound in cord. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf was at the foot of that stack with a knife in his own right hand and the handle of it turned away from him, and a tally-board stands on the top of that stack, face up, and the figure of this city's own is on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and there is a line of grey grit across the face of it that was not on it before, and about four people a day walk past the open end of that yard and not one of them has ever stopped at it.

At the open end of his own yard the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said the thing that is said, and it was short.

"**That figure has been on the top face of that board all flood and nobody has ever come into that yard to read it, and nobody is going to.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody in that yard thanked him for saying it and nobody in that yard said that he was right, and nobody in that yard asked him where that figure came from, and the man who carries things for a living went past the open end of that yard and did not stop in it and did not look at the top of that stack, and nobody in that yard said one word to the man of about thirty-eight about the satchel.

---

At the near gate there is standing water across the row about the depth of a man's boot, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living went into it with the satchel held up off his own hip in his own left hand so that the bottom of it was above the water. He went through it in three goes, one for the near side of the row and two for the standing part, and he did not put that satchel down on either side of that gate and did not open it and nobody came along that row in the ordinary course and asked him to set it down. A man of about sixty with that handcart came up to that gate from the other side about the seventh hour and stopped at it and looked at the water across the row, and he is the man who put a fence up out past the last named house in this flood and he was not sent and nobody has asked him about that fence since he put it up, and he looked at the water and at the man going through it and said nothing at all and took the handcart back the way he had come.

Nobody in this city has ever asked that man of about sixty a question about a fence and nobody in this city has ever asked him where a thing came from, and he put a coil of rope in that cart himself and the head of that mallet is bound in cord and that cord is not new.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said the thing that is said, to the road at the far side of that gate, and it was short.

"**It is shut and it is going up that road, and there is nobody in this city I could tell what is in it, and there was never going to be.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on that and nobody on that ground thanked him for saying it and nobody on that ground asked him a second thing about it, and he went on up that road with the strap of that satchel across his own chest and about four people were on that ground at that hour and not one of them knew what that satchel was.

---

That sheet went out of this city in that satchel two days ago, measured from the morning it went out, on the round, and it has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, a figure of this city's own standing in that one place and a line of words set under it, and not one word of either is written out on any page in four hundred miles, and there is no barrow in this city that has anything on it on any day of this flood and the three that went down that other road in this flood went down it one apiece out of a party of four and are not on this road at all.

The water at that near gate was a good deal lower by the tenth hour than it had been at the seventh and there was a line of his own boot prints in the wet of the road on the far side of it, going up, and about four people came down that road after him and about four of them put a foot in one of those prints and about four did not, and nobody in this city has taken a measure of how far along that road that man is and nobody in this city is going to.
