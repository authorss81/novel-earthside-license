# Chapter 0572 — Four Who Know And Four Who Do Not

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of both her own hands on the spine of that register on the shelf behind that bench when the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and he came up it without his board, which is the way he comes up it when he is not selling anything. That stair has a date in chalk on the door at the foot of it and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before he came.

The sheet that came up that road in a satchel with a flap was lying on the table under the one window with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place was not empty, and nobody in that room looked at it while he was saying what he had come to say.

---

He put his own hands in his own pockets and stood at the near end of that bench and looked across that room at the table under the one window and then at the back of that bench, and he said it once and he did not stop in the middle of it.

"**A figure of this city. It has lain face down there since before the water came.**"

Not one of the nine in that room would say so. Nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and a man at the step said the thing that is said, to the middle of that room.

"Nobody has ever asked him what is on the other side of that board."

"**Nobody has, and he is at the near end of eleven miles of flats and nobody in this city has asked him about that board at any hour of this flood, and I am not going to be the one who starts it and neither is anybody in this room.**"

That was two things said to a step and the second of them was not improved on by anybody either.

---

She took that register off that shelf and put it down on the bench on its spine and opened it at the head of the column of it that holds figures and put the flat of her own hand on the top of that column and shut it and put it back on that shelf with its spine to the wall. The column of that book that holds figures has five figures in it, one under another, in the order they were made in, and the day each was made is written beside it, and the newest of those five stands at the head of that column and the oldest at the foot of it, and nothing has been struck out of any of the five and nothing was added to that column that morning and nothing was taken out of it. The column on the right of that page is the full height of that page from the head of it to the foot of it, and there is nothing whatever in it at any hour of that day.

There is one tally-board at that wharf and it is lying face down on a stack of boards, and there is a figure of this city on the side of that board that the sun does not get to, and no hand in this city has turned that board over, and about four people walk past that yard in the ordinary course every day of this flood and there is a number lying face up in the open on the top of a stack there that none of them can see.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned out into that room, and he looked at the man of about thirty-four once and then at the back of that bench and said nothing at all. About four people in that room had the whole of it before the tenth hour and about four had not, and not one of those nine said out loud which of them had and which of them had not.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went down that stair with his hands in his pockets and his own board was two stalls along in that row standing face in against his own trestle with the bare back of it out in the row, and nobody in that room said one word to him about his going. The second slate on that shelf was upright with its face in to the wall and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it was not moved and it was not turned over and it was not written on, and the keeper's was the only hand in that room that came near that shelf all morning.

About four people in this city know what is on the far side of that board and about four do not, and one of the four who knows was standing at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket, and the other of that four was at that salt wharf at the far end of those flats, and none of the four who do not know is going to be told one word about it this week.
