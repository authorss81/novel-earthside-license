# Chapter 0590 — The Wall Said One Thing And A Man Agreed With It Out Loud

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her own right hand on the table under the one window at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and seventy-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and the sheet with the figure in the one place under the heading on it and a line of words under that figure was lying face up on the same table about a hand's width from her own hand, and the bone that was used on the folds of that sheet was lying beside it. She drew that sheet toward her across the wood by about a hand's width and squared it against the edge of that table, and then she took her own hand off the table and put it flat on the wood beside that sheet and left it there. There was a date in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever gone under one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them was sent for anything, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of that bench and was not asked anything by anybody in that room.

Through that one window the store's board is on its two nails on the outside wall at the end of that market, carrying three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and the stone above the top edge of that board goes on up in the colour it keeps, and about four people at that end of that market were standing in that row at that hour and the rest of that row was empty.

The wall above the top of that board said one thing that morning and it came about the middle of it, out of the store's own stone, the stone the old figures are chalked on, and nothing in this flood has ever put anything on that stone and nobody had been near it to make it do that.

> A line of words put under a figure by a person who did not count that figure is not a record of what the figure counts. It is an agreement, made in a room, about what the figure counts, by people who did not agree, and a reader four hundred miles off cannot tell the difference between the two, and a figure with an agreement under it cannot be read without the agreement and cannot be checked against it, because a number cannot say what it counts.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall refused it in about four seconds. It was a handful of words and he had his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket while he said them and the piece of chalk in that pocket has not come out since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and did not come out of it on that morning.

"**That is right and I have never said so out loud in a room in my life, and now I have.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody in that room said that he was right, and he did not ask to be thanked and did not ask to be argued with. He has said what is wrong with a page in about nine words, three volumes running, and been thanked by nobody three times, and agreeing with a page out loud in a room in front of about nine people is the one thing he has never done in this flood, and he did it at about the fourth second and about four people at that end of the market below heard their own names come out of a room they were not in.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to that sheet, and it was short.

"**It is a rule and not an answer, and it does not settle that line of words and it does not take it off, and there is nothing on any surface in this city that says whether I am right to have written it.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said nothing at all about any of it and was not asked anything about any of it, and what his figure is a figure of is not in that room and nobody in that room knows it.

---

Nobody in that market said that thing on that wall out loud again. About four people at that end of that market read that board afterwards and about four did not, and the wall did not say anything else on that day, and it is not going to say anything else in this flood.

The store's own chalk was in its box on the step below that window with the lid of it standing open, and that chalk was not used on that morning, and the piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man at the far end of that bench was not out of that pocket at any hour of that day, and what is written on that sheet was written in ink. Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall and its column that holds figures holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of those five has been struck, and the column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom, and on the same shelf a second slate is standing edge up with its written face to the plaster and a day cut into the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and nobody in that room went near it, and the keeper's hand went to the book and not to that.

---

At about the half hour after the tenth the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register folded that sheet along the two folds that came up that road in it, pressed the bone along the whole length of each fold with the heel of her own hand, set the bone down on that table, and put the flat of her own hand on the folded sheet for a moment and then took her hand off it. That sheet is shut on that same table with the figure in the one place under the heading and the line of words under that figure, and it is not going to that road today and it is not going to that road on any day before the round, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was not in that room and will not be in it again until that round is on the road, and there is no satchel in that room.

The wall above the top edge of that board said one thing at about the middle of that morning and it came out of the store's own stone where the old figures are chalked, and the stone above that top edge has not been washed on any morning of this flood but one, and that one was the man who washes that board and no second person, and nobody in that row has ever asked him why he washed that stone the one time he washed it, and nobody in that row has ever asked him why he has not washed it since, and he is not going to be asked either of those things by anybody in this city.
