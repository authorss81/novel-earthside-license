# Chapter 0575 — It Took About A Quarter Of An Hour And There Was No Vote

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her own hand on the table under the one window, on the wood beside that sheet, from about half past eight in the morning on the two hundred and sixtieth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and she kept it there while the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour with his own right hand on the rail and his own left hand hanging empty at his side. That date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one sheet of anything had been sent to anybody to ask them to come.

He had walked up that stair from the near end of eleven miles of flats with nothing in his hands. He has a tally-board of his own and a knife of his own and a barrow of his own, and there is a figure of this city on the side of that tally-board that the sun does not get to, and that board was not in that room and was lying face down on a stack of boards at that wharf while he was standing in it.

---

He stood at the end of that bench and he did not sit down and he named it before he said anything else about it, in about eleven words, in his own mouth, to the room.

"**That figure will never be read again except through my words.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it and nobody in that room improved on it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took her own hand off the wood beside that sheet and put both of them flat on the front edge of the bench.

"The reason for that is yours, and you have not given it to the room yet."

"**The figure that came down that road twenty-three days ago, measured from the morning it came up, has been read bare by everybody in this city since the morning it arrived, and a line of words is going under the one I am sending back, and when that is done the figure cannot be read on its own by anybody in four hundred miles, and the words are mine and the figure is not.**"

Nobody in that room told him that he was right.

---

Nobody in that room can say what the figure in that one place is a figure of. About four people in that room had worked out that the figure that came down that road on the five hundred and fifty-second day of this flood and the figure on the side of that tally-board are of one kind of thing, and about four had not, and not one of the nine of them could have said out loud what the kind of thing is, and nobody asked the man whose board it is where that figure came from and he did not say.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned out into that room, and he said it to the table and it took him about four seconds to say it.

"**Whatever goes in that place leaves this city on the round and it cannot be walked back, and that is the whole of what the words under it will do, and it is not nothing.**"

No mouth in that room said that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on him and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and looked at the face of that board and then at the man of about thirty-eight and said nothing whatever.

---

there was no vote and there has never been one in this city. The four days that would be a cooling are not a thing this city can arrange on a morning, and a room that is handed something to decide finds out what it has been handed on the morning it is handed it, and that is how every one of them has gone in this city.

Nobody in that room filled the one place on that sheet and nobody in that room wrote a word under anything, and not one surface in this city carried a line of words under a figure at the end of that morning or at the beginning of the afternoon. A line of words was agreed to on that day and it is not written yet, and what is in it is not in that room's keeping.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf stood in that room for the whole of it and he is not on any page that carries his own figure, and being in that room did not help him, and no part of that decision needed him to be in that room, and he is not going to be thanked for it by anybody in this city because there is nothing to thank.

Behind that bench that book is on its shelf with its spine to the wall, and its column of figures holds five, in the order they were made in, and the column on the right of that page is empty from its head to its foot. On the same shelf there is a second slate, up on its edge with its face to the wall, and a day is cut across the head of it and nothing is under that day, and nobody in that room went near that shelf at any hour of it, the keeper included.

It took about a quarter of an hour, from the ninth hour until about the half hour after it, and there is nothing on any surface in this city that says what happened in that quarter of an hour, and nobody in that room wrote any of it down, and the man who decided it was at the near end of eleven miles of flats that afternoon with his own knife in his own hand and his own board lying face down on a stack of boards, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that will put a decision and the man who took it on the same surface.
