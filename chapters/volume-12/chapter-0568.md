# Chapter 0568 — A Figure That Came Down The Road And A Figure That Went Up It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register opened the register on the bench at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and fifty-third day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and put the flat of her own left hand on the head of the column of that book that holds figures and kept it there. That morning's day was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, and no name has ever gone under a day on that door. About nine people were in that room at the seventh hour and none of them had been sent for anything.

Five figures stand in that column, one under another in the order they were made in with the day written beside each of them in her own hand, and the newest of the five is at the head of it. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page and there is nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom, and she did not look at it. On the shelf where that register stands there is also a second slate, up on its edge and facing the wall, carrying a day across the head of it and nothing at all under that day. Nobody lifted it that morning and nobody turned it and nobody wrote on it, and the only hand in that room that came near that shelf was hers, and it was not the hand that touched it.

---

The figure at the head of that column is a figure of this city and it went out of this city in a satchel with a flap on the day it was sent and it is not coming back, and it came off the fourth of the four mornings on which a figure of one kind of thing was made on foot across eleven miles of flats, and that morning was the five hundred and twenty-first day of this flood. The figure at the foot of that column is a figure of this city and it is the same figure that is on a wall at the end of that market in chalk, in the middle of three sets of figures, and it has been on that wall since this city put it there and it has been at the foot of that column ever since. The set of figures on the left-hand end of that board came down that road four hundred miles in an office's own hand, and the one on the right-hand end of it is a figure of this city in chalk on a later day and it is not in that book at all and never has been. Not one of the three on that board carries a line of words under it.

The figure in the one place under the heading on the sheet lying on the table under the one window is a figure of this city and it came down that road sixteen days ago, measured from the day it came up it, in a satchel with a flap, and this city did not put it there. Neither of the two figures is written out on any page in four hundred miles, and no page puts the two of them as two figures and no page puts them as one figure, and no page says where either of them came from.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he said the thing that is said, to the two of them, in about four seconds.

"**One of those came up that road and one of those went up it, and they are the same kind of number, and there is nothing in this city that will put the two of them in one order.**"

Not one of the nine in that room agreed with him aloud, and nobody said he had it wrong either. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was in that room and was not asked which of the two of them she thought was the one a person ought to be using, and she did not say.

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to the window.

"Then take the one that came down the road and write it up on that board and the two of them will agree."

"**Nobody is writing a figure of this city up on that wall, and the wall has three on it now that do not agree with one another and none of them has ever been rubbed out, and adding a fourth would not make the other three agree and would make four.**"

Nobody in that room answered him. Nobody in that room said that he was right, and about four people in that room had worked out by that hour that the figure that came down that road and the figure that went up it are of one kind of thing and about four had not, and not one of the nine said so out loud.

---

The sheet was not laid beside that book and was not in one hand with that book and was not compared out loud in a mouth in that room, and at about the eleventh hour the register was shut and put back on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and the sheet stayed face up on the wood under the one window with its one place showing and nothing under that figure anywhere on it.

Two floors down and one stair down from that table there is a figure of this city at the head of a column of a book in a room, and on the outside wall at the end of the market there is the same figure again at the foot of that column, and on the table in that room there is a figure of this city in a single place under a heading with nothing under it, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that carries one of them out of the place it is in and puts it in the order the other two are in.
