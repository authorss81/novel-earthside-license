# Chapter 0598 — A Question Put To One Man And Not To A Room

At about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eighty-third day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had the flat of his right hand over his inside breast pocket at the near end of the bench in the room over a market. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with his board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room. That morning's date was in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair, in the ordinary way, and no name has ever gone under one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them was sent for anything. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the one window. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of that bench with his hands on the wood of it.

The question was put to one man and not to that room, and it was put at about the ninth hour, and it was short, and nobody in that room heard it.

"**What is a person called, in a column, on a page, when the only thing anybody will ever use the name for is to be able to say where the person was.**"

The room did not hear that. The man it was put to did not answer it at that hour and has not answered it at any hour since. He said nothing at all back to the man who put it, and the man who put it put the flat of his other hand down on the front edge of that bench, and did not put it a second time.

About nine people went on doing what they had come into that room to do. About four of them read the days written beside the five figures in the column that holds figures, and about four did not, and one man at that table turned his cup round on the wood and said nothing to anybody. Nobody in that room knew that a question had been asked. Nobody thanked the man who asked it and nobody improved on it, and nobody asked either of those two men a second question about anything at all that morning. The man of about thirty-nine took his board up off that bench, put it under his arm, and said nothing whatever about any of it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the one window, and it took him about four seconds.

"**Three of them went down that road out of a party of four, and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on it, and a road that can be shown is a road that can be put in a column as empty.**"

No mouth in that room improved on what he said and none of them thanked him for it and none of them argued with it, and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it. Nobody in that room asked him about his own board or about the figure on it either. The man of about thirty-four looked at the back of his own board out in the row through that one window and said nothing whatever.

---

The man who put that question is the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall. He did not say before he put it that it was a better question than the last one, and he did not say after it that it was worse, and there is no surface in this city on which either of those two things has been written down.

The man it was put to has a space on his own board about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two. He took a name off that board in his own hand about nine years ago and has never said so to anybody. Nobody in that room asked him about that space this morning and nobody in that room was ever going to. The question that was put to him at the ninth hour was about a name in a column and not about the one that is not on his own board, and nobody in that room joined the two together and nobody in this city is going to.

That road went down out of this city on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood with three barrows on it, one apiece, and a party of four is four, and one of that four said no before anybody was asked. Nothing in this flood has counted the days since and nothing in this flood is going to.

---

That question is now standing in this city with nobody holding it. It was said once, at about the ninth hour, in a room with a date in chalk on the door and about nine people in it, and nine people did not hear it and the one man it was put to has not answered it at any hour since. It is not on the board on the wall at the end of that market, and it is not in the book on the shelf behind that bench, and it is not written on either side of the two columns in that open page. It is not going to be written down by anybody in this city. A later room may put it to a man again, in daylight, with a date on the door. Nothing in this volume is going to put it to anybody twice, and nothing in this volume is going to answer it.
