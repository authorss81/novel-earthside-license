# Chapter 0598 — A Question Put To One Man And Not To A Room

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had the flat of his own right hand over his own inside breast pocket at the near end of the bench in the room over a market at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eighty-third day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with his own board under his own arm and the face of it turned out into that room. That morning's date was in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair, in the ordinary way, and no name has ever gone under one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them was sent for anything, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the one window and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands on the wood of it.

The question was put to one man and not to that room, and it was put at about the ninth hour, and it was short, and nobody in that room heard it.

"**What is a person called, in a column, on a page, when the only thing anybody will ever use the name for is to be able to say where the person was.**"

The room did not hear that. The man it was put to did not answer it at that hour and has not answered it at any hour since, and he said nothing at all back to the man who put it, and the man who put it put the flat of his own other hand down on the front edge of that bench and did not put it a second time.

About nine people went on doing what they had come into that room to do, and about four of them read the days written beside the five figures in the column that holds figures and about four of them did not, and one man at that table turned his own cup round on the wood and said nothing to anybody. Nobody in that room knew that a question had been asked. Nobody in that room thanked the man who asked it and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room asked either of those two men a second question about anything at all that morning, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took his own board up off that bench and put it under his own arm and said nothing whatever about any of it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf said the thing that is said, to the one window, and it took him about four seconds.

"**Three of them went down that road out of a party of four, and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on it, and a road that can be shown is a road that can be put in a column as empty.**"

No mouth in that room improved on what he said and none of them thanked him for it and none of them argued with it, and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it, and nobody in that room asked him about his own board or about the figure on it either, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at the back of his own board out in the row through that one window and said nothing whatever.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall is the man who put that question and he did not say before he put it that it was a better question than the last one and he did not say after it that it was worse, and there is no surface in this city on which either of those two things has been written down. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board has a space on his own board about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two, and he took a name off that board in his own hand about nine years ago and has never said so to anybody, and nobody in that room asked him about that space this morning and nobody in that room was ever going to, and the question that was put to him at the ninth hour was about a name in a column and not about the one that is not on his own board, and nobody in that room joined the two together and nobody in this city is going to.

That road went down out of this city on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood with three barrows on it, one apiece, and a party of four is four, and one of that four said no before anybody was asked and nothing in this flood has counted the days since and nothing in this flood is going to.

---

Out past the last named house that road has a corner at it, and about four people in this city have walked as far as that corner and about four have not, and it is the only part of that road this city could be shown by anybody at all, and the three barrows that went down it are not standing on the corner of it and are not standing anywhere this city can reach, and about nine people in a room over a market this morning were told nothing whatever about any of that, and one of those nine was asked a question and did not answer it.
