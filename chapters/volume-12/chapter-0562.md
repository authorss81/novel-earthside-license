# Chapter 0562 — Sixteen Posts And Eleven Withies And Nobody Counting Them

The man of about sixty put the coil of rope down on the top of the shut road at about the sixth hour on the two hundred and forty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, with the hazel mallet standing up against his own thigh and its head bound in cord where the cord has gone grey and hard, and he took his own right hand off the top wire of that fence and put it flat on the near post of the corner and left it there while he looked along the top of the fence to the far end of it and back again.

The date was in chalk on the door of the room over the market a long way from that ground and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. Out past the last named house the road has been shut since the water came and the fence stands along the top of it. There is no line in the ground along that road and no post in it and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on it.

---

That fence is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies, and the withies are bound to the posts with the same cord through all eleven of them, and the corner at the far end of it is a corner that runs about the width of a hand and stops, and nobody has taken a cord round it. Nobody in this city has a cord round that corner. Nobody took a measure of that fence on that morning and nobody moved a post in it and nobody bound a withy that was not bound.

A person with a tin pail came up the low side of that road at about the seventh hour carrying it in one hand by the handle and a fold of sacking over the other arm, and went along the inside of that fence to the corner and could not get past the corner, and came back again with the pail about half full. She came up that road again at the eighth hour and again at the ninth and again a little after that, and she came back down it four times, and she did not speak to anybody on that ground and nobody on that ground spoke to her.

---

A man at the near end of that fence said the thing that is said, to the ground.

"She is going to be carrying that half pail up that road until there is no water left to carry."

"**She will, and I put that fence up, and nobody sent me and nobody has asked me why, and if you want to know what that corner is for I do not know either and I am not going to pretend to you that I do.**"

Nobody on that ground said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody on that ground asked him a second question about the corner, and the man of about sixty picked the hazel mallet up off the road where he had stood it against his own thigh and carried it and the coil of rope back along the top of the road to where the road comes in under the last named house, and he did not stop at the corner and he did not look at it again.

---

The road that goes up out of this city and away in the other direction is a different road from that one and it is four hundred miles long, and a sheet has come up it four times in this flood and gone up it twice, and the road it comes up is open from end to end and has been since the water came down. On the shut road there is nobody at all, and the four who set out in this flood were four, and three of them went down the four hundred miles with a barrow apiece on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and one of the four said no before anybody was asked and that is all that is said about her in this city.

Nobody on that ground knows where the three are and nobody in this city is going to be shown one person on that road, and no mouth on that ground said anything about the three, and the man of about sixty was on that ground for about an hour and was not asked about them and did not say.

---

The wind came off the low side along that shut road at about the ninth hour and moved the top wire of that fence about the width of a thumb and let it go twice, and the corner at the far end of it stood open on the inside, and the withies on the last two posts of it had slack in them that a hand could have taken up in about as long as it takes a man to walk from the corner to the near end of that fence and back.
