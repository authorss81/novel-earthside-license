# Chapter 0566 — The Corner Nobody Has Taken A Cord To

The man of about fifty-seven stood at the corner of that fence at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and fifty-first day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, with his own two hands down at his own sides and his own coat open at the front, and he came out of the house he came out of and the length of new rope is over the back of a chair in that house and it has never been used and no hand in this flood has lifted it off that chair. Nobody in this city has asked that man anything at any hour of this flood and nobody asked him anything that morning and he was not asked where that fence was or by whom or when, and he did not say.

The fence along the top of that shut road is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies, and the withies are bound to the posts with the same cord through all eleven of them. Nobody counted those posts and nobody counted those withies that morning, and nobody took a measure of that fence and nobody moved a post in it and nobody bound a withy that was not already bound.

---

A man came along the top of that road at about the eighth hour with his own two hands full of a short length of cord with a loop tied in one end of it, and he came to the corner and stood at it, and he looked down at the two posts of that corner and at the slack in the withy on the one that is nearest the shut end of the road, and he did not put that cord round the corner. He stood at that corner for about as long as it takes a man to walk from the corner to the near end of that fence and back, and then he went back along the top of that road with the cord in his own two hands and he did not use it and he did not leave it.

Nobody on that ground said anything to him about it and nobody on that ground said that he was right, and the man of about fifty-seven did not turn round while that man was standing at that corner and did not say a word to him and was not asked by him for anything.

---

A woman at the near end of that fence said the thing that is said, to the road.

"That corner is the one place on this road that is not finished and it is about as long as a man to get round it on foot."

"**You are not going to get round it on foot and neither is anybody else, and it has been like that since the water came and it is not going to be otherwise before the middle of the week, and I have not come out here to see to that corner and I have not come out here to be told about it either.**"

Nobody on that ground said that he was right. Nobody on that ground improved on him and nobody on that ground thanked him, and about four people on that ground heard that sentence and about four did not, and the woman at the near end of that fence did not ask him a second question and he did not volunteer one, and he is not going to be asked anything about that fence in this city by anybody.

---

Three barrows went down the four hundred miles of that road in this flood with a man on the wheel of each, and the day they went was the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on that road and nobody on that ground that morning said anything about the three. The shut road is not that road. The shut road runs out past the last named house and stops being a road about where that fence stops, and it has been shut since the water came, and about four people were on that ground in the ordinary course and about four were not, and the fence is between that ground and the far end of that shut road and it is the fence and not anything else.

Whether that fence stands between the right two things is a question nobody on that ground asked that morning and nobody answered, and nobody took a measure of that fence in order to ask it.

---

At about the ninth hour the wind came along that shut road and there were three sets of wheel-ruts in the wet of it with standing water in all three, and the deepest of the three was the middle one, and the corner of that fence stood open above all three of them with nothing at all round it.
