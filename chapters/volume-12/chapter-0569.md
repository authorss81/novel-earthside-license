# Chapter 0569 — A Hurdle He Made And A Bar He Replaced

The man of about fifty-nine came up the stair at the back of that room at about the tenth hour on the two hundred and fifty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, with a hurdle under his own right arm and the head of it up past his own shoulder, and he came the whole way up those steps with it and set it against the wall at the near end of that room with its foot on the boards and its top rail about level with his own chest. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About four people were in that room at that hour and the rest of it was empty.

He made that hurdle himself out of hazel and a bar goes across the bottom of it, and there is a bar in it now and that bar is not the bar that was in it, and he took the old one out and put a new one in himself and the new one is about a head's width off the width of the old one and is set a finger's width higher on the left-hand side than on the right-hand side. The two marks where the old bar sat are still in the wood on both uprights, and they are about a head's width apart and they are below the marks the new bar has made.

---

A woman near the middle of that room said the thing that is said, to the hurdle.

"That thing is standing in the middle of a room and it is not doing anything."

"**It is standing up out of the way against a wall, and that is what it is for, and it has a bar in it that I put in myself and I am not going to stand here and talk about a bar.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on him and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room asked him a second question about the hurdle or about the bar, and about four people in that room had worked out that he had come up that stair with something in his own hands and about four had not.

---

Nobody in that room asked him where he had been and nobody in that room asked him when he was going out on that road again and nobody in that room asked him about the three who went down it. He has been asked once before, in this city, by a person who said out loud at the time that she was not going to say it better than it had been said the last time it was said in that room, and he did not answer it then and the room did not hear it. Nobody asked it again this morning and he did not offer it and he is not going to be asked it in this city by anybody.

Three barrows went down the four hundred miles of that road in this flood, one of them to a man, on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, out of a party of four of whom three went and one said no before anybody was asked. A party of four is four. Nobody in that city can be shown one person standing on that road and no mouth in that room said anything about the three this morning, and the man of about fifty-nine was in that room and was not asked about them and did not say.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench with his own hands on his own knees and the satchel with the flap on the floor under it and the flap open and nothing in it, and nobody in that room asked him what was in it. Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall and it was not opened at any hour of that morning, and behind that register the second slate stands on its edge with its face in to the wall with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move and it was not turned over and it was not written on, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf.

He put his own right hand flat on that new bar and pressed it down about as far as the wood would go, and took it off again, and the marks that the old bar had left were still in the wood underneath his own hand and nothing had been planed out of them and nothing was going to be.
