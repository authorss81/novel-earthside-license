# Chapter 0581 — Two Empty Places In One Room

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and sixty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, with that board under his own arm, and he took it out from under his arm and stood it up against the near end of that bench with the face of it turned out into the room, and then he put the flat of his own right hand on the top rail of that bench above it and left it there. The date on that door was in chalk and no name has ever been written under it, and the wood under that date is bare and has been bare for as long as that date has been standing on that door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them had come up that stair for anybody else.

There are nineteen rates on the face of that board in three columns with the rows going across, and along the bottom of the column between the other two there is a space about two fingers wide with nothing whatever in it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the far end of that bench with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the far window with her own back to the shelf behind that bench, and neither of them looked at that space at the hour the board went up against the wood.

---

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to that space.

"That is a place for a name."

"**It is a place a name was in and somebody took it out himself, and nobody in this room is going to put one in it and nobody in this room is going to ask him.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it to a step and not to a man, and he did not look at the man whose board it was while he was saying it, and he put his own hand deeper into that pocket afterwards and took nothing out of it.

A woman at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the same space.

"It could be a rate for a thing nobody here sells."

"**It is not a rate and I am not going to say what it is, and you may all look at it until the light comes off that window.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that either. Nobody in that room said a word to the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board about that space, and he did not say a second thing, and about four people in that room went along the rows of that board with a finger while he stood there and about four did not go along a single row of it, and not one of the nine of them said out loud which of the two groups had.

---

Three surfaces can be seen from that room and they do not read the same way. The board that was standing against that bench reads across itself, a row at a time, and nineteen of the rows have something at the end of them. On the outside wall at the end of that market, through that one window, the store's board is on its two nails carrying three sets of figures of which none has been rubbed out and not one of the three is carrying a line of words under it, and it reads across itself too, and the three sets do not agree with one another and nobody at that end of that market has ever said out loud which of the three is the one to read. And behind that bench there is a book standing on that shelf with its spine to the wall, and its column that holds figures reads down the page one figure under another, and there are five of them in it and the fifth was put there before this flood, and the column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came away from that window, put the flat of both her own hands on the front edge of that bench, drew the book out from behind it by about the width of a hand and set it down on the wood with the spine of it toward the far wall, and then she opened it at the head of that column, ran her own first finger down the five figures and stopped at the fifth, and shut it, and put the book back where it had been with the spine of it to the wall, and did not say one word while she did any of it and about nine people in that room watched her do all of it.

---

A man at the step said the thing that is said, to that book.

"You looked at that column and shut it again the same way you have shut it every morning."

"**A column of five is a full column and a full column does not take a sixth thing in the middle of a morning, and I have not been asked what the other one on that page is for and nobody in this city is going to ask me.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right. Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and the column on the right of that page is the full height of that page and is empty in it this morning and will be empty in it at the end of that day, and nobody in that room put a word in it, and nobody in that room said what it is for.

On the same shelf as that book there is a second slate standing on its edge with its face turned in to the wall, and a day is cut across the head of it and nothing whatever is under that day, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on at any hour of that day, and the only hand in that room that came near where it stands is the keeper's.

The face of that board is worn smooth in a band about the width of a hand across the top of it, at the height of the rail of that bench, and the space at the foot of the column between the other two is on a part of that board that is not worn, because about nine years of a board being carried under one arm has not gone near the bottom of it, and nobody in that room turned that board over to look at the bottom of it.
