# Chapter 0578 — Nine Words From The Near End Of That Bench

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and sixty-third day of the Bare Month, a Friday, with his own board under his own arm, and he took it off from under that arm and stood it against the near end of that bench with the face of it turned out into that room, and then he put the flat of his own left hand down on the top rail of that bench above it and left it there. That date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name under it and there never has been one, and the light came in at the one window of that room and not at the door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room for something of their own.

He stood at the near end of that bench and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the far end of it with her own back to the shelf behind her. The last time a question was put in that room it was put from that end of that bench and it was answered from this one, and the two people who did that are not the two people standing at the two ends of it this morning.

---

A man at the step said the thing that is said, to the door.

"Somebody had better put a name under that date before the end of the week."

"**Nobody in this room is going to write a name under a date on that door, and there is a bare place under that date for as long as that door stands up, and I have looked at that bare place every morning since the water came and it is still bare.**"

Not one of the nine in that room took it any further than she had taken it, and no mouth in that room thanked her for it or put a second question to her about it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the far window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and said nothing at all about the door or about the woman.

---

He looked at the shelf behind her for about as long as it takes a man to count nine before he said it, and he said it to her and not to the room, and it was nine words long and he said it once.

"**What do you call the thing on that shelf.**"

Not one of the nine in that room thanked him for putting it. Nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room answered it for him and nobody in that room said one word while it was asked, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not turn round and did not look up from the front edge of that bench where her own hands were lying flat.

She said it once, out loud, in her own mouth, in the local language, to a man and not to a room.

---

Nobody in that room asked her to say it again. Nobody in that room asked her to say it in a different way and nobody in that room said one word about it, and about four people in that room heard the whole of it and about four heard the first part of it, and not one of the nine of them could have said to anybody else in four hundred miles what she had called it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board did not repeat it and did not write it down and did not ask her whether it was the right thing to call it, and his own board was standing against the bench beside him with the face of it out and the space about two fingers wide at the foot of the middle column of it still not filled.

The two of them did not discuss it. The two of them did not stand in that room and work out what it had been about, and nobody in that room asked either of them a second question, and about four people in that room worked out that the man at the near end of that bench had asked the woman at the far end of it one thing and about four worked out nothing at all.

---

On that shelf behind her there is a second slate standing edge up with its face in to the wall and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it has been standing there since before the water came and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on at any hour of that day, and no hand but the keeper's went near that shelf. There are nineteen rates on the face of that board under that man's arm in three columns with the rows going across, and a sheet with one place ruled under a heading was lying on the table under the one window where it had been lying since the morning it came up, and nothing was put in that one place and nothing was written under anything in this city on that day, and the one place was still showing what came down that road.

That slate was on that shelf at about the tenth hour with the same day cut across the head of it and nothing under that day that it had at the ninth hour, and the light had come round far enough to lay the edge of its own shadow across the spine of the book standing in front of it, and nobody in that room moved it out of that shadow and nobody put anything into it.
