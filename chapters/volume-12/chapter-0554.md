# Chapter 0554 — A Number And Nothing On The Paper Saying What It Counts

The sheet on the table under the one window was turned about at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and thirty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and nobody's hand was on it while it was turned. It was put down on the near corner of that table and the two of them who did it stepped back from it together and left it lying with its top edge toward the far side of that table and its one place in the light, and nobody in that room put a finger on it after that. There is a date in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever gone under one. About nine people were in that room at the eighth hour and not one of them had come up that stair for any reason but their own.

---

There is a heading at the top of that sheet and there is one place ruled under that heading, and the one place is not empty. A figure of this city is in it. It is in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in this room, and the figure is right, and it came down that road four hundred miles with a heading over it and nothing else on it, and the shape of that sheet is the shape of two other sheets that have come down that road in this flood and not the shape of the three that have four places ruled under a heading on them.

The heading at the top of that sheet is a heading. It is a line of type with no full stop on it and it does not say what the figure under it is a figure of. There is nothing on that sheet beside that figure and nothing under it and nothing on the other side of it, and there is no place on that sheet for the person who made it, and there never was one.

---

A man at the window said the thing that is said, to the sheet, and he said it once, and nobody in that room improved on him, and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room argued with him, and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it.

"**Nobody here can say what that number counts.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room said that he was wrong, and about four people in that room had worked out by that hour that there is nothing on that paper about what the number is a number of and about four had not, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall kept his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket two stalls along in the row below that window and said nothing whatever about any of it.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Then somebody has to go and find out what it is a number of."

"**I am not going and neither is anybody in this room, and the sheet came down that road and it will go back up that road, and there is nobody four hundred miles off from here who is going to be shown what it counted.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right the second time either. Nobody bettered a word of it and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody in that room said that the woman near the step was wrong, and nobody in that room said the name of any office or any clerk, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living sat at the far end of that bench with his hands on his knees and said nothing at all, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall and its cover shut. Five figures stand in the column of that book that holds figures, one under another, with the day beside each of them, and that column is full from the head of it to the foot of it. The column on the right of that page runs the whole height of the page and there is nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom, and it has been empty every day of this flood, and nobody in that room has ever said one word about it. Behind that register on the same shelf a second slate stands on its edge with its face in to the wall and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf and hers did not go near that slate.

The light off that one window came across that table and stopped a hand's width short of the far edge of it and made a straight line along the wood that did not move for as long as a man stands at a gate, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not put the flat of her own hand down on that wood beside that sheet, which is what she does, and the sheet went back on that table face up and stayed face up and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had already gone down the stair at the back of that room and did not come up it again that morning.
