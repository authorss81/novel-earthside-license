# Chapter 0570 — A Room Working Out What A Thing On A Table Is For

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put his own two hands flat on the top of the bench at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and looked at the sheet lying on the table under the one window, and there is no satchel in that room and there has not been one in that room since the morning it came up that stair. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and no name has ever been written under a date on that door. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them came up that stair on their own two feet and nobody in this city sent anybody a sheet to tell them to come.

That sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place is not empty. The figure in that one place is a figure of this city and nobody in that room wrote it, and it is right, and it is not written out on any page in four hundred miles. There is one place on that sheet and there has never been a second one, and there is nothing on that sheet under that figure and nothing beside it and no place on that sheet for the person who made it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the table, and she said it once and she did not say it again that morning.

"**That is a form with a heading on it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place has a figure of this city in it that this city did not put there, and the form is to be answered with a figure of this city and to go back up that road, and nobody in this room is going to be told what that figure counts and nobody in this room is going to be told what the one that goes in under it counts either.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right. Nobody in that room improved on her and nobody in that room thanked her and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, and about four people in that room had understood the whole of that by the time she had finished and about four had understood about half of it, and not one of the nine said so.

The four days that would have been the cooling are not available in this city, and they cannot be arranged on a morning, and this room is finding out what it is being asked on the morning it is asked, and this is the fifth volume running that has found that out at a table.

---

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Then it goes up on the quarterly round and there is no place on it for the day and no place on it for the week either."

"**The round goes up that road four times in the year and it goes up with whoever is going up that road that week, and nobody in this city has written down which week that is at any hour of this flood, and I know what is in that satchel and I did not open it on the road and I am not going to open it in this room, and those are the last two things I am going to say about it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room said that he was right, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at that window with his own board under his own arm and said nothing whatever about the round, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and said nothing whatever about the round either.

---

There are six documents in this flood, three of them with four places ruled under a heading and three of them with a single place ruled under a heading with a figure in it, and one of the three of one place went out of this city in a satchel with a flap and is not in this city and is not coming back. No two of the six touched at any hour of that morning, none of them was ever held in two hands together, and nobody set one of them beside another and said what the other one was in that room. Behind that bench the register stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall with five figures in the column of it that holds figures and the column on the right running the whole height of the page empty from the top of the page to the bottom, and on that shelf with it stands a second slate, edge up, face to the wall, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day; it stayed where it was, it was not turned over, it was not written on, and no hand in that room but the keeper's came near that shelf.

Not one surface in this city carries a line of words under a figure. Every figure on every board and in every column and in every place ruled under a heading on every sheet of paper in this flood is a number with nothing under it, and nothing was written under anything at any hour of that morning, and nothing is going to be written under anything in this city on a day that anybody in this room can name.

On the outside of that door at the foot of the stair, under the date, there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand, and nothing has ever been written on it, and nothing was written on it that morning, and there is nowhere in this city that says which week the road goes up.
