# Chapter 0588 — Two Hands On One Sheet

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf drew that sheet to the middle of the table under the one window at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and seventy-third day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and squared it with the edge of that table and put his own two hands flat on the wood on either side of it, and then he uncapped a pen. The door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room carried that morning's date in chalk, and there is no name under it and there never has been one. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room for something of their own, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the far window with the front of her own apron in her own two hands, and about four people at that end of the market below went past the end of that row in the ordinary course and not one of them came up that stair.

That sheet came up this road thirty-six days ago, measured from the morning it came up, in a satchel with a flap, and the figure that was in the one place under the heading on it has been on that table in that room ever since and is not on it any more, and what went into that one place at about the ninth hour on that Monday is written out on no page in four hundred miles, and the man whose figure it is was standing at the table when it went in and wrote it with his own right hand in ink, and it is not his handwriting that anybody four hundred miles off will be able to tell from the handwriting of the words under it.

---

He wrote the figure large and in the middle of that one place and it took him about as long as it takes to write a number, and he put the pen down flat beside the sheet when it was in, and nobody in that room said one word about the figure and nobody in that room said one word about what it is a figure of and nobody in that room asked him where it came from.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came off that far window, took the pen up off the table, and wrote a line of words under that figure in her own hand, and it went under it in one go and it was not written on the back of that sheet and it was not written on the heading and there is no second place under that heading. Nobody in that room said one word about what is in that line and nobody in that room asked her to write it again and nobody in that room improved on it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, standing at that window with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket, and he had it out in about four seconds.

"**Two hands have been on that paper this morning and a reader four hundred miles off is going to see one document and is not going to be able to tell it into two.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not say a second thing about that sheet that day.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the shelf behind that bench, and it was short.

"**I did not enter that figure in the book and the book still has five in it, and a figure of this city's own is going out of this city on a sheet and is not going into that book, and nobody in this room is going to say that is wrong.**"

Nobody in that room said anything about that either. The column of that book that holds figures holds five figures, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written beside each of them, and none of those five has been struck and no sixth figure was put in that column on that morning and none was taken out of it, and the column on the right of that page runs the whole height of that page with nothing whatever in it from the top of the page to the bottom. On the same shelf a second slate stands on its edge with its face turned in to the wall and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on at any hour of that day, and the only hand in that room that came near that shelf was the keeper's.

---

At the near end of eleven miles of flats there is a yard with a stack of boards in it, and a tally-board is lying face up on the top of that stack with a figure of this city on the side of it that the sun gets to now, and it has been face up since the two hundred and sixty-second day of that month and it is not going to be put back, and that figure is written out on no page in four hundred miles and nobody in this city has ever been shown the working of it, and nobody in that room said one word about it and nobody in that room asked where it came from. A man of about thirty-eight decided on the two hundred and sixtieth day of that month, in that same room, that that figure was the one that would go into that one place, and he said the price of it out loud first and the reason for the price was his, and there was no vote, and being in that room did not help him then and it did not help him this morning either.

The wall above the top edge of the store's board at the end of that market said nothing at any hour of that day. The store's own chalk was in its box on the step below that window and was not used, and the piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man at the far end of that bench was not out of that pocket at any hour of that day, and what went onto that sheet went onto it in ink.

---

That sheet is lying on the table under that window with the figure in the one place under the heading and a line of words under that figure, and it is not going anywhere today and it is not going anywhere until the round goes up that road, and nobody in that room is going to write a sheet telling anybody anything and there is no eighth sheet in this flood, and the room is still the room it was at the ninth hour with nine people in it and nothing written in the book behind that bench and nothing in the column on the right of that page and nothing on that slate standing on its edge on that shelf.

Two hands wrote on that sheet that morning and neither of them wrote the whole of it, and the figure in the one place under that heading is in one hand and the line of words under that figure is in another, and about nine people in that room read both of them before the light came off that window and not one of those nine said which of the two hands was the more careful.
