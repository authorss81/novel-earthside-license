# Chapter 0083 — The Field, Two Days Out

On the fifty-eighth day of the new month, which was the eighty-third day of the flood and a Sunday, a man of thirty-two walked four miles down the Reach road in the wrong weather to find out what a woman of forty-four was going to do on Tuesday, and she was in the field when he got there, and she told him four things and one of them was that nobody had ever asked her, and the last thing she said to him was not what he walked down for, and a cost column was entered for the eleventh time in one month and has no figure on the end of it.

**The licence is thirty-three days old. The first of March is two days off. A year of certainty is a year of promises, and the promises were made by a city about a field, and a field is not a promise.**

---

**He went because nobody sent him and nobody could have sent him, and there is a form about that which he wrote himself on the thirty-second day of the new month, and it is the only rule he has ever written that has ever worked.**

He got to the top of the Rise at about the tenth hour of the morning and stopped, because from the top of the Rise you can see the whole of the bottom field and it is a hundred and forty acres and every ditch in it is level.

That is the finding and it has been on the page since the twentieth day of the new month and nobody had ever put it in a sentence before Elsa Skell said it, on a walk-round, in about nineteen minutes, to eleven people in the wrong weather: **a year of certainty is a year of promises.** The year of certainty was a thing three jurisdictions gave this field about four years ago. What it bought was a ditch that is level. **The level of the ditch is the promise, and the promise was that somebody would be standing in it in March.**

Below the Rise, on the flat, there were about nine people working a ditch in a line, and one of them was a woman of forty-four who had not spoken to a man from another world since the eighteenth day of the new month, which is the day she spoke to him once and then did not do it again, and he had not seen her since, and about nine people in this city have entered that as a fact and not as a fault.

**Elsa Skell reads the edge.** She is one of the four who read the join-line at the Lock of Salt — Nyla Ferrow reads the line, Peth Lomas reads the number, Elsa Skell reads the edge, and Renna Ord, fifty-eight, cannot read and reads the join by standing on it twice a day and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading. **The four are entered as not agreeing about the dressed edge, three at a line and Peth Lomas a sixteenth under, the second time, and both entries stand.**

She saw him at the top of the Rise and did not stop working, and about nine people in the field saw a man from another world standing at the top of a bank in the wrong weather not coming down, and it is entered that he stood there for about four minutes before he came down, and that the four minutes were the whole of the visit as far as anybody in that field was concerned, and that a woman of forty-one had been asked one thing in a room over a chandlery at about the eighth hour of that morning and had answered it in nine words, and that not one of the nine people in that field knew, and that the asking had not stopped the winter by a single hour of it.

"You have come four miles," she said, when he got to the ditch.

"I have."

"**Then you have come to ask me a number,**" she said, "**and I am going to give you the number before you ask, because I have given you three numbers in eighty-three days and I am tired of watching a man wait for one. And then I am going to tell you the fourth thing, and the fourth thing is not a number, and you did not come for it.**"

---

**She gave him the number first, in the local language, with her hand still on the spade, and it is in the book in her own words and it is four words long and nobody has improved on it.**

"**A hundred and forty.**"

"That is the acres."

"**That is the acres and it has been the acres for four years,**" said Elsa Skell, "**and I have given you four numbers in eighty-three days and every one of them was true and every one of them was a number, and you have written all four of them down, and none of them was the thing, and I have never once given you a sentence and you have never once asked me for one.**"

"Then what is the thing."

"**The thing is that the level of that ditch was bought with a promise and the promise is not mine and it was not the field's,**" she said, "**and the year of certainty is gone and the ditches are level and level is what you get when nobody is guessing any more. Every year I have worked this field I have got a better ditch and a worse year. That is not a complaint. That is the trade.**"

**And then she said the second thing, which is about him, and the first of its two sentences is nineteen words, and it is in the book, and about nine people in that field heard it and two hundred and thirty people are not going to hear it at all.**

"**You did not come down that bank to find out what I am going to do on Tuesday morning.**"

"**You did not come down that bank to fix anything, because there is nothing here that wants fixing, and there is something here that wants deciding, and those are not the same and you have known the difference since the thirty-ninth day of this flood and you have used it on a page eleven times and never once on a person.**"

---

**And the third thing is the one nobody in eighty-three days has asked her, and she gave it to him standing in a ditch, and the girl of seventeen wrote it down at the top of the Rise with a slate on her arm and could not hear all of it and has said so.**

"I have been asked three times for a number and once for a date," said Elsa Skell, "**and nobody in this city has asked me whether I want the field.**"

She let that stand for about nine seconds, and it is entered that about nine people in the field stopped what they were doing, and that the two women nearest her did not stop.

"**Not whether I want to keep it. Whether I want it at all. It is a hundred and forty acres and it is not a gift and it is not a favour and it is not a symbol, and a man from another world is going to write about it because he cannot help it, and if nobody asks me then in nine months there will be a page about a field and on the page there will be a woman who works it, and the woman who works it will be me, and I will not have been asked anything.**"

"You do not want it," he said.

"**I do not know whether I want it, and that is the answer, and it has taken me four years to be able to say a sentence like that out loud in a field, and I have said it to a ditch and not to you, and you are going to have to decide what to do with a woman who does not know.**"

"**The field is not yours to save,**" she said, "**and it is not yours to write about. If you write about it I will read it, because I read everything anybody writes about this field, and I will tell you in a gateway what you got wrong, and I have done that once and I will do it again.**"

---

**And then she gave him the fourth thing, and the fourth thing is the reason he came, and it is a date.**

"The twentieth day of the new month," she said. "**That is the day we work the crew's credit out. Not the day the field is sown and not the day the ditch is cut. The day the credit is worked out, and the crew comes out of that field whether the field exists or not, because the credit is worked out against the standing of the land and not against the crop, and the standing of the land has been the same for four years and it is level, and level is what you get when the promise is good.**"

"So the crew is paid out of a field that has not been planted."

"**The crew is paid out of a field that has been promised,**" said Elsa Skell, "**and a promise is worth what a person is willing to dig for, and on Tuesday I am going to stand in a gateway at about the eighth hour of the morning and say a number out loud in daylight to about nine people, and that number is going to be the number of acres I am putting my life into for a year, and nobody in this city has ever asked me to say it out loud, and I am going to say it out loud, and I would like one person in this city to have asked me first.**"

**And a man of thirty-two said the only sentence he had, and it is ten words long, and it is in the book, and it is the eleventh time in eighty-three days that a sentence in this city has been about a person being asked instead of a person being described, and she had heard about the first of those ten at about the sixth hour of the morning and had said nothing about it and has been asked about that and has entered that she was not asked.**

"**Then I am the one who will ask you first.**"

"**No,**" said Elsa Skell. "**You are the one who will write it down, and those are different, and you know they are different, and if you ask me first you will be a man who asked a woman a question in a ditch so that he could write down that he had asked.**"

Nobody in that field said anything. **It is entered that the sentence was correct, and that it was the second time in ten days that a woman had refused a man a thing in a way that could not be refused afterwards, and that the first time was in a yard at Four Elms and the second time was in a ditch four miles down a road.**

---

**At about the second hour of the afternoon the girl of seventeen came down the bank and asked the question that got entered, and she asked it out loud and in the local language and in English, and about nine people in that field heard it, and it is a question and it is not a polite one.**

"A man of forty-four in a field of a hundred and forty acres is going to say a number on Tuesday," she said, "**and this city has eleven people in it who are on no roll and about nine people who are not in the room, and I want it written down that we are not going to fix any of that today, because I would like somebody to be able to read in nine months that we decided not to and did not forget.**"

"And who decided," said the girl of seventeen, and a woman of thirty-four years in Saltwake, who had come down the road in the wrong weather because she always comes down the road in the wrong weather and who reads nothing at a lock and writes nothing down, stood behind her and let her answer it.

"Nobody decided," said the girl of seventeen. "**That is why I am writing it down.**"

---

**And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the cost column was entered for the eleventh time in one month, in a yard under the lock chapel, in front of about forty people, and it has no figure on the end of it, and the eleventh entry is larger than the tenth, and a larger entry is not a dearer column.**

It was entered by a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read and who has asked to be the figure on the end of it since the twentieth day of the new month, and it is the eleventh time and she has never once put a figure, and the eleventh entry names the field, the ditch, the hundred and forty acres, the standing of the land, the two days, the eighth hour of the morning, the gateway, and the fact that nobody has asked her.

"**A smaller entry is not a cheaper column,**" she said, when somebody said it was getting long. "**A longer entry is not a bigger bill. It is a bigger hole and I have been standing at the end of it for eighty-three days and I would like somebody to notice that the hole has a shape now and the shape is a woman standing in a gateway on Tuesday.**"

**And at about the sixth hour of the evening a man of thirty-two went back down the Reach road and stopped at a ditch on the level ground above the field, and stood in it, in the wrong weather, alone, for about four minutes, and a woman of forty-four watched him from forty yards away and came over and told him what standing in a ditch is.**

"**That is a thing you do,**" she said, "**and it is not a thing that finds out anything. A ditch takes water. A ditch is level because somebody made it level and paid for it and stopped. You cannot learn a field by standing in a hole in it, and you cannot learn a field by writing about it, and I have watched a man from another world do both of those in eighty-three days and come down here today to do a third one, and the third one is standing still.**"

"Then what does it do."

"**It puts you in it,**" said Elsa Skell. "**That is all it does and it is not nothing, and I am not going to be the woman who tells a man from another world that standing in a ditch is not a thing a person may do. It is a thing a person may do and it costs him nothing and it has bought this city one sentence, and the sentence is that he came.**"

---

**Nothing was gained. A woman of forty-four said a number and a date and two sentences about a man and one sentence about a field, and the third of those is the first sentence anybody in this city has said out loud in eighty-three days about whether she wants the field at all, and she said that she does not know, and nobody has asked her, and that is the finding and it is not a resolution. A cost column was entered for the eleventh time in one month and has no figure on the end of it and the eleventh entry is larger than the tenth. A girl of seventeen got a sentence entered that says this city decided not to fix something and did not forget. Nine Elms has refused for the third time and its water is not received and its quarter is gone, and a woman of forty-eight in the fourth house has been down to the fourth channel every morning since the twenty-ninth day of the new month clearing a drain that silts in a thaw, by herself, because the levy that used to do it is gone, **and she has not been asked whether she will carry any of it and this city is not going to find out this week, and that is entered as a thing this city has decided not to do and not as a thing this city has forgotten.** Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty-three days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is eighty-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the measurement column and it is not filled and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The Narrow Mark was not opened. The near gate is condemned and the four go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nothing was knocked this week and nothing was used as a reason to open anything. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-fourth time and the salt was not sent. The fourth line of a term sheet is in force, the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, and a page that would have spread it across four people was refused nine days ago by the meeting-ground that wrote the line, and that is not resolved. The first of March is two days off.**
