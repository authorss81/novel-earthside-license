# Chapter 0085 — The First of March

On the sixtieth day of the new month, which was the eighty-fifth day of the flood and a Tuesday, and which is the first of March in this world's calendar, a woman of forty-four stood in a gateway four miles down the Reach road at about the eighth hour of the morning and said a number out loud in daylight to about nine people, and the season of the bottom field was settled in, and at about the ninth hour forty men went under the near gate unpaid, and at about the eleventh hour a sentence was said in a room that had not been said in a room in thirty-nine days, and at about the second hour of the afternoon a man of thirty-two was named in a column that had had no figure in it for eighty-five days, and the field was not paid, and at about the eighth hour of the evening he stood four feet back from a wall in ice and said a number that had not moved, and a woman of fifty-eight asked him whether the field had.

**The licence is thirty-five days old. There is one ruled line left in the measurement column and it is not going to be filled today and nobody in that gallery is going to fill it.**

---

**The season was settled in at a gateway, in daylight, at about the eighth hour of the morning, and about nine people were in the gateway and about forty were on the flat below it, and a number was said once and then not said again.**

That is what settling in is in this valley and it is not a ceremony and it has no form. A year of certainty was a thing three jurisdictions gave this field about four years ago, and what it bought was a ditch that is level, and a level ditch is a promise that somebody will be standing in it in March, and the standing is a number said out loud in daylight to people who can hear it, and after it is said the year is what it is.

Elsa Skell is forty-four and she works the bottom field and she is one of the four who read the join-line at the Lock of Salt and she reads the edge. On the fifty-eighth day of the new month she told a man from another world that nobody in eighty-three days had asked her whether she wanted the field, and that she did not know, and that she was going to say a number out loud on Tuesday anyway.

She said it. She said it in the local language, in daylight, standing in a gateway, and it is in the book in her own words.

"**Ninety-one,**" she said. "**Out of a hundred and forty. Forty-nine are not mine and have not been mine since a year of certainty, and I have worked forty-nine acres of somebody else's ground for four years and I have been paid in a ditch and a ditch does not come back.**"

"**And you do not know whether you want the ninety-one,**" said a girl of seventeen, who had come down the road with a slate on her arm.

"**I do not know whether I want the ninety-one,**" said Elsa Skell, "**and I have said a number anyway, in daylight, to about nine people, because that is what settling in is and nobody has ever asked me whether I wanted it and if I wait until somebody asks me then the number gets said in April by a man in a coat and it will be worse.**"

Nobody argued with her. **It is entered that about nine people heard the number and about forty heard it from the flat, and that nobody repeated it, and that the not-repeating is a rule in this valley and has been one for a hundred and forty years.**

---

**At about the ninth hour of the morning forty men went under the near gate at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and they were not paid, and they turned up.**

Nineteen of the day crew and twenty-one of the flood crew. The rail on the two hundred steps was found half-rotten on the fifty-second day of the new month and has been condemned ever since, and four people go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge, and today the weather was the wrong weather and four of them went down it and came back up it wrong-footed, and nobody has entered a fault because there is not one.

They were not paid because the field was not paid, and they turned up anyway, and the reason is the oldest sentence in this city and it is thirty-nine days old and it was said on the twenty-first day of the new month to about forty men under that gate and it is the reason the whole of this city is shaped the way it is:

> **You do not stand under that gate at six because it is right. You stand under it at six because somebody owes you and you have not been paid yet.**

A docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said it about six weeks ago in a market to about nine people and it has been in four rooms since and it has never once been on a page, and it is on a page now, and she has asked for it to be entered as hers and as the rule of a shift and not as the finding of a room.

**And then a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read said the other sentence, in a room, in daylight, at about the eleventh hour of the morning, and about forty people were in it, and it had not been said in a room in thirty-nine days.**

> **The day the bottom field is not paid in March, this stops, and the join stops being worked, and the join will be where it is.**

Nobody in that room argued with it and about nine people in that room had known it for two months and about nine people in that room had not known it and one of the nine was a man of thirty-two who had never once heard it said in a room.

"You have known that since the twenty-first day of the new month," said a woman of fifty, who is the notary of this city.

"**I have known it since the twenty-first day of the new month and I did not say it in a room for thirty-nine days because every time I have said it somebody has gone and fixed a thing,**" said Renna Ord, "**and I have watched two things get fixed in eighty-five days and I have watched the man who fixed them and I am not going to be a woman who hands a room a lever on the first of March. Today there is no lever. Put the sentence in.**"

---

**And the four went down the two hundred steps in ice at about the eleventh hour of the morning with a gauge, and they came back up, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken, and one ruled line is still left in the column, and this is correct.**

Nyla Ferrow reads the line. Peth Lomas reads the number. Elsa Skell reads the edge, and she was four miles away and sent a report by a boy, which is entered and is the first time in eighty-five days that one of the four has reported instead of stood. Renna Ord reads the join by standing on it and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading.

They did not agree. **Three of the four went down the two hundred steps in ice with a gauge and the fourth sent a report down the road by a boy, and three of them came back at a line and Peth Lomas came back a sixteenth under it. The third time, and the second time was on the forty-sixth day of the new month, and both entries stand and neither has been deleted, and about nine people in that room have now watched the same four people fail to agree three times in a fortnight and nobody has struck a line out.**

"**Then we do not take a reading,**" said Peth Lomas, who is fifty and a mason and not of the crew and who has carried a page two hundred steps in the wrong weather before tonight. "**You do not write a number into a column when four people do not agree about where the edge is, and a column that gets a number out of a disagreement is a column that gets whatever anybody wants out of it in about nine months. I have been wrong twice in eighty-five days and I would like to be wrong in a book and not in a wall.**"

"You have been wrong twice and both of them were in the direction you wanted to be wrong," said Renna Ord, and then she corrected herself out loud, because that is the finding she wrote on the forty-ninth day of the new month and she will not have it said wrongly in her own absence: "**I have been wrong twice about numbers other people could see, and that is a different thing, and Peth has been wrong about a number only he can see and the difference is in who can check it, and I do not know which of us is the worse off and I have said so.**"

**It is entered that the twenty-fifth reading was not taken on the first of March, and that one ruled line is left in the measurement column, and that the column is not continuous, and that the last two readings are both eight and seven-eighths, and that the two steps in it are in an unnamed mason's own hand entered before the number was read on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and that a dressed join does not narrow, it gets dressed, and that the main breach is closed and dressed and measured and silent and has never said a word.**

---

**And at about the second hour of the afternoon, in a yard under the lock chapel, a cost column was opened for the eleventh time in one month and a person was put in it, and the column has no figure on the end of it and it did not get one.**

Renna Ord has asked to be the figure on the end of this column since the twentieth day of the new month and has never once put a figure there, and there have been eleven entries, and the eleventh is the largest of the eleven, and a larger entry is not a bigger bill.

And then a woman of twenty-four said a thing that took about nine minutes to be understood by everybody in the yard, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is about a hole.

"**The column is not a column,**" she said. "**It is a sentence with a number missing out of the middle of it. It has been a sentence with a number missing out of the middle of it for eighty-five days, and this city has spent eighty-five days learning that it must not guess the number, and that is correct, and that is why nobody has been hurt by this city in eighty-five days. And the number is not a number. It has never been a number. Nobody in this yard is looking for a figure. There is a person standing behind it and we have all known that since the third day of the flood and the only reason it has taken us eighty-five days to say so out loud is that a person is a thing you can name in a yard and a number is a thing you can argue with, and this city has been arguing with the wrong noun for eighty-five days.**"

Nobody argued for about nine minutes. Then a man of thirty-two said the only sentence anybody in that yard had been waiting eighty-five days for, and he said it about himself, in the first person, in daylight, with a date on it, and it is five words long and it is in the book in his own hand and it is the whole of what he contributed to the day.

> **The charge is on me.**

"And it cannot be paid," said Elsa Skell, from a gateway four miles down the Reach road, in daylight, and not in person, and about nine people in the yard heard her say it because a boy of about nine was standing in the yard with a slate. **It is entered that she was not in that yard and that those five words were carried down the Reach road by that boy, who ran the whole four miles in the wrong weather, because the woman who had said nothing the last two times a page was read out had been asked to say it and she said it to a boy and he carried it and nobody improved on it.**

"You cannot pay it," said a docker of fifty-eight, "**because you have no money and you are owed eleven ounces of silver and a fee for a case that is going to a building nine hundred miles away, and because the thing you are carrying is a village's water and a village's quarter and a drain in a fourth channel, and none of those are a thing a person can pay with.**"

"Then what do we write."

"**We write that,**" said Renna Ord, "**and then the work stops, and it is entered as not done and not as short and not as carried forward, because a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody, on any day, for any reason. I said that on the thirty-third day of the new month and it has been right every day since and today is the day it was written for.**"

---

**So the join stopped being worked, at about the third hour of the afternoon, on the first of March, and it is entered as a fact and not as a defeat, and about forty people were in the yard when it happened and about two hundred and thirty were not.**

The working on the tide is once a month now that the join is shut. It was to be worked on the first of March because the first of March is the day the standing of the land is read and the join is read against it, and it was not worked, and **the day's work is entered as not done**, and the man who would have done it is thirty-five and his shoulder cannot be set and he is not the man who is waiting for it and has never been, and nobody in that yard has ever printed a list of who is waiting.

**The four who hold the door do not agree about the dressed edge for the third time in a fortnight. Both entries stand and neither has been deleted. The twenty-fifth reading was not taken and one ruled line is left in the column. The day's work is entered as not done. The season of the bottom field is settled in at ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty. The field is not paid. And the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and an undertaking of seven clauses and a date is unsigned and unrefused in about nine people's hands, and none of those three facts is the reason the field is not paid and all three of them are on the page.**

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the evening, in ice, on the walk-round of the Lock of Salt, a man of thirty-two stood four feet back from the wall of the second bay and read a number off it and said it out loud, because a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read asked him to, in the local language, at the ordinary volume, in front of about nine people.**

"**Eight and seven-eighths.**"

He was four feet back. He is barred from opening, inspecting, signing and stopping the instrument, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month, at his own request, in his own hand, with the date on it. He performed no working today. He holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat.

"**Did it move,**" said Renna Ord.

He was quiet for about nine seconds, and about nine people in that gallery counted them, and he was not counting them to be impressive, he was counting them because he had promised himself on the thirty-second day of the new month that he would not answer a woman who cannot read faster than he can answer a woman who can.

"**No,**" he said. "**It has not moved. It has not moved one sixteenth of an inch since the twenty-fourth day of the new month and it does not narrow and it does not get dressed tonight because nobody dressed it, and there is one ruled line left in the column and I did not fill it and I was not going to and nobody asked me to.**"

"That is two things," said Renna Ord.

"**It is three things,**" he said. "**The number has not moved. The field has.**"

Nobody in that gallery wrote either sentence down, and it is entered that nobody wrote them down, and that at about the ninth hour of the evening a woman of fifty-eight took a slate and copied the second one into it herself, in her own hand, without asking anybody, and put the slate in her coat, and has carried it every day since.

---

**Nothing was gained, and a field was not paid. The season of the bottom field was settled in at ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty, said once in daylight in a gateway by a woman of forty-four who has said in three days that nobody had ever asked her whether she wanted it. Forty men went under the near gate unpaid and turned up anyway, and the oldest sentence in this city was entered on a page for the first time in eighty-five days and it is a docker's and she asked for it to be. A woman of fifty-eight said the sentence she has been carrying for thirty-nine days and said that she had not said it in a room since the twenty-first day of the new month because every time she had said it somebody had gone and fixed a thing, and there is no lever today. The four went down a condemned rail in ice and did not agree for the third time in a fortnight, three at a line and one a sixteenth under, and both entries stand and neither has been deleted, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken and one ruled line is left in the column. A person was named in a cost column that has had a figure missing out of the middle of it for eighty-five days, in five words, in his own hand, and a boy of nine ran it four miles in the wrong weather. The join stopped being worked, the day's work is entered as not done, and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty-five days old and is given nothing by it. The black line in his right palm is eighty-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word. The Narrow Mark was not opened and nothing in this city opened anything today. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nothing was knocked this week and nothing was used as a reason to open anything. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-sixth time and the salt was not sent. Nine Elms' water is not received, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two. The first of March is today and it is over.**
