# Chapter 0091 — The Name, in a Room, with a Date on It

On the first day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-first day of the flood and a Monday, in a converted salt store in the yard under the lock chapel, a man from another world spent about nineteen minutes finding out that the four best things his city had built in ninety-one days could not be pointed at a piece of paper, and then a man of forty-one in a very good coat said two names out loud in daylight, and nothing follows the name.

**The licence is forty-one days old. The undertaking went down a river two days ago, on the sixty-fourth day of the new month, which was the eighty-ninth day of the flood, and it is on a nail in an arch, unsigned, unrefused. The field was not paid on the first of March. The black line in the right palm of the man from another world is ninety days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**There are four things in this city a person can put a hand on, and Adrian Vale tried all four in about nineteen minutes on the first day of the month after this one, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and none of them would do the job.**

A form of four items with a price of salt under them and a fifth item that is a question, on a slate on a nail in the arch. A slate with a question on the front and an answer on the back. A page inside a stone at Four Elms, put there on the fifty-fifth day of the new month, which was the eightieth day of the flood, and put there again on the sixty-fifth day of that month, which was the ninetieth. Nineteen words on a second slate.

**A form names a duty. A slate names a question. A page in a stone names a debt. Nineteen words name a proposal. None of the four can end a document, and not because they are badly made. All four are about a person, and a document with no person in it cannot be answered by a page about a person.**

That is on the page in two words and it was a notary who said them.

"**Who is it about,**" said Sera Vail, and about four people in that room tried and nobody could answer.

"So the question is not what we have," said Adrian Vale. "**The question is what a page can be about that is not a person.**"

---

**He got there by being wrong out loud four times, and it is entered that he was wrong out loud four times, because a man who is right in a room nine times in a fortnight is a man who has stopped listening, and about four people in that room had begun to wait for it.**

He tried a body first, and a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall told him why a body will not do in one sentence and did not improve on it.

"**A body cannot be refused, or it is not a body, and the only bodies in this city that can end a document are nine hundred miles away, and they have not sent anybody.**"

He tried the Crown next, and the same notary told him in about nine seconds that none of the four things is a Crown instrument, and that if he made this one he would be building the fifth thing that office has put in a case for this district in forty days, and that those are two different counts.

He tried the room, and about four people said no at once and for four different reasons, and one of those came from a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, and it is the shortest sentence anybody said in that room all day.

"**A room is a place. It is not a thing anybody holds.**"

And then he stopped, and about nine people in that room waited, and he said the thing he had been in the room for, and it took him about nine seconds, and it is not the nineteen words on the slate.

"**A document says what it is, in its own words. And if it says what it is, that is a thing with a name. And a name is a thing you can put a page in front of.**"

"Then name it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**I have not got it,**" said Adrian Vale, "**and the reason is that the last three things I said out loud in a room in this city were nineteen words on a slate, a page I could not fetch, and a bar that did not reach, and I am not going to say a fourth until I know which of the three it is.**"

---

**And then a man of forty-one in a very good coat, who has been in this city since the sixth week of the flood and has not said one wrong thing in ninety-one days and is not convicted of anything and is not going to be, said it. He had a slate. He did not go to the front of the room. About nine people in that room noticed where he was standing and about two hundred and thirty people in this city are going to be told that he stood at the front.**

"**I have been in this room fifty minutes and I have not said anything and I have not been asked anything, and I came the whole way down off the top of the road for this.**" He put the slate on the barrel, the way a girl of seventeen puts a slate on a barrel, and did not hold it. "**I am going to say a thing I have not said in a room in four months, once, and then this room can do what it does with it, and I am not going to help.**

"**The thing that has been getting at this city since the fourteenth week has a name. The name is Single Witness.**"

Nobody in that room made a sound, and it is entered that nobody did, and that the door of the salt store was open and that the yard outside it went on being a yard.

"**Single Witness,**" said Ivenn Marrow, "**and that is the first name and it is the one that matters. I have said it in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and I said it four days early instead of in the four months I promised this city, and in a room that is not mine, and I did not ask anybody to open it, and I would like the room to enter all three, because a man who says a name and then gets to explain himself afterwards has said the name for himself.**"

"Both entered," said Sera Vail.

And then she asked him the question that about nine people in that room had, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is the whole of it.

"**What is a witness a witness of?**"

And Ivenn Marrow said a second name, and he said it in about four seconds, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has carried a long way and set down.

"**That is the second name and it is the older one and I have never seen it written in a book in my life and I have read every book that office keeps. The name is First Grammar.**"

---

**Nobody wrote either of them down for about nine seconds, and then a girl of seventeen wrote both, in a column four lines high, in her own hand, because nobody else was going to, and the whole of this city's method has been that a thing nobody wrote down did not happen. Both names went into the book in his own hand, at his asking, with the date on them.**

"Entered," said Sera Vail, "**in your words, and I am not going to improve on it, and I am not going to decide what you meant, because that is the whole of what a notary is for, and I have spent two days in this week watching people want an office to do it.**"

"And where you got it," said a man of forty-two, who had walked nine miles to a boat-yard four days ago and been right about the document, and who is the man who said on the sixty-third day of the new month that the man who proposed nineteen words will do it.

"**From a person who will not say and from a book I will not name,**" said Ivenn Marrow, "**and three people have asked me that in ninety-one days and I have answered it the same way each time, and nobody has yet made those words mean anything.**"

It is entered that nobody argued, and that the man of forty-two entered that he had failed three times in four days and did not intend to try a fourth in this city.

---

**And then about nine people in that room asked the question they have been asking for ninety-one days, and nobody in that room could get past it.**

"If it has a name," said the man of about forty-four with the stall, "**can it be refused?**"

And the answer came from a woman of seventy from a village of nine households, who had come down on the cart that brings the salt and had not been walked down there, and it is twenty-four words and it is hers, and she has said it once before, on the sixty-second day of the new month.

> **A guild that can be asked has a standing, and a standing cannot be refused, and I know it because we have refused three.**

"You have not answered him," said a man of thirty-two. "**He asked about a name.**"

"**A name is not a refusal,**" said Ilda Marsh. "**A name is a description. I have had a name of mine in four rooms in ninety-one days and every one of them wanted something with it and not one of them asked me anything. That is what a name is for. It is the column at the end of the page, the one that says where the person is.**"

And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and whose name is not on anything stood up and said the rest of it, and she is on no roll and has no standing and said so first, and she has said since the sixty-fourth day of the new month that she is not going to be one of the people who says she tried, and she said that again first.

"**And a column is a roll, and a roll goes to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year, and this city has spent ninety-one days refusing to be described, and about nine people have been right about that every single day of it. And this morning, in daylight, in a room, a man said the name of the thing that has been doing the describing, and the room wrote it down.**"

"**I am not saying he did it to do that,**" she said. "**He has not said one wrong thing in ninety-one days and I have listened for all ninety-one and I cannot find the wrong one. And I would like this room to notice that I have listened for all ninety-one days, and that nobody asked me to, and that there is no page anywhere that says I was.**"

---

**And then, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, a man of thirty-two drew a page in a converted salt store in about eleven minutes, and it is a heading and seven lines, and it is a kind of page nobody in this city has built before, and about nine people in ninety-one days have built four others of kinds that they also cannot name, and the room did not get round to that this afternoon.**

> **The first day of the month after this one.**
>
> **A page that ends one document, once.**
>
> **The document is one that claims to be the work of a person.**
>
> **The document's own condition is the ground. No other ground is used.**
>
> **Whether the condition was met is not argued. It is set down.**
>
> **This is a record and not a finding. It does not say why.**
>
> **Nine witnesses. One of them is named by herself, and her name may be taken back, and nothing whatever happens to the page.**
>
> **The page is not a Crown instrument, is not this city's, and is on no roll of anything.**

"Read it back," said Sera Vail, and it was read back in the local language and then in English, and nobody changed a word of it, and the reason nobody changed a word of it is that there is nobody in the room qualified to, and that is not an accident. It is the design, and it is the only page in ninety-one days of which that is true.

And then nine people were asked, one at a time, in daylight, in a room, whether they would witness it, and about two hundred and thirty people in this city do not know that it happened until they are told in about four months.

They were Nyla Ferrow, seventy, who keeps a floor and washed a rule off it. Peth Lomas, fifty, a mason, not of the crew, who has said no four times in ninety-one days. Elsa Skell, forty-four, of Corrow, who came four miles and has not entered those four miles anywhere, and gave the reason on the page, which is that the column is full and she is not going to be the twelfth. Hettis Roke, forty-four, of the outer ward. Sera Vail, fifty, notary. Priya Shah, twenty-four, of the first group. Ilda Marsh, seventy, of Nine Elms, who said she was witnessing it against her own village's interest. Garret Slee, sixty-one, whose hand is at the bottom of the document the page is aimed at, and who said yes before he was asked.

"**Nineteen years I have copied other men's sentences and not had a name at the top of anything, and this morning for the first time somebody has asked me whether I will stand at the bottom of a page that ends one, and I have said yes, and I would like it entered that I said yes before I was asked, and that that is the only thing I have wanted in nineteen years.**"

And the ninth was Renna Ord, fifty-eight, who cannot read, and she is named by herself, and she is the only one of the nine whose name may be taken back, and she read the page out twice and asked for it in English as well, and then she said that if her name were taken back the page would stand and nothing whatever would happen to it, and that this is the first page anybody in ninety-one days has asked her to stand behind instead of being read to.

Nobody took her name back.

---

**The page is dated and sealed on the first day of the month after this one and it is in the lockable room off the salt store, and the inside of it is folded twice and there is no second copy, and the notary who sealed it entered that there is no second copy and said out loud that she had done that on purpose, and that a page nobody can read from the inside is a page nobody nine hundred miles away can check, and that she does not know whether that is protection or a hole, and that a man with a stall has already said both halves of that sentence in this city about a stone.**

And it is entered, and entered twice, that nobody in that room is going to be able to say why the page works, or why it will work, or what it does to a document that nobody who has not read it can argue about. The page itself does not say. **A page that does not say why it worked is the most dangerous page in this city, and it stays that way, and nobody in ninety-one days has written down a reason and nobody in this room is going to be the first.**

---

**Nothing was gained. A man of thirty-two found the only kind of thing a page in this world can be aimed at, which is a condition, and built it, and it is dated and sealed and nobody can read the inside of it, and it will be used once and only on a thing that claims to be the work of a person, and it has not been used on anything at all.** Nine people witnessed it, one of them named by herself. **A man of forty-one in a very good coat said two names in a room, in daylight, with a date on them, in his own hand, four days early, in a room that was not his, without asking, and entered that he cannot tell afterwards whether he said them to help this city or to file it. He is not convicted of anything and is not going to be.** A woman of seventy said twenty-four words that are hers, and that a name is a description and a description is the column at the end of the page. A woman who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing said that this city has spent ninety-one days refusing to be described and has described the thing that was doing the describing.

The undertaking is on a nail and went down a river two days ago. Nine Elms' water is not received and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two. The field is not paid, and the cost column has eleven entries and no figure on the end of any of them. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in ninety-one days and was not opened today. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room.

Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month, at his own request, in his own hand, with the date on it. He holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is ninety days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. **And there are twenty-five things the wall has said in this city and it has never once registered a refusal of one of them, and it said nothing at all today, and the count of refusals in a second book kept at home, which has never been shown to anybody, is twenty-two, and the count in his book at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference of nine is entered and nobody in this city can say what the nine are.**
