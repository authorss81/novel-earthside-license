# Chapter 0063 — Eleven Ounces

On the thirty-eighth day of the new month, which was the sixty-third day of the flood and a Monday, a man of thirty-two went looking for eleven ounces of silver in a city of eleven hundred people, and found out that he was the eleventh person in Saltwake who knew where all of it was, and that the tenth had spent nine days assuming that somebody else was doing the sum.

**There is no silver in this city that a city can use.** Not a little. None. Not a clipped piece, not a bezel, not a spoon handle, not a wire. The Crown charges a fee of eleven ounces of silver for the schedule of crossings, and it is entered as owing with no date, and it has been entered ten times, and the office that charges it is nine hundred miles away, and a clerk of that office has said out loud that the office does not charge a fee it cannot collect.

About forty ounces are in private hands. That is the whole of it. That is the total. The arithmetic is not a conspiracy and must never be written as one, and there is nothing in it to be angry at, and the reason there is nothing in it to be angry at is the reason it is dangerous.

---

**Where the forty ounces are, and every one of these five facts was true this morning before anybody looked for it.**

> **One. A chest in a chandlery on the upper road, under a floor that was lifted in the flood, with about nineteen ounces in it, and a woman of sixty-one who knows the number and has not counted it since the flood.**
>
> **Two. Eleven ounces in a house on Silt Row, in a box under a floorboard, belonging to a woman of sixty-one who has not spent a coin in nine years.**
>
> **Three. A pouch in a boat-yard at Long Water, about four ounces, in a locker, belonging to a man who bought it off a boat that came down from the north and does not know the price he got.**
>
> **Four. Some in the Measure's strongbox, entered on a page in a ledger by a man of fifty-eight who does not know whether it is entered as money, because the Measure does not use the word.**
>
> **Five. The rest in about nine people's pockets, one to two ounces each, carried by people who were each given a piece of something and have not put nine pieces of something together.**

**About eleven people in this city know the total.** That is the finding of the day and it is not an elite. It is nine householders, one chandler, one docker and the man from another world, and the reason none of the eleven has ever said the number out loud is not fear and not loyalty and not a conspiracy.

**It is that saying the number out loud is making a list.**

It is a list of about eleven people who each know how much money there is in a private house, and a list of houses with money in them is exactly the shape that this city spent sixty-two days learning to be afraid of, and the fear is real, and the fear is of persons, and the eleven are persons.

**Nobody in Saltwake has ever seen a name come out of one of those cases in nine hundred years, and that is the only protection anybody has ever been offered by that office, and a number said out loud in a yard would not be a page and would go nowhere and would be in about nine mouths by the following morning.**

---

**And the man from another world asked four people that day and got the same sentence out of three of them, and it is not the sentence he was expecting, and it took him until the second hour of the afternoon to hear what it was.**

He expected *no*. He got *ask me why*.

Noor Haddad's ledger is the group's public record of what it owns, who it is for, who may use it, and who is owed. Adrian Vale's cash is on it and his phone is on it, and the price a phone would fetch in this city is on it in her hand, and he argued with her about that figure on the eleventh day of this flood and lost. He went to her at about the ninth hour of the morning and asked, in the plainest words he had, whether anything in that ledger could become eleven ounces of silver in a building nine hundred miles away.

"**No,**" she said. "**And I want to say the next part out loud, because you have been asking people all day and I would rather you had this from me than from a man in a good coat.**

"**You do not own anything. You are on my page as owing me for a lease and owing this city a season of a field, and there is not one line in this book that says you have money in it, and if you had any I would have to write it down, and I would rather write it down than have you tell a clerk in Aurel that you had.**"

That is the sentence he got three times out of four, and it is not *no*, and by the second hour of the afternoon he had understood that it was not *no* at all. **A roll is the only thing in this world that makes a person findable, and being on no roll is at the same time the protection and the disappearance. The two are one fact and not two facts, and a person cannot have one of them without the other.** Nadia Brandt worked that out yesterday in about an hour, on a salt bin. A quartermaster says it to a man with a ledger in about nine sentences and does not know she is saying a thing that is on a wall in about nine rooms.

The fourth person he asked was Ivenn Marrow, in a yard, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and Marrow answered in about nine seconds and did not soften it and did not offer anything, which is the fourth time in sixty-three days:

"I have been in this city eleven weeks and nobody has ever once asked me what a district is. I have been reading a form about one for four days. **The office is not asking you for silver. If the office wanted silver only, it would have written silver only, and it wrote silver, and I am telling you that I do not know which of those two sentences you should be reading, and I have said it in a yard in front of eleven people and it should be entered that I did not say it to you in a room.**"

"That is a very careful thing to say to a man who has spent sixty days being careful."

"It is the only careful thing left in it," said Marrow, "**and I would like it read back twice, because I have noticed that in this city the careful sentences get entered and the useful ones get forgotten, and I am the man in the good coat and neither of those is a favour to me.**"

---

**The Measure offered eleven ounces as a loan against the crew's shift list, in a yard, at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it was refused in about eleven seconds, and it is the fourth term in a fifth coat, and the coat is now a receipt.**

Ottavia Reek is the second of the Brass Measure. She has been refused four times. She drafted the last one herself, as a materials account with forty men in it and the fourth term in a note in another hand, and entered that she knew before she opened it and brought it anyway, and she is not a villain and she is a factor with a quarter and the price of salt went up a third on the twenty-seventh day of the new month.

This one was not a materials account. This one was a slip of the same paper, in her own hand, and it was handed to Adrian Vale in a yard under the chapel arch at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it says in four lines that the Measure will advance eleven ounces of silver against the crew's shift list for the coming quarter; that the sum is to be carried at the retail price of the day; that no document, register, roll or description of any person is required for the advance; and that the list of the crew is the security.

**"The list of the crew is the security."**

The fourth term. Salt, timber, iron, rope, lime, chalk, a boat, the rail in nine days, all of it against a list, and this time in a good coat's own handwriting on a slip about the size of a palm, and **this time the coat is not a coat, it is a receipt, and a receipt is not an instrument and cannot be refused in a room because a receipt is not a thing a room can be asked about.**

Renna Ord refused it in about eleven seconds and she has now said the rule four times in sixty-three days, and it has been in the book since the fortieth day of this flood and it is Volume 01 canon and is not re-counted here:

> A wage off a second page is not a wage. It is a favour with a handle on it.

She said it in the local language and then, because about forty people were in that yard and about nine of them were crew, and because a rule that has been said three times and has not been obeyed by a factor is a rule that needs a witness, **she said it again in English, and it is the first time in sixty-three days she has spoken a language that is not hers on purpose.**

"It is a lock that a guild can close," she said, in English, to about nine people. "**And this is the fifth coat and I have refused four of them and I am not going to refuse this one twice.**"

Reek did not argue. It is entered that she did not argue and that she folded the slip once and put it inside her coat, **and that a woman of about fifty-eight who was standing four feet away and who cannot read said afterwards, to a man from another world, in the plain language of a person who has been a docker for forty years:**

"**A receipt is the only one of the five she ever brought us that we were not allowed to be angry about, and I would like it written down that I noticed that on the day and not afterwards.**"

**The receipt is on a table in a yard in the office of the Concord at the salt wharf. Nobody has torn it up. About nine people have looked at it.**

---

**And the woman who has not spent a coin in nine years said why, and it is not about money, and she has never said it to anybody, and she said it to a man from another world because he asked her a question she could answer in one sentence.**

Orla Specht is sixty-one and keeps a kitchen in Silt Row with a blue door that has to be lifted on its hinge. She has eleven ounces in a box under a floorboard and she has not spent a coin in nine years and she buys what she needs on a slate from two doors down and settles it in kind, by carrying two crates of salt a week for a man two doors down who has never once asked what they are for.

Adrian Vale did not know any of that when he knocked. He had a page of arithmetic in his own hand with forty on it and eleven on it, and he asked the question he had been asked to ask four times that day, which is: *is there a way to raise eleven ounces.*

Mrs Specht said: "**No.**"

And then she said, in English, which she has not done with him before, and it is entered that she chose English, and the reason she gave for choosing it afterwards is that his face does not work in this language when he is asking for something and she wanted to be certain he understood the answer:

"You have it in a book. You have been writing the number in a book for two days. **The number is right. I have the eleven.**"

"Then may I ask you—"

"You are going to ask me why I have not spent it, and you are going to write my reason down, and I am going to let you, **and I am going to do it in a language I know so that it is my sentence and not yours.**"

**And here it is, in her own words, and it is seventy-one words, and it is the only thing anybody in this city has said about silver that is not about silver, and nobody in the room understood all of it and about four people understood all of it:**

> "My mother was carried out of a yard in the fourth month of a flood year by four men who were owed money by her and they came anyway, and one of them carried her and one of them carried the box, and I was nine, and I have not spent a coin in nine years because **a coin is not a coin, it is the fourth man in a yard.**"

Nobody in the room spoke for about eleven seconds, and the eleven seconds are entered, and the girl of seventeen counted them without meaning to and entered that too.

"I have never said that to anybody," said Orla Specht. "Not to my sister, and my sister has been dead four years. **You asked me a question I could answer in one sentence and that is the only reason I said it, and it is not a reason you get very often and I would like it entered that I would have said no to a person who had asked me a better question.**"

**The silver exists. That is the finding of the thirty-eighth day of the new month. Adrian Vale entered in the book that he had spent two days treating an absence as a fact about currency, and that the absence was not in the currency, and that forty ounces of silver in eleven private places in a city of eleven hundred people is not a treasury and has never been one.**

---

**And about nine people in a yard turn out to have thought about this and had each assumed somebody else was doing it.**

That is the third finding of the day and it is the one that is going to be true in four months, and it is not about silver at all.

The nine are the nine who know the total, and every one of them has been certain for between nine days and two months that somebody else in this city was going to raise eleven ounces and pay the fee, and not one of them had asked anybody, and all of them had reasons, and the reasons are entered and none of the reasons is cowardice.

Marek Osley's was arithmetic. He has a quarter and a bad winter and a page of salt that comes up a third every time he looks at it, and he had worked out that eleven ounces would cost the city about a season's bottom field and had decided that was somebody else's season.

Orla Specht's was the one in the block quote above.

Peth Lammay's was that a carrier is paid by the person whose description is in a case and that a fee is not a case and that she was not going to be the woman who made that argument in a village.

Wenna Cray's was that the fee is a statement about what an office thinks a district is, and that the sentence she wanted to say about it was four words long, and that she had said it once already on the twenty-ninth day of the new month and would not say it twice for a fee.

**Adrian Vale's was the reason of the day and he entered it in his own hand in a private book at about the eleventh hour of the night, and it is thirty-three words, and it is the sentence the whole day stands on:**

> **Everything I have written for sixty-three days about a roll is a roll of a different kind, and I have been standing in it, and I did not know it was a floor.**

He wrote it and then he wrote underneath it, and the second line is not entered in the book and has never been shown to anybody, and the girl of seventeen does not know it exists:

> **The credit of this city is not a chest of silver. It is a list of about eleven people who each believe they are the reason something happened.**

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, under the arch of the lock chapel, four people changed a line of salt in a cut in the boarding and took four chalk depths with a straight edge, and a fifth person asked the obvious question out loud, and got told, correctly, that it was not the same thing.**

Nyla Ferrow is seventy and keeps that ground and she reads the join-line. Peth Lomas is fifty and a mason and not of the crew and he reads the number. Elsa Skell is forty-four and of Corrow and she reads the edge, and she has not spoken to Adrian since the eighteenth day of the new month, and she spoke to him now. Renna Ord is fifty-eight and cannot read and reads the join by standing on it twice a day and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading. They are the four and the only four, and they are also the four who sign the fresh inspection.

Elsa Skell said: "**We have one price in this city and it is on that slate, under four items, and it is a double handful of salt twice a day, and it is the only figure in the whole of this book that anybody can pay.**"

"And the Crown's fee is eleven ounces of silver," Adrian said.

"Yes."

"That is not the same thing."

"**No,**" said Nyla Ferrow, and put the chalk down, and said nothing else, and it is entered that she was asked whether she thought the office would take salt, and that she said four words: "**Ask them and find out.**" And it is entered that about nine people heard those four words and wrote them down, and that about two hundred and thirty did not hear them, because they were under an arch at the ninth hour of the evening on a Monday in the middle of a winter.
**Nothing was gained. The fee is entered as owing with no date. The Measure's receipt is on a table in a yard in the office of the Concord at the salt wharf and it has not been torn up and about nine people have looked at it. The trial licence is thirteen days old. The main breach is closed and dressed and has never said a word; the ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired; the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The nineteen lines of what a person in this city may be asked to turn up for were read to about two hundred and forty people yesterday, and about nine of them understood that it was a widening. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster, has performed no working, and is party to a licence that gives him nothing. The black line in his right palm is sixty-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is four knocks into being knocked on and nobody has to open anything for that to happen and nobody has asked what to do about it. The review is in twelve days, on a slate on a nail, with a date on it and no questions on it.**
