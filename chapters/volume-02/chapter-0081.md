# Chapter 0081 — The Roll and the Case

On the fifty-sixth day of the new month, which was the eighty-first day of the flood and a Friday, a notary of fifty put a wooden case on a table in a room over a market and counted in front of about nine people what was going into it, and a woman of thirty-eight who is on no roll at all was asked one thing by a person who is not the man from another world and said the thing she had come to say, and a girl of seventeen read a number out loud at the end of it, and nobody in that room said the word *budget*.

**The licence is thirty-one days old and it is in force and it is weaker than the one it went in with. The door is shut and has been opened twice in eighty-one days and was not opened this week. March is four days off.**

---

**The case is a case a carrier is paid for, and a case with nothing in it is a case nobody can charge to carry, and the woman who has known that for eleven years said it out loud before anybody had opened the lid.**

Peth Lammay is twenty-four and a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail and she has lived over a boat-yard at Long Water for eleven years, and she came up the road at about the second hour of the afternoon with the case under her arm and put it on the table and said her standing sentence before she had taken her coat off.

"**I will bring a case whatever is in it. Bring me a case.**"

"You have brought it eleven times," said Sera Vail.

"**I have brought it eleven times and twice there was nothing in it and I was paid for both and I have never told the office that and I am telling you now because a case with nothing in it is a case nobody can charge to carry, and if you send me a case with the emptiest thing in it that anybody in this city has sent to Aurel, then I get paid the same and you get paid nothing, and that is the arrangement and I would like it written down so that neither of us is being kind.**"

Nobody in the room argued with her, and about nine people in the room understood at once that the safest thing in the case was the emptiest thing in the case, and that this had been true for eleven years and that nobody in Saltwake had ever noticed it because everybody had been thinking about what to send instead.

**The four things went in first, and they went in because they are the only four that carry a sentence a person said, and a sentence a person said is a thing an office has to answer in a paragraph.**

The eleven questions, in the order they were read, with the second page bound behind them because a notary insisted on the binding and gave her reason and it is in the book: *sent beside the questions it is a thing an office has to answer in a paragraph.* And the first page of them carries the four words and the four words, **on the same sheet and not on a separate one**, so that an office cannot lift the question off the page without lifting the answer with it. And the honest useless form, sixty-one lines, complete, true, and carrying nothing anybody can act on, and it is the emptiest document in the case and it is the safest one, and the notary entered that the safest thing in the case is a document this city designed to be unhelpful and that the designing was the protection.

Then the second honest useless form, which is worse than the first and is not a lie, with the cross in the receiving-party cell and nineteen words under it: *no receiving party has been asked and none has agreed and this is not the same as none existing.*

And then a fifth thing, which was not a document at all, which was a strip of paper with two lines on it in a notary's own hand, and it went in third and it went in because the office had better have it in the same case as everything else.

> **The office has been told what this district has. This district has been told what the office will take. Neither of those is on any page the office has sent, and both of them are now on a page in this city.**

"That is the only line in that case that is mine," said Sera Vail, "**and it is the only line in it that was written by a person about her own office, and I would like the girl to enter that I put it in the third place and not the fifth, and that I have been thinking about the third place for two days.**"

---

**And the roll of strangers goes in the same case, and the roll has a hole in it, and the hole is not a smaller number.**

The roll is written in the notary's own hand and it goes to Aurel four times a year in a wooden case carried by a licensed carrier of the second-table mail. It has not gone since the third week of the new month, and on the thirty-seventh day of the new month Sera Vail entered nine lines saying that she did not send it, with two reasons, one true and one convenient, and they were not the same one, and the true one was that a description obliges somebody to answer for a person and is the only protection anybody in this valley has ever been offered by that office, and the convenient one was that eleven people who have asked to go home are eleven people an office may act on.

**On the fifty-sixth day of the new month she sent it, and she entered that she had decided to send this one and not the last one, and gave one reason, and the reason is sixteen words:**

"**A district that has not sent a roll is a district somebody will describe for it.**"

Then the girl of seventeen counted the names on it in about four minutes at the bottom of the table and read out that the second list is not on it, all eleven of them, and that two people in this city are on no roll at all and have never been on one, and that a hole in a page is not a smaller number, it is a place where a number used to be, which is her sentence and is twenty words and she asked for it herself nineteen days ago, in a room, and nobody offered it to her, and it is on the page.

"**Then the roll goes with a hole in it,**" said Peth Lammay, "**and I will carry a hole, and a hole in a roll is a thing a man in Aurel can fill in, and he will fill it in with the name of somebody he has got from somewhere else, and that is not me being clever. That is what a hole is for.**"

"**Then the roll goes with a hole in it,**" said Sera Vail, "**and it is entered, and the hole is entered as a place where a number used to be, and the number is eleven and the eleven are named to me and not to the office, and I am fifty and I have been the notary of this city for twenty-six years and I am not going to be the reason eleven people get a name put on them out of a gap I made on purpose.**"

**It is entered that the roll went with a hole in it, and that about nine people in a room over a market understood the hole at the same moment and for two different reasons, and that one of the two reasons was the carrier and the other was a girl of seventeen.**

---

**And then a woman of twenty-nine who is of the second list asked a woman of thirty-eight who is of the second list one thing, and Adrian Vale was not in the room, and had asked not to be, and had been told that was allowed and that nobody was going to thank him for it.**

Nadia Brandt has said the sentence that made a thing possible eight times and the sentence that named a hole eight times, and on the thirty-seventh day of the new month she told a woman of twenty-four and not the man, and on the forty-fifth day of the new month she told him the second half of it in about nine words, and on the fifty-sixth day of the new month she asked a woman of thirty-eight a question in a room in daylight with about nine people in it and the girl of seventeen taking it down.

There are two people in this city on no roll at all. One is a girl of sixteen who came out of a chapel floor on the twenty-fifth day of this flood and has said the three things she has said in a room in about nine seconds each and has not signed the form asking whether she wishes to be described, and one of the four reasons given for not signing is that a person who has said no to a form is a person who has been asked and has said no and is now on a page. The other is a woman of thirty-eight who came out of the same floor and has been in this world fifty-six days and has not been asked a single thing about any of it.

"What do you want said about you," said Nadia Brandt, "**and I am asking it in English and then in the language of this city and the girl has both, and if you want it said in neither then the answer is neither and I will write *neither* and the answer is a real answer.**"

Odile Ferrand is thirty-eight. She has been in this world fifty-six days. She has worked, which is a thing about her that is on no page in this city, and she has been paid in the only currency this city has and she has never once asked what it was going to be called, and she looked at the table for about nine seconds and then she said it, and it is in the book in her own words, and about four people in that room heard the whole of what it cost her and about five heard a woman saying a name.

"**I have a daughter. Her name is Margit Solveig Renn. And I have never said it out loud in this world and I said it in a room in Leeds and in a room in Dunedin and I said it to a man on a ship who did not write it down, and the reason I came is that I have never once said a thing out loud in a room with a person in it who wrote it down, and I have been fifty-six days in a city where everything anybody says is written down, and nobody has asked me one thing.**"

The girl of seventeen wrote it in both languages and read it back twice, and nobody in the room corrected a single letter of it, and it is entered that about nine people were in the room and that the name is on a page with a date on it in a book that is not going to Aurel, and that a name on a page is a thing that can be found, and that the woman who said it said the second half of it herself before anybody else could.

"**A name on a page is a thing that can be found,**" she said. "**I know that. I have known that since about the fourth day and I did not say the name for fifty-six days and I am saying it now to a person who wrote it down, and that is the thing I came here for, and I would like it entered that I chose the room and not the sea.**"

Nobody thanked her. **It is entered that nobody thanked her, and that she did not wait to be thanked, and that a woman of twenty-nine who asked her is not thanked either and did not ask to be.**

---

**And then a man of thirty-two was told what had happened in a room, and he was told it afterwards, in a yard, by a woman of twenty-four, and his whole response is in the book and it is thirteen words long and he said it in the local language because he has learned it in a city where a thing said in a yard is a thing that happened:**

"**She chose the room. Nobody was owed her the choosing and nobody is.**"

And that is the whole of what Adrian Vale said about it, and it is entered that he said it in about nine seconds, and that he had asked not to be in the room for a reason he gave in five words — *because I would ask her* — and that the notary accepted the reason and entered it and did not improve on it, and that the reason is correct.

---

**At about the seventh hour of the evening the case was closed, and a fee of eleven ounces of silver was entered for the twenty-second time, and the salt was not sent, and the girl of seventeen read the number out loud.**

Peth Lammay took the case down to the wharf at about the eighth hour with a strap and a wax and a second strap, and the roll was in it and the roll had a hole in it, and the case is going to a building nine hundred miles away and about nine weeks later there is a date in it for this city, and it is a hearing, and a hearing is a date, and about nine people in this city now believe it and eleven people in a kitchen above a chandlery have not been told.

The fee was entered before the case went and not after, because a fee entered after a thing has gone is a note about the past. It is eleven ounces of silver and it has no date on it and it is the twenty-second entry, and the twenty-second time a page in this city has said that a district owes an office eleven ounces of silver and is not going to say when, and the count has risen by exactly one on every page this city has written since the fiftieth day of the new month, and a page is not a day, and about nine people have noticed the difference between the two and nobody has decided whether that is a discipline or a habit.

"**Twenty-two,**" said the girl of seventeen.

Nobody in the room said the word *budget*. **It is entered that nobody said it, and that it is the first time in eighty-one days that a number was read out in that room and no one in it gave it a name, and that a man of forty-four who keeps a stall wanted to and did not, and that he has said since that he wanted to.**

---

**Nothing was gained. A wooden case went down to the wharf with the eleven questions, the second page, the four words and the four words, two honest useless forms and a strip of paper about what the office has been told, and the roll of strangers went with a hole in it, and the hole is entered as a place where a number used to be. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-second time and the salt was not sent. A woman of thirty-eight said her daughter's name out loud, in a room, in two languages, to a person who wrote it down, and chose the room herself. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty-one days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is eighty days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the measurement column and it is not filled and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The Narrow Mark was not opened. The near gate is condemned and the four go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge and did not go down it this week. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nothing was knocked this week and nothing was used as a reason to open anything. A girl of sixteen and a woman of thirty-eight are on no roll at all and one of them has said a name out loud in a room and one of them has not been asked anything. Nine Elms' water is not received, the credit was refused, the quarter is gone, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, and that is not resolved. March is four days off.**
