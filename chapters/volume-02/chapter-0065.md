# Chapter 0065 — One of the Seventeen Is Paid

On the fortieth day of the new month, which was the sixty-fifth day of the flood and a Wednesday, the first mixed trade in Saltwake happened on a floor above a chandlery, badly, in the wrong weather, and it was four transactions, and three of them were a mistake and one of them was not, and the mistake that was not a mistake was nine words long and was the worst sentence anybody has said about work in this city.

**The price of salt went up a third on the twenty-seventh day of the new month and the price on the nail under the chapel arch is salt, and there is no money in this city in any coin a wage can be counted in, and the only thing anybody in Saltwake can be paid in is a thing they can carry.**

So the four transactions are not a market. They are four people finding out what a thing is worth when nobody has a word for what it is worth, and the word they all reach for is the one this city has used for sixty-five days, which is *turning up*, and it is the wrong word, and it took the whole day to find out how wrong.

---

**The first transaction. A Veyran household buys nine weeks of counting from a man of thirty-one with a hand that does not close, at a rate the man set himself, and he is paid, and the mistake is that he set the rate himself and had no idea what a rate is.**

Teodor Blaz counted the double handfuls of salt into a slate for the keeper's form for nine weeks. He could not do it with his right hand. He had been doing it with his left and his chin for sixty-five days and nobody had noticed, because nobody had asked him, because a man who cannot make a fist is not asked about a figure.

Ilsa Roke, of the house on the upper road, asked him. On the Sunday she counted the salt herself for one day, badly, and got a different number, and on the Monday she offered him nine weeks of counting at a rate he set himself, and she wrote down whatever he said, and he said:

"Two salt a day. That is what I would want for it."

"**You do not know what two salt a day is,**" said Ilsa Roke. "**That is most of a hand's worth and I have nineteen years of this house and I would have paid four.**"

"I cannot hold four and write it at the same time."

"Then I will write it and you will say it out loud every morning and I will check it, and that is a better arrangement and it costs me more, and I am telling you that you have set your own price wrongly and that you have done it because nobody has ever asked you what your work is worth, and that is not a fault of yours and it is a fault of sixty-five days."

**He was paid, in salt, into a sack, in her kitchen, on the fortieth day of the new month, and he counted it twice and got the same number both times, and it is entered that he counted it twice and that the second count was not doubt.**

**The mistake was the rate and it was his and he entered it himself in thirteen words and asked for the entry:**

> "I set it and I was wrong and nobody made me set it."

---

**The second transaction. A chandler buys nine weeks of chalk-marks from a girl of sixteen and does not pay her, and the reason is not malice, and it is nine words long.**

Kirsty Lund is sixteen. She came out of a chapel floor four feet beyond the threshold slab on the twenty-fifth day of this flood at about the eleven hour, in about four seconds, and she was not hurt. She has been copying the shapes of nineteen stones into a slate for nine days and a docker of fifty-eight has been showing her which ones to leave out, and she is the fastest marker in this city and she does not know she is the fastest because nobody has ever told her and nobody has ever asked.

Emrys Vaile is a chandler of forty-four with a yard on the upper road and nineteen names on his own book and a good hand for figures. He wanted the chalk-marks. He came to the floor above the chandlery on the Wednesday morning, and he said:

"**Nine weeks. I will pay for it at the end.**"

"And what is the end," said Kirsty Lund.

"When it is done."

"And what is it a week?"

He did not know. Nobody in the room knew. It is entered that nobody knew and that about nine people understood within about eleven seconds that nobody knew, and that the girl of sixteen had understood at the second he said it and had said nothing.

He did not pay her on the Thursday.

**And when she came to his yard on the Thursday afternoon and stood in it and asked him for it, in the plain language of a sixteen-year-old who has been in this city forty days and has had three beds, he said this, and it is nine words long, and it is in the book in his words:**

> "I am not going to pay for a habit."

And then he said the rest of it, which is also in the book, and which is about four times as long, and which nobody argued with:

"I have had four people on a chalk-mark in nine years and three of them stopped. I do not know what they were doing and neither do they. I am not a bad man, I have a yard and nineteen names on my book and I have never once had a person say to me that they had done nine weeks of anything. **If it is a thing then I will pay for it. If it is a thing that stops after a fortnight then I am paying nine weeks for a fortnight and I have four children and there is salt at a third.**"

**Nobody said it was right. Nobody said it was wrong. A docker of fifty-eight with a barrow, who cannot read, was in the yard at about the ninth hour of the morning and put a rule on a ledger in about four seconds, and the rule has been in the book since the fortieth day of this flood and it is Volume 01 canon and is not re-counted here:**

> A wage off a second page is not a wage. It is a favour with a handle on it.

The man who keeps a stall by the wharf said the other half of it and it is fourteen words and it is his: "**a wage off a second page is a wage she can be removed from.**"

He said it about a woman of forty-one of the second list, two transactions later, and it is entered that he said it in a yard about somebody else in front of about forty people, and that the person it was about was standing in the yard, and that she said yes.

---

**The third transaction. A man who keeps a stall by the wharf buys a fortnight of translating from a woman of forty-one of the second list, and pays her, and the mistake is the second page.**

Perry Lask is forty-one. She is the first person in this world to hold a workway. She accepted it on the thirty-second day of the new month, in about four seconds, after saying one sentence first, and the sentence was that she had been waiting since the ninth day of this flood for the wall to say something she recognises and that she was not going to pretend to a room that she checked it first.

She can read the local language the way an instrument reads a page, and about four people in this city need that and cannot get it anywhere else, because the group is one interpreter wide and the interpreter has been a woman on a stair who has declined twice to find a sentence and given one.

The stall-holder bought a fortnight of it and paid her, in salt and in two meals, in front of two witnesses, **on a second page.** Not a roll. Not a book. A page, in a hand that is not a clerk's, in a market, with two names on it and a fortnight on it and a rate.

**And she is the one of the three of the second list in these four transactions who is named in a Crown schedule** — a schedule of crossings, quarterly, in a form required, sealed, sixty-one lines, with a column headed *name of the person who went* — **and her name is in that column because she went through a seam in a wall on the twenty-fifth day of the new month and came back in about eleven minutes, and she is owed the standing charge and does not owe it and is on no roll at all otherwise.**

So: a wage in this city, on the fortieth day of the new month, is held by a woman whose name is in a document that goes to a building nine hundred miles away in a wooden case four times a year, **and neither of those two facts had ever been in the same sentence in this city before this day, and they are now, and about nine people in the room noticed and about two hundred and thirty were buying salt.**

The woman who keeps a stall by the wharf noticed. She said nine words to the girl of seventeen and the eight are in the book and they are the reason the third transaction is entered as a mistake and not as a thing that happened:

> "She is paid and she is described. Both."

---

**And the fourth transaction was the one that was not a mistake, and it was nine words long, and she was paid in salt, and the ninth word is the name of the metal the fee is in.**

A household on the upper road bought nine weeks of slate-copying from a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read.

Renna Ord has been copying the shapes of nineteen stones into a slate since the thirtieth day of the new month, three at a time, four times up and down a nine-mile road, and in ten days she has copied about nine of the nineteen and she has entered that she got tired of waiting and that this is not a noble sentence. She cannot read what she is copying. She is copying it because anybody may pick up the work, including a docker, and a woman of seventy who cannot read it, and she picked it up in about nine seconds because nobody else had.

The household that bought it is the one with a burnt lintel and there is a woman of sixty-one in it who cannot read and has nine years of paper, and the person who came to the floor above the chandlery on the Wednesday morning was not the woman of sixty-one. It was her nephew, who is about thirty, and who wanted a copy of the shapes because he is putting a new lintel in and his wife wants the shapes cut into the end of the bench.

**He paid her in salt. He did not know what else there was.**

**And she said nine words, and the girl of seventeen wrote them down, and they are in the book in her own words because she cannot read and dictated them to a person who could and asked that the person be named:**

> "They have paid me in salt, not in silver."

And the ninth of them is the word for the metal the fee of eleven ounces is in, and it is the word nobody in this city has been able to say in four months without a clerk from a building nine hundred miles away in the same sentence, and a woman of fifty-eight said it in a room of about nine people on the fortieth day of the new month, and about four of the nine understood what she had done.

---

**And the twentieth thing this wall has said came up in that room at about the fourth hour and nine minutes of the afternoon, in front of about nine people, and it stood about nineteen seconds, and it is the best offer anybody has been made in this city since the flood, and it was made to a woman who has already said yes to a worse one.**

> **A HOLDER'S WORK MAY BE DESCRIBED.**
>
> **The holder's own work may be described, in any document, in her own words, without anybody asking her first.**
>
> **Nobody may be asked to describe it.**
>
> **The holder does not owe the standing charge. The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

Nobody in that room saw it but about nine people. It is in the colour of a form the Registry sends four times a year. It has no lie in it anywhere, and it is better than the nineteenth field was, and it is better than the eighteenth was, and **it puts nothing in the grammatical position where a cost goes — not the person, not anybody, nothing at all.**

**It is a licence to be written about.**

Read the third line against the first. A person who cannot be asked to describe her own work is a person whose work can be described by anybody else, in any document, forever, and the document is better for it, and the woman is safer in it, and there is no mechanism anywhere in the sentence that could be pointed at and called the price.

**Perry Lask said yes in about four seconds, the way she said yes to the nineteenth, and she did not say a sentence first this time, and it is entered that she did not, and that the not saying is the whole of the finding.**

Adrian Vale saw the position in under a second. **This time he was in the right room and it was the right day and he had been told what a bar was for fifty days and he could refuse it, and he said so out loud, in the local language, and it took him under a second, and the man saying it is on no roster of anything and is party to a licence that gives him nothing:**

"I refuse it. **The page I wrote bars this instrument and anybody using it. It does not reach it and I know that. I am refusing it anyway, because I have nothing else.**"

"It does not reach it," said Perry Lask. "**You said so yourself on the thirty-second day. So what did you just do.**"

"I read a page out loud."

**And it took the room nine seconds to refuse it, and the nine seconds are the finding.**

Nobody refused it on a ground. There was no ground. A pale field in a room is not the instrument, nobody used anything, there was no hand on any lever, and the bar was not broken and did not reach, and that has been true for three fields now. What happened instead was that **a page exists, and nine people in a room can read a page out loud in about nineteen seconds, and a woman who had said yes once in her life looked at nine faces and changed her mind in nine seconds, and none of those nine faces was a person with authority and all of them was somebody she eats with.**

> **A rule about a thing did not stop it. A page, read out loud, by people who are nobody in particular, stopped it. And a page is not a rule, and a page does not stand up on its own, and the next time there may not be a room.**

The refusal is entered. The refusal is in a book at a table on the fortieth day of the new month and it is not a refusal of the instrument and it is not a refusal of the person and it is a refusal by a man who has no standing to refuse anything and did it in about nine seconds, **and Renner Sallow's second book, which is kept at home and has never been shown to anybody, has a column for refusals in it, and the column has twenty in it tonight and had nineteen in it this morning.**

**And the acceptance is entered too, in her own words, with a date, and nobody has yet asked her whether she meant it, and that is entered as a thing nobody has done.**

---

**And the room then did the one thing nobody had done about the workway in eight days, and it is on the page, and nobody was asked anything.**

The procedure written on the thirty-second day of the new month has nine clauses and nine weaknesses on its face and a review date of **the fourth day of the month after this one**, and that date is not inside this month, and **a document that has a review date and nobody has thought about what happens on it is a document that is going to review itself.**

So the room decided, on the fortieth day of the new month, in about nine minutes, what a holder of a workway in this city is going to be asked on that date. It is four things and three of them are refusals and it is in the book in a girl's hand in a column four lines high:

> **One. That the holder is asked, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, what she thinks the work is. It is already clause five and it is repeated here because the first time it was written nobody in the room had met the holder.**
>
> **Two. That she is not asked to do the work, because the work is not written down anywhere and the document says so in twenty-eight words on its own face, and nobody in this room has invented it in the three days since.**
>
> **Three. That she is not asked to account for the work, or to justify holding it, or to say whether she has performed it, and that a standing which can be audited is a post with a person in it, and this is Vask's sentence from the thirty-second day of the new month and it is adopted because it is right and not because she asked.**
>
> **Four. And the fourth is the whole of it, and it is a question and not a duty, and it is the same shape as the fifth item on the form on the nail: that the person to be asked is told and asked, may say no, that the refusal is entered, that a refusal is asked again on a different day, and that the day the asking becomes a duty somebody is to take it off the wall in daylight and say so.**

**She has not been asked. It is entered that on the forty-first day of the new month she was told that a date existed, in nine seconds, by a man of thirty-two who is barred from every roster and holds nothing, and that she said four words, and that the four are in the book and are hers, and that they are six words and she was asked to check:**

> "**Then ask me on the day.**"

**Nobody asked her anything on the fortieth, the forty-first, the forty-second, the forty-third, the forty-fourth or the forty-fifth day of the new month, and it is entered that nobody did, and that four people in this city had the asking available to them in ten days and did not do it, and that one of those four has entered in a private book that he had it available to him about nine times a day and took it none of those times, and that the reason is not restraint and is that he does not yet know what he would do with the answer.**

---

---

**And on the second day the chandler paid her, and he overpaid by a ninth, and the overpayment was refused, and both of those are entered.**

It is entered that on the second day of the chalk-marks, on the Thursday, Emrys Vaile put the money on a ledge in his own yard in front of two people, and that the amount was a ninth more than the fortnight's rate, and that the girl of sixteen counted it and said the number out loud, and that he said the ninth was a year over the winter, and that she said it was a habit and he said it was an accident.

**She sent it back on the Friday. It went back into his yard and it is in his book as a refused overpayment, and his book is not a roll and does not go to Aurel, and he has entered on the face of it, in his own hand: *refused by the person, not offered twice.* That is the first page in the history of this city on which a person has refused money and the refusal has been recorded as hers and not as a shortage.**

And the mixed trade of the fortieth day of the new month is entered as four transactions, three mistakes and one habit:

> **Four transactions. Three of them were a mistake and the three are entered as mistakes.**
>
> **The rate the man of thirty-one set himself was too low and he set it and was wrong and nobody made him set it. A chandler did not pay a girl of sixteen because he was waiting to see whether it was a habit, and the waiting is entered, and the habit is entered, and the first payment was on the second day and it was a ninth over the rate and the ninth was refused.**
>
> **A woman of forty-one was paid on a second page and is described in a Crown schedule, and both of those are entered, and neither one is a fault of hers.**
>
> **One of the four was not a mistake. A docker of fifty-eight who cannot read was paid in salt for nine weeks of copying shapes she cannot read, and she said nine words about it, and the ninth of them is the name of the metal the Crown's fee is in.**

**Nothing was gained. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, has performed no working, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence that is fifteen days old and gives him nothing. The first Wayhouse exists on a page with a date. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date and a woman of fifty-eight has just said the word *silver* out loud in a room of nine people for the first time in four months. The main breach is closed and dressed and has never said a word; the ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired; the boards under it have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The black line in his right palm is sixty-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The review is in ten days, on a slate on a nail, with a date on it and no questions on it. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody has to open anything for that to happen, and nobody has asked what to do about it.**
