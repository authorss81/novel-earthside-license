# Chapter 0084 — In the Lantern Concord's Own Words

On the fifty-ninth day of the new month, which was the eighty-fourth day of the flood and a Monday, a man of forty-one in a very good coat walked into a room over a market with a page in his hand that nobody in that room had written and every sentence in it came out of somebody in that room, and he said so, clause by clause, in about nine minutes, and then nine miles up the Reach road in the Weighers' chapel at Dray, which is not the Salt Weavers' chapel in Saltwake and was too small, two hundred and forty people were asked whether they understood it, and about nine of them did, and the finding of the day is that the reason it was not refused is that it is the most durable answer anybody in eighty-four days has been offered.

**The licence is thirty-four days old. The first of March is tomorrow. The door is shut.**

---

**He put it on the table face down and then turned it round, and he did not editorialise once, and about nine people in that room have said since that that was the frightening part.**

Ivenn Marrow is forty-one and a Crown Law scholar from Aurel and he has been in this city since the sixth week of the flood and he has not said one wrong thing in eighty-four days. He has been neither refused nor accepted by this city since the day he came out of a wall. He offered a room, in daylight, in about four months, written down, dated, in his own hand, with no office behind it, and asked for the offering to be entered, and entered that he did it on purpose and that he wanted to be held to something.

**He is not the Crown's and he is not the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month, and he has said that out loud twice in about nine seconds and both times before anybody asked him.**

"I am not bringing this from Aurel and I am not bringing it from that instrument," he said, "**and I want that said first, before anybody in this room decides what they think of me for carrying it. I found it. I am not going to tell you where and I am not going to be asked where, and if I am asked I will answer in a room and not in a yard.**"

"Whose is it," said a notary of fifty.

"**That is the wrong question and I have brought you a better one,**" said Ivenn Marrow, "**which is: whose sentences are in it. I can answer that one, and I can answer it in about nine minutes, and I have read it four times and every clause of it is a sentence a person in this city said out loud, in a room or a yard or on a road, in the last eighty-four days, and I have the dates, and I read the dates out.**"

He read the dates out. **It is entered that about nine people in that room went quiet at the third one, and that about two hundred and thirty people in a chapel nine miles up a road did not know that there were dates.**

---

**And here is the whole of the page, and it is entered as a document and not as an instrument, and it was entered in about four minutes, and a woman of fifty read it into the book in full and in the local language before anybody was allowed to say anything about it.**

> **The undertaking of the Lantern Concord concerning the Lock of Salt.**
>
> **First. The Concord is a body of about a hundred and forty persons. It holds the lock, it holds the roll, it holds the notary and it holds the gauge. It is not a person. It is not anybody's servant. It does not go home, it does not get old, and it does not want a different winter.**
>
> **Second. The standing charge of the Reed Reach is received by the Concord and held by it. The fourth line of the term sheet of the twenty-third day of the new month returns that charge to the private debtor upon a village's refusal; from the date of this undertaking there is no private debtor of it, and the charge does not return, and the water of Nine Elms is received, and the quarter is restored to the village.**
>
> **Third. Nobody is asked to carry any part of this. It is carried by the Concord. The cost of the carrying is entered, and it is not charged to any household, and the charge on the fourth house at Nine Elms stops this month, and the levy stops, and the drain in the fourth channel is cleared by whoever is nearest, as it was before the twenty-ninth day of the new month.**
>
> **Fourth. This undertaking costs the district nothing and costs the Concord nothing and costs no person anything. It is not a favour. It is dated, and it may be ended by the Concord in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and ending it returns the charge to whoever is carrying it on the day it ends.**
>
> **Fifth. The licence continues. The review continues. A quorum is four and an opening is two, and nothing in this undertaking alters the count of either, and the Narrow Mark is not opened by it and is not shut by it.**
>
> **Sixth. The person who wrote the page asking four people whether they will carry the standing charge is not a party to this undertaking, is not named in it, is given nothing by it, and owes nothing under it, and that page is not withdrawn by this undertaking and its refusal is not undone by this undertaking.**
>
> **Seventh. No person is described by this undertaking. No person's name appears at the top of it. No person is entered on any roll by having read it, and no person is bound by anything in it who has not been asked.**
>
> **Dated: the fifty-ninth day of the new month.**
>
> **Copied out of this city's own book by a hand that is not named on it.**

---

**Then a room of about nine people found out in about nine minutes why it was not going to be refused, and the man who found it out keeps a stall, and it is the worst thing anybody has said about a document in eighty-four days and he is not a lawyer and has never claimed to be.**

"I have been reading it since the fourth hour," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and I have been looking for the lie in it since the fourth hour, and there is no lie in it, and I have got to the end of it twice, and here is the thing and nobody else has said it. It is the most durable answer anybody in this city has been offered since the flood.**

"**And I want that in the book in my own hand, and I want the second half of it in the book in my own hand as well, and the second half is that I have been saying the worst thing anybody in this city has said about a document for twenty-eight years and I have never noticed, and a document that is the most durable answer anybody has been offered is the document I would have written if I had been the one writing it.**"

"It is not an answer to anything," said a woman of twenty-nine of the second list. "**It is a page with seven things on it and a date.**"

"**It is an answer to the thing that has had a man standing behind a number for eighty-four days,**" said the man of forty-four. "**Your four lines of a term sheet send the whole charge back to one man when a village says no. This page takes it off him and puts it on a hundred and forty people and it costs nobody anything, and no village loses anything by refusing any more, because there is no longer anybody for a refusal to land on. And the man it takes it off is the only man in this city who cannot be angry at it, because he is owed it, and being owed a thing is not a reason to refuse it in a yard.**"

"You have just described the best offer this city has had in two volumes," said Adrian Vale.

"**I have just described the only offer anybody has made that cannot be taken back, and those are not the same and I would like the room to hear the difference, because I have been the man who says the clever sentence and I have never once said the useful one and I am saying it now because nobody else in this room will.**"

---

**And then about nine minutes went by and nobody said anything, and the thing that came out of the nine minutes is the whole of the chapter.**

**A guild is a hundred and forty people. A hundred and forty people can be refused. A hundred and forty people get tired, get old, move, die, argue, and one of them can go to the meeting-ground and say no on a Tuesday. A guild does not go home. A guild is not a person. A guild cannot be angry, and cannot be *told* it is wrong, and cannot be asked how it feels about the drain in the fourth channel, because a channel is not a feeling.**

"And that is the whole of the answer to the question I asked," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and it is seven words and it is not mine. It was said in a room on the thirty-second day of the new month by a woman of forty-one who has not been asked anything since the fifty-eighth day of the new month, and a Hearthguard of forty-four carried it into a yard five days ago and said it about the man who wrote the page in front of about two hundred and forty people, and it is a guild that does not go home.**"

"It was said by me," said Perry Lask, "**on the thirty-second day of the new month, in a room over a chandlery, and I said it then and I have said it four times since and nobody wrote it down, and it is the best thing anybody has said in this city in eighty-four days and it is going to be the reason that page is signed.**"

"**Nobody has said it is going to be signed,**" said the girl of seventeen.

"**Nobody has said it is not going to be signed either, and that is the finding and I would like it entered, and I would like it entered in a column four lines high.**"

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the thing nobody was going to say, and he said it badly, and it is on the page, and it is the only sentence in eighty-four days that he has said that nobody in this room had not already worked out.**

"I want to say that it is a false oath," he said. "**And I have been standing here for nine minutes wanting to say it and I have not said it, because a false oath is a thing that is not what it says it is, and this is exactly what it says it is, and the man from another world who says it is a false oath is saying it because it frightens him and not because it is one.**"

"A man who does not know the difference should not use the word," said a keeper of about seventy, who keeps the lock chapel and has said things in this city for fifty-one years.

"**Then I will not use the word. I will say the other thing. Every clause in it is a sentence this city said, and this city said them to each other over eighty-four days, and about two hundred and thirty people have heard every one of them, and a sentence somebody has heard is not a promise to that person any more, and this page is made out of about nine sentences that stopped being promises to the people they were said to, and it hands the whole of them to a hundred and forty people who did not hear them said.**"

Nobody argued with that for about nine minutes either, and then a man of forty-four who keeps a stall said six words, and the six words were: "**That is the whole of it.**"

---

**At about the sixth hour of the evening nine people walked nine miles up the Reach road in the wrong weather and read it out on the first floor of a chapel over a market in a salt town called Dray, to about two hundred and forty people, in daylight, in the local language first and then in English, and the reading took about nineteen minutes and it was done by the man who had carried it in, because he said he would not have it read by anybody he had not read it to first. The Salt Weavers' chapel in Saltwake was too small, which is how nine people and about two hundred and forty ended up nine miles up a road, and the Salt Weavers' bench of the Concord meets at Dray in the spring and about thirty of the hundred and forty came.**

**About nine of those two hundred and forty people understood it. Two hundred and thirty heard a document.** It is entered, and it was asked about at the door afterwards by about four separate people, and about nine people understood it and had understood it before the reading and not because of it, and one of the nine is a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read and who said afterwards that she had known by the second clause and had stopped listening at the fourth.

"I have heard a form read out," said Renna Ord. "**I have heard nineteen lines read out and eleven questions read out and a page of nineteen reasons read out in the wrong order on purpose. This was not that. I did not know it was going to do that.**"

And at the door of the Weighers' chapel at Dray, in the wrong weather, with about two hundred and forty people going out behind them into it, a woman of twenty-nine who reads Crown Law and water joins and the history of a route said the thing nobody has said better, and she said it on the forty-fifth day of the new month on a walk-round in ice to about nine people, and about two hundred and forty people in a salt town heard the end of it.

> **A city is not a room. A room is people with a page on the table, and a page on the table turns people into the answer to the question, and about two hundred and thirty people hearing a document and not agreeing with it is not a failure of the document.**

---

**Nothing was gained. A document was entered as a document in four minutes and was not refused and was not accepted, and the reason it was not refused is that it is the most durable answer anybody in eighty-four days has been offered, and a body of about a hundred and forty people that holds the lock, the roll, the notary and the gauge does not go home, does not get old, and cannot be refused by the people it costs, because it is not a person and it does not want anything. A man of forty-four who keeps a stall said the reason out loud and then said, in the same breath and on the page, that it was the worst thing anybody in eighty-four days had said about a document, and that the two things are the same observation and he had made them at the same time on purpose. A man of thirty-two wanted to call it a false oath and was told he did not know the difference and did not use the word, and said the other thing instead, and the other thing is that a sentence a person has heard is not a promise to that person any more. A woman of twenty-four said a sentence said twice is a mechanism. A woman of forty-one said the best line in the page was hers and had been said four times without being written down. A page of seven clauses and a date went out of a salt town at about the seventh hour of the evening and **nobody has signed it and nobody has refused it and it is in about nine people's hands, and a page that cost this city nothing to refuse is going down a river in a wooden case with a review in it, and nobody in this city knows when a river delivers anything and about nine people have stopped guessing.** Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty-four days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is eighty-three days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the measurement column and it is not filled and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The Narrow Mark was not opened. The near gate is condemned and the four go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nothing was knocked this week and nothing was used as a reason to open anything. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-fifth time and the salt was not sent. Nine Elms' water is not received, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, and it is not resolved. The first of March is tomorrow.**
