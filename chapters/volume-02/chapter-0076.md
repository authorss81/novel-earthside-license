# Chapter 0076 — The Form of the Continued Instrument

On the fifty-first day of the new month, which was the seventy-sixth day of the flood and a Sunday, about forty people met in a converted salt store to write down what a review had changed, and a notary of fifty said in nine words that the instrument she had written was worse than the one she had been asked to write, and then spent the whole day finding out whether worse and honest are the same thing, and in the evening a keeper of about seventy read one new line out in an empty yard to nine people, and one of the nine was a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, and she asked whether it was on the slate, and she was told that it was on the page and not on the slate, and that this was on purpose.

**The licence is twenty-six days old. The review is behind this city. March is nine days off. A season of a field four miles down the Reach road is still unpaid and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything.**

---

**The notary said it in the first four minutes, standing up, before she had sat down, and she said it again at the seventh hour of the evening and it was the same nine words both times:**

> **This instrument is weaker than the one it replaces.**

Nobody argued with that, because she had written it and she was the notary and it is not a finding she gets to make about her own work, and she said so before anybody raised it.

"Then it is not a finding," said the girl of seventeen, who keeps the entries in a column four lines high. "**You have to enter it as what a person said.**"

"I have entered it as what a person said," said Sera Vail, "**and the person is me, and I have been the person in this room who says things for twenty-six years, and that is the job, and I would like the room to notice that the sentence I have to enter is that I have made a worse thing than the thing I was asked to make, and that I did it correctly, and that those two are the same fact, and that I have now said that sentence twice in five weeks.**"

Then she put the two instruments side by side on a trestle, and about forty people looked at them for about nine minutes, and the whole of the day's argument is what they found.

**The first difference is the weakest thing anybody in that room had ever seen and it took them nine minutes to say it out loud. The instrument that went in on the twenty-fifth day of the new month had a figure-shaped hole in it. The instrument that came out of yesterday has the hole described.**

The old one said: *the cost of this instrument is entered at its present size and a season of the bottom field is carried against it, and no figure is on the end of it.*

The new one says: *the cost of this instrument is entered at its present size, and the difference between that figure and the truth is a gap, and the gap is the following, and the following is named: a season of a field four miles down the Reach road, and a quarter of a Reach, and the fourth line of a term sheet, and a hand that will not close, and a rail, and twenty days of a fever-house, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge.*

"That is a worse instrument," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and I do not know how to argue with it, and I have been arguing with things in this city for seventy-six days.**"

"You argue with it by finding out whether it is cheaper," said a man of about thirty-two.

"It is not cheaper. **It is the same price with a description of it attached, and a description is the only thing in this world that has never cost anybody anything until an office reads it.**"

Nobody argued with that either, and it is entered, and the notary entered that a man from another world said the sentence that makes the whole of this volume's argument in thirty-two words on a Sunday morning in a converted salt store, and that she is not going to put it in the book because **it is a finding and findings may not be entered by the man it is about.**

---

**Somebody proposed three, at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it was refused in about nine minutes, and the reason is a sentence from the fortieth day of this flood and it has been in the book since.**

The licence says four to authorise and two to open. Four is the quorum that authorised the route on the fourth day of this flood and four does not change and cannot; two is the count required per opening, and the argument for the reduction is Nell Bray's and it is the volume's standing argument: **a working can be made cheaper without being made private.** A boy of nineteen was asked for nothing and came anyway, and that is the proof of it.

After a review that was survived by a refusal, in a room where the door stayed shut, about nine people in a converted salt store thought that the *authorising* count could come down as well, and a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close proposed **three**, which is not four and is not two, and which has no argument behind it at all except that it is one fewer than the number that is there.

Nell Bray is a docker in a felt hat of about sixty. She was not in the room. She was sent for and she came and she refused it in about nine minutes and she refused it by reading a sentence out loud that a man wrote in a notebook on the fortieth day of this flood, which has been in the book ever since.

"**Four was a quorum and two is an opening, and if you make an opening cost a quorum nobody will ever open it, and if you make the quorum cheap you will have a man who opens it alone.**"

"That is the same sentence," said the man of about thirty-one.

"**It is the same sentence and it is a different clause, and I am sixty and I have watched a city in five weeks learn the first half nine times and the second half never once, and you have come in here this afternoon with a number that is neither, and there is nothing in this world for three. Three is not a quorum. Three is an opening somebody can be alone in.**"

"It is refused," said Sera Vail, "**and the striking is in the book with her name on it, and the reason is hers, and I am not going to improve on it.**"

---

**And then the thing that was added was not a number, and it is the first thing in seventy-six days that anybody in this city has added to an instrument for the benefit of a person who is not a witness, and it took about four hours and it nearly did not go in at all.**

It came from the keeper, and she is seventy and she has had a nail under the chapel arch for fifty-one years and a form on it with four items and a price written under them, and the fifth item was added on the twenty-fourth day of the new month and is the only one of the five that is a question and not a duty.

"**The licence survived yesterday because nobody had asked anybody,**" said Nyla Ferrow. "**And I said so out loud on the forty-seventh day of the new month in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and the eleventh question is the only question on either page that could have taken the licence off this city in an afternoon, and the answer to it was four words and a docker's hand, and a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read wrote the same four words on the front of that slate three days before, in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and it is on the front of that slate and it was there for three days and nobody turned it over.**"

"Then the line is easy," said the girl of seventeen. "**No person is asked. The answer to the eleventh question is the answer to the eleventh question, and it is on the page, and if nobody is asked then nobody is described, and nothing changes and nothing needs changing.**"

"**No,**" said Nyla Ferrow, "**and I will tell you why, and I have been a keeper for fifty-one years and I have never said a sentence in a room that I did not say under the arch first.** A person who is never asked is not a person who has refused. I have said that in this yard nine times in seventy-six days and I have watched about nine people hear it and two hundred and thirty hear the other thing. **If nobody is ever asked, then in four months somebody writes a description of seventeen people and every line of it is true, and none of them has ever been asked anything, and this city will have done that to them and will not have noticed, because not asking is the same as refusing and it is not.**"

Nobody in that room had an answer to that, and it is entered that nobody had an answer to it for about nine minutes, and that a woman of twenty-nine in Saye's room, who has never once claimed a sentence that is not hers, supplied the shape of it in about thirty seconds:

"**Then the asking has to be bounded, dated and refusable, or it is a form, and we already know what a form does.**"

---

**So the line is this, and it went on the same page as the question and the answer of yesterday, and not on a separate sheet, because a notary insisted on that in about nine seconds and gave a reason that is in the book:**

> **The person this instrument costs may be asked, once, on a named day, in daylight, in a room, and the answer may be no.**
>
> **The refusal is entered. The asking is not repeated on a second day. The person is not entered on any roll by having been asked, and the answer is not a fault and is not a wage and does not go in the roll.**

**And one of the nine names who may stop the working is entered, in her own words, as somebody who may also ask the question.**

She is Renna Ord, fifty-eight, who cannot read, and she is the only one of the nine who is named by herself, and the only one whose name may be taken back, and she read the line out in the local language twice and asked for it to be read to her in English as well, and then she said sixteen words and they are in the book as her sentence and she asked for the striking of the other three of the nine to be left alone.

"**Nobody has asked me anything in seventy-six days except you, and you asked me once.**"

---

**And the day is not on the line, and the day is the whole of the argument, and it took about nine minutes and the room nearly lost the line over it.**

"There is no date," said Sera Vail.

"There has to be a date," said the girl of seventeen. "**A line with no date in it is the old form of the oath. The old form binds the gate and the standing of the man in it and it was taken out of this city sixty years ago and it is in a Crown procedure of about two hundred years and it is in the Registry's notice and it is in a hundred and thirty words a man in a good coat said in a yard on the fortieth day of this flood, and a thing that asks a person to be asked with no date on it is a standing, and a standing cannot be refused.**"

"It is on the wall without a date," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and nobody has taken it off, and it is the form on the nail and it is the best thing this city has made.**"

"**The form on the nail is watched twice a day by four people and a price is written under it. That is why it is not a standing. A thing with a price and a person watching it twice a day is a form. A thing with a date and a room and an answer that may be no is a question. A thing with neither is a standing, and the standing is the old form of the oath, and I wrote the rule that stopped the standing in a room on the forty-sixth day of this flood and I am not watching it be built again on a Sunday.**"

"**Then put a date on it,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and it has to be a day a person can be at.**"

And it is a day a person can be at, and it is named, and it is the reason the line went in at all, and it is a Sunday seven days from today, which is a market day and a day the eleven of the second list are all awake, and it is entered that about nine people worked out the date in about four minutes and that the reason it is a Sunday is that a person who has been asked nothing for seventy-six days can turn up to a market on a Sunday without anybody noticing that they turned up.

**And the cost column was entered for the tenth time in one month, at about the sixth hour of the afternoon, and it has no figure on the end of it, and it is entered that the tenth entry is smaller than the ninth and that a smaller entry is not a cheaper column.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, in an empty yard under the lock chapel, a keeper of about seventy read one line out to nine people, and one of the nine was a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read.**

She had been asked to come and she came and she stood at the back with her hood up, and the keeper read the line out in the local language, at the ordinary volume, in the way she reads the binding, and then a mason of fifty read it in English for the four people who do not speak it.

Then the docker of fifty-eight asked the question, and it is in the book, and it is hers:

"**Is it on the slate?**"

"It is on the page," said the girl of seventeen.

"**Then it is not on the slate,**" said Renna Ord, "**and I want to know that that was on purpose, because I put four words on a slate on the forty-seventh day of the new month and a keeper of seventy told me in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people that nobody was going to turn it over, and she was right, and I want to know if she is right twice.**"

"She is right twice," said Nyla Ferrow. "**A slate is a thing you can read standing up in the rain. It is read out twice a day in a yard to everybody who comes. It is the best thing in this city and it is the only instrument that has ever worked, and it works because about two hundred and forty people can stand in front of it at once.**"

"And this is not that."

"**This is not that. This is a question, and it is asked of one person on one day, and the answer may be no, and the no goes in a book and not on a nail.** A question that is put on a slate is a question that is asked of everybody on every day, and that is a form, and a form is the fifth item of the form on the nail with four items and a price under them, **and the fifth item is the only one of the five that anybody can use, and it is used once, on a day, of one person.**"

"And if nobody comes?"

"Then nothing happens and it is on a page with a date and nobody has been asked and nothing has been described and the seven days have gone by."

"And if somebody comes and says no?"

"Then it says no, in a book, with a date on it, and they are not on a roll and it is not a fault and it is not a wage, **and I have asked for it to be entered that a woman of forty-one said *then ask me on the day* about something else on the forty-first day of the new month, and that she is not being asked now either, and that nobody has noticed.**"

"**Somebody noticed,**" said Renna Ord.

"**I did,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and I am seventeen and I am on no roll in this city and I wrote it down, and the width of the column is entered as a fact for the sixteenth time in this year, and that is the only reason I am saying it.**"

---

**Nothing was gained and one line was added. A notary of fifty entered that the instrument she wrote is worse than the one she was asked to write, in nine words, twice. A number was proposed and refused and the refusal is in the book with a docker's name on it and the reason is hers. The cost column is entered for the tenth time in one month and has no figure on the end of it and the tenth entry is smaller than the ninth and a smaller entry is not a cheaper column. A day is named and the day is seven days from now. The review's eleventh question and the review's eleventh answer are on the same page and not on a separate sheet, and the answer is four words long and it is not a noun. A person of the nine who may stop the working is entered as somebody who may also ask the question, in her own words, in sixteen words, and she asked for the other three to be left alone and nobody has touched them. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence twenty-six days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is seventy-five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the column and it is not filled and it is not going to be filled. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and the second page says in nineteen lines that this city is not asking about it and why. Nine Elms' water is not received. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventeenth time. March is nine days off. The door is shut.**
