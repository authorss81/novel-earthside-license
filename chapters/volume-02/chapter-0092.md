# Chapter 0092 — What the Instrument's Own Condition Was

On the second day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-second day of the flood and a Tuesday, about two hundred and forty people were in a converted salt store in daylight, a man of forty-two who had said on the sixty-third day of the new month that the man who proposed nineteen words would do it was in the room and said so again, and at about the fourth hour and nine minutes of the afternoon a pale field came up in front of about nine people and stood for about nineteen seconds and was answered by a person, and then the whole of the voiding of the undertaking of the Lantern Concord took about nineteen minutes, and nobody was convicted of anything.

**The licence is forty-two days old. The undertaking was on a nail and on a river and is void from this day and the nail still has it on it. The field was not paid on the first of March. Nine Elms' water is not received and has not been received for twenty-three days, counted from the forty-fourth day of the new month, which was the sixty-ninth day of the flood.**

---

**The argument had been named on the sixty-third day of the new month in a yard for a Tuesday, and it was had on the day it was named, and a girl of seventeen read the nineteen words out from a slate in about nine seconds because the slate is on a nail in an arch and she had not taken it down.**

"**Put a person at the top of it, and make that person able to say no, out loud, here.**"

"That is the old form of the oath," said Jonah Pike, forty-two, of the White Ledger, "**with a person in it instead of a guild. A person who stands at the top of a thing and answers for it alone is a man in a wet coat with a name on a list in a building nine hundred miles away, and there is no stone in this city that stops that. I said that on Friday and I have said nothing since. And the thing on the nail is the same instrument with a guild in it, and it costs nobody anything, and if you put a man at the top of it you have the Crown's document with a wet coat instead of a seal, and I am not going to help anybody pretend those are two different problems.**"

And then a woman of twenty-nine of the second list said the thing that had to be said, and it is in the book in her own words.

"**Then the choice is not between a man and a guild. It is between a man and a document nobody can check, and I have never in ninety-two days seen anybody refuse a document, because a document does not ask.**"

"Then the man," said Jonah Pike. "**It is the only thing on the table. I said on the page that you would do it and I am not taking it back now that it has turned out to be true.**"

"I will do it," said Adrian Vale, in about four seconds and without qualifying it, and it is entered that about nine people were waiting for him to qualify it and that he did not, and that a woman of fifty said afterwards that this was the first time in ninety-two days he had said a thing out loud in a room without putting four sentences in front of it, and that she had not decided whether that was courage or the beginning of the worst mistake of his life in this world.

"Then the person at the top of it is you," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and the page has no name on it and it says a person, and you are the only person in this room who ever put those nineteen words in front of about two hundred and forty people, and I am not going to pretend that is an accident anybody chose.**"

"It is not an accident I chose," said Adrian Vale. "**It is an accident I proposed. That is worse, and I would like the room to keep the two apart.**"

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four said the rule, and it is hers, and she said it once in ninety-two days, on the fifty-third day of the new month, in about nine seconds, about a person who is not her, and nobody claimed it then and nobody claims it now, and about four people in that room had been waiting for her to say it again since.**

> **A hold is a working without a witness, and the residue of one goes into the person who did it.**

"**Are you talking about him,**" said a man of thirty-two, "**because he is not working and nothing has happened yet.**"

"I am talking about him," said Mina Okoye, "**and I am not going to be talked out of it by a man who thinks I would say a thing about somebody I am not standing next to. You asked me once and I will say why in one sentence and then I am not going to say anything else about it.**"

"Go on."

"**It means that if a page has a person at the top of it and nobody is asked whether that person will have it, then the person is not a person doing a working. The person is a working.**"

"Are you saying that about the page in the lockable room, which has nine witnesses on it and one of them is named by herself?"

"No. **I am saying it about the person at the top of the page and not about the nine. The nine were asked and Renna Ord read it out twice like a woman reading a price at a market. The nine are witnesses. The one at the top is not going to be, because the only instrument in this city that could ask him whether he will have it is the page that has him in it, and that page does not ask, and nobody can make it ask, and that is not a fault in the page.**"

---

**And Adrian Vale asked her whether she meant it, and he asked her and nobody else did. It is entered that nobody else was permitted to ask on his behalf, and that a woman of twenty-four tried and was refused in one sentence by the notary, whose reason is on the page: *he is not the person it is about and he does not get to have it asked for him on her behalf, and if I let him do that in this city then every page in it is a page he arranged.***

"Did you mean it," he said. "**About me. And not about the person you said it about the first time.**"

"**I meant it, and the reason is that you are going to be asked whether you will do a working, and you will say yes, and nobody is going to ask you what is at the top of the page, and you are going to find that out after.**"

---

**And then a pale field came up in the air in front of about nine people and stood for about nineteen seconds, and it is the twenty-sixth thing the wall has said in this city, and it was answered by a person, and the person is the man it is about.**

> **A document may be ended by any person who stands at the top of it and answers for it alone.**
>
> **The person is not described by the document that is ended. The person is not on any roll.**
>
> **The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

Adrian Vale read it twice and then said yes out loud in the local language, in about four seconds, without translating it, and asked for the answer to be entered, and it was entered, in a column four lines high, in a girl of seventeen's hand.

And the girl of seventeen asked which book it goes in, which is the fifth time in ninety-two days she has been unable to tell anybody, and a clerk of the Registry at Aurel who has sat at a table in this city since the fourth day of the flood said he kept a book at Aurel and a second at home, that the two had never once agreed, that he had entered that they never had, and that nobody had ever answered one before.

---

**And then the voiding took about nineteen minutes, and it is entered that there was no ceremony, and that the room knew what was being done in the first minute and not in the last, and that the whole of it was the reading of one thing and the writing of one.**

Adrian Vale brought the page out of the lockable room at about the fourth hour and forty minutes of the afternoon. The inside of it was folded twice. He did not read it out, and he was asked why not, and his reason is the first sentence of what follows, and it is fifteen words as it stands, and the rest of it is about ninety.

"**I have not read it out because I do not know what is on it. I know what I wrote on the outside of it and I know what condition it is aimed at, and the inside of it is not mine, and I am not going to stand in a room and pretend a thing to about two hundred and forty people. I brought it in and I did not read it, and both go in.**"

And the notary read the outside, and it is the whole of the document, and it is in the book in her own hand, and it is six lines.

> **This undertaking claims to be the work of a person. Its hand is at the bottom of it.**
>
> **An undertaking of this kind is not in force until a person stands at the top of it and answers for it alone.**
>
> **No person stands at the top of this one. No person has stood at the top of it since it was dated the fifty-ninth day of the new month, which was the eighty-fourth day of the flood.**
>
> **Its own condition was not met. It is void from the second day of the month after this one.**
>
> **This is a record and not a finding. Nine witnesses. One of them is named by herself.**
>
> **It does not say why.**

And then the room did the thing about nine people in it had been waiting ninety-two days to be allowed to do, which was to ask a question.

"Where is the condition?" said the man of about forty-four with the stall. "**You have told me the condition was not met. I want to see the part that is not different.**"

"It is in the first sentence of the document," said Jonah Pike. "**It says the Concord is a body of about a hundred and forty persons and that it is not a person.**"

"That is a description of the Concord."

"**That is the condition, and it is in the document's own words, and the document wrote it, and that is the only reason this can be done at all. A thing that argues for itself is a thing you can check. A thing that is silent can only be believed.**"

And a girl of seventeen went out into the yard and came back in about nine seconds and said that the undertaking was on the nail, and that it was where it had been on the first day of the month after this one, and that there was no mark on it, and that the string had not been touched.

Nobody argued about that. **It is entered that nobody argued, and that about nine people in that room understood that a page had been opened and a document had been ended, and that there was nothing on the nail to show for it, and that the record is the only place the voidness exists, and that a notary's word is worth more in this city than a wet nail, because a wet nail can be read by anybody and a notary can be asked.** The undertaking is void, and it is on a nail, and on a river, and in a room in Aurel in a wooden case, and no office has been told yet, and a hearing is about eight weeks off.

---

**And nothing was convicted, and the Concord did not lose the lock, the roll, the notary or the gauge, and it gained a term it has never had.**

"**The lock, the roll, the notary and the gauge are not in the undertaking,**" said Sera Vail. "**They are in the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month and they were never in this. And the fourth clause of the thing I have just ended said it could be ended in a room in daylight with a date on it, and nobody in this room needed that clause and nobody read it before today, and I would like that entered. A document ended by a clause in itself has been overruled. This one has been found not to have started.**"

"And the term," said the man of forty-two.

"**The term is this,**" said Sera Vail, "**and I am entering it in my hand because there is nobody else who can. The Concord agreed to a thing with no person at the top of it, and today a person ended it without asking the Concord, and the Concord is not the holder and cannot be the holder. So the Concord has a day on it, and it did not choose it. For nineteen years the count of eight went down a river four times a year and the only thing anybody could say about it afterwards was that nobody came. Now the Concord can stand in a room and say: that is not the day we agreed to either.**"

---

**And then a woman of seventy from Nine Elms asked a man of sixty-one a question, and it is the only thing that happened in that room anybody in it will still be able to say in four months, and nobody had asked him before, and she asked him once, and the answer went in, and the difference between those two facts is what this volume has been doing.**

"You copied it out," said Ilda Marsh. "**Did you understand what you were copying.**"

And Garret Slee, sixty-one, who has kept a book of this city's words for nineteen years and whose name is not at the top of it, answered in his own words and asked for it to be taken down in his hand and not anybody else's.

"**I understood every word of it and I could not have told you what an undertaking is. I have been asked twice in four days who read my book and I refused twice and I entered that I would stop asking. Nobody has asked me anything else in nineteen years. You have just asked me the first question anybody in this city has ever thought of putting to me, and it is the one I have wanted, and I am sixty-one, and I would like it entered that I have wanted it since the nineteenth year I kept the book, and that the reason nobody asked is that everybody assumed I had nothing to lose, and I have had nothing to lose for nineteen years, and it is not a gift.**"

He was not convicted of anything. **It is entered that nobody asked him before, that one person asked him after, and that the difference between those two facts is what a page that ends one document is for.**

---

**And the cost of it is not a figure, and it is the thing in the fourth clause, which returns the charge to whoever is carrying it on the day it ends.**

Nine Elms' water is not received, and the quarter is gone and has been gone for twenty-three days, and the drain in the fourth channel is being cleared by one woman of forty-eight, by herself, every morning since the twenty-ninth day of the new month, and the undertaking was going to stop that, and it did not, and nobody in about two hundred and forty people said her name out loud.

And the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force and it is this city's and it was not given up, and the document that has just been ended did not use it and did not need it and did not repeal it.

"The document was correct," said a man of thirty-two. "**Every clause in it is a sentence somebody in this room said out loud. It cost nobody anything. It would have given Nine Elms its water and their quarter and a man of thirty-two the whole of it back, which with a fourth line in force is his life, and I have known that for four days and have not said it in a room, and I am saying it now because in about four minutes it will be true.**"

"It is true now," said Ilda Marsh. "**And I would like it entered that I said yes to a page yesterday that took my water off me this afternoon, and that I have refused three things in ninety-two days and this is the first time I have agreed to something and had it taken off me the same afternoon, and I am not going to be the woman who says the room was wrong.**"

---

**And at about the sixth hour of the evening a form of four lines came off a shelf in a room over a market, and somebody finished it, and it is not a person, and this is the fifth time anybody in this city has built the same kind of thing and the fifth of the five is a hole with four lines in it.**

> **This undertaking is offered to me by name.**
>
> **I may refuse it. My refusal is entered. My refusal does not stop the undertaking. The undertaking proceeds without me. I am not on any roll by having been asked.**
>
> **Name. Date. The person asking. My answer.**
>
> **Who it is about: the holder.**

"A form about a person may not be used on a document that has no person in it," said Sera Vail, "**and a form about a person may be used on a document that has a person in it, and this document has a person in it now for the first time in its life, and the person is the holder, and there is no holder, and the answer to *who is it about* has been available since the first of March and nobody in ninety-two days has wanted it.**"

"So it is a form about a person."

"**It is a form with a person at the top of it for the first time and nobody has filled it in, and the heading is a person who does not exist. A page in ninety-two days of this city has finally got a person at the top of it, and the person is nobody.**"

**And it is entered that about nine people in this city have now built the same kind of thing five times in ninety-two days and none of them has a name for what it is, and that the page in the lockable room is not one of the five, because it has been used and a thing that has been used is not a thing anybody is building any more.**

---

**Nothing was gained, and one document was ended, and the ending took about nineteen minutes, and the finding is entered as a record and not a finding, and nobody in that room and nobody in this city is going to be able to say why it worked.**

The undertaking of the Lantern Concord is void from the second day of the month after this one, on the ground that its own condition was not met, and its own condition is in its own first sentence, and it is a thing the document said about itself. **Nobody was convicted of anything. The man of sixty-one is not convicted of anything and was asked a question he has wanted for nineteen years. The man of forty-one in a very good coat is not convicted of anything and was not in the room when it was decided. The man of forty-two is not convicted of anything and was right.** The Concord does not lose the lock, the roll, the notary or the gauge, and gains a term it has never had: a day on it that it did not choose. **The page is spent, it can be used once and it has been used once, and it does not say why it worked and it is not going to be asked.**

Nine Elms' water is not received, the quarter is gone, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid, and the cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth. A fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the thirty-third time. The licence is forty-two days old, in force and weaker, and the door is shut and has been opened twice in ninety-two days. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken.

Adrian Vale performed a working today, the first of his life in this world, in a converted salt store, in daylight, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and he has not yet been told what it cost. He holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and the black line in his right palm is ninety-one days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. **And there are twenty-six things the wall has said in this city and it has still never once registered a refusal of one of them, and the count of refusals in a second book kept at home, which has never been shown to anybody, is twenty-two and did not go up, and the count in his book at Aurel is thirteen and did not go up either, and a field answered by a person is in one of two books and nobody in this city knows which.**
