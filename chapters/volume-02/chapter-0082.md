# Chapter 0082 — Then Ask Me on the Day

On the fifty-seventh day of the new month, which was the eighty-second day of the flood and a Saturday, a room over a chandlery on the upper road spent a whole day arranging the order in which one question would be put to one woman on the Sunday morning, and the order was arranged so that three refusals would be standing before anybody opened their mouth, and the person who put the question was a man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set, and nobody in that room expected him and about nine people in it are entered as having been wrong about that.

**The licence is thirty-two days old. March is three days off. A woman of forty-one is not whole and has not been asked one thing about any of it, and the Sunday that has been named since the fifty-first day of the new month is tomorrow, and it is the fifty-eighth day of the new month, and the field four miles down the Reach road happens on the same morning and knows none of this.**

---

**She said the sentence that this whole thing is made out of on the forty-first day of the new month and it is six words long and nobody has improved on it and it is in the book in her own words:**

"**Then ask me on the day.**"

She is Perry Lask, forty-one, of the second list, and she is the only person in this world who holds a workway, and the workway is a procedure of nine clauses and nine weaknesses that a city invented badly in a hurry on the fortieth day of the new month, and the work itself is not written down anywhere and the document says so on its own face in twenty-eight words.

She has not been asked to do a single thing. **She has done one, on the fifty-third day of the new month, on a boundary nine miles up the upper road, in the wrong weather, having told nobody, and the residue of it is in her arm.**

---

**A bone-setter looked at the arm again on the Saturday morning at about the third hour and found nothing again, and said the only sentence anybody in this city can say about it, and said it twice because nobody believed her the first time.**

Hesper Saye is fifty-eight and keeps the low room by the salt wharf and does no private work, and she has told four hundred men things about a shoulder or a scalp and been wrong about ninety of them, and she does not round the figure.

"**I cannot find it,**" she said. "**I have looked at it four times in four days with three pairs of hands and there is nothing on it that a body should have and nothing on it that a body should not have, and I have written that finding down twice, and a written finding of nothing is a finding and not a failure, and it is the first finding in ninety days that was a shape and not a fact.**"

"And the shape," said a woman of twenty-nine who works in Saye's room.

"**The shape is that she will not die of it and will not be whole of it and nobody will be able to say what was wrong with her,**" said Hesper Saye. "**And I have been a bone-setter for thirty-one years and I am telling you that the only thing anybody can do about that is ask her. That is all that is left. Everything else we have tried is looking.**"

Nobody in the room said anything for about nine seconds, and then a notary of fifty said the thing that made the day possible.

"**Then we ask her,**" said Sera Vail, "**because on the fifty-first day of the new month a man fought two hours and ten minutes in a converted salt store for a line that says the person this instrument costs may be asked, once, on a named day, in daylight, in a room, and the answer may be no, and the refusal is entered, and the asking is not repeated on a second day, and the person is not entered on any roll by having been asked, and the answer is not a fault and is not a wage and does not go in the roll.**"

"And the day is tomorrow," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**The day is tomorrow, and the day is a Sunday, and it is a market day, and it is the fifty-eighth day of the new month, and it was named for that reason on the fifty-first day of the new month, six days ago, by about nine people in about four minutes,**" said Sera Vail, "**and nobody has told her. She has not been asked whether she wants it on that day. She was not asked whether she wants it.**"

**It is entered that a woman of fifty, who is the notary of a city of eleven hundred people, discovered at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the fifty-seventh day of the new month that this city had arranged a question for a woman without once asking her whether there was going to be a question, and that she stopped everything for about eleven minutes, and that the eleven minutes were her own and not anybody else's.**

---

**Then four things were read out, and three of them are refusals, and they were decided on the fortieth day of the new month, and somebody put them in that order on purpose.**

> **One. She is asked what she thinks the work is. That is all that is asked.**
>
> **Two. She is not asked to do the work. The work is not written down anywhere and the document says so in twenty-eight words on its own face, and a document that admits it does not know cannot be used to make a person do it.**
>
> **Three. She is not asked to account for the work, nor to justify holding it, nor to say whether she has performed it, because a standing which can be audited is a post with a person in it and this instrument has nobody in it.**
>
> **Four. She is told and she is asked, and the answer may be no, and the refusal is entered, and a refusal is asked again on a different day, and the day the asking becomes a duty somebody is to take it off the wall in daylight and say so.**

"**I put them in that order,**" said Sera Vail, "**and I put them in that order on purpose, and the reason is two sentences and it is mine. I put the three refusals first so that the question would be the last thing in her ear. If she says no to the question then the three refusals are already standing and the question falls over with them, and she is not left having said no to a thing she was not owed an answer to.**"

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has done with a person in this city in eighty days," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**Then say the second one, because I know what it is,**" said Sera Vail.

"**A sentence said twice is a mechanism. I said that in a yard behind a chandlery on the forty-eighth day of the new month and I have not stopped saying it, and if this line about asking her once is read out on a day and then read out again on another day, then nobody is asking her and the room is establishing that it may.**"

"**Then somebody is to take it off the wall in daylight and say so,**" said Nyla Ferrow, who is about seventy and has kept the lock chapel for fifty-one years, "**and I will do it and I will not need anybody's permission and I would like it entered that I have been waiting nine days for something to do that was not a floor.**"

**It is entered that about nine people in that room understood in the same nine minutes that the four things had been put in that order so that the refusals would come before the asking, and that about nine people in that room had not understood it and heard a notary being careful.**

---

**And then they found out who was going to ask her, and Adrian Vale was not allowed to, and the reason is four sentences and one of them is his own and the other three are a woman of fifty's.**

"I am not going to be the one who asks her," he said, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, before anybody had suggested it.

"**You did not get a vote,**" said Sera Vail, "**and that is not a finding and it is not a rule and it is what I have been thinking about for four hours, and here it is in nine words: you fought the line. Everybody knows what you are.**"

"Nine words," said the girl of seventeen. "**You fought the line. Everybody knows what you are.**"

"**And that is not why,**" said Sera Vail, "**and I am going to say the other three reasons so that nobody can build anything on the first one. One: if he asks her then the answer is about him. Two: the line exists because of him and a man who owns a line is not a person a room can be angry at when he uses it. Three: he was four feet back on the fifty-third day of the new month and he has not been able to say one word about that in nine days and I am not going to give him a way to make it into a favour.**"

He said that the third of those was the reason and she said she knew it was and entered that he said it and entered that she was not going to let him have it.

**And then four people said no, and about nine minutes went by, and a man of thirty-five said yes, and nobody in the room had expected him, and he said what he was going to do with the four hours and then he gave the reason, and the reason is nineteen words and is his own and is in the book.**

Dante Kuo is thirty-five. His right shoulder went on the thirty-first day of this flood while steadying a door frame, it was reduced twice, it cannot be set, and it did not go in the last ten days and no scene has made it whole and none is going to. He refused to be a man who is one fact on the forty-ninth day of the new month and he was amended into a line on a board for the worse at his own asking, and he is a man who took four days' notice once and used them to ask somebody else first.

"You have four hours," said a woman of twenty-four. "**What are you going to do with them.**"

"**I am going to go and ask three other people what she is going to hear,**" said Dante Kuo, "**and I am going to do it before I come back here, and not afterwards, and I will tell this room all three answers and I will not decide anything with them.**"

"And the reason."

"**Being useful is a description and not a safety, and I have been the useful one since the thirty-first.**"

**Being useful is a description and not a safety** is nine words of a woman of twenty-nine, said in a long argument on the forty-first day of the new month to two men who were both arguing about the wrong thing, and he has been quoting it for nine days and about nine people in this city have heard him quote it and none of them knew where it came from until the girl of seventeen told them, at the bottom of the table, out loud, with the day on it.

**It is entered that he asked three people and that all three told him the same thing, and that the thing was: *do not stand where she cannot see the door.* And it is entered that he stood where she could see the door.**

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the morning on the fifty-eighth day of the new month, which is a Sunday and a market day, in a room over a chandlery with the door open on both sides, a man of thirty-five asked a woman of forty-one one question in daylight in a room, and about nine people were in it, and the girl of seventeen had two books open.**

He asked it in the local language because she had asked for that on the fortieth day of the new month and he did not translate it and he did not stand in front of her. He asked it in the plain words of the fourth item on a page, which are: *what do you think the work is.* And then he did not say anything else for about nine seconds, and the nine seconds are entered, and about two hundred and forty people were not in the room and will hear about this on Monday in a version of it that has a shape in it and none of the shape will be in this.

She did not answer for a while. Then she said nine words.

"**A river promised a stone. I am the woman.**"

The girl of seventeen wrote them in both languages and read them back, and it is entered that nobody in the room corrected a single word of them, and that a bone-setter of fifty-eight wrote down afterwards that the arm did not change and that she had looked at it and that the looking was not the point.

**It is the first description of her own work that anybody in this world has ever had.** There is a document that says the work is not written down anywhere. There is a wall with nineteen shapes on it and the meaning of them unwritten. There is a form on a nail with four items and a price under them. **And there are now nine words in a girl's two books, in a woman's own words, about a thing she did on a boundary nine miles up a road in the wrong weather with nobody asked, and it is the only one of all of those that is written by the person it is about.**

"**You are not obliged to accept it,**" said Sera Vail, in the flat voice, and wrote as she said it, "**and it goes in as what a person said and not as a finding of this room, and it is yours, and you may take it back on a date, and the date is the fourth day of the month after this one, which is the date on the procedure, and nobody is going to put those two dates in the same sentence without asking you first.**"

"I am not going to take it back," said Perry Lask.

"**Then it stands, and I am going to write one more line and I am going to read it to you before I write it, and it is this. The three refusals of the fortieth day of the new month stand, and the question was asked once, on a named day, in daylight, in a room, and the answer may be no and the answer was not no. And the refusal is entered, and the asking is not repeated on a second day, and you are not entered on any roll by having been asked, and the answer is not a fault and is not a wage and does not go in the roll. And it is all of it on one line, and the refusals and the answer are on the same line, because they were decided together and they are the same decision.**"

"**Why is that the same decision.**"

"**Because you have just found out what the four things were for,**" said Sera Vail, "**and the four things were arranged so that the refusals would come first, and if the refusals and the answer are on one line then a room in four months cannot take the answer off the line and leave the refusals standing, and that is the whole of what this instrument has learned to do in nine days and it is nine words long and I am not going to say them in a room of nine people.**"

---

**Nothing was gained, and one woman described her own work for the first time in the history of this world. A bone-setter found nothing for the fourth time and entered that she found nothing. Three refusals stood and one answer was given and all four of them are on one line with a date on it, and a notary of fifty said in nine words that she was not going to say the other nine in a room of nine people. A man of thirty-five asked a question that he was not supposed to ask and stood where he was told to stand. A man of thirty-two was not allowed to ask and said so first and was told that he did not get a vote, and a woman of fifty told him three reasons and entered that he wanted the third one and was not going to have it. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty-two days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is eighty-one days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the measurement column and it is not filled and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The Narrow Mark was not opened and the review did not authorise an opening. The near gate is condemned and the four go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nothing was knocked this week and nothing was used as a reason to open anything. The fourth day of the month after this one is the date on a procedure of nine clauses and nine weaknesses and about nine people in this city now have a date they did not have on Friday. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-third time and the salt was not sent. March is three days off.**
