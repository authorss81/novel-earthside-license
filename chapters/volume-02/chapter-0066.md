# Chapter 0066 — The Man With a Shoulder

On the forty-first day of the new month, which was the sixty-sixth day of the flood and a Thursday, a man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set had a scene for the first time since the seventh week of this flood, and the scene was small, and the argument that was owed in it was thirty-one days late, and both men knew inside about nine minutes that the argument was not about the argument, and neither of them said so.

**Dante Kuo. Thirty-five. Right forearm reduced on the tenth day of this flood and fine. Right shoulder went on the thirty-first day at about the fourth hour of the afternoon while steadying a door frame, was reduced twice, and cannot be set, because there is nothing out of place. He will not be able to put both hands above his head and will not notice for a year. He does not die in this volume and no scene in this book may make him whole.**

He is Hearthguard captain in everything but name. He has been sleeping in a room over a market with eleven other people since the twenty-eighth day of this flood, on a mat, in a winter, on a shoulder, and he has not complained once, and **the reason he has not complained is in the book and it is his and it is forty-seven words, and it is the reason he is not to be pitied, and pitying him would be a form of not asking him:**

> "Because a man complained about a shoulder in the fifth week of this flood and a page got written about him, and the page was right, and I have read it, **and I would rather be the second kind of man in this city than the first.**"

**It is entered that the man on the page is a man of the flood crew and not the man under the board behind the ordinary wall, and that the two injuries are in different books, and that nobody may put them in one sentence.**

---

**And the man who read him four lines is twenty-four and lives over a boat-yard at Long Water and is on no roll at all, and she was made to answer for the nine days in a yard behind that boat-yard on the Thursday afternoon, and she was right about four of the five things she said and wrong about the fifth, and the fifth is the one that is in the book.**

Peth Lammay is a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail and has run the upper river and the Reach for eleven years and she carried the second Crown letter. She also reads four languages and one of them is English, which nobody in this city has ever fully priced.

"You read me four lines," said Dante Kuo, "and you did not tell me for nine days."

"I read you four lines and I did not tell you for nine days, and I want to do this in a yard, in daylight, with about nine people, and not in a room with a man on a mat."

"Give me the fifth thing. There were four lines and you have said four sentences and I want the one that is wrong."

"That it was not done to you," said Peth Lammay. "**That is the wrong one. It was done to you, and it was done to you because you are the only man in this city who can hold a page down with a shoulder, and I am not sorry about it, and I have been not sorry about it for nine days, and I would like that entered. And I would also like it entered that I have read that column and I know whose hand is in it and I have never once been asked about it, and I am not going to pretend I did not look.**"

**She was right about four of the five and wrong about the fifth, and both of those are on the page, neither above the other, and Adrian Vale entered the finding and it is seventeen words long:**

> "A kind person withheld a fact for nine days and it was still a kind thing done."

And Peth Lammay said: "**Then say the other half of it, which is that she would do it again, and I want that on the page too, and I want it on the same line.**"

It is on the same line.

---

**He has been put on paper work for five days without being told, by a man in a good coat, and the shoulder did not go.**

That happened in the third week of the flood. A man came to a room over a market with eleven men in it and said *paper work, four hours a morning, it is warm, you can do it with one arm* — and he did not say whose decision it was, and he did not say for how long, and Dante Kuo said yes, and did the paper work, and the shoulder did not go, and it has not gone in twenty-eight days.

He found out on the fortieth day of the new month. A page of a book, in a lane behind a boat-yard at Long Water, with a column about a foot long in it, and his name in the column, and the column headed — he could not read the heading. He cannot read well. He can read a gate's own tally and a roster and a boat's load and that is the whole of it, and it is entered that he asked a man of twenty-four to read him four lines and the man of twenty-four read him four lines and did not tell him for nine days.

**He did not go to anybody. It is entered that he did not go to anybody and that the reason was not forgiveness and not fear and not loyalty, and that he said the reason himself in a room over a market on the forty-first day of the new month and it is sixteen words long:**

> "Nobody asked me and I am not going to be the kind of man who goes."

**The shoulder did not go. Nothing has happened to the shoulder. It is not settable and it is not whole and it will not be noticed for a year and it is not a clock and it is not a punishment and it is not a lesson.**

---

**The argument was had in a room above a market, in about forty minutes, and it is the argument from the twenty-first day of this flood.**

That argument is: the first opening of the route should have been spent on a rescue instead of on a return. It was had first in this city in the sixth week of this flood, in a converted salt store, in about eleven minutes, between a man who had been in this world forty-one days and a man who had been in it twenty-one, and it was about a route and it was not about a route, and it was not finished, and the sentence about it — *there is no working route in this world* — has been in the book since.

"I am going to say the sentence," Adrian said, "and then I am going to shut up about it, and I want it entered that I am saying it on the forty-first day of the new month and not on the twenty-first, and that it is twenty days late on the clock and a good deal later than that in this book, and that the lateness is mine and not his."

"Say it."

"**The first opening should have been spent on a rescue instead of on a return, and I have known that since the twenty-first and I have not said it out loud in a room, and every day I have not said it has been a day I got to keep the room.**"

"That is a fair sentence," said Dante Kuo. "**Say the true one.**"

"I do not have a true one."

"Then say the fair one properly and I will tell you what is wrong with it, and I have been in this city twenty-one days longer than you and I am not being cruel, I am being the only person in this building who has carried a thing out of a hole."

**And then, inside about nine minutes, both of them stopped.**

It is entered as a fact and not as a conclusion. Nobody noticed the ninth minute. Adrian noticed it eleven minutes later, in the entry, and wrote that he noticed it late and that the lateness was the finding.

The rescue was on the twenty-eighth day of the flood. The return was on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, and it was a man of nineteen called Eli Mercer who said no eleven times to people who wanted something and then said yes once to a page and came back in about eleven minutes and said four words about a room that was taller.

**The first opening was not spent on a rescue and there was no rescue to spend it on, and the first opening was a breach of its own terms by a different man, and the return was the only lawful opening the instrument has ever had, and the licence that made the return lawful was signed and sealed eleven days after the rescue.**

The argument was over in about nine minutes and had been over for about nine seconds, and neither man said so, and about nine other people were in the room and about four of them noticed and the other five did not.

---

**And what they said for the next about nine minutes was the thing they had both been avoiding for fifty days, and it is in the book because the girl of seventeen was in the room and took it down and the width of her column is entered as a fact for the fifth time in that year.**

Adrian's question, which he put into words for the first time in sixty-six days, and it is eleven words:

> "**Have you been kept safe in this city by being useful.**"

Dante Kuo did not answer for about eleven seconds. Then:

"**Yes.**"

"Then I am going to do the second thing and I am going to do it now, because you are the only man in this city who can be honest about a stretcher and I have been waiting for it and I am not going to wait any longer. **Why is the only reason anybody wants you here the fact that you are the only man in this city who can carry a stretcher.**"

"Because I am," said Dante Kuo, "and because you all have already done the arithmetic and I can see you doing it, and I want to say one thing about that before you finish it, and it is not a complaint.

"**If the man who can carry the stretcher goes, the man who cannot is not a man. He is a mouth.**"

He said it in the plain language of a man who has been eleven weeks in a wall of a world and who is not a scholar and has no interest in anybody's argument.

---

**And the answer that is actually true was given by a woman of twenty-nine who had been in the room the whole time, and she did not soften either of them, and she has never softened anybody about anything.**

Nadia Brandt is twenty-nine and of the second list and came out of a chapel floor on the twenty-fifth day of this flood, and she has been on no roll at all since the third week of the new month, and on the thirty-seventh day she worked out in about an hour on a salt bin that a roll is the only thing in this world that makes a person findable and that being on no roll is at the same time the protection and the disappearance.

She had been standing by the window for twenty minutes with her arms folded.

"You are both wrong and you are wrong in the same way," she said, "**and the way you are both wrong is that you are arguing about whether being useful is safe.**"

Nobody spoke.

"**It is not safe and it is not unsafe. It is a description.** A description is a removal with a column for where the person is. That is in this city in a yard in about two hundred and forty people's hearing and it is not mine and it is not anybody's.

"And I have been the person on the second page twice this week and I have been paid on it and the wage I was paid on is one I can be removed from, and that is not a fault of yours and it is a fault of the page. **And the man who wrote a rule about that page and got nine nine-clauses out of it is the same man who is asking you whether being useful is safe, and you have both spent nine minutes and neither of you has said the true thing, and the true thing is that the question is not whether being useful is safe, because nothing about being useful is safe or unsafe, it is whether being useful is the only thing anybody knows you for.**"

**Adrian Vale has written the finding down, and the finding is hers, and she has not been thanked for it and has said she does not want to be thanked for it and is going to be asked about it in four months by somebody who will not accept it.**

---

**And then he offered the one thing he has never offered anybody in this world, and he offered it to a man of thirty-five, and he was refused in four seconds, and the reason is the sentence this volume is about and it does not belong to him.**

"I have one thing I have not offered anybody," Adrian said. "Not in sixty-six days. Not to a notary, not to the crew, not to a woman on a stair, not to a man in a good coat. I have never offered it once and I have had it the whole time and I have been sitting on it like a man sitting on a piece of paper.

"**I would like to be asked.**"

"No," said Dante Kuo.

"Four seconds. Before you say anything else — **I would like to be asked, and I will write down whatever you say, and you can refuse, and the refusal goes in a book and does not go anywhere.**"

"**No,**" said Dante Kuo, "and here is why, and it is twelve words, and I have had it since the sixth day of this flood and I have not said it out loud to anybody.

"**You are the only person here who gets asked. You, not me.**"

And then he said the rest of it, and it is forty-three words, and it is in the book in his own words because he said it in a room with about nine people in it and asked for it to be entered whole:

> "If you ask me, it stops being a thing I do and it becomes a thing I am a part of. And I have been in this world sixty-five days and I would like to have done one thing that nobody was keeping."

**Both of those are correct. The refusal is correct and the reason is correct, and one of the two is the sentence the whole of this volume is about, and neither man said which one, and neither man knew, and it is entered that Adrian Vale wrote in a private book that night that he had been refused a thing he invented by the person it was for and that the refusal was the correct one and that he had no way at all of telling.**

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the evening, a man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set was put on the nineteenth line of a page.**

The nineteen lines of what a person in this city may be asked to turn up for were read out in a yard on the thirty-sixth day of the new month, and the yard did not know what to do with them, and the seventeenth of them is a person's answer going nowhere, and the nineteenth of them is this one, and it is the only one of the nineteen that is a question:

> **A person may be asked whether he is able to do a thing. The answer may be no. The answer is entered, and the answer is not a fault, and is not a wage, and does not go in the roll.**

Nyla Ferrow put the slate on the nail under the chapel arch at about the eighth hour of the evening on the forty-first day of the new month, and read the nineteenth line out, and then read the eleventh, because the eleventh is the one that does the work:

> **No name is written at the top of anything on this board.**

**Dante Kuo's name is not on the slate. The slate has no name at the top of it. The nineteenth line is a line about anybody and it is the only line in nineteen lines that anybody can use, and it was used tonight, in a yard, in about two hundred and forty people, for a man who is not of the crew and is not a stranger and is not on a roll of anything and cannot make a fist.**

He is to be asked whether he can do a thing. He may say no. The no goes in the book and is not a fault and is not a wage and does not go in the roll.

**About two hundred and forty people did not notice. About nine did**, and one of the nine is a Hearthguard of forty-four who is not related to the man except by a house, and one is a girl of seventeen with a column four lines high, and one is a man of thirty-two who wrote six pages about what a person may not do and one sentence about what a person may do, and the sentence is twenty-four words long and it is Tamsin Quill's and she said it once on a stair.

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening the two of them went out onto the walk-round and stood on the two hundred steps, and neither of them opened anything, and nothing happened, and the number is the number.**

The rail is condemned. It has been condemned since the twenty-seventh day of the new month and it is not mended, and the timber is four weeks away at a price the Measure has tied to the crew's list, and the four people who go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge are four named people and are not these two.

Adrian Vale went down to the second step and stopped, four feet back. A man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set stood on the step above him and looked at the wall of the second bay for about nine minutes without saying anything at all.

**The dressed join is eight and seven-eighths of an inch and it is dressed to a line and it does not narrow. It was stepped twice, on the eighteenth day of the new month and on the twenty-fourth, and both steps were entered in an unnamed mason's own hand before the number was read. There is one ruled line left in that column for a reading that has not been taken, and it is one ruled line, and it was not filled tonight, because a number is only taken on the right water at the right hour with the same edge, and it is the ninth hour of the evening and the water is not what it was.**

"How is it," said Dante Kuo.

"It is the same," said Adrian Vale — and then, because a man of thirty-five is owed a sentence and not a number, in the local language: "**The join is dressed. A dressed join does not get narrower, it gets dressed. It does not knock and it has not said a word since the twenty-fifth day of this flood. And there is one line left in the book for a reading nobody has taken, and I have not taken it, and I am not going to take it tonight.**"

"**Then it is the same,**" said Dante Kuo, "**and that is a thing a man can stand next to, and I wanted to say so out loud before we went up.**"

**They went up. Neither of them opened anything. The black line in a man's right palm is sixty-five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch, and there is no field in the air, and it is entered that there was no field in the air, and it is entered that about four people were standing in that gallery and that two of them looked for one.**

---

**Nothing was gained. The shoulder does not go and is not whole and is not repaired. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, has performed no working, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence that is sixteen days old and gives him nothing. The main breach is closed and dressed and has never said a word. The ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The black line in his right palm is sixty-five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The review is in nine days, on a slate on a nail, with a date on it and no questions on it. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody has to open anything for that to happen, and nobody has asked what to do about it.**
