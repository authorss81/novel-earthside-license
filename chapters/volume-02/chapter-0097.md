# Chapter 0097 — No Share, No Interest, and a Refusal That Cannot Be Bought Out

On the seventh day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-seventh day of the flood and a Sunday, a floor above a chandlery by a wharf was written into a book in about four hours by fourteen people, and the three sentences that hold it together were said for the first time by a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and whose name was not on anything, and a man of forty-two argued in about nine minutes that one of the three cannot be administered and was right, and a man from another world was in the room for four hours and said four words and was not asked anything, and the room did not look at him once after the third hour, and that is entered as the first fact of the seventh day of the month after this one.

**The licence is forty-seven days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The black line in his right palm is ninety-six days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The Open Hand is a cooperative of traders from this world and from another, under local law, which in this book means somebody in the room had to say no to something. It has no share, no interest, and a right of refusal that cannot be bought out, and those three are the whole of it, and every one of the three is expensive, and the finding of the day is that the cheapest one of the three is the only one of them that is worth having.

**No share.** Nobody in the Open Hand holds a part of it. It is not divided and it cannot be divided, and there is no part to sell, and no one can be given a piece of it, and a person who leaves has nothing to take with them because there was nothing to take.

**No interest.** Nobody is paid for being in it and nobody is paid for bringing anybody into it. A person in it earns what their work earns, which is nothing extra, and it is not a wage, and it does not go on any roll, and it does not go in a wooden case four times a year.

**A right of refusal that cannot be bought out.** Any person in it may refuse any transaction, in any form, at any time, without a reason. The refusal cannot be bought, cannot be traded for, cannot be contracted away in advance, cannot be performed by anybody on another person's behalf, and it costs the person who makes it.

---

**And the three were said, and the first two were easy, and the third was said by a woman who has never had a name on anything and who has said since the sixty-fourth day of the new month that she is not going to be one of the people who says she tried, and who said that again first, in about nine seconds, before she said anything else, and then said all three of them in about forty seconds without stopping.**

"I have no standing and nothing at stake and I have been in nine rooms in four days and nobody asked me what I thought in any of them, and I am saying three sentences and then somebody is going to agree to them, and when they agree I will have a stake, and I have never had a stake in anything in my life, and I would like it entered that I noticed it happening rather than being given it."

Nobody wrote that down for about nine seconds, and then a girl of seventeen of seventeen wrote it down in a column four lines high, in her own hand, and it is on the page.

"The third one cannot be administered," said a man of forty-two, of the White Ledger, who had come down nine miles on the second day of the month after this one to watch a document be ended and had not said a word in the room until now, and who is right about eight sentences out of nine and wrong about the ninth in the way that is his whole character and not his fault.

"**Go on,**" said the woman of about thirty-four.

"**A right that cannot be bought out is a right that cannot be enforced. Enforcement needs somebody. Somebody who enforces is an office. An office is a standing. A standing cannot be refused. And that is the instrument that has been standing in this yard on a nail since the fifty-ninth day of the new month, which was the eighty-fourth day of the flood, and I did not bring that up to be pleasant, I brought it up because I have spent two months building a structure that does not come round to one man and I am the first person in this room who should tell you that you have just built one.**"

Nobody argued with him, and it is entered that nobody argued with him, and that about nine people in that room had the sentence *that is a lock that a guild can close* in their heads, in nine words said in a yard on the tenth day of the new month by a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read, and that nobody said it out loud, and that Jonah Pike did not know it had been said and would not find out for about four months.

"So the third one is wrong," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**The third one cannot be administered,**" said Jonah Pike, "**and I have been right about that in nine rooms in ninety-seven days and I am going to be right about it here, and I am also going to say the other half, which is that it is the only one of the three that is worth having, and I do not think anybody in this room has an answer and I think the answer is that there is not going to be one.**"

"Then it is a rule with no one behind it."

"**Then it is a rule that cannot be broken by anybody who was given power,**" said a keeper of about seventy who keeps a floor, "**and I have kept this floor for fifty-one years and I have watched four rules get argued about in fifty-one years and every one of them was a rule somebody was going to check. This is the first rule anybody has brought me that nobody is going to check. I want to keep it. That is not because I trust you. It is because there is nothing in it for anybody to buy and there is nobody in it who can be made to.**"

---

**And then, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, a trader from the Reach asked to buy a whole catch at one price, and one person in the room said no, and the no was honoured in about four seconds by everybody including the trader, and it went on a page in the words of the person who said it, and the page is not a roll.**

The person who said it was the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, and she is on no roll and is not one of the seventeen and is not one of the four households on the levy of the first Wayhouse, and she pays that levy on what the other four pull, and the reason she was able to say no in about four seconds is that she has been saying no in markets for nine years and nobody has ever once written down what she said, and today somebody did.

> **I was asked for the whole of it at one price and I said no and here is the no.**
>
> **The price was a good price. He did not try to beat me down. He offered what he thought I wanted.**
>
> **I am not writing his name. I am not writing why. I am writing that I have been in nine rooms in four days and nobody asked me what I thought, and this is the first time in nine years anybody has written down a thing I said no to, and I am not going to be the woman who says I tried.**

"That is not a roll," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and that is not an instrument, and it is going on a board in this room and it is staying on this board, and if anybody in four months asks me where the records of the Open Hand are I am going to say that, and they are on a board, and the board has got a refusal on it and a rate on it and no names.**"

"Her name is on it," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close.

"**Her name is not on it,**" said the woman of twenty-four, "**and if it goes on, she loses the only thing she has got, which is that nobody in this city knows what she looks like when she is arguing.**"

---

**And then a girl of seventeen entered the thing that is the finding of the day, and it is four lines high, and about nine people in that room understood all of it and two hundred and thirty will not.**

> **A man of thirty-two was in this room for four hours on the seventh day of the month after this one.**
>
> **He was asked nothing. He said four words. He was not needed.**
>
> **The first institution in ninety-seven days that this city has built which he cannot hold and does not need and is not party to.**
>
> **Nobody has thanked him for it and he did not do it for that.**

"He has never done anything for that," said a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read, "**and I would like the room to notice that he has never once done anything for that in ninety-seven days, and that I have watched about four hundred men be given something for it, and that the four hundred men are the reason there is a book on this floor.**"

---

**And then the three sentences were entered, and the name of the woman who said them was entered, and she asked for the second thing to be entered separately, and it is on the page in her own words and nobody improved on it.**

"**Enter that I was on nothing at the ninth hour of the morning and that I am on a page at the ninth hour of the evening and that it is a change and not a promotion, and enter that I noticed it happening and not somebody doing it to me.**"

It is on the page in her own words, and nobody improved on it, and the room checked it and let it stand.

---

**A man of about thirty-one asked the question that about nine people in that room had been carrying since the second hour of the afternoon, and it is the question a body of traders asks, and it is the question the Crown asks, and it is the question the Lantern Concord has been asked nine times in ninety-seven days, and it got the same answer here that it gets everywhere, which is that nobody could answer it and then everybody found out they could.**

"Then why would anybody join it," he said. "**No share, so there is nothing to get bigger. No interest, so there is nothing to be paid for. And a refusal cannot be bought out, so a person can stop the whole thing any afternoon by saying no. That is three doors shut and one door locked and a fourth one you cannot oil. I have been in four markets this month and I would not put my hand on any of it.**"

"Because there is no charge on it," said a woman of about thirty-four, "**and the Brass Measure has a quarter and a bad winter and the Concord has a fee in silver that nobody in this city can pay, and the four of you from the other world are named on a page that goes down a river four times a year, and there is nothing in this room that is going to describe you and there is nothing in this room that is going to cost you the price of a bad week. That is the whole of the offer. It is cheap because there is almost nothing in it.**"

"That is not a reason to join," said the man of thirty-one.

"**No,**" said the woman. "**It is a reason not to leave. There is a difference and I have spent nine years finding it out and I am not going to give you a better sentence than that at the seventh hour of the evening.**"

And then a keeper of about seventy said the last thing about the third of the three, and it is in the book, and about four people in that room understood it and about four did not.

"**The third sentence is the only one of the three that can be wrong on a Tuesday. I have watched four rules get argued about in fifty-one years and every one of them was a rule somebody was going to check, and a rule nobody checks is a rule that holds until somebody with more salt than sense decides to push it, and I do not care, because the push has to be made in the open, in a room, in daylight, in front of a floor, and everybody in this room can leave. I have a pail and a rail that is going and I leave when I like. That is what a rule with nobody behind it is. It is a rule that everybody can get out from under.**"

**And it is entered that none of the seventeen has land rights, no class, no standing and no workway, except one, and she is not whole, and a cooperative of traders does not change that, and nobody in about nine people in that room pretended that it did, and that a woman of fifty entered that she has been a notary for twenty-six years and that the Open Hand is the first instrument in this city that she is not the notary of, and that she is not going to be asked to be, and that this is the correct arrangement.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening the room emptied, and the floor was left with a ledger on the table that nobody had asked for, and about nine people who were going to be trading on it in the morning, and the ledger has rates in it and a date in it and a refusal on it and no names.**

**And the ledger is not a roll. And it is not going to Aurel. And it is not an instrument, and it does not answer for the first Wayhouse or for a Wayhouse in another village, and nobody has the key to it except the people in the room, and four of them are from another world and ten of them are not, and none of the ten can be found by any office on it, and the four can, and that is the price of the four and it is on a page.**
