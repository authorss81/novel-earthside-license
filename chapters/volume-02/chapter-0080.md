# Chapter 0080 — The Shrine

On the fifty-fifth day of the new month, which was the eightieth day of the flood and a Thursday, a page went on the inside of a shrine on a boundary at Four Elms that somebody else had put there, with a date on it and a price under it and a right of refusal in it, and about nine people kept it, and it is the first page in this city that has been put somewhere that is not this city's building, and nobody here has ever done that, and there is no procedure for it, and March is five days off and the review did not pay the field.

**The licence is thirty days old. It is in force and it is weaker than the one it went in with. The door is shut. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the twenty-first time and the salt was not sent. Nine Elms' water is not received. The whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, and that is not resolved, and is not going to be resolved before March.**

---

**A shrine is not an instrument, and the argument about that took nine minutes, and the woman who kept it made it in about four sentences and has not been asked anything about it in thirty-nine years.**

It is a stone about the height of a man's chest with a cut in the flat of it, on the north side of a road that forks at Four Elms where the ground drops away. It has stood where it stood for a hundred and forty years. The cut in it is not the boundary; the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream once, in living memory, at a public act with witnesses, and the cost column says the bottom field failed and four hundred people were rehoused. **The line in the shrine is where the water was before that, and the shrine is a thing that says so, and it has been saying so since before anybody alive was born, and it is not on paper.**

Marda Rood is about sixty-one. She keeps it. She kept it before her husband was alive and she will keep it after. She has a grandson of eight who went missing on the twenty-seventh day of this flood and was found at four in the morning by four people who were not sent, and she walked eleven miles in the night to ask a boy who came out of a wall a question, and she was told the truth.

"**It is a stone,**" she said, "**and a stone is not an instrument, and an instrument is a thing with a person in it, and there is no person in this and there never has been, and that is the whole of what makes it worth anything.**"

"Somebody will say it is a working site," said a woman of twenty-four. "**A cut in a flat stone at a place where the boundary has been is a decision somebody made with a knife. I have read about nine pages in this volume that say a decision is a working.**"

"**Then it is a working that finished a hundred and forty years ago and it has a hundred and forty years of nobody agreeing to it afterwards, which is the only kind of working I have ever heard of that nobody has been harmed by.**"

"And the page," said Adrian Vale. "**If a page goes on the inside of it, then in about nine months this stops being a stone and becomes the place a document is kept, and the moment a document is kept somewhere, that somewhere is a thing people can go to, and a thing people can go to is a room, and a room is people with a page on the table.**"

"**Then somebody will want to know what it is,**" said Marda Rood, "**and I will tell them, at the door, standing up, and that is the whole of what a stone at a road is for.**"

"**And put the household on it and not me,**" she said, before anybody had asked her anything. "**A name on a page is a thing that can be found, and I have buried two, and this is the only thing in this valley that remembers anything and it is not going to start by being a person.**"

---

**The page has six things on it, and the six things are the six things of the first Wayhouse with the middle taken out and a stone put in, and the price is written under the six and is not a seventh, and that arrangement is on a nail in a lock-chapel arch in this city and it is not a decision anybody made for this page.**

> **First. It is a stone and it is not an instrument, and nothing may be worked at it, and nobody may make it into a working site, and there is no pattern in it.**
>
> **Second. It is not a Crown document, and it does not go in a wooden case, and it is not in the roll of strangers, and it is not in the book of the Lantern Concord, and the Concord has no copy of it and has not asked for one and will not be given one unless the household that keeps it asks for one in daylight with a date on it.**
>
> **Third. The household that keeps it is the household of Four Elms that has kept it, and whoever is holding it now after her, and a household is a door with people in it and not a name on a page.**
>
> **Fourth. The page may be taken down by that household, on a date, for its own reasons, and it is not asked why twice, and a household may say no and need not give a reason and is not entered anywhere.**
>
> **Fifth. The page is dated, and the date is the fifty-fifth day of the new month, and a page with no date on it is a form and a form becomes a standing and a standing cannot be refused.**
>
> **Sixth. Anybody may come to the stone on the north side of the fork at Four Elms and ask what it is, at the door, in daylight, in any weather, and be answered there, and being answered is not agreeing to anything.**
>
> **And under the six, and not as a seventh: the price is a quarter of a pound of salt a month, cut out of that household's own winter allotment and not bought from anybody, and the price is on the page so that in nine months somebody will not be able to call this a favour, because a favour is a thing that stops.**

**It is the first page in this city that has been put somewhere that is not this city's building.** There is no procedure for it. It is not a Wayhouse and it is not an instrument and it is not in the form required for anything and it is not going to Aurel, and about nine people in this city have a reason about that shrine and none of them is the Crown and none of them is the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month.

"Then we are inventing law in a yard on a road," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and nobody said the word legal and about nine of us have it in our heads.**"

"**We are putting a page on a stone,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and a stone is not this city's building and it is not anybody's, and if that is law then the word has been in my head since the thirty-ninth day of this flood and I have been carrying it about for forty-one days without knowing.**"

---

**And the Reed Commons' position hardened on the Thursday morning, in a converted salt store, in a document that took about nineteen minutes and is the fourth page of a thing nobody in this volume has read end to end, and the fourth line is in it and it is Ilda Marsh's and it is in force.**

Hessa Ruun is the water-count of the Reed Reach and she is the closest thing the wetland has to a notary and nothing like one. She came up the Reach road on the Thursday with the answer of the meeting-ground at Verrow and read it out in nineteen minutes in a converted salt store in about forty people, and it is a refusal, and the refusal is of Adrian Vale's page.

> **The meeting-ground has been given a page. The page says that the whole of the standing charge is on one man and that a man is not one person and that it is therefore to be asked, separately, in daylight, in a room, of each person the charge is made of, whether they will have it on them.**
>
> **The meeting-ground refuses the page and does not accept the argument in it, and the fourth line is in force, and the whole of the charge remains on the private debtor, and the meeting-ground will say this again on a date and the date is entered.**

"**Why,**" said Adrian Vale, in a room, with a date on the page, which is the only way he is ever able to ask a question in this city.

"Because it is a description," said Hessa Ruun, "**and we have spent nine years learning that a description is the only protection anybody has ever been offered by that office, and we are not going to be told at the eleventh hour that a description can be a kindness, because in four months a man in a building nine hundred miles away is going to read a page that says four people were asked and he is going to read the four names off the back of it, and they will not be on the front, and he will have them.**"

"And the fourth line stands."

"**The fourth line stands and it took nine days and it is ours and we are not giving it up for a page.**"

A woman of twenty-four said the thing that had to be said, and she said it in nine seconds, and it is in the book in her own hand:

"**Cost it in that order. First: a wage is owed to a person and a person is described on a roll, and this page is not a wage, so that sentence does not bite today. Second: a wage off a second page is a wage a person can be removed from, and this page is a second page, and it asks four people whether they will carry a thing, and if a woman of Nine Elms says no then she is a woman who refused a charge, and that is a column in a roll in about four months. Second sentence bites. Page refused. Correct.**"

Nobody argued. **And it is entered that the fourth line of the term sheet of the twenty-third day of the new month is in force, that the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two, that the page that would have spread it across about four people has been refused by the meeting-ground that wrote the line, and that this is the second time in eleven days that a correct instrument has been used correctly against this city and against him, and the first time was a Crown officer with a column four lines high and the second time is a page of this city's own writing.**

---

**And at about the eleventh hour of the morning, on the north side of a fork at Four Elms, a page went on the inside of a stone.**

Marda Rood put it there herself. She is about sixty-one and cannot be managed and she had it in a piece of cloth and she put it in against the inner face of the stone, under the lip where rain does not get, and the cut in the flat of the stone is on the other side and is a hundred and forty years old and has nothing to do with it.

**Nobody stepped over it. That is entered, and it was asked about, and a woman of seventy who keeps the lock chapel asked about it on the Thursday afternoon in about nine seconds and was told the answer in twenty-five words and the twenty-five words are hers:**

"**A page on a nail is a page people read. A page in a stone is a page people have to come and ask for.**"

---

**At about the sixth hour of the evening, in the yard under the lock chapel, the license, the door, the fee, the field and the review were all said out loud in about two hundred and forty people's hearing, by a girl of seventeen reading a page, and none of it was good news and all of it is entered.**

"A trial licence thirty days old, in force, continued, and weaker than the one it went in with." "A door that is shut and is not going to be opened and has been opened twice in eighty days." "A fee of eleven ounces of silver owing with no date for the twenty-first time, and the office has been told what this district has and this district has been told what the office will take, and neither of those is on any page the office has sent." "Nine Elms' water not received for the coming quarter, and the credit refused, and the quarter gone." "A season of a field four miles down the Reach road, unpaid, entered ten times in one month, with no figure on the end of it, and **March is five days off and a woman in Corrow has five days in which to decide how much of her life to put in the ground.**"

"And the review," said the girl of seventeen, "**answered on the fiftieth day of the new month, in daylight, in about two hundred and forty people, against eleven questions and a second page and a slate, and the eleventh question was four words long and the answer was four words long and it was not a noun, and it is on the page, and the page is a hundred yards from this yard.**"

"Nobody turned the slate over," said Renna Ord, from the back.

"Nobody turned the slate over," said the girl of seventeen, "**because the question was a good question and the only thing this city has never wanted is a good question turned over.**"

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, at the top of the upper road, a woman of sixty-one who cannot read was reading nineteen shapes aloud to a docker who has a slate, because anybody may pick the work up, and eight of them are different and further down the road and have not been looked at, and one of them is not a shape.**

Sabra Vint is about sixty-one. She has a house with a burnt lintel on the upper road and nine years of paper with nineteen shapes on it and eight of them different, and she has never once written down what she thinks the shapes mean, and she has come down two hundred steps four times in nine years, and she said on the nineteenth day of the new month, in a room with a burnt lintel, that she is sixty-one and would like to stop, and that somebody has to be the one who writes down what they think they mean, and that is the only thing she has done wrong in nine years.

Renna Ord is fifty-eight, she cannot read, and she has been copying shapes off a nine-mile road into slates for about three weeks, and she has copied about nine of the nineteen and then got more slate, and she cannot read what she is copying, and a man of thirty-two told her the truth on the fourth step of the lower gallery on the forty-fifth day of the new month and she did not ask him again and it is entered that she did not ask him again.

They went up the road together at about the ninth hour of the evening on the fifty-fifth day of the new month and they read the nineteen out loud, one at a time, and the woman of sixty-one could not read and the woman of fifty-eight could not read and between them they got through nine of the nineteen in about forty minutes.

At the eleventh stone, about two hundred yards short of where the eleven end, there is a name cut in the flat top in letters about a hundred and forty years old in a Hearthguard's hand of that century, in a different cut from the eleven that follow it, and it is one of the eight, and Renna Ord read it out loud as a shape because it is a shape, and a Hearthguard of forty-four put his hand on it on the thirty-fifth day of the new month and said four words.

"**That is one of the eight,**" said the girl of seventeen, who had come up with them and who is on no roll in this city.

"**And the other seven are further down this road,**" said Hettis Roke, "**and nobody has looked at them, and I have been up here once in nine years and it was on the thirty-fifth day of the new month, and I have not been up here since, and I am not going to pretend I am going to be able to.**"

"Nineteen shapes," said Renna Ord, "**and eight of them are different, and a name is not a shape, and there is a wall in a room above a market where all of it is written in two hands with a date on it and Eight at the bottom of the cell and no name on it, and nobody has written the meaning in nine years and a woman of sixty-one says she would like to stop.**"

"**Then somebody writes it,**" said the boy of nine, who had come up as well.

"**Not tonight,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and not by him, and not in a room, and not in nine minutes. It is a word and a word is a page and a page has a date on it and a price under it and somebody who can be angry at it, and a woman of sixty-one asked to be asked properly in a room in daylight on a different day and not this year, and the day the asking is on is a Sunday in three days and it is taken.**"

"Then what is being done on the road tonight."

"**A road,**" said Renna Ord, "**and a woman who cannot read saying a name out loud to a docker who cannot read, in the wrong weather, with a slate.**"

---

**Nothing was gained, and the batch closes on a road.**

The licence is in force and weaker. The door is shut. The fee is owing with no date for the twenty-first time and the salt was not sent. The field is unpaid and March is five days off and the review did not pay it. The fourth line of a term sheet is in force and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two and a page that would have spread it was refused by the meeting-ground that wrote the line, and that is not resolved and is not going to be resolved in this volume.

**A page is in a stone on the north side of a fork at Four Elms, with a date on it and a price under it and a right of refusal in it and six things on it, and the first of the six says it is a stone and not an instrument and nothing may be worked at it, and the sixth says anybody may come and ask what it is at the door and be answered there, and a household may take it down on a date and is not asked why twice.**

The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the measurement column and it is not filled and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is entered as a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and the second page says in nineteen lines that this city is not asking about it and why.

Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence thirty days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working in ten days. The black line in his right palm is seventy-nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. A woman of forty-one is not whole and will not be asked what happened. A woman of forty-eight in a village of nine households has not been asked whether she will carry a charge that is on a man.

**And about nine people are going up the road in the five days that are left in a month that is not March, and eight of the nineteen shapes are further down it, and seven of the eight have never been looked at, and one of them is a name.**
