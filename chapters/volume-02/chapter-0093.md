# Chapter 0093 — What It Cost, in About Nine Minutes

On the third day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-third day of the flood and a Wednesday, a man who had performed a working for the first time in his life in this world came down two hundred steps of a river wall in the wrong weather and could not get to the top of them for about four minutes, and then he stood in the lower gallery in front of a dressed stone and found out in about nine minutes, in daylight, in front of about nine people, what a working had taken off him, and the answer is a person and not a figure, and the person was him.

**The licence is forty-three days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The black line in his right palm is ninety-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch, and there is nothing in this city that would say by how much it has not moved.**

---

**He had done it yesterday in a converted salt store in front of about two hundred and forty people and he had walked out of the room afterwards and gone up the road to the top of the wall and come down again, and he had slept, and he had not been sick, and there was nothing on him, and it was the first time in ninety-three days that nothing at all had happened to his body and the first time in ninety-three days that something had happened to him.**

The walk-round is two hundred steps down a river wall with a low parapet and a rail that is half-rotten and condemned, and four people go down it in the wrong weather with a gauge twice a day. He was at about the ninth step from the bottom when his knee went, and he sat down on the stair, which is not a thing anybody in this city does on a walk-round, and about four people who were on the wall above him stopped, and nobody came down.

"Do not come down," he said. "**The rail is condemned and the stair is ice and I am not going to have four people on a rail for a man who can sit down.**"

So they stood on the wall and waited, and it took him about four minutes to get to the bottom, and when he got there he stood up in the lower gallery on the second bay and looked at the wall, and about nine people were on that wall and in that gallery by the time he had been standing there for about nineteen seconds, and nobody had sent for anybody, and they had all come on their own.

---

**What he could see on the second bay was the pale edge, dressed, and the number, and the two of them at the same time, which he has done since the fourth day of this flood. What he could see that nobody else in the gallery could see was a third thing, and it was not on the wall.**

"**There is a line in this gallery that I have never seen before,**" he said, "**and it is not in the stone, and it goes from the dressed edge about eleven inches and stops, and it does not go anywhere, and I can see where it would go if it went anywhere, and that is the only reason I know it stops.**"

"Can you see where it goes," said a woman of about thirty-four, "**or do you want to be the man who says that.**"

"I can see where it goes."

"Then it is not a fact anybody else has," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and I am not going to write it down as one, and I would like it entered that a man said a thing about a room on the third day of the month after this one and that about nine people in this gallery believe him and about nine of us cannot check it, and that those are the same number, and that the whole of ninety-three days of this city is that sentence.**"

Nyla Ferrow, seventy, who keeps a floor and has had a nail in an arch for fifty-one years, said one thing about it, and it is hers.

"**I have walked that floor for fifty-one years and I know that gallery the way I know my own hand, and I would like it entered that there is nothing in it I have not seen, and that a man of thirty-two has just told me I have been looking at something for fifty-one years that I did not see, and I am not going to insult either of us by pretending that does not shake me.**"

---

**And then, at about the second hour of the afternoon, a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf asked the question that about nine people in that gallery had been carrying since the first of March, and she asked it because she has been in nine rooms in four days and nobody in any of them has asked her anything, and she asked it in daylight, in front of nine people, and she said afterwards that she was sorry about the room and was not going to apologise twice.**

"There is no office in Saltwake for a hand that holds things," she said. "**And there has not been one for ninety-three days, and I said on the fifty-eighth day of the new month that a person with no work in this world is the thing the eleventh question was about, and the answer was four words, and they were yours, and they were right.**"

"Nobody has asked them," said Renna Ord, who cannot read.

"Nobody has asked them," said the woman, "**and the man who can now open a single-holder threshold is the man nobody has asked anything, and he is on no roster, and he has no work, and he is not a holder of anything, and I want somebody in this gallery to say out loud what has to be true for a person like him to be allowed to do that, and I do not think anybody in this city knows.**"

**And it took about nine minutes to establish three things, and all three of them are on the page, and none of them is a ceremony, and nobody in that gallery stood up.**

One. **He can open a threshold for one named living person, once, and only if two witnesses who do not live with that person say yes, and only if the place on the other side has agreed to receive them, and only after a fresh inspection, and he may not open the same route twice in a row. That is what a single-holder threshold is and it is four lines and it is not a licence and it is not a page anybody may carry.**

Two. **He cannot do it today, and he is not going to be able to do it for as long as the Narrow Mark stands closed, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in ninety-three days and the hundredth day is seven days away and it is not going to be opened then.**

Three. **He has no mark of his own and he is not going to be given one, and Eli Mercer's mark belongs to Eli Mercer and to nobody else, and the other mark in this world is in a ground and does not transfer out of it, and there is nobody in this world who can carry a person back, and that is not going to change in this volume of days.**

"Who enters that," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"Nobody," said Adrian Vale. "**Nobody enters it. That is the first thing I have got that has nobody's name at the top of it, and I have been in this city ninety-three days waiting for something to be mine and I have just found out that the reason nobody has asked me to hand it in is that it cannot be handed in, and I would like somebody to write that down before I improve on it.**"

---

**At about the third hour of the afternoon, a woman of twenty-nine asked the question that the day turned on, and it is on the page, and she asked it because nobody else in that gallery was going to.**

"What did it cost," she said.

"**I do not know,**" said Adrian Vale, "**and I have known that since the fourth hour of yesterday afternoon and I have not slept on it, and I am going to tell you what I do know and then I am going to stop talking.**"

**And what he did know took about nine minutes, and it is on the page in his own words, and about nine people were in that gallery, and nobody may summarise it for him, and it is entered that nobody did.**

"It is not a door that has closed. I want that first because I have watched this city spend ninety-three days believing that everything is a door.

"**I have not lost a road. I have lost a knowing. For ninety-three days I have carried about with me, in the ordinary way a man carries a thing he has not looked at, the idea that if I ever wanted to go back there was a way to work it out. Not today. Not for a year. Worked out. And it is gone, and it went yesterday afternoon in a room in about four minutes, and it is not going to come back, and I am not going to be able to explain to anybody in four months why, and I am telling you now so that nobody in this city spends the next four months thinking it is a door they can oil.**

"**And I want the other half said, because the other half is worse and nobody is going to say it for me. It is not gone. It is still there. I know where it is. I know what it is made of, and I know that every time I go near a wall in this city for the rest of my life I am going to be able to see the line that goes eleven inches and stops, and I am not going to be able to use it, and I am not going to be able to stop seeing it, and that is what a working costs the person who does it, and it is in the book as a sentence of somebody else's, and I am not going to say it as mine, because I did not coin it and I am not going to.**"

"It is mine," said Mina Okoye, "**and you are not going to say it as yours, and you are right not to, and I want everybody in this gallery to notice that he has just given away the only sentence he has ever wanted and he has given it back to me in front of nine people so that nobody could say he took it.**"

---

**And then the nine people were asked, one at a time, in daylight, in the lower gallery, what he had given up, and it was asked in the local language and then in English, and it was asked by a woman of twenty-four who entered that she was asking because nobody else in the gallery was going to, and that a person who has given something up is the only person in the world who knows what it was.**

Eight of the nine could say.

Nyla Ferrow, seventy: "**You have given up the word later.**" Peth Lomas, fifty, a mason, not of the crew: "**You have given up being a man who is going somewhere.**" Ilda Marsh, seventy, of Nine Elms: "**You have given up the right to say you will go back and mean it as a plan.**" Hettis Roke, forty-four, of the outer ward: "**You have given up a door that was never a door, and that is worse, and I have watched about four hundred men be given something worse and be told it was a gift.**" Renna Ord, fifty-eight, who cannot read: "**You have given up not knowing what it costs.**" Garret Slee, sixty-one: "**You have given up the only thing you had that was not a page.**" Sera Vail, fifty, notary: "**You have given up the right to be a question.**" Priya Shah, twenty-four, of the first group: "**You have given up the right to be a visitor, and you did not know you had it, and that is the one I would like entered twice.**"

All eight are in the book in their own words and none of them has been improved on and none of them has been summarised by anybody for him, and it is entered that he wrote them down himself in a column four lines high in a girl of seventeen's book, and that his hand did not go over any of them.

---

**And the ninth was a man of thirty-five whose right shoulder went on the thirty-first day of this flood, was reduced twice, cannot be set, did not go, was not made whole, and is entered in a page in a stone as a debt with no line that will mend it. He has asked one question in ninety-three days that nobody wanted him to ask and got it asked, on the fifty-eighth day of the new month, which was the eighty-third day of the flood, and a woman of forty-one answered it in nine words because a notary of fifty would not let anybody else do it.**

"**Nobody in this city was told what he had.**"

And the gallery went quiet, and it is entered that it went quiet, and that about nine people in it had understood in about four seconds, and that the ninth speaker had not understood anything at all and had answered the only question he had been asked in his whole life, which is what a man answers when somebody asks him what was taken off a person and he does not know what the person had.

"That is nine words," said a man of thirty-two, "**and it is the truest thing anybody has said about me in ninety-three days, and I am not going to improve on it, and I am going to write it down exactly as it was said.**"

He wrote it down. **And it is entered that he wrote it down in a column four lines high in a girl of seventeen, and that he did not add anything under it, and that when a woman of twenty-four asked him in about nine seconds whether he was going to put what he had lost on the same page for completeness he said no, and that the reason he gave is in the book in his own words, and it is this: *I have just found out what this thing does and it puts the loss where a person is. I am not putting it in a second column.***"

---

**And then a notary of fifty said the thing that the eight sentences and the nine words had made, and it took about nine seconds, and about nine people in that gallery were not going to be able to unhear it.**

"**Eight people have just said out loud where a man's life is thinner than it was yesterday, and every one of them was asked and every one of them answered, and I have written all eight in their own hands and the ninth in his, and that is a page, and a page is a description, and I have spent twenty-six years telling this city that a description is a removal with a column for where the person is, and there is a column for where eight people are on it, and it is on a slate in a gallery, and it is not going to Aurel, and I would like everybody in this room to notice that the same sentence has been true for ninety-three days and that nobody had ever noticed it until eight people said a sad thing in a row.**"

Nobody argued. **It is entered that about nine people in that gallery understood that about nine people in this city have just written down where eight people are, and that a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped, and that this one does not stop, and that a cost which does not stop is a standing, and that he wrote a sentence in a yard on the sixty-third day of the new month about a standing and a document and it was not about himself, and it is about himself now.**

---

**Nothing was gained that anybody can carry away, and the cost is a person, and the person is named on a page in a slate in a gallery, and nobody has asked him anything and nobody is going to.**

Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing and not in a ceremony, and nothing in this volume of days will announce it. He can open a single-holder threshold: **one named living person per opening, one return mark carried, willing local witnesses who do not live with the person, and the same route may not be opened twice in a row by him. He has opened none, and there is no clerk in this city who would be told if he did.** He is barred from the trial licence's own rosters, holds no land right and no title and no workway and no seat and no mark of his own, and he is still the man who reads the number off a wall four feet back from it. He does not reach the next stage in this volume and there is no office for what he now is, and there has not been one since the fifty-third day of the new month, and the field that would create one was answered by nobody at all on that day and has not been answered since.

The licence is forty-three days old, in force and weaker. The door is shut and has been opened twice in ninety-three days. The undertaking is void and is on a nail and on a river and in a wooden case in Aurel. Nine Elms' water is not received, the quarter is gone, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid, and the cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth. A fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the thirty-fourth time. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room.

The black line in his right palm is ninety-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. **And there have been twenty-six things the wall has said in this city and it has never once registered a refusal of one of them, and a city that named a thing and sealed a page and got nothing at all out of the wall on the first day of the month after this one is a ninth silence, and the pattern is nine for nine, and it is nine and not ten because the wall said a thing yesterday in the room where the undertaking was ended, and a day the wall speaks on is not a silence however hard the day was.**
