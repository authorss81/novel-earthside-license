# Chapter 0095 — Two Documents and a Road

On the fifth day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-fifth day of the flood and a Friday, two pieces of paper were put on a table in a converted salt store by two people who had no reason to be in the same room, and a girl of seventeen tried to write both of them in one column and was stopped, and about nine people in that room worked out that the thing that had happened in five days was not a victory, and that this city has now built two things it can be angry at and cannot stop, and that neither of those two things is a person and that the whole of ninety-five days is that a city which could not be angry at a page has become a city with two pages in it and no way to reach either.

**The licence is forty-five days old. The undertaking is void and is on a nail and on a river and in a wooden case in Aurel. The field was not paid on the first of March. The hearing is about eight weeks off and there is one hearing and one clock.**

---

The first of the two was brought by a keeper of about seventy, who took it off a nail in an arch and carried it two hundred steps down a river wall in the wrong weather, and about nine people in the yard watched a woman of seventy carry a document she had spent fifty-one years keeping a nail next to, and nobody in that yard said anything about it.

The second was brought by a girl of seventeen, who could not bring the page itself because the page is inside a stone on a boundary nine miles up a road in a shrine a household keeps, and so she brought a rubbing, in charcoal, on the back of a copying list, and she entered that it is a rubbing and not the page and that about four people in this room had said so before anybody asked.

"They are the two documents in this city," said Sera Vail, "**and I have put them on a table and I want them looked at, and I want it entered before anybody looks at them that I have not put them on the same page and am not going to, and that the reason is a rule and not a preference.**"

"What rule."

"**A description and an instrument are not the same shape and this city has found that out in ninety-five days at a cost I have been in four rooms for.** A description asks and names. An instrument does neither; it is offered to a body and it costs the body nothing and it has a hand at the bottom of it that is not named. **A page that asks and names is a page a person can be angry at. A page that does neither is a page nobody can be angry at, and about nine people in this room were able to be angry at both of those sentences in different weeks and nobody has written down why.**"

---

**And the girl of seventeen tried to write them both in one column anyway, because she is seventeen and it is four lines high and two things that arrived on the same table in one hour are two things that go in one column, and a woman of fifty let her get four lines down and then stopped her, and her reason is eleven words and it is hers and nobody improved on it.**

"**Two things, two columns. The gap between them is the finding.**"

"It is untidy," said the girl of seventeen.

"**It is untidy and it is the only honest arrangement of the two, and you did four lines before I stopped you and I am leaving the four lines in and striking nothing, and I would like a person to look at them in about four months and say whether the two columns were worth the mess.**"

Nobody in that room said the word *mess* was wrong. **It is entered that about nine people in that room understood that the two documents are opposite in almost every way — one is offered to a hundred and forty people and costs nobody anything and the other is owed to twelve named people and costs a quarter of a pound of salt a month out of a household's own winter — and that the room wanted very badly to put them in one column, and that the wanting is the finding and not a mistake.**

---

**And then the man of about forty-four with the stall said the thing, and it took about nine seconds, and it is the finding of the fifth day of the month after this one and it is in the book in his own words, and about nine people in that room had it and about two hundred and thirty have not.**

"**A document becomes a thing a person can be angry at when a person can be angry at it. We wrote that down on the sixty-second day of the new month about a guild, in a boat-yard, on a step, with a woman of seventy standing on it. This city has now got two of them. The page in the stone, which asks and names, and a piece of paper on a nail which does neither and which nine days ago a page ended in a room in about four minutes without touching it. That is two things a person can be angry at. And we cannot stop either of them. I have been angry at both of those papers in five days and I have not been able to do a single thing about either one, and I did not know until about nine seconds ago that those two sentences were the same sentence.**"

"You are angry at the void one," said a woman of twenty-four.

"I am furious at the void one," said the man of about forty-four, "**and I said out loud in a room on the sixty-second day of the new month that a guild which can be asked has a standing and a standing cannot be refused, and a document is the standing, and a page that ends a document is the only thing anybody in this city has ever been able to do about one, and I got what I wanted and I have not slept.**"

"**Then say the other half,**" said a man of thirty-two. "**You wanted a page that could end it and we built one and it cannot be ended. There is nothing to point the next one at. That is not fury. That is arithmetic.**"

"Nobody in ninety-five days of this city has asked that," said Renna Ord, who cannot read, "**and I am not going to pretend I asked it, and I am going to say the thing that is under it, which is that about two hundred and forty people have watched a document ended this week and about two hundred and thirty of them heard a document, and both of those are in this city tonight, and one of them is on a table.**"

---

**And a woman of twenty-nine said the sentence that closes the account of five days, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is the first time anybody in this city has said it, and about nine people understood it at once.**

"**Everything this city has decided in ninety-five days happened in a room, and the two things on this table are in every room in this city, and the rooms are the reason we can see them and not the reason we can stop them.**"

"That is the finding of five days," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, "**and I would like it entered that it is hers and that about nine people in this room understood all of it and that the rest of this city will be told four words of it inside a month.**"

---

**And then the second half of the account was given, and it is not a victory, and nobody in that room was able to say what had been won, and the girl of seventeen wrote all of it down in four lines and the four lines are on the page.**

The undertaking is void and is in a case on a river and a hearing is about eight weeks off and **nobody in this city knows what is in a case that arrives and nobody here knows what is in a case that does not arrive, and the case left on the sixty-fourth day of the new month, which was the eighty-ninth day of the flood, and there is a second thing on the quay it could be in and nobody has gone and looked.** The licence is in force and weaker, and the weakness is that it survived a refusal. The door is shut. The fee is owing with no date for the thirty-sixth time. The field is not paid. The fourth line of a term sheet is in force. A form of four lines has a person at the top of it for the first time and nobody has filled it in, and the person is the holder, and there is no holder. The second page in the stone is a list of what is owed, owed to twelve named people, and **three of them are still debts** — the work in a woman's own words in the document that says the work is not written down anywhere, and a figure on the end of a cost column, and one question to a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house at Nine Elms which has not been asked and is not going to be asked this month, and that is entered as a thing this city decided and not as a thing this city forgot.

"Ninety-five days," said a woman of fifty, "**and I can tell you what the bill is and I cannot tell you one thing we got, and I have written about four thousand pages in this city in twenty-six years and I have never once been in a room where that was true.**"

---

**And then a girl of seventeen read out the last thing on the list, and it took about nine seconds, and it is the only thing on that table that is about the future and it is on a slate in four hands on a wall above a market, and about nine people are going to use it.**

"The nineteenth day of the month after this one is on a slate and it is the only thing on the slate," she said. "**And it is not March, and it has never been March, and about nine people are going up a nine-mile road on that date in the wrong weather. Eleven of the nineteen shapes are the same and eight are different, and all eight of the different ones are further down that road, and seven of the eight have never been looked at, and the eighth is a name in a Hearthguard's hand about a hundred and forty years old.**"

"Who is going," said a man of forty-four.

"Nobody has said. **That is the state of it. A date and not a plan, and the reason it is not a plan is that the woman who has been doing it for nine years said she would like somebody to have the date and not her, and that is on the page in twenty-one words and she has not been thanked and is not going to be.**"

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening the gallery emptied, and a man of thirty-two went down two hundred steps of a river wall by himself in the wrong weather, and the rail is condemned and he went down it anyway, and nobody went with him, and nobody had been asked to.**

The second bay of the lower gallery is lit by two lamps and a door that is open on a yard where a salt line is laid. The pale edge is dressed. The number is on the wall where it has been since the fourth day of this flood, and it is the twenty-fourth reading's number, and it is eight and seven-eighths, and the twenty-third reading was the same, and there are two steps in the column in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and there is one ruled line left, and it is not filled, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken and was not taken on the first of March and is not going to be taken in this volume of days, and the reason is on the page in a mason's words: **you do not write a number into a column when four people do not agree about where the edge is.**

He stood four feet back, which is where he has stood every time, and there was nobody to read the number to.

**He read it to himself, out loud, in an empty gallery, at about the ninth hour of the evening, and it is entered that he did, and that nobody heard it, and that the number is a number and not a sentence about anybody, and that the dressed edge is dressed and does not narrow, and that he did not fill the ruled line, and that he was not going to.**
