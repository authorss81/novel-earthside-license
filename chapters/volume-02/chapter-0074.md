# Chapter 0074 — The Nineteenth Line

On the forty-ninth day of the new month, which was the seventy-fourth day of the flood and a Friday, a man of thirty-five with a shoulder that cannot be set used a line instead of being rescued, a docker's apprentice of twenty said no in front of about two hundred and forty people, the day's work was entered as not done, and about nine people in a city of eleven hundred understood all three of those things at the same moment.

**The review is tomorrow. The licence is twenty-four days old. The rail on the two hundred steps is condemned and is not mended, and forty men went under the near gate twice today at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and one of them did not go the second time.**

---

**He had four days' notice, which is what the nineteenth line gives a person, and he used them, and that is the whole of his chapter.**

The nineteenth of nineteen lines, on a slate on the nail under the chapel arch since the forty-first day of the new month, is the only one of the nineteen that is a question:

> **A person may be asked whether he is able to do a thing. The answer may be no. The answer is entered, and the answer is not a fault, and is not a wage, and does not go in the roll.**

Dante Kuo was put on that line on the forty-fifth day of the new month, at about the sixth hour of the evening, in about two hundred and forty people, and about two hundred and forty did not notice and about nine did. He has four days' notice in the line. Nobody wrote it. It is a night's notice for the things a person may be asked to turn up for, and this is not one of those things, and he read it on the nail with nine other people on the Monday and he has had since then to decide what to do about it.

What he did about it was that he asked somebody else first.

**He did it in a yard, in daylight, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and he asked Hesk.**

Hesk is the foreman of the day crew. He said the four words under the near gate for the first time in his life at the sixth hour of the morning of the forty-sixth day of this flood, before anybody had asked him for anything, and he voted against the first version of the distributed working fourteen hours later, and his vote is in the book. **He has not gone under the near gate once since the twenty-fifth day of this flood.** The book has that in it and the book has not asked him about it and he has not offered it.

"Can you go under at the eighth hour."

"No," said Hesk.

**Nobody argued. Nobody was angry. The no is entered, in a girl's hand, in a column four lines high, and the width of that column is entered as a fact for the thirteenth time in this year, and the no says what the line says it may say.**

"The reason is a word," said Hesk, when nobody had asked him for a reason and about nine people had wanted one. "**I am able to do a thing. I have not been under that gate since the twenty-fifth day of this flood and I could go under it tomorrow and I would not, and if I say no on a line then I am a no, and a no is what the line was made for, and I am not a no.**"

"Then the line is wrong," said Dante Kuo.

"It is not wrong," said Hesk. "**It is one word. It has got a yes in it and a no in it and there is nothing in it for a man like me and there is nothing in it in this world for a man like me and I have been a foreman in this city for twenty-two years.**"

**And that is what the nineteenth line is amended, and it is amended on the forty-ninth day of the new month, in daylight, in about two hundred and forty people, and it is the first time in seventy-four days that a line on that board has been changed by the person the line is about, and nobody had asked for it to happen.**

> **A person may be asked whether he is able to do a thing. The answer may be no, or it may be *I can do some and not others*, and that is an answer and it is entered, and the answer is not a fault, and is not a wage, and does not go in the roll.**

"That is a worse line," said a woman of twenty-four, in about nine seconds. "**That is a line with a clause in it and a clause is where a decision hides.**"

"It is a worse line," said Dante Kuo, "**and I asked for it and I would like the room to notice that I asked for a worse line, because I have been on the other side of this page for four days and the page is better than I am at it.**"

---

**He was asked himself, at about the fifth hour and forty minutes of the afternoon, in the same yard, and he answered in about nine seconds, and his answer was not about his shoulder, and he made sure of that before anybody could get to it.**

"Am I able to do a thing."

"You are able to do a thing," said Ottoline Vask, thirty-four years in Saltwake, who wrote the nineteen lines in about eleven minutes on the thirty-sixth day of the new month and who has asked nobody anything in this volume that anybody has entered.

"I am not able to do a working," said Dante Kuo. "**I have not been able to put both hands above my head since the thirty-first day of this flood and I will not notice for a year, and I am telling you that now because a line says the answer is entered, and because a woman of fifty-eight told me in a room in the third week of this flood that a man who is told his arm is fine stops counting things and then he has nothing, and she was not talking about an arm.**"

"Entered," said the girl of seventeen.

"**And I am not a no,**" said Dante Kuo. "**I am a man who can lift forty pounds with his left arm and cannot get his hands above his head, and I have been that since the thirty-first day of this flood, and the whole of the last five days in this book has been people deciding which of those two facts I am. I have been the one. I would like the room to have the other one for a day and see what it does with it.**"

Nobody said anything. **The answer is entered in his own words and it is the longest answer in seventy-four days that was given to a line and it is four lines high in a girl's hand and the fourth line is the one the width of the column is about, and the width of the column is entered as a fact for the fourteenth time in that year.**

**And then a docker's apprentice of twenty, who is on the flood crew's second page and is a name nobody in a yard has said out loud, said the only line he has ever said in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and about nine of them heard that a boy of twenty who says no is a fact and not a scene.**

---

**And at the eighth hour, in ice, the day crew went under the near gate at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and one man did not go, and his name is in this book and it had not been in a yard before this afternoon.**

Tam Ockley, twenty, a docker's apprentice of the flood crew, is on the second page of the flood crew's own page and is a name nobody in a yard has said out loud, and on the twenty-seventh day of the new month he went down the condemned rail and could not rise for about nine minutes and has a hand's width of frostbite on a forearm that will be all right.

He is one of the forty. He is not of the foreman's day crew; he is on the flood crew's roll and the flood crew goes under the near gate in the second hour and the day crew in the first, and they do not go together and they do not go in a line, and that is the ordinary maintenance of this lock and it is what the bottom field pays for.

"Are you able to go under at the eighth hour."

"No," said Tam Ockley.

**That is the second no of the day, and the first one that cost anything, and the two of them are not the same thing, and the book has to keep them apart, and this is the first time this city has had to keep them apart.**

"**Then it is entered,**" said Sera Vail, "and I am going to read the fourth page, because a man who is asked and says no is not a man who did not turn up, and in seventy-four days of this instrument those have been the same man every time, and they are not the same man, and one of them has a fault in it and one of them has a name in it, and neither of them goes to a door."

She read it. The join is where it is. The instrument is not suspended by anybody, not withdrawn, not ended, it stands. The record says the crew did not turn up and does not say a fault and does not name a man and does not say a name was sent for, and there is no entry of anybody going to anybody's door. The cost column stands as last entered and the difference between that figure and the truth is a gap and not an error. The next tide is not worked until four people have been down the walk-round in the wrong weather with a gauge. **And a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody, on any day, for any reason.**

The day's work was not done. It is entered as not done, and not deferred, and not short, and not carried forward, because a thing that is not done is not done. The crew was one man short. Nobody said his name at the gate and his name is in the book at the asking and not at the work, and that is entered, and it is the first time in this volume that a name has been entered at one thing and not at another.

**Nobody thanked him. The man who said no was not thanked for it by anybody in about two hundred and forty people, and the man who asked him was not thanked for asking, and both of those facts are entered, and neither of them is a complaint.**

---

**And then the room did the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the reason this chapter is on the page, and it took about nine minutes and it is entered in nine lines.**

Somebody said, in a yard, that Renna Ord had told this city four of the forty would be hurt before March, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, in no working, and that no instrument in this world can stop it, and that March is eleven days off.

"Then count them," said a woman of twenty-nine who works in Saye's room, who has never once claimed a sentence that is not hers. "**Not the hurt ones. The forty. Go and count the forty.**"

The roll of the forty has forty names on it and no name at the top of it. It is a roll with no name at the top because a roll with a name at the top is a post, and a post is a chair with a man in it, and that is the standing finding of this volume in one sentence and it is a sentence a woman of twenty-four wrote on a slate in chalk over a word in a market.

It was carried out into a yard in about nine minutes by four people and a lantern. It has **thirty-eight names on it that are doing the work this week and two that are not**, and both of the two are entered, and neither of them is a fault and neither of them is a name anybody has been sent for.

One is a man of about fifty-one who has been in a fever-house at Long Water since the thirtieth day of the new month, and nobody has been to his door, and nobody is going to, and that is the nineteenth line working exactly as it is written and it is the first time in seventy-four days that the nineteenth line has stopped anybody being found.

The other had a name struck off the top of that roll by a notary on the twenty-third day of the new month, in daylight, for a true reason, and the date that the person whose name it was put there is still there, and it is entered that the date is the only part of it anybody has ever asked about.

"So the four is a quarter of forty," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and the forty is thirty-eight, and so it is a quarter of thirty-eight, and there is no quarter of thirty-eight.**"

"**It is nine and a half,**" said Peth Lomas, fifty, a mason of Saltwake, not of the crew, who reads the number. "**Nine and a half men, and you cannot hurt half a man, and a person who tells a city that four of the forty will be hurt is telling it a thing that cannot be counted, and that is not a prediction. That is a mood with a figure written on it.**"

---

**And Renna Ord said the finding, and it is hers, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is twenty-two words, and she said it standing in a yard in ice with her hands not in her gloves:**

> **A prediction which is wrong in the direction it wanted to be is worse than one that is wrong the other way.**

"A man who has been told four can plan a winter around four," she said. "**A man who has been told four and gets five has been lied to by his own city, and a city that lies to itself about a number in March is a city that will be lied to about a number in a building nine hundred miles away, and I would like the room to understand that I said the first half of that about myself.**"

"You did not say it about yourself," said Adrian Vale. "You entered the four and the prediction both, on the same day, before anybody asked, and neither above the other. That is the opposite of lying to yourself."

"**That is me holding both ends,**" said Renna Ord, "**and holding both ends is not the same as being right, and I have been seventy-four days in this world and I have been right about a number twice, and both of them were numbers other people could see.**"

Then she said the only sentence in that yard about what the nineteenth line cost, and nobody else in about two hundred and forty people had said it, and it is nine words, and it is in the book in her own hand:

> **It cost a day's work and bought a record.**

---

**At about the seventh hour of the evening, in a room over a market, a man of twenty asked a third question.**

Curtis Okonkwo came out of the chapel floor on the twenty-fifth day of this flood with ten other people, in about four seconds, four feet beyond the threshold slab, and has been fed every day for forty-nine days by people who did not ask him whether he wanted the food, and is on no list, and has not asked for anything, and asked his first question on the thirty-fifth day of the new month on a two-hundred-step stair in three words: *are they coming.* Adrian answered and then asked a fourth thing and it was a question, and the answer to the third question has never been entered.

He asked it in English, badly, and Adrian Vale understood it, and answered in four sentences because four is what he had.

"**Nobody knows, and the only person who has written down what she thinks they say is a woman of sixty-one who has written it down for nine years and has never once written the meaning down, and she says somebody has to be the one who does that and that she would like to stop.** **A docker of fifty-eight has copied about nine of the nineteen into a slate in ten days and cannot read what she is copying, and a man of thirty-two told her the truth about two of them on a step and she has not asked him again, and eight of the nineteen are different and they are further down the road and nobody has looked at them.** **There is a slate on a nail in the chapel arch with eleven questions on the front of it and four words on the back, and anybody in this city may stand in the rain and read it, and it is read out loud twice a day, and that is the only reason I know to ask anybody anything at all.** **I have had two questions and both men gave me the wrong one and this is the third and I am not going to be told that a fourth thing is coming.**"

Nobody in the room said anything for about nine seconds.

"He is not asking about the door," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close.

"He has never once asked about the door," said Adrian Vale.

---

**Nothing was gained. A line on a board was amended by the person it is about, for the worse, at his asking. Two men said no in about two hundred and forty people and one of them cost a day's work. The day's work is entered as not done. The fourth page of the instrument was used for the second time and it does what it says it does. The roll of the forty carries thirty-eight names that are doing the work and two that are not, and the two are entered. Renna Ord's four is entered as a prediction and as a number and both stand and neither is above the other, and the finding is that a prediction which is wrong in the direction it wanted to be is worse than one that is wrong the other way, and that a mood with a figure written on it is not a prediction. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence twenty-four days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is seventy-three days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the column and it is not filled and it is not going to be filled. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and the second page says in nineteen lines that this city is not asking about it and why. Nine Elms' water is not received. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifteenth time. March is eleven days off, the season of a field four miles down the Reach road is still unpaid, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything. A review is tomorrow.**
