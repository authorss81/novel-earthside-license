# Chapter 0069 — The Third Refusal

On the forty-fourth day of the new month, which was the sixty-ninth day of the flood and a Sunday, the ninth household of Nine Elms refused for the third time, and the refusal cost them nothing, and it cost them nothing because somebody had made it affordable first, quietly, in a form with a column four lines high, and used the Crown's own words to do it.

**It is the sharpest thing that has happened in this volume and nothing was done wrong. That is the finding, and it is the finding, and it is not going to soften.**

---

**Here is what is on the page about Nine Elms, and all of it is true, and none of it has been re-litigated in sixty-nine days.**

Nine households. Water not received, at the word of the nine households rather than the assembly, and may be withdrawn on any day the household chooses. They have refused the standing charge of the Reed Reach's term sheet, and under the fourth line of that term sheet the whole of the charge returns to the private debtor, and the private debtor is a man of thirty-two from another world who is not in the room and is on no roster of anything.

**The fourth line is Ilda Marsh's. She is seventy. She wrote it and it took nine days and she used it on the thirty-sixth day of the new month and the whole of a season of the bottom field came back onto a man who had nothing, and she did it in front of every household in her village before she sent it, and it is the correct thing to have done and it is a catastrophe and both of those are on the page.**

**They have now refused three times: the receiving, the charge, and on the forty-fourth day of the new month, the credit.**

---

**The credit is legitimate. That is what makes it the finding.**

Darrel Quist is a Crown factor of the reed levy, thirty-seven, in a very good coat, with a form that is in the form required and a seal that is a real seal and a reason that is the correct reason and a column four lines high. He came out to Nine Elms on the Saturday morning, on a horse that the levy owns, with about nine other people, and he did it in daylight.

He did not bribe anybody. He did not threaten anybody. He did not break a rule. He put a page on a table in the largest of the nine houses and he read it out, and it is four lines and four lines and four lines, and the widow's column is four lines high, and the width of that column is entered as a fact for the sixth time in that year.

> **That the district's quarterly levy, being unpaid, may be advanced against the district's own goods to the value of the unpaid quarter.**
>
> **That the advance is made without interest and without security other than the goods.**
>
> **That a refusal which costs other people money is a decision about other people, and the office is required to take that into account before it treats a refusal as a refusal.**

**It is the Crown's own fifth clause, out of the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood, in a Crown's own words, in the form required, used to help nine households of a village of nine, and it is the fifth clause of the notice that this city has been reading aloud in yards for three weeks, and the clause is one that a notary of fifty and a woman of twenty-four and a docker of fifty-eight have all agreed is one of the most dangerous sentences ever sent to a district.**

"I have taught that clause to about forty people in this city," Darrel Quist said, in the largest of the nine houses, with about nine people in it, "and I have never once used it in a form required, because there was never a case. **There is a case. A village has refused three times and it has not given anybody a reason it could not give and it has a quarter unpaid and nine households and one drain to keep clear, and I have advanced the quarter, and I have not asked for anything back, and there is no security on that page except the goods, and the goods are the reeds.**"

He put the chalk down. He did not need a chalk; he had a page.

"And I want it entered that this is not a favour of mine and it is not a favour of the office," he said. "**It is the office doing the fifth clause properly, for the first time, and it took me four days to find out whether I was allowed to.**"

---

**And the road out there is nine miles and the water is in the channels and a woman of about fifty has kept a count of something in that village for twenty-two years, and she read the Crown officer's page in a reed channel on the Saturday afternoon and gave the warning she gave on the twenty-third day of the new month, and it has come true in twenty-two days.**

Hessa Ruun is the water-count of the Reed Reach and the closest thing the wetland has to a notary and nothing like one. On the twenty-third day of the new month she gave Adrian Vale a term sheet on a raft at Verrow and told him that a concession is a document, a Crown scholar reads concessions, and a village that has refused twice can be brought to a meeting-ground about a bill.

"Read the third line again," she said, on a plank in a channel nine miles from a village of nine houses.

"I have read it."

"**Then read it to me, and then say who wrote it.**"

He read it. She waited for about nine seconds.

"That clause is the Crown's own," she said, "and the Crown has never used it before, and I do not think it was written for villages. **I think it was written for a district that owes money and wants to look reasonable.**"

"Quist is not a factor. He is the officer of the levy and he said he had four days to find out whether he was allowed to."

"**Then he is the first man in this business who has said out loud that he had to find out, and I have been in this water for twenty-two years and that has happened twice.**"

She turned the page over and looked at the back of it, which is blank, and she did that for about four seconds on purpose, and then she said the thing that is in the book in her own words:

"**A favour you cannot refuse is not a favour.** I said it in that house before you got there and I will say it to the officer and I am not ashamed of having said it first, because I have said it in a reed channel for eleven years to men who were owed money by my own village.**

"And here is the second half and it is worse than the first. **When a thing is affordable, the people who cannot afford it look like the people who do not care. That is how a quarter of a Reach goes. Not by a door and not by a flood. By a page.**"

---

**And a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail read the refusal back in a boat-yard at Long Water on the Saturday evening and would not carry it, and it was carried the next day, and the reason is the best thing anybody said that day.**

Peth Lammay, twenty-four, on no roll at all, four languages, eleven years on the river, read the four lines of the returned page and put it face down on a bench.

"I am not carrying this on Sunday," she said. "**I am carrying it on Monday, and on Sunday I am going to write a line on the back of it, and the line is going to say what the cost of the refusal is in a family, and not in a quarter, because a quarter is a district's word and a family has one.**

"And I want the notary to countersign it, and I want it entered that I refused to carry it on the day it was set and carried it on the day after, and that a carrier is paid by the person whose description is in the case and nobody has ever yet paid me for a page I did not want to carry."

---

**And the quarter is a quarter, and it is not a metaphor, and it is entered in this book in the ordinary words of a woman of about seventy who has lived in it her whole life.**

Ilda Marsh sat down again at her own table and said what refusing it costs, and it took her about nine sentences and the girl of seventeen wrote all nine and the page is the reason a village is not a number in this book:

"It is the drain. It is the drain in the fourth channel and it is cleared twice a year and it is cleared by us, and if it is not cleared the fourth channel silts in a thaw and about nine hundred yards of somebody's water goes into somebody's field.

"It is two elms cut in February that would have stood until March. It is a cart of peat.

"It is a boy of fifteen who does not go to the Corrow bench in the spring, because the bench is nine miles and his father's knee is not what it was and the peat is not going to be bought.

"It is a widow of about sixty who has four hundredweight of reeds in and does not sell them in and is paid at the gate in March.

"**And it is the coming quarter, and I do not know what a quarter is in money, because I have never had one, and the man in the good coat knows and does not know what one is in work, and he wrote the page anyway.**"

It is entered that the last sentence of that was said with no anger in it at all, and that the Crown officer was in the room and heard it, and that he wrote it down in the margin of his own form in his own hand and put the form in the case with the page.

 and it is twenty-five words, and she said it standing up in her own house with the page on the table, and about nine people heard it and about two hundred and thirty have not been asked.**

> "A favour you cannot refuse is not a favour, and we have taken one, and we are going to be asked about it in March."

Nobody argued with her. It is entered that nobody argued with her, and that four people in that room agreed with her and could not say why, and that one of those four is the man who wrote the clause's remedy in a letter to the meeting-ground on the thirty-fifth day of the new month, and he agreed and said that the clause is correct and the use is correct and both of those are the problem.

**Ilda Marsh has now been asked three times in this book and has answered three times, and two of the three were in her own house, and the first was on the twenty-third day of the new month on a raft at Verrow, and the second was on the thirty-sixth, and this is the third, and the three answers are in the book in her own words and the three of them are about a cost and not about a route.**

---

**And then a woman of twenty-four said nine sentences, and the nine sentences are the reason this is the batch's worst news and not its best, and nobody in the room liked any of the nine.**

Priya Shah, twenty-four, of the second list, on no roll, has never been asked about that and has not been asked again:

"One. The clause is right. Two. The use is right. Three. The form is right. Four. That is the whole of the danger and I am going to say it four times because it is four sentences and there is no fifth one.

"Five. **The office knows what that village has because this city wrote it down on purpose.** Six. On the thirty-first day of the new month a woman with a bar in this city invented a form that carries no meaning, and it is sealed, and it is sixty-one lines, and it is the best thing any of us has ever made. Seven. **And it is in a building's shape, and it is filled in honestly, and it is on its way to an office nine hundred miles away, and there is not one line in sixty-one lines that says whether the city wrote it to be unhelpful or because it had nothing.** Eight. A form written by a city that has decided to be useless and a form written by a city that has nothing look the same on the page, and the page is what an office reads. Nine. **So the honest useless form has put a Crown officer in a village in four days, and I am proud of it, and I am afraid of it, and I said both of those on the thirty-seventh day of the new month and I am saying them again in that order.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"Nine sentences," said Darrel Quist, "and I am a Crown officer and I have to answer them, and I would like to say that the eighth of them is the reason this office is not corrupt. **A clerk who reads a form and cannot tell whether it is honest or cynical is going to believe the man in the room. That is not corruption. That is the oldest thing in the world and it is called a village.**"

"And the fourth line," said Ilda Marsh, from the table, not moving.

"**The fourth line is the reason I am here, and I will not pretend otherwise, and if the fourth line was wrong the clause would have nothing to attach to and I would have gone back down that road with nothing in my hand.**"

---

**And the credit is refused, and refusing it costs them the quarter, and the quarter is harder than it was on the eleventh day of the new month, and that is entered as what a village decided and not as a decision about other people.**

It took about nine minutes. There was no argument. The nine households were not asked as a village; they were asked as households, in their own doors, over about a day and a half, and the fourth household took four hours, and the reason that is entered is that the fourth household has a woman in it who cannot read and the page had to be read to her twice by two different people, **and it is entered that she then said yes for about two minutes and then said no, and that the two minutes are in the book, and that nobody has removed them.**

The page came back. The page goes to Aurel in the wooden case at the next quarter, with a true sentence on it, and the true sentence is four lines and the last of them is the one:

> **That the credit was offered, in the form required, in the office's own fifth clause, and refused by nine households; and that refusing it costs this village the coming quarter's levy in full, and in work, and in money, and that the cost of the refusal is not borne by the office and is not borne by any person outside this village.**

**And a Crown officer entered that he was the one who made it affordable, and that nobody asked him to, and that he will be asked about it.**

He asked for it to be entered in his own hand and Sera Vail entered it, and she added a fourth line of her own and said that a notary may add a fourth line, and the fourth line is:

> **That the office did this correctly and this city has no instrument that can make a correct thing expensive, and that this is not a finding against anybody and is the finding of the day, and I have entered it as the finding and not as a fault, and I would like whoever reads it in four months to know that I entered it myself and was not asked.**

**Nothing was gained. The reach's standing charge stays on Adrian Vale and the door stays shut and Nine Elms' water is still not received and will not be received for the coming quarter, and the whole of the village's winter is worse than it was on the eleventh day of the new month. The trial licence is nineteen days old. The review is in six days, on a slate on a nail, with a date on it and with eleven questions and a second page on the same nail, and nobody in this city has yet put a date on the page for the day the questions are read. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eleventh time and the office has been told what this district has. The main breach is closed and dressed and has never said a word. The ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster, has performed no working, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody has to open anything for that to happen, and nobody has asked what to do about it.**

---

**And at about the fifth hour of the evening, in a yard under the lock chapel, in about two hundred and forty people, a man in a good coat asked the man from another world a question in nine words, and it is the first time in two volumes that he has asked him for anything, and the question is not about a route.**

"**What do you want me to be useful for.**"

Adrian Vale did not answer for about eleven seconds, and the eleven seconds are entered, and about nine people heard the whole of it.

"**I do not know,**" he said. "And I have wanted to say something clever for about four seconds and I have not got one, and I would like it entered that I did not get one, **because you have been the most useful man in every room in this city for sixty-nine days and I have never once said thank you and I have never once said what for, and those are the same mistake.**"

"I know they are," said Marrow. "**That is why I asked, and I would like to say one thing in a yard and not in a room, and it is seventeen words, and I have had it since the fortieth day of the flood and I have not said it.**

"**I have been useful for a month, and it got me nothing, and I did not notice.**"

It is entered that nobody said anything to that, and that about nine people in that yard heard it, and that it is the first sentence anybody has entered about a man in a good coat that is not about the door, and that this city has not convicted him of anything and is not going to.
