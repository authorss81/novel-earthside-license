# Chapter 0059 — Eight of Nine

On the thirty-fourth day of the new month, which was the fifty-ninth day of the flood and a Thursday, a man of forty-two who had argued the Crown's form in nine sentences in a yard in the sixth week of the new month and been right in eight of them came back to Saltwake with the only winter money in the city, and it was the fourth term of the Brass Measure in a new coat, and it was refused in about eleven seconds in a yard in about two hundred and forty people's hearing, and he was right in eight sentences and the ninth was not about Aurel and it was about the seventeen, and the man from another world he said it to had a tenth sentence ready and it was the wrong one and he said out loud, in the yard, in front of about two hundred and forty people, that he had a sentence ready and it was wrong, which is the first time anybody in this city has heard him say a thing like that about himself. And at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the two hundred steps, with the rail condemned and the dressed join eight and seven-eighths under a wall that has never said a word, a woman of twenty-nine took the notebook out of his hand and did not give it back, and said nineteen words, and he wrote them down in the morning and did not show them to anybody for the rest of the day.

---

**Jonah Pike is forty-two and he arrived dry on the fifth day of this flood and has stayed dry, and he has not had a scene in twenty-nine days, and about nine people in this city have noticed that and about four of them have noticed that nobody has noticed.**

He came up the road from the Measure's yard at about the seventh hour of the morning of the thirty-fourth day of the new month with a ledger under his arm that was four weeks old, and he put it on a trestle in the yard under the lock chapel, and about two hundred and forty people came because a man with a ledger in a yard is a man with an offer in it, and Ottavia Reek came with him and stood four feet to his left and did not speak for about eleven minutes, which is the most anybody has seen her do.

"What is it," said Renna Ord, before he had opened it.

"**A materials account,**" said Jonah Pike. "Not a contract. Look at the page."

It was a page of numbers. Salt, thirty tons, at the works. Timber, four weeks. Iron, for the gauge and for a new shoe on the near gate sill. Rope. Lime. Chalk. A line for a boat to be hired for a fortnight. A line for the rail, and beside the rail a note in a different hand that said **the Measure can do the rail in nine days and will not do it for eleven ounces and will take the list**.

**And the list was the fourth term, and it was not a list any more. It was a column in a materials account. That is what he had done in four weeks with a document that had been refused in a yard in the third week in one sentence, and he had done it himself, and Ottavia Reek had watched him do it, and there is an entry about that.**

"You put it in a column," said Renna Ord.

"I put it in a column," said Jonah Pike. "**There is no clause on this page called *the day and flood crews' labour shall be drawn from the Brass Measure's own list.* There is a number on this page that says forty men, and there is a note, and the note is in a different hand because it is not mine.**"

"There is a note in a different hand," said Renna Ord, "**and the different hand is a guild's and the different hand is the whole of it, and I said the words to you in a yard in the third week of the new month and I am going to say them again because this is a different page and I am not going to let a page be allowed to be a different page.**

"**A thing nobody has asked for cannot be owed, and a thing that cannot be owed cannot be paid, and a thing that cannot be paid is a tax. A wage off another list is not a wage. It is a favour with a handle on it, and you have put the handle in a column.**"

And about two hundred and forty people in a yard and Renna Ord said the eleven words in about eleven seconds and it was over, and the yard did the thing it has done four times in fifty-nine days, which is to stand there.

"I know," said Jonah Pike. "**I want it entered that I knew before I opened it and I brought it anyway, and that a man who brings a thing he knows will be refused has a reason, and my reason is that we are going into the second month of the winter and the rail is going and your four are iced and there is not a second rail in this city until the ninth of next month.**"

---

**And then he said nine sentences, in a yard, in about two hundred and forty people's hearing, and about nine people wrote them down, and the girl of seventeen took all nine and has entered that she took them and that she did not agree with eight of them.**

> **One. This city is going to be short of salt, timber, iron, lime, rope and boat by the fourth month and every one of those is a thing that is made by somebody and sold by somebody and the somebody is a person with a yard.**
>
> **Two. The Measure is not the villain in this and I have never said it was and I am not going to start now, and Ottavia Reek is not the villain and she has four idle elms and a bad winter and she told me in a yard this morning that she would put the price up in the fifth month and that would be honest of her.**
>
> **Three. Adrian Vale has spent fifty-nine days making sure that a thing that goes wrong in this city has no person in it, and that is the correct project, and it is the only project I have seen anybody in this world undertake at all, and I want it recorded that I have said that in a yard and not in a room.**
>
> **Four. And you cannot run a city in which nothing that goes wrong has a person in it, because a city in which nothing that goes wrong has a person in it is a city in which nothing that goes wrong can be *fixed*, and a fix needs a hand, and a hand needs to be paid, and a person who is paid comes from a list.**
>
> **Five. And I have watched the list be the thing this city is most frightened of for six weeks and I have watched nine people in about nine rooms say the word *list* as though it were a disease, and it is not a disease. It is a thing a person is written on so that a person can be found, and this city has a woman of seventy who is written on nothing and a man of nineteen who is written on two rolls in one month and a docker of fifty-eight who is on nine of them and cannot read.**
>
> **Six. And every one of those is a good argument about a *person*. Not one of them is an argument about a *guild*, and a guild is not a person, and a guild can be refused, and a person can be refused, and the difference is that a guild does not go home and get old and want a different winter.**
>
> **Seven. And the Crown's third instrument is the shape every one of us is frightened of, and the Crown's third instrument holds a *containment* in the hands of one named person, and I argued for it in nine sentences in this yard and I was right in eight, and the eighth was about a city being able to be sent for, and I still think the eighth is the best argument anybody has made in this city in fifty-nine days.**
>
> **Eight. And a guild that holds the lock, the roll, the notary and the gauge, and that can be asked, and that has a book, is a durable answer. It is not the answer the licence has. It is the answer that would still be there in a hundred years and it is the answer I have been trying to sell since the third week and I have been selling it badly and on purpose.**
>
> **Nine. And here is the one that is not about Aurel and it has been sitting in me since the forty-sixth day and Adrian has been waiting three weeks for it.**

And the yard went quiet in the way that about two hundred and forty people go quiet, and the ninth sentence is in the book in Jonah Pike's own words and it is forty-one words long:

"**You have spent fifty-nine days making sure that none of them has a job. Not a trade, not a wage, not a place where a person is expected on an ordinary morning. You have written the page yourself. It is the longest page in this city and it is a list of things they may not have, and in it there is not one thing they may do, and in March one of them is going to be a person with no work in a city with no money, and you have made that a term, and a term is the most durable kind of promise there is, and I have watched you refuse four things that would have been seats and I have never once heard you offer one of them a day.**"

---

**And the man from another world had a tenth sentence and it was ready and it was about a Crown advocate not standing in a gateway in the rain, and he said, out loud, in about two hundred and forty people, that it was the wrong one, and that is the whole of what happened in that yard.**

"It is ready," he said, in English, and then in the local language, and the woman with the stall by the wharf translated it. "**I have had it ready for three weeks. It is about a man who is expensive and is not on this side of the water, and it was true in the sixth week of the new month and it is still true, and it is the wrong sentence and I have been carrying it around waiting for a man to need a door to be shut and he did not need one, he needed me to be wrong.**

"You said I have written the page myself. **I have. I asked for it. I asked for the bar, I asked for the list of what they do not have to be longer than the list of what they have, and I asked for it to be a term and not a favour, and I did all of that because I was frightened of a Crown and because I was right to be, and I have not once in fifty-nine days asked a single one of them what they would like to be able to do.**

"Nine sentences, Jonah Pike, and you are right in eight of them and the eighth is the best argument anybody has made in this city in fifty-nine days, and the ninth is better, **and I am not going to answer the ninth with the tenth because the tenth is about a gateway and the ninth is about March.**"

---

**And the day's business went on, and about nine people in that yard stayed, and the yard emptied by the second hour of the afternoon, and one woman of twenty-four was in a room over the market at about the fourth hour writing something that she did not show to anybody for eleven days.**

And the day book for the thirty-fourth day of the new month has this in it, in a narrow column, in a girl's hand of seventeen, and the width of the column is entered as a fact for the second time in that year, and she has entered that she did not know she was writing a list and that a list of names came out of the end of her hand because she had copied nine names off a page in a notebook that a man from another world had shown her in a yard:

> **The nine who would be angry: Renna Ord. Nyla Ferrow. Wenna Cray. Tamsin Quill. Elsa Skell. Hesper Saye. Sera Vail. Mina Okoye. Ivenn Marrow.**

And under it, in a different hand and a different ink, because the man from another world took the pen off her about the seventh hour of the evening and wrote four lines underneath and read them back to her before she let them stand:

> **These are not one person. A man who is a reason can be a reason to nine people who do not know each other, and the nine do not agree about him, and two of the nine would take him back tomorrow, and one of the nine has never been asked.**
>
> **And the two people in this city who are on no other list are on this one, and they got on it by being in a room, and the room did not decide to put them there.**
>
> **Kirsty Lund, sixteen, of the second list, who has a surname that was taken in full across two lines of a narrow column by a clerk of seventeen and who said *somebody took my surname and wrote the width of the column down as a fact*, and who is not on the roll of strangers because she was entered on the second list and the second list has not gone in a wooden case since the third week of the new month.**
>
> **Odile Ferrand, thirty-eight, of the second list, whose surname is on two lines of a narrow column twice, and who said on the twenty-sixth day of the new month that a review with no questions on it is a queue, and who is not on the roll of strangers for the same reason and has not been asked about either list.**
>
> **Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the thirty-fourth day of this new month: that a list of who would be angry has two people in it who are on no other list, and that both of them got in by being in a room, and that neither of them was asked, and that a man who was asked for a sentence about the ninth of nine sentences put them in by writing a name down while a girl of seventeen was copying nine names out of a notebook.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the two hundred steps, with the lamp on the third step of the lower gallery lit and nobody having gone down to it, a woman of twenty-nine took a notebook out of a man's hand and did not give it back.**

She is an anchorwright. She holds a register and not a licence. She was at the anchor side of the second bay for about eleven minutes of decided work on the forty-ninth day of this flood and has entered, in her own words, that she did not feel a working: *a thing with four of the four things and no man in it is not a working, it is forty men doing a Tuesday's job on a named tide, and it closed a join because a Tuesday's job closed it, and I did not do anything.*

She has corrected him in public four times in twenty-nine days, on *chair*, on *cohort*, on *charter*, and once by a procedure rather than by a voice, and she has declined twice to find his sentence for him, and the reason she gave is in the book in her own words: *a sentence in a room is not a wall.*

"Nine sentences," she said. "And the ninth one is correct and you know it is correct, and you are about to do the thing you did on the seventeenth day of the new month, which is to stand still."

"I have not said anything for about two hours."

"You are going to say *I have no answer*, and you do not have one, and I have watched you say it three times in this city and every single time it has been the end of a conversation instead of the middle of one, **and it is a door you have learned to shut with, and I am not going to let you shut it with it tonight.**"

"You are going to tell me what to write."

"**No.** I have declined twice to find your sentence and I am not going to stop now that it is nearly useful to you. What I am going to tell you is what the ninth sentence is, and then I am going to hand you the notebook back, and then you are going to have to do it."

"The ninth sentence is that I have written a page of things they may not have and not one thing they may do," said Adrian Vale.

"That is what it *is*. It is not what it *is about*." She turned round on the steps so that she was above him, which she has never done. "**The ninth sentence is not about the seventeen. It is about you. It is the first sentence that has been said about you in fifty-nine days that is not about what you are for this city, and it is about what you have written, and you have written about six pages in this city and every single one of them says what a person may not do.**

"**You have not written down once what a person may do. Not one time. In fifty-nine days. And that is not modesty and it is not humility and it is not the licence. It is that you have been frightened of a Crown for six weeks and being frightened of a Crown is a thing you know how to do, and it is the only thing you know how to do, and I have been watching you do it in about nine rooms and I did not say anything because I thought you would find it.**

"You will not find it. **So I am going to say one sentence and I am going to say it once and you are going to write it down and I am not going to say it again.**"

And she said it, and it is nineteen words, and he wrote it down in the notebook with a piece of chalk because there was nothing else to write with, on the second step of a stair above a lock, and he did not show it to anybody for the rest of the day:

"**Go and ask the city what a person with no work is for, and take the answer, and do not offer anybody a seat.**"

And then she gave the notebook back and said: "**And I want it entered that I said it and not that I found it, and that I have been asked twice this month by two different people to find your sentence and I have declined twice and I have now given you one and it is the same work and it is not the same favour.**"

And about nine people were on those two hundred steps and about nine of them heard it, and it is entered that about nine heard it and that there has not been a ninth member of that nine for some time, and that a man from another world counts to nine and that a mason of thirty-four years has left it as nine because it was easier.

And at the bottom of them, the dressed join in the gate of the Lock of Salt is eight and seven-eighths of an inch and it is dressed to a line and it does not narrow; it gets dressed. It has never said a word. The rail above it is condemned and the four will go down it in the wrong weather tomorrow with a gauge, and a city of about eleven hundred people has a licence that has opened twice, a form with five items, a rail that is going, a debt with no figure, a fee in silver it does not have, a workway with no office, a document with nine weaknesses, and a list of nine names that two people got into by being in a room.

And a man of nineteen with a shoulder that is out wrote nothing at all in an empty box in a tide table and gave the chalk back.
