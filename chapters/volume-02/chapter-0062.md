# Chapter 0062 — The List That Did Not Go

On the thirty-seventh day of the new month, which was the sixty-second day of the flood and a Sunday, a man of thirty-two went to the rope store above the chandlery on the upper road and asked eleven people a question he had no standing to ask, and got the right answer out of the worst possible person to ask it, which is the shape of most of what this city does to him.

The rope store is three rooms and a drying loft and it is dry, and eleven people live in it, and there is a list of what is owed for it on the wall and a key and nothing else. The condition on the Salt Weavers' chapel key — take the plaster off the north wall at the end of it — lapsed on the thirty-ninth day of the last month, and Sera Vail entered the lapse with her reason, and the reason was: a city that conditions a dry room for eleven people on fourteen days of work is a city that counts, and this city has been counting wrong for a month.

The list of what is owed for the room is a list of *things.* Nine of the lines are for the room. Two are for a woman of twenty-four who is owed a page, and one of them is a woman of twenty-nine who is owed a chair that has not been made.

**The list of what is owed is not a list of who lives here.** It is on the wall and anybody can read it and the last line of it, added on the thirty-first day of the new month, is not a person's name either. It is a measurement.

> **A column four lines high. On the thirty-ninth day of the flood, a clerk of the Concord took a surname in full across two lines of it and the clerk was seventeen and has entered the width of the column as a fact for the fourth time in that year.**

---

**The question Adrian Vale asked was not a good question and he entered that himself before anybody answered it.**

He said: "When did the second list last go into a case."

He said it in English first, to about nine people, and then in the local language, because four of the nine understand the local language better than they understand him, and one of them — a man of forty-four from Leeds with a hand that will not close — has never once been asked anything in this city and had not been asked this either.

The answer came from a woman of twenty-nine named Nadia Brandt, in about nine seconds, and it was wrong, and then it was right, and the wrong one is entered because she gave it first and nobody is to have heard only the right one.

"The carrier came," she said, "on the third week of the new month. She came to the Concord counter and she asked for the case and it was not ready, and she waited about an hour and she went away, and she has not come back, and she is a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail and she goes up the river on the fourth and the nineteenth."

"It was not ready," Adrian said.

"The case was not ready," Nadia Brandt said. "**Which is the same as the case was empty. And a case that is empty is a case nobody can charge to carry, and a carrier is paid by the person whose description is in it.**"

**And then everybody in that room was quiet for about four seconds and one of the reasons was that the woman who said it had said it in the wrong direction, on purpose, twice.**

---

**Here is the machine, and it has no villain in it, and it is a box.**

The roll of strangers is the Lantern Concord's quarterly description of everybody in Saltwake who is not from it. It goes to Aurel four times a year, in a wooden case, on a river boat, carried by a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail. Peth Lammay is that carrier. She is twenty-four and she has run the upper river and the Reach for eleven years and she lives over a boat-yard at Long Water, and she asked the question nobody in the hall asked on the twenty-sixth day of the new month and it was: *a letter is a thing that travels along a road.*

The case is made of the roll. The roll is made by the Concord. **The roll is written by hand, and the hand that writes it is Sera Vail's, and the writing of it is hers and not the Concord's and not the Crown's, and that has been in the book since the thirtieth day of the new month.**

The eleven of the second list were entered in the Concord's book on the twenty-sixth day of the new month, separately and separately witnessed, in the words used for the six, with a refusal clause and an express bar on seven particular words. They were entered **in a book in this city.** Two of them are not on the roll of strangers at all, and one of those two is a girl of sixteen and the other is a woman of thirty-eight, and neither of them has been asked about it.

So on the third week of the new month the carrier came, and there was no roll to put in the case, because eleven names of people who cannot go home had not been written onto the roll that goes to a building nine hundred miles away, and the reason they had not been written is in the book in the notary's own hand and it is nine lines long and it is the reason the case has not gone since:

> **Entered on the twenty-ninth day of the new month by the notary, on her own motion, and not asked for by any person.**
>
> **That the eleven of the second list are not entered on the roll of strangers for the coming quarter, and that this is her decision and not the Concord's, and that she has had two days to think of a reason and has one that is true and one that is convenient and they are not the same one.**
>
> **The true one is that a description of a person is the only thing in this world that obliges anybody to answer for them, and the roll obliges the Registry to keep a copy and answer for a person described, and no name in this city has come out of one of those cases in nine hundred years, and it should be written down that that is the only protection anybody has ever been offered by that office.**
>
> **The convenient one is that eleven people who have asked to go home are eleven people an office may act on.**
>
> **She did not send it. She is entering that she did not send it. She is a notary and not a person who is frightened of offices, and she has entered that she was, for two days, both of those things at once, and that a person who is frightened of an office and a person who has a reason are the same person on a bad week, and that the only difference between them is which one gets written down.**

**Nobody ordered the case not to go. Nobody conspired. A woman with a chain and a bad week did not fill in a column, and eleven days later a licensed carrier came to a counter and waited about an hour and went away, and a wooden case is a box and a box does not hold anybody who is not put in it.**

---

**And the man from another world went down to the Concord counter at the salt wharf on the afternoon of the thirty-seventh day and offered to carry the case himself, in a boat he would pay for, and was refused in about nine seconds, and the refusal was correct in all three of its parts.**

He is on the roll of strangers. He has been on it since the second day of this flood. His entry says **Unbound**, in his own words, with two dates on it, and neither of those dates is the sixteenth day of the new month, and the reason for that is a woman of twenty-four and twenty-five words.

A licensed carrier of Crown's second-table mail is a person the Crown has given a thing to. A man who is not one cannot be given it, and cannot take it, and cannot be sent anywhere with it.

"I am not asking for the licence," Adrian said. "I am asking to be the boat."

"Then you are asking to be the case," said Peth Lammay, who was at the counter and who had come back from a run up the Reach and had not gone home, because it is Sunday and the office is shut and she is a person who sits in rooms. "**And I have been sitting in this building for two days waiting for somebody to come and tell me one thing, and nobody has, and I am going to ask it out loud because there is no person in this city who can stop me and I have been carrying second-table mail for eleven years and I know exactly what a thing that is not asked for costs.**

"**Does anybody in this city want that case to go.**"

The room was the counter room and there were four people in it and a lamp on it and a ledger open on it and the ledger was the roll of strangers, open at the page for the coming quarter, with eleven lines ruled and empty.

Nobody answered her for about four seconds.

"A man from another world wants it to go," Adrian said, "because I think it is the honest thing and because a description obliges somebody to answer for a person and that is the only protection anybody in this city has ever been offered by that office."

"That is not what I asked," said Peth Lammay. "**I asked whether anybody in this city wants it to go.**"

**It is entered that nobody answered her, and it is entered that she asked a second time at about the sixth hour of the evening and that nobody answered her then either, and that she said the sentence that closes it and it is thirteen words and it is hers and it has been in the book ever since.**

> **I will bring a case whatever is in it. Bring me a case.**

And then she added, before she left, in about thirty-nine words and not looking at the ledger: "**And I would rather bring a case with eleven names in it than a case with nothing, and I do not know why, and I have been a carrier for eleven years and I have never once said that.**"

**And that is the ninth sentence anybody in this book has said about a wooden case, and only two of the nine were said by a person who is paid to carry one.**

---

**And the two things that are true about it are opposite, and both of them are true, and a person cannot have one without the other.**

Renna Ord said the first half of it on the thirtieth day of the new month in a yard in about two hundred and forty people's hearing, and it is in the book in her words: **a description is a removal with a column for where the person is.** The last column is the widest one and it is always blank, and the office is nine hundred miles away and it has never once sent anybody to fill it in.

Sera Vail said the second half of it in a room on the thirty-first day of the new month, in eighteen words — *the column is for the office, the office is nine hundred miles away, it has never sent anybody* — and Priya Shah asked the four questions and Vail answered one of them.

And Nadia Brandt of the second list, twenty-nine years old, worked the whole of it out in about an hour, sitting on a salt bin in a drying loft with a slate on her knees, and she did not tell anybody for two days.

**She told a woman of twenty-four and not the man.**

That is the fact on the day book for the thirty-seventh day of the new month and it is entered with the reason attached, and the reason was asked for by the girl of seventeen and given by Nadia Brandt in nine words, and the nine are in the book and they are the only sentence in it about a person's motive that is a sentence about a motive and not a sentence about a person:

> **I do not yet know whether I am complaining.**

Priya Shah, twenty-four, was told it in the loft at about the seventh hour of the evening on the thirty-seventh day of the new month, alone, and Adrian Vale was not in the room and has entered that he was told an hour later, by the notary, in eight words — *the case did not go and nobody conspired* — and that the eight were about the case and not about the woman, and that he wrote them down.

---

**And the two people who are on no roll at all are named, and neither has been asked, and the asking is a form with a date on it, and about nine people signed it and about nine people did not.**

Kirsty Lund, sixteen, of the second list, who came out of a chapel floor four feet beyond the threshold slab on the twenty-fifth day of this flood and was carried out of a yard on the back of a barrow under a blanket and was asleep on it, and who read a line of Adrian's own notebook back to him in a room, and who said *I know. It is what you wrote.*

Odile Ferrand, thirty-eight, of the second list, whose surname is on two lines of a narrow column twice because there was not room for it on one, and who named the shape of a review with no questions on it in ten words on the twenty-sixth day of the new month: **a review with no questions on it is a queue.**

Neither is on the roll of strangers. Neither is on the second list as it goes in a case, because the second list does not go in a case. Neither is on the honest useless form, because that form has a column headed *name of the person who went* and neither of them has been anywhere.

**They are on the two beds, and on the two beds they are on a page with a date on it, and that is the whole of the description either of them has in this world.**

The asking is a form. It is four lines high and it says: *that a person on no roll at all is asked, in daylight, in a room, whether they wish to be described; that the answer may be no; that a no is entered and is not asked about again for a year; and that a yes is entered and the person may take the yes back on a date named on the same line.*

Adrian Vale wrote it. It took him about eleven minutes and it is the first thing he has written in sixty-two days that a person of this city could refuse, and it is not entered as an instrument, and the girl of seventeen put it in the book anyway and told him that was not hers to decide.

**About nine people signed it and about nine people did not.**

The nine who signed said one thing between them and the thing was: *we would rather be asked, and we have not been asked in a long time, and a yes on a form is not a protection and it is the first thing anybody has offered me that I can refuse.*

The nine who did not signed nothing, and four of them said why and the reasons are entered and none of the four reasons is that they are frightened.

One of the four said: a form with a date on it is still a form, and a form is a page, and a page can be carried to a building nine hundred miles away in a wooden case, and **a person who has said no to a form is a person who has been asked and has said no, and is now on a page.**

A man of thirty-one with a hand that does not close said a different one, and it is nineteen words and it is his: "**I have answered two forms about my hand and I would like a third about something I can hold.**"

A docker of fifty-eight said the shortest of the four and it is eight words and she said it to the girl of seventeen and not to the man who wrote the thing: "**Nobody has asked me either. Write mine down.**" And it was written down, and she is the ninth person to have been asked something in this city in sixty-two days, and the ninth to have answered.

And the fourth of the four said nothing at all, and it is entered that he said nothing and that the girl of seventeen entered that he said nothing, **and that a person who says nothing to a form is a person who has been asked, and that this city spent nine days learning that a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused.**

---

**And the roll of strangers is entered again on the thirty-seventh day of the new month, and it goes into the book with a hole in it, and a clerk at Aurel is going to have a page with a hole in it.**

Sera Vail entered the roll on the thirty-seventh day with the true count and with a page, and the page is not the roll and it is not in the case, and it is in the book in this city, and it reads:

> **The thirty-seventh day of the new month. The roll of strangers for the coming quarter is entered above.**
>
> **It is entered that eleven persons of the second list are named in this city and are not in the case, and that the case has not gone since the third week of the new month, and that the reason is entered nine lines above and that the reason is a decision and not an oversight.**
>
> **The page the office will hold will show a number that is smaller by eleven than the number of people in this city who are not from it. It is entered that the office is not being lied to. It is being given a page.**
>
> **Nothing in this city can tell that office the difference between a page and a hole, and this is the first time in sixty-two days that this city has entered a thing whose whole work is to be indistinguishable from a mistake, and it is not a lie, and it is not a summary, and no column has been rounded.**

The girl of seventeen wrote the sentence that goes under it, and it is twenty words, and it is her own, and nobody in the room offered it to her and she asked for the line herself:

> **A hole in a page is not a smaller number, it is a place where a number used to be.**

And Ottoline Vask entered the finding, and it is twenty-five words and it is the only finding in the history of this book with no number in it, and it is hers:

> **This city has spent sixty-one days protecting eleven people by describing them. The describing was the whole of the protection. There is no second thing.**

**Nothing was gained. The case has not gone. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date and it is the tenth time it has been entered and nobody has worked out what it is about. The trial licence is twelve days old. The route has opened twice in sixty-two days and the second list has asked to go home eleven times in one sentence each and there is no receiving community on the far side of a wall and no instrument in this world can make one from this side. A room with no window and no door anybody has found was knocked on three times on the night of the twenty-ninth day of the new month and four times on the night of the thirty-fifth, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody has to open anything for that to happen, and nobody has asked.**
