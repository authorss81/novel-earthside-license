# Chapter 0096 — Two Houses, and a Refusal in Public

On the sixth day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-sixth day of the flood and a Saturday, a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling was used nine times in a house on the upper road by two people who are not the man from another world and are not anybody's, a man of about twenty-six who had walked eleven miles was turned away at the door of the first Wayhouse in front of about nine people for a reason that was written on a page, and nobody overrode it, and on the seventh day of the month after this one he came back and read his own refusal out in his own words, and it is entered that he came back to read it and not to complain about it.

**The licence is forty-six days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the thirty-seventh time. The black line in his right palm is ninety-five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The first Wayhouse has a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, paid for by a levy on the road's trade at a rate the traders on that road agreed among themselves, kept by a household, open to a list of nobody, closable by its own household for its own reasons on a date written on the page. It is on the upper road, it is not a Crown instrument, it is not a page anybody may carry to a building nine hundred miles away, and a Wayhouse in one village is not a Wayhouse in another village and does not answer for one.

It nearly died in about nine minutes on the thirty-ninth day of the new month, which was the sixty-fourth day of the flood, because four household names went on a board in chalk in a yard behind a chandlery, and the four names came off the board and went into a book, and a keeper of about fifty read out what was on the board before she washed it.

Her name is Ilsa Roke and she is fifty-one and she is Hettis Roke's aunt by marriage and she has kept a house on the upper road for nineteen years and she is not on any roll of anything, and she is the keeper of the first Wayhouse, and she did it because it is what you do.

**And the sixth thing, which is the only reason it is still there in fifty-seven days, is that nobody may be turned away for asking what it is. A person may ask any of the six things at the door, on the step, in any weather, and be answered there, and being answered is not agreeing to anything, and nothing on the page obliges a person to sleep in it, or to pay, or to be described, or to be on anything.**

---

**And the bed was used nine times in a fortnight and four of those nine were mixed, and a mixed use is not a special thing, and it is entered that nobody in about nine people who were in the house that afternoon had ever seen a bed used by a person from this world and a person from another world on the same night, and that by the sixth day of the month after this one it had happened four times and had not been written about anywhere except in a line in a book kept by a woman of fifty-one.**

The two of them on the third of those nights were a docker of about forty from the Reach road and a woman of about thirty-one of the second group who had been in this world since the twenty-fifth day of this flood, and neither of them paid, because the price is not money and the price is a quarter of a pound of salt and an hour of washing, and the salt came out of the levy and the washing came out of the hour.

"The page has to say what it costs and who pays it," said the girl of seventeen, in about nine seconds, standing in a kitchen with her slate, "**and it says what it costs and it says the levy pays it, and a levy is not a person, and we have had four conversations about that in ninety-six days and I would like somebody to say the sentence out loud.**"

"A levy is not a person and it is not anybody's servant and it is not going home," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and the bed is not a wage, and the hour of washing is not a wage, and nobody in this house is on a roll of anything, including the woman who pays the levy on what the other four pull.**"

"That is the woman who keeps a stall by the wharf," said the girl of seventeen.

"That is the woman who keeps a stall by the wharf," said the man of about forty-four, "**and she is not one of the four names on the levy and she has never been on anything, and she pays it, and if this house is ever going to be a thing that can be angry at then that is going to be the first thing anybody can be angry at, and I would like it entered that I said that about a house in a yard in daylight and was not asked to.**"

---

**And then a man from another world was in the room and everybody knew it, and about nine people looked at him, and he said nothing, and it took about forty minutes for the room to stop looking, and the girl of seventeen entered it in four lines and the four lines are on the page.**

> **A man of thirty-two was in the room of the first Wayhouse for forty minutes on the sixth day of the month after this one.**
>
> **Nobody asked him anything.**
>
> **He said four words and none of them were about the house.**
>
> **Nobody has thanked him for that and nobody should.**

"Four words," said a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read. "**What were they.**"

"They were *that is right*," said the girl of seventeen, "**and he said it about the washing and not about the bed, and I have written down which, because somebody in about four months is going to want to know whether this house works because of him.**"

"It does not work because of him," said Ilsa Roke, from the step, where she had been standing the whole of it. "**It works because four households agreed a rate on the road and I have kept a house for nineteen years. It is not his and the price is not his and the room is not in it, and I would like that written at the top of the page and not at the bottom.**"

---

**And then, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, a man of about twenty-six came up the upper road and asked at the step whether anybody travelling could have a bed, and he had walked eleven miles, and he had a name and he is not one of the seventeen, and about nine people were in that house and in that yard.**

The keeper answered him at the step, in the local language, in about nine seconds, and she gave him all six things and she gave them without being asked and she did not hurry, and then she told him the one thing that is on the page, and it is the fourth thing.

"I have a man in the back room who is dying," said Ilsa Roke. "**And I am the only person in this house who can sit with him, and I have written it on the page and I have written the date on it, and it is the sixteenth day of the month after this one and it is written there for the sixteenth and not for this afternoon, and I am closing it for today and I am not going to tell you why twice, because a keeper who is asked to explain a refusal has already lost the fourth thing, and I have been married for twenty-two years and I know what I am looking at.**"

The man of about twenty-six stood in the road. **About nine people in that yard watched it, and it is entered that none of them said anything, and that about four of them started to, and that a woman of twenty-nine of the second list said *wait* out loud to about four people at once and stopped it, and that she entered afterwards that she was wrong to have said it and that she was right to have said it, and that both of those are hers.**

**The refusal stood. The man of about twenty-six did not argue and did not ask again and did not go to four other doors in that yard, and he stood in the road for about nine minutes and then he went down the road, and about nine people watched a rule be obeyed against a person who was standing in front of them, and it is entered that this is the first time in ninety-six days that anybody in this city has watched that happen, and that about nine people understood what it was and about two hundred and thirty would not have.**

"He is not angry," said a woman of twenty-four.

"He is not angry because she gave him the reason," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**The reason is on a page and the date is on a page and the page says the household may close it for its own reasons, and it is not asked why, and it is not asked a second time, and the fourth of the six things is the only reason that refusal is worth anything. If she had said nothing she would have been a harder woman and the refusal would have been worth less.**"

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening the page was nailed to the inside of the door frame with the date on it and the reason on it, in the keeper's own hand, and it is entered that nobody in about nine people who saw it said that a reason on a page is a description.**

It is a description. **And it is entered that the person on the page is a man of about twenty-six who is not on any roll, that the reason is about a household and not about him, that his name is not written, and that the reason does not describe him at all, and that a notary who was told about this said that it is the first description in ninety-six days that is not a removal, and that she is not going to be asked to prove it and does not intend to try.**

---

**And on the seventh day of the month after this one, which is the Sunday, in the morning, he came back, and he came back to read it, and it is entered that he said so in about nine seconds before anybody had asked him anything, and nobody had asked him anything.**

"I have come to read it," he said, "**and not to complain about it, and I want the second half of that entered in my own words, because the first half is a thing anybody would say and the second half is the thing I mean.**"

**And then he read his own refusal out, in his own words, in the road, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and it is in the book exactly as he said it and nobody improved on it.**

"**On the sixth day of the month after this one I asked at the door of this house for a bed and a bowl for one night, and I was told at the step that the house was closed for that day, and the reason given was that there is a man in the back room who is dying and the woman of this house is the only one who can sit with him. I was not asked why, and I did not ask why, and I have not been told anything since. I walked eleven miles that day and I have walked twenty-two in two days. I am not angry and I want it entered that I am not angry, because there is a page on the door and it says the household may close it for its own reasons, and it does, and I asked at the step and I was answered at the step, and that is the whole of what happened. I want it also entered that if it had been a different reason I would be angry and that I do not know yet whether I will be in a different place in a month and I have not decided.**"

Nobody in that road said anything for about nineteen seconds, and then the keeper came out and read her own page out in her own words, and it took about nine seconds, and it says what it says.

**And it is entered that a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling is now kept in a house on the upper road by a household that is not anybody's servant, at a price of a quarter of a pound of salt and an hour of washing, out of a levy the traders agreed among themselves, open to a list of nobody, closable by its own household for its own reasons with a date on the page; that a refusal of it was exercised in daylight in front of about nine people and was honoured by nobody overriding it; that the price is on a page and the page says what it costs and who pays it; that the person refused came back the next morning to read it and not to complain about it; and that none of it is the man from another world and the room he was in did not include him.**

**And it is entered that a person with no work in this world is still a person with no work in this world, and that a bed and a bowl fixes that for one night and for one night only, and that nobody in about nine people in that road pretended otherwise, and that the Narrow Mark was not opened, and that nobody crossed, and that the page in the stone at Four Elms has twelve names on it and the door of a Wayhouse has none.**
