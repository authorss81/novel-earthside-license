# Chapter 0100 — Two Houses and a Road

On the tenth day of the month after this one, which was the hundredth day of the flood and a Wednesday, a boundary shrine on a fork at Four Elms was given back to the household that keeps it, with a third page nailed to the inside of the stone, and a question that this city owes to a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house eleven miles away was entered on that page as unasked, on purpose, with a date on it, and nobody in about nine people in that room argued for asking her, and at the top of a nine-mile road a woman of about sixty-one who cannot read read nineteen shapes aloud to a docker who had a slate, and on a river wall a man from another world read a number off a wall four feet back from it and said it out loud, and it was a number and not a sentence about anybody.

**The licence is fifty days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The cost column has eleven entries in one month and no figure on the end of any of them and there is no twelfth. The measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left. The fee is owing with no date for the forty-first time. The black line in his right palm is ninety-nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**A shrine is a record, and a record that cannot be removed, and a page can be put inside one, and a boundary stone on the north side of a fork at Four Elms has been keeping a page for twenty days, counted from the fifty-fifth day of the new month, which was the eightieth day of the flood, and a second page for ten days, counted from the sixty-fifth day of that month, which was the ninetieth day of the flood.**

It is a stone with a cut in the flat of it, and the cut is where the water was before the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream at a public act with witnesses, and it has been saying so for a hundred and forty years, and it is not on paper. **Nothing may be worked at it. There is no pattern in it and nobody has ever worked at one. It is not an instrument and it is not going to be one.** A page with no price on it is a favour and a favour is a thing that stops, and the first page has carried a price since the day it went in, and the second page carries a quarter of a pound of salt a month cut out of the keeping household's own winter allotment and written under the list and not as another item on it, and the household refused a second price and gave the reason that a favour is a thing that stops, and the reason is written on the page so that in nine months somebody can check it.

**And the household that keeps it did not want her name on the first page and did not want her name on the second one, and she said no twice, and her reason has been on the page since the eightieth day of the flood and is not counted again here: *a name on a page is a thing that can be found, and I have buried two.***

---

**And on the hundredth day of the flood, about nine people went up nine miles in the wrong weather to give it back, and giving it back is not a thing anybody in this city has ever done, and there is no procedure for it, and the finding of the day is that a thing this city built and put in another household's stone ought to be handed back before somebody dies and takes the permission with her.**

"**It is not the Lock of Salt's,**" said a woman of about sixty-one. "**The Lock of Salt's is a building and it has a salt line and four chalk depths and a keeper. This is a stone on somebody's fork and the somebody has a name and the name is on no page of ours, and if the Lock of Salt's wants a shrine on the boundary then the Lock of Salt's can go and ask her, and I am not going to be the instrument that asks her, and I have said that out loud and about nine people heard it.**"

"That is not the reason," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then I will give you the reason,**" said Marda Rood, "**and it is this. Three pages in a stone is a shrine. One page in a stone is a wall somebody kept. Two is a mistake. I have had a grandmother who was burned out of a house and I have had two neighbours whose names went into a book, and a name in a book is a name that can be looked up, and a stone is the only place in four hundred miles where the person holding it is not anybody's servant, and a notary of fifty said that on the eightieth day of the flood in nine seconds and she was right and it has not improved with three pages on it.**"

"Then the third page comes out."

"**The third page goes in and the first two stay, and I will tell you why, and the why is the whole of this day. I am sixty-one. In about four years somebody in this village is going to have to keep this stone and it is going to be a stranger, and the stranger is not going to know whose fork it is or what the cut in the flat of it means, and there is no page anywhere in four hundred miles that says what it means except three pages in a stone that a household keeps. That is the whole argument for a page in a stone. It is not for us. It is for the stranger. And a stranger cannot be angry at a page and that is exactly why it works.**"

Nobody argued with her, and it is entered that about nine people in that room understood that she had just argued for the thing she had refused twice, and that she had done it about a person who does not exist yet, and that this is the fourth time in a hundred days that a woman in this city has refused something and then argued for it on behalf of somebody who was not in the room.

---

**And the page that went in on the hundredth day of the flood carries a date, a price and a right of refusal, and it is the second Wayhouse, and it is not the Lock of Salt's, and nobody in about nine people in that room asked a man from another world to write it and he did not write it.**

> **The second Wayhouse, at the fork at Four Elms.**
>
> **A bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, paid out of the keeping household's winter allotment, at a rate of a quarter of a pound of salt and an hour of washing, and the salt and the washing are counted and are not money.**
>
> **The keeping household is a household and not a person and is on no roll and does not go to Aurel in a wooden case.**
>
> **The keeping household may close it, for its own reasons, on a date written on this page. It is not asked why. It is not asked a second time.**
>
> **It is open to a list of nobody.**
>
> **This page may be taken down by the keeping household on a date. It is not asked why twice.**

"**The price is the same as the first house,**" said a keeper of about fifty-one who had kept a house on the upper road for nineteen years, "**and it is not a page anybody may carry to a building nine hundred miles away, and a Wayhouse in one village is not a Wayhouse in another village and does not answer for one, and I have walked nine miles to say that and I would like it entered that I walked nine miles and did not get paid for the walking and am not on a roll and do not expect to be.**"

"It is a refusal," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and it is the first refusal anybody in this city has put into a document on purpose, and the Lock of Salt's has had a rule about a man on a board for eighty days and has never once had a rule about itself, and I would like somebody to notice the date.**"

---

**And then the third thing went on the page, and it is not a debt, and it is the reason the page was worth a nine-mile walk.**

> **To a woman of forty-eight in the fourth house at Nine Elms: one question, asked once, in daylight, in a room, whether she will carry any part of the standing charge. The answer may be no. It has not been asked.**
>
> **It has not been asked because on the sixth day of the month after this one this city chose not to ask it.**
>
> **That is a decision and not an oversight. It is entered with a date so that in four months nobody can say it was forgotten.**

Nobody in that room argued for asking her.

"It is entered that nobody argued for it," said the girl of seventeen, "**and I want to be exact about why, because in about four months about nine people are going to say this city decided not to fix something and did not forget it, and that is the entry from the fifty-eighth day of the new month, which was the eighty-third day of the flood, and the entry has been standing for seventeen days and this page is the second time anybody has put it in stone.**"

"You are not going to ask her because she does not know you exist," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and every version of this that is asked in the next four months is asked by a person or a page or an instrument, and all three of those tell her that a charge exists, and a person who has been doing a thing by herself every morning for forty-six days and has not been asked has not refused anything, and the moment somebody asks her she has a decision to make that she did not have at six in the morning, and we would be handing her a decision and calling it a courtesy.**"

"It should have been asked a month ago."

"**It should have been asked a month ago and it was not and that is our debt and it is written on a stone and not on a form, and a form goes to a building nine hundred miles away.**"

---

**And about nine people were in the room who kept it, and they are the people who kept it, and that is the whole of the account: a keeper of about seventy with fifty-one years of a nail in an arch, a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing, a man of forty-four of the outer ward whose grandfather's hand is on a stone nine miles up the road, a woman of twenty-nine of the second list, a girl of seventeen, a woman of twenty-eight who is of the first group and has no work, a keeper of about fifty-one of the first house on the upper road, a man of sixty-one who keeps a book of this city's words and is owed a sentence from a man who read it, and a man of thirty-two from another world who is on no roster of anything and put none of this in writing.**

"**You did not write it,**" said the woman of about thirty-four.

"No."

"**Then you did nothing,**" she said, "**and I have been waiting a hundred days to say that to a man in a room and I have said it and I would like it entered that I said it and that it is the first thing I have said to him in a hundred days that was not an argument.**"

---

**And the road is nine miles, and it goes up from Saltwake past the salt works to the fork at Four Elms, and eleven households keep it, and they are not a council and there is no levy and there is no clerk, and the keeping is a day each in a bad week and a quarter of a day in a good one, and it is a promise and not a sum, and the eleven households are named on nothing.**

"**Eleven households keep that road,**" said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**and nobody has ever asked them to, and they have never once been given a page, and the road was not built by anybody in this city, it was walked by people coming down it, and it is the only thing in ninety-nine days that nobody in this room can be angry at and everybody would miss.**"

"And a man from another world has no road at all," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and that is the cost of the thing he did on the second day of the month after this one, and it is entered, and he is not going to be asked about it today, and nobody in this room is going to say the word loss to him.**"

"About eight weeks," said a man of forty-one in a very good coat, who had come up nine miles and had not offered a document and had not said one wrong thing in a hundred days, and who is not convicted of anything and is not going to be, and who is publicly useful and privately dangerous and is the reason this city has three questions it did not have at the fourteenth week of the flood.

"I have a room. **A room, in daylight, with a date on it in my own hand and no office behind it, and I promised it in about four months on the fifty-fourth day of the new month, which was the seventy-ninth day of the flood, and it is in the book, and it is about fourteen weeks away, and I said the name of a thing in a room on the first day of the month after this one and I entered that I cannot tell you afterwards whether I said it to help this city or to file it, and I have not been asked which and I am not going to be.**"

"And the letter," said a woman of fifty.

"**Sixty days,**" said Ivenn Marrow. "**Counted from the fortieth day of this flood, which was the day a channel was offered, and from nothing else, and I have not mentioned it in sixty days and I am not going to mention it at a funeral or a festival and there is not going to be one, and I would like the count entered by somebody who is not me.**"

It was entered. **And it is entered that the Bright Pilgrims question is one question sharper than it was on the twenty-fifth day of this flood, which was the day the licence was signed, and it is not answered: the names went to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year, one of eight is cut on a stone in a hand a Hearthguard was taught to read, seven have never been looked at, and nobody living can say where the rest went. The eighth column of a quarterly description is in a case on that river with a question in it and no name, and a clerk of that office may not ask a question and may not refuse one, and the hearing is about eight weeks off, and there is one hearing and one clock.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening, at the top of the upper road, in the wrong weather, a woman of about sixty-one who cannot read stood in front of a wall above a market and read nineteen shapes aloud to a docker of fifty-eight who has a slate, because anybody may pick the work up, and the work is not a post, and a person who picks it up is not repaid and is not on a roll and is not a holder of anything.**

Sabra Vint is sixty-one and has a house with a burnt lintel on this road and nine years of paper with nineteen shapes on it, and eleven of them are the same and eight are different, and she has written them down every year for nine years and has never once written down what she thinks they mean. The copying is finished: all nineteen are in four slates that four different people can read out loud, and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read has one of the four, and the meaning is still unwritten, and the date on a slate is the nineteenth day of the month after this one and it is the only thing on it, and nobody has written anything on that wall.

"She is not copying," said the girl of seventeen, to a man of thirty-two, on the road, in the wrong weather. "**She is reading. She has been reading them since about the third hour of the afternoon and the docker has been writing all nineteen down again from her mouth, and that is the eleventh time anybody has looked at this work and the eleventh time in nine years that it has been done by somebody who is not the person who started it, and I would like that entered in the day book and not on the wall.**"

"Then who is doing the nineteen stones."

"**She is. She is sixty-one and she is doing them and she has been saying for a hundred days that somebody has to be the one who writes down what they think they mean, and nobody has done it, and a docker is doing it tonight with a slate, and a docker is not a mason and is not repaid and is not on a roll, and a woman of sixty-one is standing in the cold reading them out loud so that the work can leave her hands before she stops.**"

And then a man of thirty-two went down two hundred steps of a river wall by himself, in the wrong weather, on a rail that is condemned, and nobody went with him and nobody had been asked to, and he stood four feet back from the wall of the second bay, which is where he has stood every time since the fourth day of this flood, and read the number out loud in an empty gallery.

**The number is eight and seven-eighths. It is the twenty-fourth reading and the twenty-third was the same, and there are two steps in the column in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and there is one ruled line left in it, and it is not filled, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken and was not taken on the first of March and was not taken on the ninth day of the month after this one and is not going to be taken in this volume of days.**

**The joined edge is dressed. There is no field in the air. The number is a number and not a sentence about anybody. The day book says what it cannot do, and it says it in four lines, and the four lines are as wide as the column they are written in, and not one word of them is a summary.**
