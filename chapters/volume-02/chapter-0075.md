# Chapter 0075 — What Are They Called: The Review

On the fiftieth day of the new month, which was the seventy-fifth day of the flood and a Saturday, a review was held in a converted salt store in daylight with about two hundred and forty people in it, against eleven questions and a second page and a slate, and the trial licence survived it, and the licence is twenty-five days old, and it is weaker at the end of the day than it was at the beginning of it, and nobody in that room lied about either of those things.

**A review is a queue with a date on it. This one had questions on it and it was a review, and the questions were written by the people already in the room, and about nine people in this city are not already in the room, and two of them are on no roll at all, and one of the two is holding the slate.**

---

**The four went down at about the seventh hour of the morning, in ice, in the wrong weather, and they came up at about the ninth hour and they did not disagree, and that is the first time in seventy-five days that the four of them have agreed about anything, and it is entered, and nobody celebrated it.**

The rail on the two hundred steps is condemned and is not mended and the timber is four weeks away at a price the Brass Measure has tied to the crew's list. They went down with a gauge in a tin. They read the dressed edge. Nyla Ferrow read the line. Peth Lomas read the number. Elsa Skell read the edge and came up from Corrow in the dark to do it. Renna Ord stood on it.

"I do not agree with myself about it," said Renna Ord, on the third step, with her boots off, "**and I want that written down in a different column from the one they used, because three of them are masons and I am not, and I have been wrong about a number in this city four times and I have been right about one.**"

"Then the four agree," said Nyla Ferrow, "**and one of us is standing on it and saying she does not.**"

"That is not disagreeing," said Peth Lomas. "**That is the same thing twice and it is now a different number.**"

**It is entered that the four of them did not disagree on the fiftieth day of the new month and that the reason is entered, and the reason is that a review of an instrument is not a working and there is nothing to disagree about. The twenty-fifth reading was not taken and is not taken and there is one ruled line left in the column and it was not filled.**

---

**The store held about two hundred and forty people at about the ninth hour of the morning and about the same number at the seventh hour of the evening, and both numbers are entered, and Tamsin Quill, twenty-nine, an anchorwright and the keeper of the public bridge register, said the thing she had said on a stair on the forty-fifth day of the new month, and she said it in this room instead of on a stair, and about nine people heard her say it twice in seventy-five days and about two hundred and thirty heard it once:**

"I said the rest of it on the forty-fifth day of the new month on a stair, and I am going to say it in this room instead, and the rest of it is that a review is a room with a page on the table, and a page on the table turns people into the answer to the question, and about two hundred and thirty of us are about to hear eleven questions and agree with most of them, **and that is not a failure of the questions and it is not a failure of us. It is the sound a room makes.**"

The slate came in at about the ninth hour, on the arm of a girl of seventeen, and she put it on a barrel and stood where she could see it and where nobody could stand in front of it.

The form on the nail was still on the nail. It was brought in as a second thing, and it is the second time in seventy-five days that form has left that arch, and it has four items and the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item added on the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue will go or say who is in a room. **It was on the barrel for eight hours and about nine people read it and two hundred and thirty did not know it was in the room.**

And the second page was on the barrel too, in nine hands, and the nine copies are the ones the woman of sixty-one who cannot read had written out with her own hand in nine days, and the notary asked for the copies on the forty-sixth day of the new month and has entered that she asked and why.

---

**The first of the eleven turned out to be a door, and the room found that out in about nine seconds, and it is the same eleven words that a woman of seventy read out of a yard five days ago at the ordinary volume in the open, and a room that has heard a question is not a room that has answered it, and about two hundred and thirty people in this room are about to find out the difference in public.**

> **Has the route been inspected before every opening, in whatever weather there was, and where the weather was the wrong weather, has that been entered as what it was.**

"There are two openings," said the girl of seventeen, in the day book, in a hand of thirty-two that is not a Veyran hand. "**The twenty-eighth day of this flood, in breach of the terms then in force, entered as a breach. The twenty-fifth day of the new month, Eli Mercer, nineteen, two witnesses neither of them of the crew, a fresh inspection signed the same morning, about eleven minutes.**"

"And the second one?" said Sera Vail.

"Lawful. The licence was signed and sealed that same morning and the count per opening is two and the witnesses are not of the crew and the return mark was separately established and separately costed and it belongs to him and nobody else."

**And about nine people in that room heard the difference between a lawful opening and a breach and two hundred and thirty heard two, and it is entered that the number of the eleven questions that are doors is now six and the number that are traps is four, and the notary who drafted both numbers on the forty-third day of the new month is not permitted to grade her own paper and said so in about four seconds.**

---

**The second was the notary's own, and she read it herself, and then she read her own column, and the room watched a woman of fifty open her own cost column in daylight in front of two hundred and forty people for the first time in twenty-six years.**

> **Is the cost of this instrument entered at the size it is now; and where the cost column stands as last entered, is the difference between that figure and the truth a gap, and is the gap entered, and has anybody been told.**

"The column is entered," said Sera Vail. "**At its size. Nine times in one month, the ninth being the mending of a rail, and it has no figure on the end of it and it is not going to get one, and March is ten days off, and a woman in Corrow has to decide in ten days how much of her life to put in the ground.**"

"And the gap?"

"**The gap is a season of a field four miles down the Reach road, and a quarter of a Reach, and the fourth line of a term sheet, and a woman's shoulder, and a rail, and eleven days of a fever-house, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge, which was returned to a private debtor on the thirty-sixth day of this flood by nine households who refused for the third time.**"

She put the page down on the barrel.

"**And the gap is a gap and not an error, and it is entered as a gap, and it has been told to about nine people in this city and to nobody else, and I am the reason it is not on a page going to a building nine hundred miles away, and I would like the room to notice that a notary has just said that in daylight and has not been asked.**"

---

**Nobody in about two hundred and forty people took advantage of the fact that the third of the eleven was a trap, and the notary who drafted it had told the whole yard on the forty-third day of the new month in about four sentences. The reason nobody took advantage of it is the fourth line of a term sheet, and it took nine days to write.**

> **What would have to be true for this instrument to be continued, and who would have to say so, and in what order, and on what date.**

They worked on it for about forty minutes and the answer turned out to be three sentences and a name.

"**It would have to be true that a village of nine households has not refused the standing charge, or has refused it and been paid for the refusing, and the answer is that the ninth household of Nine Elms refused for the third time on the forty-fourth day of the new month and the refusal was affordable, because a Crown factor of the reed levy used the Crown's own fifth clause correctly, and the credit was refused and the quarter is gone.**"

"**So it is not true,**" said Sera Vail, "**and the instrument is not continued, and the licence is twenty-five days old and it ends in a room full of people and I have just said so.**"

**And a woman of seventy read out a line from the second page, in nineteen lines, in nineteen hands, and it is the fourth line of the Reed Reach's term sheet and it took nine days to get through the meeting-ground on the twenty-third day of the new month, and it is Ilda Marsh's, and it is on the page in the plain words of a woman who has lived in it:**

> **And on a village's refusal the whole of the charge returns to the private debtor.**

**And Adrian Vale said, out loud, in a room, in daylight, with two hundred and forty people in it, that the whole of the charge is on him, and that it has been on him since the thirty-sixth day of this flood, and that the third question's answer is his own name, and that the date in it is the twentieth-fifth day of the new month, and that there is no other name available.**

"**Then the instrument is continued,**" said Sera Vail, "**because a term sheet is a term sheet and the fourth line has been used correctly and a village that cannot afford to be right is still right, and the instrument goes on in a form weaker than the one it went in with, and I am going to read the words that make it weaker, and I drafted the question and I am not allowed to grade my own paper and I would like that understood.**"

**The instrument is continued. It is not renewed. It is continued for the term that was on it, and it is continued with three things added and no figure and one refusal entered, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge remains on a private debtor who is on no roster of anything, and that is entered, and nobody in the room said the word *weak*, and the notary said it, and it is in the book in her own words: *I have written a worse instrument than the one I was asked to write and I have done it correctly, and both of those are the same fact, and I have now said that sentence twice in five weeks and I would like the room to notice it the second time and I do not think it will.***

---

**At about the fourth hour of the afternoon the second page was read, all nineteen of it, in nineteen hands, at the ordinary volume, and it was read in the wrong order on purpose, for the second time in seventy-five days, by a keeper of about seventy who owns the nail.**

She read the reasons before the questions. She had done that once before, on the forty-third day of the new month, in that yard, and she had given her reason then in five words and it is on the page: *they will remember the not-asking.*

And the room heard about nine hundred words of reasons for not asking a city has not asked, and the number of people in the room who understood that the not-asking was the instrument is entered as about nine, and the number who heard nineteen lines of a document being read out in the weather is about two hundred and thirty, and neither number is a count of anything except what a person thought, and the notary entered both.

**And at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, the eleventh question was called, and the keeper said that the eleventh was on the back of the slate, and turned it over, and there were four words on the back of the slate in a woman's own hand in chalk a week old, and the room read them, and two hundred and forty people said them out loud after her, because that is what you do with four words in a yard.**

> **What are they called.**

**Nobody in that room said what the answer was. Nobody had to. It was on the front of the slate, in the same chalk, in a woman's own hand, under the eleven questions, and it had been on the front of that slate in a yard for three days, and Nyla Ferrow had said so out loud on the forty-seventh day of the new month in front of about two hundred and forty people and about nine of them had understood her and about two hundred and thirty had not, and the reason nobody had turned the slate over before the fifth hour of the afternoon is that the eleventh question had never needed to be looked at, because the room knew the four words on it and the four words were the question and the question was a good question and a good question is the one thing in this book nobody in a yard has ever wanted turned over.**

**And Renna Ord said the answer, and she was not asked, and it is in the book that she was not asked.**

> **Nobody has asked them.**

**Four words. It is not a noun. It is entered, in a column, on the same page as the question, and not on a separate sheet, and the notary insisted on the same page in about nine seconds and the reason she gave is that a question and its answer on two pages is a form and a question and its answer on one page is a thing somebody can be angry at.**

Then it was read back twice, at the ordinary volume, in a room with about two hundred and forty people in it, and Renna Ord read it back both times in the local language, and the girl of seventeen read it back in English both times, and the second reading is in the book in two languages with the two renderings in two hands.

**It is entered that the reading back took about nine seconds each time, and that the room did not go quiet, and that about nine people in that room understood what had just been done and about two hundred and thirty believed a review had been survived, and that nobody in that room lied about either of those numbers, and that the notary entered both of them and that she has entered that she believes one of them and not the other and has not said which.**

**And then the room tried to find out who *they* was, and it took about ninety seconds, and this is the whole of what the review actually was.**

"Who," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, in about two hundred and forty people's hearing. "**Who is *them*. You have answered a question about a group and you have not given us the group.**"

"The seventeen," said Adrian Vale.

"**Which seventeen.**"

And the room went to work on it in daylight, in about ninety seconds, and it is entered in nine lines in a girl's hand and it is the most useful thing anybody did all day.

The six of the first group are named on one page. The eleven of the second group are named on another. Both pages are in this city's book and not in the roll of strangers, and the first page went into the roll on the fourteenth day of the new month and the second page has not gone into any case at all since the third week of that month, **because a notary of fifty did not fill in a column and entered that she did not send it, with two reasons, one true and one convenient, and that they are not the same one.**

Eleven of the seventeen are on a page in a book that has a date on it. Two of them are on no roll in this city at all and have never been on one: a girl of sixteen who marks chalk on a floor for a wage, and a woman of thirty-eight who named the shape of a review in ten words and who has not been asked a question this volume. One of the seventeen is a man of forty-four who has asked for a page saying that he does not want to go back and has not been argued with. One of them is a boy of nineteen who walked into a room with no window and came back out and said four words and has not been asked a second question.

**Nobody in that room could produce a name.** Not one. Not because the names do not exist — they are on two pages in a book a hundred yards away and any person in that room could walk and read both — but because the two people who are on no roll at all could not be named from memory by anybody in that room, and neither of the two was in it.

"**I can say how many,**" said the girl of seventeen. "**I can say how many are on a page and how many are on no page and how many of the ones on no page have never been asked anything by anybody in this volume. I cannot say either of the two names and I have been in this room for eight hours and I have looked at both of them at the back of it and I do not know either.**"

"**Then the answer stands,**" said Renna Ord, "**and if the answer does not stand because a room cannot produce two names, then the question was never about a group and it was about a column, and the person who worked that out is seventeen and is on no roll and did it on a pail in a yard three days ago.**"

"That is how a licence dies," said a Hearthguard of forty-four, from about nineteen feet back.

"Yes," said Renna Ord.

"Then say the rest of it."

"**The rest of it is that it has just been answered by not doing a thing,**" she said, "**and that a review survived by refusing is a review that has written down that this city refuses, and that the next review will have that on the table in front of it in a room, and a room that has seen a city refuse once refuses twice.**"

**And that is the weakness, and it is in the book in a girl's hand in a column four lines high, and it is the sentence a man of thirty-two wrote out in a private book at about the eighth hour of the evening and did not show to anybody, and the sentence is nineteen words:**

> **The licence survived because a city said no, and a city that says no says it in a room.**

---

**Four other things happened in that room after the eleventh question and none of them was on either page.**

**A Crown letter was not answered.** The notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood, and the letter of the thirty-first, and the hundred and ten days, and the demand for a registered holder or a statement of permanent closure: the review is a review of a local instrument in the words of this city, and the second answer of the twenty-second day of the new month is not in the form required and is nine lines long and was sealed, and **the room was asked whether it wished to answer a Crown letter in front of about two hundred and forty people with a date on it, and the answer was no, in about nine seconds, and the notary gave the reason and the reason is that a form invented under two hundred and forty people's eyes in a room is how the honest useless form happened, and this city is not inventing another one in the same week.**

**The door stays shut.** The Narrow Mark is not opened. It has been opened twice in seventy-five days and it is not opened a third time in this month, and the review does not authorise an opening, and the licence's own second page says a controlled opening needs a receiving community that understands the purpose and there is none on the far side, and no instrument in this world can make one from this side.

**Nine Elms' water is not received.** It is not received today and it will not be received for the coming quarter, and the reason is on a page in a book in a converted salt store, and the reason is that a Crown officer was correct.

**And a seat was offered for the fourth time in five weeks, and the instrument said nobody again.**

A Hearthguard of forty-four moved it. He moved it in about nine seconds, in daylight, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and he moved it because he had thought about it for four days and he said so, and his reason is on the page and it is his:

"**A route with nobody answering for the next test is a route that nobody can be angry at, and a seat is a place to be angry at, and if there is no seat then the only people in this city who can stop that door are nine named people and every one of the nine is a person the door costs.**"

"I refuse it," said Adrian Vale, "**and I have refused it three times and I am not going to make a fourth refusal into a performance.**"

"No," said Hettis Roke. "**I did not offer it to be taken. I moved it because it has to be in the book that a man moved it and lost it, and because I have been sitting in this room for eight hours watching about nine people understand things and I wanted one thing in the record that was mine.**"

Then Sera Vail read the line, and the line says **nobody**, and it has said nobody since the twenty-fifth day of the new month, and the licence says that nobody answers for the next test.

**And the last thing in the room was the last column of the fourth Crown instrument, and it is the only seat in nine lines of Crown table, and it is headed *person to be asked*.**

Adrian Vale had put his own name in it on the thirty-first day of the new month, and a woman of twenty-four had told him in twenty-five words that he had made himself the only person in this city an office can write to, and he had asked for the twenty-five words twice and written them on a slate and had not answered them.

It was answered on the fiftieth day of the new month, in a converted salt store, in daylight, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and it was not answered with a name.

"The column asks for the name of one person who may be asked," said Sera Vail, "**and the answer of this district is that the person who may be asked is a question, and it is on a nail in the lock-chapel arch, and it is the fifth item of a form of four items and a price, and it is the only one of the five that anybody can use.**"

"That is not a person," said a clerk of the Registry at Aurel.

"That is not a name," said Sera Vail, "**and I am aware that it is not a name, and I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I know exactly what a column that cannot be filled in does to a file, and I have decided to send it, and I would like it entered that I decided to send it and that the decision took me a day and a half and that on the first day I could not have told you why.**"

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the evening, in ice, on the walk-round of the Lock of Salt, a man of thirty-two stood four feet back from the wall of the second bay and read a number off it and said it out loud, because a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read asked him to, in the local language, at the ordinary volume, in front of about nine people.**

"**Eight and seven-eighths.**"

That is the number. It has been eight and seven-eighths since the twenty-fourth day of the new month, which is day forty-nine of this flood, and before that it was eight and seven-eighths, and before that it was eight and five-eighths, and it stepped twice, on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth, both times entered in an unnamed mason's own hand before the number was read, and a crack does not step, and a press is a curve and a work is a decision. **The join is dressed. It does not narrow. It gets dressed. It has never said a word.**

He was four feet back. He is barred from opening, inspecting, signing and stopping the instrument, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument of the twenty-second day of the new month, at his own request, in his own hand, with the date on it. He performed no working today. He holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat.

**The black line in the heel of his right palm is seventy-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

**There is no field in the air.** It is entered that there was no field in the air, and that about four people were standing in that gallery and that two of them looked for one, and that a review survived in daylight in a converted salt store in front of about two hundred and forty people produced nothing at all out of the wall, **and that this is seven for seven, and the seven things are a man dying, a city committing a man to a thing for the rest of his life, a city closing a join in its own gate, a city signing a licence, a city finding out that its door does not go home, a city finding out what a person with no work is for, and a review survived.**

A number, and a dressed edge, and no sentence about anybody.

---

**The licence is in force and it is weaker. The door is shut. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixteenth time and the salt was not sent. Nine Elms' water is not received and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two. March is ten days off. A girl of seventeen who is on no roll in this city stood in a converted salt store for eight hours holding a slate flat and wrote down what about two hundred and forty people believed and what nine people understood, and both are on the page, and neither is above the other, and the width of her column is entered as a fact for the fifteenth time in that year. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and the second page of the instrument says in nineteen lines that this city is not asking about it and why, and it was read out in full this afternoon in a room with about two hundred and forty people in it. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent. There is one ruled line left in the column and it was not filled. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room, and it stood on a barrel in a salt store for eight hours and about nine people read it.**
