# Chapter 0079 — A Rule About a Thing

On the fifty-fourth day of the new month, which was the seventy-ninth day of the flood and a Wednesday, a man of thirty-two convened a room in daylight with a date on the notice in his own hand, in order to own a failure in public before somebody else owned it for him, and about nine people came, and he got it, and the fourth line of a term sheet was used against him correctly in a room and he could not answer it, and he answered it with a page, and the page moved a decision out of a person and into a document, and nobody in that room could tell whether that was the right thing, and a woman of forty-one of Silt Row entered four sentences that were every one of them correct, and a Hearthguard of forty-four said two sentences in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and one of the two had been said about a woman of forty-one and about him in a room and had never been said about either of them, and nobody in that yard said the word *resign*, because there is nothing to resign.

**The licence is twenty-nine days old. March is six days off. The bar is not broken. It is perfect. It is scoped to the wrong thing.**

---

**The notice went on the wall over a chandlery on the upper road at about the seventh hour of the morning in his own hand, and the girl of seventeen took it down and put it in the book before anybody had read it, and the notice is four lines and it is not a document anybody can refuse, and that was deliberate and he has entered that it was deliberate.**

> **The fifty-fourth day of the new month. The seventh hour of the morning. A room over a chandlery on the upper road, and after that the road to Four Elms, and after that a yard at Four Elms.**
>
> **Called by Adrian Vale. About the work done by Perry Lask on the fifty-third day of the new month and about a page he wrote on the twenty-sixth day of this flood.**
>
> **Any person may come and say anything.**

"I have written a notice nobody can refuse," he said, "**and I have wanted to do that for about nine days and I did not do it, and the reason I did not is that a notice is a form and a form is what I do instead of asking.**"

"Then you are doing it now," said the girl of seventeen.

"I am doing it now," he said, "**and a notice nobody can refuse is a door with my name on it and I have noticed that about two seconds after I nailed it up, and it is on the page, and we are starting.**"

---

**He got it in about eleven minutes and it is in the book in his own words and nobody improved on it, and the book has three sentences and about nine people in a room and the date on it in his own hand at the top.**

"The bar says a workway may not be offered to any of the seventeen by this instrument or by anybody using it. **It is the longest page in the licence. It is in my own hand and the date is on it and I fought for two hours and ten minutes to get a sentence I did not think of onto it, and the sentence that got it accepted was not mine and was about paper.**"

"It is not broken," said Sera Vail.

"**It is not broken. It did not reach.** A rule that covers a document is a rule about a document. **A pale field in a room is not the instrument and nobody used anything and there was no hand on any lever and the page is perfect and it does not reach, and I wrote it, and I have known what it was since the thirty-second day of the new month and I said it out loud in that room in thirty-one words and then I spent nine days using it about other things.**"

"And the thing it did not reach was a person," said Ottoline Vask, thirty-four years in Saltwake, who has not gone to a boundary stone in nine years and did not go to one yesterday either.

"**The thing it did not reach was a person, on a boundary, on the fifty-third day of the new month, in the wrong weather, and I was four feet back, and I was not barred, and there was no page, and there was no instrument, and I have written that down as the whole of the problem and it is correct.**"

"Somebody said something in this yard on the thirty-first day of this flood," said a woman of about forty-four who keeps a stall, "**about a man from another world being in a city that has no procedure, and I would like to say the same thing now, and I am not going to be allowed to.**"

"No," said Adrian Vale. "**You would not, and I would refuse it in about four seconds, and I have refused better versions of it twice this month from people I have never met, and I would like the room to enter that I was offered a way out of this in the first ninety seconds and turned it down, because in about nine months somebody is going to write that a man from another world could not have known, and that is the sentence the Crown writes, and it is not true, and the only place it can be stopped is here.**"

"Do not do that in this yard," said Wenna Cray, forty-one, of Silt Row, who has entered four statements of error in the history of this book and is on the first page of one against his name. "**Do not be a man who is excused in a yard. I have watched a room do that to a person twice in five weeks and both times it was kind and both times the person was worse afterwards.**"

---

**Then the fourth line of a term sheet was used against him, and it was used correctly, and it was said by a woman of thirty-one of Nine Elms on a road, and he could not answer it, and it had been said to him once before in seventy-nine days, in a room, four days ago, by a woman of seventy reading it off a second page, and this is the first time it has been said to him by a person who is not a notary and not standing up.**

The fourth line is Ilda Marsh's, and it is the same line in the same words on the same page, and a room of two hundred and forty people heard it read out on the fiftieth day of the new month and about nine of them have written it out again since, and it is on the page in the plain words of a woman who has lived in it:

> **And on a village's refusal the whole of the charge returns to the private debtor.**

The ninth household of Nine Elms refused for the third time on the forty-fourth day of the new month. The refusal was affordable, because a Crown factor of the reed levy used the Crown's own fifth clause correctly, in the form required, and advanced the quarter against the district's own reeds. The credit was refused. The quarter is gone. Nine Elms' water is not received. **And the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, and has been since the thirty-sixth day of this flood, and it is forty-three days old.**

Petra Oake, thirty-one, of Nine Elms, has asked twice whether there is a person on the other side of the chapel floor and been answered once in the worst possible way. She came up the Reach road on the Wednesday with two other people and she caught the party on the road at about the second hour of the afternoon, four miles down, where the ground is level and the ditch has no guess in it any more.

She did not begin with the fourth line. She began by using it, correctly, in the form required, and Adrian Vale has entered that she used it correctly and that a correct instrument used correctly against a person is a thing that has happened to this city three times in twenty-one days and that the third time is the first time it was used on him.

"**Read your own page,**" she said, "**and then answer me this. On a village's refusal the whole of the charge returns to the private debtor. The private debtor is you. Say it.**"

"I am the private debtor."

"**Then you are a person,**" said Petra Oake, "**and a person is not one person, and I have come eleven miles to say one sentence to you and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it twice.**"

She said it.

"**The whole of the charge is on you and there is a woman of forty-eight in the fourth house at Nine Elms who has been down to the fourth channel every morning since the twenty-ninth day of the new month clearing a drain that silts in a thaw, by herself, because the levy that used to do it is gone, and she has never asked anybody for anything in her life and she does not know that you exist.**"

**And then she said the other half, and it is twenty-four words, and it is in the book in her own words, and about nine people standing on a road in a winter understood all of it:**

"**A rescue is a working. A working needs a pattern. A floor that has come open once is a hole and not a pattern.**"

The last sentence of that is a sentence a woman of thirty-four said on a walk-round on the twentieth day of the new month, and it has been in this book ever since, and nobody has improved on it, and it is not a lesson and it is not going to become one, and a woman of thirty-one of Nine Elms used it on a road on the fifty-fourth day of the new month to tell a man of thirty-two that he is now the shape of a rescue.

He could not answer it.

**It is entered that he could not answer it, and that he tried for about nine minutes, and that about nine people watched a man of thirty-two fail to find a sentence, and that it is the first time in seventy-nine days that anybody in this book has seen him not find one on the first attempt, and that about four of the nine people in that road thought they were watching a man be trapped and about five of them were watching a man be told what he is.**

---

**They went up the road to a shrine on a boundary at Four Elms, and nothing happened there, and he wanted it to.**

It is a stone about the height of a man's chest with a cut in the flat of it, on the north side of a road that forks, where the ground drops away. **It is not an instrument and it is not a form and it has no date on it and no price and it is not a Crown document and it is not a page anybody may carry to a building nine hundred miles away.** It has been standing where it stood for a hundred and forty years and it is kept by a household and about nine people in this city have a reason about it.

The line in the flat of it is not the boundary. The boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream, once, in living memory, by the three jurisdictions together at a public act with witnesses, and the cost column said the bottom field failed and four hundred people were rehoused. **The line in the shrine is where the water was before that. The shrine is a thing that says so, and it has been saying so since before anybody who can read it was born.**

Marda Rood is about sixty-one and is the grandmother of a boy of eight who went missing on the twenty-seventh day of this flood and was found at four in the morning by four people who were not sent. She keeps the shrine. She has kept it since before her husband died and she does not say why and nobody has asked her, and she asked a question at the foot of a stair on the twenty-seventh day of this flood and was told the truth, and she is one of about nine people in this city who cannot be managed.

"**Every other thing in this valley that remembers anything is on paper,**" she said, "**and paper goes in a case and a case comes back empty, and I have been married thirty-nine years and I have watched a roll of a whole district go down the river and come back with nothing in it and nobody come, and nobody ever comes. This is a stone. A stone does not go anywhere.**"

"Why did we come up here," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close.

"Because it is the nearest thing with a cut in it," said Adrian Vale, "**and I wanted to stand on the place where the thing happened, and I have wanted that since yesterday and I did not do it because I did not want to be a man who goes and stands on a place. It is on the page that I came.**"

---

**And he answered it with a page, in a room, in daylight, with a date, and what the page does is move a decision out of a person and into a document, and everybody in that room can see that that is the thing he does, and not one of them can tell whether it is the right thing.**

He wrote it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon in a room over a chandlery and read it out twice and it is nine lines and it goes on the same page as the review's eleventh question and answer and not on a separate sheet.

> **The whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on one man. A man is not one person.**
>
> **Therefore it is to be asked, of each person the charge is made of, separately, in daylight, in a room, whether they will have it on them, and the answer may be no, and a no is entered and does not move the charge, and nobody is asked twice.**
>
> **No person is named on this page. A page that names them is a roll, and a roll goes to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year, and the whole of the argument of this city for seventy-nine days has been that describing people is the only protection anybody here has ever been offered by that office.**

**And the room did what about nine rooms in this city have done seventy-nine times, which is to understand exactly what the page does and to be unable to say whether it is a protection.**

"It protects her," said a woman of twenty-nine who works in Saye's room. "**Nobody can put it on her without asking her and it cannot be entered against her because she is not on it.**"

"It describes her," said a woman of twenty-four. "**It says there is a woman of forty-eight in the fourth house at Nine Elms. That is a sentence about a person. It is in a book. The book is a hundred yards from a door and a door is a room and a room is people with a page on the table.**"

"It asks," said Adrian Vale. "**It asks and it does not name, and a page that asks is the only kind of page in this city anybody can be angry at, and I know that, and I wrote it, and I have written six pages about what a person may not do and one sentence about what a person may do and the sentence is not mine and it is on a stair somewhere and it is twenty-four words long and it says to ask the city and take the answer and not to offer anybody a seat, and I have not offered anybody a seat since the forty-ninth day of this flood.**"

"Then it is a good page," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It is a page, and I am the only person in this room who cannot tell you whether it is the right one, and I am going to say that out loud and I would like the girl to enter it, because a man who knows and says he does not know is the only thing I have got.**"

---

**And then a woman of forty-one of Silt Row entered the fifth statement of error in the history of this book, against a person, naming him, in four sentences, and every one of them is correct, and she had written it at about the second hour of the afternoon on a road and shown it to nobody.**

"You wrote a bar about a document. A bar about a document is a bar and not a wall, and the wall is where the work happened." "**You have said that sentence out loud in a room nine times since the thirty-second day of the new month and every one of the nine times it was about something else.**" "On the fifty-third day of the new month a woman did a work on a boundary stone and you were four feet back and there was no instrument to bar you from and you have written that down as the whole of the problem, and the whole of the problem is a rule you wrote about a thing." "**You are going to answer a charge about persons with a page about persons, and nobody has asked the persons, and I do not know whether that is a protection or a removal and neither do you.**"

It is entered. All four stands. Nobody in that room said a word, and about nine people heard all four of them, and about two hundred and thirty were not in the room and will hear about four of them later and will be told it was about something else, and that is entered as what usually happens.

"Can I answer it," he said.

"You may do one of three things and you may not do the other two," said Sera Vail. "**You may answer it in public within the term. You may withdraw it. Or you may say publicly that you stand by it. The third clause is a real procedure and it has been a real procedure since the thirty-first day of this flood and I wrote it and I would like the room to understand that using it is correct, and that I cannot make it easier, and that this is the one I think you are going to have to do.**"

"Why do you think that."

"**Because the third clause is the only one of the three that does not require a sentence from you,**" said Sera Vail, "**and there is a woman of about thirty-one standing in this room who has walked eleven miles and a woman of forty-one who has written four sentences on a road and about two hundred and forty people in a yard who are about to be told that a man from another world owns a failure, and every one of those people came for a sentence from you.**"

He stood by it. In a yard, in daylight, in front of about two hundred and forty people, in nineteen words, and it is in the book and it is the whole of what he said:

"**I stand by it. All four are correct. I am not going to answer a word of it today.**"

---

**And then a Hearthguard of forty-four said two sentences in that yard, and about nine people heard both of them, and the first of them had been said four times in seventy-nine days and the second had been said once, in a yard, on the thirty-fourth day of the new month, by a man of forty-two who is not a person this door costs.**

"**A rule that only covers the thing you can see is a rule about a thing and not about the world.**"

Nobody said anything.

"And the other one," said Hettis Roke. "**A guild that does not go home.**"

He let about nine seconds go by, which is the number of people who understood it and it is also, this time, the number of seconds.

"I said the first of those to a woman of forty-one in a room over a chandlery on the thirty-second day of the new month, and I said it to this man in that same room, and I have never once said either of them about the man who wrote the page, **and that is the difference and I have been waiting nine days to say it in a yard.**"

"It is about the Concord," said Nadia Brandt, twenty-nine. "**A page of yours went into the book of a hundred and forty people this morning.**"

"**Yes,**" said Hettis Roke. "**A hundred and forty people, and the hundred and forty do not go home, and their children do not, and a page in that book is a thing a person in that book can be angry at for four generations, and he put one there today in daylight with a date on it, and I have not said one word against it and I am saying it now, because a man who is owning a failure has got about nine days' worth of people who are on his side and in nine days they will not be, and the reason they will not is on a page in a book, and the page is his.**"

Adrian Vale did not answer that either.

**And a man of forty-one in a very good coat, who has been in this city since the sixth week of the flood and who has not said one wrong thing in seventy-nine days, had been standing in that yard for the whole of it and had carried a bench to an old woman at about the fourth hour and put it at the side of her and not at the back, and about nine people noticed the bench and about two hundred and thirty did not, and he had offered nothing, and then he was asked whether the two sentences were the shape of a thing he has been getting at, and he said that they were, in one word, and then he said the other half of it, and it is in the book in his own words.**

"He is right and I am not going to pretend he is not," said Ivenn Marrow, "**and I have an answer to the second of those two sentences and I am not going to give it to you in a yard.**"

"Nobody expected you to," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close.

"**I am going to give it to you in a room, in daylight, in about four months,**" said Ivenn Marrow, "**and I have written it down and dated it, and it is in my own hand and no office has it, and you may ask me for it and I will not refuse you and I will not enjoy refusing you either.**"

The girl of seventeen took it down. It is on the page, and the date is on it, and it is in a column four lines high.

"That is a promise," said Nyla Ferrow, "**and a promise with a shape in it is a thing a person can be held to, and he has just handed this city a thing to hold him to, and he is not convicted of anything.**"

"**I am not convicted of anything,**" said Ivenn Marrow, "**and I have just handed you the only weapon anybody in this city will ever have against me, and I would like it entered that I did that on purpose and that I have wanted to be held to something for about nine days and did not know what.**"

**And nobody in that yard said the word *resign*, because there is nothing to resign. He holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat. He is party to a trial licence twenty-nine days old and is given nothing by it. The seat of holder of the pull ended at noon on the forty-ninth day of this flood and is vacant and its terms went with it. There is no post, no office, no bench, no guild, no Crown instrument and no instrument of this city's own that has his name at the top of it, and there is nothing at the top of anything, and a man who has nothing to resign cannot be asked to.**

What was entered, at about the eighth hour of the evening, in a yard at Four Elms, in daylight, with about two hundred and forty people present, is that a man of thirty-two owned a failure, and stood by four sentences that were correct, and said nine words, and was told two things by a Hearthguard, and answered neither, and that about nine people in that yard understood both of the two things and about two hundred and thirty understood neither, and that nobody said the word *resign*, and that the reason nobody said it is entered, and the reason is in the notary's hand and it is twenty-one words:

> **There is nothing here to resign from, and a man with nothing to resign is not a man being let go.**

The fourth line of a term sheet was used against him correctly by a woman of thirty-one of Nine Elms who walked eleven miles to do it, and it said that a man is not one person, and he could not answer it, and he answered it with a page that asks and does not name, and about nine people in a room could see that that was the thing he does and could not tell whether it was the right thing.
