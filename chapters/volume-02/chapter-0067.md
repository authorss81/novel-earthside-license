# Chapter 0067 — A Price on Good Faith

On the forty-second day of the new month, which was the sixty-seventh day of the flood and a Friday, a city of eleven hundred people paid a fee of eleven ounces of silver to a building nine hundred miles away in the only currency it had, and the office took it, and the rate was generous, and the generosity is the finding, and the finding is that **a district which can pay a fee in the only thing it has is a district whose good faith has a price on it and a buyer.**

The salt was not sent. Nothing was gained. The fee is entered as owing with no date for the eleventh time and both of the facts that were created on the thirty-first day of the new month by a schedule of crossings are now on a page in this city and neither of them is on any page the office has sent.

---

**It began with a woman of seventy who keeps that ground, on the night of the thirty-eighth day of the new month, under an arch, in four words.**

*"Ask them and find out."*

Nobody asked them. It sat in a book for four days, and on the forty-first day a woman of twenty-four worked out how a letter goes, and Peth Lammay said the sentence that a letter is a thing that travels along a road, and the road to Aurel goes down a river, and there is a licensed carrier of the Crown's second-table mail on it four times a year.

**So the question went out in a way that this city has not used in sixty-seven days: not in a hall and not in a yard, in a book, by a person whose job it is, with no rider and no seal that a notary had to press.**

Renner Sallow is the second clerk of the Registry at Aurel. He has been at a table in this city since the fourth day of the flood and he has never been asked to stop and is never going to be, and he has been counting fields in a book in a stranger's language since the eighth day of this flood, and the count is eleven, because he saw the twentieth on the fortieth day of the new month in a room with nine people in it, and it was entered that he was one of the nine. He has never once been asked to leave and this city has not convicted him of anything and is not going to.

He read the question in a room in the office of the Concord at the salt wharf on the forty-second day of the new month, at about the second hour of the afternoon, and he did not answer it in the room. He said he would answer it in writing and that his answer would be in the same form as the schedule of crossings, **and that the office does not answer questions in rooms.**

"That is not a refusal," said Adrian Vale.

"It is not," said Renner Sallow. "**It is a document, and I have decided which shape it is in, and the shape is mine and not yours, and I would like that entered because it is the only power I have and I am using it to stop you from guessing.**"

---

**The answer came back on the evening of the forty-second day of the new month, and it is four lines, and it is the same document and not a new one, and Sera Vail said so out loud before anybody had finished reading it, and it is the first time in five weeks she has said the opposite of what she has been saying.**

> **From the Office of Crossings and Tides, at Aurel. In the matter of the schedule of crossings, quarterly, in the form required, at the fee of eleven ounces of silver.**
>
> **The fee is discharged in full by the delivery of salt, at the retail price of the day of delivery, to a quantity equal to eleven ounces of fine silver at the office's own assay, being taken out of the district's own production.**
>
> **The office adopts that rate as a standing rate for this district and will not review it in this quarter.**
>
> **No further sum is charged for the schedule of crossings for the quarter in respect of which this payment is made.**

**And the notary said, in about nine seconds, and it is in the book, and it is the first time in sixty-seven days that Sera Vail has said that something from that office is the same document and not a new one:**

"It is the same instrument. It is the same form required, the same quarterly date, the same eight columns, the same fee. **It is the fourth document and it is still the fourth document and it has not become a fifth, and I want that in the book, because the day it becomes a fifth is the day this city has a queue.**"

**And the Measure heard about it at the second hour of the afternoon and Ottavia Reek was in her own yard and had about four minutes' notice and the sentence she said in it is the reason she is not a villain in this book and will not be.**

"I have offered this city thirty tons of salt on credit four times since the twenty-seventh day of the new month and been refused four times, and every one of those refusals was correct and every one of them was made by people who owed nothing to anybody.

"**And this morning a building I have never been inside told them in a form that they may pay a Crown fee in the exact substance my yard is standing in, at a rate that is generous, and I have had the worst morning of my year and it is a morning where a person nine hundred miles away did my work for me for nothing.**

"I am not going to stand here and say that is worse. **It is worse for Saltwake and it is better for me, and I have got a quarter and a bad winter and I have four idle elms, and I would like somebody in this yard to notice that I said the second half of that out loud.**"

Nobody did notice. It is entered that nobody did.

"And I will say one more thing and then I will go and do a count," she said. "**The brass is not going to hold the rail. I have the timber and my price is the crew's list and your crew's list is a field, and a field is in March, and a brass office has just told a clerk nine hundred miles away that this district's currency is wet and comes out of the ground. I would like somebody to tell me what that does to a lever in a yard in February.**"

**Nobody could tell her. It is entered that nobody could tell her and that the question went into the cost column, and that the cost column is entered for the ninth time in one month and still has no figure on the end of it, and that a season of a field four miles down the Reach road is settled in March, which is a month in this world's calendar and is fifteen days off.**

---

**And the rule has been said four times in sixty-seven days and it did not fit, and the woman who said it said so herself, and that is the fourth thing this day knows.**

Renna Ord was in the office of the Concord at the salt wharf at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and a man of about thirty-one asked her, in the yard outside, whether this was the same as a guild being made to pay.

"No," she said. "**And I want that written down in the right order, which is that I have said that rule four times and it has been right four times and it does not fit this, and I am not going to stand in a yard and pretend a thing is the thing I have been fighting when it is not.**

"That rule is about a list of men. A wage off a second page, a list that a guild can close, a lock that a guild can close. This is not a list of men. **This is a list of what we have.** And I have never in forty years had a rule for a list of what we have, and I have been in this city long enough to know that a thing with no rule on it is the thing a person is proud of, and it is also the thing that gets used."

---

**And under the arch of the lock chapel, at about the ninth hour of the evening, the four people who change the salt and take the four chalk depths found themselves looking at the one price this city has ever printed, and one of them said four words, and about nine people heard them and about two hundred and thirty did not.**

The price of the salt is written under the four items on the slate on the nail, and it is not a fifth of them, and it is a double handful twice a day, and it costs the keeper eleven weeks of her own ration.

Nyla Ferrow is seventy and she reads the join-line. Peth Lomas is fifty and reads the number. Elsa Skell is forty-four and reads the edge. Renna Ord is fifty-eight and cannot read and reads the join by standing on it.

**On the thirty-eighth day of the new month, asked whether the office would take salt, Nyla Ferrow said four words. On the forty-second day of the new month it has been asked and answered, and she said the other four words, and they are in the book in her own hand because she is seventy and does not lend anybody her slate:**

> "**Then the price on that board is a price in two currencies.**"

And it is entered that nobody in the yard knew what to do with that, and that a mason of fifty said four sentences about it and the fourth of them was: *"The form is still a form. It still cannot close anything and it still cannot say where a residue goes. Two currencies does not make it more of a promise, it makes it a better price."*

---

**And the rate is generous. That is the whole of the trouble. Sallow had assayed the district's own production in his head, from two thirds of a form filled in honestly by a woman with a bar, and the salt in Saltwake is worth about two and a half times what the office has offered, and the office has offered it anyway, immediately, in writing, in a form, on the same evening, without a committee.**

**A Crown office that will take salt for silver is a Crown office that wants salt.**

Nobody in that room said the word *leverage*. About nine people in that room had it in their heads by the fourth hour of the afternoon and about two hundred and forty people in Saltwake did not have it in their heads at all and were not told.

Ottoline Vask said the thing that is actually the thing, in about eleven words, to about nine people:

"**There is no salt within four hundred miles of this gate but ours, and the price of it went up a third on the twenty-seventh, and an office that has just learned that is not being kind.**"

"What is it doing," said Adrian Vale.

"**It is putting a number on us,**" said Ottoline Vask. "**It is finding out what we can be made to do, and it is doing it kindly, and I would rather it were rude, and I would like that entered because it is the worst kind.**"

---

**And the twenty-first thing this wall said came up at about the fourth hour and nine minutes of the afternoon, in that room, on a page that is otherwise about a fee, and it is the best of the twenty-one, and it puts nothing at all where a cost goes.**

> **THE DISTRICT MAY BE DESCRIBED.**
>
> **The district may be described, in any publication, as a district that pays in kind, and the description may be published, and the district will not be charged a fee again for as long as it pays in kind.**
>
> **Nobody is asked. The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

It is the best of the twenty-one because there is nothing in it to point at. It is not a workway. It does not put a person in the place where a cost goes. It does not say *the person is the whole of the cost*, and it does not need to, because the person is not in it anywhere. **It puts nothing in that grammatical position at all, and a thing that puts nothing there is not a cheaper version of the other twenty and it is a different animal: it is an offer to describe a place.**

About nine people saw it. The girl of seventeen saw it and wrote down the four lines of it in her own hand without knowing what she was writing, as she did on the thirty-second day of the new month, and it is on the page, and the girl has entered that she wrote it and did not know.

It was refused in under a second, and the reason was given in three parts, and the third part is the one that matters, and the three parts were given by Adrian Vale, in a room of about nine people, standing up, in the local language:

"One. A district that is described as paying in kind is a district that has told the office what it can be made to do.

"Two. A description is a removal with a column for where the person is, and that is not mine, it is in this city in about two hundred and forty people's hearing, and the column for where a place is is very wide and it is blank at both ends, and **Aurel is nine hundred miles away and it can send anything at all and it has never once sent anybody.**

"Three. **And a thing an office knows it can make a district do is not a favour and it is not a courtesy and it is not even a bargain. It is a mechanism, and it will be used in a quarter I am not in, by a man I will not meet, for a reason I cannot refuse, because I will have told him in advance and in writing and in a form that he asked me for.**"

**And the refusal is entered, and the count of refusals in Renner Sallow's second book, which is kept at home and has never been shown to anybody, is twenty-one, and it was twenty at the fortieth day of the new month, and Renner Sallow did not read the number out loud in the room and was asked for it in a yard on the following morning and gave it and asked for the yard entry as well.**

---

**And the salt was not sent, and the finding stands anyway, and both of the facts that did not exist on the thirty-first day of the new month now exist and neither of them can be un-learned.**

> **On the thirty-first day of the new month this city did not know that the Office of Crossings and Tides would take payment in kind, and did not know that the district had salt worth more to that office than the fee, and did not know that a district may be described as a thing that pays in kind.**
>
> **On the forty-second day of the new month it knows all three, and has told the office nothing, and has been told by the office, in writing, in a form, and both of those are facts and not opinions and a fact cannot be un-learned by a person.**
>
> **The salt is not sent. The fee is entered as owing with no date for the eleventh time. The eleventh entry is in a girl's hand and she keeps the column and she counted them and the count is a fact and the count is entered as a count.**

And under the eleventh entry, in a notary's hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening on the forty-second day of the new month, in a converted salt store, a line was added. It is in the book and it is one of the four lines that are on that page and it is the fourth:

> **That the office has been told what this district has, and this district has been told what the office will take, and neither of those is on any page the office has sent, and both of them are now on a page in this city.**

**And then Renner Sallow said one sentence, in about nine seconds, and it is thirty words, and it is entered in full in both languages, and it is the sentence this volume's politics are built on, and he has never written it in a book before and has said so:**

> "An office that will take your goods for its fee is telling you what it thinks you are, and it is also telling you what it thinks you will be."

And Adrian Vale, who has written six pages about what a person may not do, stood in a converted salt store with a bar in his hand and four people in it and found that he had nothing to add to that, and **said so out loud, in about nine words, in the local language, and asked for it to be entered as a finding and not as modesty:**

"**I have wanted that sentence for four months and I have never found it, and I am not going to pretend I wrote it, and I would like it written down in a clerk's hand from a building nine hundred miles away and not in mine.**"

**Nothing was gained. A mechanism was identified before it was used, and that is the only kind of winning this book allows, and it is entered that it is the only kind and that the sentence was nine words and the mechanism was not seen by anybody else in the room.**

**The trial licence is seventeen days old. The main breach is closed and dressed and has never said a word. The ground under the chapel is a scar and is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item added on the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster, has performed no working, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat. The review is in eight days, on a slate on a nail, with a date on it and no questions on it. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and nobody has to open anything for that to happen, and nobody has asked what to do about it. And a woman of twenty-four of the second list said in a room this month that she was proud of a document and afraid of it, in that order, and has not said which one of the two was which.**
