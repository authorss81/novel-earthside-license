# Chapter 0073 — The Twenty-Second Field

On the forty-eighth day of the new month, which was the seventy-third day of the flood and a Thursday, two days before a review, a woman of seventeen was written on a page for the first time in this volume, and in a yard behind a chandlery at about the third hour and forty-one minutes of the afternoon the wall said the same true thing it said on the forty-second day of the new month, in a better coat, and refused to be a new thing, and the refusal was correct, and the interesting half was not the refusal.

**The licence is twenty-three days old. The review is on the fiftieth day of the new month. Eleven questions, a second page, a slate, and four words on the front of a slate with the question they answer on the back of it, and about nine people in this city know that and about two hundred and thirty do not.**

---

**The girl of seventeen was written on a page on the morning of the forty-eighth day of the new month, and she noticed, and she asked, and the answer was given by a notary and it was true and the notary entered that she does not know whether it is.**

The page is the copying list for the review. Nine slates and one page are going into the wooden case, and the second page is written out nine times in nine hands, and the list says who wrote which one, and her name is on it in a column four lines high.

The width of that column is entered as a fact for the eleventh time in this year.

"I am on a page," she said.

"You are on a page," said Sera Vail.

"**With a date on it.**"

"With a date on it."

"And no name at the top of it," said the girl of seventeen, "**and about nine people have copied it out by hand, and it is going in a case to a building nine hundred miles away, and I am sixteen and I am on no roll at all.**"

"Then it is a page and it is not a roll, and I would like to say why in about nine sentences and then I am going to stop." Sera Vail put the list down. "A roll is a standing thing. It is a thing a man may be asked about, and a thing that comes back from a case with a column filled in, and it goes four times a year whether anybody wants it or not, and **it is the only thing in this world that makes a person findable.** This list is a list of nine slates and who copied them, it goes once, in this case, and there is no second copy of it in any building anywhere, **and if this district is described in the coming quarter, your name is on a piece of paper in a converted salt store and not in a wooden case, and that is a different thing and it is a smaller thing, and it is a thing you can be angry at.**"

"And if somebody copies this list out?"

"Then it is nine more pages and the same list and nobody is going to do anything with any of them, **and that is the first protection anybody from another world has ever been given in this city and it is that a thing nobody can find cannot be removed.**"

"And you do not know whether that is true."

"**I do not know whether that is true,**" said Sera Vail, "**and I would like it entered that a notary of fifty who has read Crown procedure for twenty-six years has just told a girl of seventeen that she is probably safe and has entered that she does not know.**"

---

**Then the twenty-second field came up in the yard behind the chandlery at about the third hour and forty-one minutes of the afternoon, in about nine people, and it stood about nineteen seconds, and about nine of the nine saw it, and it was not a new thing.**

> **THE DISTRICT MAY BE DESCRIBED, IN ANY RETURN, AS A DISTRICT THAT SETTLES ITS ACCOUNTS IN KIND.**
>
> **The description may be published.**
>
> **The district will not be charged a fee again.**
>
> **Nobody is asked.**
>
> **The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

It is the twenty-first. The twenty-first was on the forty-second day of the new month in the office of the Concord at the salt wharf, in the same room, in front of about nine people, and it said:

> **The district may be described, in any publication, as a district that pays in kind, and the description may be published, and the district will not be charged a fee again for as long as it pays in kind; nobody is asked; the cost is entered; there is no further cost.**

The twenty-second says the same four things and takes the condition off.

**That is the whole of the difference and it is one clause, and it is the difference between a leash and a gift, and a gift is worse.**

"The twenty-first was right and this one is harmless," said a man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not close, and about eight other people in that yard said one version or another of the same sentence in about the next minute.

Then the second clerk of the Registry at Aurel, who has been at a table in this city since the fourth day of this flood and has never been asked to stop and is not going to be, said nine seconds of the truest thing anybody has said in a yard in a week, and it was about his own office, and he gave it in the local language and had it taken down in both.

"**It is not harmless. It is the same offer with the condition taken off, and the condition was the only part of it that could be refused.**"

"Which means what," said Perry Lask, forty-one, of the second list, who is the only person in this world who holds a workway and who has said yes to two fields in four seconds and has not been asked whether she meant it either time.

"**It means that in the first version, the office had a hand on your district's behaviour forever, and in this version it has handed the behaviour back and kept the name.**" He put his own book down. "**A thing that is described and charged a fee again can stop being described. A thing that is described and is not charged a fee again is described forever, and forever is not a punishment, it is an exemption, and an exemption is a standing, and a body with a standing is a body that may be asked questions.**"

"A district is not a person," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**A district is not a person,**" said Renner Sallow, "**and that has been the shape of my entire profession for nineteen years, and I would like to say the rest of it in about nine sentences, and I have not said it in nineteen years, and I have wanted to.**"

"**Every Crown form this office sends has a line for the body that may be asked about the return.** A holder, an advocate, a keeper, a magistrate, a guild, a ship. **The word is different every time and the grammar is the same: somebody in this place may be written to.** A district is not a person. **A district that has been given a permanent exemption is the only body in ninety years of these forms that could be written to without anybody's name being on it, and the reason it could is that there is no name, and a body with no name that may be written to is the shape my office has been trying to find since before I was born.**"

The yard was quiet for about nine seconds.

"**I am the only one of the nine who needs telling,**" he said. "**I have been that in nine other rooms in this city since the fourth day of this flood and I have never once been one of the nine and I have worked that out in about nine seconds and it has taken me nineteen years in a building to be able to say it out loud, and I would like the girl to write that I said it about my own office and not about anybody else.**"

---

**Adrian Vale refused it in under a second, out loud, in the local language, before he knew who it was for, and he has entered that for the second time in seventy-three days he has said no first and understood later and that it is still the worst habit he owns.**

"No."

Nine people in a yard behind a chandlery looked at him.

"**No,**" he said, "**and I am going to say why in three parts and the first part is that it is the twenty-first again, and the second part is that the condition has been taken off, and the third part is the one a clerk of that office has just given me and gave it to me before I got here, and I am not going to take it off him and use it, because a sentence is not mine until I have earned it and I have not earned that one.**"

"It is earned," said Renner Sallow.

"**Then it is yours and I will say the third part in my own words and get it wrong, and the getting-it-wrong is on me:** a district that has been told in writing that it does not have to pay a fee is a district that has been told what it is, in a form, by an office, and **an office that knows what a body is, in a form, in writing, has described it, and there is no line in this city anywhere that can tell the difference between a description and an exemption, and in four months somebody is going to be able to point at this and use it, and it will not be me and it will not be him and it will be whoever reads the case.**"

"You could have said the clerk's version," said a man of about thirty-one.

"**I could have, and he is a better writer of English than I am, and if the sentence that stops a thing belongs to a man who has spent nineteen years in the building that made it, then it is his and not mine, and I have been wrong about that in the opposite direction four times in this volume and I am not going to do it a fifth.**"

Then the count, which the girl of seventeen keeps and which has never been wrong.

"The twenty-second," she said. "**Refused in under a second, out loud, by the man whose bar did not reach on the fortieth day of the new month, and there was no page read out and nobody needed one.**"

And Renner Sallow added his own, because he has been counting in a book at Aurel since the eighth day of this flood and has never been asked to stop, and he opened it and said the number without being asked for it, and it is **thirteen**, and the count of refusals in the second book he keeps at home and has never shown to anybody went to **twenty-two**, and Adrian Vale asked for both numbers to be entered with the clerk's on the top line and his own under it, and the girl of seventeen entered them, and the width of her column is entered as a fact for the twelfth time in that year.

"There is a difference of nine between the two books," said Renner Sallow. "**It is the same difference on the fortieth day of the new month as it is today, and I have never been able to make it smaller, and I have never been able to find out what the nine are, and I would like that written down as a thing I have failed at and not as a thing I have hidden.**"

---

**And then Priya Shah, twenty-four, said the sentence that the next instrument written in this city will need, in a yard behind a chandlery, in about four seconds, and she wrote it on the back of the copying list because there was nothing else to write on, and it is in the book in her own hand and it is her sentence and nobody has claimed it:**

> **A form that repeats itself is not asking twice. It is establishing that it may.**

Nobody argued with it. It is entered that nobody argued with it, and that it was said by a woman of twenty-four who has now produced the sentence that made a thing possible in this city seven times, and that about nine people in that yard have understood that this is a different kind of sentence from the other six, and that a sentence that makes a thing possible and a sentence that makes a thing *repeatable* are not the same instrument, and that only one of them can be used on a city.

"It is not a threat," said Renner Sallow, from about nineteen feet back, and it is the only time in seventy-three days he has said a thing in a yard in that tone. "**I want that entered, because I know what it looks like from where I am sitting. It is not a threat. It is a form, and it has come round twice, and the second time it has taken a condition off, and if it comes round a third time it will take the word *district* off, and the fourth time it will not need the word *return*, and at that point my office will have a page it can send to any place on this river with nothing in it but a body, and no name, and a thing that may be written to.**"

---

**And the twenty-second field is entered in the book as a repetition, and the entry is the entry, and nobody may move it.**

> **The twenty-second field is the twenty-first again with the condition removed. It is entered as a repetition. It is entered as a repetition by a man who has refused four of these and who entered that refusal in under a second without reading it, and who has entered, in the same line, in his own hand, that he does not know why he can still do that and that he does not believe it is a talent.**

**And the last thing on that page is the line the girl of seventeen wrote at about the seventh hour of the evening, at his request, in a column four lines high, and he asked for it himself and it is sixteen words long:**

> **I have never once asked what happens to the ones that are not answered by me.**

There are four of them. Three were refused: a man of thirty-one in about four seconds, and a woman of twenty-four who wanted one, and a man of forty-four who was offered nothing. **One was accepted twice, in about four seconds each time, by a woman of forty-one who said one sentence before the first time and nothing at all before the second, and the acceptance is in the book in her own words with a date on it, and it is the best offer anybody in this city has been made since the flood, and nobody in this city has asked her whether she meant it.**

He did not go and ask her. It is entered that he did not go and ask her, on the forty-eighth day of the new month, at about the seventh hour of the evening, in a room over a chandlery with about nine people in it and a woman of forty-one in the next room with a slate on her knee, and that he wrote down that he was not going to, and that the reason he wrote down is that he has been a man who asks for eleven days running about this city and has been wrong about the asking twice and right about it once, and that the once was a question on a stair to a woman of twenty-four and the answer took nine days and four kitchens, and that a review is in two days and there is no time and he is not going to pretend there is no time.

**In two days a page is going to be read out in daylight in a room to about two hundred and forty people, and nine of them will know that the answer to the eleventh question was on the front of a slate three days ago, and a woman of forty-one will hear a question about a person read out in a language she does not speak, and he does not know what she will do, and he is not going to find out by asking her tonight.**

---

**Nothing was gained. A field was refused in under a second and the refusal is the fourth interesting half in seventy-three days and it is not the interesting half. A mechanism was identified a second time in a better coat by a clerk who had four years in a building to be able to say it about his own office. Fifteen words were written on the back of a copying list by a woman of twenty-four and they are the next problem in this city. A girl of seventeen is on a page with a date on it for the first time in this volume and a notary of fifty has told her she is probably safe and has entered that she does not know. Adrian Vale is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, on no roster of the licence and on no roster of the instrument, holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and is party to a trial licence twenty-three days old and is given nothing by it. He performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is seventy-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and one ruled line is left in the column and it is not filled. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired. The ground under the chapel is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items with the price of the salt written under them and a fifth item that is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. A room with no window and no door anybody has found is three knocks and four knocks into being knocked on, both entered and neither deleted, and the second page says in nineteen lines that this city is not asking about it and why. Nine Elms' water is not received. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fourteenth time. March is twelve days off, the season of a field four miles down the Reach road is still unpaid, and the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything.**
