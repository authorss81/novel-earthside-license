# Chapter 0049 — The Working

On the twenty-fourth day of the new month, which was the forty-ninth day of the flood and a Monday and the next tide, forty men of this city went twice under a gate in the rain at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and a man stood four feet back on the sill of the third bay with a folding steel rule and said out loud that he was not in it, and was made to say it twice, and the join in the main breach stepped at the seventh hour and was closed at about the ninth in eleven minutes of decided work, and the residue of a good working at a gate went somewhere that nobody in that gallery found out for about two hours and that was not the chapel, and nobody died and nobody was torn and one man did not come back for the second time under the near gate and there is no entry of it and no fault and nobody went to anybody's door, and a number on the wall of the second bay did not change and is not going to, and a column of costs was entered for the seventh time before the tide turned and not after, and a man's final term ended at noon with a date on it, and a boy of nineteen put a stick of chalk down on a ledge and did not write in a table, and a woman wrote in the fourth volume until about the eleventh hour of the night.

---

**The four went down at about the fourth hour of the morning, at a falling tide, in the wrong weather, with a gauge.**

That is condition two, and it was carried out on this tide before it was carried out on any other, and it took about forty minutes, and the whole of the forty minutes is in the book because the four of them each said what they were looking at and the four statements do not agree and were entered with the disagreement on the page.

The gauge stood in the outer wall of the lock chamber at the second bay with a foot of daylight over the top of the twenty-eighth mark, which is four inches below it, and that is the reading. It is not what it read on the twenty-seventh day of this flood. **Therefore the tide is worked, and the only thing that decided it was a groove cut into a piece of oak nine days ago by a man who did not say why.**

Hettis Roke said what he was looking at, and it is in his own words, and it is four sentences:

"I am looking at a stick with one mark cut on it nine days ago by a man in a hurry who is a better mason than I will ever be, and I do not know why he cut it and nobody has told me, and I have been a Hearthguard of this city for nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years had to decide a working tide off a mark that a man cut that morning.

"And it is the right decision and I made it and I will not be second-guessed about it. And in nine years somebody is going to cut that mark on a Tuesday in a hurry and there will not be four of us standing here, and the sentence at the end of that day will be *the mark was cut*, and there is not one thing in nine pages or six conditions that can tell the two apart, and I have said so in a yard and nobody wrote it down, so I am saying it here and it is being written down."

Tamsin Quill said: "**Then what is it.**"

"It is four people turning up in the wrong weather," said Roke, "and Ovin Sarr said that in a yard on the twenty-first day of the new month and he was right and it is the only instrument in this city that has ever worked, and it is not a gauge, and it is four people, and the gauge is a piece of evidence they are arguing with."

Nell Bray said, in a felt hat: "I have said for nine days that one number is not a number a river keeps and I am not going to stop saying it today, and I am also going to say that Roke is right, and I would like both of those in the book in that order."

Peth Lomas said: "I am the fourth and I am a mason and I am not a crew, and I said so on the twenty-first day before anybody asked, and I want it said that a man who has never been asked anything has no opinion about a mark and I am here because four people is four people and not because I think the water is in a mood."

**And the four did not agree, and the notary entered that they did not agree, and the entry is the reason the route survived the next two years.**

---

**And the post ended at noon in a yard, in daylight, in about four minutes, because a term is a term and a date is a date and neither of those is a question.**

Hettis Roke read it out at about the twelfth hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, in the yard under the lock chapel, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and it took about two minutes, and it is the ninth page of the instrument of the sixteenth day of the new month and the seat of holder of the pull:

> **A seat, readmitted on the sixteenth day of the new month for a final term ending on the forty-ninth day of the flood, with the terms changed: the ground may not begin without the holder of the pull being told of it and asked for, and he is not the answer if he cannot be got. The term ends on the day named on it. It is not renewed by anybody without a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and this page is the date.**

And then nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and a woman of fifty-eight counted them, and Vask entered that she counted them, and that it is the second time in five weeks she has entered a silence with a number on it.

"No room has been convened," said Sera Vail, in the flat voice. "A term does not need a room to end. A room is what a person gets if they want to be renewed, and a person who wants to be renewed has to ask for it in daylight in a room, and nobody has asked, and so the seat is vacant from noon today and **a vacant seat is not a fault**, and there is a page about that from the fourth day of this flood and I read it in a hall in front of two hundred and eleven people and nobody has forgotten it.

"And I am entering that the terms went with it. The ground may not begin without him being told and asked for. That was a term of the seat, and the seat is gone, and so the term is gone, and nobody is to pretend otherwise in nine months because the ground under that chapel is a floor a person could be frightened on."

And Wenna Cray said, from about nine feet back, in about four seconds, and it is in the book:

"**Then somebody had better put him in the form.**"

And the notary said, "Explain it."

"You have got a form on a nail inside that arch with four people turning up twice a day," said Wenna Cray, "and the fourth item says four people and a slate and no name at the top of anything, and the man from another world reads numbers off a wall for a living and has been in that gallery nine times in four days, and if anybody is going to be told that something is about to begin on that floor it should be him, and it should be a question and not a duty, and a question is not a post."

And it was carried in about four minutes, and the form on the nail in the lock chapel arch now has a fifth item, and it is in the keeper's hand and it is her item and not the notary's:

> **Five. That if there is anything to be told about that floor, the person who reads the join-line in this city is told of it and asked whether he will come, and he may say that he will not come, and that answer is entered, and a person who has said no is asked again on a different day and not on the same day. And this is a question and not a duty, and the day it becomes a duty somebody is to take it off this wall in daylight and say so.**

And Vask read it aloud, and said, "That is mine, and it is in my hand, and if it turns into a post I will read it out in a yard," and Adrian Vale said, in English, "**Say so on the fourth page and not in a yard,**" and she said, "Good," and it is entered that he asked for it to be written and not said aloud.

**And the crew went under the near gate at the sixth hour and at the eighth hour, at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, and forty names were on the roll and no name was at the top of it and the wages column was empty, because the city has no money and the city's credit is a field four miles away.**

Nineteen of the day crew and twenty-one of the flood crew. Hesk, who is the foreman and who has said the four words under that gate in his life once before the twenty-first day of the new month, stood on the sill and said them at their ordinary volume and did not say them better than anybody.

Adrian Vale was on the sill of the third bay at about the seventh hour with a folding steel rule and a stub of chalk, four feet back from the wall, in the rain, and a docker of about fifty came down the gallery steps and stood next to him and did not leave.

"Two things," said the docker. "One, you are not in this. Two, say it out loud and say it in the local language."

"I am not in this," Adrian said, in the local language, to about twenty men who could hear it. "I have no part in this working. I am not on the roll, I may not open it, I may not inspect it, I may not sign it and I may not stop it, and I asked to be struck off that roster on the twenty-first day of the new month in my own hand and it took two hours and ten minutes to get accepted and a woman of twenty-four said the sentence that got it accepted and she said it about a page."

"Say the second thing."

"I have a rule and a stub of chalk and I am going to read a number off a wall and say it out loud, and that is all I am going to do today, and I have said so in a room every time anybody has asked me for forty-nine days."

The docker looked at him for about four seconds, and then said, "**There is no reason for me to tell you that, and I am telling you anyway. Hold your rule.**"

And about nine people in that gallery noticed that, and Vask entered that she was one of the nine and that a docker who has been asked a question by a man holding a rule is a woman who has decided something and is not going to say what, and that about nine people in this gallery have spent five weeks wanting that decided thing to be about the light under the chapel, and that it was not, and that is entered.

---

**The join stepped at the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and the step is entered as a fact with the decision behind it written before the number was read, in a mason's own hand, and the mason is the same man who took the prop out of the near gate on the eighteenth day of the new month, and he is a man of thirty-four years who has not given his name and is entered in the book as the man who took the prop out of the near gate, and Vask entered him and Adrian did not ask her to.**

He wrote it at about the seventh hour, before anybody was in the second bay with a rule, and it is in the book in his own hand and it says what he did and the hour he did it, and the entry was read out in a yard afterwards at his request and not at anybody else's.

> **The old staging leg came out of the near gate and the new one went in at the sill, and the sill took nine minutes longer than it should have because we were working to a reading and not to a shape, and I ordered it and I did it and I am not going to pretend I did not think about the pale edge, because I have thought about the pale edge every day since the twenty-fifth day, and I ordered it anyway, and it is in the book before the number.**

And the two sentences about whether he has thought about the pale edge are his and not hers, and both are true, and the second is the one he wanted in the book, and he got it twice.

Wenna Cray was in the gallery and asked, in front of nineteen men, the question she asked on the eighteenth day of the new month, which is that the column has a number in it and the number has a date on it and neither of those is a fault, and that a fault can be given to somebody and a fact can only be carried.

"It is a fact," said Adrian Vale, "and the mason wrote the thing he did in his own hand before I was in the room with a rule, and I did not ask him to, and I want that entered again because it is the second time in nine days and about nine people in this gallery have already decided the number is a boy's."

"And who has decided that the number is a boy?" said Wenna Cray.

Nineteen men in a wet gallery looked at the floor.

"The number is not a boy's," said Adrian Vale. "The number is a wall. **And a crack does not step.**"

---

**And then it was closed, and the closing was not a climax, it was a Tuesday's work.**

Ottoline Vask was at the pale edge with a bar. Tamsin Quill was at the anchor side, which is the side of the second bay where the load goes into the arch, and she had her hands on the stone and she was not saying anything. Hettis Roke was on the step with the hour, and he had a slate, and every eleven minutes or thereabouts he said a number out loud in the local language and it went into the book as it was said.

The pale edge moved in the direction it has moved four times, in measured amounts, and the amounts were called aloud and entered as they were called, and the man with the bar did not do it in one movement.

"**A closed join does not get narrower, it gets dressed,**" said Ottoline Vask, at about the eighth hour, to about thirty people in a wet gallery, and about four of them wrote it down and about nine have said since that they did not need to write it down. "That is the whole of what a mason can tell you about this morning. The edge is not a crack closing. It is a joint being dressed to a line. **And if you have a number on a wall that says eight and seven-eighths, that number is not going to get better this morning, and it is not going to get worse, and it is going to be on that wall for the rest of this world's life, and there is nobody in this city who is going to take it down.**"

**It took about eleven minutes of decided work.** Not a rite, not a held silence, not four hundred years of anything. A bar, a line, a woman calling a number every eleven minutes, nineteen men of the day crew on the sill at their ordinary volume out of the line and not together, twenty-one of the flood crew holding a stage, and a fourth person counting.

The residue went somewhere. Nobody in that gallery knew where for about two hours, and the people who would have known were not in that gallery.

Nobody died. Nobody's skin was opened. Nobody was torn, and the man who did not come back for the second time under the near gate at the eighth hour was not torn, and his name is on the roll and the roll has a column for what has been paid and the column is empty, and **there is no entry of it and no fault and nobody went to anybody's door**, and Renna Ord was seen at about the seventh hour of that evening standing at the top of the two hundred steps in the rain for about four minutes, not going down, and about nine people saw her not going down, and about nine people have said that they understood exactly what the four minutes were and that it was not the second time she had done that in five weeks.

He is called Ferris. He is a gate-hand of about fifty and his reason was the shortest sentence in this city's book, given on the twenty-first day of the new month in a yard of forty-one people: *I have been asked and I have not been asked before and I do not know the difference yet, and I would rather say no for a week and be asked again than say yes for good.*

He was asked again on the twenty-fourth day of the new month at the sixth hour and he was at the near gate at the sixth hour, and he was not at the near gate at the eighth hour, and the design worked exactly as it was built, which is that a man who has not been paid does not come and there is no fault and nobody goes to his door.

Ferris has not been paid. The city has no money. The roll has been made and the cost has been entered seven times and the wages column is empty and will be empty until a season of a field four miles away is settled, and in March it will be settled or it will not be, and if it is not then the shift stops.

Renna Ord entered her own prediction of the twenty-first day of the new month as a prediction and not as a fact, and she entered the figure and the date, and Vask entered the right date beside it, and neither is above the other:

> **As said by Renna Ord on the twenty-first day of the new month: before March, four of the forty will be hurt doing the ordinary work of this lock, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, and it will not be in a working, and no instrument in this world can stop it.**
>
> **As entered by the notary on the twenty-fourth day of the new month: that the figure of four is the figure of a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read; that the date given by her is the twenty-eighth day of the new month; and that the twenty-eighth day of the new month is the fifty-third day of the flood, which is four days from this one and not the nine days she has been saying; and that she has been giving that number out loud since the twenty-fifth day of the flood and has not had it right once, and that she is the plainest arithmetic in this city, and that both of these are entered and neither is above the other.**

And Renna Ord read both entries, in a yard, on the evening of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and said nine words, and they are in the book:

"**I will be proved wrong on that day. I hope to be. Write it down.**"

And Vask entered that a woman who hopes to be proved wrong in her own prediction is a woman who has understood something about a cost column that a notary has understood for twenty-six years and did not have a name for, and that this is the first time in five weeks that anybody in this city has said something to the notary and not about her.

**And the twenty-fourth reading was taken at the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, before the join was dressed, on the same water and at the same hour and with the same edge as the twenty-third, and it is eight and seven-eighths.**

Which is the same.

The step is in the stone and the number is on the wall, and both are true, and the step cannot be looked up and the number can only be taken, and that is the whole difference and it took Vask about nine minutes to say it to nineteen men in the wet.

"There are two ruled lines in that column after the twenty-third and I ruled them both myself about nine days ago and one of them is now filled in, and there is one left," she said. "A ruled line is a fact and a missing line is a fight. And in nine years somebody in a room is going to want to know whether the step or the number is the finding, and the answer is that **a decision can be looked up and a number can only be taken**, and that nobody in this city is going to agree about it and that is not a fault, and that it is a fault, and that both of those are also in this book somewhere and I have read them and I do not know which is right and I have said so."

The docker's *nine* is on the wall of the second bay and it is nine inches across the join toward the near gate and it was written on the twenty-fifth day of this flood, and the boy of nineteen's number is four inches under it and nine inches to the left, in a boy's hand, and there are five chalked marks on that wall.

Both numbers stay. Neither has been moved since the eighteenth day of the new month. It is entered that the number the docker wrote measures a join that has been closed and dressed, and that a number on a wall is not wrong and is not right, and that it is going to be there for the rest of this world's life, and that a person can go and look at it whenever they like, and that this is the only thing in the history of this book that is going to survive it unchanged and nobody has to agree about it.

**And the cost was entered before the tide turned and not after, and it was entered at its new size, and it was the seventh time, and Sera Vail said the sentence she has said six times and then said the other half of it, which she has never said.**

> **And then the cost column, entered at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, before the tide turned, at the size it is now, being the seventh entry of a season of the bottom field and of the whole of the Reed Reach's standing charge returned to a private debtor, and of one day of four men's time taken out of the tide crew's wages, and of the flood season's shift at the ordinary rate, and with no figure on the end of it.**

"There is a rule in this book," said Vail, "that I have said six times and I am going to say it and then I am going to say something I have not said.

"**A column that has been re-entered six times in a month is a column this city can afford.** And the reason is that about four hundred people in this city have never met the man the column is in.

"This is the seventh time. And a column that has been re-entered at its new size seven times in one month is not a column this city can afford. It is a column that is being kept honest. And the difference between those two things is about four hundred people's lives, and it took me four months to work it out and I have entered a season of a field six times in a column and the sixth time I wrote *this city can afford* and I want it in the book that I wrote it.

"And I want it in the book that this is **the first thing in five weeks that I have been able to enter that I am not ashamed of**, and I do not know what to do with that and I have not slept."

And about nine people in that yard said afterwards that they had not known the notary could be ashamed of anything, and Vask entered that she was one of the nine.

**And nobody was told where the residue went until about the eleventh hour of the night, and a man who is not a docker and not a mason and not on the roll stood on a tarpaulin over a hole in a floor because the rule says whoever is nearest, and he put his hand flat on the boards and they were warm.**

The tarpaulin and two new boards are over the ground under the lock chapel and the date is chalked on the underside of the boarding, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of the flood. The binding in the yard says the five words twice a day in the open in any weather by whoever is nearest, and on the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month whoever was nearest at about the ninth hour was a man of about fifty with a barrow who is not on any roll in this city and who has entered in the book that he is a man with a barrow and that he was nearest.

At about the ninth hour of the morning he felt the boards move. He entered that in his own words, in a room above the market, at about the ninth hour of the night, and a girl of seventeen took it down, and the width of the column is entered as a fact for the third time in the same year:

> **The north corner came up about a finger's breadth and went back. No water came up and nothing came out of it. It was not a crack and it did not open. It went back flat and it stayed flat. And I put my hand flat on the boards after it went back and the boards were warm, and I do not know what that means, and I have been nearest to that arch for nine days and I am not going to say what I think it means, because I do not think it, I have just got a warm hand.**

Nobody in this city can say where the residue of a good working at that gate goes. That is not modesty and it is not a limit of what the book knows; it is that a working's residue travels further than the hands that made it can see, and the only honest instrument for it is a form of watching a scar and not a promise about one, and the form has five items on a nail in a chapel arch and the fifth of them was added at noon today.

It was not the chapel. Nothing came out of it, nobody came out of it, and no floor came apart in front of about three hundred and ten people for the second time in fifty days, and the residue of a good working at a gate that had been done by a city and entered in a book went a finger's breadth under a floor and came back and left the wood warm, and that is the whole of what anybody in this city knows about where it went.

Renna Ord said that in the lower gallery at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, in about four seconds, in front of about two hundred and forty people, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is the shortest thing anybody said on that day:

"**That is not a thing we did. That is a thing that did not happen to us.**"

And about four people in that gallery said afterwards that it was luck and not skill, and one of them said it out loud, and Hettis Roke entered the sentence as: *that the residue of a working at a gate three hundred yards from that floor went to that floor and not to some other, and that nothing in six conditions, nine pages, a cost column or a set of terms chose it, and that this city has been getting lucky about it for nine days and has not once asked itself what it has been lucky about.*

And about nine people in that gallery said afterwards that a mason had said something longer and better about the same fact, and Vask entered that she was one of the nine and that the reason she had not said it is that a woman of fifty-eight said it first and better, and that this is the first time in five weeks that a docker has got in first, and that Vask entered the fact and not the feeling because she is fifty years old and has learned it.

**And the main breach is closed, and it did not speak, and the number on the wall did not change, and a boy of nineteen wrote a word in a table and nobody asked him what he decided, and the box for the forty-ninth day of the flood was still empty at about the eleventh hour of the night, and a woman wrote in the fourth volume until the lamp went out.**

The main breach is a join in a gate and the join is dressed to a line and it is closed, and the main breach has never said a word on any page of this book and it said nothing this morning while nine named people of this city stood in a yard in daylight with the right to stop it and did not use it.

Tamsin Quill was at the anchor side of the second bay for about eleven minutes of decided work and has said since that she did not feel a working and has been asked about it four times and has given the same answer four times, which is that a working needs a pattern and a hand and a willing witness and a cost, and that there were four of those and there was nobody in it, and that **a thing with four of the four things and no man in it is not a working, it is forty men doing a Tuesday's job on a named tide, and it closed a join because a Tuesday's job closed it, and I did not do anything.**

That is in the book in her own words and Renna Ord read it out in a yard and about nine people in that yard said that it was the truest sentence anybody had said since the twenty-second day of the new month, and Adrian Vale entered that he had said the same thing in a hall three days ago in fewer words and that a woman of twenty-nine had said it better in eight words, and that the eight words are on the walk-round on the twentieth day of the new month and are: **the ordinary work of that lock is the working that closes the join, and the hand in it is not a man.**

And on the wall of the second bay the docker's *nine* is under the boy's number and both are where they were, and the pale edge is dressed, and the twenty-fourth reading is eight and seven-eighths and there is one ruled line left in the column, and the twenty-fifth reading is not taken, and the person who takes it will be somebody who has not been asked.

At about the eleventh hour of the night of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, in the lower gallery, the lamp was on the third step and a girl of seventeen was on the step below it with the fourth volume open on her knees and Sera Vail was writing, and a boy of nineteen came down the two hundred steps in the rain at about the half hour past the eleventh hour with a stick of chalk in his hand, and the row for the forty-ninth day of the flood was still empty, and it is a Monday's row, and it has been empty for nine days.

He stood in front of it for about four minutes and then he put the chalk down on the ledge, and the reason is his and it is in the book in his own words and it is eleven words long:

"**I wrote that word once. I am not going to be the man who writes it every time.**"

And Renna Ord was in that gallery and has still never been asked what she decided on the thirtieth day of the salt year, when she wrote the word against the thirty-third with a barrow and a stick of chalk, and nobody asked her tonight either, and about nine people in that gallery have said since that they wanted to ask her and that there is something about a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read being asked a question about a table by a room that has wanted something from her for five weeks that stops about nine people, and Renna Ord has entered that she noticed and has entered no reason and that is the only entry of that shape in the fourth volume.

Adrian Vale did not fill it in. He has entered that he did not fill it in, that nobody asked him to on this day, and that he is not going to be asked, and that the row for the forty-ninth says nothing at all and the row for the fiftieth, which is a Tuesday, is empty, and that the last act of this flood is not going to be his and that he has written that sentence in a notebook in four different inks over five weeks and that it is still true on the morning it happened.

**Nothing gained.** A join is closed and he closed nothing. Eleven minutes, and his part in it was standing four feet back on the sill of the third bay with a rule, saying a number out loud in the local language, and a docker told him to hold his rule and he held it, and the black line in the heel of his right palm is forty-eight days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch, and the pale fragment-mark is in a wall in a wall of an outer channel and a thin pale ghost of it runs out toward his wrist and that is all he has and it is not a use.

**The crew is the wall, and the wall is a crew, and in March a woman in a village four miles away has to decide how much of her life to put in the ground**, and the column she is deciding it with was entered for the seventh time at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth day of the new month, before the tide turned, with no figure on the end of it, and it is going to be entered again, and it will be entered again until somebody stops, and the last of it is a page with a date on it, and an empty box with nobody's hand over it, and a woman at a table who has not slept.
