# Chapter 0036 — The Terms of a Rescue

There are six items in the terms of a crossing in this city, and there have been six since the twenty-seventh day of the flood, and they were read in a yard in the rain in front of a hundred and forty people and entered in the book with the hour on them, and they are the same six items, word for word, with one word changed, and the changed word is *before*, and it went from *before the bar goes in* to *when there is time*, and nobody in two hundred and forty people noticed it, and a mason of thirty-four years in this city read the whole of it aloud in the local language and stopped at it and could not say why she had stopped.

---

Adrian wrote them between the seventh and the tenth hour of the evening of the thirty-seventh day of the flood, at a table in the low room by the salt wharf, in a hand that was not a clerk's hand, and he wrote them because a rescue needs terms, and the terms for a rescue already existed, and a man who writes a new document when the right one is in a book gets a new document, and a new document has no disagreements in it.

He had them in front of him. They are on the page. Six items, entered on the twenty-eighth day at the sixth hour, read in a yard in the rain, with Vask reading the mason's part herself because *if something goes wrong in about an hour then these terms are going to be read in a great many voices in a great many yards for a year, and I want them in mine.*

> **One. The standing terms of the instrument are broken in public, tonight, because the instrument is being used before the Concord has ruled on whether it may exist, and the breaking is entered as a breaking and not as a thing that was always permitted.**
>
> **Two. One named living person goes, and the name is given by the person and not chosen for him.**
>
> **Three. The cost is the standing labour in the yard doubled for the day, a season of the bottom field entered against the instrument, and the day of four men's time that yesterday's inspection came out of, entered again because it has now been taken twice.**
>
> **Four. Adrian Vale reads the line and marks it. He is not in the opening. He has no place in it and no vote in it, and if the join does anything he did not expect he is the man who says the number and not the man who decides.**
>
> **Five. If the person does not come back within the hour, the instrument is closed by the four witnesses and no second person is sent, ever.**
>
> **Six. The ground under this chapel is not a place anybody may go, in either direction, and the list on the chapel door stands, and if anybody in this city in the next hundred years uses a thing in this wall to fetch anybody out of the ground under this chapel then the list on the chapel door is read in the yard first, in the open, before the bar goes in.**

He copied all six. He changed the preposition in the third. He wrote the site into the first item in place of the words *at the outer wall of the outer channel of this lock* — which is what the twenty-eighth said, and which was the site then, and which is the site now, and which is available now only in the sense that a wall is available to be looked at.

And he wrote, instead:

> **The site is a dressed joint, described on the twenty-fourth day of the flood in the words of the mason and the anchorwright of this city, entered in this book and not named.**

Which is true.

It is the truest sentence in the document. It is what Ottoline Vask said in a yard on the twenty-fourth day of this flood in front of about sixty people — that the thing under the chapel is twenty-two feet of dressed face running north, a return of nine feet running east, a doorway shape, a patch of the same stone tooled to a different finish, and that she would not name it, and that *a quarter of a thing is not a thing*. It is in the book in her words and Quill's and not summarised, and it is the only description anybody in this city has of the thing under the chapel floor.

And there are two dressed joints in this city. One of them is the seam in the outer wall of the outer channel where a boy of nineteen went in on the twenty-eighth and came out in eleven seconds with a numbered paper, and it is the instrument called the Narrow Mark, and it is continued and unrecognised and may not be used. The other one is under a tarpaulin in the yard of the lock chapel, four feet beyond the threshold slab, and it came open on the twenty-fifth day at the eleventh hour and eleven minutes and took eleven people out of a floor, and it is entered in this book as **SCARRED**, which is the first place in the history of this city entered as damaged and not closable, and the local phrase for it is *a black mark in a place*.

A description is not a place. Adrian knew that. He had it on a wall in his own hand under Vask's own words and he had said in a hall on the thirtieth day that a rule which is fair and small is fair in a room and the room is not where the person is standing. He wrote the truest sentence available to him at about the eighth hour of the evening on the thirty-seventh day of the flood, at a table, with a pen, alone, in about nine minutes, and he did not see it, and he has written the finding up in four paragraphs and the fourth paragraph is the one that matters:

> *I had a site available to me and I did not use it. There is exactly one place in this country through which a person has ever come out of this world, twice, and on both occasions it was the same eighteen inches of dead-straight seam in the outer wall of the outer channel, and on Thursday night I could have written four words that would have sent him two hundred steps down a walk-round to a wall on the outside of the lock.*
>
> *I wrote the description instead, because the description is what I have, and because the site is the instrument, and because the instrument is continued and may not be used and there was no version of Thursday night in which I could put a man in the Narrow Mark without breaking the terms of an instrument four days after a hearing that said no.*
>
> *And a description of a dressed joint is true of two places, and there are two, and one of them is a scar in a place, and the man I sent at about the fourth hour of the morning went to the scar because the scar is the only one of the two that has ever had a person come out of it in front of three hundred and ten witnesses.*
>
> *I did not choose the scar. I chose a true sentence about a joint, in a room, at a table, in about nine minutes, and the sentence did the choosing, and that is the fourth time in thirty-seven days that a true sentence of mine has cost a person something and the fifth time it has cost somebody else.*

---

The pale field came up over the table at about the ninth hour of the evening of the thirty-seventh day, in the air, in the colour of a paper form, and stood there for about nineteen seconds, and three people in the room saw it happen: a man with a mark in his hand who had been in a yard four hours ago asking to be sent, a woman of twenty-nine with a basket who had walked eleven miles that morning, and Adrian.

> **THE TERMS OF A CROSSING MAY BE WRITTEN BY THE PERSON WHO WILL BE CARRIED THROUGH IT.**
>
> **A person who is carried through a working is not answerable for the working.**

It was the best one yet. It was better than *a debt may be closed* and it was better than *a holder who does not come may be replaced*, and it is still the same construction, because the shape is the shape: the sentence is true, and the thing that has gone missing is in the position where a cost goes.

And this one did not put the cost on a field, or on a village, or on a third party. It put it on a man's body, and it did it by taking one word away.

Adrian said no. Owen Park watched him say it and then watched him not say anything for about forty seconds, and then he said, in English, in a room with a woman in it:

"That is the closest any of them have come and I do not think you know what you just refused."

"Tell me."

"Every one of the eleven you have written down is a rule about a *cost*," said Owen Park. "A field. A debt. A village's water. A man's name in a book. And you have spent five weeks learning to look for the thing the sentence has moved out of the way of, and you have got very good at it, and this one has not moved anything out of the way. It has taken something *away*. It has said a thing is free." He shifted his weight off the good leg. "And there is no free thing. That is the whole of what a working is. Mina Okoye said it in a doorway in a yard a fortnight ago and it has been the most useful sentence anybody in this city has produced all month and every one of you has been using it as a description. It is not a description. It is a rule, and the rule is that the price of a working goes *somewhere*, and there is not one door in this world with a hole in the floor of it and the price of that thing is not in the person who walked through."

He looked at his own right hand.

"You have just been told that if I go through that floor then whatever happens to me there is not mine, and it is not anybody's, because I will not be answerable for it and there will be nobody else in the room. And you said no. And I want it entered that you said no in under a second and that you did not have to think about it, and that I do not know why, and that I have been in a room with you for thirty-seven days and it is the only fast no I have ever seen you give."

"I can tell you why," Adrian said.

"Then tell me."

"Because a working with no person in it is a working with no cost in it, and a working with no cost in it is a good day's work at a lock at the top of a tide." He put his hand flat on the table. "On the twenty-fifth day of this flood, nineteen men of the day crew and twenty-one of the flood crew said four words under this city's own near gate, at their ordinary volume, with a count and a cost and four witnesses, and it did the work it was set to do, and nine inches of a join went toward the near gate in the safe direction, and it is the best thing anybody in this city has done in a month, and the price of it arrived under a chapel floor three hundred yards away at a time nobody chose and opened the oldest damaged thing in this river and took eleven people out of a building without asking.

"And there was no person in *that* working. There was no man to be answerable for it. And the residue of it landed in the only place it could find, which was the oldest scar in the river, and it opened it, because a rule that has broken goes to whatever is nearest." He sat down. "That is what it means for a person not to be answerable for a working. It means the working goes on. Somewhere. Without anybody."

Nobody said anything for a while.

"Write that in the terms," said Tamsin Quill, in the local language, and then in English, to him. "Adrian. Write that exact sentence in the terms you are writing, in your own words, with the date on it, and read it out in the yard. It is the fourth item and the fourth item is the only one anybody reads, and I have watched nine drafts of terms in a month and not one of them had a line in it that said *this is the thing that happens*."

So he wrote it in. It is the fifth item in the terms as read, and the second sentence of it, and it is the sentence that the yard heard at about the tenth hour of the evening of the thirty-seventh day of the flood:

> **And it is entered in these terms, in the words of the holder of the pull, that a working with no person answerable in it is a working whose cost lands in the nearest place that can be opened, and that the nearest place that can be opened to this city is under the floor of the yard of the lock chapel, four feet beyond the threshold slab, and that this is entered before the crossing and not after it, and that the man going is told it in his own language by a person who can translate it.**

---

She read the first two items and then stopped.

Ottoline Vask is thirty-four years in Saltwake and has worked the Lock's stone and has refused, in front of a yard, to say what four feet of a thing is, because *a man with a quarter of a thing who says the whole of it is a man selling*. She has written a list on the chapel door of what may not come out of a hole, in her own square hand, in chalk, and item four of that list is *anything that answers*, and she looked at it on the twenty-fifth day at the eleventh hour and eleven minutes and said one word.

She stopped at the sixth item.

There are about two hundred and forty people in that yard. The four witnesses of the instrument are standing in it. There is a family from Long Water at the front of it, and a boy of nineteen who has said the word *lore* in a yard in front of three hundred people and holds the only other mark in the world, and a man of thirty-six with a sling on his right arm who asked in this yard four hours ago to be sent, and a woman of fifty-eight with a bowl who said *no* four hours ago and was outvoted, and a docker with a barrow.

She read the sixth item. She read it correctly, in the local language, at the volume she uses for mason's clauses, which is a volume that has been arguing with builders for thirty-four years.

And then she said:

"That is not the item."

"It is the item," said Adrian. "It is word for word the sixth item of the twenty-eighth, with one word changed."

"It is word for word the sixth item of the twenty-eighth with one word changed and the changed word is the whole of the item, and I am going to say the two sentences so that this yard has both, and then I am going to stop talking, because I have read six terms in this yard in my life and I have never once refused to read one and I am about to and I want the reason on the page before I do it."

She turned round to the yard.

"The twenty-eighth of this month, at the sixth hour, in the rain, at the foot of two hundred steps, a mason of this city read this item, and it said: **the list on the chapel door is read in the yard first, in the open, before the bar goes in.**"

"And tonight it says *when there is time*," said Tamsin Quill, from the front, in English, and then in the local language.

"**When there is time.**" Vask put the sheet down on the trestle. "There is a difference and I am the only person in this yard who is going to notice it and I am going to notice it out loud because there is a woman of thirty-four from Long Water standing at the front of this yard who has been asked to read a word in a book and cannot read, and because there is a man of nineteen standing four feet from her who is going to say the thing I am about to not say.

"Here is the difference and it is the whole of my trade. **A rescue is a working.** That is not a figure of speech, it is the definition and it is the reason the six items exist. A working needs a pattern, and a hand, and a willing witness, and a cost, and it does not matter in the slightest what anybody wants. The list on that door is on that door because the fourth thing on it is *anything that answers*, and I wrote it, and I wrote it for a hole I have been in once before and did not tell anybody about, and the reason the list is read **before the bar goes in** is that a man who has heard a list cannot be surprised by what the bar finds, and a man who has not heard a list is a man who opens a floor on other people's instructions, and that is how a person ends up holding a bar with his whole weight and somebody else's child on his other arm.

"And a floor that has come open once under three hundred and ten people is not a pattern. **It is a hole.** I have said that sentence in a yard in front of six men about a wall under a cap in the third week of this flood and I was right then and I am right now. A pattern is a thing that is known to hold. A hole is a thing that is known to have held once, at a cost, with eleven people out of it and a man who cannot lift his arm.

"And the item I have been asked to read tonight has been changed from *before* to *when there is time*, and *when there is time* is a thing that is said by people who are not going to be under the floor."

She picked the sheet up and held it out to Adrian.

"I will not read this document," she said. "I am putting it down and I am putting my name against it and I am saying the true thing, and the true thing is not that it is wrong. The true thing is that **a rescue is a working and a working needs a pattern, and a hole is not a pattern, and I have been in one.**"

She put it on the trestle and stepped back from it.

"And I want it in the yard now, in my words, before anybody votes, because I am about to be outvoted and I would like the record to show that I said it first and did not say it afterwards."

---

They outvoted her in about four minutes. A family of a missing man and a boy of nineteen and about sixty people who had not understood a word of it and one docker who voted against and was counted.

Eli Mercer voted for it, and he said why, in English, and it is in the book in his words because he asked for it to be in his words:

"I have been in it. On the twenty-eighth, at the sixth hour, in the rain, in eighteen inches of dead-straight seam in the outer wall of this lock, and it took me out of the world and put me in a room and it took eleven seconds and it brought me back and it gave me a piece of paper. And I have been asked nine days running by people in this city whether I was frightened, and I was, and I have not lied about it once.

"A mason who has been in one of these holes and did not tell anybody for thirty-four years has just told two hundred and forty people, in a yard, in the rain, that she was frightened, and did not know what was in it, and has not gone near one since. That is not an argument against sending a man. That is the argument **for** it, and it is the only argument in this yard and it is about the only thing I know anything about. There is a place on the other side of a wall and a man went into it and came back and told us what was in it, and that is the entire body of human knowledge on the subject, and it is eleven seconds long, and it is better than nothing by a very long way."

He stopped.

"And I am voting for it and I am not speaking for anybody, and I have said in a hall that I do not speak for anybody, and I have a mark in my own hand and I am not going up there, and if somebody asks me why I will say the true one: because he asked, in this yard, four hours ago, in front of all of us, and nobody else in this yard has asked for anything in a month."

And Rennet Vaughan stood up at the front and said four sentences, and they were translated twice and both translations are in the book, and the second is the one that is the notary's and not a woman's and has never been traced to a source.

"**I do not want it and I am not going to say that I do not.** My husband went out at nine o'clock at night to open a gate because our water is not where it was and a household at Long Water could not promise its gate. I want him back. I have a boy of nine. I want him back.

"And I want it said in this yard, tonight, by somebody, that the man who is going is not going to Long Water and is not going to be able to fetch him, and that I know that, and that I have known it since about the third hour of the afternoon when a man in a good coat said it to me in my own kitchen and would not let me pretend otherwise, and that he said it to me twice and he came back a second time to say it again, and that I would like that in the record in his words and not mine.

"So I am going to say the rest of it, because a woman of this city is not going to be able to say it for me and I am not going to be able to say it for her. **A man from another world has said in this yard that if there is a person on the other side of that floor then it is a place. And I would like a person, and I would like it to be a person who has not had a night of it, and I have not got anybody else, and I am not ashamed, and this yard can have whatever it wants to think about me afterwards.**"

---

Hettis Roke had a waxed slate under his arm and he had said nothing for about forty minutes, which is not like him, and he read the fifth item at about the eleventh hour of the evening because the fourth witnesses of the instrument were standing in that yard and it is their job and it had not been done.

And then he stopped, before the sixth, and said the sentence.

It is the sentence from the twentieth day of this flood. He read it out at the north door of that yard in the rain with about ninety people in front of him, and it was read back into the book at the time, and it is four words and a clause and it is the sentence that has been on the page of this book more often than any other sentence anybody has said in a yard in a month.

"**Nobody is going down to fetch anybody.**"

There was a noise in the yard.

"Because there is nobody to fetch." He put the slate down on the trestle. "That is the clause. That is how it was written on the twentieth and that is how it is in the book and it has not been amended by anybody and it is not going to be amended tonight by me. And I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to stop, and I would like the notary to have it, because I have been a Hearthguard for nineteen years and I have said a great many things in yards and about a third of them were wrong and I have never once been asked to be responsible for one."

He looked up.

"There is a man in the reed at Long Water. There has been a man in the reed at Long Water for thirty-one hours. And I wrote a sentence on the twentieth of this month that nobody was going down to fetch anybody, and I wrote it about a dig and a bar and about a floor four feet from an arch, and I wrote it because it was true on the twentieth and because I was tired and because there had been nine days of a city asking a man to fetch a girl, and the sentence was the truest thing I had. And there is a family at the front of this yard and a woman of thirty-one who walked eleven miles in the night a week ago and asked a boy a question at the foot of a stair, and the answer to her question was *no*, and she has been carrying that answer for a week, and tonight she is asking for a yes, and I cannot give it to her, and I am not going to pretend that the sentence I wrote on the twentieth is a reason to refuse her, because it is not a reason, it is a *description of a day that is not this day.*"

A woman at the front of the yard said, loudly, in the local language: "Then who is it for?"

And Hettis Roke, who is forty-four and has a waxed slate and nineteen years, looked at about two hundred and forty people in the rain and did not have an answer.

"I do not know," he said. "That is the whole of it. I have written four hundred things in that yard in nineteen years and I do not have a second version of that sentence, and I have been asked by a woman at the front of my own yard who has every right to ask it, and I do not have one, and I am not going to make one up at the eleventh hour of the night on the thirty-seventh day of a flood for the sake of a man I have known for five weeks." He picked the slate up. "Enter that. Enter it in the yard, in those words, with the hour, and put it in the same entry as the terms, and do not let anybody take it out of it, including me."

It is in the book. It is on the same page as the six terms, under them, above the notary's seal, in Hettis Roke's own words with the hour on it, and the page is the page that survives.

---

They read the terms at about the eleventh hour of the evening of the thirty-seventh day, in the yard, in the rain, and the yard was the yard, and the ground under the chapel was covered with a tarpaulin that has been on it since the twenty-fifth, and a mason was sitting beside it with a bar across his knees, and nobody put the bar in anything.

The first use of the mark was not the rescue. That was in the terms and it was in the yard at about the ninth hour before the vote, and Owen Park had asked for it and Adrian had agreed to it in about eleven seconds, and the reason is on the page and it is that a test is cheaper than a rescue, and that a test at the second hour of the morning, at the top of a flood, with a tide table in a gallery with an empty box in it, is a thing this city can afford, and a rescue is a thing it cannot.

Item five as read, then: **the first use of the mark is a test and not a passage, at the second hour of the morning, at the site named, with two named persons of this city, and the mark may not be used for any other purpose until the test has been entered.**

And at about the second hour of the morning of the thirty-seventh day, which is the small hours of the thirty-eighth, Owen Park was at the top of the walk-round.

Two hundred steps down with a half-rotten rail and a low parapet, and at the bottom the outer channel, and in the wall of the outer channel, under the cap of the old landing, eighteen inches long and three fingers wide, dead straight, a closure left by a quorum working on the ninth day of this flood, and the lip of it covered exactly by a thumbnail.

Vask was there with a lamp and a bar and did not touch it. Hettis Roke was on the step above him with a horn he had not blown. A docker with a barrow was at the top of the stair because Renna Ord had said the man with a mark in his hand does not go anywhere alone and had not said it to anybody else.

Owen Park put his right hand flat on the lip of it, with the mark under his hand, and he was the only man in this world who could put a mark on a join, and the join was a closure made by four witnesses in the fourth week of this flood and it is in a book and it is not used.

Nothing happened for about four minutes and a half.

It was not dramatic. That is entered in the book because two people in the book wanted a dramatic account and were refused by the notary, and the notary is right: it was a man standing at the bottom of a wet stair with his hand on a cold stone in the dark at two in the morning, and nothing happened, and then he took his hand off and washed it and said one sentence and came up two hundred steps.

"Adrian," he said, at the top of the stair, in the dark, with a lamp that was not lit. "It is not a door."

"No."

"I want to be very careful about what I am about to say and I want to say it once and I want you to write it down, because I have never in my life been able to say a thing and then not say it, and I am going to try." He took about four seconds. "That is the place I went on the twenty-eighth, through the instrument, in front of a hundred and forty people, with four witnesses and a cost and a fresh inspection in the wrong weather, and it was made by four people and it is in a book. And I have just put my hand on it for four minutes and half and nothing has happened, and I want the number in whatever you are going to write, and the number is *nothing*, and I want it written in the yard on Thursday, in front of everybody, in the same entry as the terms, so that on Friday morning there are two hundred people in this city who know it did not work here.

"And the other thing is the thing I asked for at twelve o'clock and I am going to say it once. **I am not going up there at four in the morning because of a tide.** I am going up there because there is a mark in my hand and a floor four feet from a chapel arch and that is all the material I have, and I have looked at a tide table in a gallery with a man in it who told me that the man who writes the word in that box is the last man in the city who can decide anything, and I have been in this city thirty-seven days and I have learned one thing, and the thing is that a place on this earth opens when somebody works it, and I am going to work it, and it is not going to be a tide, and there is no tide at four in the morning, and I know that, and everybody in the yard knows that, and I am going anyway."

He looked up the bank at the tarpaulin and at the arch and at the dark above it.

"So I am not going to wait for the tide. And when somebody writes a statement of error about it on Friday, and somebody will, because I would, I would like the word *tide* to be in it."
