# Chapter 0045 — What a Route Is For

On the twentieth day of the new month, which was the forty-fifth day of the flood and a Thursday, a woman of twenty-nine who reads Crown Law gave a notary the reason she had not entered a word in a book for two days, and on the same afternoon, two hundred steps down a river wall, an anchorwright and nine dockers showed a man from another world that the join in the Lock of Salt gate can be closed by a city, and told him the price of it, which is a crew, and told him the objection to it, which is a mason's, and on the twenty-first day of the new month, which was the forty-sixth day of the flood and a Friday, two documents were put side by side in a yard in front of about two hundred and forty people, and about ninety of them refused the third instrument in public, and a man argued for it in nine sentences and was right in eight of them, and a person in the room asked whether the crew knew, and nobody in that yard had asked.

---

The reason Tamsin Quill gave Sera Vail on the morning of the twentieth day of the new month, in a room with four people in it, is in the book in her own words, and it took about a minute, and it is that she said it out loud in a room of about two hundred and forty people on the seventeenth day of the new month before she had found out whether the word was hers.

> **I said it in a hall before I had read it in a book. I have read it four times and it is in a Crown procedure of about two hundred years and it is in the third line of the old form of a maintenance oath in a yard in this city, and the yard is the oldest of the two, and I do not yet know which of them it came from.**
>
> **And a word that a woman of twenty-nine says out loud in a room of two hundred and forty people in the local language is a word in this city's mouth from that moment, and I have been in this city all my life and I know what that costs, and it costs about nine months, and I have watched about four people in five weeks find out what that costs and I am not going to be the fifth.**
>
> **So I have not entered it and I have said the request myself and I have said the hour. I would like it entered that I asked and not that I was asked.**

And the notary entered it, and said the two sentences she has said in one form or another on every page of this book for forty days, which are: *that is a reason* and *that is the reason for the reason.* And Adrian Vale was not in that room and was told about it in a yard at about the eleventh hour and wrote down four lines about it, of which three are about a woman of twenty-nine and one is about himself and the one about himself is: *she has spent two days of the little credit she has in this city keeping a word out of a book so that somebody can find out where it came from, and I asked her for two days and I did not know what I was asking for.*

The walk-round is two hundred steps down the outer wall of the outer channel with a low parapet and a half-rotten rail, and it took them about eleven minutes to go down it on the afternoon of the twentieth day of the new month in weather that was not rain and was not not-rain, and the tide was on the ebb and the river smelled of the thing it has smelled of for a month, and eleven people went down it: Tamsin Quill, Adrian Vale, Renna Ord, Nell Bray, Ovin Sarr, Peth Lomas, and four masons and a foreman, and Hesk, who has not gone under the near gate once since the twenty-fifth day of this flood and came down the walk-round on his own account and told nobody.

They stopped at the cap of an old landing where the outer wall is eighteen inches of dressed seam and three fingers wide, closed, in the closure made on the ninth day of this flood, and it is the shape a door would go in and it is not a door and it is not openable and it has carried exactly one person in its life on the twenty-eighth day of this flood at the sixth hour at the top of the flood, and that use was entered as a breach and is not a precedent.

**What Tamsin Quill said at the cap of the old landing, in about nineteen minutes, to eleven people standing in the wrong weather, and it is in the book because Renna Ord asked for it to be and Renna Ord does not keep a record of anything:**

"That is a socket. It is the negative of a shape and it is not a door and it will not open and no amount of money will open it and that is not a failure, it is what it is for.

"And I want a man from another world to look at it for about four minutes, because I am not going to describe it to him twice."

And Adrian Vale looked at it for about four minutes with his right hand on the stone and did not read anything, because there is no join-line in a closed socket, and the seam in that wall is eighteen inches long and three fingers wide and every branch of it is in the same ratio to the mark in his hand as a coin is to the die it was struck from, and the mark is the size of a thumbnail, and a thumbnail laid over the lip at one end of the seam covers it exactly, and he has known that since the ninth day of this flood and has not been able to do anything about it since.

"**Nothing," he said, in the local language, to eleven people, and Tamsin did not correct him. "I cannot read that. There is nothing in it to read. I have been able to read one join-line since the ninth day of this flood and it is nine feet to my left in a gate and this is not it, and the reason it is not it is that a socket is a shape a door would go in and there is no door in it and a join-line is a relationship between a pattern and a place and there is no pattern in a piece of wall that was shut on purpose two hundred years ago.**"

"Good," said Renna Ord. "Then we do not need him for it, and I want that in a book, because I have been waiting nine days for somebody to try to use him for it."

And Tamsin Quill said: "**I did not bring him down here to use him for it. I brought him down here because I am going to tell him something and I want him to be standing next to a thing that cannot be opened, and I want him to look at it while I say it.**"

**What she said was this, and it took about nine minutes, and Ottoline Vask read it back in English afterwards for four masons who would not have had it.**

"The join in the gate can be closed. That is not a hope and it is not a discovery. It is a thing four hundred years of this city have been doing by accident twice a day and it is written down nowhere and there are about nine people alive who could tell you about it.

"Three jurisdictions repeat the same maintenance oath at that lock, and the arch at the chapel yard is where three jurisdictions meet, and that is the whole reason the place is what it is. A rule repeated in one place by more than one body of people, often enough, in the same words, is a **pattern**, and a pattern is the first thing a working needs, and a working needs a pattern and a hand and a willing witness and a cost, and this city has had all four of those at that gate every morning and every evening for four hundred years, and it has been spending them on nothing, because nobody has ever written down what it was for.

"So: the working that closes the join in that gate is the ordinary work of that lock. It is not a new working. It is the same forty men, the same four words, the same ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, on a named tide, in a public gallery, with a cost entered before the tide turns and not after, and with four witnesses and with the receiving community able to stop it, and it is done every tide until the join is shut and then it is done once a month, and it is the ordinary maintenance of a lock with the thing it is actually for written in a book in front of the people who do it.

"**And the hand in that working is not a man. It is the crew.** And there is not one instance of that in this world that anybody has heard of, and I know it is not one, and I have thought about it for nine days and I am going to say the reason and then you are all going to argue with it and I would rather you argued with it on the walk-round than in a yard."

**The argument took about an hour and a half and it happened in four places at once and the four places are in the book, and the finding is entered by Renna Ord in her own words, and the four places are the walk-round, the Salt Weavers' chapel above the river market, the yard under the lock chapel, and a room in which about nine people were not in any of them and are owed an answer.**

**Ottoline Vask's objection is the batch's spine and it is her own and it is on a walk-round in the wrong weather, and it is this.**

"I have been a mason in this city for thirty-four years and I am going to give you the two sentences I have and then I am going to go home.

"**One. A floor that has come open once is not a pattern. It is a hole.** I said that in a yard in the rain on the twelfth day of the new month about a rescue and I was outvoted in four minutes by a family and a boy of nineteen and I was right, and I have not taken it back, and there is a grave behind an ordinary wall because of the other thing that happened in that four minutes, and I am not going to use the dead.

"**Two. The forty words under that near gate are the toll and they are the pattern, and a city cannot spend a thing and be its own foundation at the same time.**"

Nobody in that group said anything for about four seconds.

"So say the rest of it," said Adrian Vale.

"The rest of it is this and it is about four miles from here. **Those forty words are what has been spending the bottom field an hour at a time since the eighteenth day of this flood**, and a man from another world worked that out on that day and said it in a room and nobody in this city had done it in sixteen days, and it is true, and it is in a book.

"And they are also the only reason the place is a pattern. Three jurisdictions, same words, same place, four hundred years, twice a day. Take them out and you have a river with a gate in it. Leave them in and you have the ground of a city.

"And the cost of them is not a season. A season is a number, and the number is entered in a book, and I have entered six of them. **The cost of them is that a hundred and forty acres four miles down that road can no longer promise a gate-opening on a date, and the ditches are level, and the guess has been taken out of the water, and the thing a promise is made of is gone.** And a field does not fail like that. A field fails in March, and everybody knows what a field failing looks like, and this has not failed, the shoots went back under, and the soil is the same, and the *year of certainty* went out of it on an ordinary morning, and there is no season of it, and there will never be a figure on the end of it.

"So you have a city that wants to close a join by doing the thing that has already taken the thing out of a field, and you are going to do it more often than it has ever been done, because you are going to do it on every tide.

"**And I am not saying you cannot. I am saying you should know that you are proposing to make the foundation more expensive on purpose, in writing, in a book, with the price named, and that the price is not money and never will be, and that the four villages whose gates cannot be promised are four miles away and are not in this group.**"

And Peth Lomas said, "That is the best thing anybody has said on this walk-round in thirty years and it is not mine, it is hers, and I have been putting my name under other people's sentences for a fortnight and I would like somebody to ask what that is for."

**Renna Ord named the price and it is a crew, and she does not keep a record and Renna Ord has asked for this one to be entered and has said why.**

"Forty men. Nineteen of the day and twenty-one of the flood. Twice a day, at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, because a docker in a felt hat made us promise that in a ledger on the eighteenth day and I am not going to be the one who breaks it now.

"And I am not going to pretend that is a small thing. **I have had six men hurt since the twenty-fifth day of the flood and none of them in a working.** Four of them carried something with the wrong arm because they could not feel it, and one of them is a boy of nineteen who asked for a rule in a gallery on Tuesday and was not given it the first time, and one of them is a man of fifty-eight who has told four hundred men things and been wrong about ninety of them, and that is not the same list and I am putting them next to each other on purpose.

"And I am telling you what I am going to tell you in a yard on Saturday, so write it down now: **before March, four of the forty will be hurt doing it**, and it will not be four of them at once, and it will not be in a working, and it will be on an ordinary morning, and it will be one of them at a time, and there is not one instrument in this world that can stop that. It is a hold without a witness and the residue of it goes into the person who did it, and that is a woman of mine from another world and it has been in this city's book since the twenty-fifth day of this flood and it is the whole of the argument and it does not need me.

"And every tide, four people have to go and look at it in the wrong weather before anybody does anything. Not a form. Four people, turning up, in the wet, at a falling tide, with a gauge, and if the gauge says what it said on the twenty-seventh day of this flood then four people turn round and come back and the tide does not get worked and nobody is harmed and nothing is entered except that they turned round.

"And I want the other half of it said, because nobody says it: **that is the price of a door in this city and the price is the crew and the crew is the wall.**"

**And then Renna Ord said the sentence that is the answer to the whole of the twentieth day of the new month, and she said it while she was standing on a walk-round with a handrail that is half rotten.**

"There is one thing left and it is the only thing that makes it any good, and I am going to say it and then I am going to go and work.

"**Nobody can open it.**

"Not you. Not me. Not a notary. Not a quorum. Not the Hearthguard and not the Concord together in a room with a document. **Forty men turn up or it does not happen.** And the only way anybody in this city can stop it is nine named people saying one word, and one of the nine is me and I have not been named by anybody, I have named myself, and I want that on a wall and not in a book.

"And that means there is no opening in it, and no day anybody decides anything, and no hour, and no man in it whose business it is. It is a wall that this city goes along, and it goes along it, and on a day when forty men do not turn up, there is no join worked and no scar and no hole and no entry except a line in a book that says forty men did not turn up, and **that is not a fault and it is not a suspense, it is a door with no handle in it, and that is the only reason I am standing on this walk-round instead of in a yard saying no.**"

And Adrian Vale said: "Then I have to accept that I cannot guarantee it."

"Yes," said Renna Ord. "**You have to say that out loud in a room on Saturday and you have to say it before anybody asks you, and if you do not say it then about nine people in that room will spend the whole of the rest of the day deciding whether you knew.**"

And he said: "I am not going to be able to say the rest of it on Saturday in a room of two hundred and forty people."

"Then say the rest of it here," said Tamsin Quill, "and I will be the one who takes it into the room, and I am not doing that, so say it here and say it badly."

So he said it there, on a walk-round, in the wrong weather, to eleven people, and it is in the book because Nell Bray asked for it to be, and Nell Bray does not keep a record:

> **I cannot promise you that it will close. I can promise you what the number is going to be and who is going to read it and what it costs, and I have been the man with the rule in that gallery for forty days and I have never once been able to say to anybody in this city that a thing is going to work.**
>
> **I do not know. That is two words and I have said them twice in a yard in five weeks and I am saying them a third time on a walk-round to eleven people, and about nine of you have stopped walking and I would like all of you to keep walking because I have not finished.**

**The twenty-first day of the new month was a Friday, and the yard under the lock chapel had about two hundred and forty people in it at about the tenth hour, and two documents were put side by side on a trestle under the arch, and about nine people understood what was happening and about two hundred and thirty did not, and that is entered as a fact and not as a failure.**

The first document is the notice of the Registrar of Crossings and Tides of the twenty-eighth day of the flood. It is a form with a seal and a quarterly sitting and a Crown advocate and a stipend and it requires, before the next quarterly sitting, either a registered holder with a Crown-recognised workway and a Crown advocate, or a statement of permanent closure, and it notes that the holder is entered in the roll of strangers as *Unbound*, and that a seat with no workway is not a form that office can accept, and that a position may be filled by a person who can hold it.

"**Those two documents both say the same thing and they do not agree with each other, and the notary put that on the page on the fortieth day of the flood before I had read it, before I had understood it, and before I had been in a room with a man in a good coat, and she was right, and I have written down twice in a notebook that I was going to want to say a notary saw it first, and here it is, and I have said it, and I would like about nine people in this yard to know that the first person in this city who got that right was a woman of fifty who was in a room with about four hundred people in it on the thirty-ninth day of this flood and was sick of being asked to be careful.**

"The two instruments that look like they agree — the Crown's form and the good coat's sentence — **both of them are the same shape, and there are three of that shape in this city in five weeks and only two of them are the Crown's.** The narrow mark in the wall two hundred steps down the walk-round is not one of them and is not the same size as anything and is not a door. The Crown's third instrument of Monday night is one. The Registry's notice is a second. The hundred and thirty words in the fourth volume are a third, and the third one is not from a court and not from an office, and it is the one I was asked about on Wednesday evening in a room above the salt works, and I refused it in this yard last night with the true reason, and the true reason is in a book.

"So: there are three. And a notary has written down that the two that come from Aurel do not agree with each other. And the one that does not come from Aurel is the one I am frightened of, and **I have known what it is since Monday night and I have not got a name for it and I am not going to make one up, because a quarter of a thing is not a thing, and that rule is not mine, it is a mason's, and she said it in a yard on the twenty-fourth day of this flood and a man on a table on the thirty-eighth day gave his life away about two hours after it.**"

**Then ninety people refused the third instrument in public, in the same yard, in about nine minutes, and it is the largest single act of refusal in the history of this book and it is worth nothing, and Vail could not enter it as a refusal of the city and entered it as ninety statements of error, one for each person, and the reason is the fifth clause of the notice of the Registrar of Crossings and Tides, which says that a refusal which costs other people money is a decision about other people, and which Adrian Vale has not read aloud in a yard until that moment, and which he read aloud in about forty seconds, in the local language, out loud, to a room that wanted him to lose.

> "And I did not write that clause. It came in a letter on a sheet of oiled cloth on the twenty-eighth day of this flood and a woman of this city read it out loud in a room by a salt wharf on the afternoon of that day to a dying man because she could read English and he could not say four words.
>
> "**A refusal which costs other people money is a decision about other people.** And about ninety people in this yard have just refused an instrument that has a quarterly stipend in it, and a quarterly stipend is a thing a lot of this city is owed, and I want to ask the notary and everybody else in this yard to wait about ninety seconds while I say the rest of it, and then nobody has to be satisfied.
>
> "**These ninety refusals are not refusals.** They are ninety decisions about a hundred and forty other people in four villages, made in nine minutes, by people who are owed money, about a form that would have paid them, and a decision like that is not a refusal, and I am not saying the ninety are dishonest, and I am not saying the ninety are wrong. I am saying that a refusal a person cannot afford is not a refusal, and about nine people in this yard can prove it tonight, because nine of them have been refused a payment by an office in Aurel in the last five weeks and one of them is me and the whole of a season of a field came back to me on the thirty-sixth day of this flood because a village of nine households said no to a charge.
>
> "And I am not going to be the man who stands in a yard and lets ninety people into a book with a thing they did not do, and I am not going to be the man who stands here and says *do not do that* either, because it is nine minutes old and they are owed money and I am the man who is the private debtor of a season and I have no standing in a room about money.
>
> "**So it goes in as ninety statements of error, and each person has to answer it, and if the answer is that they cannot afford the refusal then the answer is in the book and the city can see it.**"

**Jonah Pike argued for the Crown's form in nine sentences, and he is right in eight of them, and Adrian Vale has entered that he was right in eight and that the room did not notice which one was wrong, and that he noticed.**

"One. A single-author containment closes faster than a committee and everybody in this yard knows it. Two. Eight. And Jonah Pike said, in the local language, in about four words: "**Say that again in a month.**"

And Adrian Vale said: "I will not."
