# Chapter 0047 — The Sentence He Could Not Say

On the twenty-second day of the new month, which was the forty-seventh day of the flood and a Saturday, a man from another world stood up in a hall in front of about two hundred people and said yes to a thing he believes will fail, in a city where a man died inside the last version of that thing on the thirty-eighth day of the flood, and the sentence he said had not been said in a room by him since the thirty-first day of the month, and it was not *no*, and it has never been *no*, and about forty people in that hall had asked him to promise and he could not say the word and a woman of twenty-four took the word off him in English in front of all of them and gave a reason that was about a page, and a woman of forty-one from Silt Row entered the third statement of error in the history of this book against a person and named him and was correct, and a man in a good coat spent two hours being the most useful person in the room and said nothing wrong at all, and at about the tenth hour of the night of that day a man who has said *yes* went down two hundred steps with a lamp and could not sleep and did not go up them.

And then at about the tenth hour of the morning of the twenty-second day of the new month the thing was put to the city, and it took about forty minutes, and the thing was: **whether the holder of the pull will commit himself in a room, in daylight, with his name on a page, to the distributed working at the Lock of Salt** — which is nineteen men of the day crew and twenty-one of the flood crew, twice a day, at the ordinary volume, out of the line and not together, on every tide of the flood season and once a month after it, until the join in the main breach is shut; four named persons inspecting before every tide in the wrong weather with a gauge; the cost entered before the tide turns; a roll with no name at the top of it; a flood-season review with a date on the page before the day; and no single person anywhere, including him, able to open it or to stop it.
