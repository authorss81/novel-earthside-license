# Chapter 0371 — A Sheet With A Day On It And Nothing At The Top Of It

A room over a market had the fifty-sixth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Monday, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and got a hand's width up the wall and stopped there and stayed there, and the cold was in the grain of the bench and came up out of the flagstones by the fourth hour and did not go down again, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet of paper, a pen, a lump of chalk, a slate, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and a coat with a thing in the inside of it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register wrote the day on the door before she started and not after, and then she wrote the day on the sheet, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years puts a day in two places on the same morning, and that the one in chalk is a promise and the one in ink is a fact, and that this morning both of them said the same morning and neither of them said what the day was for.

The sheet was ruled into two. She ruled it herself with the back of the pen because there is no form in four hundred miles with a room on it, and then she folded it twice with a thumb and not with a bone.

This morning is the fifty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and the room it is for is on the sixtieth day of that month, a Friday, and that is four days counted from this morning including this morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a sheet which takes three days arrives warm and a sheet which takes four does not, and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever found out what a warm one is.

There was a day on it and a place on it and nothing else. The place was written out long, the way a place has to be written when it is not a house.

"**The room over the market with nine steps on the stair and a door at the top of them that has the day on it. Come up the nine steps. There is no name on this and there is not going to be one, and the reason is not manners.**"

She stopped there, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has written a line on a wall for about a year and has a piece of chalk in his own pocket that he does not use knows what four lines with a place in them and nothing else is, and that he has read about nine of them, and that nobody has ever asked him to read one out loud.

"The reason is that if you come to that room on that day, then afterward there is nothing whatever in four hundred miles that can say you were there."

"**And that is the whole of what a person gets out of a sheet of paper in this flood, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that it is more than that, and I would like it noticed that I have now said the same thing about every one of these for a month without improving on it once.**"

Nobody said anything about it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been writing these for about a month has just said out loud, in a room with a date on the door, that the price of the thing she writes is that nobody afterwards can do anything for you, and that she has not improved on it since the first one and is not going to.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the nine steps at about the eighth hour and stood at the end of the bench with the flap of the satchel down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living and who has never once improved on a thing anybody said to him took a folded sheet off a table in front of about nine people and did not look at the face of it, which is a thing a man only does when he has been given something to carry and not something to read.

She did not ask him to sign it and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who writes these has never once asked a man to sign one of them and has said out loud on two separate mornings that she was not going to.

"You are not going to open it."

"**Every one of these I have carried I have not opened, and I am not going to open this one, and the reason is not that I am careful. The reason is that a man who has opened them is a man who has read days of a city he was not asked about, and I have been in this city since before the water and I have still not read a day of it.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living was told the thing he was going to do before he did it, and that the woman who told him was the woman who wrote it, and that about nine people in this city have now been told by two different people on the same morning that the price of a sheet of paper is that it does not know who read it.

"**And I would like it noticed that I got that by carrying things and not by being asked for anything, and that a room in this city has been doing the first one to me for a month and has never once done the second.**"

He put the satchel on his shoulder and went down the nine steps, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just said out loud that a thing he has carried has cost him nothing has also said out loud, in the same three sentences, that there is a difference between carrying a thing and being asked for one, and that a room in this city has been doing the first one to him for about a month and has never once done the second.

---

Then Adrian Vale, who was at the shelf behind the table, took something out of the inside of his coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in that room in the mornings for about a fortnight has not brought anything into it, and that what he brought this morning was not a thing he was reading for the room.

It was a sheet of paper folded into four and it was not new. It had been folded and unfolded more times than that. There is a date on the back of it and there are two names on the front of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man took a piece of paper out of the inside of his own coat in a room in daylight with a date on the door and put it flat on the table about nine feet from a sheet that had just gone out with nothing at the top of it, and that neither of those two facts was said out loud by anybody.

He read it from the top down. He did not turn it over. Nobody in that room asked him what it was and nobody in that room was going to, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who was asked one question about what a column is for on the forty-second day of that month in this room is the only person in this city who is not asked anything at all, and that the not-asking is not a courtesy and is not going to become one.

He put it back in the inside of his coat and buttoned it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man put a thing away in a room full of people rather than in a yard or on a road, and that about four of them have worked out that the reason is that a thing in the inside of a coat is the only kind of page in this city that nobody can be shown, and that nobody in that room is going to be the one who says that either.

---

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back where the light does not reach, with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand flat over the face of it, and he said nothing at all for the whole of the morning. Nobody asked him anything and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in the same room for three Mondays in a row without being asked one question has now been in a room where a sheet went out, and has said nothing, and that the not-saying is the whole of what he has ever done in that room and it is not going to change.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket from about the eighth hour and did not take it out, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a piece of chalk in there since the forty-third day of the Short Month has sat through a sheet being written and sent and did not offer it, and that nobody in that room expected him to, and that a man who writes for a living was in a room all morning with a piece of chalk in his hand and nothing in front of him that wanted writing on.

---

The room emptied at about the fifth hour and the flagstones were colder than they had been at the seventh, and about four people who came down that stair have worked out since that a room in this city has been writing sheets with a day on them and nothing at the top of any of them, and that a man in it had a page in his coat with two names on the front of it, and that the room held both of those things on the same table on the same morning, and that nobody who was in it said one word connecting them, and that not one of the nine people who came down that stair is going to.

Nobody in this city has said out loud what the room on the sixtieth day of that month is for. The sheet that went down the nine steps at about the eighth hour did not say it, and a room that has been writing a day in chalk on a door for about a month did not say it, and there is no form in four hundred miles that anybody could be sent to ask, and about four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that four days is the whole of the warning anybody gets and that three of the four of them are gone and the fourth is not going to be used, and that a room over a market is going to fill up at about the seventh hour on Friday and that nobody in this city knows what it is going to be for.
