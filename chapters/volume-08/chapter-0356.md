# Chapter 0356 — A Door She Is Not Asked To Open, And Four Seconds

A step on the low ground in this city had a date in chalk on the wall beside it and nothing above the date, and the light went off the low ground at about the second hour of the afternoon and did not come back until the following morning, and the cold was in the shutters and in the boards of the stair inside, and what was under about four pairs of hands that afternoon was a door, a latch, a pail, and about nine people's washing.

---

The woman of about fifty-two had not opened the door to anybody and had not said she would not, and about four people on that low ground have worked out since that a house behind a wall with a sheet under a stone against it is a house that has decided nothing, and that a house which has decided nothing is the most difficult thing on that wall for anybody with a slate under their arm.

A sheet had gone to the wall on the Friday morning with a date in chalk beside it and nothing above the date, and there are about nine houses behind that wall and a sheet cannot be divided between nine households, and about four people on that low ground have worked out since that this was known to everybody except the person who wrote it, and that it is the only way a question of this kind has ever been asked in this city, and that the asking arrived on a Friday for a room on the Tuesday, and that is four days, and there is no fifth way to do it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came up the step with a slate under her arm and a piece of chalk in her other hand and stood there, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a woman who had a whole morning in front of her chose to stand on a step before she said one word, and that the standing was not a courtesy, it was the whole of what she had come to do, and that she had walked nine hundred paces to stand on it.

---

"You have had four days."

"**You have had two.**"

The woman of about fifty-two did not move from the step, and about four people on that low ground have worked out since that the correction is exactly right and that there are two different counts and both of them are true, and that the sheet went out on the Friday and this is the Sunday, which is two days and no argument, and that the four are counted from the morning it went out including the morning it went out, and that she said neither of those two things because saying them would be doing the arithmetic for the person she was talking to.

"I have had the sheet since Friday. It is on the wall with a date on it. The room you are going to say it in is on the Tuesday. That is the four."

"That is the four, and I am not going to argue with a woman about a day on her own door after nine hundred paces, and I am not going to be beaten about a count by somebody standing on a step." The register-keeper did not put the chalk down. "**The date is the fortieth of that month on the sheet and the forty-first on this wall, and both of them are the correct ones.**"

"Nobody is going to say anything is wrong."

The chalk stayed in the other woman's hand and the register-keeper did not put it down, and about four people on that step have worked out since that a woman who has been refused let the refusal stand for about as long as it takes to fill a pail before answering it at all, and that the answer she gave was about what she was going to do next and not about what she had been asked.

"**Then I am going to say the thing once, and you are going to write it down, and I want that understood before I say it, because I have got nine people behind this door and about four seconds.**"

---

"**A share is a thing I have got. A place is a thing I have not got. You are asking me to put the one into the other, and a person asked to do that has been asked to give up the only one of the two that is theirs.**"

That was about as quick as a person can get a sentence out, and about four people on that low ground have worked out since that it is the fastest anything anybody has said on that wall in a flood, and that it was not said quickly because she had not thought of it, and that it was said quickly because she has had a sheet under a stone against her wall for two days and a date in chalk above it and has had nothing to do in the meantime but know what it was.

There were about nine people in that kitchen and eight of them had not been asked anything in nine years either, and about four of them have worked out since that the two women at the door were the only two people in the room who had been asked anything, and that one of the two had got it out faster than anybody on that wall has ever got anything out and the other one had not asked, and that neither of those is the same as the other, and that this household is not going to be told which one is which.

The register-keeper wrote it. About four people on that low ground have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register wrote down a refusal at the top of a step with a day on it and nothing under the day and no name anywhere on it, and that the price of no name at the top of anything is that nobody can do anything for you, and that the price of no name under this one is that nobody can show afterwards that anybody was ever asked.

The chalk stayed in the other woman's hand the whole time, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register came nine hundred paces with a piece of chalk and did not use it, and that a piece of chalk is the oldest instrument in four hundred miles, and that a date on a wall is the only thing anybody has managed to do with it for about a month.

Then the woman of about fifty-two opened the door herself, which nobody asked her to do, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a woman who has been asked nothing for nine years opened her own door on a Sunday afternoon and did not stand aside, and that the register-keeper went in because she was invited in and not because she had knocked.

---

"I am not going to ask you what your days are."

"**I know you are not, and nobody has ever asked me, and if I say the number to you now then it is the first time in nine years I have said it to anybody, and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

"You are not going to be thanked by me at all."

"**Then do not write the number down.**"

The kitchen had a second room off it with the door open and about four people in there who could hear everything and had not come out, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a household in this flood has an inside and an outside of itself, and that a woman with a slate in the inside of it is a woman who has been let in, and that nobody in the outside of it was asked whether it agreed. About four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-nine came nine hundred paces up a step in this flood with a slate and a piece of chalk and did not knock and did not ask, and that the two of those are the only two things a person can do at a door that are not a form, and that she did not do the first out of politeness and did the second out of something else, and that a door on that low ground was not opened by anybody for anybody all afternoon and that nobody in that kitchen asked it to be.

"Do not write the number down."

"**I have written the day and what you said and nothing else, and there is no name on it, and if a clerk four hundred miles off ever asks me who refused a share I am going to have to say that I do not know, and that is the arrangement and I made it.**"

"You are not going to say what a share is."

The second room off the kitchen had about four people in it and the door was open and every one of them had heard all of it, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a household of about nine has an inside and an outside of itself and that the only thing anybody has ever asked in nine years has been asked on the step and not in either.

"**I am not going to say what a share is.**"

"Why not."

"**Because a person who explains a word to a woman with a slate is showing her she needed telling, and nobody has shown me that in nine years and I am not going to start on a Sunday.**"

---

The kitchen was about eleven feet across and there were two more people in it than there had been at the second hour, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a household gets larger in a flood and does not get smaller, and that the number of people in a house is the one figure in four hundred miles that has never once been entered on a form, and that nobody in this flood is going to ask anybody for it.

The child came in twice and was not spoken to either time, and about nine people in that kitchen have worked out since that a household of about nine has been in a room for about four hours with a woman with a slate and that eight of the nine heard every word of it, and that not one of the nine was asked anything, and that a household is not a queue and a household does not go in a room and come out of it with a day against its name, and that a door on a low ground is not an instrument, and this is the one thing in four hundred miles that is not one.

"Go and tell your father."

"**I am not going to tell my husband anything until I have thought about it for a week, and I will tell him before I do anything else, and that is my half and I have had it for nine years without anybody asking me for it.**"

The register-keeper wrote the day on the slate and nothing under it and put the chalk in her apron and did not clean her shoes, which she always does, and about four people in that kitchen have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register cleaned her shoes in front of nine people at about nine hundred paces a month ago and did not clean them this afternoon, and that both of those are about a person and neither of them is about a form.

---

She went back down the nine hundred paces with the slate under her arm and the day on it and nothing under the day, and Tamsin Quill was at the other end of a lane and was not in that kitchen and has not been in it and is not going to be, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that two women of about twenty-nine in this city have been in the same argument for about a month and have never once been in the same room, and that this afternoon one of them stood on a step and had the whole of it out before the other one had finished setting it up, and the other one is a street away with nine places marked on a sheet in her apron, and that neither of them knows about the other one today and that the not-knowing has kept them both standing for a month.

A share is a household's, and a household is on nine places in about four hundred miles, and a form has a place for it, and the form came up the road on the thirty-sixth day of that month in a satchel and was read out in a room on the fortieth, and the fourth of the four places under its heading is now a thing a woman has said no to out loud on a step, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the wall behind her and nothing above it.
