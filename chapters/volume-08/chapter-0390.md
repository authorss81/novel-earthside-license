# Chapter 0390 — What The Three Of Them Are Carrying, And A Saturday With Nothing On It

A room over a market had the seventy-fifth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Saturday, and about nine people were in it by the ninth and nothing at all happened in it for the whole morning, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and lay along a bench and did not reach the table, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a form on a table, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and a folded sheet of road in the front of an apron that stayed in the front of the apron.

---

A room in this city has been working towards something for about a month and this was the morning it did nothing, and nobody in it decided that. The form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and the second slate was on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people sat on a bench for about five hours and looked at a table.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not in that room and had not been up those nine steps, and the book lives on a shelf and the shelf has been there for about a month, and a woman who has not been in a room on a Saturday in about a month is a woman who has decided what a Saturday is for and has not said so.

Nobody was asked anything. There is no form in four hundred miles with a place for a room that is not asking, and the not-asking has been the arrangement in that room since a Thursday a week ago when a man with a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket said out loud that they were not going to say what the room was for, and nobody argued, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the arrangement has held for eight days and is now the only thing about the room anybody can rely on.

---

She asked one question, and it was not about whether it would go, and it was two words and a verb.

"What will you carry."

The woman of about twenty-one was at the bench with her hands in her apron and the pen with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife in the apron with them, and she had been asked one question in this flood in that room before, and nobody in that room was going to be the second one to ask her anything and nobody after that morning was going to be the second one either, and this one was asked by a different person and it was not the same question and everybody in the room knew the difference between the two of them before it had finished being asked.

"**What I am standing in.**"

She said it without taking the pen out of the apron, and she said it in about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that room worked out since that a woman who has stopped waiting to be told what she is for has just answered a question about luggage, and that the answer has nothing in it at all except a pair of boots and a coat, and that it is the truest thing anybody has said in that room about a road.

Then she said the other half of it, and she said it to the table and not to the woman who had asked her.

"**And I would like it said out loud in this room that nobody is going to write down what the three of us are carrying. Not on a form and not in a book and not on a slate. That is the only way any of us is going to be able to say afterwards that we were not sent.**"

---

Nobody argued with it. The room agreed to it in about four seconds, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and had a piece of chalk in it that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and there is a woman at that bench who could have watched a piece of chalk go onto a slate in about four seconds and did not, and nobody asked her to, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who writes a line on a board for a living has just sat in a room where a piece of chalk was the obvious instrument and did not take his hand out of his pocket, and that the woman who would have had to ask him for it was not going to.

The man of about twenty-five had his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it and did not open it. The man of about forty-eight sat down the moment he came in and had a folding knife with a hinge that does not spring on his knee, and he shut it and opened it about nine times in five hours and did not say anything at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in a great many rooms in this city and has not been asked anything in about nine of them has been in a room for five hours on a Saturday and has not said one word in it, and that nobody in that room found that remarkable and that about four of them found the opposite.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back where the light does not reach, with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and he had been at the back of eleven rooms in a fortnight without being asked one question in any of them, and he had said one sentence in one of them, on a Sunday, about two empty thirds in a room, and about four people in that room had worked out since that the man at the back is not a man who says nothing because he has nothing to say.

---

Tamsin Quill had her hand on the front of her apron the whole morning and did not take the sheet out, and about four people in that room had worked out since that a woman who put a page on a bench in front of about nine people a week ago, and then put it on a step in front of one person on Tuesday, has now kept it in an apron for two days running and is not going to put it anywhere at all.

"It is not going in anything they take."

"**It is not going in anything they take, and it is not going against a wall, and it is not going into a book, and it is not going to be written out on the back of a form, and there is a crease in it that has gone soft and that crease is the only part of this page anybody in four hundred miles is losing.**"

Nobody said anything about that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman has just told a room that the only thing anybody can be shown about her is a fold, and that she has said it flatly, in daylight, with a date on the door, and that she is not going to be asked about the fold by anybody in this city and that the fold is hers and would not go with the page if she gave the page away.

The three of them came down the nine steps at about the second hour of the afternoon and the light was still on the boards at the foot of that market, and the fourth of the four is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and a party of four is four, and nobody who came down that stair on this morning counted anything.

A room over a market spent a Saturday doing nothing and agreed out loud, in about four seconds, that what three people take out of it will not be written down, and the reason is not tidiness and is not modesty, and the price of it is the same as the price of every other thing this room has decided: after this, there is nothing whatever in four hundred miles that can say they were here, or what they had, or who sent them. About four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room which has spent five hours doing nothing has made the last decision anybody in this city is going to make about those three before the road, and that the decision was to leave a blank where the column would go, and that a blank is the only thing in four hundred miles a return cannot be filled in from, and that the room has known that for a month and has only just said it in front of anybody.
