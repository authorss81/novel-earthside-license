# Chapter 0362 — A Number Said By Somebody Else, And The Price Of It

A market in this city had about nine stalls in it under a roof of sailcloth and sacking, and the light came in over the sacking at about the seventh hour and made the whole of it the same grey, and the cold was in the boards of the trestles and in the stone under them and out of the throat of everybody standing in it, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a board with nineteen rates on it, a piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man who keeps the first stall, a lump of chalk on the edge of the trestle, and a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it.

---

The sheet went out of the room over a market before the market opened. It was folded twice with a thumb and not with a bone, and a stone off a doorstep went on top of it, and a satchel with a flap went over the shoulder of the man who was carrying it, and about four people in that market have worked out since that this morning is the forty-seventh day of that month, a Saturday, and the room it is for is on the fifty-first, a Wednesday, and that those are four days counted from this morning including this morning, and that there is no fifth way to do it and there never has been.

It went to a place and not to a house. About four people in that market have worked out since that a place with about nine households behind one wall in it is the only thing in four hundred miles a sheet can be divided by nobody, and that a sheet which goes to a place is read by whoever comes out, and that a notice with a name at the top of it would have been read by about nine people and this one will be read by about four, and that the four are the ones who came.

Nobody has written to that wall in four days, and about four people in this city have worked out since that the four days are not a delay and that a notice has never once been a courtesy in this flood, it has been a distance a person is given to decide in, and that nobody has ever yet used the whole of it.

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the stall nearest the water end had not been able to make a sound since before the light, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a woman who says a figure out loud at the head of her own stall every morning of this flood and cannot say it this morning is a woman who has lost the only thing anybody in this city knows her for, and that the thing she has lost is not her voice.

The man beside her had been saying it for her since the seventh hour.

"About four hundred and forty."

He said it flatly, at the head of her stall, in about the same words she says it in, and he said it about nine times over the morning, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket and has not used it once since the forty-third day of the Short Month has spent a morning saying a number that is not his, out loud, in a market, in about the words of the woman standing next to him.

She put salt on the stall with her hands while he said it and never once looked at him, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a woman who cannot speak and a man who can is the oldest arrangement in the world and that the two of them have had it for about a month and that in this flood it is the first time it has cost anything.

"You are saying it wrong."

"**I have heard it every morning for about a month and I have never once asked you where you got it, and I can say it, and saying it for you is the first thing I have done in this flood that I could not have done without asking you.**"

"You did not ask me."

"**I am not going to now either, and I would like it noticed that I have not, and that a man who has said a number all morning for a woman beside him has not asked her for the number, and that those are two different things and the second one is the reason the first one was possible.**"

Nobody said anything. About four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has asked in advance not to be thanked for anything he has done in about a year has just done the largest thing anybody on that market has done in a month and has not mentioned thanking, and that he is not going to, and that the not-mentioning is the whole of the arrangement and it is older than this flood.

---

Then her voice came back at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it came back the way it comes back, which is all at once and with no warning anybody gets.

"**About four hundred and forty.**"

He stopped in the middle of a number and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who had been saying a figure out loud in a market for about four hours stopped in the middle of it, and that nobody in that market has ever seen a man stop in the middle of anything, and that he put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket while he did it, which is what a man does to a thing he has decided not to take out.

"I have got it back."

"**Then it was never mine and I am glad of it, and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be told about it, and the two of us are going to stand here and say the same number twice a day from now on and nobody in this market is going to say one word about it.**"

"About four hundred and forty."

"**About four hundred and forty.**"

---

The number did not go. That is the whole of what happened on the forty-seventh day of that month at a market in this city, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a woman decided about a month ago that if the figure was only ever said once then the day she stopped was the day it went, and that she stopped this morning, and that it did not go, because a man beside her had said it nine times over four hours without being asked and without wanting anything, and that the belief she has been carrying for a month has just been tested and has come out wrong, and that she has not said so out loud and is not going to.

A man who has carried a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month stood on the other side of a trestle from a lump of chalk about nine inches from his hand and did not look at it, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who said a woman's number for her for four hours this morning has exactly one object in this market that he has decided not to use, and that both of those facts are about a person and neither of them is about salt.

---

The light went off the sacking at about the fourth hour and the boards were swept and the man of about thirty-four wrote his line last in a harder piece of chalk, the way he always does, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has said somebody else's number all morning has still gone to his own board last and in the same chalk, and that the going-last is the only opinion he has ever had about what goes at the top of a thing and what goes at the bottom of it, and that he has never said what it is and that nobody has asked.

A figure said out loud at the head of a stall every morning and a figure put in a column four times a year have never once been in the same room, and about four people in that market have worked out since that they were about nine inches apart this morning with a trestle between them and a man in the middle who said both of them, and that nobody has worked out which of the two is the one that will still be there in nine years, and that it is not a thing anybody has to be thanked for.
