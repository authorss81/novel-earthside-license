# Chapter 0379 — Nine Rows Nobody Can Date

A room over a market had the sixty-fourth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Tuesday, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and did not get past the table, and the cold came out of the boards of the book when it was opened and out of the flags, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a register open at a ruled line, a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people looking at the back of a book.

---

The register has been kept in this city for nine years and it has three columns. The first of the three is for what went out and the second is for what came back and the third has been empty since a Thursday in that month on purpose. About nine people in this city have known that for about a month and not one of them has been asked about it by anybody.

The middle of the book is nine years of it and it is in six hands. A register with three columns and no column for the day a sheet went out cannot tell a question that took four days from one that took nine, and a woman keeping the book found that out on the eighth day of that month and put it down in front of about nine people before anybody had asked her for it. Nobody in this city has improved on it since.

Then she turned to the back.

"There are nine rows back here and I have not looked at them since the eighth day of that month, which is fifty-six days back from this morning, and I am putting the number to it so that a person cannot come back in a week and say it was recent. They are in an instrument this city stopped writing in years ago. I can read the hand and I can read every word of what was asked. I cannot tell you what day any of it happened on."

She had said it in a room in daylight with a date on the door: the only rows in nine years of a book that have a day in them a person could stand behind are nine rows nobody can date. She had not said that before this morning in this room and she had not said it anywhere, and she has been sitting on it for nine years.

---

Nobody copied them down. About nine people looked at nine rows for about a quarter of an hour on a Tuesday morning and not one of them wrote anything. The only writing that happened in that room all morning was a day in chalk on a door and a day on a slate with nothing under it.

"We are not going to copy them."

"Nine rows."

"**A copy has to be dated to be any use to anybody, and not one of those nine can be dated, and a page that cannot be dated is the only kind of page in this city that no office nine hundred miles off can ask to have produced.**"

She has said out loud that the only pages in this city nobody can ask about are the ones nobody can date, and she has put that against another thing without saying a word about doing it: a thing which cannot be found is not the same as a thing which has not been looked for.

"If one of the nine of you writes them out on anything, we have turned the only nine rows in a book in this city that nobody can ask about into nine rows somebody could ask about."

"**And I am not going to be the one who does that, and I would like it said that I said it in one breath and that nobody argued, and that a woman who keeps a book for nine years is the only person in this room who could do it in about a minute with the back of a knife on a slate, and that I have not done it and am not going to.**"

The room agreed to it in about four seconds. A room in this city has just agreed not to do a thing that would have made a page more useful, and this is the first time anybody in four hundred miles has refused a document on the grounds that it could be made to work. Nobody in that room thinks that is a principle, and about four of them think it is about nine rows at the back of a book. About four people in that room have worked out since that a person who refuses a page is still doing the work of an office, and that nobody in four hundred miles can tell the two apart.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket the whole of that quarter of an hour. A man who writes a line on a board for a living was about nine inches from a page that nobody in this city has been able to copy in nine years, and he had a piece of chalk in that pocket and not a pencil, and either of them would have done it, and he did not take anything out. Nobody in that room expected him to, and it is the fifth time in about a month.

The man of about thirty-nine was at the wall by the step at the back with the face of his own slate under his right hand, and he said nothing at all for the whole of it. A man who is right about pages has been at the back of a room for a sixth time in a fortnight and has not been asked one question, and a man who has taken a name off his own board in his own hand about nine years back has been sitting about nine feet from nine rows nobody can date and has not said a word. Nobody in that room was going to ask him why and nobody did.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the book was shut and put back on the shelf it lives on. The third column of a register in this city is still empty on purpose, and the nine rows at the back of it are still nine rows nobody can date, and nobody in this city has written either of those two facts down anywhere. A form with a heading and four places under it is on a bench with the fourth place empty because a man said out loud in this room last Tuesday that he had never filled it in and never gone to find out. Nothing was entered this morning and no name went under any day.

The two things a room in this city has decided not to do in about a month are both about not making a person findable, and the price of that is that nobody in this city is. The four people who have not left are four, and the room has just worked out the whole of what it is doing and has not been asked about it by anybody. A woman who keeps a public register shut a book on a shelf at about the fourth hour on a Tuesday and has not told one person in this city what is in the back of it. About four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that a thing which cannot be found is not the same as a thing which has not been looked for, and that this city has now decided which of those two it can live with.
