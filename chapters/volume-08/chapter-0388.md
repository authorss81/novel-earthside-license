# Chapter 0388 — A Sheet Ruled Into Two And Not Carried Down The Stair

A room over a market had the seventy-third day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along the bench to the table and stopped there, and the cold was in the nib of a pen and in the crease of a sheet of paper that had not been folded yet, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a book open face down, a pen, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and a sheet of paper with two lines ruled on it in ink and nothing written at the top of it.

---

She ruled it herself with the back of the pen, and it took her about as long as it took to fill a pail, and there is no form in four hundred miles with a room on it, and the seven lines of this morning's sheet are the seventh set of lines in this flood that a woman has ruled by hand at that table.

The first six had gone out. The first went out on the thirty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and each of the other five went out four days before the room it was for, and the six rooms have been on six different days, and the six sheets have not been the same number of days apart and nobody in this city could tell you offhand which two were closest. Each of them had gone to a place and not to a house, and each of them had had a day on it and a place on it and nothing at the top of it, and not one of the seven has a name at the top of it and nobody in this city is going to add one.

"The seventh one is for the seventy-eighth day of that month. It goes out in the morning. It has not gone out yet and it is not going out today, and I would like it said out loud in this room that a sheet which is made on one morning and sent on the next is a sheet that has been sat on by about nine people, and that I am not going to pretend that is not what is happening to it."

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and was looking at the two lines on the paper and not at the writing that was not on it yet.

"We are not going to change it."

"**We are not going to change it, and I would like the reason said out loud by whoever wants it said, and the reason is that a thing which changes is a thing somebody can be sent to ask about the difference between, and there is no version of a notice in this city in which anybody four hundred miles off is going to send anybody to ask about a difference between two sheets that say the same thing.**"

Nobody improved on it. A man who writes a line on a board for a living has just told a room in daylight with a date on the door that the reason a city cannot change its own notice is that changing it would make it checkable, and that is not modesty and is not care, and about four people in that room worked out since that the whole of what a notice in this flood is worth is that it cannot be compared with anything, and that a woman who has written six of them has just refused, in one breath, to write a seventh that is better.

"**I have been asked nothing about it, in case anybody in this room was about to and has now decided not to, and I am telling you that I will say the same thing either way and that the reason I am saying it now is that I would rather it be in the room than be a thing I was asked for in a corridor.**"

The room accepted that in about four seconds and nobody said anything about the accepting.

---

She wrote the day on the sheet. The place went on long afterwards, the way a place has to be written when it is not a house, and it took her a while and she did not hurry it, and there was nothing at the top of it, and about four people in that room watched a woman leave the top of a piece of paper empty for the seventh time in about a month and not one of them asked her why, and the reason has been said out loud in that room on six separate mornings and it is that if you come to that room on that day then afterward there is nothing whatever in four hundred miles that can say you were there.

Then she folded it. Twice, with a thumb and not with a bone, the way a person folds a thing that is going to be put against a wall by somebody else.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and he had been at the back of ten rooms in a fortnight without being asked one question in any of them, and he watched a sheet get made and did not say a word, and about four people in that room had worked out since that the only person in it who could have told anybody what the sheet was going to do is the one at the back, and that the room is not going to ask him, and is not going to ask him about anything else either.

---

Then somebody said the thing that is actually wrong with it, and the man who said it was the man of about thirty-four, and he said it before anybody had worked it out, which is a thing he does.

"**We cannot tell anybody what it is for. We said that out loud in this room about a week ago and nobody argued with it, and it is the right thing to have said, and it means that on the seventy-eighth day of that month about nine people will come up nine steps and not one of them will know why, and not one of them can be told, and there is no form in four hundred miles for telling them afterwards that they came for the right thing.**"

The room did not have an answer. A room which has decided out loud that it will not say what it is for has given up the only instrument it has, which is a warning, and a warning that says the same thing six times in a row is a warning that a person can safely ignore, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the seventh of seven notices is going to go out to a place and not to a house with a day and a place and nothing at the top of it, and that about nine people are going to come up a stair on a Tuesday because a sheet was against a wall, and that the whole of what any of them will be able to say afterwards is that they came, and that coming is the only thing about this flood that cannot be taken back off a person and is not worth anything to anybody.

---

The sheet went on the table at about the fourth hour and it did not go down the nine steps, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was not in that room and had not been asked to come, and a sheet that goes down those stairs goes down them in a satchel with a flap by a man who carries things for a living and has never once opened one of them.

The door was shut at the fifth hour with the seventy-third on it in chalk and nothing above it, and the second slate was on the shelf behind the table all day with a date on it and nothing under it, and nobody went near the shelf, and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room in this city has made its seventh notice and has not sent it, and that a notice which is not sent is a room admitting out loud that it does not know who is coming to it, and that the room over a market has admitted that about four times in a fortnight and has got very good at it.
