# Chapter 0351 — A Form Comes Up The Road, And A Notice Goes Out In A Hand

The road came up into this city in one long pull from the low ground and the light was on the water at one end of it and off the low ground early at the other, and the cold was in the shutters of the room at the top and in the boards of the stair, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a satchel with a flap, a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, and a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it.

---

The satchel came up the road in the inside pocket of a man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, and nobody in this city had sent for it, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a thing which arrives without anybody having asked for it arrives because somebody four hundred miles off decided that it should, and that the two of those are not the same thing and that the carrier is the one who has to walk.

He came off the road at about the sixth hour with the flap down and went up the stair with the satchel in his hand and his thumb under the flap, and he did not open it, and the not-opening was not a decision he made that morning. He has carried five sheets in this flood and opened none of them, and this one is the sixth thing he has carried, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living and has never opened one of the six things he has carried in this flood, of which five were notices and one is this, is not a man who has forgotten.

There is a bench along the wall of that room that nine people have been leaning on in turns since the seventh hour, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a bench nobody can sit on in this flood is the only piece of furniture anybody has ever built for a mood, and that nobody has put a date on it.

The stair of the room over a market is nine steps and the ninth one is loud, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man who carries things knows which step on a stair in this city is loud before he has been up it once, and that he went over the ninth one quietly on purpose, and that being quiet about a thing like that is the only reason anybody in this city knows about it.

---

The room was cold until about the seventh hour and then it was not, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the table with her cap off her pen and her second slate on the shelf behind her where it has been, with a date on it and nothing under it.

"Put it on the table and do not open it in front of me."

"**I was not going to open it in front of you or on the road.**"

"Nobody is going to notice anything. Put it down."

He put it down. It was folded twice and the fold had been made with a bone and not a thumb, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a fold made with a bone is a fold made by somebody who handles a great deal of paper and does not like the sound of a crease, and that the person who made it is not in this city and has not been in this city since before the water.

She did not touch it for a while. A woman who reads forms for a living can tell a form by its fold in about four seconds and about four people in that room have worked out since that she did not need the four seconds and that she took the length of a pail instead, and that the taking is a thing a keeper of a public register does so that the room does not get on with it before she is ready.

"**That is a return.**"

"That is a form with four places under a heading on it and nothing written in any of them."

"It is a return, and one of those comes up this road four times a year for a man on the boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of it, and he has filled one in four times a year since the winter before the water, and nobody has ever asked him what it is for." She put the cap back on the pen. "**It is read out in four days, in daylight, with the day on the door, and a thing that is read out is not the thing it was this morning.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who reads forms for a living is a person whose whole trade is being the one who says what a thing is in front of nine people, and that she has spent a fortnight saying what other people's things are and that this morning she has brought one of her own into the room and put it on the table and is not going to say a word about it until Saturday.

A woman who reads forms for a living has said that a page in a satchel is not a page until a room has heard it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this city has spent a month finding out that a room is the only instrument it has, and that the instrument works in daylight and in the local language and with about nine people in it.

---

Then she took a sheet and wrote on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a register wrote four lines and did not read them back, and that a man who carries things for a living copied them out afterwards in her hand, and that he did it in her hand on purpose and said so.

"**You are going to copy it in mine.**"

"**I carry it and do not decide it, and a stranger's hand at the top of a wall is a thing somebody can act on.**"

"**Then copy it, and nothing at the top of it, and no signature, and not my name at the bottom either. The day this city puts a name on a piece of paper is the day the paper is about the name.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that she dictated the whole of it without looking at what she had written, in the manner of a woman who has dictated a great many things in a great many rooms and has never once been shown what any of them looked like, and that the man copying had to keep up with a woman who does not slow down for a man with a pen in a cold room.

Four lines went onto the wall in a hand that was hers. There was a date in it and a place and a thing that would happen, and there was no name anywhere on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the price of no name at the top of a piece of paper is that nobody can do anything for you, and that a notice with a name on it would have been read by about nine people and this one will be read by about four, and that the four are the ones who came.

"Read it back to me."

"**There is a form on the table in the room over a market. It is read out on the fortieth day of that month, in daylight, in the local language, with the day on the door. Come.**"

"That is four lines and it is a question, and it is in writing because a question asked in a room is a thing nine people remember nine different ways, and a question asked in writing on a wall is a thing that four people can all be right about." She did not look at the sheet. "**This morning and the Saturday. Four days, and the four days are the whole of it.**"

"Suppose it went out on the Thursday instead."

The man of about thirty-one did not answer that one straight away, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood and opened none of them has never had a three-day instrument explained to him and has never needed one explained to him, and that the two of those facts are the same fact and that he has not been asked which of them he would rather have.

"Then it is three, and a three-day thing does not fail. It arrives warm."

"You have watched three days not work."

"**I have watched three days arrive.**"

---

The lane behind the market goes up about forty paces and then runs along a wall, and there are about nine houses on the low ground behind that wall, and about four people who have come out of those doors have worked out since that a wall with nine houses behind it is a place and that a sheet left on that wall goes to all nine, and that nine households cannot be divided by four days, and that nobody has ever divided them and that nobody is going to try today.

He went up the lane with the satchel on his shoulder and the sheet in his hand and a stone off a doorstep in the other one, and he put the stone on the step, and he put the sheet against the wall, and he put the stone back on the sheet.

"**There is a form on the table in the room over a market.**"

About four of the nine doors on that wall were shut and about five of them were not, and nobody came out of any of them, and about four people who have come out of those doors since have worked out since that nine households behind one wall is a place and a place cannot be asked a question the way one house can, and that this is the second time this flood that a thing has been true and the first time anybody has written it down.

The light had gone off the low ground about an hour ago and the door was a door and not a person and he had spoken to it anyway, and about four people who have come out of those doors since have worked out since that a man who leaves a paper against a wall and says the contents of it out loud to the wall has told nobody and has done the whole of the telling, and that the two of those are not the same and that he does the second one about nine times a season.

"**It is the fortieth of that month and I am not going to say it twice.**"

He went back down the lane at the pace of a man who has finished, which is a different pace from the one he came up at, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a carrier's two paces are not the same pace going down as coming up and that this is the only trade anybody in four hundred miles has where the work is done at the end and not in the middle.

She was at the bottom of it, in the doorway, with the chalk in her hand and the cap of the pen in her left.

"You copied it in my hand."

"**I copied it in your hand and did not sign it, and I was told not to and did it anyway, because a carrier does not get a decision.**"

"**Nobody in this city is going to thank you for a sheet of paper and I have not thanked you once in this flood.**"

---

He went home with the flap down, and about four people on that road have worked out since that the form is on a table in a room over a market, and that about four people in that room have read the heading on it and none of them has read the four places under it, and that a heading is the easy part of a page and four ruled lines under a heading are the part nobody has looked at.

He said it to the satchel on the stair, because there was nobody else on the stair.

"**I have not opened one of the six things I have carried in this flood and I am not going to open the seventh, and a man who will not open a thing is not a man who cannot read.**"

Nobody wrote it down. A room over a market has a register in it with three columns and a slate on a shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and a form on a table with a heading and four places ruled under it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the day the fourth of those four places is read out is the Saturday, and that a man carrying the fifth sheet up a lane at about the second hour of the afternoon knows that and is not going to say it.
