# Chapter 0368 — Three Barrows, And A Figure Nobody Answers

The boards of a salt wharf in this city are the only flat thing for about four streets and the light comes up off the river on them in one long sheet and does not move all morning, and the wind came over the wall at about the seventh hour and stayed there, and there is nothing high enough anywhere near that wharf to lay a shadow across it, so that the light never goes off anything until the sixth hour, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a tally-board, a knife, three barrows, a coil of rope, and salt.

---

The salt has nowhere to go. Nothing has come off these boards in a cart since before the water, and it has been coming off a barrow every morning of this flood and going up onto the boards at the top end, and the stack at the top end of those boards is now higher than a man and there is only a strip of clear board left at the bottom of them, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man with a tally-board has been cutting salt off a barrow and putting it on a pile that cannot be got off this wharf by anybody, and that he has not said so, and that nobody has asked him.

The river came up over the wall at about the eighth hour the way it does and went back again a foot from where it had been, and the wind took the fine off the top of the stack and put it in about nine people's faces, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who has been working in one long sheet of light that does not move all morning has about five hours of it every day and has never once come off the boards, and that other trades in this flood do not have that and have never had it.

---

There were three barrows on those boards and only one of them belonged to anybody. The man of about thirty-eight who works them had one of them, with his name nowhere on it because barrows in this city do not have names on them, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that the other two have been standing at the bottom end of the boards since before the water with their handles up and their wheels dry, and that a man who has been walking past two barrows every morning for about five weeks has not asked whose they are and has not offered them to anybody.

He worked them himself, one at a time, the way he works, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who could put two men on two barrows and get twice the salt up in half the time has not done it, and that nobody has offered to get on one of them, and that the two of those facts are the same fact and that neither of them is going to change in about a week.

The man of about thirty-nine came along the boards at about the ninth hour with a board of nineteen rates under his arm and the wind coming off the water at him, and he did not stop, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who trades nineteen rates in four markets has come down to the only flat thing for about four streets in a city with nine of them, and that a board of nineteen rates is a useless thing to bring to a pile of salt, and that he brought it.

---

"You have got two barrows standing there doing nothing."

"**I have got two barrows standing there doing nothing and I have been walking past them for about five weeks, and nobody has sent for a second man and I have not asked for one, and I would like it noticed that a man who is not going to ask for a second man this morning is not going to ask tomorrow either.**"

"Nobody has said what they are for."

"**Nobody has said what they are for and I am not going to, and there are three of them on this wharf this morning and one of them has a man on it and that is the entire account anybody is going to get, and the two that are standing at the bottom end are going to be standing at the bottom end next week as well.**"

Nobody said anything about the two barrows, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man has just told two other people standing in a wind that there are three barrows and that he is not going to say what two of them are for, and that he has made a decision in the middle of a wind and has not said it out loud as a decision, and that a decision which is not said out loud as a decision cannot be asked about afterwards.

Then the man of about thirty-nine put his board down on the boards, which nobody in this city has ever seen him do, and stood about nine feet off, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who has carried a board of nineteen rates in four markets for four years has put it down flat on a wet board at the bottom of the river, and that he is not a man who puts things down.

---

The man of about thirty-eight put the barrow down and wiped his hands on his coat and said it to the boards, which is a thing he has not done in about nine years.

"A share with the post taken out has been in the inside of a coat in this city since the thirteenth day of the Long Month, which is the hundred and sixty-eighth day of this flood, and this is the fifty-third day of the Bare Month, and that is exactly two hundred days."

Nobody in earshot said anything. About four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who has been putting a number in a return four times a year since the winter before the water has just said a number out loud about a man who is not on those boards and is not in this city, and that he has not said how he knows, and that nobody has asked him, and that the man it is about has neither said yes nor said no since the day the coat was shut and is not going to today.

The man of about thirty-nine did not look up either, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man with a board of nineteen rates under his arm has carried a figure round four markets for four years and has never once said one out loud where anybody was standing, and that this is the first time anybody can remember a man on those boards saying a figure out loud about somebody he had never said a word to, and that the wind took about a third of it.

"You know what is in it."

"**I know how long it has been in there and I have known that for about a fortnight, and I am not going to tell you how I know, and you are not going to ask me twice.**"

---

He put his board back under his arm and went up the boards at about the fourth hour, and the man of about thirty-eight worked a fifth barrow load up onto a stack that cannot be got off the wharf by anybody, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that there are two barrows standing at the bottom end of about the only flat thing for four streets with their handles up and their wheels dry, and that nobody in this city has said whose they are, and that about four hundred and forty people on eleven miles of flats make the salt that is going up that stack and have never once been asked a question about where it goes, and that the man who has been cutting it off a barrow every morning of this flood has not said either, and that about four people on that wharf have worked out which of the two of them is the one carrying the other, and that neither of them is going to be asked.
