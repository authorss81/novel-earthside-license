# Chapter 0360 — Before The Season Turns, And The Season Is Not A Day

A room over a market had the forty-fifth day of that month in chalk on its door and about nine people in it from about the seventh hour of the morning until about the tenth hour of the evening, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along a bench and then off the bench, and the cold came out of the floor at about the fourth hour, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a slate, a lump of chalk, a form, a deed of about forty years ago with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and a second slate on a shelf with a date on it and nothing under it.

---

A journey has to be made in this flood before the season turns, and the season is not a day, and nobody in four hundred miles has said what it is, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the only instrument this world has for a journey is four people who have not left in two hundred and fifteen days, which is this morning's day less the hundred and forty-fifth, and the day the four of them were written down for the first time is the hundred and forty-fifth of this flood and is on a page in this city.

Nobody in that room said the figure out loud. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has been keeping a number of days in its head for about seven weeks and has never said it in daylight, and that the reason is that a number of days in a mouth is a countdown, and that the only instrument this world has for a journey is four people standing still, and that a countdown turns standing still into a thing that is running out.

The light moved along that bench and off it, and the cold came up out of the flagstones at the fourth hour and stayed, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has a ninth hour in it that nobody uses, and that this one filled it the way a man fills an afternoon, by sitting on a bench and letting a day go past a form.

The form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a document which has been read out in a room in daylight with the day on the door has been in two rooms now and has not been filled in in either of them, and that a document that goes back is the ordinary end of a document and not a defeat, and that this one is not going back yet.

Adrian Vale read the deed. He had it open at the page with the pin in the corner and he read the sheet that was pinned to it from the top down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a sheet on a pin is a copy of an entry in a public register in a town four hundred miles inland, made by a clerk about forty years ago out of an entry a hundred and forty years old, and that it is the only page in four hundred miles that says where a line in an orchard runs, and that nobody in this city can go and look at the page the copy was made from.

The fence at the top of a shut road, four feet off that line, was finished on the thirty-eighth day of that month by a man nobody sent, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the two of those things are not joined and have not been joined and are not going to be, and that a fence is a fence and a page is a page and that four feet is a number a man has in his mouth and not a number he took off anything.

---

The man of about fifty-seven had the length of new rope nobody had used over the back of his chair, and he had not had it over the back of his chair on any of the six days he had been in that room, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a new rope in this flood and never used it has had it over the back of a chair when he means to be in a room for a while.

"There is no third thing."

"There is no third thing in this world and there has not been for about four hundred days, and I have been in nine rooms about that in a fortnight and you have been in six of them, and neither of us has been asked where the line runs." He turned the deed about so that the sheet was the other way up on the table and then turned it back. "**Either somebody walks it and two parties agree where it runs, or nobody walks it and it is wherever the last person who walked it said it was. That is on a page in this city and I am not going to be the one who walks it.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a deed of about forty years ago has been opened in that room four times in about a fortnight and read out loud twice, and that the second of those readings was in a room with nine people in it and a date on the door, and that nobody has ever asked the man of about forty-three, who is not in this city, whether the copy is the entry.

Nobody in that room told him he was wrong, and about four people in that room have worked out since that nobody in it is wrong, and that a man of about fifty-seven is right about most of what he says in a city where a page is the only instrument anybody has, and that being right is what has cost him a winter and a season and an afternoon, and that a man being right and a man being told he is right are two different rooms and this city has only got one of them.

---

Then he said the thing that cost him, and he said it flatly, and he did not put the rope on the table.

"**A page with my own name at the top of it is a page somebody can ask for, and the day after that there is a column, and I have been a column twice in my life and both times it was somebody else's, and I am not going to be a third time in a document I could have said no to.**"

Nobody said anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who bought half a holding about forty years ago and cannot show anybody where the half is has just told nine people what happens after the first line of a page, and that he has told them in a room with a date on the door and not in a yard and not on a stair, and that he did not ask for anything when he said it and has not asked for anything since he got here about a fortnight ago.

She had the book open on the table with her cap off the pen in her left hand, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the third column of that book has been empty since the thirty-first day of that month on purpose, and that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years said in this room on that day that she would walk out of it rather than fill it, and that she is not going to fill it and nobody has asked her to.

"**I am not going to be asked about the fourth column and I am not going to be asked about the third one, and if anybody asks me about either I will leave the book open on the table and go out of the room, and I said that in here on the thirty-first day of that month.**"

---

The last hour of that day, and Adrian Vale said the shortest true thing he has said in about two hundred rooms in this city, and he said it to the table and not to anybody, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he said it standing up and that he had read the deed and the pinned sheet and the empty column and the slate on the shelf all day and had put none of them into it.

"**A journey made by three people produces nothing that anybody can send. There is no form in four hundred miles for saying who is going on it.**"

Nobody acted on it. Nobody was asked whether it would go. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has said the true half of a thing out loud in a room with a date on the door and has stopped where the true half stops, and that the other half of it is that the only instrument this world has for a journey is four people standing in a room, and that a person standing in a room is a person on a page, and that he has just said the first of those two out loud and has not said the second, and that nobody in that room is going to ask him for it.

---

She wrote on the slate in chalk at about the ninth hour of the evening, in front of nine people, and put the day on it and nothing under the day, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register has now done that three times inside about a fortnight, and that it is the whole of the vocabulary a fact has in this city, and that the other two of the three were the same shape and none of the three of them says anything to anybody.

"There is one more sheet going out on the Saturday for the Wednesday, and there is one after that on a Monday for the Friday, and that one is the one that matters and I have not written it yet and I am not going to write it in this room on a Thursday night with nine people in it who have just heard a man say that there is no form for saying who is going."

Nobody in that room asked to be in it. The room emptied at about the tenth hour, the form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, the deed went back into a coat with the pin still in the corner of it, the second slate went back on the shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and about four people who came down that stair have worked out since that a fence of sixteen willow posts is finished at the top of a shut road, and that a man nobody sent put it up and has not been asked about it once in three mornings, and that he is not going to be, and that three people are going to have to go down a road with a barrow each, and that a barrow is heavy and a road is a road and the season is not a day.
