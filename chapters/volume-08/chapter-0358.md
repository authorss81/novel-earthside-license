# Chapter 0358 — Nine Hours, And Nobody Asks Anybody Anything

A room over a market had the forty-third day of that month in chalk on its door and about nine people in it from about the sixth hour of the morning until about the ninth hour of the evening, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went past the middle of the floor and off it again, and the cold came up out of the flagstones at about the fourth hour and stayed, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it, and about nine people.

---

The form had been on the table since the Saturday and had three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and about four people in that room have worked out since yesterday that the man who cut salt off a barrow on a set of boards at the bottom of this city answered a question yesterday that the woman standing at this table asked him, and that the answer is not in this room, and that the woman who asked it is.

A woman of about twenty-one had been written into this room and she had come up the stair at about the seventh hour with a pen in her hand with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth of them, and that she has stopped waiting to be told what she is for, and that a person who has stopped waiting is the only person in this city who is not owed anything by anybody.

She is one of four. Three of them said yes in their own words, in three rooms, on three days, and about four people in that room have worked out since that three yeses in three different words from three different people is the only document this flood has produced that nobody in it has a copy of, and that the fourth of the four is not in this room and has not been asked to be in it and is not going to be, and that a party of four is four, and that the fourth of them said what she said in a room with a date on the door and that is where she said it and that nobody in this room is going to repeat it.

A room with a day on the door and about nine people in it that has run out of forms does not empty, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a room in this flood empties when somebody is asked something they have to think about, and that nobody in that room was asked anything for nine hours, and that the door at the back with no name on it was the only thing in it that moved.

The man of about twenty-five was not in the room either. He had nine words in the inside of his coat that he wrote himself and had not said in two rooms, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who is written into a sheet and has said yes in his own words and then written himself nine words and put them in his coat is a man who has a page of his own that nobody in this city can ask for, and that he has not opened his coat in front of about nine people and is not going to.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came in at about the second hour of the afternoon with the slate under her arm and went out at about the fourth hour, and about four people in that room have worked out since that her coming and going is the only clock in it, and that a woman who has been in six rooms in about a fortnight and stayed in five of them came in, looked at four places on a form, and left, and that she did not sit down.

"The fourth of them is a share and it is a household's."

"I know what the fourth of them is. I was not in this room on the Saturday and I have not asked anybody what was said on the Saturday and I am not going to."

"You have not asked me."

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in it have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register for nine years has been asked about a Saturday by a man in her own room and has said no in three words, and that the three words were the same three words a woman of about fifty-two said on a step on Sunday and that neither of them knows the other's.

"**I have not asked you and I have not asked anybody else, and I have been in two rooms this week with a form in them and I have not asked one question about it, and that is not a position I am proud of.**"

"Nobody in this room is going to ask you about the Saturday either."

"**Then we will both be here all day saying nothing about the most interesting thing in it, and about four people in this room have noticed that, and about four of them have worked out that neither of us is going to be the one who starts.**"

She looked at Tamsin Quill for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then she went out at about the fourth hour, and the door stayed open behind her for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then it was shut, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has not been thanked for anything in a month left a room without being thanked for walking nine hundred paces in the other direction yesterday, and that it was the same woman standing in both of them and that the arrangement is working.

---

The middle of the day is the part of it that about four people who have worked in rooms in this city find hardest, and about four of them have worked out since that a room with nothing in it is very loud at about the second hour in the afternoon, and that this room had a form in it and nine people in it and nothing whatever to do, and that is worse, and that nobody in it said so.

The man of about thirty-eight came up the outside stair at about the tenth hour of the morning and stopped on the step at the back with no name on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who cut salt off a barrow all morning yesterday came up a stair this morning and did not come in, and that he had a slate in his hand and had not had one in his hand on the boards.

"Were you in this room on the Saturday."

"**No.**"

"Right."

"**I have answered one question and I am not going to say anything else about the Saturday, and I want you to know before you go up those stairs that you asked me that because somebody asked you something yesterday, and that I worked that out in about four seconds, and that I am not going to be a person who passes it on to the next one.**"

He looked at the step for a moment. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has never been asked anything in nine years asked the first question anybody in this city heard him ask, and that he asked it to a woman who was not in the room, and that she told him what it was, and that neither of them is going to say anything else about it, and that this is the second day of a thing which has no page on it anywhere.

He did not come in. He went back down the outside stair and the boards at the bottom of this city were wet and loud and he went along them and cut salt off a barrow at about the eleventh hour.

---

Nine hours is a long time in a room with about nine people in it and a form on the table that three of the four places under a heading cannot be filled, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has spent a month refusing to say anything has not been given anything to say this morning, and that this is the fifth such morning in a row, and that the woman of about twenty-one with the pen ground narrow wrote nothing on any of them, and that nobody watched her not write it and nobody has said one word to her about it, and that not-watching is the whole of what a room can do for a person and that nobody in this city has put it in a form.

Tamsin Quill did not ask her about the ninth line. Tamsin Quill did not offer the folded sheet to anybody, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been in a documented check with a man since the seventeenth day of the Short Month has carried nine places on a sheet in her apron into two rooms this week and has not taken it out in either of them, and that the not-taking is not a courtesy and is not a mood.

The room emptied at about the ninth hour and about four people who had come up the stair stood on the step at the back with no name on it, and about four of them have worked out since that nine hours went past in that room with a form on the table that nobody could fill and a sheet of road in an apron that nobody was given, and that not one person in nine hours asked one other person one question, and that the room over a market now has a day on its door that means a thing nobody can act on, and that the two women of about twenty-nine in this city have been in the same argument for about a month and have still never once been in the same room, and that one of them came in at the second hour and went out at the fourth and the other one was there all day and has not left the step.
