# Chapter 0397 — A Fence, And A Wall With Nine Boards Gone Off It

A shut road out of this city has a fence along the top of it and the fence has been finished for about a month, and the light came up that road on the eighty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over, and the wind was up the same road in pushes, and the cold was out of the north and out of eleven withies that are still bound with the same cord, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart, a coil of rope, a length of new rope nobody has used, and a barrow with a hurdle of hazel rods in the bottom of it.

---

Sixteen posts, and the last three of them went into the ground on the thirty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and nine of them went in on a Saturday, and four more on the Monday after that, which made thirteen, and a man of about fifty-seven walked the length of the fence in the cold on the forty-ninth day of that month with his hand flat on every post and got the same figure the man who built it has been carrying in his head, said it out loud to nobody, and has never once been asked how he knew it.

The eleven withies are bound with the same cord. That cord is on the head of a hazel mallet in a handcart at the bottom of that road, and a piece of cord is the only thing in this city that is on a fence and in a cart at the same time, and that it is a fact about a piece of cord and is not joined to anything.

Nobody comes up that road to look at the fence. It has been standing about a month, and it is the first thing on that road, and nobody who has come to it has been told what it is for.

---

The man of about fifty-nine came up it at about the seventh hour with his barrow and had the hurdle in the bottom of it because a man brings a thing he made up a road with him, and he put the barrow down on the low side at the fence and did not pick it up again, and a man who walks a road four hundred miles long has come to the top of it, put his barrow down, and stopped, which is the whole of what he has ever done on a road.

Then he looked down the road and there were three people on it.

"**There are three people on this road going the other way from me and I have been up here before and there has never been anybody on it at all, and I am not going to go after them and I am not going to ask who they are, and I would like it noticed that I have been up this road more than once in nine years and this is the first time there has been anybody on it and the first time I have not wanted to know.**"

Nobody asked him. A man who walks a road four hundred miles long has just said the truest thing anybody has said on that lane in about a fortnight and nobody improved on it, and the thing he is not asking about is three people with a barrow each and a fourth person who is not on the road and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and that a party of four is four, and that none of that is on a page.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was on the low side of the fence with a length of new rope over his arm that he has never used, and the man of about sixty came up the road behind the handcart at about the eighth hour with the mallet in the cart and the coil of rope on top of it, and neither of them slowed down and neither of them asked the other one anything, and that has been the arrangement for about a fortnight.

The man of about sixty put the cart down at the top of that road and stood in front of his own work and looked at the three of them going down below him.

"You have not told me what it is for either."

"**Nobody in four hundred miles has ever asked me what that is for and I have never said and I am not going to be asked now, and I would like it said out loud in this lane that there are three people on that road past it and I have not been told who and I am not going to ask, and that is the first thing I have ever wanted to know about a thing I made and I am not going to find it out.**"

Nobody said anything. A man who has been putting posts into the ground for three mornings and finished on a Thursday and has been back up that road more than once since has just told a lane that he has begun to want to know what his own work is for, and that he is not going to find out, and a man who does not ask is not a man who does not care and that the two men standing at the top of that road have both been right about most of what they have said in a city where a page is the only instrument anybody has, and that being right is what has cost one of them a winter and a certainty and an afternoon and is going to cost him a fourth thing this morning and it is not a thing anybody can put on a form.

Then the man of about fifty-seven said the thing he came up to say, and he said it to the fence and not to the man nine feet off him.

"**A man who built a thing nobody sent him for has just said out loud that he wants to know what it is for. I have wanted to know where it is since before he started and I am not going to be told, and neither of us is going to be asked, and that is the arrangement and it has cost me what it has cost me and it is not going to be settled this flood by either of us.**"

---

They came down off that road into a lane past two gates in the afternoon, and the lane is about nine houses long and does not go anywhere anybody needs it to go, and down one side of it there is a wall with a shape on it that used to be something.

Nine boards came off that wall on the thirty-second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and about four inches of dry stone is at the foot of it where the wet does not reach, and a wall with nine boards gone off it and about four inches of dry stone at the foot of it is a wall that has been emptied and not a wall that has been written on, and that nobody in this city has ever said whose the nine boards were and nobody is going to, and that there is not a barrow in this city with anything on it and there is not going to be one.

The woman of about fifty who keeps the shut gate at the end of that lane had the brass key on a tarred cord in her apron pocket and she came out at about the fourth hour and wrote a date on the post and did not rub anything out, and she said four words out loud to the post and the four of them were a date and they were not for anybody, and she has said a date out loud to that post every morning since before the water and has never once said one to a person.

The man of about fifty-nine went past the wall with his barrow and put his hand flat on the part of the shape that has gone softest as he went by, and did not stop, and nobody on that lane asked him about it, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a shut road out of this city has a fence on it that a man nobody sent put up and that a lane below it has a wall with nine boards gone off it that nobody has said a word about, and that the two of those are a fence and a wall and that this city has now spent a month finishing one of them and emptying the other, and that the three people who went down the road on the seventy-sixth day of that month are six days out of this city and are on no page in four hundred miles, and that the fence is the first thing anybody leaving this city will come to and that nobody is going to tell them what it is for.
