# Chapter 0380 — Nine Places On A Bench, And About Nine People

A room over a market had the sixty-fifth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Wednesday, and about nine people in it by about the eighth, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and lay along the bench from the wall to the middle of the table, and the cold was in the crease of a sheet of paper that had been folded in four for a month, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, the second slate off the shelf with its date on it and nothing under it, a folded sheet of road in an apron, and about nine people.

---

Tamsin Quill took it out of her apron and put it on the bench, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has carried a page in the front of an apron for about a month put it down flat on a bench in the middle of a room in daylight with the day on the door, and that she did not hand it round, and that she put it down about the same way a person puts a plate down.

It is one sheet with about nine places on it and it is folded in four and the four creases are in the same four places, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a page nobody in this city has seen except nine people on the boards at the foot of a market in the open was on a bench in a room long enough for about nine people to look at all of it, and that about nine people looked at it, and that not one of the nine said one word about it while it was there.

She unfolded it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a sheet which has been folded in four every morning for a month was unfolded on a bench in that room for the second time in this flood, and that the first time it was unfolded it was on the boards at the foot of nine steps in the open, and that about four of the nine people in this room were on those boards that morning, and that not one of the four has said so out loud.

---

"About nine places on it."

Nobody said anything.

"**There are about nine of you in this room. Those are two nines and they are not the same nine, and I am the only person in this city who drew that sheet and I am telling you the two nines are not the same nine before any one of you works it out and starts asking me which nine is which, because I have been asked nothing about it for about a month and I am not going to be asked about it now.**"

Nobody improved on that. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman has said out loud, in one breath, in a room in daylight with a date on the door, that two numbers in one room are not the same number, and that she said it before anybody thought of asking, and that she said it flatly and did not stop afterwards, and that about four of the nine in that room have worked out since that a person who has carried a page in an apron for a month and has not offered it to anybody has this morning offered it to nine people at once by putting it down, and that that is a different act and it is hers and nobody is going to argue with it.

Then she said the other thing, and she said it to the sheet and not to the room.

"**It is not going to anybody. It is not going to be written out on anything, and it is not going into a form, and it is not going to a man who fills in four places four times a year, and there is no office in four hundred miles that is going to be sent it.**"

Nobody in that room said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man with a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket has just heard a page in this city described as something that does not go into a form, and that he did not take the chalk out, and that about four of the nine in that room have worked out that the woman who said it looked at nobody in particular when she began it and at him in particular when she stopped, and that both of those are the same sentence.

"I have been offered a way to give this to somebody since about the time the fence was finished."

"**And I have not taken it and I am not going to, and the reason is that the moment this is on a page in front of somebody else, then the nine places on it are nine places somebody else has, and I drew them and they are mine, and that is the only thing in this flood I have that nobody has sent for.**"

---

The room accepted it in about four seconds and nobody said anything about the accepting, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has just been given a refusal out loud by the person it belongs to, and that the refusal was not a refusal of a form and was not a refusal of an office, and that it was the first refusal in this city in about a month that nobody went out and told the other person about, and that the sheet went back into the apron folded in four with the same four creases, and that about four people in that room have worked out that a crease which went soft on a Wednesday morning about a week ago went into an apron that morning anyway and is still soft, and that nobody in that room said a word about that either.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket for the whole of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who keeps the first stall sat through a page being put on a bench and taken off it, with the chalk still in his pocket where it has been since the forty-third day of the Short Month,, and did not offer to write it out, and was not thanked, and did not ask.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall at the back where the light does not reach, with his own slate flat on his knee and his hand on the face of it, and he said nothing at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who is right about pages has been at the back of six rooms in a fortnight and has not been asked one question, and that this morning a page with about nine places on it was put on a bench about nine feet from where he was standing and nobody in that room asked him whether it should be on a page.

The man of about twenty-five had his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it and about four people in that room have worked out since that he has nine words in the inside of that coat that he wrote himself and has not said in three rooms, and that about four of the nine in that room have worked out since that a man with a page of his own in his coat does not ask a woman to hand hers over, and that that is the whole of the arrangement between the two of them and it is older than this flood.

---

The room emptied at about the fifth hour and the bench was where it had been at the seventh and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room in this city has a form on a table that is going out on somebody else's calendar with a name at the top of it, and a page in an apron that is going nowhere with no name at the top of it, and a book on a shelf whose third column has been empty on purpose since a Thursday in that month and whose nine rows at the back cannot be dated by anybody, and that those are three documents and that not one of the three can be produced by anybody who was not in that room, and that about four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that three of the four who have not left are three people who said yes in their own words in three rooms on three days, and that none of them has been asked a second question about it by anybody since, and that the fourth of the four is not a hole in a party of four and is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and that nobody who came down those nine steps counted anything, and that about four of them have worked out that a city which cannot show one person on a road is going to go on not being able to, and that a sheet in an apron is not a road and is not going to be turned into one by anybody in this city.
