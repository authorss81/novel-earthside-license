# Chapter 0382 — Three Barrows And A Handcart, In Rain

The boards at the foot of a market in this city are not boards to stand a cart on, and the light came off the water in one flat grey sheet at about the seventh hour on the sixty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and came off it again before the eighth, and the cold came up out of the boards themselves rather than off the air, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a rope, a lump of chalk on the edge of a trestle, a tally-board, a knife, three barrows with their handles up, and a handcart with a hazel mallet in it whose head is bound in cord.

---

The rain came at about the second hour and it came across the boards from the water and not down, so that it went into people's faces and under the sacking at the same time and made a market of about nine stalls into a market of about nine wet mats. Nothing in this city is built for rain coming in sideways, and the boards at the foot of a market are the flattest thing for about four streets, and a flat thing in a wind is a thing that collects it.

The man of about thirty-eight worked his own barrow with his left hand on the shaft the way he had worked it every morning of this flood, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the end of the rope because a man who has carried things for a living knows what a rope is for, and neither of them said anything about the arrangement while they were making it.

The two barrows that belong to nobody were at the bottom end of the wharf boards with their handles up and their wheels dry, and they had been there since before the water, and about four people on those boards had worked out since that a man who has walked past two barrows every morning for about seven weeks has not asked whose they are and has not offered them to anybody, and that on this Friday he was the one offering them.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came to the bottom end of those boards for the two barrows that were not his and stood at that end of them with his knife in his hand, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that end of the boards as well, and the two of them had not asked each other one question about anything in about nine years.

"You are not going to say whose those two are."

"**I am not going to say whose those two are, and I have never once said, and I am not going to start on a Friday, and I am going to push them up these boards this morning and that is the whole of what I have to say about them.**"

He put his hand on the nearer one and turned it over. There was nothing on it. A barrow in this city has nothing on it, because a barrow in this city is a thing with a handle and a wheel and a man who owns it, and a man who owns a barrow does not write his name on it, and the two of them at the bottom end had not been touched by anybody at all.

The man of about thirty-eight said the second one with his own barrow four feet behind him and his eyes on the wheels, and he had come down to that end of the boards to ask and was going to be answered in his own hearing or not at all.

"The wheels are dry."

"**The wheels are dry and they have been since before the water, and a wheel that has been dry for that long is a wheel nobody has had to use, and I have worked out standing at this end of these boards that there is exactly one thing in four hundred miles a person can take without anybody being able to say they took it off somebody, and it is a barrow that has not been used.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the head of the boards with his hand on nothing, and the man of about thirty-nine came along them at about the third hour with a board of nineteen rates under his arm and the rain going past both of them at once, and he did not stop, and a man who trades nineteen rates has no reason to be at the bottom end of a wet wharf and he was at the bottom end of a wet wharf.

The man of about thirty-four put his right hand on the shaft of the first one.

Nobody said anything about which hand that was. The man of about thirty-four has carried a stall up and down nine steps every morning of this flood and has opened trestles with a hand that has to be a right hand, and a man with a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket has taken the weight of a barrow on a wet board on a Friday morning with the right one and did not say why, and the man of about thirty-eight, who has been working boards for nine years, did not ask him why, and between the two of them the question of who was going to suffer was settled in the time it took to say nothing about it.

---

They got the first one up the boards at about the third hour and the second one would not go up wet, and the reason it would not go up was not the weight of it. A barrow on a board goes up on its wheel if the board is dry and it goes up on its shaft if the board is not, and the shaft takes the load into whoever is holding it, and the man of about thirty-four held the second one for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then put it down in the water and stood there with his right arm across his chest.

"You have to go up on the shaft in this."

"**I know how to go up on the shaft in this. I have gone up on the shaft in this every morning since before the water and there is one of me.**"

The man of about thirty-eight went up the second one himself and did not say that he had offered and did not say that he had not, and the rain was on both of them and the man of about thirty-four stood at the head of the boards with his right hand hanging and watched a man of about thirty-eight do the work of a second man and would not let anybody help him, and that is not modesty and has not been mistaken for it by anybody in this city.

The man of about thirty-nine was still at the bottom end with his board under his arm when the second one went past him, and he did not get out of the way so far as he could have, and about four people have worked out since that a man who does not step back from a barrow in a lane is a man who has decided he is not going to be asked to take it.

---

The handcart came up the boards at about the fourth hour behind the man of about sixty, and nobody in this city had sent for it and nobody was going to. The cart came up on two wheels and a man walking, and in it there was a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord and a coil of rope, and the two of those things are the whole of what the man of about sixty has brought with him this flood, and he has not used the rope and nobody has asked him about it and the cord on the mallet is the same cord that is on the fence at the top of a shut road, and that is a fact about a piece of cord and is not joined to anything.

He put the cart at the top of the boards and took the mallet out and stood with it in his hand and did not hit anything, and the man of about thirty-eight looked at the mallet and looked away. A man who has carried a mallet up a wet hill in a flood and has not used it has come to be seen carrying it, and the carrying is the whole of what he came for, and nobody is going to say so to him.

---

By the fifth hour there were three barrows standing in a row under the sacking at the top end of the boards with their handles up, and one of them is the man of about thirty-eight's and has been since before the water, and two of them have belonged to nobody since before the water, and there is one of them for each of the three people who are going, and nobody in this city has written that down anywhere and the writing of it is not going to happen in this flood.

The man of about thirty-four got his right hand open about a quarter of an hour before the light went, by putting it in the cold water at the bottom of the boards, and he did not say that he had been unable to close it since the fourth hour, and the man of about thirty-eight knew and did not say that he knew, and a barrow that took a right hand to get up a wet board is a barrow that will take a right hand again on whatever it is put to, and there are three of them standing under a sacking at the top of those boards for whoever is going to be holding them, and nobody in this city has asked any of those three people one question about what they are going to be holding, and nobody is going to.

The man of about thirty-nine went up the boards at about the sixth hour in the rain with his nineteen rates under his arm and had not touched any of the three and had not said a word to any of the people holding them, and a man who trades rates in four markets came down to the only flat thing for about four streets in a city with nine of them, and stood at the bottom end of it in the rain for about an hour and a half, and nobody asked him what for.
