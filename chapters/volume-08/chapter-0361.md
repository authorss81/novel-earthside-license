# Chapter 0361 — A Hurdle He Made, And A Bar He Replaced

The four-hundred-mile road goes out of this city and comes up out of the low ground in one long pull, and it is not a lane and it has not got a name on it that anybody here uses, and the light came up it on the forty-sixth day of that month from behind a shoulder of ground about nine miles off and stopped short of the gate at the head of it, and the cold was in the barrow and out of the north where there is nothing at all to stand between it and a man, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a hurdle of hazel rods he had cut and bound himself, a bar cut to the pattern of the bar that had gone through, a knife with a hinge that works, and about a mile of road going away from a city.

---

He had walked it four times in nine years. About four people on that road have worked out since that a man who walks a road four hundred miles long four times in nine years is not a man who is going somewhere, and that a man with a barrow is not going somewhere either, because a man going somewhere takes the barrow along with him and a man walking a road puts it down at the end of it and goes back.

The bar on that gate had gone through at the bottom about nine years back, the way an ash bar goes through, and he had cut a new one out of the old one's pattern, because a man who cuts a bar to a pattern has the pattern in his hands and does not have to ask anybody what a bar ought to be. About four people on that road have worked out since that this is the fourth bar he has cut to a pattern and the first one that was not his own gate, and that nobody has sent for any of the four and that he has not offered any of them.

The hurdle went up on the same day as the bar and it was the second he had ever made, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who makes a hurdle is a man who has watched one being made for a long time, and that he has made four of them in nine years and hung two of them, and that the arithmetic of that is four, two and nine years and none of it is on a page in four hundred miles.

The hurdle that was on that gate had opened. The rods had warped where the wet had got into them and the weave had gone slack across the middle, and a barrow with a stack on it would go under the middle of it, and the binding had come out of the top three rods and was lying in the wet at the foot of the gate in about nine pieces.

---

He put the barrow down at the stone end and did not start on the hurdle. He put his hand flat on the new bar where it sat in its drop and pushed it up and let it come down twice, which is what a man does to a drop he is not sure of, and it came down the same way both times, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who checks his own bar twice before he starts on anything else has checked it before and is checking it again, and that neither of those is the same as a man who is worried about it.

Then he cut the old hurdle off. He took the binding out of it first, all nine pieces, and put them in the barrow, because cord is the one thing on that gate that is not made of willow and is worth carrying off, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has taken four things off a gate and left everything else on it has decided what the gate is made of and what it is finished with, and that the two of those are not the same list.

The rods he left. All of them, including the two that had gone soft, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who leaves soft wood on a gate does not do it because he cannot be bothered and does not do it because soft wood is what a hinge is made of, and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to be told that by him and he has not said it.

---

The hazel was in the barrow and the knife and about nine rods of it were good and four were not, and he worked from the good ones and put the bad ones in the bottom of the barrow flat, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has a bundle of rods and a morning does not sort them before he starts and sorts them by the time his hands have told him which of them are, and that the sorting is the only part of the job anybody watching from the road would be able to follow.

He made the hurdles with the uprights four and the weavers in between, and he did not cut the weavers square and he did not cut them at an angle either, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a rod cut square will not take a binding and a rod cut at an angle will not hold a gate up, and that this is the whole of what he knows and that he learned it by making four and not by being told.

The light came round the shoulder and got to the gate about the fourth hour, which is earlier than it had been on any of the other mornings, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been at a gate three times in a week and knows the light knows the light better than the gate does, and that the light is a thing that is about the weather and the gate is a thing that is about a bar and that the two of them have nothing to do with each other.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came out past the last named house at about the third hour of the afternoon with nothing in his hands, and the man with the barrow did not stop, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has carried nine things in this flood and has not opened one of them has walked out past the last named house and kept going, and that nobody sent him and that he is not going to say what he is doing out here and that the not-saying is the same in a man with a shut satchel as it is in a man with a shut gate.

He stood about nine feet off and looked at the hurdle.

"You have taken the binding off it and left the rods."

"**The cord was mine and the willow was not, and I have made four of them and I am making the fifth and there are about nine rods in this barrow that are not going into it.**"

"You have walked up here before."

"Four times. Nine years."

He did not look up, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been asked a question by a stranger on a road has answered it in two words and then carried on, and that two words is about what it takes.

"**It is four hundred miles and there is nothing at the end of it that I have ever been able to get used to, and I go anyway, and I have never worked out why, and I am not going to work it out on a Friday morning in front of a man with a bag.**"

Nobody asked him anything. About four people on that road have worked out since that a man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living has not asked one question in four times up this road, and that the man with the barrow has not asked him one in four either, and that the two of those have been standing on the same road for about as long as it takes a man to weave nine rods and that neither of them has broken it.

---

The hurdle went up at about the fifth hour and the new bar went into its drop and the gate was a gate, and about four people on that road have worked out since that two objects on one gate is two objects and not one gate, and that the man who put both of them there has not been asked whether it is one gate or two and has not said, and that a man of about thirty-one who carries things has stood nine feet off and looked at the two of them and has not written a word about it and is not going to.

Then the man with the barrow did the thing he did, which was to stop working and stand with his hand on the bar and look down the road.

"I have walked this four times in nine years and I have never once seen anybody on it."

About four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has walked a road four hundred miles long four times in nine years has just said out loud, on a Friday, to a man he met by accident, that he has never seen anybody on it, and that it is not a complaint and it is not a question, and that it is the truest thing anybody has said on that road and that there is no form in four hundred miles with a column for it.

"You are looking at me."

"**I am looking at the road and you happen to be standing on it, and I am not going to be asked which of the two I was doing.**"

Nobody asked him which. About four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been asked nothing in four hundred miles by anybody has just been handed a question-shaped opening by a man with a barrow and has not taken it, and that the not-taking is the fourth time and that it is the arrangement and it costs nothing and it has never cost anything.

---

The man of about thirty-one went back down the road at the pace of a man who has finished, and the barrow was still at the gate with four rods in the bottom of it and a hurdle up and a bar in its drop, and about four people on that road have worked out since that there is now a gate at the head of a four-hundred-mile road with a bar in it and a hurdle on it that a man put in nine years back and put in again this morning, and that no form in four hundred miles has a column for what a man does at a gate when nobody has sent him, and that nobody in this city is going to be asked about the gate, and that the man who said the thing about the road this morning did not know that anybody in this city has been working out what a road is for and is not going to be asked about that either.
