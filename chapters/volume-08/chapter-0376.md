# Chapter 0376 — The Order It Is Done In, In A Yard

A yard behind the kitchens at the end of a lane in this city has a wall down one side of it and about nine lines strung across the other, and the light came over that wall at about the seventh hour on the sixty-first day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and got onto the ground in one sheet that reached about halfway across before the sun cleared it, and the cold was in the lines themselves and out of the ground underfoot where water had stood, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was about nine kettles, a tin in two hands, a folding knife with a hinge that does not spring, and about nine people doing a thing in the same order they had done it in yesterday.

---

The order was the thing. It comes off the lines at the back first, because that is the wall that has been in the sun longest, and then along the wall, and the two lines nearest the door go last, because the door stands open half the day and they get nothing until about the fourth hour. About nine people in this city have been doing the same nine things in the same nine order for longer than anybody standing in that yard has been in this city, and not one of the nine has ever written it down. There is no form in four hundred miles with a place for the order things are done in.

A man of about forty-eight was at the second kettle with a folding knife open on his knee and shutting it with his thumb to make the joint take. He has been in a great many rooms in this city and has not been asked anything in about nine of them, and he has been in this yard about nine times and has not been asked anything here either. Those two are the same arrangement, and the man has never once mentioned that he can do it.

Nobody in that yard asked anybody anything. The man standing at the gate end with his hands behind his back is a man nobody here has met. The man of about fifty-two who digs is eleven miles the other way, and nobody in this yard is going to ask him anything.

---

Adrian Vale had been in the room over the market the day before and had come down the nine steps at about the ninth hour of the evening and gone along the lane. A man who has not been in this yard in his life stopped at a gate on a Saturday morning and watched about nine people do nine things for about five hours without being asked to. He was not asked to, and he did not go in and say good morning to anybody.

He worked it out at about the third hour. He said it out loud in order with his hand going up and down the air in front of the line as he went, and he said the whole of a thing nine households have been doing for years out loud in a yard, in order, in about a quarter of an hour. The order he gave is the order it is done in, and about four of the people in that yard have noticed a thing about one of the nine that they are not going to say out loud. The man who said it did not notice it either, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that the one who noticed it first has been waiting nine mornings for a stranger to say it out loud so that she would not have to.

"I had it the other way round at the second kettle."

"**You had it the other way round at the second kettle, and the rest of it is the way it is done, and I am not going to tell you which one you had wrong, and neither is anybody else in this yard.**"

A woman of about sixty-nine has just stopped a man from getting an answer in the middle of a yard at about the third hour of a Saturday, and the man did not ask for it twice. The whole of what the nine people in this yard are going to have to decide about is which one of the nine he had wrong.

"Why is that."

"**A man who is told has it right tomorrow and a man who is not told will have it wrong tomorrow and will think about it, and this yard has nine things in it and about nine of us have found that out standing here.**"

She had spoken four words to him in four hours and she was not going to correct him, and she gave him a reason that was about him and not about her nine things. A corrected man is a fast man, and a yard is not improved by a fast man in it. Nobody in that yard had worked that out before she said it, and about four of them have worked it out since, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-nine has kept the ninth of the nine to herself for a number of years and has never once been asked for it by anybody.

---

The tin was in her hands the whole time and it was empty and she did not open it. A woman of about sixty-nine brought a tin to a yard the way she brought one to a room on a day in that month, and a tin is the thing a person brings who is not going to be given a page. She is the one the order belongs to. She has never once been asked about it, and nobody in this yard is going to.

The sun came off the wall at about the fourth hour and the sheet of light went off the ground in about as long as it had taken to come onto it, and the two lines nearest the door got what was left of it. Those two have been last every single time, and nobody in that yard thanked a man for saying so out loud, and nobody was going to, and he did not look for it.

He went back along the lane at about the sixth hour and did not go up the nine steps. A man worked a thing out at about the third hour of a Saturday and said it out loud in the order it goes, and the ninth of the nine is a thing four of the people in this yard noticed and one of the people in this yard has not said a word about, and the man who said the whole of it has gone away along a lane without being told. The woman with the tin went back inside about a quarter of an hour after he had gone. A man came into a yard and came out of it with the order nine things are done in, and there is no form in four hundred miles with a place for that, and he is the only person in this city who has it, and nobody asked him to write it down.
