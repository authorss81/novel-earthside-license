# Chapter 0387 — A Wall With A Shape Going Off It, And Two Men In Front Of It

A lane past two gates in this city is about nine houses long and it does not go anywhere anybody needs it to go, and there is a shut gate at the end of it and a wall down one side of it with a shape on the wall that used to be something, and the wind came down that lane at about the seventh hour on the seventy-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and went along it in one push and did not turn, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket, a wall, a shut gate with a bar of ash in two brackets, and a brass key on a tarred cord in the pocket of the woman who keeps that gate.

---

The shape has been going off the wall since a Tuesday in that month. It is not a writing and it is not a mark anybody put there on purpose, and it is the shape of something that was against that wall for a long time, and it goes off a wall the way a wet thing goes off a stone, which is from the top down and then from one side, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that it is nearly gone and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to be able to say what it was, and that is the whole of what it is.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came along the lane at about the seventh hour and stopped in front of it, and he did not go up to the market. He has chosen a wall over a board of boards and has stood in front of one at about the seventh hour on a Wednesday in the cold, and he has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month.

The man of about twenty-five came along the same lane at about the eighth hour with his coat buttoned and his hands in the pockets of it, and nine words he wrote himself are in the inside of that coat, and he has not said them in four rooms, and he stopped about nine feet off the wall and looked at the shape on it for a while.

---

Nobody asked either of them about it. A lane with about nine houses on it is not a room and has no day on a door and there is no form in four hundred miles with a place for a man standing in front of a wall, and the not-asking on that lane is not the arrangement it is in the room over the market, because nobody has ever decided anything about that lane and about four people on it have worked out since that a lane in this city is the only kind of place in it where a man may stand and do nothing for an hour and be entirely correct.

The man of about twenty-five took his right hand out of his coat and put it flat on the wall, about shoulder high, on the part of the shape that has gone softest, and he did not say anything while his hand was there, and he took it off again and put it back in his pocket.

The man of about thirty-four saw him do it. He was nine feet off with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and he watched a man put a hand on a wall that has nothing on it, and he said nothing about it then and he said nothing about it afterwards. Two men who have never asked each other a question in a room have now got a wall between them, and neither of them has used it.

---

Then he said the thing, and he said it out loud, on a lane, in the open, to the wall, and he said it before anybody asked him for it, which is a thing he does and has done about nine times in this flood.

"**It is going to be a wall with nothing on it by the end of this week, and I have a piece of chalk in this pocket that I have not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and I am not going to write on it.**"

"You have not got anything to say to it."

"**No, and that is the part I would like said out loud in this lane, because the reason I am not going to write on it is that I have not got anything that is mine to put there, and that is the whole of the reason, and it is not modesty and it is not the weather.**"

The man of about twenty-five looked at him and said nothing. There is a thing a man can do with a wall in a lane and there is not an instrument in four hundred miles that can say whether it was done, and a man who writes a line on a board for a living has just said out loud in the open that he has nothing of his own to write, and the man standing nine feet off him has nine words in a coat and did not take them out, and neither of those two men is going to say a word about the other one and both of them know it.

---

The woman of about fifty at the shut gate came out at about the ninth hour and wrote a date on the post above the day below it, in chalk, and did not rub anything out, and there is a bar of ash in two brackets across the gate and a brass key on a tarred cord in her apron pocket. She said four words out loud to the post, and the four of them were a date, and the four of them were not for either of the two men. She has said a date out loud to that post every morning since before the water and has never once said a date to a person, and nobody on that lane is going to ask her about the brass key and nobody has.

The gate went and the bar went across at the seventh hour, and the man of about thirty-four went on to the market and the man of about twenty-five went up the lane the other way with his hands in his coat, and the wall went on going off it in the wind with nothing put on it and nothing taken off it, and about four people who have come out of doors on that lane have worked out since that a wall with a shape going off it and a wall with nothing on it are the same wall, and that two men stood in front of one on a Wednesday and neither of them wrote anything on it, and that the woman at the gate at the end of that lane was not asked one question about either of them and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody.
