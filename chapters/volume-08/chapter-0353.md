# Chapter 0353 — Three Left, And A Thursday

The light came up the shut road at about the sixth hour on the thirty-eighth day of that month and it was in front of the work for the third morning running, and the wind was up the same road in pushes that had been up it since before the water and had never once been written down, and the cold was out of the north and out of the wet in eleven withies bound with the same cord, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart, a coil of rope, a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, and three willow posts standing in the cart with their tops out.

---

He came up the road at about the sixth hour and nobody had sent him and nobody was waiting for him, and the day was a Thursday, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who has put thirteen posts in the ground over two mornings and has three left in a handcart is going to finish a fence because it is a Thursday and not because anybody told him which day to come, and that coming back three times in a week is not a thing anybody arranged.

Sixteen were cut in a copse about four miles the other way. Nine went into the ground on the Saturday with seven left in the cart, and four more went in on the Monday, which made thirteen, and the three in the cart this morning were the last three and there was no fourth place to put a thing. He had walked out to the copse on the Tuesday afternoon with a saw that wanted oil and got oil out of a tin somebody had left on a wall, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who went back on the Tuesday for a saw is a man who expected to be back on that road before the day was out, and that nobody knew that except him.

He cut the withies off the tops of the three with the knife he had for it and he did not trim the roots, because a willow root trimmed is a willow root that has to be argued with, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who has put in thirteen posts knows what a root does, and that nobody has ever asked him and that he has not said.

---

The first of the three went in where the rope told him to put it, and the second went in about a pace along, and the third one had about a foot and nothing to do with it at the stone end, because a man who puts posts in a line by walking a rope out along it arrives at the end of the rope and finds that he has run out of road.

He stood with the mallet in his hand for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then he put the last post where the one before it was not, and it went in about a foot and a half and stopped, and he hit it twice more and it stopped again, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who has built a fence over three mornings solves the end of it the same way he solved the end of it on the Monday, which is to move the thing and not the rule, and that this is the whole of the engineering, and it is on no page, and nobody has asked him for it.

Four feet is not a measurement. It is a number that man has in his mouth, and it was in his mouth on the Saturday and it is not in it this morning, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man does not say the same true thing twice in four days to nobody, and that a man who has run out of things to say about a fence says them once and then goes home.

The withies went on in eleven places bound with the same cord, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a fence made out of willow and cord on a shut road is a thing four hundred miles has about nine hundred of, and that this one is the only one of them that anybody has put up at the same place as a mark in a field that nobody has measured, and that the two of them are about four feet apart, and that four feet is the number that man has in his mouth and not a number he took off anything.

The stone at the far end of the fence is a stone nobody put there. About four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who began a fence at a row of trees, because a row of trees is a thing you can see from a road, and finished it at a stone nobody put there, has put the two ends of it in the right order, and that he did not choose the stone and did not go and look for it and that the road simply ends where it ends.

---

He stood with his back to the wind and put his hands on the mallet and said two things out loud to nobody, because there was nobody on that road but him and the trees at the top of it.

"Three is the number I had left."

"**A man who has three left has a morning.**"

About four people who have come out of their gates on that lane have worked out since that those are the only two sentences that man has said on that road in three mornings and that neither of them is about a boundary, and that one of them is a number he counted and the other one is a thing he has noticed about himself, and that the second of the two is the reason there was a third morning at all.

Nobody was at the top of the road. About four hundred paces behind that fence there is a mark in a field which has no word on it on any page in the world, and nobody measured it, nobody dug it, nobody counted it, nobody put a rod in it, nobody went along it, and nobody in this city has been to it in a fortnight, and the fence is four feet off it and does not know what it is four feet off and is not going to be told.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the road at about the third hour of the afternoon with nothing in his hands, and the man with the mallet did not stop, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who has come up a shut road four times in a fortnight with his hands empty is a man who has learned something about that road which has nothing to do with what is on it.

"That is the last of them, and you have said a number to yourself three times this morning."

"Three is a morning's work when you have cut them yourself."

"I have been up here three mornings and I have not opened one of the things I carry, and I did not open the one that came up the road on the Tuesday, and I am not going to open anything else this flood, and I would like it noticed that nobody asked me to say that and I have said it four times now to four different people on four different mornings."

"**You have said it to me twice and I have never asked you for it once, and that is the arrangement and it costs you nothing and it costs me less.**"

"Somebody is going to ask you what a form is for."

"**Then I will be asked by somebody and not by you, and I will say the thing I say, and you are not going to hear about it beforehand and neither am I, and I have said this to four people in a month and I am saying it to you on a road with sixteen posts in it.**"

---

Sixteen posts and eleven withies and a Thursday, and about four people on that lane who watched it from the second house have worked out since that a fence with three posts still to go is finished enough for anybody on a road to be able to talk about it, and that a finished fence is a different thing from an unfinished one, because a finished fence can be counted, and that nobody is going to count it except whoever comes up this road next.

The cart went down the road with the mallet in it and the coil of rope on top of it, and the rope is a length of rope that has been used and is not new, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that there is a second rope in this city which is new and has never been used and that it belongs to a man who bought half a holding about forty years ago, and that the two ropes have never been in the same cart and are not going to be.

---

The light went off the top of that road at about the fourth hour and the fence stood along it in a line with nothing in the middle of it, and a handcart went down it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a sheet of paper has to go out of a room over a market in the morning, and that this morning is a Thursday and the room is on the Tuesday, and that four days counted from the Friday is four and counted from this morning is three, and that nobody in this city is going to write one on a Thursday for a room on the Tuesday, and that a three-day thing does not fail and does not arrive late either, and that it arrives warm.
