# Chapter 0367 — A Nib Ground Narrow, And Two People Not Saying So

A room over a market had the fifty-second day of that month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went across the floor and stopped at the wall on the far side, and the cold came out of the glass of a window on the near side that has not shut properly since before the water and had begun to shut properly about a week ago, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a slate, a lump of chalk, a form, a folding knife with a hinge that does not spring, and a pen with the nib ground narrow.

---

The window shut at about the eighth hour without anybody touching it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a window which has not shut since before the water shut about a week ago because a man of about forty-eight took the catch off, worked at it with a knife that does not spring, and put it back, and that about nine people in four hundred miles know that and that about nine of them are in this room, and that not one of the nine has mentioned it.

The man of about forty-eight was against the wall near the end of the bench. He had the knife on his knee and he opened it and shut it twice with the thumb of his other hand to make the joint take, and then it would hold about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has to do that before he can use a tool has been doing it for years and has never mentioned it and is not going to, and that a woman who keeps a public register has watched him do it four times since the fortieth day of that month and written nothing down about it, and that a woman who has kept a register for nine years knows exactly what she is not writing down.

The woman of about twenty-one was at the bench with the pen in her hand and had been at the seventh hour, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she is owed nothing by anybody in this city and that is the only reason a person can put a pen down, and that she has not used it, and that this is the second day running and that nobody in the room has watched her not use it.

---

The man of about twenty-five was at the back with his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who wrote nine lines to himself on the rough side of a board off the wall of his own hut has had them in the inside of that coat ever since and has not taken them out in front of about nine people and is not going to, and that there is no form in four hundred miles that anybody could be asked to produce for a thing nobody has taken out.

Nobody was working. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has had about nine people in it and not one of them doing anything since the seventh hour, and that this is the sixth morning in about a week that it has happened, and that a form with three of its four places in a condition and one in a decision is on the table and has been on the table in three rooms now, and that there is nothing anybody can do about it before the middle of the day.

Then the pen caught.

She was writing on the edge of a slate to see whether the nib would take and the nib caught in the grain about an inch from the point and stopped, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a pen with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife is the only pen in four hundred miles that could do that, and that it takes about an inch to catch and that it caught because the last inch is not ground, and that a man who grinds a nib knows exactly where he stopped and a woman who has not been told does not.

She turned it over in her hand and looked at the point of it for a while.

"You ground that."

---

Nobody in that room said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one has said two words out loud in a room with a date on the door to a man of about forty-eight, and that the two of them are two of the three people in this city who said yes in their own words, and that they are standing about nine feet apart and neither of them has moved.

"**I ground the first inch and a half of it in about a quarter of an hour on a step outside a room, and I have never said so to anybody, and I am not saying it now because you have asked me and a man who has been asked is not the same as a man who has offered it, and I have heard four people in this city work out inside a fortnight that the two of those are not the same thing.**"

"You have heard that."

"**I have been in about two hundred rooms in this city and I have not been asked anything in about nine of them, and the other hundred and ninety are rooms somebody else sent me to, and I have heard everything that has ever been said in front of me and I have never once repeated any of it. That is a trade and not a favour.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman asked a man one question and he has answered it in three pieces with a sentence about a trade in the middle of it, and that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms and has not been asked anything in nine of them has just been asked something and has told nine people what he is for.

"Somebody told you that was a good trade."

"**A man of about fifty-two who digs said the same thing to me on a row of racks eleven miles off, and neither of us knows the other's name, and I have not asked him and he has not asked me, and that is the only arrangement either of us has ever had with anybody.**"

He said the last part of that to the knife and not to her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just admitted to grinding a woman's nib on a step outside a room has also told nine people that he does not repeat what he hears, and that the two of those are the same man and that the second one is why the first one was safe to admit, and that neither of them knew the other one was going to be in the room.

---

She did not thank him. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one has found out that the pen she has been carrying for a month was ground by a man standing nine feet away from her, and that she has not thanked him, and that the man of about thirty-four has asked in advance not to be thanked for about a year and that this is the first person in this city who has not thanked anybody without being asked to in the first place.

"The last inch is not ground and it is going to catch on everything."

"**Then you will stop writing on things and go back to not writing on things, and that will be an improvement in this room and nobody is going to be sorry about it.**"

"I have not written on anything for a month."

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman has said the truest thing anybody has said at that bench in about a fortnight and has said it as an excuse, and that the man she said it to has not noticed that it was one.

"**Then I have ground a pen for a woman who was not going to use it, and a man at the end of the first stall has carried a piece of chalk in an inside pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month and has not used it, and a man eleven miles the other way has a rod standing in the corner of his own hut that he has not picked up.**"

"You have counted."

"**I have counted because I have been in about two hundred rooms and that is what a man does in them. Those are three of us and none of the three has ever said one word about it to anybody, and that is not a thing I am going to say a fourth word about in a room with a date on the door.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just admitted to grinding a woman's nib on a step outside a room has also listed three people in this city who are each holding a thing they have not used and have not said so, and that he has put himself on the list without being asked, and that none of the three has ever been told they were on it, and that a man of about thirty-four who asks in advance not to be thanked and a man of about fifty-two who says a thing before anybody asks him for it have both done the same as each other in about nine hundred rooms in this flood and neither of them knows the other one.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the window shut behind the last of them on its own, and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a man of about forty-eight fixed a catch with a knife about a week ago and has never mentioned it to anybody and has just had it mentioned to him, and that the mentioning was two words from a woman with a pen and not a thank, and that a man who asked in advance not to be thanked has now had a woman say two words about a pen he ground himself and has not been thanked and neither of them mentioned it again, and that neither of those things is on a page, and that a window in this city has been shutting properly for about a week and that about nine people have noticed and that about four of them have told nobody.
