# Chapter 0385 — Thirty-Nine Of Forty, Under A Gate, And A Line Still Ruled

A gate at the end of a road out of this city has about nine feet of stone on either side of it and a board screwed to the one on the left, and the light came over the wall at about the seventh hour on the seventieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and got onto the ground in one sheet about as long as the wall is wide, and the cold was in the stone of the gate and not in the air, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a board screwed to a wall, a lump of chalk in the inside breast pocket of one of the men, a board of nineteen rates under a lean-to, and a ruled line at the bottom of a column that has been empty since the beginning of this flood.

---

Thirty-nine of forty men were under that gate at about the seventh hour, and they had been under it every working day since the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and one of the forty has not come for a long time and nobody has been told, on purpose, and a board by that gate has a ruled line at the bottom of a column on it which is still ruled and which is still empty.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was one of the thirty-nine. He had come at the second hour and he would go at the seventh, and about nine men on that road have worked out since that a man who keeps a stall on the boards at the foot of a market and stands under a gate from the second hour to the seventh is a man who loses a day of selling every working day and has never once said so to the person he sells to, and that the piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket has been in there since the forty-third day of the Short Month and came out of the market with him this morning and went back in with him this evening.

---

The man of about thirty-nine came along the road at about the second hour with his board of nineteen rates under his arm and stood at the end of it, and did not trade, and about nine men under that gate looked at a man with a board and then looked at the ground, because a man who has a board and is not using it has come to a gate for a reason and the reason is not about rates.

At about the fourth hour he said it, and he said it out loud on a road, in the open, where every one of the thirty-nine could hear it, and he had been in nine rooms in a fortnight without being asked one question in any of them and had asked one question on a wharf on Saturday and got a sentence he did not ask for.

"**Thirty-nine of forty, and it is three hundred days from the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and this morning is the two hundred and eighty-fifth day of this flood, and the three hundred days is counted from the eighty-fifth, and there is nothing in this city that the three hundred of you are owed on.**"

Nobody argued with him about the number, because the number was right, and a man who says a number that is right in a place where about nine men can hear him does not have to be right about anything else to be listened to.

"That is the first time anybody has said that number out loud where I could stand and hear it."

The man of about thirty-nine did not answer him. A man who trades nineteen rates in four markets and has never once written one of them down was standing on a road with thirty-nine men at a gate and had just said a figure out loud that was not his, and the first thing anybody said back to him was that nobody had ever said it where they could hear, and he had nothing to say to that and did not try.

---

The line at the bottom of the column on that board has been ruled since before the water. It is about as wide as a thumb and about as long as a hand and it is ruled with three strokes and the strokes have not been gone over and there is nothing under them. It is the only instrument in four hundred miles that this city built for the thirty-nine of them and it has been empty since the first of March and it is going to be empty on the morning after this one.

There was a man under that gate who could have filled it and everybody in this flood knows it and nobody has ever asked him about it, and he was the man of about thirty-four, who writes a line on a board for a living, and who writes it last and in a harder piece of chalk, and who had a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket the whole of the morning with his hand flat over it the way his hand goes over a thing he has decided not to do.

He did not take it out. He worked under that gate from the second hour to the seventh with about nine feet of stone either side of him and his right hand closed, and about four people on that road worked out since that the hardest chalk a man has is the chalk he has decided against, and that a man who has carried a piece of chalk in a pocket since before the Bare Month began has had about a month to decide against it and has not told anybody what he decided and is not going to.

---

The man of about thirty-four said one thing, at about the sixth hour, to the man of about thirty-nine, and he said it before anybody asked him for it, which is a thing he does, and it was not about the line and it was not about the days.

"**You are going to be asked in a room whether you said it, and you are going to say that you said it on a road and not in one, and that is going to be the whole of what you have done this morning, and I am telling you now so that it is not a thing that happens to you in front of about nine people.**"

The man of about thirty-nine looked at him. He had said one number on a wharf on Saturday and had not asked the second half of it, and he had said a different number under a gate on Monday, and he had been told what the room is going to do with it, and about four people on that road worked out since that two men who have never asked each other a question about a page have now told each other two things about what a room does with a number, and that neither of them said a word about the line on the board, and that the line was still empty when the light went off it.

The men went home at the seventh hour and the bar went across and the board was on the wall with a ruled line at the bottom of a column and nothing under the rule, and the number the man of about thirty-nine said out loud on that road this morning is the first figure anybody in four hundred miles has ever attached to those thirty-nine men, and it is on nobody's page and there is no place in this city for it, and about four people who stood under that gate have worked out since that a figure said out loud in the open is the only kind of figure that cannot be got back off anybody, and that the thirty-nine of them turn up tomorrow, and that the bar goes across at the seventh hour every working day, and that about nine feet of stone either side of a road in this flood is the only thing in four hundred miles that thirty-nine men have ever been owed anything on.
