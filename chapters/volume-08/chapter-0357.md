# Chapter 0357 — What The Column Is For, And The Only Time It Is Said

The boards of a salt wharf in this city are the only flat thing for about four streets and the light comes up off the river on them in one long sheet and does not move all morning, and there is no hill within about four miles of that wharf so that the light never goes off anything until about the sixth hour, and what was under about four pairs of hands on the forty-second day of that month was a tally-board, a knife, a barrow, a slate, and about four hundred and forty.

---

A man of about thirty-eight had been putting a number in a return four times a year since the winter before the water, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that there are two numbers and everybody in four hundred miles knows one of them, and that the one everybody knows is not the one he has, and that the difference between the two has been growing for nine years in a direction nobody has ever written down.

The river came up over the wall at about the eighth hour the way it does and went back again a foot from where it had been, and he cut the salt off a barrow and put the barrow down and cut the next one, and the woman of about thirty-four at the stall said the number out loud at the head of the stall in about the same words she says it in every morning, to nobody, while she put salt on the boards with her hands, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has a second number and has never been asked about it and a woman who says the first one out loud every morning to nobody are in the same position, and that neither of them has anybody to hand it to.

He had not stopped for anything at those boards since the spring, and not a gull, and not a boy, and not the woman of about thirty-four, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who does not look up when a bird comes over his figures has been doing this a very long time and has a way of being uninterrupted that other trades do not have.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the boards at about the seventh hour with a slate under her arm and did not stop and neither did he, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that two people who will not stop working while they argue about a number have decided the argument is not worth stopping for, and that one of them has been getting four parts in five of what she was getting a month ago and the other one has not said so.

"I keep the book the column is for. What is it for."

"**That has been nine years coming and I have got a barrow in front of me and I am not stopping for it on the boards.**"

"I am not asking you to stop. I have been standing here about a minute and you have not stopped and I am not going to ask you to."

The woman of about thirty-four at the stall said the number out loud at the head of the stall in about the same words she has said it in every morning of this flood, and nobody at the top of a wharf has ever asked her where she got it, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a woman who says a figure out loud every morning to a man who is standing three stalls from her is a woman who has decided that the figure is hers and not the board's, and that the man has never asked her about it and never will.

"**That is nine years of a man being wrong about four hundred and forty people in about the same words, and I have said it out loud every morning so that the day I stop is the day it goes.**"

"It has not gone."

"**I know it has not gone. That is not why I say it.**"

Nobody on those boards said anything about that, and about four people on them have worked out since that a man who keeps a tally for a living has a way of being uninterrupted that other trades do not have, and that a woman who came nine hundred paces yesterday to be refused in about four seconds has come down to a set of wet boards at the seventh hour to ask a man a question, and that neither of them is stopping, and that this is a way of talking and not a way of winning.

---

"You have not asked me the other one."

"There is no other one."

"**There is another one you have not been asked and I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it, and I would like it noticed that I noticed.**"

He did not answer that and the knife went into the salt, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has been carrying two numbers for nine years and has never been asked which of the two he believes has just worked out that the woman standing on his boards is not going to ask him that, and that a man noticing that he is not going to be asked a thing is a different thing from a man not being asked, and that nobody on those boards has ever seen the first one happen.

The woman of about thirty-four said the number out loud again at the head of the stall and neither of the two of them turned round, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has just worked out that a question is not coming has gone back to a number a half second earlier than he had been going to, and that a woman three stalls down has just said the other one out loud for the four hundredth morning, and that neither of those two people knows the other one exists in the same way.

---

The room over a market had the forty-second day of that month in chalk on its door and about nine people in it, and the form was on the table with three of its four places exactly as they had been on the Saturday, and the fourth of them had been refused out loud on a step the day before by a household of about nine, and about four people in that room have worked out since that three of the four places under a heading on a form in this city are in a condition and the fourth of them is in a decision, and that a decision is a much heavier thing than a condition and does not weigh anything at all.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate on his knee and his right hand flat over the face of it, and the man of about thirty-eight came in at about the second hour of the afternoon and sat down at the end of the bench that the light had been lying along all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that two men in this city have taken a thing off a surface with their own hands and neither of them has ever told the other, and that they were in a room together for about five hours and neither of them said one word to the other at any point in it.

Nobody asked him anything for about two hours. He was on no roll in that room and had asked to be on none of them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has put a number in a return four times a year for nine years is on the only instrument in four hundred miles that has ever mentioned the people that number is about, and that he is not on it as a person, and that being on it as an instrument is the whole of his standing and has been for nine years.

He was asked at about the fourth hour.

"I keep the book the column is for. I am asking you in a room, in daylight, with the day on the door, because that is the only way anybody has asked me anything in nine years and I am not going to do it on wet boards."

"I heard you on the boards."

"I heard you on the boards and I did not get an answer on the boards and I have got a slate under my arm and there is nowhere for an answer to go in this city except a room, and so I am asking you again, and I am not going to ask you a third time."

He looked at the form on the table for about as long as it takes to fill a pail. He put his hands flat on the bench on either side of his knees. The river noise came up the stair from four streets away and stopped, the way it does, for about as long as a man can count to nine.

"**It is for the ones nobody asks.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has gone about a month without a sound in it and knows exactly what one sounds like, and that this one did not make one, and that the man who had made it was the man who works on the boards at the bottom of this city every morning of this flood.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been putting a number in a return four times a year since the winter before the water has just been asked what the column is for by the person who keeps the book it goes in, and has answered, and that he answered it in seven words, and that it is on a page in this city now and it is not in any of their hands.

He did not say it again. He was not asked it again. Nobody asked him whether he believed it, and nobody asked him what he was going to do about it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said a thing once in a room in this city and has been asked nothing since has done the same thing this afternoon that a man who has written a word on a slate and then rubbed it off does, and that neither of them has told anybody what it was.

Then she did the thing she does, and the last four lines of it were hers and hers alone.

"**I am not going to write that down. I have a slate under my arm and a day in it, and I am going to write the day and nothing under the day, in front of nine people, so that nobody in this room can say afterwards that a man in this room said a thing and a woman put it on a page.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years has just chosen, out loud, in front of nine people, to be the person who did not write a thing down, and that the price of that is that in about nine years, or about ninety, a clerk will ask her for the answer and she will have to say she does not have it, and that she has said the price out loud before the thing happened, which is the only thing anybody in this city has got that a form has not.

He went down the stair at about the seventh hour and cut salt off a barrow, and nobody thanked him, and about four people who watched him go have worked out since that a man who has said a true thing out loud in a room with a date on the door, in a city where nothing goes on a page without a name at the top of it, has bought something, and that he has not been thanked for it and did not ask, and that about four people in that room have worked out since that they are not going to say thank you either, and that the not-saying is going to be the arrangement and not an oversight.
