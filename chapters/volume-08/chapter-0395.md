# Chapter 0395 — A Day In A Register, And Nothing Under The Day

A room over a market had the eightieth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and the stair went up about nine times before the light got to the bench, and the cold came up out of the floor at about the fourth hour and stayed, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a book open at a ruled line with three columns in it, a pen with its cap in a woman's left hand, a lump of chalk, a slate, and a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it.

---

The form went out on Tuesday. That is the fact the whole morning sat on.

A thing went out of this city on the seventy-eighth day of that month and there is a book in this room that has three columns in it, and the first of them takes what went out. The second takes what came back. The third has been empty on purpose since a Thursday in that month and the woman who keeps it said out loud, in front of about nine people, that she would walk out of a room rather than fill it, and she has not filled it and nobody in this city has asked her to.

About nine people had been sitting in this room for about a fortnight with a piece of paper going out on somebody else's calendar and had not once opened that book. The book lives on a shelf. The book has been shut on that shelf through every room of this flood and it is the only thing in the building that anybody four hundred miles off has ever heard of.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her forearm across the open book and she said the thing out loud, standing, in the order of the facts, which is a thing she does.

"It has got to have a day in it."

"**A register that has not been given a line is not a register that has been kept, and there is a thing that went out of this city on Tuesday and it went with a name at the top of it, and this book has three columns and the first of them is for days.**"

---

She ruled the line herself, with the back of the pen, and there is no form in four hundred miles with a room on it and no form in four hundred miles with a day on it either, and a woman who has kept a book for nine years has been writing both of those things out by hand since before the water.

Then she wrote the day in the first column and read it back before she wrote anything else, which is also a thing she does, and she put the cap back on the pen.

"**This morning is the eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the day in that line is the seventy-sixth day of that month, a Sunday, and that is four days counted back from this morning and not counted forward, and the day in that line is the morning three people went down a road out of this city with a barrow each, and there is nothing under the day.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and he had not said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then he said the thing, and he said it before anybody had asked him for it, which is a thing he does.

"Then somebody is going to want to know what went out on it."

"**Then somebody is going to want to know what went out on it, and I am not going to tell them, and I am not going to let anybody else in this room tell them either, and the price of that is going to be paid by the three of them and not by me, and I would like that said out loud in this room before anybody puts a pen near it.**"

Nobody put a pen near it.

"Then somebody is going to want to know who went out on it."

"**There is nobody in this city who can produce a copy of that name, and there is going to be a day in a book in this room that nobody in this city can fill in either, and those two are the same kind of thing, and I have worked that out at the end of my own pen and I have not shown anybody.**"

"Then it is not a copy of anything."

The pen went back on the table with the nib pointing away from the book, and nobody in that room improved on what either of them had just said, and the two people holding that book open arrived at the same thing from opposite ends of the same morning and neither of them had said the other one's half of it out loud.

A man who writes a line on a board for a living has just heard the second of the three columns of a register described as left empty, which is a thing that happens on a morning when nothing has come back, and the third described as left empty on purpose by the woman who keeps it, and a room in this city has now got a day in the first column and two columns empty and one of the two on purpose, and neither of the two empties can be asked to have been produced.

---

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and he had been at the back of every room this city has had for about a fortnight and had been right in one of them on Tuesday morning, and he said nothing at all on this Thursday and looked at the open book the whole morning. A man who reads an empty column is a man who has already read one.

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair and a board of about nine paces between him and the table. He looked at the ruled line for a while and did not say anything, and the reason is that the entry in that line is his own shape, and the only man in that room who had lost a winter and could not get it back did not have to be told what a day with nothing under it is for, and nobody asked him, and nobody is going to.

Nobody went near the shelf.

The second slate was on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it from the seventh hour to the fourth hour, and nobody in that room picked it up and nobody turned it over, and the word that was on it once in this flood was taken off it in this room in daylight with a cloth on a Sunday in that month and is not on any page in the world, and about four people in that room have known that since and not one of them has said so out loud.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the book was shut and went back on the shelf it lives on, and the line was in it with a day in the first column and the second column empty and the third column empty on purpose, and that is the only line that was entered in that book on the eightieth day of the Bare Month, and nothing else was entered in it at all.

Three people went down a road out of this city on the seventy-sixth day of that month with a barrow each and no page between them, and a woman who has kept a public register for nine years entered the day of it in a book and entered nothing else, and the price of that is that from this morning there is nothing whatever in four hundred miles that can say those three were on a road, or where they got to, or what they had, or who sent them, and the three of them are four days down that road and have not been asked about anything by anybody, and the fourth of the four is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and a party of four is four. A blank is the only thing in four hundred miles a return cannot be filled in from, and a clerk nine hundred miles off cannot ask a city to produce one, and a room in this city has spent its last fortnight arriving at a line with a day on it and nothing under the day, and that is what the fortnight bought.
