# Chapter 0364 — Two Men In Front Of It, And No Agreement Anywhere In It

A shut road above this city had a fence along the top of it and the light came up the road on the forty-ninth day of that month from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over, and the wind was up the same road in pushes that had been up it since before the water, and the cold was out of the north and out of the wet that is still in eleven withies bound with the same cord, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart, a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, a coil of rope, and a length of new rope over a man's arm that he has never used.

---

The man of about fifty-seven came up the shut road at about the sixth hour on his own, and nobody sent him and nobody knew he was coming, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has been in ten rooms in a fortnight and has not been asked one question in any of them has gone out and looked at a thing himself instead, and that going to look at a thing is the first thing a man does when being in rooms has stopped doing anything to him.

He had the length of new rope nobody had used over his arm and he had not used it, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has carried a new rope in this flood and has never used it has brought it up a shut road with him this morning, and that nobody sent him and that there is nothing at the top of that road for a rope to be any use on, and that he did it anyway.

He counted the fence. He walked it from the trees to the stone nobody put there, going along it on the low side, and he stopped at each one and put his hand flat on it and went on, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who counts a thing by touching it is a man who does not trust his own eyes past about nine paces, and that he was not counting it for anybody and that nobody was with him.

"Sixteen."

About four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man of fifty-seven has said a number out loud on a shut road to nobody, and that it is the correct number, and that nine of them went into the ground on the thirty-third day of that month, a Saturday, with seven left in the cart, and four more went in on the thirty-fifth, a Monday, which made thirteen, and the last three went in on the thirty-eighth, a Thursday, and that all of that is on a page and that a man counting them in the cold on the forty-ninth got the same figure.

"Eleven of them are bound with the same cord."

He said that one out loud too, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man has just told a shut road that eleven of the rods on a fence are lashed with one piece of cord, and that this is the second figure that man has said out loud to nobody in about a week, and that the first one was in a room and got a silence, and that neither of them got a question.

---

The man of about sixty with the handcart came down the road at about the eighth hour with the mallet in the cart and the coil of rope on top of it, and he was going down and not up, and he saw the other one standing on the low side of a fence and he did not stop and he did not go round, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has been putting posts into the ground for three mornings and finished on the Thursday and has come down that road four times since has now had to walk past a man standing on the other side of his own work, and that neither of them slowed down and that one of them said a number.

"Sixteen is right."

"**I have not asked you how many and you have not asked me and that is the arrangement, and it has been the arrangement for about a week, and I have never once been told by anybody where this is.**"

About four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who built a thing nobody sent him for has just been told by a man standing on the other side of it that he has never been told where it is, and that the telling was not a question and was not a complaint and was about as long as a man takes to say a thing he has been carrying, and that the man who said it had a length of new rope over his arm and did not put it down.

"You have not asked me either."

"**I have not asked you anything at all in about a week, and I have not been in a room with you since, and I have been on this road about four times and you have been on it about four times, and neither of us has ever asked the other one a question, and I have worked out that that is not an accident and I have not worked out what it is instead.**"

Nobody asked. About four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man of about fifty-seven has been right about most of what he says in a city where a page is the only instrument anybody has, and that being right is what has cost him a winter and a season and an afternoon, and that what it has cost him this morning is that he is standing in front of a fence he cannot move and has not tried to move, and that the man who put it up has been within nine paces of him four times and has not said one word about it, and that those are the two halves of his afternoon and neither of them is going to be asked about.

---

Then the man of about fifty-seven said the thing he had come up the road to say, and he said it to the fence, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has been in ten rooms in a fortnight and has not been asked one question in any of them has walked out to a thing and said the one sentence he had, and that the sentence is not about where it is and is not about what it is for.

"**It is the only thing anybody has put up in this flood that is going to be here after everybody who made it.**"

The man with the handcart put his hand on the mallet in the cart and did not take it out, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has finished a thing nobody sent him for has been told by a man who has lost a winter that it will outlast everybody, and that he has not disagreed with it and has not agreed with it and is not going to, and that the not-saying is the fourth time and that the man he is not saying it to has now said something true on a shut road and is not going to be thanked for it and would be the first person in this city to refuse the thanks.

"Do you want it said anywhere."

"**I want it said nowhere, and there is no form for it, and I have come up a shut road on a Monday morning to say one sentence to a fence and you are a man with a cart and I have not been asked anything and I am not going to be asked anything now, and the two of those are the same thing and it is the only arrangement either of us has ever had with anybody.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had asked a man about a gate about a fortnight ago and had asked in advance not to be thanked and was not thanked, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man with a length of new rope over his arm has just said the same arrangement on a different road to a different man without knowing that anybody else in four hundred miles has ever said it, and that there are two of them now and neither of them has met the other one and that is how this city arranges things.

---

The cart went down the road and the rope was still on the other man's arm and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that the cart went down with a mallet in it and a coil of rope on top of it and did not go up, and that the fence stood along the top of a shut road with sixteen posts in it and eleven withies bound with the same cord and a man beside it that nobody had sent, and that there are two accounts of what that fence is for in this city and both of them are held by men who have never asked the other one, and that neither of those men is going to be asked in this flood, and that the fence is not going anywhere, and that it does not know what it is between and is not going to be told.
