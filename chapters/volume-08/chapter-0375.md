# Chapter 0375 — Whether It Will Go

A room over a market had the sixtieth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Friday, and about nine people in it before the stair had been up and down four times, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along the bench and off the end of it and did not come back, and the cold came up out of the floor at about the fourth hour and stayed, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, the second slate off the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, a pen with the nib ground narrow in the front of an apron, and a folded sheet of road that nobody was given.

---

The form was on the table and it was not touched. About four people in that room have worked out since that a piece of paper with a heading and four places under it was on a table in that room for about four hours on the morning it was in a room with about nine people in it, and that not one of the nine went near it, and that the not-going-near was the arrangement the room had made for itself on the day before and had not told anybody about outside the room.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall was at the back of it with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man at the back of it has spent a morning with about nine people looking at a piece of paper that wants a thing off him, and that the thing it wanted was a mark, and that he has had one in his pocket since before the Bare Month began, and that he did not offer it, and that nobody in that room expected him to.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall at the back where the light does not reach, with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been at the back of four rooms in a fortnight without a question in any of them is at the back of a fifth, and that this is the morning the room ran out of forms, and that he said nothing at that point, and that nobody in that room noticed the point, and that about four people have worked out since that he noticed it.

The man of about twenty-five had his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who wrote himself nine words on the rough side of a board off the wall of his own hut has had them in the inside of that coat for a month and has not taken them out in front of about nine people, and that he came up those nine steps this morning with them in there.

The man of about forty-eight sat down the moment he came in, the way a man does who has learned that standing about in a room is what a person does when they are expecting to be asked.

---

The woman of about twenty-one was at the bench with her hands in her apron and the pen in the apron with them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a pen with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife was in the front of that apron at the seventh hour and was not in her hand at the seventh hour or the eighth, and that a woman who walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth of them has not had that pen in her hand in a room in a month, and that the only person in four hundred miles who could have told this room why is a man about nine feet off her with a folding knife on his knee.

Nobody was working. About four people in that room have worked out since that about nine people have now been sitting in a room with a page on it for about a fortnight with nothing whatever to do, and that the day before this one they arranged the length of a bench, and that the day before that they agreed not to say what the room was for, and that about four of them have worked out since that a room in this city has spent a month getting ready to ask a question and that the getting ready has taken all of it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not in the room that morning and about four people in that room have worked out since that the person who writes the day on the door had not been up those nine steps yet, and that the second slate was on the shelf behind the table where it has been every morning for about a month, and that nobody had gone near it, and that a woman at a bench with a folded sheet of road in her apron had not asked anybody when she was coming.

The bench was clear and the form was on the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the two of those facts had been arranged by two people who had not discussed it, and that Tamsin Quill had moved the bench at the sixth hour and the register-keeper had put the form on the table before she went, and that neither of them had said the other one anything about it.

"You moved the bench."

"**I moved it at the sixth hour and nobody told me to, and I would like it noticed that a woman who moves a bench at the sixth hour is a woman who has decided the room is going to be looked at and not written on, and that the woman who writes the day on the door has done the other thing to the same bench for about a month and neither of us has ever said one word to the other about it.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket that he has not taken out since before the Bare Month began has just been told out loud, in a room in daylight with a date on the door, that two women have been arranging the same bench for a month without speaking, and that the man did not ask a question and that about four people in that room have worked out that he was not going to.

The man of about twenty-five said nothing, with his hands in the pockets of his coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has nine words of his own in the inside of that coat and has not said them in three rooms sat through a woman saying out loud that she and another woman have never spoken, and that he has a page of his own that nobody in this city can ask to have produced, and that about four people in that room have worked out since that the only person in it who could have said something about two women not speaking to each other is a man who is not in the room, and that he is not going to be sent for.

The second slate was on the shelf behind the table, and the man of about forty-eight looked at it for a while before he said anything about it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in this city since before the water has been looking at a piece of slate with a date on it and nothing under it since about the seventh hour and has not touched it, and that about four of the nine in that room knew what was on it and about four of them did not.

"That is the second one."

"**It is the second one, and it has been on that shelf for about a month with a day on it and nothing under it, and nobody in this room has picked it up, and I have not picked it up, and I have been in about two hundred rooms in this city and I have not been asked anything in about nine of them.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man with a folding knife on his knee has just said a figure out loud in a room with a date on the door and has not been asked about it, and that he said it as a fact about himself and not as a complaint, and that the figure is his and that about four of the nine in that room have known it for about a fortnight and that not one of them had said it in this room before.

"Nobody is going to pick it up."

"**Nobody is going to pick it up, and I would like it noticed that I have said that before, in another room, to a man who was not going to either, and that neither of us has said it to anybody twice, and that the slate has been on that shelf for a month because the two of them decided that separately and never found out.**"

---

Then Tamsin Quill asked her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that it was said in the local language, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, in a room with about nine people in it, and that it was said once, and that the woman who said it was at a bench and had not stood up, and that the woman it was said to was four feet off her and had not moved.

"**Will it go.**"

Two words and a question, and she did not say who by, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been in a documented check with a man since a day in the Short Month has asked the one question that nobody in this city has asked anybody in a month, and that she asked it in a room where about nine people could hear the answer, and that she did not ask it twice.

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has been asking itself questions for a month and has not asked one out loud, and that a room which has run out of forms has asked a person something instead, and that this is what a room does when it runs out, and that nobody in that room had any idea that it was going to happen before it did.

Nobody asked her anything else. That is the whole of the second half of the morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a question was asked in a room in daylight and not one of the nine people in it asked a second one, and that it took them about four seconds to work out among themselves not to, and that about four of them have worked out since that a person who has stopped waiting to be told what she is for is not a person who has to be managed afterwards, and that nobody in that room was going to be the second one to ask her about anything, and that nobody after this morning is going to be the second one either.

---

About the tenth hour of the day the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came up to the step at the back with no name on it with the slate under her arm, and she did not come in, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years came up nine steps on the morning a question was asked in that room and stood on the step and looked in through the door, and that she stood there until about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and that a woman at a bench with a folded sheet of road in her apron did not turn round, and that the two of them were about four paces apart for as long as she was there and said nothing to each other at any point in it, and that the door stood open behind her for about as long as it takes to walk up nine steps and down them again.

---

The room emptied at about the sixth hour and the light went off the bench first and off the table after that, and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room over a market asked a person a question on a Friday morning and did not ask her anything else, and that nobody who was in it found out what anybody thought, and that the pen with the nib ground narrow went back into an apron with nothing on it, and that the second slate was on the shelf behind the table the whole day with a date on it and nothing under it, and that nobody went near the shelf.

Nobody in this city has said out loud what the room on this day was for, and it was not said on the door and it was not said in the room, and the sheet that brought about nine people up those steps had a day on it and a place on it and nothing at the top of it, and about four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that a question was asked there this morning and that three of the four who said yes in their own words were in that room and the fourth of them is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and that a party of four is four, and that nobody who came down those nine steps is going to say one word about her, and that the room has a form on its table that has been in four rooms and filled in by nobody, and that the form is going out on somebody else's calendar, and that a woman at a bench with about nine places on a folded sheet of road in her apron never took it out of the apron all day and did not offer it to one person, and that nobody in that room asked her for it.
