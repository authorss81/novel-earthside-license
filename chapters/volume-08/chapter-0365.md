# Chapter 0365 — A Wall With A Shape On It, And A Gate With Two Bars

A shut gate at the end of a lane had a wall beside it and a light that came round the corner of that wall about an hour before the middle of the day and got under a stack that was not there any more, and the cold was out of the boards that were not there and out of the wet at the foot of a wall, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a bar of ash in two brackets, a brass key on a tarred cord, a piece of chalk in the pocket of an apron, and about four inches of grey stone at the bottom of a wall with the shape of nine boards on it.

---

She put the fiftieth on the post at about the seventh hour and she did not rub anything out, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a keeper who writes a date on a post every morning has a column of them going up the post now that a room in this city would recognise, and that there is nothing under any of them, and that a column of dates with nothing under it is the same shape as the entry that went into a book in this city on the thirty-first day of that month, and that neither of the two is a record and that the woman at the gate has had hers since before the water.

The wall was bare. It is a piece of a gate wall with the dark shape of nine boards on it and about four inches of grey stone at the bottom where the wet had not got, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that the shape has been going for about a fortnight and that the wet has taken the top left corner of it and that what is left is not nine boards and is not nothing, and that the man who took them off it has not been on that lane since the morning he took them and that he is not coming back for the rest of them.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the lane at about the eighth hour with a barrow and did not stop at the gate, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has walked past a gate a barrow-load at a time for about a fortnight has come up that lane on a Tuesday with nothing in the barrow and has come to the wall and not to the gate, and that the wall is nine feet of bare stone with a shape on it and that a man who writes a line on a wall for a living has come up here to look at a shape.

"That is going."

"**I know it is going, and I know which corner it is going from, and I have known for about a fortnight and I have not said one word about it to anybody and I am not going to start with you on a Tuesday morning when you have not asked me.**"

About four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man of about thirty-four has been watching a shape on a wall go for about a fortnight and has told nobody, and that a wall with a shape on it is the only thing on that lane that is going without anybody having decided anything, and that nobody in four hundred miles can put a week on a page and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to try.

"You have come up here to look at it."

"**I have come up here because I was going this way and there is a wall on this way, and a man writes on walls, and there is a shape on this one and it is not mine and it is not a line and it is not a name, and I have got about four hundred days of knowing what a line is for and I am not going to put one next to it.**"

Nobody thanked him, because he had not asked, and because a man who has asked in advance not to be thanked for anything in about a year is a man nobody in this city has ever managed to thank, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a piece of chalk has been in an inside breast pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month and has not been used once in all that time, and that the wall is nine feet of dry stone a yard from his hand, and that the whole of what is on that wall is nine shapes and no words.

---

Then he said the thing about the other gate, and he said it to the wall and not to her, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has been at two gates in this city in about a fortnight has worked out that he cannot get the two of them to sit still in the same sentence, and that he is going to try anyway, and that the trying is the whole of what this morning has been and that nobody on that lane is going to say so.

"There is a gate four streets off where a man holds it on a turn, and it has two bars in it, and the second one is on no roll at all."

She did not look up from the post.

"**A roll has a gate on it and a keeper on it and a set of days, and that is the whole of what is on it, and that has been the case for longer than the four of us have been anything.**"

"And the second bar?"

"**A second bar is not on it and neither is the first one, and I have never asked him about either and I am not going to, and I would like it noticed that I have said all of that out loud to a wall and not to you.**"

About four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a woman who has not been asked anything by anybody since before the water has just given the whole of a roll in four sentences to nine feet of stone, and that a man with a barrow heard it, and that neither of them has asked the other one anything and that the two of them have now both said a thing about a form on the same morning about nine feet apart with a wall in between.

"You are not going to tell me where the two bars are."

"**I am not going to tell you where the two bars are, and I am not going to be told where they are. There is a gate at the end of this lane with a bar in it and there is a gate four streets off with two in it.**"

"That is two gates."

"**And they are not going to be put side by side by either of us today, and I would like it noticed that I have said that and that it is the most I have said to anybody in about a fortnight.**"

The man of about thirty-four did not answer that, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a man who has put his hand on his own breast pocket about nine times in about a fortnight and has not been refused by anybody has just been refused something by a woman standing nine feet off with a piece of chalk, and that nobody on that lane has worked out why the two of them are standing in front of a wall at all, and that the refusal was not about the thing being asked after, and that the chalk is still in the pocket and the brass key is still in the apron pocket and neither of them has been taken out.

---

He stood in front of the wall for a while after that and did not write on it, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that the shape on that wall had a corner of it gone and would have had two by the time the season turned, and that the man who could have put a line next to it did not, and that the woman who has kept that gate did not ask him to, and that the two of those are the same arrangement and it is the one they have had since the morning nine boards came off that wall on a barrow, and that neither of them has ever mentioned the other one since and is not going to.

"Do you want it said anywhere."

"**Not to anybody, and there is no form for it, and the only two things anybody in four hundred miles would put on a page about this morning are a day and a number of boards.**"

"You counted them off the wall with your hand this morning."

"**The number is nine, and there is a day on the post in chalk about a foot off where the two of us are standing, and neither of those is going on anything. You have asked me that twice now and I have answered it twice and I am not going to be asked a third time, and I am not going to be thanked for either of the answers.**"

The bar went in at about the sixth hour and the wind came round the corner and stopped as it always does when it is in, and the fiftieth was on the post and the forty-ninth above it, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a lane nine households took their days out of in the Short Month has ended up with a date in chalk on a post, a bar of ash in two brackets, a brass key on a tarred cord in an apron pocket, a shape on a wall that is going, and a piece of chalk in a breast pocket that has not been out since the forty-third day of that other month, and that six of those are objects and one of them is a thing a man has chosen not to do, and that this city cannot tell the difference between the two on any page it has, and that a man with a barrow went back down the lane at the pace of a man who has finished and did not say one word at the gate.
