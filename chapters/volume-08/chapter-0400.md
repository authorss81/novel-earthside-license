# Chapter 0400 — A Fence, And A Road Going Away Under It

A shut road out of this city has a fence along the top of it, and on the eighty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, the light came up that road from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over, the way it has stopped there every morning since the fence was finished, and the wind was up the same road in pushes, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart with a coil of rope in it, a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, a length of new rope nobody has used over the back of a chair in a room in this city, and a barrow with a hurdle of hazel rods in the bottom of it.

---

Sixteen posts. Nine of them went into the ground on a Saturday in that month, four more on the Monday after it, which made thirteen, and the last three on a Thursday, and there has not been a seventeenth and nobody has said there is going to be one. Eleven withies are bound along it with the same cord, and that cord is on the head of the mallet in the cart at the top of that road, and the eleven have gone soft in the wet and the cords have not.

A man of about fifty-seven has walked the whole length of that fence more than once with his hand flat on every post and got sixteen both times, and has said the figure out loud to nobody on a shut road, and has never once been asked how he knew it, and is not going to be. A man of about sixty put it up and has not been sent for and is not going to be asked. A fence that two men have stood at and neither of them has ever been asked where it is, and a question about it that went to a room in this city on the eighty-first day of that month and was answered by nobody, and neither of those is going to be settled this flood by anybody who is standing in this city.

---

Back in this city there is a gate where a man holds it on a turn, and it has two bars in it, and the second one is on no roll at all.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall walked past it on his way up to the market and did not stop, and a man who writes a line on a board for a living has been the only person in this city who could have said out loud where two bars are and has never said, and about four people on that turn have worked out since that a gate is a gate and that the second of the two objects in that gate was cut out of the first one's pattern, and that neither of those facts is on any roll, and that nobody in this city has counted them and no room in this city has asked him to.

Nobody is going to ask him. He has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and he has said out loud, in a lane, that he has nothing of his own to put on a wall, and a man who has said that is a man nobody in this city is going to come to with a question about a bar.

---

From the top of that road you can see down into the lane where the nine houses are, and down one side of that lane there is a wall with the shape of nine boards gone off it and about four inches of dry stone at the foot of it, and the boards came off it on the thirty-second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and nobody in this city has said whose they were, and there is not a barrow in this city with anything on it and there is not going to be one.

At the end of that lane there is a shut gate, and a woman keeps it, and she has never once been asked anything by anybody. She came out on that morning and wrote a date on the post and did not rub anything out, and she said the date out loud to the post and not to a person, and a date on a post is the only thing on that lane that has never once been wrong and that a woman who says it to the wood and not to a man is a woman who has decided what she is for.

---

The man of about fifty-nine came up the shut road at about the seventh hour with his barrow and put it down on the low side at the fence, the way he puts a barrow down at the end of a road four hundred miles long, and he has been up that road four times in nine years and has not got to the end of it and has never once been asked why. He looked down the road for a while and there were three people on it going away from him, and each of them had a barrow, and the three of them were nine days out of this city and had said yes in their own words on three different days in three different rooms, and none of the three words was a copy of either of the other two.

The man of about fifty-seven was on the low side of the fence with a length of new rope over his arm that he has never used, and the man of about sixty came up the road behind the handcart with the mallet in the cart and the coil of rope on top of it, and neither of the two of them asked the other one anything, and that has been the arrangement for about a fortnight and it is the arrangement that is going to be there after all three of them have gone.

"There is nobody going to ask you either."

"**Nobody is going to ask me, and there are three people on that road and I have not been asked one question on this road in nine years, and I am not going to ask about the fence and I am not going to be asked about the fence, and I would like it said out loud on a shut road that a man has seen three people go by and has not asked and is not going to.**"

A party of four is four. The fourth of the four is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and nobody on that road counted anything, and nobody in this city is going to be able to show anybody that she was ever asked, because she was not, and that is the whole of what she has and it is not a hole in anything.

---

Then the man of about fifty-nine picked his barrow up off the low side and went back down the road he had come up, and he did not stop at the fence and he did not look at it on the way past, and a man who has walked a road four hundred miles long and had nobody on it until three days ago came up it with three on it in front of him, and did not go after them, and did not ask, and is not going to.

The light came up the road from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over. The sixteen posts stood along the top of the shut road in a line with the wind going through the eleven withies on them, and the cord on them was the same cord that was on the head of a mallet in a handcart, and between that fence and a mark in a field there is the gap a man of about sixty has a number for and has never taken it off anything, and that mark has no word on any page in the world, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who is going to that field and nobody in four hundred miles who can say where it is.

And below the fence the road went on down into the low ground with three barrows on it, going away.
