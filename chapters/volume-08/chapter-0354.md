# Chapter 0354 — A Place And Not A House, And A Chalk He Does Not Take Out

A market in this city had about nine stalls in it under a roof of sailcloth and sacking, and the light came in over the sacking at about the seventh hour and made the whole of it the same grey, and the cold was in the boards of the trestles and in the stone under them, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a board with nineteen rates on it, a piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man who keeps the first stall, a lump of chalk on the edge of the trestle, and a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it.

---

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has about ninety people in front of him before the middle of the day and every one of them is talking about nothing, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the stall nearest the water end had been saying a number out loud on those boards every morning for about a month, in about the same words, ever since she had said it in a market in front of about nine people, and she put salt on the stall with her hands while she said it and never once looked up.

The two of them had been side by side since the beginning of the spring and had never once agreed about anything, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a woman who says a number out loud every morning and a man who will not ask her where she got it is the only pair on those boards who are being scrupulous with each other, and that neither of them has the least idea that they are.

The stall next to hers belonged to a man who writes a line last and in a harder piece of chalk on purpose so that the word will be the last thing anybody could rub out, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who writes a line last is a man who has an opinion about what goes at the top of a thing and what goes at the bottom of it, and that he has not said what it is and that nobody has asked.

"About four hundred and forty."

"**I have heard that every morning for about a month and never once asked you where you got it, and I am not going to start this morning.**"

"You are not going to ask me because you are the man who does not ask anybody anything, and everybody on these boards worked that out about you inside a week."

"**Then everybody on these boards has it about right and I have not said a word to correct it, and that is the arrangement.**"

"Nobody is going to thank you for anything, and I have said that number every morning since a market and I am not going to stop because a man has decided not to ask me where I got it."

About four people on those boards have worked out since that a woman who says a figure out loud every morning has decided that if the figure is only ever said once then the day she stops is the day it is gone, and that nobody on those boards has ever asked her to stop, and that the man beside her has heard it every morning and has not asked her once and is not going to.

---

The room over a market had the thirty-ninth day of that month on its door in chalk by about the seventh hour and about nine people in it by the middle of the day, and against the far wall there was a board, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a board in a room is not a board in a market, and that the one on the far wall had not been written on since the two hundred and twenty-fifth day of this flood and the figures on it were the figures of the day before that, and that it was the only board in four hundred miles that had not lied to anybody.

A man of about twenty-seven washed it every morning and had decided not to wash the wall above it.

"Nobody asked you to stop."

"**Nobody asked me to wash the board either. I decided the wall about four weeks ago and I have not said why to anybody, and that is not a thing I am keeping back. It is a thing about how a thing looks.**"

The cloth was not clean and he had not taken it to the water since the morning before, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who washes a board every morning and does not wash a wall is a man who has made a decision about a wall, and that a decision about a wall is a bigger thing to make in a flood than a decision about a road.

He had washed the board and had not washed the wall, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about twenty-seven is the only person in this city who has an opinion about how the wall above a board looks, and that his opinion is the only thing anybody has put on that wall in about four months, and that nobody has ever asked him for it.

---

There were about four people in that room who had come up the stair for the first time, and about four who had been in six rooms in a fortnight, and the two groups had worked out within about a minute of each other which group the other one was, and neither of them said so.

The register-keeper had a sheet on the table in front of her and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register writes every sheet that leaves this city in her own hand, and that this one had to be on a wall somewhere by tonight, and that the room it is for is on the Tuesday, and that this morning is the Friday, and that those are four days, and that nobody in that room was going to say a number about them out loud to check.

"It goes to a place and not to a house."

"I have read the four lines."

"**Then you know there is a place on the low ground with nine houses behind one wall, and a sheet cannot be divided between nine households.**"

"Which one of the nine is it for."

"**It is not for one of them. That is the first thing about a notice that everybody forgets, and if you did not know it I will not believe a word you say about a form for the rest of this flood.**"

---

About four people in that room had been looking at the board on the far wall for about a fortnight and had stopped, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a wall with nothing on it is the most looked-at object in this city, and that nobody is going to write anything on it, and that the man who washes it has made a decision about that every morning for about four months without being asked and without telling anybody.

He put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket while she was saying that, which is what a man does to a thing he has decided not to take out, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about thirty-four has carried a piece of chalk in that pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month and has not used it once in all that time, and that there is a lump of chalk lying on the edge of that table about nine inches from his hand, and that he did not look at it, and that nobody in that room was going to ask him about it.

He said one true thing about the form and it was not a rule and it was not an answer.

"**There is a heading on that and four places under it, and whoever fills those in is going to put something at the top of it, and it is not going to be me.**"

Nobody said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has refused six times in about a year to say whether a line on a wall works has just said out loud, in a room, that a form is going to have somebody's name at the top of it and that it is not going to be his, and that he did not ask to be thanked for it and was not thanked for it.

---

The room emptied at about the third hour of the afternoon and the form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition nobody in that room could have described in one sentence, and the fourth of them had not been read out to anybody yet, and the board on the far wall was dry, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a board nobody has written on is the only instrument this book has that a form has to reach around, and that a man keeps a second piece of chalk in a pocket and does not use it, and that both of those are the same fact told twice by two people who have never met.
