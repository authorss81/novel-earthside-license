# Chapter 0389 — The Seventh And Last Sheet Goes Out, To A Place And Not A House

A room over a market had the seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the sixth hour, a Friday, earlier than the seven at which it is usually written, because a sheet that goes out early is a sheet that is against a wall before the light is on it, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet folded twice with a thumb, a lump of chalk, a book shut on a shelf, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and the flap of a satchel that was open for about nine seconds.

---

"This morning is the seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the room it is for is on the seventy-eighth day of that month, a Tuesday, and that is four days counted from this morning including this morning."

She said it in the local language, standing, with the sheet in her hand, and about four people in that room worked out since that this was the seventh time in about a month she had said that sentence in a room with a date on the door, and that she had not improved on it once, and that a person who has said the same sentence seven times in a month is a person who has decided that the sentence is not the thing that is going to go wrong.

There was a day on the sheet and a place on the sheet and nothing at the top of the sheet. The place was written out long, the way a place has to be written when it is not a house. The sheet was not signed and nobody in that room asked her to sign it and she has never once asked anybody to sign one of them.

"That is the seventh. There is not going to be an eighth in this flood, and I have known that for about a week and I have not said it out loud before today, and the reason I am saying it now is that a person who is going to write one more ought to say out loud that it is the last one while there is still somebody in the room to hear it."

Nobody asked her why. A room that has been writing sheets with a day on them and nothing at the top of any of them has now said out loud that it is stopping, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the price of the price is the same as it has been every other time, which is that afterward there is nothing whatever in four hundred miles that can say you were there, and that a woman who has said that seven times has just said it for the last time and there is no form in four hundred miles for a person to be given in exchange for it.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living took the sheet off the table without looking at the face of it and put it in the satchel and shut the flap, and about four people in that room watched a man put the seventh of seven sheets into a bag without turning it over, and about four of them have watched him do the same thing six times in about a month and not one of the seven has ever been opened by anybody in this city.

"You are not going to open it."

"**I have carried six of these and I have not opened one of them and this is the seventh, and the reason has not changed since the first one, and I am not going to say the reason again in front of nine people, and I have said it twice in this flood and both of the times were in a room with about nine people in it and one of those times was this morning and I am not going to be the third.**"

The room laughed and it was not much of a laugh and it stopped. A man who carries things for a living has just told a room that he is not going to say a thing he has said twice, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the price of no name at the top of a sheet is not only that nobody can do anything for you, it is that the man carrying it cannot be asked about it either, and that in a city where the only instrument is a page, the man who has the page is the one person nobody can ask anything.

He went down the nine steps at about the seventh hour and the flap went down with him, and about four people at nine steps have worked out since that a man who has carried seven things to four walls without opening one of them is the only person in this city who can be shown to have been in seven places and cannot be shown to have been anywhere.

---

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets and the light comes up off the river on them in one long sheet and does not move all morning. The man of about thirty-eight was working them with his tally-board under his arm and his knife in his hand, and the carrier came down onto the bottom of them at about the ninth hour with a satchel, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that a man who works boards has not been asked to move and has not moved, and that a sheet went against the wall at the top end of the boards with a stone off a doorstep on top of it, and that the stone came from a doorstep in this city and about four people know which one.

The carrier put it there and did not stop, and he did not stand on the boards being asked about it, and he went up the path with the flap down, and that is the whole of what a man who carries things for a living does with the seventh of seven.

The man of about thirty-eight worked for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then stopped, and looked at the sheet against the wall, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who has been putting a figure in a column four times a year since the winter before the water has just looked at the last sheet of paper in this flood go past him and has not asked what is on it.

---

"You are not going to tell me what that says."

"**Nobody in this city is going to tell you what that says, and I am not going to be asked, and I have been asked one question in this flood in a room with a date on the door and I answered it in words of my own, and I have not been asked one since and have not wanted to be, and I am not going to be sent for the room either, and I would like it said out loud on this wharf that the reason I am not going to be sent is that a man who has been sent for is a man who has been sent for, and after that there is no way anybody standing at the far end of anything can find out whether he went or not.**"

Nobody said anything. A man who has kept a tally on eleven miles of flats for about nine years and who has been in about nine rooms in a fortnight and been asked one question in one of them has just told a man working boards that he is not going to be sent for a room in this city, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that the seventh sheet has gone to four walls with nothing at the top of it and that this man has told a wharf why he is not going to be at the bottom of the stair when about nine people come up it, and that the reason is the same reason the sheet has no name on it, and that the two of them are the same decision arrived at from opposite ends of the same city.

The man of about thirty-eight picked his knife up off the boards and went on working. The stack at the top end is higher than a man and there is a strip of clear board about as long as a cart at the bottom of it, and a sheet of paper with a day on it and a place on it and nothing at the top of it is now against the wall at the top end of it with a stone on it, and the people on eleven miles of flats make what goes up that stack, and the sheet is for a room, and the room is in four days, and nobody in four hundred miles can be told what the sheet says and nobody in four hundred miles will be able to say afterwards that any person came up nine steps to it, and the man who is going to be standing at the bottom of those steps has just said out loud on a wet wharf that he is not going to be one of them.
