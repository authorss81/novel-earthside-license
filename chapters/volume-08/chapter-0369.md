# Chapter 0369 — Four Hundred And Forty, And Nowhere For It To Go

A room over a market had the fifty-fourth day of that month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Saturday, and the stair had been going up and down all morning from about nine people, and the light came in over a step at the back and did not get past the middle of the floor, and the cold was in the grain of the bench and out of the flagstones by the fourth hour, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, a satchel with a flap, and about four hundred and forty.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour with the flap down and went to the end of the bench and sat down on it, which is a thing he has not done in any of the rooms he has been in since the fortieth day of that month, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried nine things in this flood and has not opened one of them has sat down on a bench at the end of a room instead of standing by the step at the back, and that he has come in from out past the last named house and that he has been out that way twice in about a week.

"You have been out past the last named house."

"**I have been out to the boards at the bottom of the water end, and I have been out past the last named house twice this week on foot with nothing in my hands, and I am going to tell this room the one thing I have seen out there and I am not going to be asked where I got it from.**"

Nobody in that room said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has never once improved on a thing anybody said to him has come into one and asked to be the one who says the thing, and that a man who is not going to be asked where he got a thing from is a man who has decided in advance that the price of saying it is his.

---

"**Nothing has come off those boards in a cart since before the water.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has just said a period out loud in a room with a date on the door and has said it in the only words he had for it, which is that it is since before the water, and that a wharf is the only flat thing for about four streets in this city, and that about nine people in this city make a living off what comes off it, and that a man has been cutting a thing off a barrow every morning of this flood and putting it on a pile that nobody can get off that wharf.

"**The stack at the top end is higher than a man and there is only a strip of clear board left at the bottom of it.**"

"You have been past it."

"**Twice this week, and on the second time a man with a tally-board was on a barrow in the wind at the eighth hour and did not stop and did not look up. About four people on those boards have worked out that he does not look up when a bird comes over his figures, and a man does not look up when a man with a satchel comes over his figures either.**"

Nobody improved on that. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register was told the second half of a thing about her own city by a man who carries things for a living and that she did not improve on it, and that the second half is that a man has not been asked where the salt goes and that a man who has been cutting it off a barrow every morning of this flood has not said.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand in his own inside breast pocket and did not take it out, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has a piece of chalk in there that he has not used since the forty-third day of the Short Month has sat through a room find out that about four hundred and forty people are making a thing that cannot leave this city, and that about four hundred and forty is a figure four people in this city can name out loud, and that not one of the four has ever walked eleven miles to see where it comes from.

"Who makes it."

"**About four hundred and forty of them, on eleven miles of flats the other way. I walked out there once in the spring with a satchel and nothing in it and I have not been since.**"

"And the number?"

"**Nobody has asked me how many of them there are in this flood and nobody has asked anybody else, and a man has been putting one of those numbers into a form four times a year since the winter before the water, and nobody has ever asked him where he got it either.**"

That was the whole of what he said, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has just said the number out loud in a room with a date on the door, and that the woman who says the same number every morning at the head of a stall was not in this room and is not going to be told that he said it, and that there are two people in this city who can say that figure and neither of them has ever been asked for it by anybody.

---

Then the man of about thirty-nine said it, and he said it from the wall by the step at the back, out of the light, with the slate flat on his knee under his right hand and his face not turned towards the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been at the back of four rooms in a fortnight and has not been asked one question about any of it has now said the thing nobody in this city has said out loud, and that it took him as long as a number takes to say and not one beat longer, and that he has not been thanked for it and did not ask.

"**Four hundred and forty people make it. Not one of them has ever been asked anything by anybody in this flood.**"

"There is one thing that was."

"**There are nine households behind a wall on the low ground and they were asked on the forty-first day of that month, and they are not one of the four hundred and forty. I was not in the room when that was asked and nobody has told me anything since, and that is the correct arrangement and I am not going to be the one who changes it.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has just put two populations in one sentence and that one of them is the largest population anybody in this city can name and that the other one is nine households behind a wall, and that a man who has said a name off his own board in his own hand about nine years back has just said a number out loud in a room, and that he has not thanked anybody and has not been thanked and that both of those are the arrangement.

"We are not going to send anything out there."

"**Nobody is going to send anything out there. There is no form in four hundred miles for saying who lives on eleven miles of flats.**"

"There is a place on this one for a population."

"**There is, and there is no place on it for a person, and I have been filling the population in four times a year since the winter before the water out of what I am given, and I was asked once in this city what the column is for and I answered, and I am not going to start asking anybody else. That is not a decision anybody in this room has made. It is just what there is.**"

The register-keeper had the book open on the table with her cap off the pen in her left hand, and she said nothing at all for about two hours, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years and has been in about nine rooms in a fortnight sat through the only thing anybody said in this room that was worth hearing and put none of it on a page, and that the not-putting is the fourth time she has done that inside a fortnight and that nobody in that room is going to ask her about any of the four.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room in this city has found out on the fifty-fourth day of that month that the largest number of people anybody here can name are about four hundred and forty and that they are making a thing a strip of clear board on a wharf away from being got out of this city, and that a return with a heading and four places under it is going to go out on a date with a population in it that is correct, and that the second of the four places is the only one of the four that is about people at all, and that about four hundred and forty people have not been asked for anything, and that nobody in this city is going to ask them now, and that a sheet with nothing at the top of it is not going out there, and that the price of that is the same price as every other one of them, which is that nobody can do anything for you.
