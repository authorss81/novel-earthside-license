# Chapter 0373 — Nine Places On A Sheet, And A Crease That Has Gone Soft

The boards at the foot of a market in this city are not a market on a Wednesday, and about nine stalls stood under their roof of sailcloth and sacking with the trestles taken down and stacked at the back, and Tamsin Quill was sitting on the bottom step of the nine steps that go up to the room over it, and the light came in over the sacking and lay along the boards in one long grey sheet and did not move, and the cold came up out of the boards themselves, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a lump of chalk on the edge of a trestle, a board with nineteen rates on it stood against a post, a piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man who keeps the first stall, and about nine places on a folded sheet of road in an apron.

---

She had her hands in her apron. A woman who has been in a room over that stair for about a month has not once come down the nine steps and sat on them. This is the fifty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and there is nothing at the top of that stair today, and a person who comes down nine steps with nothing to do at the top of them is a person doing the other thing. About four people on those boards have worked out since that the other thing is the only work in this city nobody has to be sent for.

She took it out. It is a sheet with about nine places on it and it is folded in four, and the four creases are in the same four places. A woman who folds a sheet the same way every time is a woman who expects to open it again. About four people on those boards have been getting up off them in the cold on about nine mornings over about a month to find that she has not, and not one of them has ever said so.

She opened it on her knees. It took about as long as it takes to fill a pail. A sheet of paper with about nine places on it has been opened on the boards at the foot of a market in this city once, and nobody in this city has ever seen the two of those things at the same time.

The wind off the water got under the bottom corner of it and she put her hand on it, and she had been keeping it flat with a hand in a wind for a month without noticing that she was doing it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall came along the boards at about the eighth hour with his coat open and his hand nowhere near his inside breast pocket, and he stopped about nine feet off her and looked at the sheet on her knees. A man who writes a line on a board for a living came to within nine feet of a piece of paper with about nine places on it and did not say one word about it. The not-saying took him long enough to be noticed and not long enough to be a question. He has asked in advance not to be thanked for about a year and was not thanked.

"**I am not going to ask you what that is, and I am not going to ask you how many places are on it, and I want you to know that I counted them from where I am standing because I could not help it, and that I have counted them and I have not asked you one thing about any of them, and that those two are the only arrangements either of us has ever had with anybody.**"

Nobody improved on that. He has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he has not used since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and he has just told a woman sitting on a step that he counted the places on her page from nine feet off and did not ask. He has done it before in a room with a man and a length of new rope, and he does not know that the two of them have ever said the same sentence, and that is what this city arranges things with. About four people on those boards have worked out since that both of the arrangements on them are older than the flood and neither of them has a name.

---

She did not give it to him. She has carried the only page in this city with anything to do with a road on it in the front of an apron for about a month and has not given it to anybody in about a month, and this morning a man stood nine feet off it and counted it and she did not hand it over, and nobody on those boards expected her to. The not-handing-over is the arrangement and not a mood.

Then she said the other thing, and she said it to the boards and not to him, and it was about the paper and not about what was on it.

"**The fourth crease has gone soft. That is the whole of what has changed on it.**"

He waited. A man who has been putting a piece of chalk in a pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month had asked a woman one question about a page in the open. It was the first question he had asked anybody in about a month, and it was about a fold and not about the page, and he noticed that about a second after he had asked it.

"How long has it been soft."

"**I have folded it the same way every time since the first day of that month and I folded it the same way this morning and the fourth one took about half of what it used to take, and a crease that has gone soft is a thing about my hands and not about the paper, and I am the only person in four hundred miles who can tell you which of those two it is.**"

She had said out loud, in the open, on a Wednesday morning, that the only thing on a sheet anybody in this city could have wanted to see has changed in a month is a fold in it. The fold is hers and is not on the sheet and would not go with it if she gave the sheet away, and she has given the reason for saying it out loud: a person who keeps a page in an apron is going to be asked about the page one day and is not going to be asked about the fold.

---

He went up to the stall and unhooked the sacking and began to take the boards down, two of them at a time and no barrow, and got his hands back to work at about the eighth hour of a Wednesday on about nine boards he can lift. She folded the sheet into four with the same four creases and put it in her apron. The fourth crease is the one that gives, and nobody is going to write that down either.

The light went off the boards at about the fourth hour the way it does on a day when nothing is being sold. There is one piece of paper in four hundred miles that anybody in this city would be able to say was the reason for a road, and it was on a woman's knees at the foot of nine steps for about half an hour in the open, and nine people could see it, and not one of the nine said a word about it. The man who was asked nothing about it counted it and told her he had counted it, and that is the whole of what has happened to a page in this flood that anybody could put in a column. The fourth crease of it will take less again by about the fourth hour tomorrow, and nobody is going to write that down either. About four people on those boards have worked out since that the crease is the only part of it that anybody is losing.
