# Chapter 0374 — A Room Deciding Not To Say What It Is For

A room over a market had the fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and about nine people in it by the middle of the morning, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and lay along a bench that four of them were sitting on, and the cold came up out of the floor and got into the seam of a slate on the shelf behind the table, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on the shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people doing the arrangements.

---

The arrangements were the whole of it. Who would be on the stair at the seventh hour and who at the eighth. Whether the bench would be clear or the form would be on it. Whether the door would have a day on it in chalk or in a lump of chalk on the sill. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has spent a fortnight finding out that it has no form for what it is for and has arrived, the day before, at arranging the furniture, and that a room which has nothing to decide will decide the length of a bench.

Nobody was asked anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one was in that room from the eighth hour until the fourth and did not ask one question about any of the arrangements, and that a woman who walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth of them is the only person in this city who has never once asked a room what it is for, and that about four people in that room have worked out since that it is the day before, and that nobody in that room is going to be the one who asks her about that either.

Then the man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and said the thing out loud, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he said it to the bench and not to anybody, and that nobody in that room asked him a question about it before or after.

"**We are not going to say what this room is for. I am saying that now, in a room, in daylight, with the day on the door, and nobody is going to ask me why, and I have been saying it to benches and to walls for about a month and this is the first time I have said it in a room to people.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man with his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it has heard the sentence he expected to hear and has not taken his hands out, and that the nine words in the inside of that coat have nothing to do with it, and that nobody in that room knows that and is not going to be told.

"The reason I am saying it is that a room which says what it is for has to be right about it."

"**And if it is wrong then the people who came up those nine steps were sent for the wrong thing, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can tell them afterwards that they were not, and that is the whole of my reason and I would like it written down somewhere that I gave it before anybody asked me for it.**"

The room agreed to it at once and nobody said anything about the agreeing, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has spent about a month refusing to say anything and has just agreed a thing in one breath, and that what it agreed was to not have a subject, and that about nine people in four hundred miles are going to come up a stair on Friday morning and not one of them is going to be told beforehand, and that the price of that is the same price as every other one of them, which is that nobody can do anything for you.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the nine steps at about the ninth hour with the flap of the satchel down and stood at the end of the bench, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living came up nine steps on a Thursday to arrange a room, and that he has never once been in this room when it was arranging anything, and that the reason he came up this morning is not in this room.

"You will not tell anybody what it is for."

"**Nobody in this city is going to be told what it is for, and that includes the four people who are going to come up those steps, and it includes the woman who is sitting at the far end of this bench with a pen she has not taken out of her apron.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living stood at the end of a bench on a Thursday and said who was not going to be told, and that he named a woman in the room and did not name the four of them, and that about four people in that room have worked out that he named the woman because she is the only one in the room who could have drawn a thing and did not.

"I have been in about two hundred rooms in this city."

"**And I have never once improved on a thing anybody said in one of them, and I have never once been the man who asked what a room was for, and I am not going to start on a Thursday.**"

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and he said nothing at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who is right about pages did not say one word on the day before the day this city has been walking towards for a month, and that nobody in that room expected him to, and that the not-saying is now four rooms in a fortnight without a question in any of them.

---

The room emptied at about the fifth hour and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room over a market has arranged the length of a bench and the string of a door and the hours of a stair, and has decided out loud, in one breath, not to say what it is for, and that nobody in four hundred miles has been told, and that a woman who keeps a register for nine years stood in that room for the whole of the morning and put none of it on a page, and that the second slate was on the shelf behind the table the whole day with a date on it and nothing under it, and that nobody went near the shelf, and that the door was shut at the fifth hour with the fifty-ninth on it in chalk and nothing above it.

Nobody sent for anybody. About four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that a woman with a folded sheet of road in her apron was not in that room on the Thursday and has not been told what the room is for, and that nobody sent for her, and that a person who is not sent for is a person who comes, and that about four people on those boards have worked out that if she does not come then about nine people are going to fill a room tomorrow and the room is going to be about nothing at all, and that a man of about thirty-four with a piece of chalk he does not use has known that since about the second hour of the afternoon and has not said it to anybody.
