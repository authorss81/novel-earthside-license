# Chapter 0374 — A Room Deciding Not To Say What It Is For

A room over a market had the fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and about nine people in it by the middle of the morning, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and lay along a bench that four of them were sitting on, and the cold came up out of the floor and got into the seam of a slate on the shelf behind the table, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on the shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people doing the arrangements.

---

The arrangements were the whole of it. Who would be on the stair at the seventh hour and who at the eighth. Whether the bench would be clear or the form would be on it. Whether the door would have a day on it in chalk or in a lump of chalk on the sill. A room in this city has spent a fortnight finding out that it has no form for what it is for and has arrived, the day before, at arranging the furniture. A room which has nothing to decide will decide the length of a bench.

Nobody was asked anything. A woman of about twenty-one was in that room from the eighth hour until the fourth and did not ask one question about any of the arrangements. A woman who walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth of them is the only person in this city who has never once asked a room what it is for. It is the day before, and nobody in that room is going to be the one who asks her about that either, and about four people in that room have worked out since that not asking her is going to cost the room more than asking her would have.

Then the man of about thirty-four who keeps the stall put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and said the thing out loud, to the bench and not to anybody, and nobody in that room asked him a question about it before or after.

"**We are not going to say what this room is for. I am saying that now, in a room, in daylight, with the day on the door, and nobody is going to ask me why, and I have been saying it to benches and to walls for about a month and this is the first time I have said it in a room to people.**"

A man with his coat on and his hands in the pockets of it has heard the sentence he expected to hear and has not taken his hands out. The nine words in the inside of that coat have nothing to do with it, and nobody in that room knows that and is not going to be told.

"The reason I am saying it is that a room which says what it is for has to be right about it."

"**And if it is wrong then the people who came up those nine steps were sent for the wrong thing, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can tell them afterwards that they were not, and that is the whole of my reason and I would like it written down somewhere that I gave it before anybody asked me for it.**"

The room agreed to it at once and nobody said anything about the agreeing. A room in this city has spent about a month refusing to say anything and has just agreed a thing in one breath, and what it agreed was to not have a subject. About nine people in four hundred miles are going to come up a stair on Friday morning and not one of them is going to be told beforehand, and the price of that is the same price as every other one of them, which is that nobody can do anything for you.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the nine steps at about the ninth hour with the flap of the satchel down and stood at the end of the bench. He has never once been in this room when it was arranging anything, and the reason he came up this morning is not in this room.

"You will not tell anybody what it is for."

"**Nobody in this city is going to be told what it is for, and that includes the four people who are going to come up those steps, and it includes the woman who is sitting at the far end of this bench with a pen she has not taken out of her apron.**"

He had said who was not going to be told, and he had named a woman in the room and had not named the four of them. He named the woman because she is the only one in the room who could have drawn a thing and did not, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a carrier can hold a fact in front of a whole room and keep it for nine years by saying it once to the wrong person.

"I have been in about two hundred rooms in this city."

"**And I have never once improved on a thing anybody said in one of them, and I have never once been the man who asked what a room was for, and I am not going to start on a Thursday.**"

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee and his right hand over the face of it, and he said nothing at all. A man who is right about pages did not say one word on the day before the day this city has been walking towards for a month, and nobody in that room expected him to. The not-saying is now four rooms in a fortnight without a question in any of them.

---

The room emptied at about the fifth hour. A room over a market has arranged the length of a bench and the string of a door and the hours of a stair, and has decided out loud, in one breath, not to say what it is for, and nobody in four hundred miles has been told. A woman who keeps a register for nine years stood in that room for the whole of the morning and put none of it on a page. The second slate was on the shelf behind the table the whole day with a date on it and nothing under it, and nobody went near the shelf. The door was shut at the fifth hour with the fifty-ninth on it in chalk and nothing above it. About four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a room which has nothing to decide will decide the length of a bench, and that on this morning it decided that too.

Nobody sent for anybody. A woman with a folded sheet of road in her apron was not in that room on the Thursday and has not been told what the room is for, and nobody sent for her. A person who is not sent for is a person who comes. If she does not come, then about nine people are going to fill a room tomorrow and the room is going to be about nothing at all, and a man of about thirty-four with a piece of chalk he does not use has known that since about the second hour of the afternoon and has not said it to anybody.
