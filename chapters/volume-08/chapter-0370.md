# Chapter 0370 — A Room Doing The Half Of A Task That Is Not Its Own

A room over a market had the fifty-fifth day of that month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Sunday, and the stair went up about nine times before about the fourth hour and then stopped, and about nine people stayed in it from the seventh hour of the morning until about the ninth hour of the evening, and the light came in over a step at the back and went off the bench before the middle of the day and did not come back, and the cold came up out of the floor at about the fourth hour and stayed, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf with a date on it and nothing under it, and a task that belonged to a man who was not in the room.

---

The task is his and the day is the room's, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about fifty-two who digs walked eleven miles in the night to be asked a question on the fifty-first day of that month and has been asked for nothing since, and that the question he was given was a task, and that the task is now the only thing on the agenda of a room in this city with a date on the door.

The task is that he will walk it. From the near end, to the stone, and back, and say whether a man standing at the other end of it would meet him or whether he would come out past the stone nobody put there, and that he will not put a hand on it, and he will not put a rod in it, and he will not leave a mark of any kind on it, not a stake and not a heap of stone and not a thing he could be found by, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has put a condition on a task before anybody could put a condition on him and that the condition is that nothing is left behind him, and that the room agreed it in one breath and has not gone back to it.

He is not in the room. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has walked eleven miles three times in about a fortnight and is not here on the Sunday, and that nobody knows whether he is at the end of it, and that a man who said he would not offer a day has not been asked for one by anybody in this city, and that the only person in this city who could say whether he has gone is the man who went out there once with a satchel and nothing in it and has not been since.

---

Nobody sent for him. That is the whole of what the room did about it for the first two hours, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has had a task on it for three days and has not sent anybody, and that a man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living went down the low ground to the left with nothing in his hands at about the ninth hour and was not asked where he was going and did not say, and that he was gone about two hours and came back and said nothing about it and that nobody in that room has asked him since.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall sat at the end of the bench with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and about four people in that room have worked out since that the stall man has spent two hours of a Sunday with his hand over a thing he decided in the Short Month not to take out, and that a room in this city has watched him do it three times now and that nobody in any of the three rooms has said one word about it.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a board of nineteen rates in four markets for four years and put it down flat on a wet board on a Friday and said a figure out loud that he has not repeated to anybody since, and who has said a number out loud in a room on Saturday and been thanked by nobody, and who has been asked nothing, has now been in this room for a second Sunday, and that about four people have worked out that he comes, and that nobody is going to ask him why.

---

Then the man of about fifty-nine came up the nine steps, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man walks a road four hundred miles long with a barrow and has walked it four times in nine years and has never been in a room in this city in his life, and that he is here on a Sunday morning with no coat on and a hurdle of hazel rods nowhere in sight, and that nobody sent him and that he did not come for anything anybody could name.

He had not been in a room in this city before that morning. About four people in that room have worked out since that the stall man has had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket for two hours of that Sunday and has just watched a man walk into one for the first time in his life and has not worked out what a man looks like when he does not know where to stand, and has not said so, and is not going to.

Nobody asked him anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that about nine people in a room with a date on the door have not asked a man who walks a four-hundred-mile road with a barrow one question, and that the man of about thirty-one, who has not asked one question in four times up this road, did not ask him one either, and that the two of them are the only two people in this city who have never asked anybody anything and that they have now been in the same room.

"You are not from the low ground."

"**I am from the head of a road out past the last named house. There is a gate at the head of it and I put a bar in it nine years back and I hung a hurdle on it, and I have walked four hundred miles of that road four times in nine years.**"

"You have not been in one of these rooms."

"**Not one. I came up nine steps this morning because a man with a satchel comes out past the last named house with nothing in his hands and does not stop at anything, and a man who does not stop at a thing is a thing you notice, and I have noticed him four times in nine years and I have never once asked him why.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has said the whole of that in four sentences without being asked one question, and that a man who carries things for a living does not know that anything has come of it, and that he has not been asked, and that the reason he gave for coming up nine steps is a thing about himself and not about this city.

---

They asked him about the man, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has asked a man about a man of about fifty-two who digs, and that the question was not whether it would go and that the two of those things have never once been confused in this city, and that the question was a different one and not a number.

"Has anybody gone for him."

"**No.**

"Somebody always has."

"**And nobody is going to. That is the whole of what I have come up nine steps for, and I have not got a barrow today and I have not got a hurdle, and I have got one thing.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has walked a road four hundred miles long four times in nine years has never once had anybody come up it after him, and that the man standing in the middle of the floor knows it.

"And that is not because nobody has ever wanted to know where he got to."

"**It is because a man who has been sent for is a man who has been sent for, and after that there is no way anybody standing at the far end of anything can find out whether he went or not.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has said the whole of it out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door on a Sunday morning, and that it is about a man who is not in the room, and that nobody in that room has been asked what the man of about fifty-nine thinks about anything, and that what he has just given the room is not a rule and is not a decision and is about the price of a thing being findable, and that the man of about thirty-nine has gone very still where he is sitting.

The room accepted it and the accepting took no time at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has spent about a fortnight refusing to say anything agreed in about four seconds on not sending a man eleven miles on a Sunday, and that the man who is not in the room does not know that about nine people have agreed, and that the man they did not send for is not going to be told and has not asked to be told.

---

The rest of the day was the other half of the task, and the other half of the task was nothing. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city spent a Sunday doing the one thing in four hundred miles that a form cannot be filled in from, which is sitting in a room with about nine people in it while a man who has not arrived is somewhere about eleven miles off, and that it produced nothing, and that the form on the table is going to go out on a date with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and that nobody in the room was asked whether any of it would go, and that a room in this city does not know what it is for, and that there is a sheet of paper in this city with nothing at the top of it that has not been written yet.

The register-keeper wrote the day on the slate in chalk at about the ninth hour of the evening, in front of about nine people, and put nothing under it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a public register for nine years has done that four times inside about a month now and that it is the whole of the vocabulary a fact has in this city, and that none of the four of them says anything to anybody.

Nobody asked anybody whether anything was going to go. The room emptied at about the ninth hour and a man of about fifty-nine who has never been in a room in this city went down nine steps and out past the last named house, and about four people who came down that stair have worked out since that there is a gate at the head of a four-hundred-mile road with a bar in it and a hurdle on it, and the hurdle on that gate has been made twice by the same man, and that a man who put it in has just spent a Sunday in a room telling nine people not to go and look for the one person who has not come, and that nobody asked him about the gate, and that nobody is going to, and that the man who has not come is about eleven miles the other way with a spade over his shoulder and no rod, and that about nine people are going to go on waiting, and that this city has no form for that and does not need one.
