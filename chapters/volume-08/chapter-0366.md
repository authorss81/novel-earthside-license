# Chapter 0366 — A Condition, And A Mark He Will Not Leave

A room over a market had the fifty-first day of that month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Wednesday, and the ninth step of the stair had been loud twice before the eighth hour, and the light came in over a step at the back and stopped halfway along a bench nobody was on, and the cold came up out of the floor at about the fourth hour and stayed the rest of the day, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a slate, a lump of chalk, a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it, a stone off a doorstep, a spade with the top of its handle worn shiny in one place, and a man who had come eleven miles in the night.

---

The sheet went out before the light got to the bench. It was folded twice with a thumb and it went to a place and not to a house, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this morning is the fifty-first day of that month, a Wednesday, and the room it is for is on the fifty-fifth, a Sunday, and that those are four days counted from this morning including this morning, and that a man with a satchel took it down nine steps and over the low ground without opening it, and that nobody in that room has ever asked him to.

---

The man of about fifty-two who digs came in at about the seventh hour and sat down and put his hands flat on the table, the way a man does before he starts on the thing he actually does, and then he took them off again, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who walks eleven miles in the dark and gets in before a man who has been in this city since before the water has decided something about the table in the first four minutes of a room, and that what he has decided is not about the room.

The rod was not with him. It is a length of ash with a flat blade on the end of it, ground down on one side and not the other, and it is standing on its end in the corner of a hut about eleven miles the other way where the wall is thickest, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has been asked for a task and has not gone and got the only instrument in four hundred miles that would tell anybody anything about a mark in a field, and that not getting it is not a decision and is what a man does on the morning after he has heard.

"Nev Cutler."

"**Fifty-two, and it says fifty-two in the hand, and the hand is my own and not a clerk's, and the trade it gives is digging, which is the word this room has been using for a man with a spade for a month and is the wrong word and is the only one there is.**"

Nobody improved on that. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has been told his own name out loud in this city twice and that both times it was in a room with a date on the door and about nine people in it, and that he has never been told it on a road, and that a man who has written a question to himself on a board off the wall of his own hut has still not opened the coat in front of anybody.

---

Then he was asked, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the asking was done by a woman who keeps a public register and that she asked it standing up with her cap off the pen, and that she did not ask him whether it was going to work and that nobody in that room has asked anybody that since a sheet went up on a wall with nine households behind it.

"I want you to walk it. From the near end, to the stone, and back, and I want to know whether a man standing at the other end of it would meet you or whether he would come out past the stone nobody put there, and I am not going to ask you for anything else."

He did not answer that straight away, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been asked a task in a room in this city has not said yes first, and that he had it ready before he came up those stairs, and that a man who puts a condition on a thing before he has agreed to it is a man who has been thinking about the shape of the work rather than the fact of it.

"I will do it. And here is the condition, and I am giving it before anybody has put one on me, and I would like it said in this room with the day on the door that I said it first and not after."

The room did not make a sound.

"**I will walk it and I will not put my hands on it, and I will not put a rod in it, and I will not leave a mark of any kind on it, not a stake and not a heap of stone and not a thing I could be found by, and that is the whole of the condition and I am not going to add to it and I am not going to take any of it off.**"

The room accepted it in about four seconds.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has spent about a fortnight refusing to say anything and has just agreed a thing in one breath with a man who had put a condition on it before anybody could put one on him, and that the condition was that the man will do the whole of the work and leave nothing behind him, and that the room has just agreed to be given something it cannot check, and that it agreed to it faster than it has agreed to anything in about a month.

---

Nobody asked him whether it would go, and nobody asked him about the road, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has come eleven miles twice in about a fortnight and has been asked a task in a room both times and has not been asked one question about whether any of this is going anywhere, and that a room in this city has three of the four people who said yes in it and the fourth of the four is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and that a party of four is four.

"I can tell you what the ground is like along the whole of it from the end, without putting a hand on it, and that is the trade and not a favour, and there is nobody in this room I would want to say it to twice."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket while that was being said, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a piece of chalk in that pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month has just watched another man in this city put a condition on a thing before anybody could put one on him, and that he has asked in advance not to be thanked for anything he has done in about a year and is not thanked.

"You are not going to be asked when."

"**I have not offered a day and I am not going to, and the day is the only thing anybody in this city has been able to put on anything for a month, and I have found the way round the whole of it by not offering one, and I would like it noticed that I have done that twice now.**"

---

The room emptied at about the ninth hour and the form was on the table with three of its four places in a condition and one of them in a decision, and the man of about fifty-two who digs went down the nine steps and across the low ground and out toward the eleven miles of flats with a spade over his shoulder that he did not put in the ground anywhere between here and there, and about four people who came down that stair have worked out since that a man has been asked in a room in daylight with a date on the door to go and walk a mark in a field and come back and say whether a man on the other end of it would meet him, and that he has agreed to it and put a condition on it, and that the condition is that nothing is left behind him, and that there is no form in four hundred miles for what a man finds when he has left nothing behind him, and that the man of about fifty-two who digs went eleven miles to be told that and will go eleven miles back for it, and that nobody asked him whether any of it will go.
