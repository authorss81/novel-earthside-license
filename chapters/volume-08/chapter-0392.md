# Chapter 0392 — A Name Said Out Loud In Daylight, And Not Written Down

A room over a market had the seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Monday, and about nine people were in it by the middle of the morning, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along the bench and stopped in the middle of the table, and the cold was in the folds of a book that lives on a shelf, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form on the table with three of its four places in a condition, a book open face up at a ruled line, a lump of chalk, a slate, and a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it.

---

The form is going out tomorrow.

That is the whole of what was settled before anybody came up the nine steps. The day the form goes out is the seventy-eighth day of that month, a Tuesday, and the room it is for is the room this is, and the sheet that brought about nine people up these steps went out on the seventy-fourth day, four days before that Tuesday, unsigned, with a day on it and a place on it and nothing at the top of it, and the woman who wrote it said out loud on that Friday that it was the seventh of those and that there is not going to be an eighth in this flood.

Nobody wrote a sheet this morning. There is no eighth one and there is not going to be one, and a room in this city has been living on the warning of a sheet for about a month and is now living on the thing the sheet was for, which is that the warning is over and the form is on the table.

The first of the four places has nowhere to start. The second is in a condition. The third is empty and is empty on purpose, and the woman who keeps the book that it goes out of has left that column empty since a Thursday in that month and has told a room full of people she would walk out of a room rather than fill it. The fourth is a household's and the household said no on a step before anybody asked her and is not going to be asked.

So four places and none of them and a form with a heading on it, and about nine people have been sitting in front of it for about a fortnight.

---

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing out loud that everybody in that room had worked out and nobody had put in the room, and she said it in the order of the facts, which is a thing she does.

"**A form with a heading on it and four places under it and nothing at the top of it is not a return. It is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper goes back, and a person gets sent for.**"

Nobody improved on that. She had said out loud, in a room in daylight with a date in chalk on the door, that the only thing standing between a piece of paper and a man riding nine hundred miles for it is a name written across the top of it, and that there is no name at the top of this one, and that there has not been one on any of the seven sheets in this flood either, and that those two facts have been in the same room all morning without meeting.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket where a piece of chalk has been since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and he did not take it out, and he did not look round, and a man who says a thing out loud before he is asked for it said nothing for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

---

Adrian Vale was at the far end of the bench with the book open in front of him.

The woman who keeps it had turned it round and it was about nine feet from the middle of the table, and he was reading it from the top down the way a man reads a thing that is not going to be lent. It has three columns. The first took what went out and the second took what came back, and there are nine years of entries in it, and he read all of them and then read the third one, which is empty, and put a finger about a finger's width back from the edge of it and did not touch it.

Nobody asked him anything. There is no form in four hundred miles with a place for a man at the end of a bench, and the not-asking in that room is the arrangement and stopped being a courtesy about a month ago. He had a sheet folded into four in the inside of his coat with a date on the back of it and two names on the front of it, and the coat was buttoned over that, and a man who has a page in a coat is the one kind of page in this city that cannot be asked for.

He was in that room for about four hours and he read a book that was not his and asked nothing and was asked nothing, and a man who reads the empty column as well as the full ones is a man who has decided that what is missing from a page is the part of it.

---

"**I am going to say a name out loud in this room, and I am not going to write it down, and I am going to say why I am not going to write it down before I say it, which is the way I do things and everybody in here knows it.**"

The man of about thirty-four had not turned round on the bench.

"**The name is the name of a woman who was in this room on Saturday, and she went down a road yesterday morning at about the seventh hour with a barrow, and she is a day out of this city and she cannot be asked and she is not going to be asked, and I have not been asked by anybody in this room to say it and I am not going to wait to be.**"

Nobody said anything. About nine people in a room over a market had just been told out loud that a name was about to be said in it, and about four of them had worked out in the same moment that the three people who went out of this room on Saturday afternoon are on a road a day out of this city, and that a person on a road a day out of this city is a person who cannot be told anything.

"You are going to put a name on that form."

"**I am going to put a name on that form and it is not going to be on a page in this city afterwards, and I am going to say the price of that out loud first, and the price is that in about nine years a man nine hundred miles off is going to ask for the person that name is on.**"

Nobody in that room stopped him, and a man who says the price of a thing before he does it is a man who has been asked to do things in about nine years and has never once had to say what a thing costs.

"**And this city is going to have nothing whatever to hand him, and that is the price, and I am paying it, and it is not being paid for me.**"

Then he said it.

---

The room was in daylight and there was a date in chalk on the door and about nine people in it, and a man said a woman's name out loud, once, and nobody in that room wrote it down.

Nobody wrote it down. There is no second pen in that room and the one pen was lying on the table with the nib pointing away from the book, and the woman who keeps the book did not pick it up, and a man who has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket and who has said a thing out loud before he is asked about nine times in this flood did not take his hand out of his pocket. The name went into the air of a room over a market and nowhere else, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the first time in about a month that a person has been named in it at all, and that seven sheets went out of this city with nothing at the top of any of them, and that the reason for all seven and for this is the same one and it is being broken in a different direction.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her hand flat on the closed half of the book and left it there.

"**Then it goes out on the top of a form and nobody in this city has it, and I want that said out loud in this room by somebody who is not me, and I have not asked for it to be said.**"

"**It is said out loud now and you did not ask me for it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would like it noticed that a man who has said no to a thing for about a year has spent a Monday morning talking a room into putting a name on a page, and that those two facts are in the same man and I have not got a speech for it.**"

The room did not make a sound. Nobody thanked him. A man with a piece of chalk he does not use has just said out loud, in a room in daylight, that he would put a name on a page he cannot see, and that the reason is that a page with nothing at the top of it goes back, and that the price is that a person becomes findable, and he is right and that being right is what has cost him the two of those.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the form was on the table with the first place having nowhere to start and the second in a condition and the third empty and the fourth empty, and the book was shut and went back on the shelf it lives on. The second slate had been on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it all day and nobody went near the shelf. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not in that room at the fourth hour, and the light on the boards at the foot of the market had not gone off the water yet, and the other of the two women of about twenty-nine in this city was not going to be in that room on the day a name is in it, and the two of them have not been in one room for about a month and are not going to be.

A room in this city has spent a Monday morning deciding that a form with four places under a heading and nothing at the top of it is a piece of paper, and a man with a piece of chalk in his pocket said a woman's name out loud in daylight in front of about nine people, and nobody wrote it down, and the price of the not-writing has been said out loud by the man who is paying it. What it costs is not going to be known to anybody in this city, including the three people it is about, one of whom is a day down a road and is not on any page in four hundred miles, and it is going to be known in about nine years by a clerk who will write a letter and get nothing back. A room costs something on the days it is open, and this is what this one cost.
