# Chapter 0378 — Two Things With Four Places In Each, Not Put Side By Side

A room over a market had the sixty-third day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Monday, and about nine people in it by the middle of the morning, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and lay along a bench and stopped at a case, and the cold was in the oiled cloth round a handle and in the grain of a bench that had been wiped down twice, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, the second slate from the shelf with its date on it and nothing under it, a case the size of a bread chest with a docket tied twice to the handle, and about nine people.

---

Both of them had been in that room for about a month and neither of them had been opened. A piece of paper with a heading and four places ruled under it has been on a bench in that room for four rooms' worth of mornings. A case the size of a bread chest has been shut on a table in the same room since before any of those four. About nine people have been looking at both of them at the same time since the fortieth day of that month and have not once put them next to each other.

Nobody had a reason they could have said out loud. A woman who keeps a register for nine years has her book open on the table in front of the bench with three columns in it and the third of them empty on purpose, and she has not offered anybody the book this morning. A man who writes lines for a living has been at the back of that room since the eighth hour with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and his eyes on about nine inches of bare bench between a piece of paper and a table.

---

Then he put his hand out and stopped about nine inches short of the form. The man who keeps the first stall, with a piece of chalk he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, put a hand out over a bench towards a document and stopped, and he stopped because he had decided to stop and not because he had run out of bench.

"I am not going to put those two together, and I am going to say out loud why, in a room, with the day on the door, and I am not going to be asked why afterwards, and I have been trying to put those two together since a Tuesday on a lane and I could not do it then and I can see that I was wrong to try."

He did not turn round from the bench.

"**A page on this bench has four places under a heading, and a docket in that case has four places in it, and not one of the eight of them is the same as any of the other seven. The date on one of them is not the date on the other one. The gate on one of them is not the gate on the other one.**"

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the end of the bench with the flap of the satchel down and had been there since about the ninth hour. He heard another man say eight places out loud and count them, and he counted them as well, and he has not said one word about either.

"That is eight."

"**And the third place in that case is a word that nobody in this city has said out loud in a room in about nine years, and I have been wondering about that word for a fortnight, and I have not said it, and I am not going to, and that is not modesty.**"

Nobody in that room said anything. A man with a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket has just said out loud, in a room in daylight, that there is a word in a document nine hundred miles off that he has declined to use for a fortnight, and the whole of what anybody in this city knows about that word is the declining.

"That is a woman at a gate."

"**That is a woman at a gate telling me the whole of a roll in four sentences to nine feet of stone and refusing to be told where two bars are, and I have not seen her since and I am not going to ask her, and about four of you know what she said and about the rest of you do not, and I would like that to stay exactly as it is.**"

The room did not make a sound. A man has just said out loud, in a room in daylight, that there are eight places between two documents in this city and that not one of the eight is the same as another, and he has said it about a word by saying that he is not going to say it.

---

"The roll at the gate has three places in it."

"**A roll at a gate has a gate on it and a keeper on it and a set of days, and that is three places and it is the whole of what is on it, and that roll is four streets off and it is not in this room and it is not going to be brought into this room.**"

A man who writes a line on a board for a living has said the whole of a roll out loud in a room for the first time, and he said it about nine feet from a case that has a different roll in it. The two of them are rolls of the same gates, and the man has just said so.

"And the other one."

"**And the other one is not going to be taken out of that case and laid on this bench, and those are two rolls of the same gates and neither of us is going to hold them both at once, and I have never once wanted to know what the difference between them is worth, and that is not a thing I have decided to be proud of.**"

Two people in that room have just said out loud, in about four seconds between them, that two rolls of the same gates are going to stay in two rooms in two streets of this city. The reason is not that anybody is keeping a secret. The reason is that nobody four hundred miles off has ever sent anybody to find out whether the two of them agree, and a document that is correct does not have to agree with a document that is correct. This is the first thing a room in this city has been able to say about a thing that is nine hundred miles off without anybody in the room being wrong, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a city which cannot check anything is not thereby a city of liars.

Nobody argued with it. A room in this city has just made a decision about two pieces of paper out loud, and the decision is to do nothing. The woman who keeps a public register said nothing at all while it was being made. She has been in about nine rooms in a fortnight and has said nothing in five of them, and the not-saying is not a position and is not going to be described by anybody in that room as one.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand and said nothing at all for the whole of the morning. A man who is right about pages did not say one word on the morning a room decided not to do the one thing it had been able to do, and nobody in that room was going to ask him whether he would have done it, and nobody did.

---

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the case was on the table where it had been at the seventh hour and the docket was tied twice to the handle under a seal the colour of a wet stone. A case that has been shut in this city since a day in the Long Month has not been opened in that room in a month and was not opened in it today. A form with four places under a heading went back onto the bench with the fourth one empty, and the two of them are four feet apart and are going to be four feet apart on every morning that this city has a morning for. The whole of what a room in this city decided today was that it is not going to find out. Not finding out is free, and the price of not finding out is the same price as the price of everything else, and nobody can do anything for you. About four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that the four feet is going to be the distance for as long as there is a room.
