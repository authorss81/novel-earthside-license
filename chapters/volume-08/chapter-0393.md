# Chapter 0393 — The Form Goes Out On Its Date, And The Wall Says One Thing

A room over a market had the seventy-eighth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Tuesday, and the stair had been up four times before anybody came up it a fifth, and the light came in over the step at the back with no name on it and lay along the bench and went off the end of the bench and did not come back, and the cold was out of the flagstones and in the grain of a table, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form with a name at the top of it, a book shut on a shelf, a lump of chalk, a pen, and a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it.

---

The name was on it and it was the only name in that room. Nobody had written it down and it had been on the top of that form since about the ninth hour on the Monday, because a man said it out loud in daylight in front of about nine people and then a woman wrote it at the top of a piece of paper and did not write it anywhere else, and there is no copy of it in this city and there is not going to be one.

A form with a heading on it and a name at the top of it is a return. Everything under that heading is what makes it correct. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register wrote the name at the top of it on Monday afternoon and has not looked at the top of that piece of paper since, and she has not been in any other room for about a fortnight.

The first of the four places has nowhere to start. It has had nowhere to start since the winter before the water and it is going out on a Tuesday with nowhere to start, and about nine people in that room have watched a man leave a blank at the top of a column for about a month and about none of them have worked out how to fill it and no form in four hundred miles is going to be able to tell them either.

The third of the four places is empty and is empty on purpose.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour and nobody had sent for him.

He had his knife in his hand and a lump of chalk in the other one and he came up them at the pace a man comes up nine steps when he has not been asked to come up them, and about four people in that room worked out in the time it took him to cross it that a man who works boards at the bottom of this city said out loud on Friday, on those boards, that he was not going to be sent for a room, and that he had come anyway, and that the difference between those two things is the whole of what a man in this city has learned in about a month.

He did not sit down. He went to the second of the four places, which is a population, and which has been empty of a figure in every room it has been in, and he put the chalk on the table and then picked it up again and put the number in with his own hand.

"**About four hundred and forty, and that is the figure I send and it has been the figure I send four times a year since the winter before the water, and there is another one and it is not going on this.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what the other one was, and nobody has asked him in about a fortnight, and a man who has said out loud in front of nine people that there are two of a figure and will not say which one he believes has been saying the safest sentence available to him for about a month and it has not protected him from anything.

"You have not been asked for that figure in about nine years."

"**Nobody has asked me for that figure since the winter before the water and nobody is going to, and the reason I put it in this morning is that it is on a piece of paper with a name at the top of it now, and a figure that is on no page at all is a figure that belongs to me and I have wanted that for about as long as I have wanted the four days of notice they give you for a room.**"

Then he went down the four places in the order they are in. The first, with nowhere to start. The second, in his own hand. The third, empty on purpose, and he put the chalk down and did not go near it. And the fourth, which is a share, and a share is a household's, and the household said no on a step before anybody asked her.

The fourth place went out empty. It was going out with a heading on it and a name at the top of it and one of four places filled in by a man out of what he is given, and it is correct, and about nine people in that room have known that since a Sunday.

---

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step, with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand, and he had been at the back of every room this city has had for about a fortnight without being asked one question in any of them, and he said one sentence, and he said it before the man at the end of the bench had the cloth out of his pocket.

"The day after a name at the top of a page there is a column. And the column wants a place, and a place is somewhere a person can be shown to have been, and there is not one on that form and I am not going to stand here and pretend that there is."

Nobody in that room told him he was wrong.

Nobody told him he was right either, and a man who is right about a thing in a room in this flood gets one turn at being right in, and he took that turn, and the room let him have all of it, and the page was going to go anyway and everybody in that room knew it before the man at the far end of the bench had put the cloth back in his pocket.

The man of about thirty-four did not turn round. He had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and he had said a woman's name out loud in that room on the Monday, and a man who has said the worst true thing in a room once in about a year does not turn round for the second one, and that is not a way of carrying on. It is a way of not being thanked for the first.

"Nobody is going to stop that page."

"**Nobody is going to stop that page and I am not going to ask anybody to, and I have said the one thing I came into that room to say and I have been right about it, and being right about it is the whole of what I have got out of this morning, and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be.**"

---

The man of about thirty-eight picked the form up off the table and put it against his chest the way a man carries a thing that is going out on a date, and went to the step at the back with no name on it, and the wall said one thing.

It said it once. It is entered that nobody in that room can be told where it came from, and the woman at the far end of the bench did not go the colour of the wall and nothing else in the room moved at all.

> Nobody has to be told what a form is for,
> and a form that has been filled in without that
> has been filled in correctly.

He was on his feet before the last of it had finished coming up, and the form was against his chest, and the refusal was out of him in about four seconds.

"**I answered, and nobody had to.**"

Nobody improved on the six of those. Nobody thanked him for them, and nobody thanked him for the other thing either, and he asked in advance about that one and has not been thanked for it at any point in this flood. A man who works boards at the bottom of this city was asked in a room in daylight on the forty-second day of that month what a column is for, and he answered in his own words, and he has not been asked anything since and has not wanted to be, and the wall has now told a room of about nine people that he did not have to be asked at all, and that is the whole of what it has taken off a man in about a month.

Then he went down the nine steps with the form against his chest and about four people who came down those steps after him watched a man who has been putting a figure in a column four times a year since before the water go down to the only flat thing for about four streets in this city and put it on the boards, with a stone off a doorstep on the top of it, which is what has been done to every sheet that has gone out of this city in this flood and is the only way anybody in four hundred miles will ever know that it did.

---

The page went anyway.

That morning is not a silence, and the count of this city's silences is one lower because of it, and that count is derived, on no page, in no mouth, and is printed in no chapter of this flood. Nobody in four hundred miles has asked the wall for a thing and nobody in four hundred miles is going to.

The second slate was on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it all day and nobody went near the shelf. The book was shut and went back on the shelf it lives on with its third column empty on purpose, and the man of about thirty-four came down the steps at about the third hour of the afternoon with his hands in his pockets and a piece of chalk in the one on the right, and nobody in that market asked him one question about what was on the form, and one of the three who went out of this room on the Saturday is two days down a road and is not on any page in four hundred miles, and a party of four is four, and a clerk nine hundred miles off is going to write to a city about a name and a city is not going to be able to answer him.

A return went out of this city on the seventy-eighth day of the Bare Month with a name at the top of it and three of its four places in a condition and one of them empty on purpose and one of them a household's, and the man who filled in the rest of it was right about the column and was not thanked and was not argued with, and the woman whose name went at the top of it is two days down a road she is not on any page for, and nothing in this city is going to be able to show anybody that she is on it at all.
