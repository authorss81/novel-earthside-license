# Chapter 0396 — Who Is A Fence For, Asked In A Room And Answered By Nobody

The eighty-first day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door of a room over a market at about the seventh hour, a Friday, and the woman who writes days in chalk on doors in this city had not been up those nine steps and was not going to be, and the cold came in under the door with the light and stopped about four feet in, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a tin in two hands, a bench, about nine people, and a length of new rope over the back of a chair.

---

The woman of about sixty-nine had carried the tin a mile again and it was empty again.

There is no form in four hundred miles with a place for a woman who comes to a room with an empty object in both hands, and nobody in that room asked her what she had brought it for, and she had been in this room once before in this flood and knows what happens in it, and a person who is not sent for is a person who came, and those are the only two kinds of coming there is.

The last question she asked in this room was on the thirty-first day of that month, a Thursday, and that is fifty days back from this morning, and the woman standing at the end of the bench with a tin in her hands has asked one question in this city in a room and got nothing back, and she is not somebody who stops at one.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of the bench with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket. The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step with his own slate flat on his knee. The man of about thirty-eight was not in the room and was on the only flat thing for four streets in this city.

And the two men who stand at a fence were both in it.

---

The man of about sixty had come up the nine steps at about the seventh hour with his handcart at the bottom of them and a hazel mallet in it whose head is bound in cord, and he had not been sent for and nobody in this city has sent for him at any time. The man of about fifty-seven had a length of new rope over the back of his chair that he has never used, and he had been in eleven rooms in about a fortnight without being asked one question in any of them, and the number of them is not a figure anybody in that room said out loud.

Nobody had said a word about either of them being in the same room. They had been within nine paces of each other on a shut road about four times in about a fortnight and had not asked each other one question, and a man who has put up a thing nobody sent him for and a man who is standing in front of a thing he cannot move have been left alone in a city where nothing has been left alone for about a month.

Then she asked it, and she asked it to the room, and she did not ask it to anybody.

"**Who is a fence for.**"

She let that stand on its own for a moment, the way a woman lets a thing stand when she has decided the words are the right size and is not going to add to them.

"**There is a fence on a shut road at the top of this city and there are two men in this room who have both stood at it, and neither of them has ever been asked where it is, and neither of them is going to be asked now, and I would like that said out loud in a room with a day on the door while there is somebody in it to hear it.**"

"You have carried that tin a mile for it."

She put the tin down on the bench and picked it up again, and a woman who has carried an empty object a mile and has said she is not going to leave it has just been asked to account for the carrying and has accounted for it without a word about the carrying.

"**A thing built by somebody nobody sent is standing in the only place a person has to go through, and it is about four feet off a mark that cannot be found because nobody is going to it, and the two men it stands between have two accounts of where that mark is and have never been asked to agree on one.**"

Nobody answered it.

About four of the nine people in that room had an answer, and about four of them said nothing, and the answer four of them had was a fence, and nobody said it, and that is the whole of what happened in that room on the eighty-first day of the Bare Month.

---

The man of about fifty-seven did not look at the man of about sixty and the man of about sixty did not look at him, and neither of them said a word, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a question asked to a room lands on everybody in it and that the two men it was about did not get asked it and that the not-asking was not for them and was not courtesy and was the third time in about a fortnight that anybody in this city has arranged not to ask a man something on purpose.

The man of about sixty said his number out loud to the fence, the way he says things on a shut road.

"About four feet is what I have in my mouth."

"**I did not take it off anything and I have said that out loud on this road once and I am not going to say it again, and nobody has ever asked me how I got the number and nobody is going to.**"

The man of about fifty-seven put his hand on the length of new rope over his arm and did not use it, and the two men the question was about had not been asked anything in about a fortnight and were not being asked anything now, and the man who had just said it was not an answer had noticed.

"That is not an answer."

"**It was never going to be an answer. She did not ask either of us for one, and the reason she did not ask either of us for one is the whole of what I have worked out about her since she asked it, and I am not going to improve on it in a room with a date on the door.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either, and he had not asked to be, and the man of about sixty put his hand on the mallet in the cart at the bottom of the stair and did not take it out.

---

The two questions rhyme and neither of them has been answered, and this city has now asked twice, in a room, in daylight, with a date on the door, what a person and a fence are on a page and in a road, that both of them were asked by a woman who had an object in her hands and by nobody else, that the second one is harder and it got less, and that a woman who has been told nothing twice has come back a third time, and there is no form in four hundred miles for being the person who asks.

The tin went back into the apron it came in and nothing else went anywhere. The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the bench was where it had been at the seventh and the length of new rope was over the back of a chair with a man in it who was not asked one question, and a shut road at the top of this city now has a fence on it that two men in this city have stood at and that neither of them has been asked about, and that the two of them were in one room this morning for about four hours and the room did not ask them, and that the question went to the room and the room did not have it, and that is the state of it, and it is not going to be settled this flood by anybody who is standing in this city.
