# Chapter 0391 — Three Barrow Wheels Going Out On A Sunday

The four-hundred-mile road goes out of this city and comes up out of the low ground in one long pull, and there was no light on it at all on the seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, because the sky had gone the colour of the inside of a lid in the night and had stayed that colour, and the cold came up out of the ground where the ground had been wet on Friday and had not stopped being wet, and what was under about three pairs of hands that morning was three barrows with their shafts up, a barrow that belongs to a man who works boards at the bottom of this city, and two barrows that have belonged to nobody since before the water.

---

They got them out past the last named house by about the seventh hour, one at a time and about nine feet apart, and nobody carried anything but the shafts. The man of about twenty-five had the wharf man's barrow, and nobody said why that one and nobody was going to, and a man who has worked boards at the bottom of this city for nine years gave a barrow away on a Sunday morning without saying one word about it and was back on those boards before the light came up off the water.

The man of about forty-eight took the second one and did not take it the way a man takes a thing he has been asked to carry. He took it, and looked at the wheel, and then took the other end.

"You have not looked at that wheel."

"**I have looked at it twice and it is the second one of the three and it is the only one of the three with a nail standing up out of the hub, and I am not going to tell you about it because you will look at it anyway and I would rather be the man who did not say.**"

The woman of about twenty-one took the third and it came up light. There was a coat across the bottom of it and a pair of boots and the pen with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife, and that was the whole of what was in it, and it was the lightest of the three by more than a man can lift.

"You have not put anything in that one."

"**There is nothing to put in it, and I said on Saturday what I am carrying and it was in a room with a date on the door, and I am not going to say it again on a road, and nobody has asked me a second question about it in any case, and I would like it noticed that the not-asking has now held for a day and a night.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A woman who has answered the only question anybody in this city has asked her about herself has not been asked a second one, and the reason is not that she is owed something. The reason is that a room in this city stopped about a month ago and has not started again.

---

The rise out of the low ground is the first thing on that road that is not flat and it is about nine hundred paces long and it is where a barrow finds out what a man is made of. The ground had gone off it in the night and the two ruts had filled and gone soft at the top, and a wheel that has been standing on boards since before the water does not care for either.

The third one went up backwards, one man pushing on the shafts and one man at the wheel, and it went up about four lengths of the rise and stopped and would not go any further, and the man of about forty-eight put his shoulder under the side of it and pushed for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and it moved about a foot and stopped again.

"That is the ground, not the barrow."

"**That is the ground and I know that is the ground, and I have walked up it twice on my own and I have got a barrow up it about half of nothing, and we are going to go up it again and it is going to be four feet shorter each time and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it.**"

They went up it four times between the seventh hour and the ninth. Nobody counted them out loud and nobody counted them at all, and a man of about forty-eight with a knife in his pocket that does not spring has spent about two hours of a Sunday getting a barrow up about nine hundred paces of wet ground with a woman and a man who has not opened his coat, and no form in four hundred miles has a place for that and none of them went looking for one.

---

Nobody on that road was looking for anything.

There is nothing at the end of four hundred miles of it that anybody in this city has ever seen, and the town there has a public register in it and no one going down this road is going to see the town, and nobody sent them and nobody is going to ask them what they went for. There is a page a hundred and forty years old in that town with eight names at the foot of it and the party of four is four and the fourth is not a hole in it and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and the page is not going to be fetched by three, and that is not a failure and has not been written down anywhere.

A man who has a barrow in his hands in the cold and a page in a coat and about nine words in the inside of that coat is not a man going somewhere. He is a man walking. A journey in this flood produces nothing that anybody can send, the three of them have set out with three barrows, and there is no form in four hundred miles with a column for a man with a barrow.

---

The light did not come. That is the whole of the weather on that Sunday and it is not a trick of the telling. The lid came down at about the fourth hour and the road went the colour of the ground and stayed that colour, and they went on at the pace of a barrow on a rutted road with a lid on it, and the man of about twenty-five walked in front with the wharf man's barrow and did not open his coat, and the woman of about twenty-one walked in the middle of the three of them, and the man of about forty-eight walked at the back with his hands in his pockets and his folding knife shut.

Nobody got stronger. That is what a barrow does on a road and it is worth putting down plain, because a road in this book has cost people things four times and it has never once made anybody better at anything. A man of about forty-eight went up that rise four times and was slower on the fourth than on the first. A woman of about twenty-one walked about a mile and her feet are her own and no instrument in four hundred miles has an opinion about them. A man of about twenty-five has nine words in the inside of his coat and had them there at the seventh hour and had them there at the ninth and will have them there tonight, and the ground at the top of the rise was not different at the ninth than it was at the seventh and the three of them were the same three.

---

They stopped where the road bends to take the light off the low ground, which on that Sunday there was none of, and they stopped because the man of about forty-eight sat down where he stopped, which is a thing he does and has done since before the water, and about four people who have walked past three barrows at a bend have worked out since that a man who sits down the moment he stops is a man who has learned that standing about in front of other people is what a person does when they are expecting to be asked.

The three barrows stood on the low side of the bend with their shafts up. The nail was still standing up out of the hub of the second one and the man of about twenty-five put his boot on the low side of it and looked at it and did not say anything about it, and the woman of about twenty-one put her hand on the rim of hers and left it there.

"We are not going to see the end of this road."

"**We are not going to see the end of this road and I have known that since a Friday in a room and I have not said it out loud until now and I am not going to be asked, and I have got a knife in my pocket that does not spring and it has not been any use to me on that road and it is not going to be.**"

Nobody asked him. Three people with three barrows stopped at a bend in a road on a Sunday in a flood and the fourth hour came and went and nobody said what was at the end of it, because there is no end of it anybody in this city has ever been able to put in a column, and a man who has been in a great many rooms and has not been asked anything in about nine of them has said the truest thing anybody has said on that road and did not improve on it and is not going to.

Three people went down a road on the seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month with a barrow each and no page between them, and about a mile past the last named house the road bent and the light did not come, and there was nothing ahead of them that anybody four hundred miles off could send for and nothing behind them that anybody four hundred miles off could be shown. That is the whole of what the three of them did on that Sunday, and it produced nothing, and it is the only thing this flood has that a return cannot be filled in from.
