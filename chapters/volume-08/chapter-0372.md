# Chapter 0372 — The One He Has Never Filled In

A room over a market had the fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Tuesday, and the stair had been loud before anybody came up it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along the bench and stopped short of the far wall, and the cold was in the crease of a form that had been folded and unfolded more times than that, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a form, a slate, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people who had come up a stair they had all climbed before.

---

The form was on the table where it had been for about a month and three of its four places were in a condition and one of them was in a decision, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a piece of paper which has been in the same orientation at the seventh hour and the ninth hour on a great many mornings is not a document anybody is working on, and that it is on that table for the fourth time this flood, and that a thing that has been in four rooms has still not been filled in in any of them.

Nobody was working. About four people in that room have worked out since that about nine people have been sitting in a room with a page on it for a month and that the only thing any of them has done to it is look at it, and that there is no form in four hundred miles for what nine people do when they cannot fill in four places, and that the answer has turned out to be a room.

Then the stair was loud again, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who works boards at the bottom of this city at about the eighth hour every morning of this flood came up nine steps at about the eighth hour on a Tuesday, and that about four people had worked out before he was through the door that he had not been up them in a room since the day he was asked a question in one.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book open on the table in front of her with her cap off and the pen in her left hand, and she did not ask him anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been in about nine rooms in a fortnight and has said nothing in five of them sat through the eighth hour without saying anything at all, and that the not-saying is now the sixth of them.

The man of about thirty-eight sat down at the near end of the bench, which is about nine feet from the table, and he put his hands on his knees, and he did not take a slate out, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has kept a tally-board on a set of boards for nine years has come up nine steps and sat down at the end of a bench, and that a tally-board is the only thing in four hundred miles he has ever filled in on his own.

Nobody asked him anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who was asked one question in this room on the forty-second day of that month has not been asked one since and did not want to be, and that the not-asking has held through a Saturday and a Sunday and a Monday, and that on this Tuesday the sentence came out of the man at the end of the bench who writes his own line last and in a harder piece of chalk, and that he said it to the form on the table and did not turn round, and that a thing said to a table in a room is not a question and everybody in that room knew the difference before he had finished it.

"You have not filled the last one in."

He looked at the form for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

"**I have filled in the first one, the second one and the third one four times a year since the winter before the water, out of what I am given, and I have never once filled in the fourth one, and nobody has ever sent me anywhere to find out how you fill in a fourth one.**"

Nobody improved on that. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man said the whole of a thing in one sentence and then stopped, and that the man who stopped him was a man who writes a line last on a board so that the word is the last thing anybody could rub out, and that he did it out loud, in a room, about nine paces off.

"That is a fact about a house and not about a form."

"**And it is a fact about a house, and I have assumed it since the winter before the water, and I have never gone to a household to find out whether I am right, and about nine people in this city have now heard me say that out loud and not one of them is going to send me.**"

The room did not make a sound. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been putting a number in a column four times a year since before the water has just said out loud, in a room in daylight with a date on the door, that he has left one of four places empty for about nine years on an assumption he made on his own, and that the assumption is that a share comes out of a household, and that about nine people in this city have now been told that the reason the fourth place has never been filled in is not that it cannot be filled.

---

"You have never gone to a household."

"**I have never gone to a household, and I am not going to, and I am not going to be asked, and if I am asked I will say the same three sentences again in the same order, and one of those three is that nobody has sent me.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a man has just said out loud that he expects to be asked about the thing he has not done, and that he has said what he will say when he is, and that the price of that is that a man who prepares the three sentences a room gets from him is a man who has decided he is not a person in the room and is only a thing in it that answers, and that about four people in that room have worked out that this is the second man in about a month to say something of that shape in this room and that neither of them knew the other one had said it.

The woman of about twenty-one was at the bench with her hands in her apron and about four people in that room have worked out since that a pen with the nib ground narrow and cut down with a knife has been in that apron for a month and has not been in a hand this morning, and that she walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth of them, and that a person who has stopped waiting to be told what she is for is the only person in this city who is not owed anything by anybody, and that she has been in this room four times and has not taken the pen out once.

The man of about thirty-nine was against the wall by the step at the back with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand, and he said nothing at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been at the back of four rooms in a fortnight without being asked one question about any of it was in a room on Tuesday morning where a man said the truest thing anybody has said in it since a word came off a slate in a cloth, and did not say one word, and was not going to, and that nobody in that room was going to ask him whether he agreed with it.

---

The fourth of the four is not in this city and is not going to be asked about anything by anybody, and a party of four is four, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has just been told that a share of something has to come out of a household has one of the four of its people sitting in it, and that the other two of the three are not in this room this morning, and that all three of them said yes in their own words in three rooms on three different days, and that none of those three words is a copy of any of the other two, and that a room in this city has got used to one of them at a time.

Nobody asked him whether he was going to go and find a household. Nobody in that room was going to, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just said out loud that he is never going to go and find a household has been told, by nine people not asking him anything, that the finding out is not his, and that about nine people in four hundred miles have spent a month deciding that a thing cannot be sent for, and that this morning the thing that cannot be sent for is a man with a tally-board.

The room emptied at about the fourth hour and the form was on the table with the first place having nowhere to start and the second one in a condition and the third one empty on purpose and the fourth one empty because a man had said out loud that he had never filled it in and never gone to find out, and about four people who came down nine steps have worked out since that a form with a heading and four places under it has now been in four rooms and has been filled in by nobody, and that the fourth of the four places has a new reason for being empty and that the reason is a man and not a household, and that the household has still not been asked for its days by anybody, and that the man went back down nine steps to the only flat thing for about four streets in this city and worked there until the light went off the water, and that he did not carry a form back down with him, and that he did not take a tally-board out and put a figure on it before he went, and that about four people on the boards at the bottom of that market have worked out since that the fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month is the only day in about a month on which a man in this city has said out loud that he has never once filled in one of four places in a form, and that nobody asked him why.
