# Chapter 0399 — A Sheet Of Road Taken Out Of An Apron And Folded The Same Way

The boards of the salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and the light came up off the river on the eighty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in one long sheet and did not move, and there was no wind on them at all for the first time since before the water, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a tally-board, a knife, a stack higher than a man, a strip of clear board about as long as a cart, and a woman's hand flat on the front of an apron.

---

She had her hand on the front of that apron from the seventh hour and did not take it off, and a woman who has had a page in an apron for about a fortnight and has said out loud that the only part of it anybody in four hundred miles is losing is the soft crease in the middle of it, and she has not taken it out since a Tuesday when she put it on a bottom step in the open in front of one person and that person said no before the fourth fold was open.

Tamsin Quill came down onto the boards at about the seventh hour and the man of about thirty-eight was working them with his tally-board under his arm and his knife in his hand, and he has been working boards at the bottom of this city for about nine years, and he did not look up, and a man who does not look up is a man who has decided that there is nothing in this morning that is going to be asked of him.

She did not look up the road either. A woman who came down nine steps every morning of this flood for about a fortnight walked past the bottom of them this morning and did not go up and did not look at them, and that the reason is not the weather and is not the hour.

---

She took it out at about the ninth hour.

She took it out on her own, with nobody near her, on the clear strip of board at the bottom end of those boards, and she opened it along the four creases she has always opened it along, and about nine places were on it, and she did not say any of them out loud and she did not point at any of them, and nobody on those boards came over and looked at it.

"**I am going to stand here and look at this and I am not going to give it to anybody, and I am not going to put it on a wall or against one, and it is not going in a book and it is not going to be written out on the back of anything, and I have said all of that once already in a room and I am not going to improve on it here.**"

The man of about thirty-eight kept working.

"He is not going to look at it."

"**He is not going to look at it and he is not going to be asked to and I am not going to ask him, and he works these boards every morning and he has for about nine years, and I have watched him do it for about a fortnight and I am not going to be one more thing that happens on them.**"

Then she folded it. She folded it the way she folds it, which is the same way every time, and the fourth crease went through about as easily as the other three, and she put it back in the front of her apron and put her hand on the front of her apron again, and a woman has just done the one thing on that morning that nobody on those boards could have done, and it took her about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and it produced nothing and was not for anybody.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came along the boards at about the tenth hour with the flap of his satchel down and stopped about nine feet off, the way a man stops who has been putting things down flat on wet boards since a Friday in that month, and he has carried seven sheets in this flood and has not opened one of them and has said out loud in about nine people at a time twice that he is not going to say the reason again.

Nobody asked him about the sheet in the apron. He looked at the front of the apron for a while and then he looked at the strip of clear board, and a man who carries things for a living has never in this flood asked a person what they are carrying and is not going to start on a Monday morning on a wharf.

"**You are not going to ask her what she has got in it.**"

"**I am not going to ask her what she has got in it, and I have not asked anybody what they are carrying in this flood, and I have carried seven things to four walls without opening one of them, and I am not going to be the man who asks a woman in an apron on a wharf board, and that is not a courtesy and it is not a position.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and two people standing about nine feet apart on the only flat thing for four streets have said out loud, in the same breath, that neither of them is going to ask the other one anything, and that they arrived at it from opposite ends of the same city and about a month apart, and that the arrangement between them is not a decision anybody made.

---

The man of about thirty-eight worked until the light went off the water and she stood on the boards for about as long as a woman stands when she has decided that a day is not going to be the day anything happens, and a room over that market has an arrangement in it that has not moved for about a month, and that the woman who is at the bench in that room from the sixth hour of the morning is standing at the bottom end of a wharf on a Monday, and that the woman who keeps the register in that room comes up those nine steps at the tenth hour and does not come in and stands on the step at the back with no name on it until the fourth hour of the afternoon, and that the two of them have said one word to each other in about a month.

None of that is going to be said out loud today and none of it is going to be said out loud in this flood, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who would have been at the far end of a bench reading a page that was not his is not at the far end of anything this morning and has not been for about a fortnight, and that nobody in this city is going to ask her about that, and that nobody is going to say anything to anybody about it either, and that the space where he is not has been standing still since the sixth hour of the morning on a day in the Short Month with no apology in it and none going to be put in it.

A woman took a page with about nine places on it out of an apron on the only flat thing for four streets in this city, opened it, and folded it the same way she has folded it every time, and gave it to nobody, and the price of a page nobody is given is that nothing whatever in four hundred miles can say that it exists, and that a woman who has decided to pay that with a page in an apron is not doing it because she has run out of people, and that the three people on a road eight days out of this city are carrying nothing at all but a coat and a pair of boots and a pen with the nib ground narrow, and that this city has run out of documents and not out of people, and that is a different thing and it is the whole of what is left.
