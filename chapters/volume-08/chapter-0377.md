# Chapter 0377 — A Roof Down By Hand Before The Weather

A market in this city has about nine stalls in it under a roof of sailcloth and sacking on four poles, and the roof stays up between market days because a roof that comes down on a wet morning does not go back up in a wet morning, and there was no light on those boards at all on the sixty-second day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, because the sacking had gone the colour of the inside of a lid, and the cold was under the trestles where the water had come up through the stone in the spring and never went out, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a lump of chalk on the edge of a trestle, four poles, a coil of line, a bucket, and about nine people standing under a roof waiting for the weather.

---

It came at about the second hour and it was not a shower. The sacking went from grey to black while a man was still holding a pole, and the first drop of it went through onto the boards about nine feet from the wall, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a market roof in this city is made of two things, one of which is the sailcloth and the other of which is the sacking, and that the sacking is the one that fails, and that about nine people knew that before it happened and about none of them said it.

Four poles came out of the sill in about a quarter of an hour. About four people on those boards have worked out since that a roof with nine stalls under it came down by hand in a squall in about a quarter of an hour, and that the two ends of it went first because the two ends are where the weight is, and that the man who took the near end had a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket and had both hands on a pole and could not get at it, and did not try, and about four people on those boards worked out that he did not try and that nobody in that market has ever seen him try.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living went to the far corner with a coil of line and did what he had come to do with it, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living turned up at a market on a Sunday morning and tied a line and did not stay, and that about four of them have worked out that he has been out past the last named house twice in about a week on foot with nothing in his hands and that this morning he came with a coil of line in his hands instead, and that nobody on those boards has asked him where he was going and he did not say.

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the stall nearest the water end had salt out on the boards in three heaps at about the seventh hour because the boards are swept at the end of a day and a market is not run on a Sunday, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a woman who says a figure out loud at the head of her own stall every morning of this flood was standing over three heaps of salt at the seventh hour on a Sunday with her hands in the cold, and that he was beside her, and that she said it before he got to it.

"About four hundred and forty."

He stopped. About four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who said a woman's number out loud for her for about four hours on a Saturday in that month, and stopped in the middle of it, has now said nothing at all on a Sunday, and that the nothing was not a decision he made this morning, and that a man who has been saying somebody else's figure for about a fortnight does not decide to stop, he is overtaken by the person who owns it.

"**I have got it. You have got it. That is the whole of what has happened on those boards this morning and neither of us is going to say anything else about it, and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to say that I said it first, and that is so that the market does not work it out.**"

Nobody worked it out. About four people on those boards have worked out since that two people standing at the end of a trestle in a squall have agreed in about four seconds that the arrangement between them is over without either of them saying it is over, and that it is not over, and that the two of those are the same thing and that both of them know it, and that about four people on those boards have worked out that a man who has said a woman's number for her for about a fortnight has just handed it back in the open in front of nine people and then told her not to be thanked for handing it back.

---

The bucket went along the line at about the third hour and it was the only thing in that market that was still doing what it was for. About four people on those boards have worked out since that a squall in this city is a thing that happens on top of a market, and that a market is nine stalls and about nine boards on a trestle, and that a roof down by hand is not a thing anybody has to be told how to do, and that the one thing in that market that had to be done by somebody who knew it was the water coming off a roof where it had been put for the night.

The woman of about thirty-four said the number twice more before the light went, both times to nobody in particular, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a figure a person says at the head of her own stall every morning has now been said out loud for the first time in a market on a day when the market was shut, and that the man beside her said it zero times, and that neither of those two numbers is on any page and that a stall is not a page and never has been.

The light went off the sacking at about the fourth hour and the boards were wet and the roof was on the ground and about nine people were under about half of it with the line still tied, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a market in this city has lost a morning and about nine boards and has not lost a single thing anybody could put in a column.

The man of about thirty-four went up the boards at the pace of a man who has finished and did not say one word at the far end, and the chalk was in his pocket where it had been since the forty-third day of the Short Month, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who writes a line on a board for a living spent a squall with both hands on a pole and could not get at the one thing in his pocket and did not try, and that nobody in that market had expected him to.

A woman of about thirty-four said her number out loud three times in a squall on a Sunday and nobody in this city is going to write any of it down. The carrier was under the other end of that roof with a pole in his hands and about four people on those boards have worked out since that he saw all three of them and carried a pole and said nothing about any of them, and that the last thing he did before he went up the boards was to tie the line on the far corner, which nobody had asked him to.
