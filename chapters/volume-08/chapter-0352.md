# Chapter 0352 — A Folded Sheet With About Nine Places On It

A room over a yard in this city had a shutter that had been scraped and painted shut twice and had gone back to being a shutter, and the light in it at about the fifth hour of the afternoon was the last of it off a wall nobody could see the top of, and what was under about four pairs of hands that evening was a folded sheet of road, a slate, a cup with the cold out of it, and about nine places.

---

Tamsin Quill had the sheet in her apron and did not take it out, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a woman who has kept a thing folded in an apron for about a month takes it out when somebody is going to look at it and does not take it out when somebody is going to ask her for it, and that the difference is in the person and not in the sheet.

The six gates on that stretch of road had been walked in this flood by people with an instrument and a page, and had given six answers, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a gate is a thing you can be shown a page about, and that the two parties who own the two ends of a stretch of road have between them described it and neither of them has been on it with the other, and that six answers is a thing you can write down and that two accounts is a thing you cannot.

The sixth gate had been walked in this flood and had given an answer, and about four people in that city have worked out since that six gates walked have given six answers, two of which were about a turn and not one of which was a name, and that a stretch of road which two parties can describe and a third cannot is a road where the description is the boundary, and that nobody has walked it since.

Adrian Vale had come in at about the fifth hour and had not sat down and had not said anything about the form on a table a street away, and about four people in that room have worked out since that two people who have been in a documented check with each other for three volumes have got to the point where the thing neither of them says is the largest object either of them owns.

"Ask me for it."

"I am not going to ask you for it."

"**Then I have said the whole of what I came to say, and I would like it noticed that I said it first, and that you did not have to be asked twice and did not have to be asked at all.**"

"You do not want me to ask you for it."

"I do not want you to ask me for it, and I want you to want to ask me for it, and both of those have been true for about a month and neither of them has cost either of us anything yet." She turned the cup a half turn on the boards. "**And I am not going to tell you what it is going to cost, because I have not worked it out, and a thing you know and do not say is a thing you have chosen, and I have chosen it.**"

Nobody was in the room but the two of them, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a fracture between two people is not repaired by either of them deciding to be careful, and that the carefulness is visible, and that it is the only thing either of them has been able to do about it for about a month, and that this city has a rule about rooms with a date on the door and has no rule at all about two people in a room over a yard with the shutter painted shut.

---

The only list anybody in this city had of anything to do with that road was four knots in a piece of string that a boy of thirteen had tied on a cart rail, and nobody had asked him for it and he had not given it to anybody, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a boy who ties four knots on a cart rail has decided that a road is a road by how many times you have to turn, and that nobody is going to ask him and that he is right not to be.

She folded the sheet a second time when she got up, and she folded it the same way she folds it, and the ninth line on it had nothing on it, and about four people in that city have worked out since that nine places with four marks against them that are turns and not names is the most honest list in this flood, and that it is in an apron, and that it is not going into a room, and that a person who has nine places and will not say them out loud is not withholding anything from anybody, because nobody has asked.

---

The light on the low ground had been off the bottom of that lane for about an hour by then, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a room over a market is the one room in this city that is warmer than the street in the evening, and that everybody who is not in it knows that, and that about four people have been walking past the bottom of that stair all week for the light coming out from under it.

The room over a market was cold again by about the seventh hour and the form was on the table where a man who carries things for a living had put it that morning, folded twice with a bone, with a heading across the top in a hand nobody in this city writes and four lines ruled under the heading at the same distance apart and nothing in any of them.

He did not ask her for the sheet. He read the slate first, which took about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then he read the second slate on the shelf, and then he took a finger and put it about a finger's width from the edge of the empty third column in the register that was open under the slate, and he did not touch it, and about four people who would have been in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has been asked nothing in about nine of them has now read an empty column as well, and that nobody has ever asked him why and that nobody is going to.

"You are not going to ask me for the sheet."

"I am not going to ask you for the sheet."

"**Then take the nine places off it yourself, and you cannot, and that is the whole of what I came here to hand you.**"

He said one true thing, and it was about a page and not about the four places on it, and he said it to the table and not to her.

"**A page does not have to be about anything to be correct. That is the whole of what I know about pages, and I have told two people that in about two hundred rooms, and it is not on a page in this city, and I am telling you it because you have nine places on a sheet and four of them are turns, and a page does not have to be about anything to be correct.**"

She did not answer that. About four people who would have been in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a check on a man for three volumes has just been handed the only sentence he has that is worth anything and has not answered it, and that not answering it is not a refusal and is not a reconciliation, and that the two are a long way apart and this city has no instrument for either of them.

---

The register-keeper was not in that room and had not been in it, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register writes every sheet that leaves this city in her own hand, and that nobody in that room was the one to write tonight's, and that the next one has to be on a wall somewhere by the Friday, and that the room it is for is on the Tuesday after the Friday, and that those are four days and there is no fifth way to do it.

She went down the nine steps of that stair before he did and the ninth one was loud and neither of them said anything on it, and about four people in that city have worked out since that two people who are not asking each other anything in a room will walk down nine steps in a particular order, and that the order is not a decision and is not a reconciliation and is the only thing about the evening that either of them did.

Nobody said any of that out loud in the room. The form lay on the table with a heading and four places ruled under it and nothing in any of them, and the cold came off the flagstones, and the two of them stood at opposite ends of a table with nine places on a sheet between them that had not moved, and about four people in that city have worked out since that a page can be entirely correct and entirely useless on the same afternoon, and that those are two different faults, and that this one is not a fault at all.
