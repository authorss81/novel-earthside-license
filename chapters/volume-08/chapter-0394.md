# Chapter 0394 — A Figure Said Out Loud On The Boards, And The Working Behind It

The boards of the salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and the light came up off the river on the seventy-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in one long sheet that did not move all morning and did not go off anything until the sixth hour, and there is nothing high enough within about four miles of that wharf to lay a shadow across them, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a tally-board, a knife, a strip of clear board about as long as a cart at the top end of a stack that is higher than a man, and a piece of paper with a stone off a doorstep on top of it.

---

The paper has been on that strip of clear board since about the second hour of the afternoon yesterday and it is going out on Thursday on a cart that goes out on a Thursday, and nobody has turned it over, and nobody in this city is going to turn it over before the cart comes, and a man who has put a figure in a column four times a year since the winter before the water put it there himself and carried it down nine steps with it against his chest and did not tell anybody he was going to.

About four people walk past that end of the boards every morning. There is a name at the top of the piece of paper. None of them has read it and none of them is going to, and a man who has walked past that end of those boards every morning of this flood has worked out since that a page with a name at the top of it lying under a stone off a doorstep on the only flat thing for four streets is the most public object in this city and the least copyable one, and that those two facts are the same fact.

---

He worked a barrow load up onto the stack at about the fourth hour and then he stopped and came down to the clear board and stood at the top end of it with the chalk still in his hand from the day before, and a man who has carried a piece of chalk in four hundred miles on one day only is a man who has come back down to the paper and not to the stack.

"**About four hundred and forty.**"

Nobody said anything. A man who works boards at the bottom of this city has said a number out loud on them about four times in about a week and the wind has taken about a third of it every time, and about four people on those boards have heard it four times and have not once asked him where it comes from.

"**I have said that number four times and I am not going to say it a fifth time for the number. I am going to say it once more because of what is under it.**"

He put his hand flat on the paper, not on the name, on the second of the four places, and kept it there.

"**A name at the top of a page is not paid for by the person whose name it is. It is not paid for by the woman who wrote it on, and she did not want to and said so. It is paid for by the man who filled in the rest of it correctly out of what he was given and had nowhere to put what he knows.**"

"Nobody on those boards has ever heard you say that."

He did not look up off the paper and the wind took about a third of what he said next.

"**That man is me, and I have been him four times a year since the winter before the water, and on Tuesday I was him again in a room with a date on the door and about nine people in it, and there is a name at the top of that page and I put a figure in the second place in my own hand.**"

Nobody on those boards improved on that. A man who has been filling in a column from what he is given for about nine years has stood on the only flat thing for four streets and said out loud, in the open, where anybody walking past could hear it, that the price of the column falls on him and not on anybody else, and this is the first time anybody in this city has put the price of anything on a man who was not being asked about it.

"**And there is a working behind that figure, and I am going to say what the working is and not what the figure is. In about nine years a man nine hundred miles off is going to write to this city and ask me where it came from.**"

"Then write it on the back of it."

He put the chalk on the boards and picked it up again, and a man who is going to be asked in about nine years where a figure came from has just been offered the way out of it by somebody who does not trade in figures at all.

"**I have it in my head and I cannot put it on that form, because there is no place on that form for where a figure comes from, and if I write it on the back then the back of it is a page in four hundred miles, and I would be the first man in this city to have made one.**"

Then he took his hand off the paper and picked his knife up off the boards and went back up to the stack.

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody has thanked him for anything since the forty-second day of that month and he asked in advance not to be, and a man who says the price of his own work out loud on a wharf in a wind does not do it to be thanked and the not-being-thanked is the price of the other thing.

The man of about thirty-nine came along the boards at about the fifth hour with a board of nineteen rates under his arm and stopped about nine feet off, and he had been at the back of every room this city has had for about a fortnight and had been right in one of them yesterday morning in about as long as it takes to say it, and nobody in that room told him he was wrong and nobody in that room told him he was right, and the page went out anyway.

He stood there with the board under his arm and did not put it down, and he did not say a word, and about four people on those boards have worked out since that a man who is right about what a name at the top of a page does is standing on the only flat thing for four streets listening to the man who put the name there being told what it costs, and that the cost is not his and was never going to be his, and that he is not going to be told that either and has not asked.

He went back up the boards at about the sixth hour. The paper was still under the stone at the top end of the clear board with a name at the top of it that nobody in this city can produce a copy of, and the man of about thirty-eight worked the last barrow load of the day up onto the stack, and a cart will come off this wharf for it and nobody in this city can tell you which one, and a page is on the only flat thing for four streets under a stone off a doorstep, and there is no way of asking after it except by standing at the top end of the boards and waiting, and nobody is going to.

---

A page went out of this city on the seventy-eighth day of that month with a name at the top of it and the man who filled in the rest of it was right about the column, and on the seventy-ninth day he said out loud on a wharf in a wind, to nobody who had asked him, that the price of a name at the top of a page is not paid by the woman whose name it is and not by the woman who wrote it and is paid by the man who fills in the rest of it correctly and has nowhere to put what he knows. The woman it is about is three days down a road and is not on any page in four hundred miles and has not been asked about anything by anybody. The man who filled in the rest of it is on the only flat thing for four streets with a knife in his hand and about nine years of a figure in his head and no page anywhere to put the working on, and nobody thanked him and nobody is going to, and a cart will come off that wharf for it and will take a name four hundred miles out of this city, and this city will not be able to say what was under it.
