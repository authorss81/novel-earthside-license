# Chapter 0641 — A Woman Says Out Loud What It Is To Be The One Everybody Looked At And Nobody In That Room Does Anything About It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the top rail of that bench with her fingers spread along it, and she was not behind the bench and she was not in front of it. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and twenty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in the room over a market, in daylight, and there were about nine people in that room and not one of them had been sent for anything.

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room there is a date written in chalk, and the three hundred and twenty-sixth day of the Bare Month is on it in full. Nothing whatever stands under that date and the wood below it is bare for the width of a hand.

That book is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and the page of it that is open is ruled into three. Five figures go down the column in the middle, one under another, with a day written in beside each of them, and not one of the five has been struck out of anything.

The column on the right of that page is as wide as the one beside it and carries one thing near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page. Behind that book, up on its edge on the same shelf with its written face turned to the plaster, stands a second slate with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It did not move at any hour of that morning, and the only hand which came near that shelf was hers.

The sheet on the table under the one window is shut along the two folds it came up that road in, with the bone lying in the near fold of it, and nobody in that room had opened it or turned it over at any hour. A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing, and this city has got two of them standing in one room now, and neither of them is a kind of thing anybody in that room is going to enjoy.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine said it at about the ninth hour, in the local language, to the middle of that room and not to any one person in it. She took her own hands off the rail and put them flat on the top of the bench instead, and she did not raise her voice.

"**Every day of this flood every person in this room has looked at me and not at the book, and I am the only one here who could have said what that column was for, and none of you asked, and I am saying it now so that it is in the air and not in my own head, and that is the whole of what I get out of it.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it, and the man it was about was not in that room and did not thank her either.

A woman with a hand-basket had been standing at the one window since about the eighth hour with the basket on the floor at her own feet. She had come up that stair for a figure of this city's own written out with its day beside it, and the keeper of that book had been at the near end of that bench since the light. The woman with the basket shifted her weight from one foot to the other and picked the basket up off the floor and set it down again and said nothing at all about it, and nobody at the near end of that bench turned round.

A man at the far end of that bench put his own two hands flat on the top of it and disagreed, and he disagreed for a reason that was his own and not anybody else's.

"A cost said out loud in a room is not a cost after that. It is a thing the room has said, and anybody who wants it afterwards can pick it up."

She did not answer him and nobody answered him. He sat back where he had been sitting and said no more about it at any hour of that morning, and about four people in that room had worked out what he meant by it and about four had not, and neither of those two groups said one word to the other before the light went.

A woman at the near end of that bench asked her, in the ordinary way, whether anybody was going to do anything with what she had just said, and she did not answer that one either, and she turned the question round into the ordinary way and asked the woman whether she thought it was her own to do.

"It is nobody's to do. It is mine to have said."

---

Two people in that room wanted two different things out of that morning and neither of the two of them got what it was.

A man at the step wanted that page turned round on that bench, so that the people at the far end of that room could see what was standing at the head of the column on the right of it, on the grounds that about nine people ought to have been in a room at the same time as a thing happened to a page in it. He asked for that twice, once at about the tenth hour and once after the light had come off the one window, and the second time he asked the keeper and not the man who had written on that page.

"Turn it round. Nine people in a room and a page turned away from eight of them."

The woman with the hand-basket wanted a figure of this city's own written out for her with its day beside it. She asked once, at about the tenth hour, and the keeper said she would come to her when the room was thinner, and the woman with the basket stood at that window until the light had gone off it and then went back down that stair with the basket in her own hand and what she had come up it for still inside it.

Neither of them got what it was. That page lay on that bench with its spine to the wall and the head of it towards the light, and the woman with the basket did not come back up that stair that day, and nobody in that room was going to be told that she had stood at that window for the whole of that morning.

The woman of about twenty-nine said one more thing out loud, at about the half hour after the tenth, to the man at the step and not to the room. She did not put her hands in her sleeves to say it.

"**You can shout up that stair as many times as you like and I am not turning that page round, because the only thing that is going to happen to it this morning is that about four people in this room will have seen it and about four will not, and that is what has already happened.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody improved on it, and the man at the step did not shout up that stair a third time.

---

At about the eleventh hour a man came up that stair with his own business, which was the day written in beside the figure at the foot of the column that holds figures, on the grounds that a figure of this city's own with a wrong day beside it is a figure of a different day. He asked the keeper for it once, in the ordinary way, and she said the book was not coming off that shelf with nine people standing in that room.

He waited at the near end of that bench until about the half hour after the eleventh. She did not take that book down in that time and nobody in that room asked her to, and he went back down that stair with his own question and the whole of the morning he had spent on it, and nobody in that room was going to be told that he had been at that bench.

A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing that four hundred miles off can read and four hundred miles off cannot check. There are two of those in this city and both of them are in that room, one at the head of a column in a book on a shelf and one in a ruled place on a sheet on a table, and they are in two hands and on two days and nobody has said one word about which of the two came first.

Nobody in that room wrote a notice that day, and there is not going to be an eighth of them in this flood, and every one of the seven that went out of this city in it was written by somebody sitting down in a room to write it.

---

At about the twelfth hour there were about four people left in that room. The man at the far end of that bench who had said a cost out loud is a different thing from a cost out loud, and he had said so once and had not said it twice, and the woman who keeps that book let him have said it and did not agree with it and did not argue with it either.

A thing said out loud in a room is not the same as the thing being in the room. The cost was named at the ninth hour on the three hundred and twenty-sixth day of the Bare Month by the person it falls on, in her own mouth, in front of about nine people, and nobody thanked her for naming it, and nothing in that room was done about it at any hour of that day.
