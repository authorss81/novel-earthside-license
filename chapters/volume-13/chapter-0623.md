# Chapter 0623 — About Four Of Them Found Out At The Ninth Hour And About Four At The Eleventh

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour with the flat of his right hand over his own inside breast pocket, and he took the hand off the pocket and put it on the top rail of that bench.

It was the three hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. There is no name under the date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair, and there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below that date. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and he has a piece of chalk in that pocket that he does not use, and neither the board nor the chalk came up that stair with him.

There were about nine people in that room at the ninth hour and every one of them was in that room before anything was said in it, and none of them had been sent for anything. There is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one, and the four days that would have been given to a thing like this are not in this city.

On the table under the one window there is the fourth of the four sheets of one shape in this flood, a heading and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever in that one place. The bone it was folded around is lying in the near fold of it, and nobody in that room had put a hand on it at any hour of that day.

---

The man of about thirty-four said it to the middle of that room, and he said it while looking at the sheet on the table and then at the rest of the room.

"**Nobody has told this room what that word is for.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it, and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it.

About four people in that room had worked out by that hour that this room was being asked for something and had not been told what, and about four had not, and the two of those groups sat where they were sitting and did not tell each other.

He said one more thing about it at the same hour, and it was to the man nearest the step and not to the room, and it was the fourth time anybody in that room had heard somebody say that same thing out loud that morning.

"**That is not an oversight and it is not the water and it is not anybody in this room being slow. This room found out what it was being asked at the ninth hour this morning, and the ninth hour is when it found out.**"

The man nearest the step wanted it settled before anybody else came up that stair, and said so to him.

"Then somebody in this city did know and did not come."

The man of about thirty-four did not turn about while he answered the man who had asked it.

"**Or nobody in this city knows, and I have been in this room since the light and so have you, and neither of us has been told anything, and a room that has not been told is not behind anybody.**"

Nobody said that the man who said it was right. About four of them repeated that second sentence to one another at about the tenth hour in the ordinary way, and about four had not heard the first one at all.

One of those four found out what the room was being asked at about the eleventh hour, from a man who had heard it at the ninth hour. Neither group was told anything the other was not told, and that was the arrangement of the morning, and nobody arranged it.

---

Two of the people in that room wanted two different things out of that morning and neither of them got it. A man who had come up that stair at about the ninth hour with his own business wanted that room to say out loud, in the ordinary way, what the thing on that table is a place of.

He asked twice in that morning, in the ordinary way, and both times the answer he got was that nobody there had been told. The second time, the person who gave it to him said it more slowly than the first time.

What the other of them wanted was for that room to go on the whole of that morning not knowing, on the grounds that a thing this room has been asked for and has not been told the purpose of is a thing this room has not agreed to yet, and that four people in that room had already been asked for it and five had not.

She said that to the man with his own business, once, and he said the opposite of it back to her, and neither of them gave the other one word. About four people in that room heard the whole of that and about four heard one side of it, and none of the nine of them changed what they were doing at that table.

A man came up that stair at about the half hour after the eleventh with a length of cord and a folded paper under his arm. He stood at the foot of it while nine people talked in the room above him, and then he went back down and went off along the row with the cord under his arm. Nobody knew he had been at the foot of that stair, and nobody in that row was going to be told why he did not come in.

---

Seven notices have gone out of this city in this flood and every one of them was written by somebody who sat down in this city to write it. None has gone out since, there is no eighth on its way, nobody in that room wrote one that day, and nobody in that room said on which day the next one goes out.

Seven documents have come up that road or gone out of it in this flood, and that is the number of documents and not the number of notices. Three of them carry four places each and the places on them are in the order they are printed. Four of them carry one place each, and the fourth of those four is the one on that table with nothing in it.

One of the other three is standing on the shelf opposite that bench with a figure of this city's own in its one place, and has stood there since the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood. Nobody laid one of those two sheets near the other, and nobody had both of them in one hand.

A word goes in a place on a page and nobody four hundred miles off can check it, and that is a different kind of place from a number's. This room had worked that out on an earlier day and had not written it down anywhere.

---

Nobody filled the one place on that sheet at any hour of the three hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month. Nobody put a word in the column on the right of that page, and nobody said what the word was for, because nobody had been told what the word was for.

That is what a spent notice costs in a city where there is no eighth of them and the seven that went out went out a long time ago. At the thirteenth hour the light had come off that one window and the bone in the near fold of that sheet was still lying in the near fold of it.

The room had found out what it was being asked on the morning it was asked. About four of the nine people in it had been carrying that since the ninth hour and about four of them had been carrying it since the eleventh, and neither of those two groups told the other one anything at any hour of that day.
