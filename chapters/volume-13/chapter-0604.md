# Chapter 0604 — A Sheet Turned About And Nothing On The Other Side Of It

A man at the far end of that bench turned the sheet on the table about on the wood with the tips of two fingers on its near corner and nothing else, and nobody in that room put a second hand on it while it went round, and when it came to rest facing the other way the heading was across the top of it again and the one place was where it had been. It was about the eighth hour on the two hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light came off the one window and lay on the table under it and on the wall opposite the bench in a rectangle with the edge of the sill at the top of it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that table with his hands in his pockets. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along, and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and neither of those two things came up that stair with him. He said the thing that is said, to that sheet, and it took him about as long as it takes a man to put a bucket down.

"**Nothing on this page says what that one place is for.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying it. Nobody in that room improved on it, nobody argued with it, nobody told him he was right, and nobody in that room came at him afterwards with a second question about it. He put his hands back in his pockets and looked out of the one window at the front of the store and said nothing else about that sheet for the rest of that morning.

The date on that door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room was in chalk, and the two hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month was written in full on it, and there is no name under it and there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below the date. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the far end of the bench. She has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty.

---

What the back of that sheet had on it was nothing. Not a second place, not a mark, not a figure, not a day and not a name. The other side of it is the blank side of a sheet, and the fold that runs down beside the near place is the same fold that was in it when it came up that road, and the bone was still lying in that fold where the man who carried it had put it back.

What the top of that sheet had on it was a heading. It was a heading and it was not a sentence. It stood over that one place the way a heading stands over the thing it is the heading of, and it did not say what the thing was, and nobody in that room could say either, and about four of them had been looking at that heading for an hour before any of them admitted to anybody that they could not read it.

A woman at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table, and she wanted that sheet left where it was lying.

"It is going to wear if it keeps going round on that wood."

"**A sheet wears at the fold and not on the face, and this one has two folds in it and the near one has the bone lying in it, and nobody in this room is going to say what to put in that one place.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it. About four people in that room wanted somebody in that room to say out loud what that one place was for, and about four wanted nobody to say it, and the two groups went on being in that room all morning without either of them saying so.

A man at the near end of the bench said the thing that is said, to the room, and it was short.

"That came up the road for this room."

"**Nothing in this room has been told what it came up that road for, and a man that says it came for this room has not been told anything, and he is not the first one to say it and he will not be the last.**"

Nobody in that room argued with him about that and nobody improved on it and nobody told him that it had not come up the road for this room. Nobody in that room went out of that room to find out where it had come up the road from, and nobody asked the man who had carried it up that stair what was in it, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was not in that room that day and has never opened anything he has carried in this flood.

---

The register was on its shelf behind that bench the whole of that morning and it did not come off that shelf. The column of that page which holds figures stands full from the head of that page to the foot of it with five figures in it, one under another, in the order they were made in, with the day written in beside each of them, and not one of the five has been struck. The column on the right of that page is empty from the head of that page to its foot, and it was empty at the eighth hour and it was empty at the eleventh hour and nobody in that room put a word in it at any hour of that day. The second slate stands on that shelf up on its edge with its written face to the plaster, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and no hand but the keeper's came near it.

Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood. Three of them have four places on them in the order they are printed. Four of them have one place, and the fourth of those four is the one that was lying on that table, and three of those four are in this city or are not in this city, and nothing in that room put any two of them next to each other and nothing in that room said one word about how any two of them are related.

That room spent the morning of the two hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month on the surface of one sheet, and the one thing it did not do was open the book it keeps. The register stayed on its shelf behind that bench from the eighth hour to the eleventh with its spine to the wall, and the five figures in it went another morning without anybody in that room reading a day out of them, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book was standing at the far end of that bench for the whole of it and did not open it, and nobody in that room thought to ask her why, and about four people in that room had not realised until the end of the morning that the book had been there at all.
