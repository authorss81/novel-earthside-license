# Chapter 0649 — A Woman Takes A Book Off A Shelf And Looks Down The Column Beside Five Figures And Puts It Back Where It Was

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands under that register on the shelf behind that bench, and she drew it out from under the second slate and set it down on that bench in front of her and opened it at the head of the column that holds figures. It was about the sixth hour on the three hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room, and the light off the one window had not come across the top of that bench yet.

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room there is a date written in chalk, and the three hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month is on it in full. Nothing whatever is under that date, and the wood below it is bare for the width of a hand.

---

She looked down the column on the right of that page from the head of it to the foot of it, and there was one word near the head of it and there was nothing whatever anywhere else on that page, and she did not put a hand on that word and she did not turn that page over and she did not rule another line on it.

The five figures in the column beside it stand one under another in the order they were made with a day written in beside each of the five, and not one of the five has been struck out of anything, and no sixth has been entered and none has been taken out. Those five were the same five at the eleventh hour of that day that they had been at the sixth hour of it and they are the same five that were in that book when the water came.

Behind that book on the same shelf, up on its edge with its written face turned to the plaster, stands a second slate. A day is cut across the head of it and nothing whatever stands under that day. It was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on at any hour of that day, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was hers, and that hand came off the spine of the book and did not go back to it.

---

A man came up that stair at about the seventh hour and asked her for a figure of this city's own written out with its day beside it, and he said it in the ordinary way. She said she would come to him when the room was thinner, and he sat down at the far end of that bench and waited until about the half hour after the ninth hour, and then he got up and went back down that stair without it, and nobody in that room was going to be told that he had waited at that bench for two hours and a half.

A woman who keeps a stall in the market below came up that stair at about the eighth hour and wanted to know what that column on the right of that page was for, and she wanted to know it in front of the people in that room. She said so once to the middle of that room and then said it again to the near end of that bench, and nobody in that room told her and nobody in that room asked the woman who keeps that book either.

"Nine people in this room and not one of them knows. Ask the one who keeps it."

The woman of about twenty-nine said one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, to the middle of that room and not to the woman who had asked, and she did not raise her voice to say it.

"**I have been asked in front of this room what that column is for and I am not going to answer that in front of this room, and nobody in this room has ever been told either, and the two of those are the same thing and not two things, and I am not going to stand here and be the reason a page in this city has a thing in a column in it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody thanked her for saying it, and the woman who had asked it went back down that stair and traded at her own front for the rest of that day. About four people in that room had that in their own heads by the eleventh hour and about four did not, and neither of those two groups said one word to the other before the light went off that one window.

---

That morning produced two people in that room who wanted opposite things out of it, and neither of the two of them got what it was.

There are nineteen rates on that board in three columns, and at the foot of the column between the other two there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers with nothing whatever in it, and there is no name on any page in this city that belongs in that space, and nobody in that room has ever asked him what was in it.

A man at the step wanted a copy of the number at the head of that column on the right of that page, on the grounds that a thing nobody can copy is a thing that is not there. He asked for that twice, once at about the ninth hour and once at about the tenth, and he asked the keeper both times, and nobody in that room copied it onto anything.

"Write it out on anything and I will take it down to my own front myself."

A man who trades rates on a board went past the open door of that room at about the tenth hour on his way down to that market and did not come in, and nobody in that room saw him go past and nobody in that row is going to be told that he did not come in.

Neither of those two men got what it was. Nothing was copied onto anything in that room on that day and nobody in that room asked that man of about thirty-nine one thing about that space or about any other thing.

---

A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing, and this city has two of them standing in one room and one of them is not in that room any more. One is at the head of the column on the right of a page of a book on a shelf behind a bench. The other went up a road out of this city on a sheet with a heading and one place ruled under that heading.

They are in two places and in two hands and on two days, and nobody in this city is going to be able to say afterwards which of the two came first.

A line of words set under a figure on a form is a different kind of thing and it went out of this city once in this flood and it is outside this city now, and nothing on any surface in this city carries one of those, and that is a state of this city and not a silence.

She shut that book at about the half hour after the tenth hour and put it back on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and her own hand flat on the spine of it for a moment after it was there. Nothing went into that column at any hour of that day and no second word went into it and no sixth figure went into the column beside it, and the five figures in that column are the same five that were in that book when the water came.

---

At about the eleventh hour that room over that market was empty except for the woman of about twenty-nine, and the light off the one window had come across the top of that bench and across the place where that book had been lying, and the second slate was on its shelf behind that bench standing up on its edge with its written face to the plaster and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

The only person in this city who could have said what that column was for had that room to herself in it at the half hour after the tenth hour of the three hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, and nobody came up that stair.
