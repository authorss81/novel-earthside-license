# Chapter 0626 — A Satchel With A Flap Goes Up And Down A Stair Four Times And The Second Of The Two Middle Days

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had both hands on the strap of that satchel where it crossed his own chest and he took them off it and set the satchel down on the bottom step of the stair at the back of that room with the flap down over what was in it. It was about the eighth hour on the three hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in the room over a market. A range of fifty days has two days in the middle of it, and neither of the two is the middle of anything.

He has a satchel with a flap. He knows what is in one thing he has carried and has never opened anything he has carried in this flood. There is nothing in that satchel at any hour of that morning, and he has not put anything in it.

The date on the outside of the door at that bottom of that stair is in chalk, the three hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month in full, and there is no name under it and there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below that date.

The register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, five figures in the column down the middle of the page with a day beside each of them, and the column on the right of that page bare from the head of it to the foot of it. Behind the register, on its edge on the same shelf with its written face to the plaster, a second slate stands with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, not picked up and not turned over and not written on, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's.

---

Two people in that room wanted two different things out of that morning, and both of them wanted them said out loud in front of the man with the satchel. A man at the near end of that bench wanted a sheet carried up that road flat against a body under a flap, so that a thing going out of this city could not be read by anybody but the person carrying it.

A woman at the one window wanted that same sheet rolled in the hand, so that a person could get a hand at it in weather without stopping on a road.

The man of about thirty-one did not say which of those two was correct, and he was not asked which of the two he would rather do. He said it to both of them, with the satchel on the step at his own feet, and he said it without stooping to pick the satchel up.

"**I have carried four of that shape up that road and I have not opened one of them, and I am not going to decide now how the fifth one goes and I am not going to be asked to, and I will do it the way it happens when it is a day for doing it.**"

He said one more thing about it before he picked that satchel up off the step, and he said it to the stair at the back of that room and not to anybody in it, and it was the thing nobody in that room had asked him for and it was the only thing he said that day that he had not been asked for.

"**There is a thing in this room going into a place on a page before another thing in this room goes into a place on a sheet, and I have carried the one that goes up that road four times, and nobody here has asked me what I make of the order of the two of them, and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to say.**"

Nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it. About four people there heard the whole of it and about four heard the first part of it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was not in that room at any hour of that morning.

Nobody told him that any of it was true. The man at the near end of that bench went on wanting his flat way and the woman at the one window went on wanting her rolled way, and neither of them gave the other one word about it at any hour of that morning.

The woman at the one window wanted to know what he would do if it started to come down on the road itself, and she asked him.

The man of about thirty-one set the satchel back down on the step at his own feet before he answered her.

"**Then it goes under the flap the way he wants it and it gets wet the way she wants it, and I have carried a thing in the rain before and it is not the carrying that a person drowns on, and I am not going to be told in this room on this morning how to carry a sheet up a road.**"

Nobody said that she was right and nobody improved on that. The man at the near end of that bench put his hand flat on the top of his own board and said nothing further about it, and the woman at the one window went back to the window and said nothing further about it either.

The two of them went on wanting two different things for the rest of that morning without either of them putting it to him again.

He then did the thing that room had not asked him for, which was to go up and down that stair with the satchel in his hand and the flap down.

He did it the way each of those two people had wanted it done, once each way, and then once more each way, because the light in the walk at the back of that room changes between the bottom step and the top of it and the two ways are not the same way twice.

---

A woman came up that stair at about the tenth hour with two buckets in her two hands and found the walk at the back of that room full of a man going up it with a satchel. She stood on the bottom step a while, and then she went back down those steps with her two buckets and went along the row to the channel instead of to the store, and that took her the length of that row twice in one morning.

The man of about thirty-one did not know she had come up that stair, and nobody in that room knew, and nobody in that row was going to be told. About four people along that row saw her go past the store with her two buckets and about four did not.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room at about the eleventh hour with his board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room. He has nineteen rates on it in three columns, and at the foot of the column between the other two there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers with nothing whatever in it.

He did not say one word about that space at any hour of that morning, and nobody asked him about it. Nobody joined that space to the column on the right of that page or to the one place on a sheet on that table, and the two of those are two places and not one, and nobody said that they were the same.

---

The satchel with a flap went down that stair at about the half hour after the eleventh, and it went down it with the flap down over nothing at all, the way it had come up it.

A word is going into the one place on that sheet and another word is going into the column on the right of that page, and the order in which those two of them go has been said out loud in that room in the last two days. The man with the satchel was not asked for an opinion on that order by anybody at any hour of either of those two days. He had one, and he did not give it, and he was not thanked for not giving it, and nobody in that room knew he had one.

The walk at the back of that room stood clear at the bottom of it at the eleventh hour for the first time since the morning began, and the light had come off the one window of that room onto the near edge of a sheet lying on the table under it, with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever in that one place.

Nobody had turned that sheet over, and the bone in its near fold had not moved once all morning. The woman with the two buckets got down to that channel at the half hour after the eleventh, filled them both, and carried them back up that row with about half a bucket of somebody else's water in one of them.
