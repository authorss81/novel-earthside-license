# Chapter 0602 — A Sheet Laid Flat On A Table With A Bone Still In The Near Fold Of It

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had both hands flat on the table under the one window of the room over a market and the satchel with a flap lying open on his forearm above them, and he took the thing out of it and set it down on the wood and took his forearm out from under the flap. It was about the ninth hour on the two hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and it had come up that road that morning on the quarterly round. He has a satchel with a flap, and one thing he knows what is in.

The date on that door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room was in chalk, and the two hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month was written in full on it, and there is no name under it and there never has been one under a date on that door. There is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below the date. About nine people were in that room at that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything. Through the one window of that room you could see the front of the store and the step at the foot of the wall, and the box of the store's own chalk was on that step with its lid lying down on the rim of it, and that lid does not shut.

What was on the table under that window was one sheet, a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading. There was nothing whatever in that one place. It was not a figure and it was not a name and it was not a mark of any kind; it was an empty place the size of a hand, ruled on three sides, with the heading standing over it. The two folds were still in that sheet and the bone it had been folded around was lying in the near one. Nothing had been taken out of it. The satchel with a flap went back down that stair with nothing in it, and the flap was not turned over on the way past the last step.

---

Nobody in that room said what that one place was for at any hour between the ninth hour and the thirteenth.

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the man who had brought it, and it was short.

"You have had that up here before."

"**I have carried four of that shape up this road and I have not opened one of them, and this is the one that came up this morning.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what was in any of them and nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the one window with his board under his arm, the face of it turned out into the room, and he has nineteen rates on it and one name that is not on it, and there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers at the foot of the column between the other two with nothing whatever in it. He did not put that board down and he did not say one word about the space.

The man who had brought it said the thing that is said, to the sheet on the table, and it was short.

"**There is a bone in the near fold of it and the bone is where it was folded and nothing has been taken out of it, and that is the whole of what I know about it.**"

Nobody in that room asked him how he knew that there was a bone in it. Nobody argued with him about the four and nobody said that he was right.

A man with a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along had come up that stair with his hands in his pockets, and he has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use. He said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was short.

"Nobody in this room is going to put anything in that place this morning."

"**I have carried a sheet up this road with a figure in that one place and I did not know what the figure was a figure of, and I am not going to say one word about what that one is for.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody said that he was right. About four people in that room had worked out by that hour that nothing was going to be put in that place that day, and about four had worked out that something was going to have to go in it eventually, and the two groups did not speak to each other about it at any hour before the thirteenth.

---

The man who trades on a board was in a market at the far end of that city that morning and had left it to come up that stair, and the four hours he spent in that room were four hours he was not standing in that market, and nobody in that room knew that and he did not say it. He had come up the stair with his board under his arm and he went back down the same stair with it under his arm and the face of it turned in against his ribs, and the space at the foot of the column between the other two on it was the same width it had been at the ninth hour.

The man with a board face in against his own trestle went back down the row and put his hands on the front edge of it and left them there a moment and then took them off. Two stalls along from him, the store's board was on its two nails with the same three sets of figures on it and the same stone going on up above the top edge of it in the colour it keeps. Nobody in that row had washed that stone that day and nobody in that row was going to.

About nine people were still in that room at the thirteenth hour, and the sheet was still on the table under the one window with the bone lying in the near fold of it and the heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading with nothing in it. Nobody in that room had touched it, nobody had turned it over, and nobody had put a hand on the bone. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that city's only public book was not in that room that morning and was not sent for and did not come, and the book with the five figures in it and the column beside them was on its shelf behind that bench where it had been since before this flood.

That sheet was the only thing that has ever come up that road out of this flood with a place on it left empty for the people it came to, and it lay on that table until the thirteenth hour with the bone still lying in the near fold of it, and about four people in that room had worked out that it was so and about four had not, and the bone was the only thing about that sheet anybody in that room was able to see.
