# Chapter 0645 — A Sheet Folded Shut Along The Two Folds It Came Up That Road In Goes Down A Stair In A Satchel With A Flap

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on that shut sheet on the table under the one window, one at the near fold of it and one at the far edge of it, and she waited until about the seventh hour before she put either of them under it. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in the room over a market, in daylight, and about nine people were in that room and not one of them had been sent for anything.

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room there is a date in chalk, and the three hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month is on it in full. No name is under that date and the wood below it is bare for the width of a hand.

That sheet has a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place is filled with a word that is neither a figure nor a person. It came up that road on the six hundred and second day of this flood, it was read out in that room in the local language on the sixth hundred and fifth, the one place was written in on the six hundred and thirty-ninth in her own hand with a pen and ink, and the sheet was shut on that table on the day after that and has been shut on that table ever since.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was on the bottom step of that stair with a satchel with a flap in his own two hands, and he had the flap up and the satchel open and his own arms out to either side of it, and nobody had told him to come up that stair and he had not asked to.

He has carried four sheets of that shape up that road in this flood and he has never opened one of them. He knows what is in one thing he has carried and he is not going to be asked what is in this one, and nobody in that room asked him.

She put it out loud to that room, in the local language, at about the seventh hour, and she put it before she touched the sheet, and it was the whole of what she said about it.

"**I am folding this along the two folds it came up that road in and pressing the bone down the length of both of them, and my name is not on it and is not going on it, and nobody in this room is going to open it, and the word in it goes four hundred miles and I am not going with it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody in that room thanked her for folding a sheet of paper. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at that bench and said nothing whatever about any part of it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with his own hand flat on the face of his own board and said nothing at all.

---

Two people in that room wanted two different things out of that morning and neither of the two of them got it.

A man at the step wanted that sheet opened in that room one last time before it went, on the grounds that about nine people ought to have had the chance to see a word going out of their own city, and he said that out loud twice, once to the keeper and once to the man with the satchel.

"Open it in this room. Once. Let the nine of us see what is going out of our own city."

A woman who keeps a stall two along in that market wanted to be let into that room for the folding of it, on the grounds that a thing going four hundred miles ought to be folded by somebody the row will recognise.

"My own two hands folded it the last time one of these went and nobody in this room knows that."

Neither of them got it. That sheet was folded shut at about the half hour after the seventh hour and nobody opened it at any hour of that day, and the woman two stalls along was not let up that stair and went back down it and traded at her own front for the rest of that morning, and nobody in that room was going to be told that she had asked.

She pressed the bone along the whole length of the near fold with the heel of her own hand, and then along the whole length of the other one, and she did it twice over the second of them because the first pass left a lip at the top of it. Nobody in that room helped her with it and nobody in that room asked her why the bone was in that fold.

---

The sheet went into that satchel at about the ninth hour, and the man of about thirty-one put the flap down over it, and the strap went over his own shoulder once and not twice, and he carried it to the bottom of that stair and stood there with it.

Seven documents have come up that road or gone out of it in this flood, and this is the second of them that is going out of this city with words on it, and it is not withdrawn and it is not coming back. A line of words set under a figure went out on the five hundred and ninety-fifth and is outside this city. A word in a place under a heading is going out now. Those are two different kinds of place and no page in this flood says so, and nothing in this flood has an instrument for taking a word out of a place and leaving that place bare.

There is one trough, one bucket and one rag in this city. The rag was on the stone lip of that step at the end of that market row at the fourth hour of that morning and at the seventh and at the ninth, and it was not inside that trough at any hour of that day.

---

At about the eleventh hour that room over that market stood with about four people left in it and the light coming off the one window onto a bare table.

That register was back on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall. Five figures go down the column in the middle of that page with a day written in beside each of them and none of them struck out, and the column on the right of that page carries one thing near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and nobody has ever been told what that column is for, including by the man who wrote in it.

The second slate stood on that shelf on its edge with its written face to the plaster and a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and the only hand which came near that shelf was hers.

That sheet is four hundred miles up a road that goes out of this city, in a satchel with a flap, folded along the two folds it came up that road in with the bone lying in the near fold of it, and it went out of this city this morning and it is not coming back to this table.

The room it came from has a name on that sheet and on nothing else at all, and the word that went four hundred miles is the name of that room and it is on a sheet in a satchel with a flap and not on anything else in this city, and nobody in this city is going to be told what that name is or where it has got to.
