# Chapter 0614 — Nineteen Rates Counted Two Ways And Two Ends That Do Not Agree

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the string at the top of that board over his left shoulder and his right hand flat on the face of it against his own hip, and he was not trading. It was about the sixth hour on the two hundred and ninety-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, at that market, in ordinary weather. He has nineteen rates on that board in three columns, and at the foot of the column between the other two there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers with nothing whatever in it, and the two ends of that board do not agree with each other about anything at all.

At the end of that market the store's board hangs on two nails. It carries an account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and two figures of this city's own that do not agree with each other, and nobody has rubbed any of the three out and there is nothing under any of them. Above the top edge of it the stone goes on up in the colour it keeps and nobody washed it that day. About four people went past that wall before the light came good; about four stopped; about four did not, and not one of the ones who stopped said out loud which of the three there is the one to read.

---

A woman at the stall next along came to the near end of his board and put the flat of her hand flat on the face of it below the column between the other two and said the thing that is said, to that board, and she wanted one thing off it.

"That one, from the top."

The man of about thirty-nine did not take his hand off the face of that board and he said the thing that is said, and it took him about as long as it took her to ask.

"**Read it from the top, and you will get what everybody standing at this end of it gets, and I am not going to stand here and put my thumb on the line for you.**"

Nobody in that row improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it. She went round the front of his board to the other end of it and read it from that end, and what she read at that end was a different line, because that board is written in one direction and the other end of it is the other end of it, and she came back to where he was standing and said the thing that is said, to him, and it was short.

"Those are not the same."

The man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the string at the top of that board and said the thing that is said, to her, and he said it without any haste at all.

"**They are the same board and they are the two ends of it, and a person who stands at the end where the writing starts will read it one way and a person who stands at the end where it stops will read it another, and neither of those two people is wrong and you have to pick an end before you can use it.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right and nobody argued with him about it and nobody improved on it. The woman with her hand on the face of his board took her hand off it, and stood at the near end of that market for about as long as it takes a person to decide not to argue, and then she went along the row and did not come back to that board that day, and the nineteen rates on it were nineteen at the sixth hour and nineteen at the eleventh hour and not one of them was written out anywhere at all.

The man of about thirty-nine counted that board the other way from the one he would use to trade on it. He put two fingers on the face of it at the end where the writing stops and went up from there, and he counted it and stopped, and he said the thing that is said, to the two fingers, and it was short.

"Nineteen either way. Two ends. They do not agree."

A man going past with a basket on his head said the thing that is said, to that board, and he wanted it settled.

The man of about thirty-nine said the thing that is said back to him, and it took him about four seconds and no longer.

"**I have counted that board from the end I read it at and I have never counted it from the other end, and I have done that this morning, and nobody in this row has asked me what the two ends of it come to and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to say them out loud either.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody argued with him about it. He took his two fingers off the face of his own board and put his hand flat on the column between the other two, and the space at the foot of that column with nothing whatever in it was the same width it had been at the sixth hour, and he did not put a finger in it and he did not say one word about it and nobody in that row asked him about it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine looked up at the one window of the room over that market the way he looks at a board. What is on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room is a date in chalk and the two hundred and ninety-ninth day of the Bare Month written out in full, and under that date there is nothing, and below the date there is a strip of bare door about as wide as a hand. Nobody in that row went up that stair that day and nobody in that row was sent up it.

Two stalls along the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was behind his own trestle, his board turned face in against it so that the bare back of it stood out in the row. There is a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, and it has not been out of that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it was in that pocket at the fourth hour of that morning and at the eleventh hour of it and at no hour between. Nobody in that row spoke to him at any hour of that day and nobody in that row has ever asked him one thing about that pocket, and nobody in that row has ever asked him one thing about the bare back of his own board.

Nobody in that row settled which of the three sets of figures on the store's board is the one anybody should be reading. Nobody in that row printed a figure off that wall, or off a board under an arm, or off anything else. The one name that is not on the board the man of about thirty-nine carries under his arm is not on any page in this city, and he has never said it to anybody, and nobody in that row asked him what it was, and nobody in that row joined it to the two ends of that board or to the space at the foot of the column between the other two or to anything else at all.

---

The two ends of that board do not agree with each other and they did not agree at the sixth hour and they did not agree at the eleventh hour and nothing on either of the two ends was rubbed out. The man of about thirty-nine put that board under his arm with the face of it turned in against his ribs at about the eleventh hour and went off along the row with the string over his shoulder, and the space at the foot of the column between the other two on it is still about as wide as two of his fingers and there is still nothing whatever in it, and nobody in this city has been shown where a name came off a board, and the woman who wanted one line off that board took her morning elsewhere and did not say to anybody what she had read at the other end of it.
