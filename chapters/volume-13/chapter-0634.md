# Chapter 0634 — A Second Line Of Grey Grit On A Board Nobody Ever Reads Where It Is Lying

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went into the yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats at about the seventh hour and got down on one knee at the foot of that stack so that his own eye came level with the top face of it. His own board stood up against his trestle two stalls along, and the chalk in his own inside breast pocket had not been out of that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood. Neither of those two things came out to that yard with him.

A stack of boards stands against the wall of that yard with its ends squared off, and the top of it stands about as high on a man as his own chest. On the top of that stack, squared with the boards under it and with nothing else in that yard, there is a board with a figure of this city's own cut into the side of it that the sun gets to, and there is a line of grey grit lying across that figure from one end of it to the other, and there is a second line of grey grit on it now as well, lying across the first at an angle to it.

The first of those two lines came off those boards on the five hundred and ninety-second day of this flood and nobody wiped it. The second came off the same boards and nobody wiped that either and nobody has ever been told which of the two boards it came off, and it is not going to be.

That yard did not get the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf at any hour of that morning. Nobody in that city has ever been shown where the figure on that tally-board came from and nobody has ever asked him about it, and no page in this flood prints what that figure is.

People went past the open end of that yard one after another at their own times that day. Not one of them stopped at the top of that stack and nobody took their number and nobody counted them, and the figure on that board was no more read on that day than on any other morning of this flood since the water came.

---

The man of about thirty-four said it to the top of that stack while he was on his knee at it, and it came out of him in about nine words and it was about that surface and not about a person.

"**That board is for somebody to stand and read it.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on it and nobody thanked him for saying it. He got up off his knee and looked down at the top face of that stack once more from standing and it looked exactly the same from standing as it had from the ground.

Two men came into that yard at about the eighth hour with a board between them and wanted the top of that stack for a board of their own. He told them, once, the way round that yard that they would not need to stand on anything. He did not offer to move the tally-board for them and nobody asked him to, and that board is not turned over on its face on any day of this flood because a board turned over carries what is lying on it to the other side and leaves it lying there.

A man who had been in that yard before the light came back through the open end of it and asked him, without any preamble, whether he was going to say anything about the grit. He said it to the top face of that stack from standing.

"**Grit does not take a figure off a board and it does not put one on it either, and I have not wiped it and I am not going to, and there is nobody on this ground that I am going to say that to twice.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and the man who had asked went back out through the open end of it without looking at anything on that stack, and nobody took his number either.

---

At about the tenth hour he went up the stair at the back of the room over that market and came into that room and stood at the near end of that bench. What is on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair is a date in chalk, and the three hundred and nineteenth day of the Bare Month is on it in full, and there is no name under that date and the door below it is bare for the width of a hand.

About nine people were in that room at that hour. The register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and there were five figures in the column that holds them with a day written in beside each of the five and none struck out, and the column on the right of that page bare from the head of it to its foot. Behind it a second slate stands up on its edge on that shelf with its written face to the plaster, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's.

On the table under the one window there is a sheet with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there is nothing whatever in that one place. The bone that sheet was folded around is lying in the near fold of it and nobody had turned that sheet over at any hour of that day.

He said one more thing out loud in that room, and it was the second thing he had said that day and nobody had asked him for either of them, and it took him longer than the first one had taken.

"**A page is the only surface in this city that a person four hundred miles off can pick up and look at the back of.**"

Nothing in that room made a better thing of that and nobody there thanked him and nobody told him he was right. Nobody in that room came at him afterwards with a second question about either surface, and about four people in that room had it in their own heads by the twelfth hour and about four did not.

---

Two people at that bench wanted two different things out of what was left of that morning and neither of them got what it was.

A man at the step wanted that sheet weighted down before the light came off the one window, because the wind comes round that corner of that market in the middle of the day and has lifted paper off that table before.

"Put a weight on it before the light goes or it will be off that table."

A woman near that window wanted that sheet left exactly where it lies, on the grounds that the bone is in the near fold of it and that turning it over to weight it would put that bone in the wrong place.

"Leave it where it is. The bone is in that fold and it came up that road in that fold."

Neither of them got it. Nobody in that room put a weight on that sheet and nobody in that room turned it over, and it lay flat on that table with its heading the right way up for the people on the far side of it and its one place under that heading empty.

A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing and there is one of them on that table. A line of words set under a figure on a form is another kind, and this flood has sent that kind out of this city once, on the five hundred and ninety-fifth day, and it is outside this city and came back with nothing and the words themselves are on no page in this city.

---

By the light of that one window at about the half hour after the eleventh he went back down that stair and along that row and out to that yard again on his own two feet, and he did not go down on one knee at that stack a second time. The board lying face up on the top of that stack had two lines of grey grit lying across the figure on it and neither of them had been wiped and neither of them was going to be, and people went past the open end of that yard all day and not one of them has ever stopped at it.
