# Chapter 0628 — A Woman Asks One Man One Thing About Him And Not About The Book

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the top of that bench and she had come round to the near end of it from the far end where she keeps that book, and she stopped there and put her weight on her own hands. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name under that date and a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below it. There were about nine people in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before anything was said in it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with the flat of his own right hand over his own inside breast pocket, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along, and the piece of chalk in that pocket has not been out of it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and nobody in this city has ever asked him what it is for. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register has a second slate on the shelf behind that bench with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on at any hour of that morning, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was hers. What she asked him that morning was not about that slate and about nothing on that shelf.

---

She said the thing that is said, to him, and not to that room, and it was one question and she put it the way a person puts a thing they have thought about for some days.

"You have had that in your pocket since before the water came and you have not taken it out. What do you call what you are not doing yet?"

Before she asked it, the man of about thirty-four had said one thing out loud at that bench to nobody, and nobody in that room had asked him for it and nobody in that room improved on it.

"**She has asked me one thing a week for as long as she has been in that room and I have not answered one of them, and I am not going to start with a question of hers in front of the other eight, and I am going to answer this one because she asked it and not because I was asked for it.**"

Nobody in that room heard the question go except the people near the near end of that bench, and nobody in that room answered it for him, and nobody in that room asked him it a second time, and nobody in that room came at her afterwards about having asked it.

He took his hand off that pocket and put it flat on the top of that bench about a hand's width in front of her own hands, and he looked at the floor of that room while he answered, and the light in that room at the ninth hour came off that one window and lay along the top of that bench and across the back of her own hands and across the back of his, and nobody in that room thanked him for answering it and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it.

---

"**He said it once, out loud, in his own mouth, in the local language, to a woman and not to a room.**"

---

Not one of the nine in that room asked him for a second time. Nobody in that room asked him to say it a different way and nobody in that room said one word about it, and about four people in that room were near enough to that bench to have taken in most of what he said, and about four were not, and the two of those groups did not exchange one word about it at any hour of that morning. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not repeat it and did not write it down and did not ask her whether it was the right thing to call it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not repeat it either and did not put it on anything, and the two of them did not stand in that room and work out what had been said, and nobody in that room asked either of them a second question about it.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the middle of that room, in the ordinary way, and what he wanted was to know whether anybody had understood what had just been done at the near end of that bench, and what he got was about four people saying nothing at all and about four other people saying that it was not for them to say.

---

At about the tenth hour a woman at the one window waited until the keeper came back past that window and said the thing that is said, to her, in the ordinary way, and what she wanted was a figure of this city's own written out for her on a slip of paper, with its day beside it, the way she had been given one of them two years ago by somebody who is not in this city now. She waited by that window from the tenth hour until about the half hour after the eleventh, and the keeper of that book was at the near end of that bench for the whole of that time, and at the half hour after the eleventh the woman at the one window said that she would come back another morning, and she went back down that stair, and nobody in that room knew she had waited by that window and nobody in that market was going to be told.

There is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one, and nothing that was said at the near end of that bench at about the ninth hour of the three hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month went out of that room, and it is not on any page and it is not on the door at the foot of that stair, and there is no road in four hundred miles that anybody is going to be told anything about it on.

---

A thing was said once at the near end of that bench in a room over a market in daylight in front of about nine people, and the piece of chalk in the man of about thirty-four's own inside breast pocket was in that pocket by the time the light went off that one window, and nobody in that room had seen it at any hour of that morning and nobody in that room asked him for it. Four of those nine were standing near enough to that bench to take in most of what he said and the other four were not, and neither of those groups has said a word about it since, and the man himself has not repeated it and has not written it down and has not asked her whether it was the right thing to call it. Nobody four hundred miles off is ever going to hear it, and there is no notice going anywhere in this city this month or the next, and there is not going to be an eighth of them.
