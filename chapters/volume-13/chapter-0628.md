# Chapter 0628 — A Woman Asks One Man One Thing About Him And Not About The Book

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the top of that bench. She had come round to the near end of it from the far end where she keeps that book, and she stopped there and put her weight on her own hands, and the light off the one window came along the top of that bench and stopped at the near end of it about a foot short of where she was standing.

It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, in that room, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name under that date and a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below it. About nine people were in that room at that hour, and every one of them had come into it before anything was said in it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with the flat of his own right hand over his own inside breast pocket. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along, and the piece of chalk in that pocket has not been out of it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and nobody has ever asked him what it is for.

The woman of about twenty-nine has a second slate on the shelf behind that bench with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It was not picked up and not turned over and not written on at any hour of that morning, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was hers. Her own book lay open on the far end of that bench with five figures in the column that holds them, one under another, none struck, and the column on the right of that page empty from the head of it to its foot.

The shelf with the second slate on it stood three feet behind her own shoulder, and the question she was about to ask was not about it.

He had been at that end of the bench since about the seventh hour and had said nothing at it. A woman with a hand-basket had asked him twice about a length of rope and got a nod both times.

---

A quarter of an hour before she asked it, he had said one thing out loud at that bench to nobody, and nobody at that end of the bench had asked him for it.

"**She has asked me one thing a week for as long as she has been in that room and I have not answered one of them, and I am not going to start with a question of hers in front of the other eight, and I am going to answer this one because she asked it and not because I was asked for it.**"

She put her question the way a person puts a thing they have thought about for some days. She asked it once, and then she shut her mouth and waited, and the waiting was the longer half of it.

"What you have had in that pocket since before the water came, and have not taken out. What do you call what you are not doing yet?"

He took his hand off that pocket and put it flat on the top of that bench about a hand's width in front of her own hands, and he looked at the floor of that room while he answered, and the light lay along the top of that bench and across the back of her own hands and across the back of his.

---

"**Work that has not had its day.**"

---

Seven words, and he said them at the speed a man says a thing he has had in his hands for a long time.

He did not say them again. He did not write them down and he did not ask her whether that was the right thing to call it, and she did not repeat them either and did not put them on anything, and the two of them did not stand in that room and work out together what had just been said, and nobody asked either of them a second question about it.

Not one of the nine asked him for it a second time. Nobody asked him to say it a different way and nobody said one word about it, and about four people were near enough to that bench to have taken in most of what he said and about four were not, and the two of those groups did not exchange one word about it at any hour of that morning.

A man at the far end of that bench asked, in the ordinary way and to the middle of that room, what had been said at the near end of it, and what he got was about four people saying nothing at all and about four other people saying that it was not for them to say.

---

At about the tenth hour a woman at the one window waited until the keeper came back past that window and asked her, in the ordinary way, for a figure of this city's own written out for her on a slip of paper with its day beside it.

She had been given one of them two years ago by somebody who is not in this city now. She waited by that window from the tenth hour until about the half hour after the eleventh, and the keeper of that book was at the near end of that bench the whole of that time.

At the half hour after the eleventh the woman at the one window said that she would come back another morning, and she went back down that stair. Nobody in that room knew she had waited by that window, and nobody in that market was going to be told.

There is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one, and nothing that was said at the near end of that bench at about the ninth hour of the three hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month went out of that room. It is not on any page and it is not on the door at the foot of that stair, and there is no road in four hundred miles that anybody is going to be told anything about it on.

---

A thing was said once at the near end of that bench in a room over a market in daylight in front of about nine people. The piece of chalk in the man of about thirty-four's own inside breast pocket was back in that pocket by the time the light went off the one window, and nobody had seen it at any hour of that morning and nobody asked him for it.

Four of those nine were near enough to that bench to take in most of what he said and the other four were not, and neither of those groups has said a word about it since. The man himself has not repeated it, and nobody four hundred miles off is ever going to hear it.

There is no notice going anywhere in this city this month or the next, and there is not going to be an eighth of them.
