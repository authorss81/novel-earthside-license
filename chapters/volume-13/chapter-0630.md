# Chapter 0630 — A Room Reads Out An Order It Is Going To Keep And Writes Neither Word Down

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of her own right hand on the near edge of that sheet on the table under the one window and turned it about so that the heading stood the right way up for the people on the other side of that table, and then she took her hand off it and did not pick it up. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room, in daylight. The date on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room is in chalk and the three hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month is written on it in full, and there is no name under that date and there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below it. There were about nine people in that room at that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything.

That sheet has a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever in that one place, and the bone it was folded around is lying in the near fold of it where the man who carried it up that road put it, and nobody in that room had turned that sheet over at any hour of that day. The register was on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and behind the register, up on its edge on that same shelf with its written face turned to the plaster, stands a second slate, and the head of that slate carries a day cut across it and nothing whatever underneath that day. Nothing in that room lifted it, turned it over or wrote on it at any hour of that morning, and the only hand that came near that shelf was the keeper's.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to that room, in the local language, and it was the order that room was going to keep, and she said it once and she did not say it twice.

"**One word goes into the column on the right of that page of that book before the other word goes into the one place on that sheet, and neither of those two words is to be read out in a room, and that is the order of this room and it is not written down anywhere.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for saying that and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it, and nobody in that room asked her which of the two of those words was hers and which was not hers. About four people in that room had worked out before she opened her mouth which of the two of them was going to go first, and about four people in that room had not, and nobody in that room had been told either of those two things, and the two of those groups went on being in that room all morning without either of them saying so.

She then took the register out from under that second slate with her own two hands, set it down on that bench, opened it at the head of the column that holds figures, and put the flat of her own left hand on the open page, and she took her hand off it again. There are five figures in the middle of that page put in one under another in the order they were made with a day written in beside each of them, and not one of the five is struck out, and the column on the right of that page is the same width as the one beside it and is bare from the head of it to the foot of it. She looked down that bare column once, from the head of it to the foot of it, at about the speed of a person reading a page, and she shut that book and put it back on its shelf with its spine to the wall, and nothing went into that column at any hour of that morning and the five figures in the column beside it were the same five at the thirteenth hour that they were at the ninth.

---

Out of that day two people in that room wanted two different things, and neither of them got what it was. A man at the near end of that bench wanted that sheet to go up that road on the quarterly round whatever was or was not in that one place, on the grounds that a thing in this city being settled was worse than a thing in this city being wrong somewhere four hundred miles off, and he said that out loud twice in that morning, once at about the tenth hour and once at about the half hour after the eleventh, and he said it in the ordinary way to the middle of that room.

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to him, the first of the two times he said it, and she did not raise her voice to do it.

"**That is not settled and that is not the same as wrong, and a thing that goes out of this city with nothing in a place in it is a thing that says out loud to four hundred miles that there was nothing here to say, and I am not going to be the one who sends that up there.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for it, and that man said the opposite of it back to her at about the half hour after the eleventh, in the ordinary way, and the woman of about twenty-nine said the same thing to him the second time she was asked and said it more slowly than the first time.

The second of them wanted nothing to go up that road out of this city until that room had settled for itself what a word in a place is for, and she put it to him once, in the ordinary way, and he gave her the other side of it, and the two of them did not come to anything at all between them that morning, and four people in that room were close enough to hear the whole of it and four were not, and nobody in that room chose between them out loud and nobody in that room wrote either of the two words on anything.

A man in that room who had not worked out which of the two words was going first asked the woman of about twenty-nine, at about the half hour after the eleventh, a second question about it, in the ordinary way, and she said the same thing she had said at the ninth hour and she said it the same way, and he did not ask her a third time and he did not say afterwards that he had worked it out.

---

Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood and three of them carry four places each and four of them carry one place each, and the fourth of those four is on that table with nothing in it. Seven notices have gone out of this city in this flood and there is no eighth and there is not going to be one, and nobody in that room wrote one that day and nobody in that room said on which day the next one goes out, and the sheet that went up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth carrying a line of words set under a figure is not one of those seven and is not counted among them by anybody. A word in a place is read by anybody four hundred miles off and is checked by nobody four hundred miles off, and that is a different kind of place from a number's, and this room had worked that out on an earlier day in that same room and had put it nowhere at all.

---

She turned that sheet about again at about the half hour after the twelfth and put the heading of it the wrong way up for anybody who came into that room after that, and she left it that way, and nobody in that room turned it back. The order was read out in that room in daylight in front of about nine people on the three hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month and it is not on that sheet and it is not in that book and it is not on the door at the foot of that stair, and the four people in that room who had known which of the two words went first before she said so had not told the four who had not, and the four who had not did not find out by being told either, and the one place on that table was empty at the ninth hour and empty at the thirteenth hour and nothing had been turned over, and a sheet went up a road four hundred miles long on the round some days after that day with a word in it, and not one word of what that room had said that morning went with it.
