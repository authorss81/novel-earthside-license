# Chapter 0609 — A Chair Out From Under A Shelf And A Shelf Looked At From The Floor

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both hands on the back of a chair at the back of that room and he drew it out from under the shelf on the end wall and turned it about and set it down in the open with its seat facing the wall. It was about the sixth hour on the two hundred and ninety-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along, and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and that board had been left where it stands and the piece of chalk did not come out of that pocket at any hour of that morning. About four people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything and none of them had anything else to do.

The date on that door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room was in chalk, and the two hundred and ninety-fourth day of the Bare Month was written in full on it, and there is no name under it and there is a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below the date. The register stands on the shelf behind that bench with its spine turned to the wall, and the page of it that is open is ruled into three columns down the page, one of them at the left-hand edge, one of them in the middle and one of them on the right. The middle one of those three is full from its head to its foot with five figures put in one under another in the order they were made in, with a day written in beside each, and not one of the five struck. The column on the right of that page is the same width as the one beside it and has nothing whatever in it from the head of that page to the foot of it. A second slate stands on that shelf up on its edge with its written face turned to the plaster, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

---

He got down on the floor of that room and looked along the underside of that shelf with his head turned sideways, and he did not put a hand on the shelf and he did not put a hand on the register and he did not put a hand on the second slate, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf at any hour of that morning was the keeper's, and that hand came off the spine of the book and did not go near it again. He was on that floor for about as long as it takes a man to look along four feet of the underside of a shelf and see nothing, and then he got up off the floor and put his hand flat on his own thigh.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to him, and it was short.

"Whatever it is, it was not on that shelf when I dusted it."

"**That chair has been under that shelf since before this flood and it has not been moved once in that time, and I have not found what I came up that stair to look for and I am not going to say what it was.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him what it was he had come up to look for. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench and has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty, and she said the thing that is said, to the chair, and she wanted it back where it had been.

"There is a pail under that chair that is not yours to shift."

"**Then the pail can go on the floor and the chair can stay out, and I am not asking you about the pail and I am not asking you about that shelf either, and nobody in this room has anything else to do this morning.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody argued with her about it and nobody argued with him about the other thing, and the two of them went on like that for about as long as it takes a person to cross a floor and come back, and the pail went on the floor and the chair stayed where he had put it.

---

About four people were still in that room at the tenth hour. The chair went back under that shelf at about the ninth hour with its seat facing out the way it had been facing, and it went back by the man who had drawn it out and not by anybody else, and the pail went back under it. That shelf was not touched at any hour of that day and the register was not taken off it and the second slate was not picked up and not turned over and not written on, and nobody in that room has ever been told what is written on that second slate or is going to be told, and the day cut across the head of it is the only thing on it.

That column on the right of that page was empty at the sixth hour and empty at the tenth hour and nobody in that room put a word in it and nobody in that room said why it is empty. Nobody in that room asked the woman of about twenty-nine anything about that page, and nobody in this city has ever asked her, and a person who does not know what a column is for should not write in it, and that reason is on a page in a finished volume and is not in this room.

The one thing that changed in that room on the two hundred and ninety-fourth day of the Bare Month was a chair, and it changed twice and went back the way it was, and the pail went back under it, and nobody in that room has said since which of the two of them moved it, and not one of the five figures in the book on that shelf was altered by any of it and not one word went into the column beside them.
