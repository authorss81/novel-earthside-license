# Chapter 0644 — A Room Asks A Question All Morning And The Only Person In It Who Could Answer It Is Not Asked

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with the string at the top of that board over his own left shoulder and the palm of his own right hand flat on the face of it, and he had come up that stair at the back of that room before anything was said in it. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and twenty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that room, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair and nothing under it.

Nineteen rates are on that board in three columns, and at the foot of the column between the other two there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers with nothing whatever in it. He has not put a finger in that space at any hour of this flood and he has not said one word about it.

About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them had come into it before anything was said in it, and not one of them had been sent for anything.

The register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall, and the page of it that is open is ruled into three. Five figures go down the column in the middle of that page, one under another, with a day written in beside each of them, and not one of the five is struck out.

The column on the right of that page is as wide as the one beside it, and there is one thing at the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page. Behind that register, up on its edge on the same shelf with its written face turned to the plaster, a second slate stands with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that morning and the only hand which came near that shelf was the keeper's.

The fourth sheet of that shape in this flood was shut on the table under the one window, folded along the two folds it came up that road in, with the bone still lying in the near fold of it. Nobody in that room opened it at any hour of that day and nobody turned it over and nobody put a weight on it.

---

The question went round that room in the ordinary course of that morning, one person to the next along, for about an hour. It was put four times and about four people answered it in the ordinary way by saying that they did not know, and about four said nothing at all.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book's own second slate was at the near end of that bench with her own hands flat on the wood in front of her from the ninth hour to the thirteenth. Nobody in that room put that question to her at any hour of that day.

She answered three other questions in that hour, twice for a figure of this city's own with its day beside it and once for a thing she went to that shelf for. Nobody in that room put those three together out loud with the fourth, and nobody in that city is going to be told why she was not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the face of his own board and put it flat on the top rail of that bench and said it to the middle of that room, and it took him about four seconds, and about four people in that room were near enough to have taken all of it in.

"**I have never asked her what that column on the right of that page is for, and I have said so out loud twice this week already, and I am saying it a third time this morning because it is a Monday and I would rather say it three times and be wrong once than say it twice and be asked why I stopped.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody said that he was right and nobody thanked him for saying it the third time, or for saying it either of the two times before it, and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it.

A woman near that one window said, in the ordinary way, that a man who said a thing three times in a week was a man who wanted to be asked whether he had meant it.

He said it to the top rail of that bench and not to her, and it was shorter than the first one had been.

"**I have not said it three times for me. I have said it three times so that if anybody in this room ever does ask her, there is a record in the air of this morning of me not asking, and I am not going to be thanked for a thing I have not done.**"

Nobody improved on that either and nobody in that room said a word against it, and she went back to that window and said nothing further about it.

A man at the far end of that bench wanted that third saying of it struck out of the air, on the grounds that a man repeating himself in a room makes a room think it is agreeing with him, and he said so to the middle of that room and the man of about thirty-nine answered him once and did not say it a fourth time.

"It is not me making this room agree with me. It is me putting my own hand on record about a thing I have not done."

"Put it on a page then, and not in the air."

---

Two people in that room wanted that morning to come out in two different ways and neither of them got what it was.

A man at the step wanted somebody in that room to write down somewhere, on anything, what had been said out loud in that room over the last three days. He asked for that twice, once at about the tenth hour and once at about the half hour after the eleventh, and both times he asked the keeper, and what he got both times was that there is no page in that room that is going to carry it.

"Write it on the back of that slate. Nobody has to be able to read it."

A man at the far end of that bench wanted that sheet on that table to stay shut for the rest of that week and go up the four-hundred-mile road on the round with what was in it and nobody in that room opening it, on the grounds that a thing four hundred miles off reads a shut page exactly the same as an open one. He put that to the middle of that room once and nobody answered him.

Neither of them got it. Nobody in that room wrote anything down on that day, and nobody in that room opened that sheet, and nobody in that room settled anything about either of those two men.

---

A line of words set under a figure on a form is a kind of thing this flood has sent out of this city once, and that one is outside this city and came back with nothing. Nothing on any surface in this city carries one of those now, and that is a state of this city and not a silence.

A word in a place on a page is a different kind of thing, and one of those went into the column on the right of that page on the six hundred and thirty-eighth day of this flood in a hand that is not the keeper's, and one of them went into the one place under the heading on that shut sheet on the day after that in her own hand with a pen, and neither of those two has been read out in a room and neither of them is on any page of this flood.

Seven documents have come up that road or gone out of it in this flood: three of them carry four places each and four of them carry one place each. The second of those four went out of this city on the five hundred and forty-fifth day and is not in it and is not coming back. The third went back up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth with a figure of this city's own in its one place and a line of words set under that figure, and it is outside this city and is not coming back either. The fourth of those four is shut on that table under that window.

Seven notices have gone out of this city in this flood and every one of them was written by somebody in it, and there is no eighth and there is not going to be one. Nobody in that room wrote one on that day and nobody named a day on which the next one goes out, and the sheet that went up that road on the five hundred and ninety-fifth is not counted among those seven by anybody.

---

At about the thirteenth hour that room had about four people left in it and the light had come off the one window onto the top of that bench.

There are seven notices gone out of this city in this flood and no eighth, and a room that has run out of things to do to a page does not get a notice to say so, and nobody in that room wrote one that day and nobody in that row is going to be told that a woman at the near end of that bench was never asked what the column on the right of that page is for.
