# Chapter 0620 — A Room Working Out What A Sheet On A Table Is For

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of her right hand on the near edge of that sheet on the table under the one window and turned it about so that the heading was the right way up for the people standing on the other side of that table, and then she took her hand off it and did not pick it up. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in that room, in daylight. She has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty, and that slate was on its shelf behind her with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day and it did not come off that shelf at any hour of that morning.

Somebody had been out at that door before the light and put the morning on it in chalk in the ordinary way. The three hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month is on the outside of that door in full, and no name is under it, and below it is a bare strip of door about as wide as a hand. There were nine people in that room at the ninth hour and not one of them had been sent for anything, and every one of them was standing there before that sheet was turned about.

There is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one. The four days that would have been the cooling are not available in this city, and that room found out what it was being asked on the morning it was asked, and nothing that happened in that room on that morning was done in advance of anything.

---

What was on that table was one sheet with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there was nothing whatever in that one place. It is a place about as wide as a hand, ruled on three sides, and the heading standing over it is a heading and is not a sentence and does not say what that one place is a place of, and nobody in that room can say either. The bone it was folded around was lying in the near fold of it where the man who carried it up that road put it back, and nobody in that room turned that sheet so that the bone would lie down and nobody in that room put a hand on the bone.

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to that room, and she said what the room had been asked for, and she said it out loud and it took her longer than anything else she said that day.

"**This room has been asked for a word and not for a figure, and the word is about a place in this city, and the sheet goes back up that road with the word in it, and not one of the four of us in this room has been told what the word is going to be for.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for saying it and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it and nobody asked her a second question about it. About four people in that room had that in their own heads by that hour and about four did not, and the two of those groups went on being in that room all morning without either of them saying so.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to that table, and he wanted it settled and settled now.

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to him, and she did not raise her voice to do it.

"**I have not said the word is for this room and I have not been told that the word is for this room, and I have said what this room has been asked for, and the two of those are not the same thing and you are welcome to go and look for yourself.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to that one place, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and that piece of chalk was in that pocket at the ninth hour and at the thirteenth hour and at no hour between them was it anywhere else, and he did not say one word about it.

"A place like that is a place somebody has to put something in, and this room has not been told what, and I am not going to be the one who decides what, and nobody in this room has asked me what I would put in it."

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on it and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it. Nobody in that room asked him what he would put in that one place, and nobody asked the woman of about twenty-nine what she would put in it either, and about four people in that room wanted somebody to say out loud what a word in that one place was for and about four wanted nobody to say it.

---

The register came off its shelf behind that bench at about the eleventh hour with the keeper's own two hands under it and she set it down on that table and turned the page to the head of the column that holds figures and put the flat of her left hand on the open page. That page is ruled into three, and the one down the middle of it is where figures go. There are five in it, one under another, entered in the order they were made and each with its day written in beside it, and not one of the five has been struck out of anything. Beside it, on the right of the same page, is a column the same width, and there is nothing in it at all, and she looked at it for about as long as it takes a person to look at one thing, and she shut that book and put it back on its shelf with its spine to the wall.

It did not move. The keeper's own two hands came to that shelf twice, once to lift the book off it and once to put it back, and the other of the two of them never went near the slate standing on that shelf with its written face to the plaster, a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood, and four of the seven are of one shape with a single place on each. The first of those four is standing on the shelf opposite that bench with a figure of this city's own in its one place; it was read out on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood and it has never been returned and it has never been withdrawn. Another of the four was filled on the five hundred and thirty-eighth day and left this city in a satchel with a flap on the five hundred and forty-fifth, and it is not here and it is not coming back, and nobody in that room put it on that table. A third of the four came up that road with a figure already in its one place, and that place was written into a second time on the five hundred and eighty-eighth day with a figure that was not the first one, and it went back up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth carrying a line of words set under that figure. That line is outside this city and is written out on no page in it. The fourth of them is on that table under that window with nothing whatever in its one place.

---

Nothing went into that one place at any hour of the three hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month, and nothing went into the column on the right of that page at any hour of it, and nobody in that room filled the one and nobody in that room filled the other, and the satchel with a flap went down that stair at about the half hour after the twelfth with nothing in it and the strap of it over one shoulder.

A room over a market worked out what a sheet on a table was for on the three hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month, out loud, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and it did not decide anything and it did not put anything anywhere. The person who said out loud what that room had been asked for is the person who keeps that city's only public book, and she is not thanked, and nobody in that room improved on what she said, and the four days that would have been given to it are not in this city, and the word is not on the sheet, and the sheet is on the table, and the bone is still lying in the near fold of it.
