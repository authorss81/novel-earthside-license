# Chapter 0618 — A Word Where A Figure Would Be And Nobody Four Hundred Miles Off To Check It

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the strap of his satchel over his right shoulder and the flap of it down over what was in it, and he had both hands in front of him on the near edge of the table under the one window, and he was not asked anything. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in the room over a market, in daylight, with about nine people in it. He has a satchel with a flap, and one thing he knows what is in, and he has never opened anything he has carried in this flood and he is not going to open the thing that is in it now.

The fourth one-place form lay on that table with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever in that one place. Chalk went on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair, in the ordinary way, and it is the three hundred and third day of the Bare Month on it, and nothing has ever gone under a date on that door in this city. The one window looked out over the front of the store, and the wall at the end of that market was in the edge of what could be seen from it, and on that wall a board hangs on two nails with three lots of figures on it and nothing whatever underneath any of them, and from the day after a sheet went up that road there has been no surface in this city with a line of words set under a figure on it at all.

---

The man of about thirty-one said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was one long sentence and he said it to the sheet and not to anybody.

"**A word put in where a figure would be goes up that road and anybody four hundred miles off can read it and nobody four hundred miles off can check it, and the one that went up on the round had its words under a figure instead, and those are two places on one page and neither of them is the other place.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with it and nobody said that he was right. About four people in that room had the two of those places in their heads by that hour and about four did not, and the two of those groups did not say one word to each other about it at any hour before the light went off that one window.

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to that man, and he wanted it put in one order.

"Which of the two is the correct one."

"**Neither of them is the correct one and I have carried both of them up that road and I have not opened one of them, and I know what is in the one I carried last and I am not going to say what is in it, and a person four hundred miles off with a page in his hand is not going to ask me which of the two places on it is the right place.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the register on its shelf behind that bench, and she has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty, and what she wanted to know was out loud.

"Which of the two do we put it in."

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to him, and it was the first time that day she had spoken at that table.

"That is the question this room has been carrying since the day that sheet came up that road and it is not mine to answer and I am not going to answer it and I have not been asked it twice."

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it and nobody came at her afterwards with a second question about it. Nobody in that room put a word in the column on the right of that page and nobody in that room put anything in the one place on that sheet, and the two of those places stood in that room all morning with nothing in either of them and nobody in that room saying which of the two was the one anybody was going to use.

---

The sheet that went up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth day of this flood has a figure of this city's own in its one place and a line of words set under that figure, and that line is outside this city and came back with nothing. It is not in that room and it is not in this city and it is not coming back. Nothing in this flood has an instrument for taking a line of words off a page and leaving the place it was in bare again, and that is a fact about this city and not about a page.

About four people a day walk past the open end of the yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats where a tally-board lies face up on a stack of boards with a line of grey grit across the figure on it, and not one of the four stops at it, and the figure on that board is on no page in this city. There is a figure in this city that was counted on foot out along eleven miles of flats, and it was counted four times, on the five hundred and eleventh day of this flood and again on the fifteenth, the seventeenth and the twenty-first, and the four are written into a book in a room over a market with a day beside each of them and none of the four agrees with any of the others and none of them was made to. The three hundred and third day of the Bare Month was not one of the four. There is no fifth morning of that count in this city and there is nowhere to put one.

---

The man of about thirty-one put both hands flat on the near edge of that table and then took them off it and put them at his sides, and he said one more thing about the two places and it was short and nobody in that room answered it.

"**It is not what is in the place and it is what the place is for, and the two of those are on different pages and they went up that road on different days, and nobody in this room is going to be able to tell afterwards which of them was written first and that is the whole of what I know about it and it is all I have.**"

The satchel with a flap went down that stair at about the eleventh hour with the flap down over what was in it, and the man who carried it up that road with a line of words under a figure in it went back down it with the same satchel and the strap of it over one shoulder, and the one place on that table was empty at the seventh hour and empty at the eleventh hour, and the bone in its near fold had not moved once all day, and nobody in that room had touched it.
