# Chapter 0605 — A Sheet Read Out In The Local Language And Nobody In The Room Told In Advance

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the flat of her right hand on the near edge of that sheet on the table under the one window and she turned it about so that the heading was the right way up for the people standing on the other side of that table, and she put her left hand flat on the wood beside it and did not pick it up. It was about the ninth hour on the two hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The two hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, in full, and there is no name under it. She has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty.

Nobody in that room had been told what was going to be read out. About nine people were in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before the sheet was turned about, and not one of them had been sent for anything, and there is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one.

She read the heading out in the local language, and then she read the one place out, which is a place ruled under that heading with nothing whatever in it, and then she said what it was for, and she said it before anybody asked.

"**That one place is to have a word in it and not a figure, and the sheet goes back up that road with the word in it, and it is empty now and nobody has explained it to me.**"

Nobody in that room told her that she was right. Nobody improved on what she said, nobody argued with it, nobody thanked her for saying it, and nobody asked her afterwards what word it was going to be. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was against the wall by the one window with the strap of his satchel across his chest and has a thing he knows what is in, and he has never opened anything he has carried in this flood and was not going to open this one, and nobody in that room asked him what he had carried up that stair.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stood at the near end of that table with his hands on the front edge of it, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and neither of those two things came up that stair with him. He said the thing that is said, to the table, and it was short.

"A word. And nobody here has got one."

"**Nobody in this room has been told what that place is a place of, and a person who has not been told should not decide what goes in it.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody came at him afterwards with a second question about it. A woman at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table, and she wanted to know what happened to a sheet that went back with the place still empty.

"That goes back empty or it does not go back."

"**How it goes back with the word in it is not settled in this room, and the four days that would have been the settling are not available in this city.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that. Nobody in that room said that she was right, and nobody asked her what the four days were and nobody in this city has ever been told what they are.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the one window with his board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he has nineteen rates on it and one name that is not on it, and a space about as wide as two of his fingers at the foot of the column between the other two. He said the thing that is said, to the sheet, and it took him about four seconds.

"**There is no day on it, not on the face of it and not on the back of it, and no day by which it has to be up that road.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for that and nobody argued with him about it and nobody said that he was right. Nobody in that room improved on it. About four people in that room had not looked at the day on that sheet before he said it and about four had, and he went on holding his board under his arm with the face of it out into the room and did not say one word about the space at the foot of the column between the other two and nobody asked him about it.

---

That sheet lay on that table under the one window from the ninth hour to the thirteenth hour with one place ruled under a heading and nothing in that place, and about nine people looked at it in that room in that daylight and not one of them put a hand on it. The light came off that one window onto the near edge of it about the eleventh hour and the bone lying in the near fold of it stood up out of the paper a finger off the table where the sun got at it, and nobody in that room turned the sheet so that the bone would lie down again, and nobody in that room looked at the bone either. Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood. Three of them have four places on them in the order they are printed. Four of them have one place, and the fourth of those four is the one on that table, and the other three of them are not on that table and were not in that room.

Nobody in that room decided anything at any hour of that morning. Nobody in that room filled that place, nobody in that room wrote a word on it, and nobody in that room wrote a word in the column on the right of the page of the register that stands on its shelf behind that bench, which is empty from the head of that page to its foot, and the five figures in the column beside it went another day in that room without anybody turning a page of it. The second slate stood on that shelf up on its edge with a day cut across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day and did not move at any hour of that day, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's, and that hand had been on the near edge of a sheet of paper on a table all morning and not on that shelf.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that book said a thing out loud at the tenth hour that nobody has written down anywhere and that about four people in that room heard and about four did not, and what she said was that this room had been asked for a word and had not been told what kind of word, and that the person who had to find it was the person who kept the register, and that nobody had thought to ask her whether she was going to look for it or not, and she said it standing at the far end of that bench with her hands at her sides and she was not thanked for it and she was not argued with and nobody in that room came at her afterwards about it.
