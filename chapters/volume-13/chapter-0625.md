# Chapter 0625 — A Man At The Near End Of That Bench Says What A Decision In That Room Costs Before He Makes It

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both hands flat on the top of that bench and he leaned on them and he did not take them off while he was saying any of it. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in the room over a market, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name under that date. There were about nine people in that room at that hour and every one of them was in that room before anything was said in it. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, and that piece of chalk was in that pocket at the ninth hour of the three hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month and at the thirteenth hour of it, and it did not come out of that pocket at any hour in between.

Nobody in that room could say what the column on the right of that page is for. About four of the people in that room said so out loud in the ordinary way when the question came round to them, and about four of them said nothing at all, and one of that four looked at the floor of that room for about as long as it takes a person to look at one thing. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with her own hands in the sleeves of her own coat for most of that morning, and nobody in that room asked her what that column was for, and she did not say.

---

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the middle of that room, and what he wanted was an order about the two words that are going into two places, and which of the two of them went first.

"Can this room not just say which of the two of them goes first and be finished with it."

The man of about thirty-four took his hands off the top of that bench and put them flat on the front edge of it instead, and he said the thing that is said, to that room, and it was the first thing he said and it was what it cost before it was what he was going to do.

"**Nobody will be able to tell afterwards that a person chose it, and in about a year nobody in this city is going to be able to tell it either.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody said that he was right, and about four people in that room understood at once what he had meant by it and about four did not, and the man at the far end of that bench said that he had not understood a word of it and was asked again by a woman near the step what the cost of the order was.

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that is said, to that room and not to the woman, and it was the whole of his reason and it was his own.

"**A column somebody left alone is not a column nobody has been in, and those two are not the same object, and this room has not decided what a word in that column is for, so the word in that column is mine and it goes in before the word that goes in the one place on that sheet, and neither of the two of them is to be read out in this room.**"

That drew no thanks out of that room either, and no argument. Nobody in that room improved on it, nobody argued with it, nobody said that he was right, and nobody in that room asked him where he had got the right to decide it, and about four people in that room were in the second half of that afternoon before that room had settled whether the order had been his to give, and about four of them had settled it inside about as long as it takes a man to look at a sheet on a table.

---

There was no vote in that room. Nobody stood up and nobody was counted and nothing was put to anybody, and the four days that would have been the cooling of a thing like that are not available in this city, and that is the sixth time a room in this city has been asked for something and had to answer it in the hour it was asked it. There was no notice either. Nobody in that room wrote one, nobody named a day on which the next one would go out, and there is no eighth of them and there is not going to be one.

The man at the far end of that bench had wanted that room to be finished with the order, and what it got instead was an order, and he did not like the shape of it. He said the thing that is said, to the man of about thirty-four, and he wanted it put to the room in the ordinary way.

"Then put it to the room. Nine people. Put it to the room and let the room have it."

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that is said, to the man who had asked for that, and he did not raise his voice to do it.

"**You are asking me to do a thing with nine people in it that nobody in this city has ever done, and I have just told this room what it costs, and you are the second person today to want it cheaper than that.**"

Nobody in that room argued with him about that either. The man at the far end of that bench sat back down where he had been sitting and did not say another word at any hour of that morning, and at about the tenth hour he went down that stair and out of that door and did not come up it again that day, and took the morning he had come up it for with him, and nobody in that room knew that and nobody in that row was going to be told.

---

Two words are going into two places in this city. One of them is going into the column on the right of a page of a book that does not stand on that table, and one of them is going into the one place on a sheet that is lying on that table under the one window with a heading across the top of it and nothing whatever in that one place. Neither of those two words was written down at any hour of the three hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month. Neither of them was said out loud in that room at any hour of that day, and a word in a place on a page is read by anybody four hundred miles off and checked by nobody four hundred miles off, and that is what the order in that room was about and not about either of the two of them. At the near end of eleven miles of flats there is a yard, and a stack of boards stands in it, and on the top of that stack there is a board with a figure of this city's own cut into the side of it that the sun gets to and a line of grey grit lying across that figure from one end of it to the other, and nobody in that room said one word about that figure at any hour of that day and nobody in that room has ever been told where it came from.

---

An order for two words was settled on the three hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and nothing else was settled at all, and the four days that would have been given to the settling of it are not in this city. The order was that one of those two words goes into the column on the right of that page before the other one goes into the one place on that sheet, and that neither of the two of them is to be read out in a room, and both of the two of them are on nothing at any hour of that day. The woman who keeps that city's only public book was at the near end of that bench from the ninth hour to the thirteenth hour and was not asked one thing about either of those two words, and did not say, and the five figures in the column beside the one that had been left alone were the same five they had been at the ninth hour.
