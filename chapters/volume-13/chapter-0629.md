# Chapter 0629 — A Trough And A Bucket And A Rag Go Out To That Yard And A Board Comes The Length Of It

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had both hands on the near end of that trough and he set it down in the lee of a stack of boards in that yard and squared it with the wall, and he was out on that ground before the light and nobody had sent him anywhere. It was between about the fifth hour and about the ninth hour on the three hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in a yard at the near end of eleven miles of flats, in weather that had not decided anything. He has a bucket and a rag, and a trough that goes out and comes back, and the rag is on the stone lip of a step at the end of that market row and it was not in that trough and it was not in that bucket and it did not come out to that ground at all.

A stack of boards stands along the wall of that yard with its ends squared off, and the topmost board of that stack has a figure of this city's own cut into the side of it that the sun gets to, and it is put there straight and square with the boards under it. A line of grey grit lies across that figure from one end of it to the other. That grit came off those boards on the five hundred and ninety-second day of this flood and nobody wiped it and nobody in that yard wiped it that morning either, and nothing in that yard turned that board over, because a board turned over takes its grit to the other side and leaves it lying there. Nobody in this city has ever been shown where that figure came from and nobody in that yard asked. About four people went past the open end of that yard that morning and about four of them did not stop, and nobody in that yard took the number of either of them.

---

A man came into that yard at about the seventh hour with a barrow of his own and went up to the foot of that stack with it and found a trough standing square across the foot of it. He said the thing that is said, to the man of about twenty-seven, and he wanted the foot of that stack.

"That is the way I come in."

The man of about twenty-seven said the thing that is said, to the trough, and he had his bucket in his right hand while he said it and he did not put the bucket down to say it.

"**That trough is on the high ground of that yard because the low ground of that yard is under, and I have been out on this ground before this morning and before the morning before that, and you are welcome to come round the far end of that stack and you are not going to have me move it for you.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. The man with the barrow stood there about as long as it takes a man to decide that a trough is not worth the length of a stack, and then he took his barrow round the far end of that stack and came back out of that yard the way he had come into it, with nothing on it, and there is no barrow in this city with anything on it on any day of this flood. About four people in that yard saw a man go round the far end of a stack and about four did not, and the number was not taken.

A second man came into that yard at about the half hour after the seventh with a barrow of his own and wanted the foot of that stack for a board he had come for, and he said the thing that is said, to the trough and not to the man of about twenty-seven, and he wanted it moved six feet.

The man of about twenty-seven said the thing that is said, to that second man, and he was picking the grit off his own hands as he said it.

"**That trough was on that spot before you came round that wall and it will be on it after you have gone round it again, and I have walked this ground in this flood more mornings than there have been weeks of it, and I am not moving six feet of a man with a barrow's convenience for a man who has not been here before the light.**"

That went past that yard unargued and unthanked and nobody in it agreed out loud with a word of it. That second man put his own barrow down at the open end of that yard, took his board off the top of that stack by the long way round with an arm round it, and carried it out to the row, and it took him about as long as it takes a man to get a board over a trough that is not going anywhere, and the man of about twenty-seven did not watch him do it.

A woman came in at the open end of that yard at about the eighth hour with a hand-basket and stopped to look at the top of that stack for about as long as it takes a person to look at one thing, and then she went on down the row and did not stop at it, and she did not say one word to anybody about what was on the top of it, and about four people after her did the same.

The man of about twenty-seven said one more thing in that yard that morning, out loud, to the top of that stack and to nobody, and it was about the ground and not about the board lying on it.

"**There is water standing in the low end of that yard four days in seven and I have been out on it before the light in all of them, and nobody in this city has ever asked me what I come out here for, and I am not going to be asked, and a man that is on a stack before a yard is empty is not doing anything to it.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody in that row was going to be told what he comes out to that ground for, and about four people in that yard heard the whole of it and about four did not.

---

Two men carried a board the length of that yard at about the eighth hour, one at each end of it, and they set it down at the foot of the stack in the lee of the trough and squared it with the end of the stack. That put the top of that stack two boards higher than it had been at the fifth hour, and the board lying face up on the top of it with the line of grey grit across the figure on it was that much further off the ground, and nothing went on it and nothing came off it and the grit lay across it exactly as it had lain. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf was not in that yard at any hour of that morning and nobody in that yard said anything about that figure to anybody, and a thing that is two boards further off the ground on a day nobody counted anything is still a thing nobody has stopped at.

Two pieces of chalk are in this flood and they are not the same chalk and they were not in one hand at any hour of that day. The store's own chalk is in a box on a step at the end of the market row with the lid of that box lying down on the rim of it and the lid has never shut, and about a third of the chalk of the store's is in a heap on the flags at the end of that row with a thumb's width of grit under the near side of that rim. Two stalls along, a man has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that has not been out of it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it was in that pocket at the fifth hour of the three hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month and at the ninth hour of it. In a room over a market in this city there is a door with a date in chalk on the outside of it at the foot of a stair, and the date on it at that hour was the three hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month in full, and there was no name under it and a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand below it.

---

The ninth hour found the man of about twenty-seven at the channel with that bucket, and he filled it and brought it back and set it down beside the trough he had squared up at the foot of that stack, and the rag stayed on the stone lip of that step at the end of the market row and was on that stone lip at the fourth hour that morning and on that stone lip at the tenth hour, and it was not inside that trough at any hour of that day. The trough went back to the store on a shoulder and came out of that yard about as wide as it went in, and the board those two men had carried the length of that yard was still lying at the foot of that stack with the tally-board lying face up on the top of it under a line of grey grit that nobody wiped, and the man of about twenty-seven washed the grit off his own hands at the channel afterwards and went back up the row with his bucket, and nobody in that row was told what he had stood at the foot of that stack for.
