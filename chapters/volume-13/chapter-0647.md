# Chapter 0647 — A Man Goes Down A Road With A Closed Flap And Comes Back Up It And The Yard At The End Of It Is Not The Yard He Went To

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had his own left hand on the strap of that satchel where it crossed his own chest and his own right hand on the flap of it, and the flap was down, and he went out of that market row at about the sixth hour and down a road out of that city with the satchel shut. It was about the sixth hour on the three hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in weather that had not decided anything.

A sheet of that shape left that city two days earlier, on the six hundred and forty-fifth day of this flood, in a satchel with a flap, and it went up a road that is four hundred miles long. It is not coming back, and nothing in this flood can bring it back to a table in this city, and nobody on that road put a hand under that flap that morning.

One trough, one bucket and one rag is all this city has of the three. The trough was standing against the foot of the wall at the end of that market row with about a foot of water in it, and the bucket was beside that trough, and the rag was on the stone lip of the step at the end of that row where it is put down every morning and left. None of the three of them came down that road with him.

---

He went down that road to a yard at the far end of it at about the ninth hour and set that satchel down on the ground at the open end of it with the flap still down, and there was nothing in that yard he had come for.

There is a barrow standing against the wall of that yard with the shafts up and nothing on it, and a stack of boards along that wall with the ends squared off, and there is no tally-board on the top of that stack. About four people walked past the open end of that yard that morning and not one of them stopped, and nobody took the number of any of them.

A man came out of a doorway in that yard at about the half hour after the ninth with his own hands empty and asked him whether he had come about the boards, and he said no, and the man in that doorway said that nobody had sent for anything that week, and then went back inside.

"Are you here about the boards? Nobody has sent for anything this week."

He picked that satchel up off the ground and put the strap over his own shoulder again and stood at the open end of that yard a while. He did not open it, and nobody in that yard asked him what was in it, and nobody in that yard is going to be told.

---

He said it out loud at that open end, to the barrow with the shafts up, and nobody there had asked him for it.

"**I have carried four of that shape up a road in this flood and I have never opened one of them, and I have not opened this one, and there is no person on this ground that I am going to say what is in it to, and I am going back up that road the way I came down it and I am not going to be asked why I went down it in the first place.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and the man in that doorway did not come out again, and about four people went past the open end of that yard and about four of them did not stop at it, and nobody took the number of either of them.

---

Two people on that road wanted two different things out of that morning and neither of them got what it was.

A woman at about the middle of that road wanted her own crate of hers carried up that road from a doorway she had come out of, and she said it to the satchel and not to the man carrying it.

He said it to the crate and did not stop walking, and it was short.

"**I have not opened this and I am not going to pick that up as well, and a man that carries two things down a road opens none of them and remembers both, and I am not that man today.**"

Nobody on that road improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and she went back into her own doorway and carried nothing up that road, and there is no barrow in this city with anything on it on any day of this flood.

A man at the far end of that road wanted to know whether a sheet had gone up that road in this flood, and he wanted to know it because he was sending his own business up it on the next round. He asked it once, in the ordinary way, standing at the side of the road.

He said it to the flap of that satchel and not to the man who had asked, and he did not stop walking for it.

"**I do not know what goes up a road and I am not going to be told, and if I did know I would not be the man you would want to ask, and you should ask at the office nine hundred miles off where they sent it from and not at a man on a road.**"

Nobody on that road said that he was right and nobody improved on it, and the man with his own business did not ask him a second thing and went on up that road with what he was going to send.

---

The fourth sheet of that shape came up that road in this flood with a heading and one place ruled under that heading and nothing whatever in that one place. It was read out in a room over a market in the local language in daylight with a date in chalk on the door, and a word that is neither a figure nor a person went into that one place in a woman's own hand on the six hundred and thirty-ninth day of this flood.

The sheet was shut on that table on the day after that and went back up that road on the two days after that, and it is outside this city now and it is not coming back. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on that road, and no chapter of this flood prints how far along it anything is, and a party of four is four and three of them went and one of them said no before anybody was asked.

He came back up that road at about the tenth hour with the flap still down and went into that market row and put that satchel behind the bench in the room over that market with the flap down over it, and nobody in that room asked him what was in it, and it did not come back to that table and it is not going to.
