# Chapter 0611 — A Plank Laid Across A Wet Sill And Not There When He Came Out

Adrian Vale had both hands under the near end of that plank and he turned it about and laid it across the wet sill of the second row of flats and put the flat of his hand on the middle of it and put his weight there to see whether it would take a person. It was about the fifth hour on the two hundred and ninety-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, out along eleven miles of flats with a channel of wet silt running along the bottom row, in weather. The light was flat and there was about a hand's width of standing water in the shadow of the top row, and nothing on that ground dried from one hour to the next.

That plank had been in his own hands the evening before and it had gone down across the same sill then, with its ends on the two sills either side, and it was not on that row when he came out. He had walked out along the bottom row to where the plank had been and stood looking at the wet stone where its near end had lain, and there was no mark on the stone and no board and nothing in the water. Nobody in this city has ever been shown who took it and nobody in this city is going to be shown.

The trough had come out with him on his shoulder and the bucket was in his right hand and the rag was not in either of them. The rag is on the stone lip of the step at the end of that market, where it is put down every morning and left, and it was not in that trough and it was not in that bucket and it did not come out along those flats at all. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket and it was in that pocket, and the store's own chalk was in its box on the step with the lid of that box lying down on the rim of it, and the two of those are not in one hand anywhere on this flood's tenth day out on that ground.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had come out along that row before the light and he was on the top row with his bucket in his right hand at about the fifth hour. He has a wall he washed once in this flood and has not washed since, and that wall is the stone above the top edge of the board at the end of that market, and neither that wall nor that board was on that ground and neither of them came out with him. He went along the top row to about the middle of it and stopped and looked down at the second row and at the place in the second row where the plank had been, and he said the thing that is said, to that ground, and it took him about as long as it takes a man to set a bucket down.

"**That board was on this sill last night and it is not on it now, and I came out here before you did and I stepped that sill on the stone, and I am not going to be the one who says where it went.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him whether he had seen anybody near that sill. He picked his bucket up again and went on along the top row toward the channel and did not use the plank and did not put a hand on it and did not look at it a second time.

A woman at the second sill with two buckets in her two hands said the thing that is said, to the plank, and it was short.

"That will hold nobody."

The man of about twenty-seven did not turn round at that. He said the thing that is said, to that sill, and it was short and it did not take him long.

"**It will hold a person who puts a foot in the middle of it and that is what a plank across a sill is for, and it was on this row last night and it is not on it now, and nobody has said a word to me about that.**"

Nobody on that ground said that she was right and nobody improved on that and nobody argued with him about it. She took her two buckets and went on along that row and did not look back at the plank, and Adrian Vale was on the far end of the same row with a trough at his feet and neither of them spoke to the other and neither of them had been waiting for the other.

Adrian Vale got the silt out from under that sill with the flat of his hand in about the time it takes to say a thing out loud, and put it in the water on the bottom row, and the water took it. He had that trough at the near end of the second row with a foot of water in it, and he tipped it out along the row so that the water went down the stone and off the edge into the channel, and about half a bucket of it did not get off the edge and stood on the stone where a woman had set out a wide basket of nothing in particular to dry. She was not in that row at that hour. She came back along it at about the sixth hour, lifted the basket up off the stone with both arms, set it down on the top row, and put her own foot in what had come off that edge, and said nothing about it to anybody.

---

The plank was not going to stay where he had put it and he knew as much of that as anybody knew, and he worked on it anyway for the better part of an hour. He shimmed the near end with a piece of the sill itself, and he turned the far end over because it was hollow on the underside, and he put his shoulder under the middle of it and bore down on it twice, and the water in the channel came up over the top of it and stood on it about a finger's width deep and he let it.

The man of about twenty-seven was at the far end of the top row with his bucket when the water came up. He said one more thing about that row and he said it to the channel and not to anybody, and it was short.

"**That channel is going to be over that sill again before the light goes and there is nobody on this ground going to be standing on this sill when it is.**"

Nobody on that ground acted on that at any hour of that morning. Adrian Vale was two rows off with his hands on the far end of a plank and he had not heard it, and about four people along that row heard the whole of it and about four did not, and the man who said it went on with his bucket to the channel and came back with it full and did not say one word about it again.

At about the seventh hour a woman with two buckets came along the second row going the other way, and the plank was on the sill at the near half of the opening and there was a second length of it at the far half, and between the two of them the sill was under about a foot of water. She went over it and went down. She got her right leg to about the knee and she got a hand on the sill edge and she came up out of it on her own and stood there a moment with the water running off her, and she did not shout and she did not call anybody and nobody on that ground had more than about as far as the top row to come from. She carried her two buckets on to the end of the row and she did not stop at the plank and she did not put a hand on it.

Adrian Vale had his back to that row at that hour and was standing in the top row with the trough on his shoulder getting the silt off the second sill's footing. He did not see her go in and nobody told him about it. About four people on that ground saw it and about four did not, and none of the four who saw it went to the end of the row to say so.

---

Four mornings of a count made on foot out along those flats are on a page in this city, and they are the five hundred and eleventh day of this flood, the five hundred and fifteenth, the five hundred and seventeenth and the five hundred and twenty-first, and the four figures they came out with do not agree with one another and were not permitted to agree. This morning was not one of those four. Nothing came off that ground in the course of it, and nobody on that ground had a slate under an arm and nobody on that ground was going to get one out.

By the time the light went off that ground the water was over that sill on the far half of the opening and had stopped short of the near half, where the plank was, and the plank was still on the stone. The man of about twenty-seven had gone back in along the top row with his bucket full. Adrian Vale carried the trough back along the top row because the bottom row was under, and the bucket in his right hand was not empty and nobody on that ground put a hand in it.

Nobody on that ground knew why that plank had gone in the night. The two of them that had been on that sill stood in about a foot of water in the long evening with their ends on stone, and a woman had gone down between them at the seventh hour, and the man who had put them there was carrying a trough back up a row he had walked out along, and he was not thanked for it and nobody on that ground had been waiting for him.
