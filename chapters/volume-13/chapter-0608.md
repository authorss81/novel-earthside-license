# Chapter 0608 — A Trough And A Bucket And A Rag Carried Out Along Eleven Miles Of Flats

Two men had a hand each on the near end and the far end of that trough and walked it out along the bottom row of those flats with the water in it slopping over the sides and coming back up again with every third step, and the bucket went in the hand of the man on the near end and the rag was over his shoulder. It was between about the fifth hour and about the eighth hour on the two hundred and ninety-third day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, on eleven miles of flats with a channel of wet silt running along the bottom row, and the sky was down on it and the light was flat and nothing on that ground dried from one hour to the next.

A man with a board under his arm was standing on the top row about the sixth hour where the two of them came round a bank of silt, and he has a board under his arm, and nineteen rates, and one name that is not on it, and a space about as wide as two of his fingers at the foot of the column between the other two. He was not trading. He looked at the trough and at the sky for about as long as it takes a man to look at two things, and he said the thing that is said, to the two men carrying it, and it was two sentences.

"**This will not be off these flats before the middle of the day and it is going to come back in about that time, and the silt in that channel is higher this morning than it was the last time any of us stood on it.**"

Nobody on that ground acted on that at any hour of that morning. The two men walked that trough a good way past that bank and put it down in the lee of the silt, and neither of them changed where he was going because of it, and the man with the board under his arm stayed where he was for about as long as it takes a man to be wrong twice and then went back along the top row without having said one word about the nineteen rates on it.

The man at the far end of that trough said the thing that is said, across the width of it to the other one, and it was short.

"Here is as good as any of it."

"**Here is where the silt is deepest and in an hour nobody on this ground is going to get a shoulder under it, and nobody is coming back out for it either.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it and nobody on that ground argued with the other one about the spot he had chosen. They put that trough down in the lee of the bank and stepped back from it and the water in it came up over both sides at once and settled.

The man carrying the near end of it said the thing that is said, to the other one, and it was short.

"We go back along the top."

"**We go out along the bottom because that is where a trough has to be emptied, and the top row is the long way at every hour, and nobody on this ground is going the long way for the sake of dry feet.**"

Nobody on that ground improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody on that ground argued with him about the way back either. The two of them carried that trough to the end of the bottom row and emptied it there and the other one said nothing about it, and about four people along that row heard the whole of that and about four did not, and the man with the board under his arm was not in that part of the ground and heard none of it.

---

The person with the slate under one arm and the tin in her two hands came along the bottom row at about the seventh hour going the other way, and the two men carrying that trough stood aside against the bank of silt to let her past and neither of them said one word to her and neither of them looked at the tin. She has no name in this city and no age given anywhere and no number and no descriptor, and she is on no roll in this city, and nobody on that ground asked her one thing at any hour of that morning and nobody on that ground thanked her. The face of that slate was turned in against her own body the whole way she went past them, and the tin was shut in her two hands, and neither the slate nor the tin was opened at any hour of that morning.

That count of one kind of thing was made on foot out along those flats on four mornings: the five hundred and eleventh day of this flood, the five hundred and fifteenth, the five hundred and seventeenth and the five hundred and twenty-first. All four of those were entered in the book in a room over a market, one under another, with a day written in beside each, and the four of them do not agree with each other and were not permitted to agree. There is no fifth morning of that count in this city and there is not going to be one, and no figure came off that ground on the two hundred and ninety-third day of the Bare Month, and nobody in this city has anywhere to put a fifth one if one ever came.

Nobody counted anything on that ground that morning. The two men at that trough did not count the steps between the bank and the end of the row, and nobody counted the length of that channel, and nobody counted how many times it had to be emptied to get a trough along it, and there is no number on any of it that anybody in this city could check.

---

The channel had moved. At the near end it had been cutting into the foot of the top row, and about the fourth hour that morning it had come back out and started cutting a new line along the bottom row instead, and the two men came back along the flats after the middle of the day with that trough between them on their shoulders rather than in their hands, and they came back along the top row because the bottom row was under water, and that trough came back a different way than it went out and it had about a foot of somebody else's water in it that had come out of the top row and not out of the bottom.

The bucket was still in the hand of the man who had carried it out and it was not empty, and nobody on that ground put a hand in it to see what was in it. The two of them carried that trough back along the top row because the bottom row was under water, and they set it down against the same wall it had gone out from, and the rag was still over the shoulder of the man with the bucket, and the water in that trough was not the water it had gone out with.
