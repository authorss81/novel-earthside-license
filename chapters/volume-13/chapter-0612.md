# Chapter 0612 — A Fence Named And A Road Named And Neither Of Them Measured

The man of about sixty had both hands on the shafts of that handcart and he set the near end of it down in the wet at the top of the shut road, and the coil of rope in the cart shifted and the hazel mallet lying across it rolled off the rope onto the boards and lay there. It was about the sixth hour on the two hundred and ninety-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, out past the last named house, in ordinary weather with the wind coming off the flats in gusts that arrived and stopped. He put that fence up himself in this flood and nobody sent him and nobody has asked him about it since he did.

Along the top of that road the fence stands, sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord, and the withies are bound at about the height of a man's chest and about four feet is a number in somebody's mouth and is not a measurement any person took. Nobody in this city has measured that fence. Nobody in this city has put a cord to it, and nobody in this city is going to.

Three barrows stand on that road a good way down it, one to a place, and there is nothing on any of them. There is no barrow in this city with anything on it on any day of this flood, and a barrow that carried a sheet up a road is not one of these three and joins them in nothing. Three of a party of four went down that road. A party of four is four. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on it.

---

The man of about sixty said the thing that is said, to that fence, and he said it while he was picking the mallet up off the boards of his own cart and putting it back across the coil, and it was one sentence and he had said it before.

"**I put sixteen of them in and nobody in this city has ever come out here and asked me a single thing about it, and I am not going to stand in this road waiting for it.**"

Nobody on that road improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him. The man with the handcart that wanted to get down the road with a load on it came back up to the top of it about the seventh hour and stood at the near post and said the thing that is said, to the near post, and it was short.

"There is a way round at the far end of it."

The man of about sixty did not move from the near end of that road and he said the thing that is said, to the man with the handcart, and it was short.

"**There is a way round at the far end of it and I know how long that way round is and nobody in this city has ever asked me how long it is, and you are not going to either, and you can take that cart down that way or you can leave it.**"

Nobody on that road said that he was right and nobody argued with him about the far end of that fence and nobody took that cart down the way round for him. The man with the handcart stood at the near post of that fence for about as long as it takes a man to decide to lose a morning, and then he turned his cart about and took it back the way he had come, and the day went out of him in a way that nobody in this city is going to be told about.

A woman with a bundle on her head came out past that last named house about the eighth hour and stopped at the top of that road and looked down it at the three barrows standing on it, and she said the thing that is said, to the barrows, and it was short.

"Who put those out there."

The man of about sixty said the thing that is said to her without looking up the road at all, and it was one sentence.

"**That is a question for three people and none of them is going to be in this city to be asked it, and it is not a question about a fence and I am not going to stand here and turn it into one.**"

Nobody on that road improved on that and nobody thanked him and nobody said that he was right. The woman with the bundle stood there a while longer and then went back the way she had come, and nobody on that road told her a thing.

---

The road under that fence is shut and has been shut since this flood began, and it goes four hundred miles, and it goes past nine named things and then past a great many things that are not named, and it does not come back to this city. Nobody on that road at about the ninth hour had a way of knowing how far along it anything was. About four people that day were on the top of that fence at one time or another and about four were not, and not one of the eight read a figure off that fence or wrote one anywhere.

The man of about sixty put his handcart back where it stands and took his hands off the shafts and wiped them down his coat, and the rope in it is a coil that has been used and the cord bound round the head of that mallet has been used, and neither of those is new. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has a length of new rope over the back of his own chair at the end of that market that has never been used, and he is no better placed in front of that fence than this man is, and nobody in this city has ever asked either of them about either rope.

A man with a barrow came out to that top at about the tenth hour, put his shoulder against the near post of that fence to get his weight off the barrow, and went back in without touching it and without counting it. Nobody in this city has ever been shown the length of that fence end for end, and nobody has a way of checking one.

---

Nothing on that road was measured on the two hundred and ninety-seventh day of the Bare Month. A fence stood along the top of a shut road with three empty barrows on it and nobody on it, and a man who had put the fence up was standing at the near end of that road with his hands at his sides wanting a question asked of him, and the woman with a bundle on her head went back the way she had come without one, and the man with the handcart took his load and his morning in the other direction, and by the time the light was off the top of that fence the man who had built it had walked back in past the last named house and nobody in this city knows whether that fence stands between the right two things, and the standing of it is not a thing that any of the four of them settled.
