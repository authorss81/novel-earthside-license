# Chapter 0616 — A Corner On That Road Nobody In This City Has Taken A Cord To

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came round the corner of that road on foot with his hands in his pockets and stopped, and the fence turned with it. It was about the sixth hour on the three hundred and first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, out past the last named house, in ordinary weather. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and he had left that board where it stands and that piece of chalk was in that pocket, and both of those things were out of that market for about two hours of that morning and nobody in that market knew where he was.

Along the top of that shut road the fence stands, sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord, and where that road turns the last of those withies is bound to the first post round the turn, and nobody has ever put a cord to that corner. Nobody in this city has measured that fence and nobody in this city has moved a post in it, and about four feet is a number in somebody's mouth and is not a measurement any person took.

That road goes four hundred miles. Three barrows stand on it a good way back down, one to a place, and there is nothing on any of them. Nobody in this city can be shown one person standing on that road. Three of a party of four went down it. A party of four is four and nobody in this city has counted anything about that road at any hour of any day.

---

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that is said, to that corner, and it was one sentence and he said it without taking his hands out of his pockets.

"**Nobody in this city has ever been round this corner with a cord to it and I have been round it on foot, and nobody is going to ask me what is past it and I am not going to tell them.**"

Nobody on that road made a better thing of what he said and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody asked him one question about it. A man with a barrow came round that corner about the seventh hour with his barrow empty and went on round it as far as a man goes to see whether a road goes anywhere, and came back and stood at that turn with his hands on the shafts.

The man of about thirty-four answered him without moving his hands out of his pockets.

"**It goes on past this the same as it goes on past the last post, and I have not been asked what is on it and I am not going to be asked, and you can go round that corner yourself and you have a barrow and nothing in it and there is nothing on the three further down that road either.**"

Nobody on that road said that he was right and nobody argued with him about it and nobody improved on it. The man with the barrow stood at that turn for about as long as it takes a man to decide that a man is not going to say more, and then he took his barrow back the way he had come, and he did not say a word at that corner to anybody after that.

A woman with a hand-basket came round that corner at about the eighth hour and stood on the corner itself and looked both ways along that road, and she said the thing that is said, to the man of about thirty-four, and it was short.

"You know this road."

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that is said, to her, and he turned the corner post over with the flat of his hand while he said it.

"I know this corner and I have not been round it with a cord and I am not going to be round it with anything, and there is no answer I could give you at this corner that would be worth the two hours it has cost me already."

Nobody on that road improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it. The woman with the hand-basket went a little way past that corner on her own and came back and stood beside him and said the thing that is said, to the fence, and it was short, and nobody in that city was behind her and nobody answered it.

He did not look at her while he said it, and his hands stayed where they were.

"**Nobody has said to me where that fence begins and nobody has said to me where it stops and I am not going to say that either, and if you want to know where a thing begins and stops there is a woman in this city who keeps a book and she is not at this corner.**"

Nobody on that road said that she was right and nobody argued with him about it and nobody improved on it, and the woman with the hand-basket did not go and ask her and the man of about thirty-four did not offer to take her, and he stood at that corner with his hands in his pockets for another half hour and did not say one more word about where anything on that road begins.

---

That fence was on that road when he came round the corner and it was on that road when he went back the way he had come, and none of its sixteen posts stood anywhere other than where it had stood at the sixth hour, and the eleven withies were bound the same way, and the last of them at that turn is bound round the first post round the turn and it is still bound that way. Nobody in this city has a number for that corner and nobody has a way of getting one.

He got back to that market at about the ninth hour. The woman who keeps the next stall along had been at her own front the whole time he was gone and she had nobody at hers, and a man who wanted a thing off her went along the row and came back and did not come back again that morning, and she did not say to anybody where the man from two stalls along was, and nobody in that row said anything to him about where he had been either.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his hands back in his pockets at that turn and said one more thing to nobody in particular, and it was short, and it was about the two hours and not about the road.

"**That is two hours out of a market and about four people saw me go past and about four did not, and I am not going to be asked where I went and I am not going to say it, and there is a corner on that road that nobody in this city has ever taken a cord to and I have still not taken a cord to it.**"

Two hours of a market went out of that day at a corner of a shut road where no cord has ever been taken and where no post has ever been moved, and the three barrows on that road stand with nothing on them and nobody in this city can be shown one person on it, and the man who came back from that corner went back behind his own trestle and left his hands where they were for the rest of that day.
