# Chapter 0639 — A Cost Named Out Loud By The Man Whose Chalk It Was And Then A Word In The One Place On That Sheet

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands on the top rail of it and the flat of his own right thumb lying along the rail where his own hand had been shut since yesterday. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, in the room over a market, in daylight, and about nine people were in that room.

What is on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room is a date in chalk, and the three hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month is on it in full. No name at all is under that date, and the wood below it is bare for the width of a hand.

That register was lying open on that bench with its spine to the wall behind it, and there was one thing at the head of the column on the right of that page where nothing had been at the seventh hour the day before. The five figures in the column beside it were the same five they had been the day before, and not one of the five was struck out, and no sixth was entered and none was taken out.

Behind that register, on its own shelf, a second slate is standing on its edge with its written face turned to the plaster. There is a day cut across the head of it and there is nothing whatever under that day. Nothing in that room lifted it, turned it over or wrote on it at any hour of that day, and the only hand that came near that shelf was the keeper's.

---

He said it out loud to that room at about the ninth hour, and it was the second time in two days he had said something he had not been asked for, and nobody had asked him for this one either.

"**What this costs is not mine and I am saying it in this room so that the person it goes from is standing in it: from today no page in this city can show that a place was left alone on purpose, and the woman who keeps that book is the one it goes from.**"

Nobody in that room improved on it and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it, and the woman it went from did not thank him for saying it out loud in front of about nine people.

A man at the far end of that bench asked her, in the ordinary way, who had chosen it. She did not raise her voice to answer him.

"Nobody asked me and I did not choose it and I am the one who wrote it."

Nobody thanked her either. About four people in that room had that in their own heads by the tenth hour and about four did not, and neither of those two groups said one word about it to the other before the light went off that one window.

The man of about thirty-four took his own hand off the top rail of that bench and said one more thing about her to nobody in that room, and nobody had asked him for it.

"**Nobody has asked her what she is going to put in it and nobody is going to ask her, and she is going to do it anyway and she is not going to be thanked for it, and that is the whole of what I know about a page.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody said that he was right, and nobody asked her what she was going to put in it.

---

Two people in that room wanted two different things out of that day and neither of the two of them got what it was.

A man near the step wanted that sheet to go back up that road with nothing in its one place, on the grounds that a page that arrives here with a space in it and goes back with a space in it is a page that tells a person four hundred miles off that there is nothing here to be had.

A woman who had come up that stair about the eleventh hour wanted to be left alone at that table with that sheet for as long as the light held.

Neither of them got it. Nobody in that room took that sheet away from anybody and nobody in that room left that bench empty, and the room did not settle anything about which of the two of them was right.

---

At about the half hour after the eleventh the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that city's only public register squared that sheet on the table under the one window so that the heading across the top of it stood the right way up for the people on the other side of that table. It has a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the bone it was folded around is lying in the near fold of it, and that one place under that heading was empty at the ninth hour.

She wrote in it with a pen and ink, in her own hand, one word. It is a word and not a figure and not a person, and it went into that one place about the half hour after the eleventh hour of that day and nobody in that room read it out and nobody repeated it and nobody asked her to say it again.

A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing, and it is a kind of thing four hundred miles off can read and four hundred miles off cannot check, and that is the whole of what that sheet has been sent out of this city to be.

Nobody in that room read the word out of that one place and nobody repeated it and nobody asked her to say it again. There is no page in four hundred miles that will say anything about what went into it, and nothing in this flood has an instrument for taking a word out of a place under a heading and leaving that place bare again.

---

Over the back of the chair at that far end of that bench there is a length of new rope, and it is as good as the day it came into this city. Nobody in that room looked at it once. No hand in that room lifted it off that chair or cut it or took it up, and the man it belongs to was not in that room and has not been asked one thing about it by anybody in this city at any hour of this flood.

Nobody turned that sheet over at any hour of that afternoon. Nobody put a weight on it and nobody put it back into a fold and nobody put it into anything to be carried anywhere, and it is not going up that road on any day before the quarterly round comes round again.

At about the thirteenth hour the light had come off that one window onto the near edge of that sheet and onto the heading across the top of it. The one place under that heading had a word in it that had not been in it the day before, and the woman who had put it there was standing at the near end of that bench with her own hands in her sleeves, and her own name is not on that sheet and was not going to be, and nothing she says about it four hundred miles off will come back down that road.
