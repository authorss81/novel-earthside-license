# Chapter 0617 — Two Numbers Out Of A Room And Neither Of Them Taken Back

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood at the head of that bench with the flat of her right hand on the wood and she said the thing that is said, to the room, and it was to start with. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in the room over a market, in daylight, with about nine people in it. She has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty. That slate is on its shelf behind her, and there is a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it did not come off that shelf at any hour of that morning.

The date on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room carries one thing only, a date in chalk, and it is the three hundred and second day of the Bare Month written in full, and there is no name under it and there never has been one under a date on that door. The fourth one-place form was lying on the table under the one window with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading with nothing whatever in it, and the first one-place form was standing on the shelf opposite the bench with a figure of this city in its one place, read out on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood and never returned and never withdrawn. Those two sheets of that one shape were in that room on that morning and nobody in that room put one of them down next to the other, nobody had a hold on both of them at once, and nobody in that room said out loud any word about how one of them stood in relation to the other.

Through the one window of that room you could see the front of the store, and the board on the wall at the end of that market was in the edge of it, three lots of figures on it with nothing under any of them and nothing taken off any of them either. Nobody in that room looked out of that window at that wall at any hour of that morning.

---

"**Count what has come up that road into this room, and count it before the light goes, and I will write down whatever the room says and I will write down both of the numbers if the room gets two.**"

About nine people in that room had an opinion about how to do that and about four of them said so out loud at once and about four kept it. A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the room, and he wanted the ones that are here.

"What is on that table and what is on that shelf is what is here."

"**The ones that are on the table and on the shelf are two of them and there are two more of that shape that are not in this room, and if the room counts the two that are here it is not counting the road, it is counting the furniture.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked her for it and nobody said that she was right. A man with his hands on his knees said the thing that is said, to the middle of the bench, and he wanted the ones that had figures in them.

"You count the ones somebody put a number in."

The woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that is said, to him, and she did not raise her voice to do it.

"You count the ones somebody put a number in and you get a different room again, and I am not going to choose between you and I am going to count it the first way and then count it the other way and put both of them in front of this room and let the room find somebody who can settle it."

Nobody in that room argued with her about that and nobody improved on it. She counted it the first way, by what had come up that road into this city, at about the tenth hour, and she said the number out loud once.

"**Seven, counting everything that has come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood, and I have counted it and I am not taking it back.**"

About four people in that room had that in their own heads by then and about four did not. Nobody in that room said which of those two groups was right and nobody said that she was right.

She counted it the other way, by what is standing on each of them, at about the half hour after the tenth, and she said the second number out loud once and she said it slower.

"**Eight, the same count done the other way round, and the difference between those two numbers is a thing I cannot account for on a page in this room, and I am not taking this one back either.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody said which of those two numbers was the count and nobody said that she was wrong about the first one. A woman standing at the back of that room said the thing that is said, to the table, and she wanted to know what happened to the two that were not agreeing.

"Then one of them is no count at all."

"**Both of them are a count and not one of them is the count, and this room has not been told which of the two is the one anybody should be counting, and the person who will have to put one of them on something is standing at the head of this bench.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for saying that and nobody improved on it and nobody came at her afterwards with a second question about it.

---

Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood. Three of the seven carry four places each, and the places on them are printed in the order they are printed. One of those three went out of this city on the three hundred and ninety-third day, and what it carried was a district, a population, a rota and a share. A second stands where it has always stood, and nobody has opened it or filled it in or drawn it back: a date, a gate, a place for whoever keeps that gate, and the days. The third of those three went out on the four hundred and thirty-eighth day with a place, a line, a day and a person on it. The other four of the seven have one place apiece. One has a figure in it and is on the shelf opposite the bench. One was filled on the five hundred and thirty-eighth and left this city in a satchel with a flap on the five hundred and forty-fifth, and it is not in this city and it is not coming back and nobody in that room put it on that table. One arrived with a figure already in its place, had a second figure written into the same place on the five hundred and eighty-eighth, and went back up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth with a line of words set under the figure. Those words are outside this city and on no page in it. The fourth is on that table with nothing in its one place, and it is to have a word in it and not a figure, and it is to go back up a road four hundred miles long.

Seven notices have gone out of this city in this flood. Every one of them was written by somebody who sat down in this city to write it. None has gone out since, and there is not an eighth on its way and there is not going to be one, and nobody in that room wrote one that morning, and nobody in that room said when the next one goes. The sheet that went up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth is not one of those seven and is not counted among them by anybody.

---

The register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and the column on the right of that page was empty at the ninth hour and empty at the half hour after the eleventh. Nothing went into it and nobody said why it is empty. The second slate was still on that shelf where the keeper's hand had left it that morning, standing on its edge with its face to the plaster, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's.

Seven and eight were both said out loud in that room in daylight in front of about nine people and neither of the two was taken back and neither of the two went on anything. The woman who said them stood at the head of that bench with the flat of her hand on the wood and let the room carry both of them out of it, and about four people in that city know which of the two she is going to use and about four do not, and the road that those documents come up is four hundred miles long and there is nobody standing on it that anybody in this city can be shown.
