# Chapter 0640 — The Wall Of That Room Said One Thing And A Man Agreed With It In About Four Seconds And Went Down The Stair

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour with the string at the top of that board over his own left shoulder and the face of it turned in against his own ribs, and he stood at the far end of that bench in the ordinary course of that market. He has nineteen rates on that board in three columns, and at the foot of the column between the other two there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers with nothing whatever in it, and he did not put a finger in it.

It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and twenty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, in the room over a market, in daylight, in the ordinary course, with about nine people in that room and about nine people at that end of the market below it.

What is on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room is a date in chalk, and the three hundred and twenty-fifth day of the Bare Month is on it in full. Nothing whatever is under that date, there never has been anything under it in this flood, and the door below it is bare for the width of a hand.

That sheet was lying shut on the table under the one window with the bone still in the near fold of it and the two folds it came up that road in pressed along their whole length, and nobody in that room had opened it at any hour of that morning. About nine people at that end of the market below knew that there was a room over them and not one of them knew what was being said in it.

Two pieces of chalk are in this flood and the two of them are not one chalk. The store's own is in a heap on the flags at the end of that market row with about a third of it in the channel, and its box is on the step above them with the lid of it lying down on the rim and that lid has never shut. The other one is in the inside breast pocket of the man of about thirty-four, two stalls along, and it went back into that pocket on the day before and it has not been out of it since.

---

About the middle of that morning the wall on the far side of that room said one thing, and that wall carries nothing on it and has nothing on it.

> A place on a page that has nothing in it is not a record of a refusal. It is the place where the refusal is kept, and the day somebody writes in it the refusal and the thing that was refused are one entry, and there is nothing on the page that says which of the two it was. Nothing in four hundred miles can take a word out of a place and leave the place empty, because a page that has been written in and a page that has been emptied are not the same page, and the difference between them is on neither of them.

The man of about thirty-nine agreed with it out loud in about four seconds. It was a handful of words and he had his own right hand on the face of his own board while he said them.

"**That is right, and I have said so out loud, and I am not going to say it again.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room said that he was right. Nobody asked him to say it a second time and nobody came at him afterwards with a question about it.

About four people in that room were near enough to that far end of that bench to have taken all of it in and about four were not, and those two groups did not exchange one word about it at any hour before the light went off that one window. A word in a place on a page is a kind of thing, and nobody in that room repeated one word of what that wall said out loud and nobody took it down onto anything.

He said one more thing out loud at about the half hour after the tenth, to that bench, and it was not the same handful of words and nobody had asked him for it.

"**I have been right about a page in about nine words five volumes running and nobody in this city has ever thanked me for one of them, and I am not going to be thanked for this either, and I am not going to say it again.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that either and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said that he was right, and nobody repeated a word of any of it out loud.

---

Two of the people in that room wanted two different things out of the rest of that morning and neither of them got the thing each of them wanted.

A man at the near end of that bench wanted that thing written down somewhere in that room so that about nine people would have it in front of them instead of in their heads, and he asked for that twice and got the same answer both times, which was that there is no page in that room that is going to carry it.

"Write it down. In anything. I do not care what it is on."

A woman who keeps a stall at that end of the market below came up that stair at about the eleventh hour for her own business and was in that room for a quarter of an hour and said that whatever had been said in it was for the people who were in it.

"Whatever that was, it was for the people standing in this room and not for the nine of us underneath it."

Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody argued with her.

Nobody in that room settled anything that morning. Nobody put a vote to anybody and nobody counted anybody and nothing was put on a notice, and there is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one.

---

At about the half hour after the eleventh he took the string off his own shoulder and put that board out of his own hands and gave it to the man next along to hold for him, and he did not say anything about it, and that man held it and gave it back.

That register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall. Five figures stand in the column that holds them, one under another, with a day written in beside each of the five, and none of the five is struck out. The column on the right of that page carries one thing near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page.

The second slate stands on that same shelf up on its edge with its written face to the plaster, and the only hand in that room which came near that shelf was the keeper's. A day is cut across the head of that slate and there is nothing whatever under that day, and it did not move at any hour of that day.

A line of words set under a figure on a form is a kind of thing this flood has sent out of this city once and that one is outside this city. Nothing on any surface in this city carries a line of words under a figure now, and nothing on any surface in this city carries one, and that is a state of this city and not a silence.

---

At about the twelfth hour he went down that stair and went along that row and did his own trading at the far end of that city. Nobody in that room asked him one thing about the space at the foot of the column between the other two on his own board, and nobody has ever asked him one thing about it in this flood, and the space on that board is about as wide as two of his fingers and there is nothing whatever in it.

About the thirteenth hour the light had come off that one window of that room onto that table and onto the shut sheet lying on it. That wall said nothing else on that day and it is not going to say anything else in this flood, and nothing in that room was settled, and nothing was granted, and no road was opened.

That door at the foot of that stair had a date in chalk on it at the end of that day and nothing under the date but a bare piece of door about as wide as a hand. The word going four hundred miles is the name of that room and it is on a sheet on a table and not on that door, and it is not going on that door, and nobody in that room wrote it there.
