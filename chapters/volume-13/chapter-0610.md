# Chapter 0610 — Two Chalks Counted In A Room And Not Put Together

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had the flat of his right hand over his own inside breast pocket and he took the hand off it and put it flat on the front edge of the bench and left it there. It was about the ninth hour on the two hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. He has a board standing face in against his own trestle two stalls along, and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he does not use, and that piece of chalk has not been out of that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood. The date on that door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room was in chalk, and the two hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month was written in full on it, and there is no name under it.

About nine people were in that room and every one of them was there before the ninth hour. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the one window and has a second slate, and a column on the right of every page she leaves empty. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of the bench with his board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he has nineteen rates on it and one name that is not on it, and a space about as wide as two of his fingers at the foot of the column between the other two with nothing whatever in it. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was against the wall by the stair with the strap of his satchel across his chest and has one thing he knows what is in.

On the step at the back of that room there was a box with the store's own chalk in it and its lid lying down on the rim of the box, and that lid has never shut and is not going to shut on any day of this flood, and there is a thumb's width of grit under the near side of that rim that nobody in that room has looked at. That box had been put on that step at about the ninth hour by somebody that nobody in that room saw do it, and there are two pieces of chalk in that room and both of them are that kind and neither of them is the other.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the thing that is said, to the man with his hand on the front edge of that bench, and it was short.

"You have chalk on you."

"**That chalk has been in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and nobody in this room has asked me one thing about it in three hundred and twelve days, and nobody is going to start this morning.**"

Nobody in that room asked him about it. Nobody in that room improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody told him he was right, and nobody in that room put a second question to him about that pocket at any hour before the thirteenth hour.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said the thing that is said, to the table under the one window, and it was short.

"When does that go back up the road."

"**I have carried four of that shape on that round and I have not opened one of them, and there is nobody in this city I could tell what is in any of them.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what was in it and nobody thanked him for that and nobody improved on it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the table, and she wanted the room to know what the room had been asked for.

"**A word goes in that one place and not a figure, and this room has to find the word, and there is not going to be an eighth notice to buy the four days that would have been the finding.**"

Nobody in that room said that she was right and nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it out loud in front of about nine people. About four people in that room had already had a go at the word on their own and about four had not, and the two groups did not exchange one word on the matter at any hour of that morning.

---

That sheet lay on that table under the one window with one place ruled under a heading and nothing whatever in that place, and it was to go back up that road with a word in it, and the word was not in it. On the shelf on the wall opposite that bench there was a second sheet of that same one shape, a heading and one place with a figure of this city in it, which was read out on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood and was never returned and has never been withdrawn, and it has stood on that shelf through every day since. Those two sheets of that one shape were in that room that morning, one on a table and one on a shelf, and nobody in that room laid one of them beside the other and nobody in that room had both of them in one hand and nobody in that room said one word out loud comparing one of them to the other.

The second sheet of that shape was filled on the five hundred and thirty-eighth day of this flood and went out of this city in a satchel with a flap on the five hundred and forty-fifth, and it is not in this city and it is not coming back and it is not on that table and it is not on that shelf. Seven documents have come up that road into this city or gone out of it in this flood. Three of them have four places on them in the order they are printed. Four of them have one place, and of those four, two were in that room, one is not in this city at all, and one had a second figure written into its one place on the five hundred and eighty-eighth day and went up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth with a line of words under the figure in it.

Over the back of the chair at the far end of that bench there is a length of new rope. It is as good as the day it was brought into this city. Nobody in that room looked at it once at any hour of that day, and no hand in that room picked it up or cut it or took it up or lifted it off that chair, and nobody in that room asked the man it belongs to one single thing about it, and he was not in that room and has not been asked anything by anybody anywhere in this city in this flood.

Nobody in that room ever put the two pieces of chalk together. The one out of the box on that step stayed on that step and the one in that pocket stayed in that pocket, and they were the two of them and they were counted and they were not in one hand at any hour of that day and nobody in that room said they were the same thing and nobody in that room said they were not. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his hand flat over his own inside breast pocket again at about the eleventh hour and it stayed there until he went down that stair, and about nine people in that room watched him go and about nine people in that room went back to what they had come in there for, and the piece of chalk in that pocket was in that pocket at the end of that day, and nobody in this city had asked him one thing about it in three hundred and twelve days, and nobody in this city is going to.
