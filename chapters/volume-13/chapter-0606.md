# Chapter 0606 — A Board Read By People Going Past And A Window Somebody Looked Up Into

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the flat of his left hand on the face of his own board where it stood against the front of a stall and his right hand on the string at the top of it, and he was not trading. He has a board under his arm, and nineteen rates, and one name that is not on it, and there is a space about as wide as two of his fingers at the foot of the column between the other two with nothing whatever in it. It was about the seventh hour on the two hundred and ninety-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in ordinary weather, at that market and at the outside wall at one end of it.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the trough against that wall with his bucket in his right hand and a rag over his shoulder, and he has a wall he washed once in this flood and has not washed since, and that wall is the stone above the top edge of that board. Nobody in that row asked him anything at any hour of that morning. The trough had the water in it up to about two hands and the rag was on the stone lip of the step and that board was on its two nails above them.

That board carries the account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and two figures of this city's own that do not agree with each other. None of the three has been rubbed out and not one of the three carries a line of words under it, and neither of those two figures is written out on any page in this city, and there is no line of words under any figure anywhere in this city this morning because the only one that ever was went back up that road on the round on the five hundred and ninety-fifth day of this flood and is not coming back.

---

About four people came past the end of that row between the seventh hour and the ninth hour and stopped in front of that board, and about four came past it and did not stop. A man with a handcart stopped and read one set of those figures and went on, and a woman with a basket stopped and read none of them and went on, and about four people went past that wall without their heads turning at it. The stone above the top edge of that board went on up in the colour it keeps and no second person in this city has put a hand on it on any day of this flood.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the thing that is said, to that wall, and he said it in about four seconds.

"**A place ruled on a sheet and a column ruled on a page are two shapes, and there are two of them in this city with nothing in them, and nobody in this city has ever been told what either of them is waiting for.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right and nobody thanked him for it and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with him about it. Nobody in that row said that the two of them were the same thing, and nobody in that row said that they were two different things either. About four people in that row had already worked out what he had just said and about four had not, and the two of those groups did not speak to each other about it.

In the room above that market there is a sheet lying on a table with one place ruled under a heading and nothing whatever in that one place, and on the shelf behind the bench in that same room there is a book with a column on the right of every page that has nothing whatever in it from the head of that page to its foot. About four people in that row had worked out that a place on a page and a column on a page are both places a person might want to put something in, and about four had not, and nobody in that row said it out loud to anybody. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was in that room that morning and is not in that row, and nobody in that row knows that and nobody in that row asked.

A woman at the stall next along said the thing that is said, to him, and she wanted the front of that row cleared.

"Move in off the front and people would see that board."

"**I am not moving in off the front of anybody's stall, and a person who wants the figures on a wall read has come to the wrong end of this row.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it and nobody said that she was right. The man of about thirty-nine took his board up off the front of that stall and stood with it against his own hip, and nobody in that row looked at the space at the foot of the column between the other two on it.

A man at the near end of that row said the thing that is said, to that board on the wall, and it was short.

"Three on it. Which one."

"**Nobody in this row is going to say which of the three is the one to read, and there is nothing under any of them to help anybody work it out.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. The man of about thirty-nine took his right hand off the string at the top of his own board and put it flat on the face of it and did not say a second thing about those three figures that day, and nobody in that row asked him what was in the space at the foot of the column between the other two on his own board, and nobody in this city is going to.

---

Seven sheets have gone out of this city in this flood, every one of them written by somebody who sat down in this city to write it, and there has not been an eighth and there is not going to be one. The man of about thirty-nine said the thing that is said again, to the end of that row, and it was short.

"**Seven went out and none has gone out since, and the sheet that went up that road on the round is not one of the seven.**"

Nobody in that row improved on that and nobody thanked him for it and nobody in that row told him he was right. The man of about twenty-seven picked his bucket up and carried it the length of that row to the channel and came back with it full, and nobody in that row said one word to him about a book on a shelf or a sheet on a table or a column with nothing in it.

The date on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of the room over that market was in chalk that morning, and the two hundred and ninety-first day of the Bare Month was written in full on it, and there is no name under it, and nobody in that row went up that stair that day.

There is one window in the room over that market, and from the seventh hour of that morning until the end of that day somebody standing in that row looked up into it about four times and about four times somebody standing in that row did not, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was one of the ones who looked up into it and he did not say to anybody that he had done it, and neither of those two counts agrees with the other and nobody in that row counted either of them.
