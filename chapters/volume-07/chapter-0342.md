# Chapter 0342 — Four Sentences, And A Cloth

A room over a market had the twenty-seventh day of that month in chalk on its door, a Sunday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went further across the floor than it had gone on any day since the days went back, and the cold was out of the flagstones and in the seam of a cloth somebody had brought in that morning and not opened, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a second slate, a piece of chalk in the inside breast pocket of a man who keeps a stall, a deed of about forty years ago with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and a length of new rope nobody had used.

---

The second slate came off its shelf at about the seventh hour and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put it on the table herself and put her hand flat on the near edge of it and left it there.

It had been on that shelf since the fourth hour of the afternoon of the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a slate goes on a shelf in a room over a market and stays on it, and that the ordinary condition of a page nobody has anything to do with is a shelf, and that this one had been on a shelf for thirty-two days with one word on it in chalk and a date beside it and no name on the line, and that the word was a scar, and that this city had exactly one use for it and had not found a second one in thirty-two days, and that the only page in four hundred miles with those three things on it had been waiting in a room for somebody to change its mind.

Nobody had written on it in thirty-two days. Nobody had copied it. Nobody had asked to see it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a word that cannot be asked for is a word that is not doing anything, and that a word that is not doing anything and cannot be asked for is a word with no cost, and that this one had a cost and the cost had simply not arrived yet.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had been in the room since before the seventh hour and had not sat down.

A man who keeps a stall in a market spends his working day talking to about ninety people about nothing, and a morning in which he has said nothing is a morning a person can tell you about afterwards, and he was now on the first hour of one and about four people in that room had already worked out that he was not going to be asked anything either, because a rule a room gives itself in about a fortnight does not get set aside for a man who has been in it six times.

He is on no roster in this city. He has asked to be on none of them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who is on no roster is a man who cannot be put in a column, and that this is the first morning anybody in this city has needed a man to be outside of one, and that the reason a person needs it is that the person is about to do the one thing in this world that can be done by somebody with nothing at all against them.

"Tell them what has changed."

"There is a man with a barrow in this city."

"He has been up that lane."

"He has been up that lane."

Two of the nine had it the same way. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has spent a fortnight refusing to say anything will take a thing four times over when it is the first thing anybody has said, and that a man who keeps a stall said three short sentences and got them repeated by about four people, and that nobody in this city is going to be told what changed and two of the four who said it back are the only people in four hundred miles who know.

"Nobody sent him."

"Nobody sent him."

Nobody said anything else for a while, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city can go a morning without saying a word and that this one had been going a month on the small amount of work a room has, and that it had run out some time before the seventh hour, and that what was left was nine people sitting in a room with a slate on a table, and that nobody in the room was frightened and that a room in this city has not been frightened in a month.

---

The register-keeper said the part out loud that has to be said in a room, and she said it flatly and she said it to the slate and not to the man.

"I can do this in a room in daylight with a date on the door and that is the whole of what I can do. I cannot enter it, because there is nothing to enter: you are on no roll in this city and I have no column for a man who has asked not to be in one, and I could not enter it if there were, because what you are about to do is not a return and it is not a withdrawal and it is not a correction. It is a person with a cloth." She took her hand off the slate and put it flat on the table on the other side of it. "**And I would like it said in this room that the third thing cannot be done at all, and that everybody in here has been working for a fortnight on the assumption that it can.**"

Nobody improved on that. A woman who keeps a public register for a living has told about nine people in a room with a date on the door that the instrument she has been the keeper of does not reach the thing they have been the keepers of. She has been saying it to herself for a fortnight and has said it out loud this morning because a man is going to use the one part of it that does work.

The man of about fifty-seven had a length of new rope over the back of his chair and the man of about forty-three had a deed out with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and neither of them had opened either of those things since the seventh hour.

"I am not going to be told I was right about that word."

"**Nobody in this room is going to tell you that either, and I have been in this room three times with that deed open and I have not once looked up from it, and I would like that said before the cloth comes off that slate, because after the cloth comes off it I will not be able to say it without it sounding like a thing I am saying because of what has just happened.**"

"I have noticed."

"You have noticed and you have not said anything about it for a fortnight, and that is the single most useful thing anybody in this city has done for me." He put the rope over his arm. "I have a winter I cannot get back and I have a season gone and I have about forty years of a boundary I cannot show anybody, and this morning I had one afternoon of a room saying out loud that a mark in my field is a mark and not a boundary, and I am about to lose it, and I am not going to be told I was right about it and I am not going to be told I was wrong about it either."

Nobody said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has asked a room for a thing it cannot give before a thing has happened rather than after, and that this is the first time anybody in four hundred miles has asked a room to say nothing in advance, and that a man who buys half a holding about forty years ago is the fourth person this month to ask a room for the absence of something and that two of the four were right.

He took the piece of chalk out of the inside breast pocket.

It was the same one. It had been lying on a table in a room in this city since the forty-third day of the Short Month and he had put it there himself and had not touched it for twelve days, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who buys a piece of chalk by putting it down where everybody can see it and then not picking it up for twelve days has bought the right to pick it up, and that the price was the twelve days, and that he has been carrying it in the same pocket since the day he said one word in a room in this city and put it back.

He looked at it. He put it back in the pocket and buttoned the pocket, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket for thirty-two days did not bring it here to write with, and that the thing he brought instead was in a cloth, and that a man can bring a thing to a room in a cloth when the thing is nothing.

---

Then he said it, and it was four sentences and he said them the way a man says four things he has had in his mouth for thirty-two days and has not said out loud in a room, which is not slowly.

"**A name is only a removal when the thing named can be found. A line in an orchard cannot be found because nobody is going to it. So a line can be named safely. The moment somebody starts going to it the name becomes a description, and the name has to go.**"

The room did not make a sound. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has refused five times across about four hundred days to say whether a line on a wall works has just said four sentences in one go about why a word is a different kind of thing, and that the two of those are the same man, and that nobody in that room has worked out how to hold both of them at once and about four of them have stopped trying.

"Ask me the next one."

"**There is no next one. That is four sentences and I have said them, and I said them on the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month in this room and then I put a word in a breast pocket for thirty-two days, and I am not going to say a fifth thing in front of about nine people who have worked out a rule in about a fortnight, and that rule is better than anything I have.**"

"Who worked it out."

About four people in that room looked at each other at that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said four sentences and then been asked where he got them from has handed a room its own reflection, and that a room in this city has spent a fortnight working out how not to ask and has just been asked the only question it can answer.

"**All of you. I did not.**"

"Then what was the fourth sentence for."

The register-keeper had her hand on the open page of the book and she did not look up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a register in this city can hear a sentence at that table and not write it, and that this is the first time in about a month that she has heard anything said at it and left the page where it was.

"**The fourth sentence was for the man with the barrow, and he is not in this room, and he is not going to be asked about it, and I have said the fourth sentence to about nine people who do not know what he looks like and that is on me and not on them.**"

Nobody asked him the next one, and about four people in that room have worked out since that four sentences is what a man gives a room when he has worked out that a room in this city has spent a month arranging not to ask anybody anything, and that he had worked it out, and that a man who works out a room's rule and then hands the room its own shape is the most dangerous kind of person in four hundred miles and is not dangerous at all.

---

The man of about twenty-five was standing at the back of the room with his left boot mended at the front with a length of cord that is not new, and he has nine words in the inside of his coat that he wrote himself on the eleventh day of that month and has not said in two rooms, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who was written into a sheet on the twelfth and put it in his coat and read it and said yes in about four words in a room on the sixteenth has not opened his coat since, and that everybody in that room knows he has something in it and that nobody has asked him and that nobody is going to.

The woman of about twenty-one was standing beside him with a pen with the nib ground narrow in her hand, and the man of about forty-eight was standing at the other side of the table with a folding knife in his hand whose hinge has gone stiff and that he opens and shuts twice with his thumb and that will then hold about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have worked out since that three of four people who came to this city with a question behind them are standing up in a room on a Sunday morning and that the fourth is not coming and has not said she is not coming and that nobody in that room is going to be told what she said on the fourteenth of that month because she said it in a room with a date on the door and that is where she said it.

Nobody in that room asked whether it would go. Not the man with the barrow, who was not in it. Not the three of the four, who were standing in it. Not the man of about fifty-seven, who had a deed in front of him and a length of new rope he had not used. Not the man of about forty-three, who had both papers out and had been wrong about why. Not the man who carries things for a living, who was at the back of it with a hand in a pocket.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had a hand in his pocket and nothing in it and had said nothing since the seventh hour, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood has watched a word come off a slate and has not said a word about it, and that this is the third time in a fortnight that he has watched something happen in this room and not said anything, and that on the first of the three he asked a question and on the second of the three he said no.

"I am not going to ask whether it will go."

"Nobody is."

"Nobody has, and I said it out loud on the twentieth of that month in a room over a shop so that a woman of about twenty-one would hear it before she agreed to be one of four, and I have said it every morning since and I am saying it now with a cloth on a table, and it is the only sentence I have and I would like it noticed that I have used it about nine times."

"It has been noticed," said the register-keeper, "and I am not going to add anything to it, and a man who says a thing nine times in a fortnight in nine rooms is not a man who is short of sentences, he is a man who has one and has put it where it would do the most good."

The register-keeper wrote nothing down and the man of about thirty-one put his hand further into the pocket where there was nothing, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman with a book and a man with a pocket and nothing in it are the two instruments this city has left and that neither of them has a column.

"It has never once done the slightest good," said the man of about thirty-one, "and I am going to keep saying it."

"Then it has cost you nothing and it has cost the room nothing and about four people in here have worked out that it is the cheapest instrument this city has, and I would like it entered in the third column of my own mind, which is the only column I have ever had."

The cloth was on the table.

He did not use the chalk. That is the whole of what a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did with a piece of chalk he carried thirty-two days and buttoned back into his pocket, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the ninth line on a wall nine miles up the road was written in a harder piece of chalk on purpose so that the word would be the last thing anybody could rub out, and that this word was on a slate, and that a slate is the one surface in four hundred miles a word can be taken off, and that a man who said it in a room in daylight on the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month chose a slate and did not know he had chosen it, and that the whole of the difference between the two things is a surface.

He put the cloth flat on the slate and he did not rub. He held it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who takes a word off a page does not rub at it, because a man who rubs at a thing on a page is a man who is going to leave a hole in it, and that he stood with his hand flat on a cloth on a slate with nobody in the room breathing, and then lifted it.

The scar came off in the cloth and the cloth had it in it and the slate had a date on it.

The fifty-fifth day of the Short Month was on the slate in chalk where it had been since that afternoon, and the line under it was bare, and there was no name on the line, and about four people in that room have worked out since that there is now a page in this city with a day on it and nothing under the day and that is the only shape any of this has ever been, and that the room has had that shape before and did not know it.

---

The slate went back on the shelf at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the room went on doing what a room does, and the man went down the stair with the cloth in his hand and the chalk in his pocket and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man took a word off the only page it was ever on and did not say the word out loud at any point in doing it, and that nobody in that room asked him to say it and nobody in that room is going to, and that he is the only person in four hundred miles who could put it on any page in the world and he put it in his pocket instead of saying it and that the two are the same act.

He was at the stair when the man of about fifty-seven said it.

"You have left a page with a day on it and nothing under it, and a room with about nine people in it who have not been asked anything, and I have been in this room six times in a month and I have got less out of it this morning than out of any of the other five."

"**Then you have been in six rooms and not one of them, and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be talked to on a stair, and there is a cloth in my hand and it is going down in my coat and that is the whole of what I have done today.**"

I would like it said that the word was on that slate for thirty-two days. On the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month it was the only page in four hundred miles that could not be shown to anybody, and there has not been one since, and that is the whole of what a man with a cloth in his hand has bought with it.

"You put it there in the first place."

"I put one word there. That is a different sentence and you are not getting it out of me on a stair." He went down.

The woman of about twenty-one and the man of about forty-eight had not sat down in nine seconds and had not sat down in twenty minutes, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the two of them are the only people in it behaving as though something had been taken off a wall in front of them, and that neither of them is on a roll, and that a person who is on nothing can be the only one in a room who reacts like a person.

"You are holding that knife the wrong way."

"I know which way I am holding it. The hinge has gone stiff and it shuts about halfway, and if I hold it like this it stays shut instead of falling open on my hand."

"Give it to me and I will hold it."

"**No. I have carried it since the seventeenth of that month and it has not opened on me once and it is going to stay shut, and you have a pen with the nib ground narrow that you cut down yourself and you have not written anything with it in any room you have been in since, and I have watched you not write with it four times and I am not going to be the fifth.**"

The man of about forty-eight put the knife back in his coat without shutting it, which it would not do, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has been in about four hundred miles of rooms in this flood and has never once handed a thing he was holding to anybody, and that the offer he has just made was about somebody else's pen and not about his own knife.

"Then I will hold my own."

"You will hold your own and it will not be used, and that is the two of us, and that is four hundred miles."

Nobody was thanked. He had asked in advance, in a room, on a day with a date on it, not to be thanked, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who asks not to be thanked is not asking for nothing, he is buying the same thing he bought with twelve days of not picking a piece of chalk up, and that what he is buying is the right to be in a room, and that the price of the right to be in a room in this city is a cloth and four sentences and a piece of chalk in a pocket, and that this is the cheapest anybody has got in and the only currency anybody has been taking.

---

And it was worse.

The household that owns that orchard has gone back to having nothing, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the naming bought that orchard one thing and one thing only, and the one thing was that a mark in a field could be pointed at in a room and called a mark, and could not be called a boundary by whoever got to the room first, and that this afternoon the ability to do that went off a slate in a cloth in one movement, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a line in a field cannot be found by anybody and so could be named safely, and that the man with the barrow has been up that lane, and that the naming stopped being the cheapest thing anybody in this city ever bought about a minute before it stopped being true.

The man of about fifty-seven did not get a winter back and did not get a certainty back and did not get a season, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he has not said a word since the cloth came off the slate, and that a man who has been in a room in this city six times saying what a page will and will not do for him has not said a word at all, and that the not-saying is not despair and is not defeat and is the only thing available to him in a room where nobody is wrong.

The man beside him had his hand flat on the table where the slate had been, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been still for an hour and a half in the middle of a room is not being careful, he is holding still, and that there is no page in four hundred miles for holding still, and that about nine people in a room in this city have just watched a word come off a slate and go down a stair in a cloth, and that not one of them was asked anything about it, and that the door of that room has the twenty-seventh day of that month on it in chalk and a thumbprint in the dust of it, and that nobody is going to be asked anything about it tomorrow either.
