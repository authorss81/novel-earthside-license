# Chapter 0336 — A Board, And A Wall Above It

A room over a market had the twenty-first day of that month in chalk on its door, a Monday, and about four people in it, and the light came in on the north side and lay along the table without anybody sitting at it, and the cold was out of the glass and in the chalk on the door where somebody's thumb had been in the dust of it, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it and a ninth line with nothing on it, a register that was not in the room, a slate, and a length of time with nothing in it.

---

A room with about four people in it and nothing to put on the table is the ordinary condition of a room on the morning after a decision, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this city decided on the ninth of that month that nobody was going anywhere, and that a decision nobody argued with leaves a room with nothing in it, and that the amount of work a room has on an ordinary day in this flood is about four minutes and that four minutes is what there is.

Tamsin Quill had the fold showing and had had it in the inside of her coat since before the light, and a woman who has folded a sheet the same way since before the water was sitting in a room with the fold turned towards her, and a person who is holding a fold like that is waiting for a road to be a road again and not for anything to happen to her, and that nobody in that room was going to get either of those out of her before the light.

The man of about forty-three had a slate in front of him and had not written on it, and a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers and has had a slate in front of him since the seventh hour without putting a mark on it is a man waiting for something to be worth writing down, and there is not going to be one, and that the not-writing is not a decision and is a man being honest with a piece of chalk.

A man was standing at the window with his hands in his coat who had walked in with her and had not said a word since the stair, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has been asked nothing in about nine of them and has said almost nothing in the rest has been next to that woman for the length of a morning without asking her for one thing, and that this is not the same as a man who has stopped minding, and that the two of them know the difference and that about four of the four people in that room do not and are not going to.

"You have not asked me for the sheet."

"**No.**"

"That is nine days now and you have not asked me for it once and you did ask me for it on the second day, and I am not asking you why, and I would like it noticed in this room with a date on the door that I have noticed and that I am not asking."

Nobody said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman put the whole of a nine-day thing on a table in about nine seconds and that the man beside her said one word, and that the room did not ask either of them a second thing, and that there are two people in this city who have agreed not to mend something between them and neither of them has said the word mend out loud in about a month and both of them know what is on the table.

---

The road up to the store on the upper road is nine miles and it goes up the whole way, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a woman who has walked nine places in three days walked nine miles in about six hours on the twenty-first day of that month and did not stop once, and that the man who came with her stopped twice and did not say why.

The store is at the top of it and the yard is in front of the store and the board is above the door, and the board is a board and not a slate and it is about a foot and a half by three and it has a frame round it, and a man who washes a board every morning and has washed it every morning since before the water does it with a cloth and a pail and takes about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and there is no form in four hundred miles on which anybody can enter the fact that he has done it today.

He came out of the store door with the pail while they were still in the yard, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that a man of about twenty-seven has been washing that board since before the water and that the board carries the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and has not been written on since the day after them, and that the figures on it are four, and that not one of the four is a figure of a day that anybody in this city can now place.

He washed it. He started at the top left corner and went along the top and came back along the bottom the way a man does when he has done it enough times to have a way of doing it, and the cloth went over the figures without pressing on them, and a man who washes a board every morning has learned in about a year exactly how hard he can press and where the paint is thin, and this is not a skill anybody in four hundred miles can be taught and that it is the only thing in this yard that anybody is going to lose if he stops.

Then he put the pail down and looked up at the wall.

That is the whole of what he did not do and he did not do it the way a man does a thing he has decided not to do, which is quickly. The wall above that board is the wall the posting is on, and the posting went up on a wall in this city about a hundred and thirty days ago with nine lines on it, and eight of them cannot be read from the ground, and the ninth is the only one anybody can.

"I am not going to be asked about that wall."

Nobody asked him, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that a man of about twenty-seven put four words into a yard this morning and then waited to be asked about them and was not asked, and that the waiting is the price of putting the four words out, and that he has said this out loud in this city before and the room he said it in had a date on the door and this yard has not got a door with a date on it and that is the whole difference between the two places.

---

He put the pail on the boards and took the cloth and folded it and did not go in.

"**I am not going to tell you what is on that wall. I have never told anybody what is on that wall and I am not starting on a Monday on the top of a road with two people who have walked nine miles, and there is no date on anything in this yard for me to put it on anyway, and I have thought about that and I have decided that is the correct arrangement.**"

Tamsin Quill did not ask him, and a woman who has asked nine doors a question each in three days stood in a yard and did not ask the man who washes a board what is on the wall above it, and the not-asking is the first time in about a fortnight that she has been in front of a person with something on a page and has walked away from it, and that she has walked away from a great many things this month and this is the first one that was on a wall nine miles from a store.

"Then tell me about the wall and not what is on it."

"**There is a wall. It is about nine feet off the boards at the end, it is the one people can read from the bottom of the yard and not the top, and about nine feet is a height a man can wash standing on a board and not standing on a ladder, and I have washed four walls in this city since the water and I have washed the bottom half of about nine of them.**"

He put his hand on the board's frame.

"**That is the reason and it is a stupid reason and it is mine. A wall that has been washed looks like a wall that is being kept, and this one is not being kept by anybody, and I have got a cloth and a pail and about four hours a day and I have decided that I am not going to be the man who makes it look like something is being done to it.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that a man who has said out loud in this city that he has decided not to wash the wall above that board has now said the reason out loud, and that the reason is about how a thing looks and not about what is on it, and that a man who will not be the one who makes a wall look attended to is a man who has understood something about this flood that about nine people who write things down have not.

---

The queue was at the end of the yard and it was about nine men deep and it was not waiting for anybody, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that it has been running since before the days went back without the man who started it in it, and that a queue is the only instrument in this city that has never once needed a page and has never once been late, and that nobody in this yard can say what it is for and that this is not the place to say it and it is not going to be said here today.

Nothing in that yard was touched. There is a chest against the wall that has been shut since before the water and there is a beam on trestles that has not been carried anywhere and about nine feet of boards with a sack on them that has not been opened and has not been counted and has not had a boot put on it, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that there is a pan in this yard with a coat over it that has not been turned out and has not been replaced, and that every one of those things is the property of nobody in particular and has been for a month, and that a man who washes a board every morning and will not wash a wall has not touched one of them, and that the not-touching is the only rule anybody in this yard has and nobody has written it down.

Two brooms were on their boards against the end wall where they had been since before the water and about four people in that yard have worked out since that nobody in four hundred miles knows who brought the second one and that both of them have been there about as long as each other, and that a yard in this city is full of things that arrived at the same time and arrived by different hands and that this is the ordinary condition of every yard in every flood and is not a mystery and has never been one.

---

The cloth went into the pail and the pail went through the door and the board stood there with four figures on it and nothing else and the wall above it stood with whatever is on it on it, and a board in this city has now not been written on for longer than it takes a man to wash it, and the man who washes it has said out loud this morning that he is not going to be the reason it looks like anything, and that the two of those facts have been true for a fortnight and that this morning is the first one of them that has been said out loud in the open air on a road nine miles up a hill, and that a woman who folds a sheet of road in the same way every time stood in the yard and did not ask, and that the not-asking was hers and took about as long as a woman takes to decide not to ask, and he has not noticed it and there was no date on anything in the yard for either of them to put it on.
