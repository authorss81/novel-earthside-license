# Chapter 0307 — Four Times A Year Since Before The Water

A man of about fifty-seven was in a room over a market on the fifty-second day of the Short Month, a Sunday, with a length of new rope over his arm that he had not used, and he had paid money for half a holding and could not show anybody where the half was, and the light came in on the north side and went off the low ground by about the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was in the shutters and out of the wind and in the boards of the stair, and what was in that room was about nine people, a deed, a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and the rope.

---

He had been in this city long enough to have been told the price of a room and long enough to have found that there is no price of a room, and he had asked one question in it, and the question had been answered.

He put the rope down on the table. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man carries a rope he has not used into a room about a boundary and puts it on the table, and that he did not put it on the table to be looked at, and that he put it there because a man who has come four hundred miles with nothing in his hands has to put something down somewhere, and that what he put down was the wrong thing and he knew it was the wrong thing and left it.

The light on the north side went off the low ground outside at about the second hour of the afternoon while he was talking, and the slate on the table went from the colour of the room to the colour of a window, and nobody in that room lit anything.

A room which is about to be told something difficult in daylight does not light the fire.

"I want one thing into a page that will not come out again. That is all. I have not come here to argue with anybody and I have not come here to be told that a thing is difficult."

"There is nothing difficult about writing it down."

"**Then write it down. Write down that a man of about fifty-seven paid money for half a holding about forty years ago through a house four hundred miles inland, and that he has never been able to show anybody the half, and that he has a deed and a copy of an old page and the two do not say the same thing.**" He did not touch the rope. "**Nine words, and it is true, and it will do. And when that is on a page, somebody in four hundred miles can put the other account beside it, and a boundary is settled by two parties agreeing where it runs.**"

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and who had had a piece of chalk in his hand since the seventh hour of the morning without using it, got up out of a chair he had not been sitting in and put his coat on, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who walks out of a room in the middle of a thing he has brought money to is a man who has worked out that he is not the other party.

"That is two parties. Who are they."

"That is the part I have not worked out and it is the part I have brought the rope for."

---

The room did not take the money and did not say no, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the hardest thing that room has done since the case came up the river, because saying no to a man is a thing a room can do and being reasonable to a man is a thing a room cannot do, and because the man of about fifty-seven was not angry, and a man who is not angry is harder to say no to than a man who is.

He said nine sentences about himself and about four people in that room counted them, and the ninth is the one that is going to matter in about three weeks when somebody in this city has to decide whether to send four people four hundred miles for a page.

**"I paid money and the money was my own and it went out of a house I work for. The half I bought is half a field and a row of trees and a corner of a channel, and I cannot dig a ditch and I cannot build a wall and I cannot pay a man to mend a fence.**" He put two fingers on the rope and took them off. "**And since the ninth day of the new month I have been standing in the middle of that half telling myself I would get used to it, and I am not going to, because you do not get used to a place you cannot find the edge of.**"

**"And four times a year since the winter before the water there has been a sheet come to me with a heading on it and a question at the bottom of it, and I have answered that sheet four times a year, and I have never once been able to read the whole of the question, and nobody has ever told me what it is for, and I have never asked, and I am asking now about a different thing entirely and I did not know until this morning that it was the same thing.**"

The room did not say anything for a while, and a woman of about thirty-three who had a question about the order of nineteen stones in her hand from Wednesday did not ask it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the reason she did not ask it is that a man who has just said out loud that nobody ever told him what his own piece of paper was for cannot be asked a question about the order of somebody else's, and that she worked that out in about four seconds and put her hand flat on the table and said nothing at all.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine did not say anything either for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then she said the thing about walking, and she said it once, and she said it in plain words, and about four people in that room have worked out since that it was the only sentence anybody said on the Sunday that had a shape to it.

"You cannot get a second account without somebody walking the line. That is how it is done and it is the only way anybody has ever done it. And the moment somebody walks it, that person is on a page with their name at the top of the column, and a page with a name in it is a thing I cannot protect you from, and I have not got to the end of what I am able to say about that and I am not going to say it in nine words to a man who has paid money."

Between the two of them the rope had been moved about a hand's width twice and put back twice, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has brought a rope into a room about a boundary has been holding it and not thinking about it, and that a rope is the only object in four hundred miles that is a distance nobody can say out loud.

"You have not told me not to do it."

"**I have not told you not to do it and I am not going to, and I have not asked you whether you would, and you did not offer, and that is three things nobody in this room expected to happen this morning.**"

---

The stair of that room creaks on the fourth step and not on the others, and the man of about fifty-seven had worked that out before anybody else in that room had and had been using it to know who was behind him, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who arrives in a room with money in his hand learns the building in about an hour and learns it to know who is coming in.

A man at the back of that room had been standing with his hands behind him and had not been in it for the first hour. He came forward and read the clerk's hand on the attached sheet from the top down to the line about the ditch that had been crossed out a hundred and forty years ago, and he read the crossing out twice, and then he gave the man of about fifty-seven the name of the clerk, which is on a page, and the town, which is four hundred miles inland, and nothing else.

He was named once, in a mouth, at that table, and the woman of about twenty-nine said Adrian and then said nothing else about him, and nobody in that room asked him one question about the boundary, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the third time in about nine days has now happened of a man standing in a room where a page was read and being asked about the page and not about the thing, and that the woman of about twenty-nine has not asked him either, and that the reason she has not is on a page from four hundred days ago in a room with a date on the door, and that nobody in that room this morning had to say so out loud and everybody in it knew.

"You read that hand. Where did you learn it."

"Somewhere else. It is not a rare hand and it is not a good one and it is about a hundred and forty years old and whoever copied it out copied the crossing out, which is more than most of them do." He put his finger about a finger's width above the last line of the list and did not touch it. "**And I am not going to be asked about the boundary and I would like it said in this room that I have noticed that, and that I am going to go on reading the sheet, because reading the sheet is the only thing in here I am any use for.**"

---

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

A room which has just been told the truth by a man who cannot be angry with any of them has nowhere to put it.

"You are not angry."

"I am not angry and I want it said that I am not angry, and I have been not angry for a very long time, and a man who is not angry for a very long time is not a man who has accepted anything." He turned his hand over on the table. "**He is a man who is waiting, and the waiting is the polite one, and it has cost me the season and a certainty, and I know the price of it and I am still doing it.**"

He put his hand flat on the rope.

"**Here is what I will pay. Four men, in whatever way this room thinks is the right way, at a rate I am going to say out loud so that it is in a mouth in a room and not on a piece of paper.**" He turned the rope over on the table. "**I have not got a rate that will frighten anybody. I have got a rate that a man of about fifty-seven who has never done this before thinks is fair, and if it is too much then somebody will say so and I will not argue.**"

Nobody in that room said so, and nobody in that room said no, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a refusal that is not free costs somebody something specific, and that what this refusal costs is money that is still in a man's hand and is not in a room's keeping and is not on a page, and that in about three weeks somebody in this city is going to have to decide whether a thing can be bought, and that nobody has written that down because nobody can write it down without a name at the top of it.

At about the ninth hour, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, in front of about nine people, he asked one question, and it was the last thing he said in that room, and about four people in that room have worked out since that it is the only question anybody has asked in three days that nobody could have made up.

"Has anybody in this city ever walked a line with a chain."

Nobody answered him.

And about four people in that room worked out at about the ninth hour that nobody had, and that the answer was going to be a yes, and that nobody said it, and that the man of about fifty-seven went down the lane afterwards with the rope over his arm and stopped at a shut gate and did not go through it, and that the woman of about fifty was at the gate and did not ask him one question, and that the two of them stood on either side of a bar of ash for about as long as it takes to fill a pail without either of them putting a question to the other, and that they are the only two people in four hundred miles behaving as though a thing they are both standing next to is not there, and that it is about nine hundred paces off that gate and it has not been looked at and it is not going to be looked at this month.

The register-keeper put her hand flat on the second slate and did not write anything on it.

"**I am not going to write that down, and I want it said in this room that I am not going to, and it is not because I do not believe it. It is because it was his and it is going to stay his, and a thing goes on a slate in my hand with my name beside it and not with his, and then it is a page and not a room, and I have kept a register in this city for nine years and I know exactly what a page is for.**"
