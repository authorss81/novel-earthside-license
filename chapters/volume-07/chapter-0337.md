# Chapter 0337 — Eight Doors In A Row, And Not One Of Them Can Show Him The Edge

A gap for a cart between two houses out past the last named house had the twenty-second day of that month on it in the weather and nothing else, a Tuesday, and about four people in it, and the light came over the top of the eight roofs in one piece and did not go into the gap at all until about the fourth hour, and the cold was out of the wind off the low ground behind and in the fold of a paper that had been in the inside of a coat since before the water, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a post with a nail in it about four feet up, a length of new rope nobody had used, a folded paper with a sum on it and a name at the top of it, and a bag with a piece of chalk and a cloth in the top of it.

---

The man of about fifty-seven had been out since before the light and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has lost a season and a certainty has gone nine miles in the dark to a place where nine households had already given their days back to themselves about forty days ago, and that he did not go to the lane past the two gates and he did not go up the road past this city and he came out here, where there are eight doors in a row and a gap for a cart in the middle of them.

There is a nail in that post about four feet up and nobody in four hundred miles knows who put it in, and everybody on that road has known about it since they could walk, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who came down that row on the twelfth of that month with nine sheets in a bag put one of them on that post because the post was the only thing there that was not a door, and that it is still on the post, and that it has been rained on and has not gone anywhere and has nothing at the top of it.

He did not take it off the post. About four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been to about nine rooms in this city in a fortnight has stood in a gap and not taken a piece of paper off a post, and that the reason is that it is a notice and it has done the only thing a notice can do, which is to be on a post in a place where eight houses can see down the row that it has arrived, and that taking it down would make this gap a place where a man once stood.

He asked at four of the eight doors and he stood back off the frame before he did it at every one of them, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man standing off a frame is a man who has been thrown out of enough rooms to know what a closed door sounds like from the inside.

He said he was sorry for the trouble and he said he would not come in and he said it in about nine words at each door, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who paid money for half a holding about forty years ago stood at four doors on a Tuesday morning and asked a question nobody had asked him, and that the question was the same four words at all four doors, and that it is not a thing anybody in four hundred miles can be shown has happened because a door is a door and four words through a half-open one is the ordinary courtesy of a road in this flood.

"Where does your ground stop."

Nobody at the four doors knew. One of them said a name, and the name was a name of a piece of ground and not an edge, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a woman behind a bread board on a turning four streets off this road gave a name for nine acres about a month ago and it is the only name for a piece of ground in four hundred miles that did not come out of a document, and that this one came from a door and is not a name for an edge either, and that the two of them are three miles apart and have never been put together.

The other three said they did not know and one of them said it in a way that about four people on that road have worked out since was the answer of a person who has been asked something about his own house for the first time in this flood and has found that he has nothing to answer with.

---

He was standing in the gap at about the fourth hour with the rope over his arm and the folded paper in his hand, and about four people on that road have worked out since that the paper is the only sheet in four hundred miles with a man's name at the top of it and that it is a receipt for money out of a house four hundred miles off, and that a man who refused on Saturday to put his name at the top of anything has been carrying, and has been carrying since before the water, the one page in four hundred miles that has his name on it, and that it is the reason he is in this city and not in a house four hundred miles off with somebody else's.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the row from the low ground at about the third hour with nothing in his hands and stopped in the gap, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has carried a fifth sheet eleven miles on Sunday and has not opened it and is not going to came out to a gap for a cart on a Tuesday and did not stop at the eight doors, and that he is the only man on that road who has been to all nine of the places in about a fortnight and has not asked any of the nine anything.

"You have asked four of them where their ground stops."

"**I have asked four of them where their ground stops and not one of them knows, and one of them told me the name of it, and I have got about nine miles of road to think about that on the way back and I would rather do it here where it happened.**"

Nobody asked him to go on, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been in about nine rooms in this city and has asked for one thing in each of them has stopped in a gap and told a man who carries things for a living the finding before anybody in this city had asked him for it, and that he is going to be asked for it again in a room in about two hours and he has given it away in a gap for a cart for nothing, and that a man who gives a finding away for nothing is not a man who is being generous and is a man who cannot keep one.

---

A room over a market had the twenty-second day of that month in chalk on its door and about nine people in it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the light came in on the north side and was off the low ground before it was in, and the cold was out of the glass and in the lay of a rope over a chair back, and what was under about four pairs of hands that afternoon was a case that was shut, a copy of an entry in a clerk's hand a hundred and forty years old pinned to the corner of a deed, and a folded paper with a sum on it and a name at the top of it.

He put the receipt on the table. About four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has refused a page with his name at the top of it in this room on Saturday has put a page with his name at the top of it on the table himself on Tuesday, and that the two are the same object and about nine people in that room watched him do it, and that it is a piece of paper the size of a hand and it says what was paid and by whom and it does not say where the ground is.

"**I have paid money and the money is my own and it went out of a house I work for, and this is what came back for it, and there is my name at the top of it, and I have carried it since before the water, and I have just been round eight doors in a row and I have asked four people where their own ground stops and not one of them can tell me, and one of them told me what it is called.**"

The room did not make a sound, and this is the first time in about a month that a man has come into this room and given the room something it did not have, and what he gave it is not a document and is not a figure and is a fact about four doors in a gap for a cart, and that about nine people in this city have spent a month arguing about two wrong documents and have just been given a right thing.

"**And I am not going to be told I am wrong. I want to say that before anything else, because I know what a room does with a thing like this and it puts it in a column, and the thing I have just said does not go in a column, it goes in a gap for a cart, and there is no form in four hundred miles for a gap for a cart.**"

The man of about forty-three had the deed out and the sheet pinned to the corner of it out, and a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers put both of them on a table in about four seconds because they were going to be wanted, and he was wrong about that, and that nobody told him and about four other people in that room worked it out on their own by the end of the afternoon.

He read the clerk's hand out loud for about nine lines. He read it twice, and the second time was slower, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a list of nineteen stones in a hand a hundred and forty years old was read out in a room with a date on the door and that the room listened to it the way it listens to everything, which is with about four minutes of work in it, and that a man who has just come in from a gap for a cart did not hear a word of it.

That is the finding, and a man who paid for half a holding has said out loud in daylight with a date on the door that a household out past the last named house cannot say where its own ground stops, and nine households on a lane past two gates gave their days back about forty days ago and did not say where their ground was either, and that the two facts are a road apart and have not been put together by anybody and are not going to be, and that the man who said it does not know that the second one happened and the nine people in that room do not know that he has never been told, and that this is the cheapest thing in this flood and it is worth a season.

---

The rope went back over the chair back and the receipt went back inside the coat and the case went out under the arm of the man of about forty-three and shut, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who lost a season on the ninth of that month has lost nothing this afternoon that he had not already lost, and that he is not leaving, and that the money is in his hand, and that about four people in that room have worked out since that a man in this city who has said he is not going to be told he is wrong has not been told, and is not going to be, and that the whole of what a room in this city can do for him is what it did this afternoon, which was to let a man put a receipt on a table and read nothing at him.

Nobody in that room was asked whether any of it was going to work, and the sheet on the post in the gap for a cart was still on the post when the light went, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who asked four doors where their ground stops was not sent to them and did not send anybody and was not asked first, and that the price of no name at the top of a sheet is that nobody can do anything for you, and that the price of a man going to four doors in a row on his own is that he came back with the answer to a question nobody had put to him and there is nowhere to put it.
