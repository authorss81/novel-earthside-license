# Chapter 0344 — A Barrow Against The End Of A Cut

The light on the twenty-ninth day of that month came in over the low ground sideways and did not get above the middle of the row of trees at the near end of that ground, and the cold was out of the north and out of the wet in the ruts of a cut that had been in that field longer than anybody on that road could say, and the wind was coming up the shut road in long pushes that got under a coat and stopped, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was the two handles of a barrow, a length of ash about four feet long with a flat blade on the end of it standing against a wall eleven miles the other way, and a stone about the size of a fist that nobody put there.

---

He had come in off the flats at about the fourth hour with the barrow and nothing else in it, and he had walked the eleven miles, and about four people who have been on that road since the water have worked out since that a man who has been putting willow in since before the light on eleven miles of open ground came eleven miles in the dark to stand at the end of a mark in a field, and that neither of those two facts is about anybody's character and both of them are about what a person does when he has been asked to do a thing he is the wrong man for.

There is no rod in that barrow.

That is the whole of what he brought. A man of about fifty-two has a rod — a length of ash about four feet long with a flat blade on the end of it, ground down on one side and not the other — and it is standing in the corner of his own hut against the thick wall where the wall is thickest, and he offered the fact of it out loud in a room on the twenty-fourth of that month before anybody had asked him for it, and he did not bring it, and nobody has asked him about it since.

He put the barrow against the end of the cut and took his hands off the handles.

A cut in that field is a thing a man puts in a field to take the winter water off it, and it is about a boot wide and about nine inches deep where it has begun to fill in again, and it stops at the stone because the man who cut it ran out of ground about forty years ago and stopped. There are about nine hundred paces between the shut gate and the row of trees at the near end of that ground.

The mark is not the cut. It is about a foot wide and about two inches deep and it runs from the near end of the row of trees to the stone, and it is not a path and it is not a watercourse and it is not a furrow, and it does not go near the cut at any point, and about four people in this city have worked out since that two lines in one field which do not touch each other are the ordinary condition of a field that has been worked for a hundred years, and that nobody had ever put the two of them next to each other on a page until a man with a barrow and a man with a handcart stood at the same stone on the same morning, and that this is the only thing the two of them did.

Nobody in four hundred miles has ever walked it.

That is not a rule. It is a fact about how many people there are and what they have been doing, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a thing nobody has ever walked is a thing that can be said out loud in a room without anybody being able to point at it afterwards, and that a thing somebody walks is a thing that can be pointed at, and that a man standing at the end of it with his hands behind his back is the first person anybody has put near it, and that he is there because a room in this city asked him to be, and that a room cannot take a man back out of a field.

---

The man of about sixty came up the road at about the seventh hour with a handcart and a length of rope over the shoulder, and nobody sent him.

He had a rope that had been used and was not new, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who was told no about a fence across the top of that road said he would come back, and that it is a good deal more than he said, and that he came into a room in this city on the twenty-fifth of that month rather than to a gate and asked one question out loud and got no answer, and that a man who has come back twice for the same thing is not a man anybody has to keep an eye on.

He did not go into the field. He stopped at the stone with the handcart and did not get past it, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man with a handcart and a length of rope who stops a body's width short of a stone in the middle of a field has worked out where the work is and has not asked, and that the not-asking is not a courtesy and that he was told no once, in this city, by somebody, and has not taken it.

For a while there was nothing but the wind in the row and the two of them at the end of a cut, and about four people on that road who came out of their gates and looked and went back in have worked out since that a man who puts a barrow against the end of a thing and a man who puts a handcart at the end of the same thing are the two most ordinary things on a road in this flood, and that neither of them has a form.

---

Then the man of about fifty-two looked along it from the end, and he did it the whole way, and he did not touch it.

He stood at the stone with his hands where he had put them and he looked along the length of a mark in the ground until he had looked along all of it, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man who has said out loud in a room that he will look at a thing from the end of it and will not put his hands on it has held to that on the first morning, and that holding to it is the whole of what he came for, and that what he came for was the holding to it and not the looking.

He did not put a foot in it. He did not stand over it. He did not go down the side of it or along it, and when he came back to the barrow he came back four paces and then stopped, and about four people watching from two gates on that road have worked out since that a man who has been out in a field and has come back four paces at a time is a man who is counting his own steps and not the ground, and that there is a difference, and that on that morning it was the only difference anybody could see.

He said one sentence, and he said it to the man with the handcart and not to the field.

"**I have stood at the end of it and I have looked along it and I cannot tell you one thing about it that I did not know at the seventh hour, and I would like that said out loud before anybody asks me what I saw, and that is all I have and I have walked eleven miles.**"

The man of about sixty did not ask him what he saw. He put the rope into the handcart and turned the handle up and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has come up a road twice for the same thing and been told no once heard a man tell him in one sentence that he has nothing, and did not ask him for a second sentence, and that this is the first time in this flood that a man has said a thing out loud that nobody could use.

---

"I have come about the road."

"Then you have come about the road."

"**I was told no about a fence across the top of it, and I said I would come back, and I have come back twice, and one of those times I was in a room and asked whether a person can be put back, and nobody in that room could tell me, and I have not asked anybody since.**"

Nobody asked him anything, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man with a handcart and a length of rope has told a man who digs two true things about himself while the two of them stood at a stone, and that neither of the two was about the road, and that the man who digs has not said a word back and was not asked and is not going to be.

"He has given you nothing."

"He has told me in one sentence that he cannot tell me one thing about it that he did not know at the seventh hour, and that is not the same as being given nothing, and it is a great deal more than this city expected to get out of eleven miles."

"**It is the whole of it. Anybody who wants it put another way can come out here and stand at the end of it.**"

The woman of the second house had not gone in yet and about four people on that road have worked out since that two men who had come up a shut road to look at a thing in a field are standing four paces apart working out what a favour is, and that neither of them is a man who gives favours.

"He will not mind."

"He did not say he would not mind and he did say he would like it said out loud before anybody asked him what he saw, and that is the closest thing to a favour a man of fifty-two has offered anybody in this flood."

---

A woman came out of the second house on that lane at about the eighth hour with a bucket and stopped, and about four people on that road have worked out since that she had not been outside before the light since the days went back, and that she came out with a bucket because a bucket is a reason to be outside, and that she put it down when she got to the gate.

"You have put a barrow at the end of our cut."

"It is not your cut and I have not put a barrow at your cut, and I have put a barrow against the end of a cut that is on ground nobody in this lane has said a word about this morning, and I would like it noticed that I have not said whose it is."

"You are going to build something."

"I am not going to build anything. I have come about the road and I have been about the road twice and I was told no once, and I came up here this morning to look at the top of it, and there is a man standing at the end of a cut looking along a thing, and I have not asked him what he is looking at and I am not going to."

The man of about sixty turned the barrow a quarter round so that the handles were not in the way, which is a thing a man does with a barrow when he is not going anywhere for a while, and about four people on that road who were watching have worked out since that a woman with a bucket at a shut gate has just been told twice in a row that nobody is going to be asked anything and has stayed exactly where she is.

"He will tell you if you ask him."

"**I will not ask him, and he will not tell me, and that is the arrangement, and you have just watched two men in a field not say a thing to each other, and that is more than has happened on this lane in about a month.**"

"Two men not saying a thing is all that happens on this lane."

"Two men not saying a thing is all that happens on this lane and it is not nothing. There are nine households on here that gave their days back out of a door about six weeks ago and I was one of them, and I have not asked any of the other eight where their ground stops, and I am not going to start on a morning when there is a man in a field."

She went back in with the bucket.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the road at about the ninth hour with nothing in his hands and stopped at the gate and did not go in, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood and has not opened one of them came up a shut road on a Tuesday to stand at a gate, and that nobody sent him and that he is the only person in four hundred miles who has been to all nine places on that lane and has not asked one of them anything.

"You have been at the end of that cut."

"**I have been at the end of that cut and I have not been in the field and I have not asked the man anything, and you have not asked me why I came up here, and neither of us is going to.**"

"You have not asked me anything either."

"I have not asked you anything and there is nobody I could ask."

The carrier put his hands behind his back then, which is what a man does at a gate, and about four people on that road who were watching from a door have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living stood at a shut gate and put his hands behind his back in the manner of a man who holds gates on a turn, and that he has never done it in his life, and that he did it because there was a man a field's width away who was doing it.

"There is nobody I could ask because a man with a handcart is not a page and has no standing, and I have known that about myself for about nine years, and it is the only standing I have ever had in this city, and I have never said it out loud and I am not going to say it again."

"Nobody asked you to say it."

"**Nobody asked me to say it, and there is a woman standing at the second house who has not asked me either, and there are about nine people on this road who have not asked anybody anything for about a fortnight, and if anybody here has the afternoon free they are welcome to come up and watch a man look along a thing and say nothing, and I would put money on the whole of them going back in before the light goes.**"

They both went back in before the light went.

Then he did not do the thing he had come for, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has come up a road again and again to be told no has come up it on a morning where nobody has told him anything at all, and that this is worse, and that he knows it is worse, and that he is going to go home down that road anyway.

---

The barrow came off the end of the cut and the man of about fifty-two took the handles again and went back down to the gate and did not stop at the stone.

Nothing was measured. Nothing was dug, and nothing was counted, and nobody put a rod in anything, and the line in that field is exactly as long and exactly as deep as it was on the twenty-eighth of that month. The handcart went back down the road behind him and the two of them did not walk together, and about four people on that road who watched them go have worked out since that a man who digs and a man with a fence are the only two people in this city who have been to the end of that mark, and that one of them will not touch it and the other one has not gone near it, and that between the two of them they have established that a man can stand at the end of it in the wind and learn nothing whatever, and that it is the cheapest and the least useful thing anybody has established in this flood and that it took eleven miles and a barrow to do it.
