# Chapter 0323 — Four Ways Of Writing One Day, And No Column For It

A room over a market had the eighth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Tuesday, and about nine people in it, and by the seventh hour the light had come in over a step at the back and was lying on the floor, and the cold was in the shutters and in the wax of a slate in a case and in the crease of the back of a register that had been shut for nine years, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a register open at the back, a second slate face down, a box with a deed in it, and a sheet pinned to the corner of the deed by a pin older than any other in the box.

---

The register was open at the back and not at the front, and that was the first thing anybody in that room noticed, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register opens a book at a page she has chosen and that the page she chose was the oldest one in it and that she has never opened that book at the back in nine years.

She had it open flat with both hands and she had not written anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has declined four things out loud in this flood and named the cost of each one before saying it has come to the eighth day of a month with a book open at a page she has not looked at since she was twenty, and that the cost of this one is going to be somebody else's.

"There are four ways of writing a day in this book."

"Nine, if you count the hand you write it in."

"**Four, and I have counted them twice, and I am going to say them out loud because I want them said out loud in a room with a date on the door before I say anything else.**"

---

She read four rows out loud and nobody wrote them down.

The first is a day counted from the water, and there are about four hundred of those in the book and they are all in the same hand and the hand is hers from the first of them. The second is a day counted from the water with a different word on the front of it, and there are about two hundred of those, and they are a different instrument and the numbers run on past the number the first instrument stops at without a mark. The third is a day with a name in front of it, and there are about nine hundred of those, and there are two different names in the book for the same stretch of time and both of them are hers. The fourth is a day with a name in front of it that is three weeks old and has no figure under the name at all.

"They are four instruments. **A person reading this book today cannot tell which one any row is in, and there is no column that says, and I did not leave the column out. I have never wanted it, for nine years, because I have never once needed to know.**"

---

The man of about forty-three who keeps other people's papers had the box open at his feet and the deed out of it and the sheet still pinned to the corner of the deed where it has been for eleven years, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who brought a document to this room a week ago and had it read out to nine people has brought the same document again and has not opened his own index, and that both of those are decisions and one of them is new.

"What do you mean you have never needed to know."

"**I mean that a question went out and came back and the day I heard the answer is the day I wrote, and I never once wanted the day I asked, and that is the same thing your man with the wax said on the fifty-ninth about a column and I have not stopped thinking about it since.**"

She wrote the eighth of that month on the second slate in chalk, and the second slate was face down when she turned it over and face up when she wrote on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she turned a slate over that has been face down on that table for a week and that she did it in about four seconds without looking at the room, and that there is something on the other side of it and that nobody in that room has asked and that the register-keeper has not said a word about it in a week.

"There is a worse thing in the same book and we are not going to get to it today, and I would like it entered that I know that and that I have said so out loud in daylight."

---

Hettis Roke had the case in his hand rather than on the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who came to this room last week and put his object face down has come again and is holding it, and that a man who is holding a thing is a man who has not decided about it and has decided to wait.

"I am not going to tell her how I keep mine, and she is not going to ask me, and both of those are in a room with a date on the door and about nine people in it and that is more than the last one had."

"Nobody has asked you."

"**Nobody has asked me and I have said the shape of it once and I am not going to say it twice, and what I am going to say is one sentence about a gate and a set of days and a number, and after that I am going to stop.**"

He put the case on the table and turned it face up and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just told a room that he is going to stop has done the only thing in this flood that makes a room behave itself, and that the sentence was not about the boundary and was not about the page on the back of the stone and was not about the four in four hundred miles.

"A gate, a set of days, and a number. That is what I have. It has been that shape for longer than the oldest thing any of the four of us who keeps it has, and I have never wanted a column in it, and I am not going to be asked what that is worth."

He said the last four words to the table and not to anybody in it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man has just said in a room that he is not going to be asked what a thing is worth, and that in this city that sentence has been said out loud about four times in about a month, and that three of the four were about a word nobody has defined and one of them is about a roll of days, and that nobody in that room has ever put the four together and nobody is going to today.

---

The man of about forty-three read the sheet out a second time and it took him about four minutes and nobody stopped him, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps nine boxes and has not read his own index back in eleven years has chosen to read a page out loud twice in a week, and that the second time is the harder one, and that the reason it is harder is that the room now knows what the page is for.

It is a clerk's copy of an entry in a public register in a town four hundred miles inland, entered a hundred and forty years before the deed was written and copied about forty years ago off a page nobody in this city can go and look at. Nineteen stones are named on it. Eight of them are different from the nine the deed names, and they are further down the road, and they are in a different order, and the room has known that for a week.

"Eight of the nineteen are further down the road and in a different order and the two documents do not agree and both of them were made to do a different job from the other one, and I said that in this room a week ago and nobody has improved on it and nobody has resolved it, and I am not going to resolve it this morning."

He put his finger on the list and did not touch it.

"**What I want said is that there are four instruments in a book in this room and a clerk's hand a hundred and forty years old on a table in this room, and they have got nothing to do with each other. The reason is that they are five instruments and none of the five has ever had to be read by anybody who did not already know which one they were holding.**"

Nobody in that room said so, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the sixth time in about ten days that a person has said a true thing out loud in that room and had the room not argue with it, and that four of the six have been said by two men with nine boxes and a waxed slate between them, and that neither of them is the man who paid the money and neither of them is going to be asked to move.

---

The second thing that was found in that book on that morning was found by the woman of about thirty-three, who had come in about the ninth hour and had not said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been right about this room for a fortnight without being told has spent a week saying nothing while other people found things and has come in late and found the second one, and that the second one is smaller than the first and about a hundred times more use.

"**The rows at the back of that book are nine years old and they are in an instrument this city stopped writing in years ago, and I cannot read it. I can read the handwriting and I can read every word of what was asked. I cannot tell you what day any of it happened on, and those are the only rows in nine years that have a day in them a person could stand behind.**"

She put her finger on the rows and did not say anything else about them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been asking this room for a fortnight to put a figure under its dates has finally found the one set of rows in nine years of a public register that has a day in it that nobody in the room can stand behind.

Nobody argued with that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a register with three columns and no column for the day a sheet went out cannot tell a question that took four days from one that took nine, and that nine rows at the back of it cannot tell anybody the day at all, and that those are the same fault nine years apart, and that the woman keeping the book found both of them on the same morning and put the second one down in front of about nine people before anybody had asked her for either.

The date on the door was the eighth of that month and it was in the new word and there was no figure under it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a room which has spent a week finding out that its own record cannot be read has got to the end of it, and that what is at the end of it is nine rows a person cannot date and nothing else, and that the one column in that book which works without a date in it is the column for what came back.
