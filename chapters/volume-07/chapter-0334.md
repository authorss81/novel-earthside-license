# Chapter 0334 — A Sheet With A Name At The Top Of It, Refused

A room over a market had the nineteenth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Saturday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in on the north side and was off the low ground by about the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was out of the glass and in the lay of a length of new rope over the back of a chair, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a case that was shut, a length of new rope nobody had used, a copy of four lines in a man's own hand on the back of a week of nothing, and a slate that had been wiped and not written on.

---

Jonah Pike came up the stair at about the seventh hour and did not stop on the landing, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has run a warehouse on a river in another country for nineteen years and has been wrong about one thing in ten for as long as anybody in this city has been able to check came into a room in a flood city and went straight to the part of it he had come about, and that a man who does that has not come to be pleasant with anybody.

He has not sat down in this room before and he sat down now, and he put the copy on the table face up and not face down, which is the opposite of what two men in this city did on the same table four days ago, and a man who puts a copy face up is a man offering it for checking and not offering it as a fact, and that Jonah Pike has never in nineteen years read a page a person had not offered him.

The man of about fifty-seven was at the step at the back with the rope over his arm and had come in late, and a man who lost a season on the ninth of that month and a certainty with it has been in this room four mornings in nine days and has not asked for anything on any of them, and today he had come in late, and that a man who has been in a room four times comes in late on the fifth.

"**You are going to tell him what to write and I am going to say no to it before you do, because I have thought about it for nine days and I know the answer and you have not asked me the question yet.**"

The room did not make a sound and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in this room four mornings and has not asked for anything has now told about nine people in daylight with a date on the door that he knows the answer to a question nobody has put to him, and that this is the third time in about a fortnight that a person in this city has done that, and that all three of them have been in a room with a date on the door and none of the three has been asked to explain it afterwards.

---

What Jonah Pike proposed took him four minutes and it was a good thing and it was correct, and a man who has been wrong about one thing in ten for as long as anybody in this city has been able to check got this one right in four minutes, and what he proposed was that the man of about fifty-seven should write out in his own hand what he had paid for and what he could not show anybody, and put his own name at the top of it, and that the sheet should be the one kind of page in four hundred miles that a person in a house four hundred miles inland can ask to have produced, and that everything in this city that settles anything is that kind of page and nothing else has ever settled anything.

The man of about fifty-seven let him finish and then he took the rope off his arm and put it on the table.

"**No. And you are right about it, and that is the part I am not going to be able to explain to you in a short answer, so I am going to try.**"

He put two fingers on the copy and did not turn it over, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has paid money for half a holding and has never been able to show anybody the half has never in his life had a hand on a page that anybody could take away from him, and that the reason he cannot be got rid of is that the half he paid for is on about nine sheets of paper that all disagree with each other and not one of them has his name at the top of it in his own hand.

"**I have been in a house four hundred miles off twice in about forty years and both times a man brought out a book and neither time did the book say where the line runs, and both times the man with the book was right, and I would not send it, because a page with my name at the top of it is a page somebody can ask for, and I have been a column twice in my life and both times it was somebody else's.**"

What he did not say out loud in that speech is that a page with his name at the top of it is a page somebody can ask for, the day somebody asks for it they will get it, and then there is a column, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the whole of the argument he made in about nine sentences and that it took him about nine days to make it.

The other half of it — a house four hundred miles off, twice in about forty years, a man brought out a book, and the man with the book right both times — is on a page in this city, and it is the only thing anybody in four hundred miles has ever done for him, and he has just refused the only instrument that would have got his winter back and has told the room where that instrument is in the same breath, and about nine people in this room had known since he sat down that he would.

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the only instrument in four hundred miles that has ever settled anything for that man is a page anybody can ask to have produced, and that he has just refused it in a room where he is right, and that nobody in that room is going to be told he is wrong because nobody in that room is wrong, and that a man who refuses the one thing that works for him in a room with a date on the door has understood something about columns that about nine people in this room have been arguing about for a month and arrived at from the other end.

---

That last part is the only thing in this flood that anybody in this city has done that a person in a house four hundred miles inland cannot take away from them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been refused the one page that would have got his winter back has just handed the room the one page that cannot, and that he did it in the same breath and about nine people watched him do it.

Jonah Pike took the copy back and did not fold it, and a man who came into a room with a good proposal and had it refused in four minutes did not argue, and a man who has been wrong about one thing in ten for as long as anybody has been able to check knows which of the ten he is standing on, and that he has not yet found out which one this was and is not going to be told in this room.

"**Then what is left.**"

"**What is left is a thing with a day on it and no name at the top of it, and nobody in four hundred miles can do anything for you with it, and that is the whole of what is left and it is not nothing. Nine households gave their days back out of a door about forty days ago and they have not asked anybody for anything since and they did not write a name at the top of anything,**"

"**And the winter.**"

"**And the winter. And the winter is mine and I have paid for it and I am not going to be argued out of it, and I would like it said in this room that a man who is right about the winter and right about the name has just given up the only instrument that would have got the winter back, and that both of those things happened in about four minutes and in one room and neither of them was arranged.**"

Nobody wrote that down, and a woman in this city has spent a fortnight asking for things to be entered in a room and not in a book, and the man who asked for this one is the only person in about nine days who has wanted a thing put on a page and then been told no by a man who was right, and that the reason nobody wrote it down is not that it does not deserve a page, and not that anybody in that room thought it was settled, and that the reason is that a thing said in a room in daylight with about nine people in it has nowhere to go afterwards, and that this room has known that for a fortnight, and that nobody in it said so.

---

The man of about forty-three had the case at his feet and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers has had that case shut since the day it came up the river and has not opened it, and that it came up before the days went back, and that a man who has shut a thing and is asked nothing about it is a man who has decided the thing in it is not his to open.

"It could be opened."

"**It could be, and it will not be, and you are the one person in this room that is wrong about today and I am not going to be the one who tells you, and about four other people in this room are going to work it out on their own by about the fourth hour of the afternoon.**"

Nobody did and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been right about nine parts in ten of the things in this city for as long as anybody has been able to check was wrong about a shut case on a Saturday morning and nobody in the room told him, and that the man who knew was not going to, and that the reason a room lets a man be wrong in it is that a room which corrects people is a room that has started asking, and this one spent a fortnight deciding not to.

The road north of the Marches was shut and had been shut since the days went back and nobody had shut it, and Jonah Pike said so out loud on a Saturday in a room with a date on the door and did not say who he thought would open it, and a road that has been shut for as long as anybody in that room can count is not a thing anybody in it is going to argue about, and that a man who has been wrong about one thing in ten is wrong about that one most days, and that there is an office nine hundred miles off that has already said in one correct sentence that nothing will be sent west of the Marches gate before the season turns, and that the sentence has cost the Marches its winter road and has cost that office nothing, and that nobody in this room can put the two facts in a better shape than they are already in.

---

The rope went back over the chair back at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the case went out of the room under the arm of the man of about forty-three, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about fifty-seven has carried a length of new rope into about nine rooms in this city since the fifty-second day of that month and has used it in none of them, and that the rope is not for a boundary and it was never for a boundary, and that a man who bought half a holding a season ago has been carrying the means of tying something down ever since and has not been able to find the thing, and that the finding of a man who has refused the one page that would have got his winter back is that he is in a room with nine people who have all watched him refuse it and that not one of them will be able to use it.

Nobody in that room was asked whether any of it was going to work, and the whole of the morning was a page that did not get made and a man who is right about why, and Jonah Pike's queue on the upper road has been running since before the days went back without him on it and is running this morning, and that a man who has been wrong about one thing in ten walked back down to a queue that is not waiting for him and will not be told about the winter by anybody, and that the cost of the morning is one winter, and it is in one man's hand, and it was there before anybody spoke.
