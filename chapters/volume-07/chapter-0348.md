# Chapter 0348 — Four Feet Off, And A Road Nobody Opened

The light came up the shut road from the low ground on the thirty-third day of that month, a Saturday, and it was behind the work the whole morning, and the wind was coming up the same road in pushes that got under a coat and shook the wet out of a length of rope, and the cold was out of the north and out of the wet in eleven willow ends, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart, a coil of rope that had been used and was not new, a mallet made of a hazel with a head bound in cord, and sixteen posts standing in a row in the cart with their tops out.

---

He had the posts cut in a copse about four miles the other way and stacked them in the cart on Thursday, and about four people who had come up that road have worked out since that a man who was told no about a fence across the top of that road in the first week of that month, and who said he would come back, has come back four times, and has spent a day cutting posts for a fence nobody asked for, and that the asking was never the point.

The road is shut. It has been shut since the thirty-ninth day of the Short Month and nobody shut it, and it does not open in this flood, and the bar at the gate of it is in its brackets and the bar has a keeper, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a shut road in this flood is a road that a man walks and a cart does not, and that a man with a handcart and sixteen posts in it is the first thing to come up that road with a load on it since the days went back, and that nobody sent him and that nobody has come to stop him.

He did not start at the stone.

He started at the row of trees at the near end of that ground, because a row of trees is a thing you can see from the road, and he set the first post in the turf of the road side about four feet out from where the grass changed, and about four people who had come up that road have worked out since that a man putting a fence in a field in this flood has to start somewhere he can point at and that the only things he can point at on that road are a row of trees and a stone, and that a fence between those two things is not a boundary and is not a wall and is not a ditch and is not a hedge, and is a fence, and that this is the first time anybody in four hundred miles has said so about one of these.

---

He did not measure anything.

What he did was put the rope out along the ground and walk it and look at it and walk it again and then move the posts, and about four people who had come up that road have worked out since that a man doing that is not measuring and is not pretending to measure, and that he is doing the thing a man does when he has been told there is a thing about four feet away from a place and has not been told what the place is, and that four feet is a number he has in his mouth and not a number he has taken off anything, and that every other man in four hundred miles would have called it four feet too.

The second post went in. The third one had to come up and go back down about two inches because the ground was soft on that side, and the fourth one he put in with the mallet and the mallet is a hazel with a head bound in cord and it is not a tool anybody in this city would buy and it is the only one he owns.

Nobody stopped him.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was on the road at about the eighth hour with nothing in his hands, and the man of about fifty-seven came up the road behind him at about the ninth hour in a coat he had not worn since the ninth of that month, and a woman of about thirty-three who keeps a room over a shop on the low ground came up behind them with a basket on her arm and did not stop, and two people out of the second house on that lane came out and stood at the gate, and about four people on that road have worked out since that this is the largest number of people who have stood on that road together in about a month and that every one of them came to it on their own and that not one of them was sent and that not one of them was asked anything.

---

The man of about fifty-seven stood where the posts were going in and did not move out of the way for a long time, and then he moved.

He has a deed of about forty years ago with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and the sheet is a copy of an entry in a public register in a town four hundred miles inland, in a clerk's hand, entered a hundred and forty years ago, and the stone at the other end of that ground has a sheet on the back of it, and the two of them do not agree, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who paid money for half a holding is standing in the middle of a fence being put up four feet off a line he cannot show anybody, and that he is the only man in four hundred miles who has standing to stop it and that he has not said a word and is not going to.

"**I could stop it.**"

"**You could stop it.**"

"That is not the same as you being able to tell me where it is."

"**That is not the same as me being able to tell you where it is either, and I have been about four feet off it since the fourth post, and you have been standing where the posts are going in for about a quarter of an hour.**"

---

By the middle of the day there were nine posts in the ground along about half of that road and the rope was on the cart and the handcart had come to a stop because a handcart does not go forward and back nine times, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a fence is the most ordinary thing in the world and that it is also the most permanent thing in the world, and that the two of those are the same fact, and that a man who has put a fence four feet off a line nobody has measured has put up the most ordinary object in four hundred miles at the exact place where it can do the most harm and where nobody will be able to take it back.


The man of about thirty-three who keeps a room over a shop on the low ground had been standing at the third post since the eighth hour with her basket on the wall and had not said anything, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a woman who keeps a room in this city has watched a fence go up and has not been asked once, and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever had to be told what a room looks like when it is watching.

"You have both said the same sentence twice and neither of you has said it to the other one once."

"I have said it to the fence," said the man of about thirty-three, "and he has said it to you, and about four people on this road have worked out that those are the two ordinary ways of saying a thing that is going to outlast you, and I have been in nine rooms in a fortnight and nobody in any of them has said a sentence twice."

"Neither of us is going to be thanked for it either," said the man of about fifty-seven.

"Nobody in four hundred miles is going to thank either of you and neither of you wants it and that is the third ordinary thing about this morning."

The woman of about thirty-three picked her basket up off the wall and set it on her arm and stayed where she was, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a woman who keeps a room in this city has now heard a sentence said four times by two men on a shut road and has not said one word of her own, and that this is the second time in about a fortnight that the not-speaking has been the whole of what a person did in a room or on a road.

"It will be there when neither of us is."

"**It will be there when neither of us is, and that is not a figure of days, and I have not got one and I am not going to be given one, and there is no form in four hundred miles that would take one if I had it.**"

"You do not know where it is."

"**I know where the row of trees is and I know where the stone is and I have put nine posts between them, and a fence is not an account of anything and it does not know what it is four feet off, and that is why it is going to be there when neither of us is, and it is going to be there because neither of us ever agreed where the line was, and the fence does not care.**"

---

Nobody in four hundred miles is going to know what that fence is four feet off.

That is the finding of the Saturday and about four people on that road have worked out since that the man of about fifty-seven is right, and that being right is the whole of what a fence does to a boundary, and that on Thursday a page in a book in this city was ruled and a straight mark in a field was written against it as a thing that may not be dug, and that on Saturday a man put nine posts up four feet off it on the strength of nothing, and that nobody in four hundred miles will ever be able to produce the four feet.

The nineteen stones are a stone in a wall four hundred miles inland and a clerk's hand a hundred and forty years old, and about four people in this city have worked out since that those are not this and cannot be joined to it, and that a woman of about thirty-three carried the order of them in on the seventeenth of that month in a room with a date on the door and has been out and looked at them more than once since, and that a man who goes up ladders for a living says he can tell that eight of the nineteen are a different stone off a floor without seeing them and is not going to guess at it in a room, and that the other eleven are the same stone, and that neither of those things has anything to do with nine willow posts in the turf of a shut road, and that the man of about thirty-four who trades on a board is not this either and has not been here.

The handcart turned round at the end of the road at about the fourth hour with seven posts still in it and nine in the ground, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has come to put up a fence and has put nine posts up and has seven left has not finished and is not coming back the same day, and that he will come back, and that nobody has sent for him and nobody will, and that the nine posts in the ground are four feet off a line that nobody has measured on a road that nobody has opened, and that on the twenty-ninth day of that month a man who digs stood at the end of that line and looked along it and said one sentence that was not an answer, and that the fence is not the answer and does not know there was a sentence.
