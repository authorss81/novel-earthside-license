# Chapter 0310 — A Word, In A Room, In Daylight, With A Date On The Door

A man of about thirty-four who kept a stall and wrote the ninth line on a wall nine miles up the road in a harder piece of chalk was asked a question in a room over a market on the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month, a Wednesday, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and stopped about the middle of the floor and never got to the far wall, and the cold was out of the glass and off the flagstones and in the joints of the boards, and what was in his hands all day was a piece of chalk that had been lying on a table since the forty-third day of that month and that he had put there himself and had not touched.

---

He had put the chalk on that table on the forty-third day of that month, in a room, in daylight, in front of about nine people, after he had asked in advance not to be thanked and had not been thanked, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who puts a piece of chalk down where everybody can see it and then does not use it for twelve days has bought a thing, and that what he has bought is the right to pick it up, and that the price of it was twelve days of not picking it up, and that nobody in that room has worked out what he has bought it for, including him.

The question was put at about the seventh hour and it was put carefully and it was not a speech.

The room had the second slate on the table and a deed with a sheet pinned to the corner of it and about nine people in it, and six of those nine were leaning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a person leans before a question is finished and straightens afterwards, and that in that room on that morning six people leaned and one man did not move at all, and that the man who did not move had not moved since the fourth hour of the afternoon on the forty-second day of that month, which is the only posture anybody has recorded him in.

He had been told the figures by a man with a barrow and would not say so and was not asked, and the figures were about a foot wide and about two inches deep, and they were about nine hundred paces off a shut gate on a road that had been shut since the thirty-ninth of that month, and nobody in that room had been near it and nobody in that room was going to be.

---

The man of about thirty-four had been in that room since the seventh hour and had not sat down and had not spoken.

A man who keeps a stall in a market spends his working day talking to about ninety people about nothing, and a morning in which he has not spoken once is a morning a person can tell you about afterwards, and he was now on the fourth hour of one.

"Say the word."

"The word for a mark of about a foot and about two inches is a word I have and you do not, and I have not said it in a room before, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to stop, and I would like it noticed that I have not made a speech."

Nobody in that room had put anything on the table all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in which a man is about to name a thing and nobody has a piece of paper on the table is a room that has agreed in advance not to make it a page, and that the agreement was made by about nine people in about four seconds each.

"You are going to say one word and about nine people are going to want the rest of the sentence."

"**Then the about nine people can want it.**"

"**Scar.**"

He said it out loud in the local language, in daylight, in a room with a date in chalk on the door, in one word, and the room did it, and about nine people in that room have worked out since that a man who has refused five times to say whether a line on a wall works has said a word out loud in about a second, and that the two of those are the same man, and that nobody in that room has worked out how to hold both of them at once and about four of them have stopped trying.

He put the chalk on the table then and pushed it about a foot towards the second slate and did not let go of it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her hand flat on the other side of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is a man passing a piece of chalk to a woman over a table, and that the chalk has been lying on that table since the forty-third day of that month, and that nobody in the room said a word about it happening.

"It is not a path. It is not a watercourse. It is not a furrow. It is not a boundary, and I am not going to tell you what it is, because the mark is not a pattern and I am not going to stand in a room and say what made it."

"That is four sentences and you have not answered the question we have been asking since Sunday."

"You have been asking since Sunday about a boundary and you have not asked me about a boundary, and I have answered the question you asked me." He did not move the chalk. "**And the reason I am not going to say anything else about it is that a word on a page is a thing I have found out what it is for in about the last fifty days, and I am not going to find out twice.**"

---

Nobody in that room asked him about the ninth line, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is now the sixth room he has been in this month and the sixth room nobody has asked him in, and that he has been asked five times in about four hundred days and the five were in kitchens and yards and not in rooms with a date on the door, and that a person is asked about a thing in a kitchen and not about a thing in a room, and that he has never worked out why and has stopped trying.

"What is on the line."

"Nothing is on the line but the word and the day, and the day goes beside it, and I want it that way and I have told the woman what is keeping the slate why I want it that way, and the reason is that a line with a name at the top of it is a line somebody can come back to, and I am not going to be a reason anybody comes back."

The register-keeper did not say anything about that.

A man who gives a reason for a thing being left off a page understands pages better than anybody in that room, and he has never once been asked to explain it, and he has explained it anyway, twice, for nothing.

"Say it again so that I can get it right."

"I am not going to say it again."

"Then I shall get it wrong."

"**You will not get it wrong, and I am not going to say it a second time, and I would like it noticed that there is a piece of chalk on this table and that it has been on it since the forty-third day of that month and that it is mine and that I have not used it until this morning.**"

---

She got it right.

That is the whole of what is on a page about it and the chapter does not say how and is not going to.

A woman who keeps a public register for a living has written out about four hundred things in nine years in about nine different hands, and a man will not say a word twice, and both of those facts are on one slate on that afternoon and only one of them is the reason she got it right. The other one is that she has been listening to men not say things for nine years, and that is the only qualification anybody in this city has for a job like that.

The word went on the second slate in chalk. The date went beside it, in the form there is, which is the fifty-fifth day of that month. Nothing went under the date, and no name went on the line, and the chalk went back into his pocket and the pocket was the inside breast one.

A man puts a thing in the breast pocket rather than the coat pocket when he intends to have it on him in about a month, and the word had been in the breast pocket for about a second.

The second slate went on its shelf at about the fourth hour, and the room went on doing what it does, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that there is now a word in this city for a thing about nine hundred paces off a shut gate on a road that has been shut since the thirty-ninth of that month, and that a word in a city is the cheapest thing anybody has ever bought, and that the man who bought it does not know what he sold.

---

He was frightened, and nobody in that room could tell, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the only sign of it was that he put his hand flat on the table where the chalk had been and left it there for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and that a man who has been still for an hour and a half in the middle of a room where a page was read is not being calm, he is being careful, and that the two have been the same thing since the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and nobody has ever been able to tell them apart.

Then he said it, to the table, at the end, in his own words, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four of them understood it.

"**I have been the only man in this city who has written a word on a wall on purpose, in a harder piece of chalk, so that the word would be the last thing anybody could rub out. I did that on the forty-sixth day of the Long Month and I have not been thanked for it and I have not asked to be.**"

He put the chalk back in the inside breast pocket and buttoned the pocket.

"**And I have found out today that there is a way to rub a word out that has nothing to do with a cloth or a wall, and it is to say it once in a room with a date on the door. I have done it about nine times in my own head this afternoon and nobody in this room can see me doing it.**"
