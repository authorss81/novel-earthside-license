# Chapter 0325 — Nobody Goes, And A Yard Eleven Miles The Other Way

A room over a market had the tenth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Thursday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in early over a step at the back with no name on it and was on the table by the seventh hour, and the cold was out of the glass and in a piece of chalk carried in a fold and in the tarred cord round a man's neck, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet with about nine places on it, a length of new rope, a second slate belonging to nobody in the room, and a case on a table with a deed in it and a sheet pinned to the corner of the deed.

---

Nobody had come to the room to be asked anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the first morning in about a fortnight on which nobody in a room over a market came in order to be asked a question, and that the reason is the six sentences of the day before, and that a room which has made a decision nobody argued with wakes up the next morning with nothing to do and about nine people in it.

Tamsin Quill had the folded sheet on the table with the fold showing and had not opened it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the ninth line of that sheet is going to stay empty and that the woman who wrote the rule about empty lines a week ago has not touched it since, and that a rule a person has made about her own work is the only kind of rule in this flood that holds.

"So nobody goes."

"Nobody goes."

"**Then I would like it said that nobody going is a decision and not a stopping, and that it is the first decision anybody in this room has made in about a month that anybody can write down, and I am not going to be the one who writes it down, and I am telling the room that now so that nobody is surprised on Tuesday, when the first of the four rooms is.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-three had the nineteen stones in her head where they have been since the forty-eighth of the Short Month and she asked the question that had been sitting under every other question in that room for about a fortnight, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been right about this room twice without being told has now asked it a third time and that the third time is the one that costs.

"Then what is the room for."

"It is for the four days and it is for what is here."

"**Those are two different things and about four of us have been treating them as one for a month and you have just separated them in a sentence.**"

Nobody argued, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the separation of those two things is the whole of what the six sentences of the day before did, and that a room which has found out it cannot fetch a page has got four days in front of it instead, and that four days is an instrument and not a courtesy and everybody in that room knows it is because a man said so in it a fortnight ago and nobody has improved on the saying of it.

---

The instrument was named before the middle of the morning and it was named by the woman who had the sheet of road, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she had it in her coat on the ninth of that month and has not put it on a table since, and that the reason she has not put it on a table is that a sheet with nine places on it and eight answers on it is a thing a room can ask to have produced, and that she has been protecting it from this room for a day and a half without saying so.

"Four sheets. One for each of the four, in the words of each of the four, in writing, and every one of them unsigned with nothing at the top of it, and one of them every day for four days, and the first one goes out in the morning of the eleventh of that month and the room it is for is on the fifteenth."

"Who writes them."

"**They do. That is the entire point of them and I am not going to explain it twice. A question in your own words is worth more than a question in anybody else's and the reason is nine years old and it is the carrier's and not the asker's, and a sheet with my hand on the top of it asking a person to do a thing is a piece of paper, and a sheet with their own hand on it is not.**"

Nobody improved on that and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman has said out loud, in daylight, with a date on the door, that the reason she wants a sheet with nothing at the top of it is not a principle and is about a person, and that the person it is about is not in the room.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was standing by the step at the back with the rope over his arm and had come in late, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who lost a season in this room on the day before came back to it on purpose on the morning after, and that nobody in this room has ever known him to come back to a room twice and that this is the second time.

He said one thing and it was a question and nobody in that room expected a question from him.

"Is there a day on them."

"**There is a day on the room and there is no name at the top of any of them and there is nothing at the top of any of them. That is four days, and that is the whole of the notice, and if it were three days it would not be a notice, it would be a delivery.**"

He looked at the door for a while and did not say anything else, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has paid money for half a holding has just been told, in a room, by a person, that the instrument this city has left is four days long and that a person who is asked to be somewhere in four days' time is a person who has been asked, and that he did not know that and about four people in this room have known it for a fortnight.

"Nobody is going to tell me it is the right thing to do."

"**Nobody in this room is going to tell you that and nobody is going to tell you the other, and I would like that entered, and I would like it entered in a room and not in a book, because a book is where a thing like that goes to be true for somebody who was not in the room.**"

---

The room carried on and about four people in that room have worked out since that a decision nobody argued with is a decision about four days, and that four days in a room over a market is four notices, and that a notice is a sheet in a person's own words with nothing at the top of it, and that a notice goes to a place and not to a house, and that a place with eight houses in it is a place and a house at the end of a lane past two gates is only a house, and that about four people in that room have worked out all of that in about four minutes without being told any of it, which is the ordinary way this city works and is not an agreement and has never been one.

Nobody was asked whether it would go. That was said out loud in that room in about nine words at about the fourth hour by the woman who had the sheet of road, and nobody improved on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a decision about four hundred miles was carried all the way to the end of a morning and nobody at any point in it thought to put the four to the four, and that the not-putting was the second half of the six sentences of the day before and about four people in this room heard the first half of it and about four of them did not.

---

The wind on eleven miles of flats came up over the racks at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and had been at them since the light and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man in the third row with a barrow at the fourth hour is doing the same nine things he was doing at the fifth hour of the morning and that the number has not changed and will not change before the dark.

The man of about fifty-two who digs had nine willow in and a bundle on the barrow and about a hundred yards of row left, and the door of the hut was open behind him, and the rod was where it had been on the Sunday, on its end in the corner against the thick wall, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a hut on the Reach has a door that has never shut in nine years, and that a woman has come out of it in the morning and stood about nine feet off this row and looked at the ground in it, and that she has not asked him anything and is not going to.

Adrian came out along the head of the row at about the fourth hour and stopped about nine feet off and stood there, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms this month walked eleven miles to stand nine feet off a man with a spade and say nothing to him, and that the nine feet is the distance at which a man working does not have to stop.

He did not say anything for a while and the man of about fifty-two did not say anything either, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that two men who have never spoken can stand in the same wind for the length of about nine spadefuls without either of them owing the other a sentence, and that this is the oldest arrangement in this flood and it is the one that has not been improved on by anybody.

The man of about fifty-two went on digging and did not look at him and did not ask him what he wanted, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has not been asked anything in this flood has not been asked anything this morning either, and that the not-asking was not arranged by the man who came out and that he is the only man in four hundred miles who could have arranged it.

Adrian worked it out. That is the whole of what he did for the rest of the afternoon and he did it standing in the wind on eleven miles of flats about nine feet off a man with a spade, and he did not say it out loud, and he did not say it to anybody afterwards either, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the plan he put on a table on the ninth of that month was correct about the facts, and that the woman who led the decision on that morning was right, and that the man on the flats has not been asked and was not asked and told him nothing, and that none of those three things is the same thing as the fourth one, which is that a man standing in the wind for the length of an afternoon can come to a different answer than the man he was standing next to, and can arrive at it, and can keep it.

He went back along the head of the row at the ninth hour and the wind was still up and the man of about fifty-two was still in the third row, and the light went off the ground at the far end of the row about a quarter of an hour before it went off the near end, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the whole of what this city got out of eleven miles of flats on the tenth day of a month with a name on it and no figure under the name was the distance between two men, and that the distance has not changed, and that a man who has stood still for an afternoon notices which end of a row the light leaves first, and a man who has been digging for a day does not.
