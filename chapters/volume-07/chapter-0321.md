# Chapter 0321 — A Rod He Does Not Carry

The light on eleven miles of flats came up flat out of the east with nothing in front of it to stop it and lay itself along the ground like water off a floor, and the cold was out of the north across about eleven miles of open ground and not off a shoulder of anything, and the wind came up behind the light and had not stopped finding the gaps between the racks, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a spade with the top of its handle worn shiny in one place, a barrow whose wheel had come off twice, and a bundle of willow about two feet long.

---

The man of about fifty-two who digs was in the third row at about the fifth hour, and he was not digging because the ground was easy. The ground under a rack's leg goes hard as a road in two seasons and he had been going round eleven miles of that since the water came, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who puts things in holes for a living does not choose the fifth hour and does not need to, because the whole of his trade is that a hole is easier to argue with at the fifth hour than at any other.

He set the willow in and tamped it with the heel of his boot and went back for the next one, and the whole of the method is about nine seconds and he had been doing it since before the light.

Nobody came out to the flats that morning and nobody had sent anybody, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that this is the second time in this flood that a man eleven miles the other way has been at work and has not been asked anything. On the first occasion he was named in a room as a man who would have refused in the time it takes to shut a gate and would have been right. Nobody has improved on that sentence since and nobody has needed to, because it is still true and it is still the cheapest thing anybody knows about him.

---

There was a hut at the head of the third row with a stovepipe and a door that does not shut, and inside it against the north wall, standing up on its end in the corner where the wall is thickest, there was a rod.

It is a length of ash about four feet long with a flat blade on the end of it, ground down on one side and not the other, and it is the only one in four hundred miles and it is the reason a man who digs is a man who digs, and it has been standing in that corner since before the water and it is not leaning and it is not in a bag and it is not under a bench.

He did not carry it out that morning. He did not carry it out on any morning. About four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a rod is a thing you carry when you are going somewhere to find something. Putting things in holes is the trade and finding things is not the trade, and it takes a different kind of morning, and a man of fifty-two who has spent his life at the first thing has no reason in the world to pick up the second one. If he did pick it up he would be a different man by the end of the road, and everybody who has ever carried a rod knows that and nobody else does.

He went out at the fifth hour and left it standing where it was and put the door to behind him with the heel of his hand because the door has never shut properly in nine years and everybody on those flats knows that about the door.

---

A room over a market had the sixth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Sunday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went past the middle of the floor because the chalk on that door had been in the new word for six days and a room gets used to its own light faster than a person does, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a second slate turned face down, a box with a deed in it and a sheet pinned to the corner of the deed, a register with nine years in it, and a book on a shelf that had not been off the shelf in eleven years.

The man of about forty-three who keeps other people's papers had the book open in front of him and his hand flat on the page and had not turned it.

"**I am not going to say his name until I have read it off a page, and I am not going to read it off a page in a hurry, and I would like it understood that I have not read this book back in eleven years and I am reading it now because there is a reason and the reason arrived on Saturday.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man at the end of nine boxes and eleven years has had a reason to open his own index for the first time, and that a man who is willing to say so in a room with a date on the door has already told the room the worst thing about himself and has not been asked for it.

The room had come to the question at the seventh hour and had been at it for about an hour, and the question was not a question anybody could have drawn on a slate.

There is a straight mark in the ground under an orchard about nine hundred paces off a shut gate, about a foot wide and about two inches deep, running from the near end of a row of trees to a stone nobody put there. Nobody in this city knows where it runs. The only page in four hundred miles that says where it runs is a copy of an entry made in a register in a town four hundred miles inland, entered a hundred and forty years ago, and nobody in this city can go and look at the page it was copied from. The mark has not been measured and it has not been dug and nobody in four hundred miles has ever put a rod in it, and there is a word for it on a slate in that room, put there in chalk, and nobody in the room is going to say the word today.

What a room can do with that is decide who would be the person to go and look, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has spent a month arguing about two wrong documents has at last found the one thing it was looking for, and that the one thing is a man.

---

A man who had come in late and had been standing at the back of the room read the clerk's hand on the sheet pinned to the corner of the deed over the man of about forty-three's shoulder, both columns, from the top down, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said Adrian and then said nothing else about him for the rest of the morning, and nobody in that room asked him one question about the boundary.

He said one thing, and it was about the form, and it was the only thing he said in the room.

"**You have all been treating that sheet as the only account of where the line runs, and it is not, and I have known it since the fifth of that month and I have not said it because I could not make it into an argument in a room that has not got a day.**"

He put his finger about a finger's width above the last line of the list on the pinned sheet and did not touch it, the way a man points at a line of a clerk's writing a hundred and forty years old without being asked to.

"**A clerk wrote what the page in front of him said, and there is a woman in this city who has a piece of paper thirty years old that says where the water went in her own ground, and the two of them are not in the same shape and neither of them is wrong, and the only person in four hundred miles who could put the two in the same shape is a man who spends his life at the shape of the ground.**"

Nobody improved on that either, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who has been standing at the back of rooms for a month reading things nobody else can read has finally said a thing that is not about a document, and that he said it in about nine words and then stopped, and that a woman of sixty-nine is not in that room and her paper is on a table in it and she has not been asked about any of it.

---

The man of about forty-three turned the page. He read three lines and stopped with his thumb on the third, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who cannot make a decision about anything on a Sunday has just made one in the time it takes a room to stop talking. The thing he decided was to read out loud, and it cost him a page in a book he has not opened in eleven years.

"**Nev Cutler.** Fifty-two, and it says fifty-two in the hand, and the hand is his own and not a clerk's, and the place it gives is the flats and not a house, and the trade it gives is digging, which is the word this room has been using for a man with a spade for a fortnight and is the wrong word and is the only one there is."

He said the name once and put his finger on the line and did not say it again, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the name of the only man in four hundred miles anybody could send for has been on a page in daylight with a date on the door for the length of four seconds, and that it is the shortest thing anybody in this flood has ever put on a page. Four of the nine heard it and about four of them wrote it down in their own heads, and none of them put it on anything.

"**He has not been sent for. He has not been asked. He has not had anybody at that gate say a word to him since before the water, and there is a woman at that gate who has not been asked anything by anybody in nine years and the two of them have never met and neither of them knows the other's trade.**"

That is the reason, and it was established in a room in daylight with a date on the door, and it is not the same reason twice, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a room which has spent a month finding out that it has no instrument has at last got one, and that what it has got is a man who has not been asked anything, and that the two facts are the same fact and were always going to be.

---

The register-keeper turned the second slate over, wrote the sixth of that month on the face of it in chalk, and put nothing under the day, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she did it with her back to a man who has been at the back of that room for a month, and that the person she has not given the name to is the one person in the room it was most worth giving it to, and that she gave it to the man with the book instead because the man with the book could check it.

She said the reason out loud, which is what she does, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the woman who keeps a public register has said out loud in daylight, with a date on the door, that a name is worth a person when it can be checked and worth nothing at all when it cannot, and that she has not put that sentence in her book, and that there is no column in a nine-year register for a name either.

"He will not be sent for. **I am going to say that out loud in this room with a date on the door because nobody in this city has ever written down a day on which somebody was not sent for, and the day it is on is a day, and the register has a column for days, and this is the first thing in nine years that I know the answer to before anybody asks me.**"

Nobody argued with her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the fourth time in about a week that a person has named a thing out loud in that room and had the room not argue with it, the thing was an act and not an opinion, and that a room which does not argue has agreed the thing is right and has not agreed to do it.

Nobody was sent. The man of about forty-three shut the book and put it back on the shelf and did not say when he would open it again.

Adrian was still standing at the back of the room with his hands in his coat and had not been asked one question by anybody in it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just told the only room in this city where a name could be checked that there is a man worth sending for has not been asked what he thinks should happen next.

---

Eleven miles the other way the wind had gone round to the west by the fourth hour and the man of about fifty-two who digs was still in the third row with about nine willow in and a bundle on the barrow and a spade that had come shiny through the top of its own handle. He had not stopped. Nobody had come out and nobody had sent anybody, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man with a barrow at the fourth hour is a man who will be at it at the ninth and is not a man anybody has to say anything to.

There was a rod standing up against the north wall of a hut with a door that does not shut, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the whole of what has happened to that man in this flood is that somebody in a room eleven miles off has said his name out loud once.
