# Chapter 0330 — A Room She Was Written Into

A room over a shop on the low ground had the fifteenth of that month written in chalk on its door, a Tuesday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in at about the seventh hour and was across the floor before anybody came up the stair, and the cold was out of the plaster and in the nib of a pen that had been cut down with a knife, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a pen with a nib ground narrow, about nine lines of pencil on a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it, a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it, and a piece of chalk with the room-keeper's thumbprint still in the dust of it.

---

The date on that door was the fifteenth of that month and it was put there at about the sixth hour by a woman of about thirty-three who keeps a room over a shop and has a bread board on the sill of the window in it, and about four people on that row have worked out since that a woman who has a bread board and a room with a window that shuts has put a date in chalk on her own door for the first time in nine years, and that nobody has asked her to and that nobody is going to.

Nobody in four hundred miles had put a date on that door before. There is no column of them going up the frame and there is nothing rubbed out and there is no second one underneath, and about four people on that row have worked out since that a door with one date on it and nothing above it is not a record, it is a notice, and that a woman has decided to put a notice on her own door and has not decided to be the woman who keeps a record.

"There is a sheet on eight doors on this row and a room on the fifteenth of that month in it, and I have put the day on my door because I am not having a person walk up my stair at the fourth hour asking me what day it is."

---

She came in at about the seventh hour and did not stop at the door, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one who has walked into a room where a sheet with her own handwriting on it is lying on a table does not stop, and that the not-stopping is the only thing in the room that was not arranged.

She had a pen with her. It is a pen with the nib ground narrow, cut down with a knife, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who was given a pen that was too broad for her and cut it down herself has decided something about being given things, and that she has not said what it is and that it is not a small thing and that it is not a large one.

"What am I for."

Nobody answered her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the first thing a person says in a room where a sheet with her own hand on it is on the table is a question about what she is doing there, and that she asked it before she sat down and before she had looked at about six of the nine people in the room, and that a person who asks that in a room with a date on the door is a person who has never been in one.

---

"There are four of you."

"There are four of us."

"And I have got a sheet."

"**You have got a sheet because you wrote one, and there are four of them and about four of us are in this room, and the other three are not in this room and nobody is going to tell you where they are.**"

Nobody improved on that and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has a sheet of road in her coat has just told a person of about twenty-one that the other three exist and have not said a word about what the four of them are for, and that a room which has a date on the door and about nine people in it has been sitting on the difference between a person and a purpose for a fortnight and has got it out of the room in one sentence.

The woman of about thirty-three put the bread board down at the end of the sill and said she was going to leave the room for a bit because there were nine people in it and a shop, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a room has decided that a thing which is about to be said is not hers to hear, and that she was not asked to leave and that about four people wanted her to stay and did not say so.

---

"Yes."

It came out before anybody had finished the question she had not been asked, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one said one word in a room with a date on the door and that nobody had asked her anything, and that the word was not an answer to a question and was an answer to a sheet, and that those are different things and that the second one is worth more.

"I will come on the eighteenth as well."

"**You will not.** Nobody in this room is going to ask you what the eighteenth is for and nobody is going to tell you either, and one of the four of you said yesterday, in a room over a market, that she is not coming to it, and I am telling you that because you are about twenty-one and I am not going to let you promise yourself a winter in somebody's good coat and find that out on a doorstep."

Nobody said anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has walked nine places in three days knows that a person of about twenty-one who has just said yes to four hundred miles has not thought about the second half of it, and that the naming of the other three of them is not a favour and cost her something to do in a room with about nine people in it.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the back of the room with his hands in his pockets and had not said anything all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said out loud in a room over a market that a hand is not a page and that a sheet with somebody's own writing on it cannot be argued with is standing in a room where a person has just agreed to be one of four and is not going to be told what the four is.

"You are not going to be asked whether it will go."

He said it to the room and not to her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries sheets for a living has just given a room the rule it arrived at by itself a fortnight ago and has given it out loud and on time, and that a rule said out loud on time in a room with a date on the door is worth nine rules written down after it.

Nobody asked her. About four people in that room have worked out since that there are nine people in it and that a woman of about twenty-one came up a stair to a room with a sheet in her own hand on a table, and that not one of the nine asked her whether it would go, and that a man of about fifty-two was named out loud in a room eleven miles off on the sixth of that month and has not been asked anything since that morning, and that he is on a different road and this room is not confusing the two.

---

She picked the sheet up off the table and put it flat and looked at her own handwriting on it for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a person who has not read their own hand for a fortnight and is reading it now in a room with about nine people in it is checking two things and one of them is whether it is still true and the other one is whether it is still hers.

"There is no name at the top of it."

"**There is no name at the top of it and there never was, and that is not a courtesy and it is not a protection. If it goes down this stair then about nine people can say whose it was, and if it goes down the stair of this building then nobody in this city can. You have just worked out which of those two things you are in, and I would like you to have worked it out before you came up, and not after.**"

She had the cut-down pen in her hand and did not write anything with it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one who cut a pen down herself so that it would make the mark she wanted has brought it into a room and not used it, and that there is nothing on that table for her to write on, and that the only sheet with anything on it is her own and it has nothing at the top of it and that is the correct shape and she does not yet know that it is.

The date on the door was the fifteenth of that month in chalk with a thumbprint in the dust of it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the first of the four people who came to this city with a question and have not left is in a room she was written into four days before it happened, with no name at the top of the sheet that put her there, and that nobody in that room asked her whether it would go, and that she does not know what she is for, and that both of those are going to be true on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the eighteenth, and that the instrument is four days long and there is nothing in this city that can be longer.
