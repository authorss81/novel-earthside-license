# Chapter 0304 — The Thursday, And The Boards That Do Not Go

A barrow of boards was standing against the wall of a shut gate on the forty-ninth day of the Short Month, a Thursday, and the light came in on the north side of that lane and stopped at the boards and did not get to the gate, and the cold was out of the wind and in the shutters of the near house, and what was in that lane under about four pairs of hands that morning was a barrow, boards, a length of new rope hooked over the barrow handle that was not his, a bar of ash in two brackets, and a wooden hook over its handle.

---

He came at the sixth hour and he was late by five days and he was exactly on the day, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man can be five days late and exactly on the day at the same time and that this is not a contradiction, it is a man who arranged a Thursday, did not come for five days, and turned up on the Thursday because the Thursday is the Thursday.

The boards were for a house on this side of the hill and had been against that wall since the forty-first day of that month counting the day they were put there as one of the days, and the wall they were against belonged to a gate that had been shut since the thirty-ninth, and a rope about as long as a man's arm was hooked over the barrow handle and it was not his and there was no fence on that road and nobody had been to take it.

He put his hand on the boards.

He did not count them. He has been carrying things since he was about eleven and he does not count boards, and about four people who have watched him do it have said since that a man who carries a thing every day for twenty years knows what it weighs without counting it and that this is the only trade anybody in this flood has where the man is better than the page, and that a man who is better than the page at his trade is the last kind of person anybody can put in a column.

**"I am not going to take them up."**

The woman of about fifty had the bar of ash in her hands and she did not turn round.

"You arranged it for this day."

"I arranged it for this day and I am not going to take them up and I would like it said at this gate that I turned up, because in about nine days there is going to be a person standing on this road who is going to say that nobody came, and that is not going to be true and I would like one sentence of it to be true." He took his hand off the boards. "**A barrow of boards standing against a wall at a shut gate is the only thing on that road. If it goes there is nothing on that road at all. And a man with a barrow who takes his own boards home on a Thursday is a man who cannot say afterwards that he had been there.**"

"You have had five days."

"I have had five days and I have used none of them, and I would like it noticed that a man who has used none of them is not the same as a man who has not had them, and that I have not been up this road since the forty-fourth and there is a woman at this gate who will tell you that I was at it on the forty-fourth and I was, and I have been down that lane every day since."

---

She worked the hook down and the gate came four inches and she put her hand flat on the post and found the draught and put the bar back in and it stopped, and the whole of that took about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and a keeper who does a gate while a man is standing at her wall deciding not to work has decided that the gate is the work and that a conversation at a wall is something that happens to the wall.

The boards settled about a half inch in the wet where they had been five days against a wall and came back up again as the wind went round the corner, which is a thing that happens to a barrow load that has stood a week, and is one of the reasons a man who has been late is not necessarily lazy.

"**You have come five days late to take your own boards up and I would like it said at this gate that I do not think that is the whole of it.**" She did not stop working. "A man who is five days late and exactly on the day has been somewhere else doing a thing he has not told anybody about, including me, and I am not going to ask him what it was."

"I am not going to say that I did not want to, because that is the sort of thing people say in a lane and then go on saying. I am going to have not asked him, and that is all of it."

About four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who works a gate through a conversation and answers one line of it and not the rest has decided the gate is the reply and the rest is a thing she is doing while she waits.

"I did not come here to be asked."

"You came here to put a hand on a barrow of boards at a shut gate on a Thursday and I have watched you do it, and that is a thing a man does when he has come up a road to be somewhere, and I have never once known a man come up this road to be somewhere." She put the bar back in. "**You are not going to be thanked and I am not going to thank you and I would like it understood that there is nothing here to thank anybody for, because a barrow of boards is not a favour and a man standing at a wall deciding something is not a gift.**"

"**Do not thank me. I am going to say that in advance, in front of nobody, and it is going to be heard by about four people on that hill and none of them are going to be able to say they heard it.**"

---

He said it about as loud as a man says a thing to a bar of ash, and it was heard, and nobody thanked him, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who asks in advance not to be thanked has asked for the one thing in this flood that has never once been given, and that he is the second person in about a month who has asked for it, and that both of them asked for it in a room where nobody could hear them and neither of them was thanked and one of them is nine miles up the upper road.

He looked at the rope on the handle for a while and did not touch it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man carrying things for twenty years can tell what rope is by the lay of it in about four seconds, and that this one is new and it is somebody else's and it was hooked on that handle by somebody who expected to be back, and that the man of about sixty with a handcart and a length of rope was told no at this gate on the forty-first day of that month and said he would come back in about nine days, and that about nine days from the forty-fifth day of that month is the fifty-fourth, and that the man of about thirty-eight knows that and has not counted it out loud and does not intend to.

"I am not going to move your rope either."

"It is not my rope and it is not yours either. Put it back the way you found it and do not improve on it."

He put it back the way he had found it and it took him longer than moving a barrow of boards takes, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who takes longer over a rope that is not his than over a barrow of his own has understood something about that lane that nobody has asked him about and that he is not going to be asked about.

---

He put his hand on the barrow again and did not turn it round, and the light had gone off the floor of that lane by about the middle of the afternoon and the cold came up off the boards and out of the wall, and the woman of about fifty went on doing the gate at the same rate she had been doing it at since the seventh hour, and the gate came four inches and stopped and came four inches and stopped, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that she has been doing that all day in front of a man who has not moved, and that a woman who works a gate for eight hours in front of a person is saying something with the gate.

Then he turned round and went down the lane, and he did not go up the road, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man with a barrow who turns round at a shut gate and goes down a lane has not decided anything about the road and has decided something about the barrow, and that the barrow was still loaded when he left and the boards were still against the wall and nothing in that lane was changed by anybody except the fact that it was now known.

"**He will be far enough down that lane that he cannot hear me, and I am saying it to the post, and I am right, and I am not going to ask him, and I have never asked anybody anything and I am not going to start with a man who is carrying my gate for me.**"

**"And that barrow is not going anywhere this week, and the man of about sixty with the rope is going to come back on the fifty-fourth and is going to be told no again, and that will be the third no anybody has given him on this road in about nine days.**"

**"And none of the three of us is going to be thanked for any of them, and I would like the wall to know all of that, and the wall has been in the sun.**"

---

The boards were against that wall at the fourth hour and they were against it at the fifth and they were against it on the Friday, and the rope was on the handle on the Friday and on the Saturday, and the gate was shut on the Friday and the bar of ash was in its two brackets and the hook was over its handle and a date went on the post that morning and the one above it was not rubbed out.

Nobody in that lane was angry with him, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the reason nobody was angry with him is that a man who carries things for a living and who gives a reason in four sentences and asks in advance not to be thanked is not a man anybody can be angry with, because being angry with him would mean somebody had to work out what he ought to have done instead, and about four people who have tried have given it up, and one of them has said out loud in a kitchen that a man who will not take his own boards home is more use in this flood than a man who would, and has not been able to say why, and is not going to improve on it.
