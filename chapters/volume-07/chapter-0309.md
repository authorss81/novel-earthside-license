# Chapter 0309 — Six Days Left In It, And Nothing After It

A sheet came back to a room over a market on the fifty-fourth day of the Short Month, a Tuesday, with a question written on the back of it in a woman's own hand, and about nine people were in that room and the light went off the low ground early and the cold was in the shutters and in the boards of the stair, and what was on that table was a slate, a lump of chalk, a deed with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and a piece of paper with nothing at the top of it that had been in a kitchen for four days.

---

The question on the back of the sheet was answerable. About four people in that room have worked out since that this was the first time in about a month that a thing had come back into a room in this city that could be answered inside it, and that the answer was four lines long and it had been written by a woman of about sixty-nine at the end of a lane in her own hand on the back of a question that had been put on her table by a man who carried things for a living, and that it had travelled about nine hundred paces and been in a kitchen and been read twice and carried back, and that it had come back unsigned.

The register-keeper read it out and then read it out again, and put it flat on the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she read it twice because a sheet that has come back is the only sheet in four hundred miles that anybody can be shown has an answer on it, and that the whole of the argument in that room since the thirty-ninth of that month has been about which of two wrong documents is correct, and that this one is a question and it is going to be answered in a room and it is going to need a day on it.

"Four days."

"It went out on the fiftieth at the fourth hour of the afternoon and it is the fifty-fourth and that is four days, and I have not been able to make myself write the afternoon on the count and neither can she, because a household does not count the afternoon of a day separately from the day. **Four days, and they are four, and nobody in this room is going to be able to prove that afterwards except the two of us.**"

---

The answer went on the slate in about nine words and then in a second line and then a third, because a person who is sixty-nine and writes in her own words does not write nine words, and the room got it down in the form it uses, which is the fifty-fourth day of that month, in chalk, on the second slate, and the date went beside it and nothing went under the date.

The man of about forty-three had been in that room since the seventh hour and he had watched the fifty-fourth go onto a slate and he had watched a second slate get turned over and had watched a second date go onto the second slate about a hand's width from the first one.

He was counting, and he was not counting for any reason anybody in the room could use.

"You have put a lot of days on that stone."

"**We put one on it every morning and we have done it for fifty-four days and it is a month of sixty, and I have been putting that month on everything since the two hundred and fifty-sixth day of this flood, and nobody in four hundred miles has ever heard of it except a clerk and a notary and about nine people in this city.**" She put the chalk down on the table. "It is not a thing anybody made up. It is a form. A notary made it out loud in this room in front of about nine people, and she said the cost of it out loud first, and she was refused by a person and then she did it anyway, and it is on a page in this city and it is the only date anybody in this city has been able to use since then."

"How long is it."

"Sixty days. It is written on the page and the page has been in a book since the two hundred and fifty-sixth day of this flood, and I have read it in front of about nine people about nine times, and it is the fiftieth day of that month that is on the second slate and it is correct."

---

The man of about forty-three put his hand on the table about a foot from the second slate and left it there.

"I have been in this city eleven years and I keep about nine boxes of other people's papers, and I have never had to write a date on anything of my own in about six years, and I am going to have to write one inside six days."

"Nobody has to write one inside six days."

"Then somebody has to write one on the day after that, and that is what I am here about, and I have been waiting since the fourth hour of the afternoon to say it and I could not." He took his hand off the table. "**This happened once before in the middle of this flood. A woman stood in this room and named a month. It was the first day of the one that came after the Long Month, and she did not announce it and nobody asked her to.**" He turned his hand over on the table. "**She said the word, and after that day people wrote the word on things, and that was the whole of the announcement.**"

A man of about thirty-seven who had been at the back of that room with a board of his own under his arm said nothing at all, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps a board and says nothing at the point where a room runs out of forms is a man who has watched a room run out of forms before and does not intend to be the first person to say so.

"And what happened when the month she named ran out?"

"Nothing happened, because she had not got that far, and that is the part I have been sitting here since the fourth hour of the afternoon about." He put his hand on the table again. "**A form that runs out does not fail. It stops being a form and it becomes a thing that has to be made up, and the person who has to make it up is whoever is standing in the room when somebody asks for a day.**" He looked at the second slate. "**That is not me and it is not the man with a rope. It is a woman with a piece of chalk, and she has been doing it for fifty-four days.**"

---

The room did not panic, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which finds out that its own calendar has run out and carries on anyway is the most frightening thing anybody has watched happen in this city since the thirty-ninth of that month, and that it is exactly the thing nine households on a lane did at the seventh hour of a Monday, and that nobody told them to and nobody could have told them to and they did it with a bar and a door and no form of any kind.

The register-keeper wrote the answer on the slate with the day on it in the form she had, which is the fifty-fourth day of the Short Month, and she said it out loud to nobody in particular.

"**That is the last answer this city will carry this month's word on. The next one falls on the first day of whatever comes after the sixtieth, and that day is a Tuesday, and there is no form in four hundred miles for a Tuesday.**"

Nobody in that room said anything. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the chalk down beside the second slate, and a woman of about thirty-three who had a question about the order of nineteen stones put her hand flat on the table, and a man of about thirty-four who was not the man of about thirty-four and who did not come to this city said four words to the man of about forty-three about a box he keeps, and the room carried on.

---

The man of about forty-three said it first, out loud, in the plainest sentence anybody had said in that room all day, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the whole of that day arrived in one sentence from a man who keeps other people's papers and has been waiting since the fourth hour of the afternoon to say it.

The woman of about thirty-three asked the woman of about twenty-nine whether the sixty-first would be the first day of something or a day with no name on it, and the woman of about twenty-nine said that she was not going to guess at it in a room.

Two women asking a question and getting the right answer is a rarer thing in this flood than a man getting a wrong one.

"The Short Month ends on its sixtieth day. That is six days from today. The sixtieth day of that month is a Monday, because the fifty-fourth is a Tuesday and six days on from a Tuesday is a Monday, and I have been doing that sum in my head since the fourth hour of the afternoon and I have got it right four times."

"And after the sixtieth?"

"After the sixtieth there is no form in four hundred miles, and there is no page in four hundred miles that says what the day is, and a clerk nine hundred miles away has never heard the name this city is using and would have to be told, and he would believe his own reckoning and not ours, and that is the way it happened last time and nobody went to find out."

About four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has been arguing about a calendar for four hours did not once look at the door, and that the door had a date on it in chalk and the date was the only page anybody in that room had that was certain.

"Last time it did not run out. Last time somebody was standing in this room with a date to write."

"Then this time somebody is not."

About four people in that market have worked out since that a room which has just found out that it has six days of writing in it and no way of writing the seventh did not stop talking, and that a man at the back of it had been on his feet four hours and had not been asked a question and had not asked one, and that the reason he was the one who finally asked is that he had nothing to lose by asking and everything to make up for the asking.

At about the fifth hour of the evening a man of about twenty-five who had been at the back of that room with his hands in his pockets for four hours and had not been asked anything by anybody in the whole time he had been in it stood up.

He came to this city with a question and three other people and an instruction that is that they open nothing, and he has not left since the hundred and forty-fifth day of this flood, and he put his hands in and out of his pockets and said out loud, in a voice that was not raised, that he could not work out what the day after the sixtieth of that month would be.

He said it out loud in a room in daylight with a date in chalk on the door, and about nine people in that room heard it, and nobody in that room answered him, and about four of them did not answer him because they had not worked it out and about four of them did not answer him because they had worked it out on the way in and had been carrying it about since the fourth hour of the afternoon and had decided that a man who is not asked anything ought not to be answered either.

Nobody in that room could say what came after the sixtieth day of that month, and the second slate went on its shelf at about the fifth hour with the fifty-fourth on it and a question under the date, and about four people in that market have worked out since that the answer in front of them is a thing that happened on a day, and the day is the fifty-fourth of a month that has six days in it, and that this is the second time in this flood that a page has run out before the work has, and that the first time a woman stood in this room and named one and the second time nobody in the room was asked, and that nobody in four hundred miles has any idea what to call the day after the sixtieth, and that a man of about twenty-five asked out loud in daylight, with a date on the door, and got nothing.
