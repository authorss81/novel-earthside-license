# Chapter 0333 — Three People, And A Stick Of Hazel In A Doorway

A room over a bakehouse on the low ground had the eighteenth day of that month written on its door in chalk, a Friday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in off the boards and across the flour dust that a bakehouse puts into the air of a room above it, and the cold was out of the plaster and in the back of a woman's knee where she had been sitting against a wall since before the light, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folding knife with a hinge gone stiff, a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it, a door with a handle on it, and about nine paces of floor nobody was standing on.

---

The three of them were in the room before the seventh hour and they had not been in a room together before.

The woman of about twenty-one came first and sat down and did not stop, which is what she does, and the man of about twenty-five came second and sat down and put a hand flat on the table and left it there, and the man of about forty-eight came third and sat down and opened his knife and shut it twice with the thumb of the other hand to make the joint take, and after that the room had three people in it who had come to this city four hundred miles behind a question and had never once been in a room with each other while they were in it.

Nobody arranged that, and nobody in this city arranged it because nobody in this city had noticed it until this morning, and three people who have each been written into a separate sheet and sent to a separate room have spent a month in one city not knowing what the other two were doing on the same day, and that the four days of notice is a machine for asking one person at a time and it has never once in this flood asked two of them at once, and that this is a property and not a fault, and that a person who is asked alone can say no.

The woman of about twenty-one had the cut-down pen in her hand, and she has carried a pen into every room she has gone into since the fifteenth and has not written anything with it in any of them, and she brought it this morning because it is in her coat and her coat is what she has.

Nobody asked her anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city arrived at a rule by itself in about a fortnight and that the rule is that nobody in it is asked anything, and that the rule has been arrived at by people who wanted a great deal to ask, and that a rule a room gives itself is worth more than a rule that is written down and handed to it.

---

She came to the door at about the ninth hour.

Nobody sent for her and nobody sent anybody to her and there is no part of this flood in which anybody was ever going to be sent to that gate for any reason, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-one came down a row of houses on a Friday morning to a door with a date in chalk on it and stood in it, and that she was not turned out and nobody said a word to her and nobody in that room had been asked to say one.

She had the stick of hazel in her hand and did not lean on it. She stands the way a person stands who has decided in advance how long she is going to be standing there, and the three people at the table did not get up and did not turn round, and the turning round was available to all three of them and about four people watching from the back of that room have worked out since that not turning round was the hardest thing any of the three did that morning and that none of them has said so since.

She looked at the door. There is a date in chalk on it and the date is the one the room is for, and she read her own date off a door in about four seconds and a woman who put a room into a sheet in her own words four days ago knows exactly what a door with a date on it is for, and that she had no reason to doubt anybody and she has not got one.

She said nothing. Nobody in that room spoke to her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city has spent a fortnight refusing to ask anybody anything and has now sat for as long as a woman stood in its doorway and not asked her one question, and that this is the single hardest thing that room has done since the day it decided that nobody was going to be sent, and that it cost nothing, and that the reason it cost nothing is that she did not have to be talked out of anything.

Then she went back up the row, and the door was not shut behind her by anybody, and the door stood open for as long as it takes to fill a pail after she had gone and nobody in that room got up and nobody in that room said one word about it then or afterwards, and that a room in this city has now let a person answer a question that was never put a second time without asking her for the answer, and that the answer was the same and the room did not need to hear it and did not hear it.

---

The woman of about thirty-three and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had been at the bread end of the room for about two hours with nothing between them.

"Is anybody going to the door."

"**Nobody is going to the door and nobody is going to send anybody to the door, and I have not said that to anybody today and I am saying it to you now, because you have been standing at the bread end of this room for two hours doing the thing a person does when they are about to go and do something they have not been asked to do.**"

"I have carried four of these this month and I have not opened one of them."

"**Then you are the only person in four hundred miles who has carried four of them and not read one, and I would like that noticed in this room with a date on the door, and there is nowhere to put it and I have looked.**"

Nobody looked for anywhere, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has just said out loud that he cannot read the things he carries, and that about nine people in this room already knew it and about four of them have been carrying a sheet of their own all morning, and that the whole of the difference between him and them is that he says it out loud and they have not.

The man of about twenty-five had nine words in the inside of his coat and had had them since the Saturday, and he came into that room at about the seventh hour and put his hand flat on the table on the sheet that is face down and did not look at the door when the light went off the boards and came back up, and he looked at the door, and that the two people at the table with him saw him look at the door, and that about four people at the back of the room saw him look at the door.

He did not say the nine words. He did not say them to her and he did not say them to the two of them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about twenty-five said in a room on Saturday that if he said it in a room then it would be in there on the day of the room when he was not able to look at it, and that this morning is the day of the room, and that the condition he set for himself has arrived, and that he has kept it, and that the cost of keeping it is that there is a person in a room in this city who was written into a sheet with him and he will never know whether the nine words would have changed anything.

The woman of about twenty-one saw him look at the door. She did not ask him about it then and she did not ask him about it at the end of the morning, and a woman of about twenty-one has been in this city since before the water and has asked one question in all that time and has been told the answer to it by nobody, and this morning she had an opportunity in front of about nine people to ask a second and did not take it, and that nobody in that room has worked out why and about four of the nine people in that room have stopped expecting to.

---

The room held until about the fourth hour of the afternoon and nothing was decided in it.

Nobody asked any of the three of them whether it was going to work, and about four people in that room have worked out since that there are three people in a room in this city who have said yes in their own words to a thing nobody put their name to and who have not been asked once since, and that a man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living said a rule out loud in a room over a market four days ago and the room had arrived at it on its own a week before that, and that the two of those facts have never been in the same room and this morning they were, and that a room which says a rule out loud to nine people on a Friday has cost itself the only thing it had, which is that nobody can now say they did not know.

The man of about forty-eight had the knife on the table and did not open it again all afternoon, and a man who opens and shuts a stiff hinge twice before he can use it did it twice this morning and not once in seven hours, and the only reading anybody can put on that is that he was not going to need it, and that this is the cheapest piece of evidence in four hundred miles and that it is a hinge and it is about a man deciding in advance that he is not going to mend anything in a room where nobody has asked him to mend anything.

The man of about forty-eight put the knife on the table and the woman of about thirty-three looked at the knife and then at the hand the woman of about twenty-one had not put down.

"She has had that pen in her hand all morning."

"**She has had it since the fifteenth of that month. And she has not put it down once and she has not written with it once and she is not going to today, and I have spent seven hours watching a stranger hold a thing she has no use for and I have not asked her about it and I am not going to.**"

Nobody in that room looked at the pen, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about forty-eight has spent seven hours watching a stranger hold a pen and has not asked her one question about it, and that this is the second time this month he has done a thing in a room without being asked to and the first time was a skylight, and that about four people in that room have worked out that the two of them are the same act and that neither of them is a trade and both of them are free.

When the light went off the boards the woman of about twenty-one said the only thing she said in nine hours, and it was not a question.

"**I have stopped waiting to be told what I am for. I would like it noticed and I am not going to explain it, because the not-explaining is the half of it and I would only be able to do the other half by having it explained, and then it would be telling and not finding out.**"

Nobody answered her, and a woman of about twenty-one came into a room on the fifteenth of that month and asked what she was for before she had sat down, and she has been in two rooms since and has asked once, and that this morning she announced in nine hours' notice that the asking is over, and that nobody in that room argued with it, and that a person in this city deciding on her own account that she is not going to be told what she is for is the only decision in this flood that nobody can put in a column, and that it costs her nothing and it is hers and that is why it is the only one.

---

Three of them came out of that room into a row of houses with the light going off the boards and about four people in that room have worked out since that they came out separately and about nine paces apart and that none of them looked back at the other two, and that this is not coldness and is the arrangement, and that a person who has been written into a sheet with two other people and has been in a room with both of them for seven hours and has heard neither of them say what the four of them are for has got about nine paces of space and is entitled to it.

Behind them the door still had the eighteenth day of that month on it in chalk, and nothing had been put under it, and there was no second date on that door and nothing rubbed out, and about four people in that row have worked out since that a door in this city with one date on it and nothing above it is a notice and not a record, and that there are four of those doors in this city in about four days, and that one of them is at a shut gate on another road and has had a date in chalk on the post every morning for nine years with nothing rubbed out and has been asked nothing by anybody, and that the two of them have never met and that neither of them knows the other's trade.
