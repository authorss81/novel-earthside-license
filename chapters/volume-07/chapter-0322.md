# Chapter 0322 — The Man In The Very Good Coat Comes Back Without A Day

A room over a market had the seventh day of that month in chalk on its door, a Monday, and about nine people in it, and the light came over the roofs of the market at about the seventh hour and stopped two feet short of the table, and the cold was out of the glass and in the seam of a very good coat and in the leather of a case that had been lying on that table since the first of that month, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet of paper with a notary's name at the top of it and five lines under that and nothing on them, a register with nine years in it, a second slate face down, and a hand going into a coat and coming out again with nothing in it.

---

He came in at about the seventh hour and did not sit down, which is what he did the last time he was in that room, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in a very good coat in a room over a market twice has decided that the coat is the only thing about him that is worth anybody's time and that everything else he says is going to be taken as a favour.

He said the thing he had come to say before anybody asked him for it, which is the way he works and the reason he is not asked twice.

"**I said I would come back when the room had put something on the form and I did not say when, and I am here on a Monday, and the room has put nothing on it, and I would like it noticed that I have come anyway and not on the day.**"

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not look up from the register, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said he will come back when a thing happens and has come on a day of his own choosing has put a second thing in the room without saying what it is, and that the second thing is that he has decided to be in the room when somebody fills the form in.

"Somebody in this room is going to fill that in, and it is going to be the first time in four hundred miles that anybody has written on it, and I would like to be in the room, and I am not going to ask anybody to hurry."

---

Hettis Roke came in at about the eighth hour with the waxed slate in its case and put the case on the table face down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the last time he was in it he put the same object on the same table face up, turned it over, and then said four sentences into a row with nothing in it, and that the difference between face up and face down is a man deciding in advance what the room is going to get.

"A form with a notary's name at the top of it is for entering a thing somebody has brought you."

"**It is.**"

"And there is nothing in this city to bring."

"And **there is nothing in this city to bring, and that is the whole of what is wrong with the form, and it is not the notary's fault, and I would like it said in this room that it is not the notary's fault and that a person who has put her name under five lines in this flood has been handed an instrument with nothing to put through it.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who holds a waxed slate in a case and has said four sentences into a row with nothing in it has now said a sentence that is about somebody else's instrument, and that he is the only person in that room who has been on the wrong end of a form four times a year for nineteen years.

---

The man of about forty-three who keeps other people's papers had the box at his feet and had the deed and the sheet in it and did not take them out, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who brought a document to a room last week and had it read out to nine people has not brought it today, and that the reason is that the subject of the room today is a blank piece of paper and not a document, and that a document is the easiest thing in the world to bring to a room and the hardest thing in the world to leave out of one.

The woman of about thirty-three, who had carried a question about the order of nineteen stones since the forty-eighth of the Short Month, put her hand flat on the table about a foot from the form and asked the question that has been sitting in that room for a week.

"Then who is it for. **You all keep saying it is for whoever has to fill it in later and nobody in this room knows who that is, and I have been in this room every day for a fortnight and I have worked out that there is one person in four hundred miles who has to fill one in and he is standing up.**"

She said it to the room and not to him, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the second time in about a fortnight that a woman has been right about the room before anybody told her, and that being right about a room is a thing nobody in four hundred miles can pay for and she has never once asked to be.

---

He put his hand into the coat and took out nine days of a page folded in four and did not unfold it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has come twice to a room in a very good coat and has not shown anybody the front of that page has now taken it out and not opened it, and that those are two different decisions and the second one is the whole of why he came.

"**A clerk in a town four hundred miles inland is going to send me a copy of that, and I am going to be the person holding it, and the first thing that happens to that copy is that somebody writes a day on it, and the day has to be a number, and the number is not on the form and it is not anywhere else in four hundred miles.**"

He put the hand back in the coat and left the page folded where it was, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has come to a room to be a person in it has spent the whole of the visit explaining a thing he has not been asked about, and that he is going to be asked about it, and that the asking is going to cost him more than the telling.

"Nobody can check it. That is not me being dramatic, that is what the woman who made it said out loud on the first day of it, in this room, with a number on the door, and she wrote a count of the days of the flood in chalk on a door and said if she is wrong then every day after is wrong on the same count. **So the one number that would go under the first line of that form is the one figure in four hundred miles that this city cannot stand behind, and I am the man who is going to have to write it, in a hand that is mine, in a room I have not been in.**"

---

Hettis Roke turned the case over on the table and put it face up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has had his object face down in front of nine people for an hour has now put it face up, and that he has done it while another man was finishing a sentence, and that the timing is not an accident and neither is the choice of moment.

"**I have sent a number four times a year for nineteen years to an office and I have never been told what it is for, and a clerk wrote it in a book and nobody came, and I have never once written a figure I was not standing there for, and that is the whole of my trade and it is not a virtue and it is not a rule anybody has ever given me.**"

He did not open the case, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has put a thing face up in front of nine people and has not opened it has done the first half of something and is going to do the second half this afternoon whether the room wants it or not.

"**That is not me refusing to give you a number. There is a space under the last line about the width of two fingers and it has been on that paper since the first of that month and nobody has written in it, and I am not going to be the man who puts a number in it, and I would like it entered that I said so before anybody asked me and not after.**"

Nobody wrote it down and the woman of about twenty-nine did not say she would, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the second time in about a week that a true thing has been said in that room and gone onto nothing, the man who said it has been in nine rooms this month and has been in this one twice, and the register has a row for this day with nothing in it.

---

The room did the form at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the woman of about twenty-nine filled it in herself, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the first entry ever made on a page with a notary's name at the top of it in four hundred miles was made by the woman who has spent nine years refusing to put a name at the top of anything, and that she did not hesitate, and that a person who hesitates on a form is telling the room something.

She wrote the day in the new word at the top. She wrote underneath it the four columns her own book has, one after another, because she has never in nine years entered anything in a shape that was not her own, and the first of them said what was asked and the second of them said what came back and the third of them said the day and the fourth one is the one that is not in her book.

"What came back was nothing. What came back is always nothing, that is what a register of nine years is, and I have never minded it and I minded it this morning."

She put the pen down and did not write a number, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the space under the last line is still the width of two fingers and that nobody in that room has any idea what is going to be written in it and that is now on a page.

Hettis Roke filled in a second thing at about the fifth hour, on the back of the same sheet, and it was a gate and a set of days and a number, and there was a space on it for a person and he did not write in it, and he said one sentence about it and it was that the space is for the man in the office and the man in the office has never once written in it and he has never once asked the man in the office to.

Nobody in that room asked him what a keeper is, and nobody in that room asked him to define a word that is on a page in this city and on a page in the Marches and has never been defined, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the fifth time in about nine days that a person has not been asked something they could have been asked, and that a man who keeps gates on a turn has now been in a room over a market twice and has put two figures on a piece of paper in it and has not been asked one question.

---

The man in the very good coat took the page out of the coat and put it back in the coat and did not say when he would come back, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has come on a day of his own choosing and has watched a form filled in from the first line to the space and has not asked anybody to hurry has got what he came for and cannot use any of it, and that the number he wanted is the one thing in that room that does not exist, and that he knew it did not exist when he asked, and that he asked anyway, in daylight, with a date on the door, on a Monday.

The form is on that table with five lines under a notary's name and four words written on it and a space about the width of two fingers at the bottom of it with nothing in it, and it is the first page anybody in four hundred miles can send for, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a page which can be sent for is a page somebody is going to fill in, and that the person who is going to be hurt by it is four hundred miles away with nine days of a copy in a coat and no number to put under the first line.
