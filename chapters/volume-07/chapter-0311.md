# Chapter 0311 — Nine Places, And A Sheet With No Day On It

A road went up out of the low ground with the light over the shoulder of the hill behind it and stopped about two thirds of the way up, and the wind came down the same shoulder and did not stop at anything, and the cold was in the folds of a sheet of paper and in the iron of a gate they had just come through and in the shoulder of the man who carried nothing, and what was under about two pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it and a lump of chalk carried in one of the folds and nothing else at all.

---

She folded it the same way every time. Long way in half, then in half again across, and the corner that came last went under, and it was not a habit anybody in this flood could have taught her because she had been doing it since before the water and had never once done it another way.

**"A sheet that is folded some other way can be taken for a notice, and a notice has a day on it, and mine has not got a day on it and is not going to get one."** She put it in the inside of her coat and it went in flat. "That is the whole of the reason and I have never given it to anybody."

The eighth of the nine places was a turning where four streets come together and there was a bread board on the sill of the first house at it, and a woman of about thirty-three was behind the board with her hands in the flour.

Tamsin Quill put the folded sheet on the sill, on the flour, and turned it so that the fold showed, and did not open it.

**"One question and I will not ask it twice and I am not going to write down anything you have not said out loud. What is this ground called, and who called it that."**

The woman of about thirty-three wiped her hands and asked her what she wanted it for, which is a question Tamsin Quill was expecting and had an answer ready for, and gave it in nine words.

Nobody on that turning had been asked a question about the ground in nineteen years and the one they were asked now was so ordinary that the woman behind the board answered it before she had finished deciding to.

"That is the nine acres. My grandmother called it that and her mother called it that and there is a gate you can walk through onto it if you are willing to walk through a field of somebody's."

"**The nine acres.**" Tamsin Quill wrote it on the sheet and did not say it again. "And nobody has ever put that on anything."

"Nobody has ever been asked, girl. Ask a person what they call their own ground and they will tell you, and it is not a name anybody writes down because there is nowhere to write it."

---

The man who was walking with her read the sheet when she opened it at the next stile and read it the way he read everything, from the top down, both times, and got to the end of the eighth line and turned it back the way she had it. She said Adrian, once, and then went on with the walking and did not say anything else about him for the rest of the day.

He did not ask her a single question.

**"There is no name at the top of it."**

"There is no day on it and there is no name at the top of it, and those are two different decisions and the second one took me a week." She did not look up. "Eight of the nine so far have said they would like to keep it, and I have been round nine places in three days and that is eight people asking me for a piece of paper that nobody can send for, and that is what a sheet with no day on it does. A sheet with a day on it is a return. A return gets answered."

He put it back the way she had it and did not improve on the fold, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms and eleven kitchens this month walked three days up a road with a woman and did not ask her one thing, and that neither of them said a word about why, and that the reason is nine years old and it is hers as much as his and neither of them has put it in a mouth.

The fracture between them was not mended that day and neither of them said so, and about four people who have been in rooms with both of them have worked out that the cheapest hour either of them spends is the hour the other one is standing next to them saying nothing, and that this has been true for about a month and that nothing has been done about it and that nothing is going to be done about it by either of them.

---

The ninth place was out past the last named house and it was eight houses in a row with a gap for a cart between two of them, and it is a place and not a house, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a place with eight houses in it is the only kind of place anybody can leave a sheet at without a name, and that a house at the end of a lane past two gates is only a house.

They were there about the fourth hour and the light was going off the low ground behind them and coming back up the front of the eight doors, and Tamsin Quill stood in the gap for the cart and did not go up to any of them.

Nothing came to a door.

She did not go back. She stood in the gap for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and then she folded the sheet the way she folds it, and put the chalk in the fold, and said the thing she had been saying to herself for three days in the voice she used for people who work for a living.

**"That is the ninth place and it is the only one of the nine where nobody came to a door, and I am not going to go back, because going back is how a person turns a question into a pressure.**" She put the chalk in the fold. "I have got nine places and eight answers and I am going to be honest about the ninth one on a piece of paper that says so."

"Write *no answer* on the ninth line."

"**I am going to write nothing on the ninth line.** An empty line is a thing somebody can fill in. A line that says *no answer* is a thing somebody can argue with, and there is nobody here to argue with, and I have got eight answers off nine people this week and the eight of them did not ask me for a form."

---

The light went off the eight doors first and then the turning, and the road down to the city was in the low ground and out of the wind, and the two of them came down it at the rate two people walk at when one of them has been walking since before the light and the other of them has not asked her for anything for three days.

The sheet was in her coat and the ninth line was the last thing on it and it was blank, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a list of what nine pieces of ground are called, with a day on it and a name at the top of it, is a return and would have to be answered by somebody four hundred miles off, and that the same list with no day and no name is a thing eight households have agreed to keep, and that between those two things the only difference is a line of ink and a habit of folding.

Seven of those eight are the two names the papers use, written seven different ways, and the eighth is nine acres, and there is now a third name for a piece of ground in four hundred miles and it came from a woman behind a bread board and not from a document. She said it to the road and not to him.

"And the finding is not the third name."

"**The finding is that a man of about forty-three has nine boxes of other people's papers and neither of them ever asked anybody at a door what they called it, and I have now asked nine and eight of them told me, and I am going to have to put that in a room and nobody in that room is going to know what to do with it.**"

"And what will you tell them."

"**That a boundary in this world is settled by two parties agreeing where it runs, and that neither party is anybody on that road, and that I have spent three days finding out what nine pieces of ground call themselves and eight of them told me and none of them is in either of the two documents.**"

He did not say that she had found something out that the room needed. He did not say it was useful. He walked down the low ground beside her with his hands in his coat and about four people who have been in rooms with both of them have worked out that the whole of what he gave her that afternoon was a road back into a city at dusk, and that a man who is not asked anything is the only kind of person in this flood that cannot be put in a column, and that the register-keeper said that out loud on the forty-sixth at a shut gate and neither of these two was in that lane.
