# Chapter 0343 — The Table Afterwards, And A Rope Nobody Has Used

A yard on the half a man of about fifty-seven bought about forty years ago had the twenty-eighth day of that month in the weather on the gate post and nothing else, a Monday, and the light came in off the low ground about half an hour late and did not get past the row of trees at the near end of it, and the cold was out of the north and in the wet of a rope that had been lying in a yard since before the water, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a length of new rope nobody had used, a gate latch that needed a hand on it, a barrow, and a folded paper with a sum on it and a name at the top of it.

---

The row of trees is at the near end of that ground and the stone is at the far end of it and nobody put it there, and the man of about fifty-seven can see both of them from his own gate on a morning when the light gets that far, and he has been able to see both of them since before the water and has never gone to the far one.

He did not go to the far one.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the road with nothing in his hands at about the seventh hour and stood in the yard and did not go through the gate either, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood and has not opened one of them came out nine miles on a Monday morning with nothing in his hands, and that what he came out for was not to be asked anything.

"So it is off."

"It is off."

"And nobody put anything else on."

"Nobody put anything else on anything, and there is a date on a slate in that room and nothing under it, and that is the whole of what there is in four hundred miles about that ground now."

The man of about fifty-seven put his hand on the gate latch and took it off again. About four people who have watched him do that have worked out since that a man who puts a hand on a latch and takes it off is a man working out whether there is anything on this side of it for the hand to do, and that there is not, and that he has known that since Saturday in a room over a shop and has come out here anyway on a Monday to look at the same two things from the same gate.

---

Then he said the thing, and he said it in a yard and not to anybody, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who paid money for half a holding about forty years ago has worked out in about nine seconds what the naming bought and what it cost, and that the two figures are the same figure, and that this is the only arithmetic anybody in this city has got right since the ninth of that month.

"**The word bought that field one afternoon and it cost that field thirty-two days, and what the afternoon was for is that a room in this city could say out loud that a mark in my ground is a mark and not a boundary.** Nobody in that room had ever said it in forty years and nobody is going to say it now. The mark is where it was on Friday and I am where I was on Friday, and that is the finding and I am not going to be told it is a sad one."

Nobody told him it was a sad one. The man of about thirty-one, who had come nine miles with nothing in his hands, did not tell him it was a sad one, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has never once improved on a thing anybody said to him, and that this is the first time anybody in four hundred miles has said in a yard that a subtraction is not a defeat and has had it let stand.

"**I told you what it cost and you have not said one word back, and I have been in about nine rooms in a month and there is not one of the nine that ever said one word back.**"

The man of about thirty-one looked at the row of trees at the near end of that ground and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has just been accused of something by a man he has known about six weeks, and that the accusation was true, and that about four people in four hundred miles have worked out at the same moment that a man who does not say anything back is a harder person to sit across a table from than one who argues.

"You have not asked me what it cost."

The barrow stood in the yard with its wheel against a rut and neither man went near it.

"No."

"I told you the number and I told you the field and I told you the room."

"**I am not going to say a word back, and it is not because I do not have one, and I would like you to know that I worked out what you cost on Sunday morning while I was being the only person in that room who was talking, and I have not said it since because you did not ask and I am not going to be the second man in this city who decides a thing is too expensive to put in a room.**"

"Then we are two."

The man of about fifty-seven put the rope down on the flat of the barrow and looked at the row of trees and neither of them said anything for long enough that about four people who live in that house worked out from the kitchen window that there were two men in a yard not talking.

"Then we are two, and there are about nine people in a room over a market who are two as well, and that is a party of about ten in four hundred miles, and none of them is going anywhere."

---

The rope is new.

It came with the holding, or it came with a man, and either way it has been in that yard since before the water and it has never been on anything, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who bought half a thing about forty years ago and has not been able to show anybody where it is has been keeping a length of new rope in his yard for the whole of it, and that a rope is for a thing that is going to be walked or a thing that is going to be tied, and that neither of those has happened and that the not-happening is the whole of what the rope is for.

He picked it up and put it down in the same place, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who has carried a rope for forty years picked it up on a Monday and put it back in the same place without measuring anything against it, and that a fence is four feet off a thing and a rope is the thing you would use to find out how far four feet is, and that he did not do it, and that nobody was standing there who would have asked him to.

---

A room over a market had the twenty-eighth day of that month in chalk on its door and about nine people in it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the light came in on the north side and lay along a table with nothing in the middle of it, and the cold was out of the glass and in the seam of a canvas bag somebody had brought in and not opened, and what was under about four pairs of hands that afternoon was a deed of about forty years ago with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, a length of new rope over a chair back, a book that was shut, and a shelf with a slate on it.

The slate was on the shelf where it had been put on Sunday at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and it had the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month on it in chalk and nothing under it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register put a slate back on a shelf on Sunday and did not say one word to the room about what was on it and has not said one word since, and that there is a shape on that shelf now that anybody in the room can see from the door, and that the shape is a rectangle of dust with a slate in it and the rectangle is the same size it was before Sunday and that is the finding.

The man of about forty-three had the deed out and the sheet pinned to the corner of it out, and he put both of them on a table in about four seconds because they were going to be wanted, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers has been wrong about that twice now in about three weeks and has not been told once, and that the reason nobody tells him is that the room stopped asking anybody anything a fortnight ago and a room which corrects people is a room that has started asking.

Nobody asked him to put them away.

---

The man of about fifty-seven put his hand on the table and left it there and nobody told him what he had, because the man of about fifty-seven was not going to be told what he had, and that was settled on Saturday and settled by him, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said out loud in this room that he is not going to be told he is wrong has a second thing he is also not going to be told, and that both of the two came out of the same mouth within about four days, and that the second one is worse than the first and nobody in that room is going to give it to him because giving it to him is the same as the other one.

The digger had agreed to come and had not given a day.

That is the whole of what anybody in that room could say about it on the twenty-eighth, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man came eleven miles into a room on the twenty-fourth and said yes with a condition before anybody asked him for it and gave nobody a day, and that a room which has spent a fortnight arranging not to ask anybody anything got a condition and no date, and that the two of those are the same arrangement, and that the arrangement is a thing that cannot be entered anywhere because there is nothing on it to enter.

A man who digs is on a page in this city once, on the sixth of that month, in a room, named out loud by a person, and he was not asked anything and has not been asked anything since. The second time, on the twenty-fourth, he came to a room and was not asked anything in his first four minutes, and said yes, and gave a condition, and gave no day, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who sent that sheet out on the twentieth did not know which day the room would be on and neither does anybody else, and that the price of nobody being asked anything is that a room finds out what is going to happen on the morning of it, and that this room is about to.

---

And the man of about thirty-one, who carries things for a living, said the one thing he said all afternoon, and he said it to the room and not to the man of about fifty-seven, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood and has not opened one of them said the one sentence that made the afternoon worse and he said it to nobody.

"**A man told this room yesterday that there is a man with a barrow who has been up that lane. A man who digs is going to stand at the end of a cut in that field on a day not one of us knows. The word came off a slate in this room on Sunday because of the first of those. There is no form in four hundred miles that holds a thing and what it costs.**"

The room did not make a sound, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who said nothing all afternoon in a room on Saturday where a man asked that room who was going up his own lane has just put the two halves of a Saturday on one table, and that neither of the two can be argued with, and that the man with the barrow has not been asked anything and cannot be, and that the slate on the shelf has a date on it and nothing under it and is not going to get anything else, and that about nine people in a room in this city have spent a fortnight learning not to ask anybody anything and have just found out what the not-asking is for, and that it is for a morning like this one.

The man of about fifty-seven took the rope off the chair back and put it over his arm and the receipt went back inside the coat and the deed went back inside a case that shut, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who lost a season and a certainty and an afternoon on Saturday walked out of a room on Monday with a length of new rope he has still never used, and that nobody stopped him and nobody followed him and nobody asked him anything, and that the table was bare by the fifth hour and the shape of the slate was still in the dust on the shelf above it, and that the bare table was the last thing in four hundred miles that anybody had been able to do with a mark in a field.
