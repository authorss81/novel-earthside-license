# Chapter 0313 — What A Notary Is For, And What She Is Not Going To Do Today

A room over a market had a date in chalk on the door on the fifty-eighth day of the Short Month, a Saturday, and about nine people were in it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and stopped about the middle of the floor, and the cold was out of the glass and off the flagstones and in the joint of a bag strap, and what was under about two pairs of hands that morning was a leather bag, a pen, a book of forms with nine forms left in it, and a date nobody in that room had written yet.

---

Sera Vail put the bag down on the table and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a notary who puts a bag down closed on a table has decided that whatever is in it is not going to be the first thing that happens in the room.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the question at about the seventh hour and she put it the way she puts every question, which is in plain words and with the cost of the answer attached to it in her own mouth before anybody else has got to it.

"There is a day coming that this city cannot write, and it is three days off, and I am not asking you to make a month. **I am asking you to make a day.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the difference between asking a person for a month and asking her for a day is the whole of a week, and that a woman who keeps a public register learned it in nine years and taught it to about nine people in one morning by asking it in a smaller shape.

The notary took her hand off the bag and told them what it cost before she answered it, which is what she does, and which is why a room that has been arguing about a calendar for a week had stopped arguing by the seventh hour.

Her name goes at the top of it. That is the first half and it is not a formality: a form with a notary's name at the top is not a piece of paper, it is an instruction, and it is a thing a room in a town four hundred miles inland can work from without asking anybody, and it will be the copy of hers. And if the days do not stop where the word on it stops, then the paper is wrong, and the paper is hers, and she will be the person a clerk writes to about it for as long as the paper lasts.

"And the second part of it."

"**Yesterday morning I had a form and this morning I have not, and I have not told anybody that in nine years, and I am telling it in this room because I am about to write a form again and I am not going to do that with it still in my pocket.**"

---

A man of about forty-three who keeps other people's papers was standing at the left of the table with his hands behind him and had a box under his arm with a deed in it and a sheet pinned to the corner of the deed.

"What is the difference between entering a form and making one."

"**Twenty-six years and one word.** I have entered forms since I was twenty-four. I have never made one. I enter a thing a person has brought me and I put a day on it and I write down that it was done in front of me."

The man of about forty-three put his hand on the box and left it there, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he had come into that room with a document and had been given a definition of a trade, and that a man who keeps nine boxes has no way at all of checking a claim like that and has to take it or leave it.

"Which of the two is this."

"**Neither, and that is the whole of what is wrong with it.** The day after the sixtieth is a day I have no form for, and that is the only day a form belongs on. I am not going to make one on a day I have got a form for, because on a day I have got a form for there is a month still standing, and a month that is still standing is a month that has not been missed."

She said the last of it to the table. A person who has been asked to make a thing and has said no to a day has to say something else out loud or the room will hear it as sulking, and she said it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that it was not sulking and that the woman of about twenty-nine wrote it down in her own head in the ordinary way she writes things down, which is to say she wrote nothing.

"**A person has nine days of writing in front of her and the thing she is going to spend none of them on is the only thing anybody has asked her for this month.**"

The man of about fifty-seven, who had a length of new rope he had not used over his arm and had not put down since he came in, said nothing at all while that was being said, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he had asked a question in this room four days ago and had been answered at length, and that a man who has been answered at length and comes back and is not answered at all is being handed the second half of an answer, and that he knew which half it was before she got to it.

---

A man who had come in late and had been standing at the back of the room read the clerk's hand on the sheet that was pinned to the corner of the deed, over the man of about forty-three's shoulder, and read it twice.

He was not a clerk and had never been a clerk, and the woman of about twenty-nine said Adrian and then said nothing else about him, and nobody in that room asked him one question about the boundary, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms and eleven kitchens this month and who is not in either of the two sheets in this room has a place in it that nobody can name, and that the not-naming is the reason nobody has asked him anything.

The copy has a day at the head of it, and it is not a day anybody in this city can write. He said so in about nine words and then stopped and stood with his hands in his coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who reads a hundred-and-forty-year-old hand about as fast as a man reading a form two days old read both of them the same morning on purpose, and that the woman of about thirty-three saw him do it and did not ask him why, and that the not-asking is the fourth of its kind in about nine days and she is losing count of them herself.

"**Then it is a day somebody four hundred miles away wrote, in a form they had, on a day they had a form for, and there is a clerk's ordinary about nine words in that line and he wrote all nine of them and thought about none of them, and that is not a criticism, that is what an ordinary is for.**"

He put his finger about a finger's width above the last line of the list and did not touch it, and then he said the thing that the room is going to remember him for, which is that the copy is the only page in four hundred miles on which anybody has written down the shape of a form, and that nobody copied the shape, only the words, and that the words are the part that was standing still that morning.

"**I am not going to say that out loud as an argument, because a room where a woman is going to be asked to make one in three days is not a room for my argument. I am saying it because nobody else in here will.**"

Nobody argued with him about it and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has read a hundred-and-forty-year-old clerk's hand and can say that a thing is missing without saying whose fault it is has been doing the work of a reader all his life and has never once been paid for it, and that the man of about forty-three with the box was the only other person in the room who could have contradicted him and did not.

---

The date on the door stayed the fifty-eighth and nobody altered it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has agreed a day to do a thing it cannot do has done the one thing a room can do, which is to have a day, and that a day is the only instrument anybody in this flood has ever had that cost nothing and could not be lost.

She picked the bag up off the table at about the ninth hour and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a notary who came to a room with a bag and went out of it with the bag shut has not been in the room and has been in the room, and that the difference is a day of the week.

"Will you be in this room on Tuesday."

"**I will be in this room on Tuesday, and I will want the door to have something on it that a person in this room can be shown, and I am not going to say what that is until Tuesday.**" She said the last of it to the register-keeper and not to the room. "**And I would like it noticed that I have not been asked what I am going to say and I have said it anyway.**"

The man of about fifty-seven went out down the stair with the rope still over his arm and did not leave it on the table this time either, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who has twice left a room with the same object in his hand has decided the object is not the reason he came, and that nobody in that room will ever be told what the reason is and that this is the cheapest anybody in this flood has been let off with.
