# Chapter 0301 — A Notice In A Hand

A lane past two gates came up out of the low ground with the light over the shoulder of the hill behind it and did not reach the gate, and the wind came down the same shoulder and stopped dead at a bar of ash in two brackets the way it has stopped at it every morning since the thirty-ninth of that month, and the cold was out of the north and in the iron of a wooden hook and in the corner of a sheet of paper, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet of paper, a stone about the size of a fist, a bar of ash, a hook, a piece of chalk, and a barrow of boards that had been standing against that wall for five days counting the day they were put there as one of the five.

---

Nobody sent the woman of about twenty-nine up that lane. She came.

She had the sheet folded once across and once up, which is the way a sheet is folded when it is going to be read by somebody standing up in the rain rather than sitting at a table, and the fold had been made that morning at about the fifth hour on the step at the back of a room over a market where there is no name on the step, and the reason she had folded it that way was that she had watched nine people fold sheets that week and eight of them had folded them the other way.

The date on the post was the forty-sixth and there were five above it going back and none of them had been rubbed out, and the bar of ash was in its brackets and the hook was over its handle, and the woman of about fifty was at the gate doing the gate in the order she does it in, which is the hook down and four inches and a hand flat on the post and then the bar back in.

The barrow of boards was against the wall on the far side of the lane where it had been put, and the boards were for a house on this side of the hill, and the arrangement for taking them up was for a Thursday, and the Thursday was in three days, and the man who was to take them up had not been up that road since the fourth and nobody had sent him anything.

**"I am going to leave a piece of paper on this wall and I am not going to call at a door, and I want that said in this lane, because a person who calls gets an answer from the house she calls at and not from the other three."**

She said it to the gate and not to the woman at it, and the wind took about a third of it, and she did not say it again.

The woman of about fifty did not look round. She put the bar back into its two brackets and the gate came against its stop and made the noise it makes, and then she spoke to the post, and not to the woman with the paper, and she said it the way a person says a thing they have said before.

**"There is a date on this post going up it about two inches at a time and the newest is the forty-sixth and it is the right one, and there is a barrow of boards on my wall that a man arranged for a Thursday. It is not mine and it is not the road's, and I have not looked at it."**

---

There was nothing on the wall to put it against but lime and a nail hole and about thirty years of weather, and the sheet went up about four feet from the ground because that is as high as she could reach and because a thing at eye height is read by a person walking and a thing at shoulder height is read by a person standing still.

The woman of about twenty-nine put the sheet against the wall on the shaded side of the gate where the wind does not get into the fold, and she put the stone on the top corner of it, and the stone was a stone off the second step of that house at the end of the lane and she had asked for it and had been given it and had said thank you, and that was the only thanks anybody has given anybody on that lane since the thirty-ninth.

The sheet was a question. It was in her own hand and it was in the words of about four households, and it said what was going to happen in a room over a market on the fiftieth day of that month, and it said the day, and the fiftieth day of that month is four days from the forty-sixth and the four days were the whole of what she had to give anybody.

There was nothing at the top of it. There was no name and no mark and nothing that anybody could come back to, and she had written nine years of registers with something at the top of every one of them, and the thing she had learned in the nine years was not a large thing and she had not said it out loud to anybody until she said it to the wall.

**"I have kept a public register in this city for nine years and I have not put a name at the top of one of these since about four years ago. If it has a name at the top of it then it is a page, and a page is a thing a clerk can send for."**

She turned the sheet a half-turn on the wall and did not flatten it with her hand, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who leaves a page slightly crooked on a wall has refused to make it a document without saying so out loud, and that the crookedness is the whole of the sentence. The wind came round the corner of the gate and got under the bottom of the fold and she put her heel on it and left her heel there for the length of two sentences.

**"If it has nothing at the top of it then it is a question, and a question can be burnt by the person who wrote it, and if it is burnt then nobody has anybody."**

She stood looking at it with her arms down for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and in that time a woman of about sixty came down the lane with a creel on her hip, saw the sheet, saw the stone on it, decided it was not hers, and went on down the lane without stopping, and that is the first test a sheet gets and that one passed it in about four seconds.

She stepped back and looked at the sheet and the stone and the four inches of gate and the barrow of boards, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who puts a question on a wall at the one end of a lane and does not call at any door has made the only decision anybody has been able to make on that lane since the Monday the days went back, and that it was not a decision about the days and it was not a decision about the boards, and that it is the reason four of those houses are going to be in a room on Friday and nobody is going to know that they were asked until the Friday comes.

---

The woman of about fifty worked the gate again and did not go near the paper, and the wind came over the shoulder and hit the ash bar and stopped, and a woman at a gate who has not been asked anything by anybody in nine years and who has decided this morning that a piece of paper on a wall is a thing that is on a wall and not a thing that is about her, has made a decision that is cheaper than any other decision anybody has made in this flood and it is not going to cost her anything at all.

**"There is a sheet on my wall with a stone on it and I have not read it and I am not going to read it, and I would like it said out loud on this lane that I have not read it, because reading it is the beginning of having been asked."** She put her hand flat on the post and left it there. "I keep a gate. That is the whole of what I do, and the day somebody sends me a paper about it is the day this post is a document and I am a line on it."

The woman of about twenty-nine did not answer that, and the not-answering was in the lane for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and a woman at a gate who has said out loud that she has not read a sheet and a woman with a register who has not answered her are two people who have both decided the same thing on the same morning about the same piece of paper, and neither of them will ever say to the other that they have.

The woman of about fifty went on to the gate, and then she said two more things, to the bar of ash and to the barrow of boards, in the same voice, and neither of them was to anybody.

**"A barrow of boards standing against my wall is the only thing on that road anybody can see from the top of it, and when it goes there is nothing there at all, and a road with nothing standing on it is a road nobody has to explain to anybody."**

She got her hook and worked the gate an inch and stopped it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a keeper who says a thing in the middle of working a gate has decided the two are the same work.

**"And I have been explaining that road to about four people a week for nine years and I am tired of it. Whoever put that stone on that sheet has not called at this wall in nine years and is not going to start, and that is not a discourtesy, it is the only thing anybody has ever given me that I could use."**

---

The hour between the seventh and the eighth is the hour that lane does nothing in, and about four people who have lived on it have worked out that this is the one hour in a day a woman at a gate is not stopped, and that a register-keeper who comes up a lane in it and talks to nobody is being given something nobody arranged for her.

The woman of about twenty-nine was a quarter of the way down the lane by then with nothing in her hands, and the wind was on her back and the light was on the low ground ahead of her and going off it, and about four people in four hundred miles have said since that a woman who walks away from a sheet she has just put on a wall and does not turn round has accepted that the sheet is now about four households and is not about her at all, and that this is the third time in about a month that the instrument has left the hand of the person who made it and become nobody's.

**"There is a thing I am going to say in a room in four days and I am saying it here first so that I cannot get it wrong in front of about nine people. A boundary is settled by two parties agreeing where it runs or by nobody walking it, and there is no third way and there has never been a third way.**"

**"And then somebody is going to ask me where the line runs, and I am going to stand in a room and not be able to answer, and I have known that since this morning on this lane, and I would rather know it on a Monday than find it out on a Friday in front of a room."**

**"That is the whole of what I came up here for. The rest was a wall, a stone, and a woman at a gate who did not ask me one question in nine years, and that is the second instrument this lane has and it is the older one."**

---

The afternoon came down that lane about the second hour and the light went off the low ground at the bottom of it and came back up the other side and did not reach the gate, and the sheet went from grey to the colour of the lime behind it and stayed that colour, and nobody altered it.

Nobody on that lane answered her, and the houses did not open, and the second house from the bottom put its curtain across about an hour later, which is a thing a house does and is not an agreement, and the sheet sat on the wall under a stone off a doorstep with the wind coming round the corner of the gate and stopping at the bar of ash, and the woman of about fifty did the gate about nine times between the seventh hour and the fourth, and the barrow of boards stood against her wall and did not go anywhere, and about four people in that lane have said since that the two most durable objects on a road that nine households have taken back are a question nobody signed and a barrow of boards nobody took away, and that neither of them is a monument and neither of them is a protest and one of them has a date on it in chalk and the other has a stone on it and the stone was given.

The walk back down that lane took about as long as the walk up had, and she did not stop at the second gate and she did not stop at the first, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who goes back down a lane without stopping has already decided what she is going to say in the room, and that the deciding was done at the wall and not on the way.

Then at about the seventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine was in a room over a market, and a slate came up off the step at the back, and about nine people were in that room, and there were three things on it and two of them were things a person in this city could do and the third one was not.

The first was the return for the quarter and the second was the case that had come up the river in a cart with a docket on it and had not been opened, and the third was four lines long and it said that the ground on a lane past two gates had to be settled before the season turned, because a holding had been split in two about forty years ago and nobody living could show anybody where the split ran.

The woman of about twenty-nine read the four lines out loud in a room with a date in chalk on the door and then put the slate down on the table with the side that had the writing facing up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she put it down face up on purpose, and that a thing put down face up in a room in daylight with a date on the door is a thing the room is going to have to argue about and not a thing the room is going to be able to lose.

**"I cannot tell you where that line runs. I have been asked three times this week by people who are not in this room and I cannot tell them either, and I am going to be asked a fourth time on Friday in this room, and I am telling you now so that nobody is surprised when I say it."**

"Then where is it?"

"**About nine hundred paces off a gate on a road shut since the thirty-ninth of that month, and I am not going to look at it, and I want that said in this room tonight with a date on the door, and I would like nobody to ask me why.**"

Nobody asked her why. A man of about thirty-one who had been standing at the back of that room with his hand on the frame put his hand a different way on the frame and did not say anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he wanted to ask her why more than anybody in it and did not, and that a person who does not ask a question is the only kind of person in this flood that cannot be put in a column, and that this is now the third time in about a month that the whole of what a room has to work with is the people in it who will not speak.
