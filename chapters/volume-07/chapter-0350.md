# Chapter 0350 — Nine Posts, And A Monday

The light came up the shut road on the thirty-fifth day of that month at about the seventh hour and it was in front of the work again, and the wind was up the same road in pushes that had been up it since before the water and had never once been written down, and the cold was out of the north and out of the wet in eleven withies that had been cut on the Saturday and had not gone anywhere, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a handcart, a coil of rope, a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, and three willow posts standing in the cart with their tops out.

---

He had come up the road at about the sixth hour and nobody had sent him and nobody was waiting for him, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who put nine posts in the ground on the Saturday and went home with seven left in a handcart came back on the Monday morning to put four more in, and that he came back early, and that coming back early is not a thing anybody arranged.

The three went in at the stone end and the last one had to be found a place, because a man who puts posts in the ground by walking a rope out along it will get to the end and have about a foot and nothing to do with it, and about four people on that road have worked out since that this is the ordinary condition of a fence at the end of a road and that a man who has built nine posts in a line and has three left is a man who has a problem about a foot, and that he solved it by putting the tenth post where the ninth one was not.

That is the whole of the engineering and it is on no page and nobody asked him for it.

The withies went on in eleven places and were bound with the same cord and about four people on that road have worked out since that a fence made out of willow and cord on a shut road about nine hundred paces off a shut gate is a thing four hundred miles has about nine hundred of, and that this one is the only one in four hundred miles that anybody has put up at the same place as a mark in a field that nobody has measured, and that the two of them are about four feet apart and that four feet is the number that man has in his mouth and not a number he took off anything.

---

The man of about fifty-seven came up the road at about the ninth hour and stood where he stood on the Saturday and did not say anything for a while.

He has a deed of about forty years ago with a sheet pinned to the corner of it, and the sheet is a copy of an entry in a public register in a town four hundred miles inland, in a clerk's hand, entered a hundred and forty years ago, and the stone at the other end has a sheet on the back of it that is wrong on purpose, and the two do not agree, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who paid money for half a holding about forty years ago is standing in front of a fence that is four feet off a line he cannot show anybody, and that he has been in nine rooms in this city in about a fortnight and that the ninth one was on Thursday and that what the ninth one gave him was a page in a book that says a straight mark in a field may not be dug, and that nobody in four hundred miles can send for that page, and that a fence is a different kind of page and it is going to be here when the book is not.

"Neither of us ever agreed where it was."

"**Neither of us ever agreed where it was.**"

The two of them said it the same way and neither of them had heard the other one say it on the Saturday, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a sentence two men arrive at separately on a shut road is worth more out loud than a sentence one of them arrived at, and that about four people on that road have also worked out that neither of them is going to be thanked for it and that neither of them wants it.

"It is four feet off."

"**It is about four feet off, and I took it out of my own mouth on Saturday and I have not taken it out of anything, and I am not going to be told about it by a man in a very good coat four hundred miles off or by anybody else, and that is the only thing I have got to put on the two of us, and I am putting it here and not in a room.**"

"Put it in a room."

"No. A room has a date on the door and about nine people in it and everything that goes in it is on a page by the fourth hour, and a fence is not a page, and I have been in nine of those rooms in about a fortnight and I have not got one thing out of any of them that I could not have said standing here."

Nobody improved on it, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man with a handcart and a length of rope has just told a man who bought half a holding about forty years ago that he is the authority on where a fence is, and that the man who bought half a holding has not corrected him, and that a fence is a political act and that this one was performed on a Saturday by a man nobody sent at a place four feet off a line nobody measured on a road nobody opened, and that the two men standing in front of it are the only two people in four hundred miles who care and that neither of them can be shown a copy of what they are arguing about.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the road at about the third hour of the afternoon with nothing in his hands and looked at the ten posts and did not go past them.

"You are not going to finish it today."

"I have three posts left and I have got them in the cart and I will finish it on the Thursday, and you are not going to be asked what is on either side of it, and I would like it noticed in advance that you are not going to be asked."

"**I have not asked you anything on any of the four days I have come up this road and I am not going to start on a Monday, and about nine people in this city know that I have not and none of them can say why, and I would like it noticed that I have not said why either.**"

The ten posts stood in the ground along the top of a shut road and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a fence with three posts still to go is finished enough for everybody on a road to be able to talk about it, and that this is the ordinary condition of a thing that will be there after the people talking about it.

"Then that is two of us."

"You carried five sheets in this flood."

"Five, and I did not open one, and I have not opened one this morning either, and I have come up this road four times in about a fortnight to watch things be finished by people who were not sent, and that is the whole of my trade this month."

He turned the handcart round against the verge while he said it, which is what a man does when he has said a true thing and would rather not be looked at, and about four people on that road have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has come up a shut road four times in a fortnight to watch work get finished by people who were not sent, and that he has not opened one of the five sheets he has carried in this flood and is not going to.

"That is not a trade."

"That is the whole of my trade this month and I have had about four hundred days of a different one, and the difference is that nobody is waiting to be asked about my hands."

"That is two of us and about nine households on this lane and about four of them are out of their doors right now, and none of them has asked either of you and about four of them will not be asked, and that is a shut road with a fence on it and it is the first thing in about a month that has been finished."

Nobody stopped him, and nobody has stopped him, and there is nobody four hundred miles off who can be shown has heard of it.

The road is shut and it has been shut since the thirty-ninth day of the Short Month and nobody shut it, and a road north of the Marches is shut the same way and is going to be shut on Monday, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a road in this flood can be shut by nobody and can stay shut for as long as it likes and that a fence on it is still a fence, and that the two facts do not have anything to do with each other and that a person who put a fence up on a road because nobody told him not to has not been on the right side of anything.

He put the rope in the cart and turned the handle up and about four people on that road have worked out since that this is the second time a man with a handcart and a length of rope has put that rope into that cart and turned the handle up on that ground, and that on the first of the two the man who digs was standing next to him and said one sentence that was not an answer, and that the sentence is on no page and has not been repeated and is not going to be, and that a man with a fence and a man with a barrow are the only two people in this city who have been to that place and neither of them can say what is there.

The man of about fifty-seven did not go down the road with him and he was not asked to.

"Then I will be here on the Thursday."

"You will be here on the Thursday and the ten posts will be here and the nine that are already in will be here, and I will come up this road every day I have a handcart free, and that is not a promise and it is not a date and there is no page on either of the two of us."

"**You will come up this road until the weather stops you and then you will come up it again in the spring, and there is no form in four hundred miles that has a column for a man coming back to a road with posts in it, and that is not a complaint, it is just where the two of us are standing.**"

The man of about fifty-seven turned the second post about a quarter of a turn in the ground with one hand and let it go, which is what a man does to a post, and it came back where it had been.

"The spring is not a day and I am not going to be given one."

"The spring is not a day and neither is the Thursday and neither is the four feet, and three things in a week that will not turn into a number is the whole of what I have got to say to you, and I have said it on a shut road on a Monday morning, and there is nobody four hundred miles off who can be sent for."

The man of about fifty-seven stood in the road until the cart had gone down it, and then he went and put his hand flat on the second post the way a man does to a gate post, and he did not press on it and did not shake it and was gone in about a second, and about four people who watched from the second house have worked out since that this is the closest anybody in four hundred miles has come to measuring anything this month, and that it was a man who wanted to touch a thing and not a man who wanted to know what it was.

---

The wall of the shut gate had nine boards off it since the thirty-second and a dry strip of stone along the bottom of it, and a bar of ash in two brackets and a wooden hook over its handle and the thirty-fifth day of that month in chalk on the post above the twenty-ninth, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a woman of about fifty has written a date on that post every morning through a month with no length on it and has never rubbed one out and has not been asked anything by anybody in all that time, and that she was not asked anything about the boards and was not asked anything about the fence, and that the not-asking is the only thing this city has done this month that nobody four hundred miles off can ask to have produced.

The register in this city has a line in it with a day at the top of it and nothing under the day, and there is a shelf in a room over a market with a slate on it and a date on the slate and nothing under it, and there are four people in this city who came four hundred miles behind a question and are not going, and one of them said no in four words in a room with a date on the door and has not been asked about it and is not going to be, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that none of those five things is a power and one of them is the price of a power and that a straight line in an orchard has no word on any page in the world and that a man in this city said one word out loud in a room in the Short Month and took it off a slate in a cloth in this month, and that nobody has asked him to say it again and that nobody is going to.

Nobody in this city got stronger this month. A man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall is at a market and will not say whether a line on a wall works. A man of about fifty-seven who bought half a holding about forty years ago is standing in front of a fence he cannot move. A woman of about twenty-one has a pen with the nib ground narrow and is not going to be told what she is for. A woman of about twenty-nine keeps a book with three columns and has left the third one empty on purpose. A woman of about fifty writes a date on a post every morning and has not been asked anything, and a woman of about sixty-nine has a sheet of paper in a room that is not in a book and does not go out of the building, and a man of about fifty-two walked eleven miles and looked along a mark in a field and came back with nothing, and a man of about sixty put a fence up on a shut road about four feet off a thing nobody has measured, and nobody sent him and nobody stops him, and it will be there on Tuesday.

That is the Monday. About four people on that road who have come out of their gates and looked at it have worked out since that a fence is the most ordinary thing in the world and the most permanent, and that a fence is the last thing anybody in this flood built and the first thing that will outlast all of it, and that about four feet is not a measurement and is the number a man has in his mouth, and that it does not matter now, and that the row of trees is on one side of it and the stone is at the end of it and the road is shut and a mark in a field is about four feet behind it with no word on it anywhere in the world, and that the fence does not care where that was, and that is what a fence is for.
