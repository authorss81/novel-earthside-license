# Chapter 0302 — A Slate With Three Things On It

The room over the market had nine people in it on the forty-seventh day of the Short Month and a slate face up on the table with three things written on it, and the light came in on the north side and stopped about a foot inside the door and never got to the back of the room at all, and the cold was in the shutters and out of the flagstones and in the iron of the stair, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a slate, a lump of chalk, a box of nails that had been carried up a stair and put down and picked up and put down again, and a query about the floor.

---

The first of the three things was the return for the quarter, which goes to a buyer nine hundred miles away with a heading and four places under the heading and nothing in any of them, and it was the only one of the three that anybody in that room could finish before dark.

The second was the case that had come up the river in a cart with a docket on it, and it was still shut, and nobody in that room had opened it since the day it arrived, and the day it arrived is the day it arrived and nobody was going to be told that again.

The third was four lines long and it was about ground.

The woman of about twenty-nine read the third thing out loud twice, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she read it twice because nobody in the room had been in a room where it was read once, and that a thing read twice in the same morning in front of the same nine people is a thing that has been entered whether anybody enters it or not.

"A holding on that lane was one piece of ground. It was split in two about forty years ago and there is a deed about it and there is a sheet attached to the deed, and the deed says where the boundary of what was bought is and the sheet says where a line ran a hundred and forty years ago, and the two of them do not say the same thing."

"Are they both wrong, then."

"**They are both correct, and that is the part nobody in this room is going to like. Every figure in the deed is right and every figure in the sheet is right, and the two of them are about two different things.**"

"One of them was made to make a sale stand up and the other one was made to make nineteen stones stand still, and neither of them was made to say where the water goes."

---

A man of about thirty-one crossed the room with a box of nails under one arm and put it down by the stair and picked it up again and put it back where it had been, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living puts a box down in a room to find out whether the floor takes it, and that this is not curiosity, it is a trade, and that a man who has carried things up stairs for eleven years knows a floor by the sound of the first corner of a box.

"You are going to put that box down and leave it there."

"I am going to put it down and leave it there when somebody tells me there is somewhere in this room to put a box, and there is not, and the floor on that side is not a floor anybody should be standing on all day." He moved the box about a foot and put it down on a different board and the board was louder. "That is the north corner, and the joist under it has been wet once, and the sill on this side has never been repaired, and it is the only cold thing in this building that comes in on purpose."

"The sill."

"**The sill, and about four of you are going to be standing at that window for three hours on Friday, and the two nearest it have cold feet by the fourth hour, and that is all I have come back up here for and I will take the box down when you tell me.**"

He moved the box back to the first board without being asked, which is about the only concession that room got all morning.

"That is not on the slate."

"That is not on the slate and I know it is not on the slate and I am not asking for it to go on the slate." He put his hand flat on the board. "I am telling you that about four of you are going to be standing at that window for three hours on Friday and the two nearest it have cold feet by the fourth hour, and that is all I have come back up here for and I will take the box down when you tell me."

---

The room did the return first, because the return is the only one of the three things on that slate that can be finished by a person in a room, and about nine people in that room have worked out since that this is what a room does when it is handed three things and two of them are possible, which is that it does the possible one first and leaves the other one where it is so that it can be looked at.

The return went out with four places under a heading and nothing in them, and the woman of about twenty-nine wrote the day on the second slate beside it and nothing else, and the man of about thirty-one carried the box of nails back down the stair and was gone for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and came back up and stood in the doorway.

"You have not asked me what a return is for."

"I have not asked you what a return is for because I do not know and I have not been asked, and a man who carries a box up a stair is not one of the four people this room has a question for." She put the second slate down on the table with the day showing. "**If you have come back up here to tell me that a return has a heading and four places under it and no name on it anywhere, then I have been keeping a register for nine years and I have known that since the first week, and there is nothing on that slate about it this morning.**"

He shifted his weight in the doorway and the stair took it.

"There is a thing on that slate about it this morning. It is four lines long and it is the third thing on it." He did not come further into the room than the door. "You cannot settle a boundary in this city with a return, and you cannot settle it with a deed or with a slate, and the only two ways anybody has ever settled one are somebody walking it and two parties agreeing where it runs."

"**And there is nobody in this room who has the walking in them and there is nobody in this room who is the other party.**"

Nobody in the room offered him a name to put in either half of that.

"Who are the two parties."

"That is the deed's business and not mine, and I am not going to guess at it in a room with about nine people in it." He put his hand on the frame and left it there. "And I am not going to be asked, and the reason I am not going to be asked is that a person nobody asks anything is a person who cannot be put in a column, and it is the only thing I have got that is worth carrying up a stair."

---

Somebody at the far end of the table asked the question that had been sitting under the third thing all morning, and it was the same question, and it had been asked in four rooms in about a month and had never once been answered, and the word in it is a word that is on a page in this city and on a page in the Marches and has never once been defined by anybody.

The woman of about twenty-nine put both hands flat on the table on either side of the slate.

"I am not going to define it." She said it before anybody had finished the sentence, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she had it ready, and that a person who has an answer ready before the question is finished is a person who has been asked the same question in four rooms and has said no four times and is not tired of it. "I have it in a book in this city and I have it in a book in the Marches and the two are not the same book, and I have read both of them and I have not worked out what the word is for, and I am not going to work it out in a room with nine people in it in front of a date."

Somebody at the far end of the table tried it once and stopped.

"You could say you had looked."

"I could say I had looked and it would be true and it would be worth nothing to anybody, and the whole reason I am not going to is that a word somebody defines in a room is a word that a clerk nine hundred miles off can use." She took her hands off the table and put one of them on the slate. "**It is not a word anybody is going to define in my hearing. If anybody in this room has one I would like it now, and I do not expect to, and that is not an accusation, it is a Tuesday.**"

Nobody had one. About four people in that room have worked out since that nine people had waited for somebody else to define a word and about four of them had been hoping it would be her and about four had been hoping it would be the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and that neither of those two people was in the room, and that this is now the fourth time in about a month that a room has wanted a word out of a person who was not in it.

---

The man of about thirty-one had been at the door for all of it and had not come in, and at about the second hour of the afternoon he came the length of the room and put his hand flat on the table beside the slate, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who crosses a room he has been standing in the doorway of puts his hand on the thing he has come about and not on the person he has come about it to.

"I am going to say a thing and then I am going to stop, and I want it said that I have thought about saying it since yesterday and I have decided to say it this afternoon and not in a room with the deed on the table."

A woman of about thirty-three said it from about nine feet off and he did not look at her.

"Everybody stops talking when the deed is on the table."

"That is exactly what I am relying on." He did not take his hand off the table. "**This room has been standing on the wrong side of a thing since before the thirty-ninth of that month, and I have carried things up that stair for eleven years and never once been able to say what the thing is, and nobody here has ever asked me.** I am not going to say what it is until the deed has been read out in daylight with a date on the door."

"Then you are not going to say it this afternoon."

"I am not going to say it this afternoon and I am not going to say it on Friday either, and if the deed says what I think it says I will say so then, and nobody will be able to put in a book that I said it first without a page." He took his hand off the table. "**And I would like it entered that a man offered it and was not asked and is going to wait, because a thing nobody asked about is worth more than a thing everybody has been told about.**"



Nobody entered it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the reason nobody entered it is that there is nobody in that room whose business it is to enter a thing nobody asked about, and that this is the same failure that put a bar of ash in two brackets and four cases in a heading and a woman of about twenty-four's hands flat on a table in about four different rooms this month, and that it is not a failure of goodwill and it is a failure of hands.

At about the fourth hour the room broke up the way that room breaks up, which is all at once and in no order, and the slate was still face up on the table with three things on it, and two of them had been done, and the third one was four lines long, and about four people in that market have worked out since that the second of the three is the case that came up the river in a cart with a docket on it and it has not been opened and it is not going to be opened, and that a room which has done two thirds of a thing and left the third third face up on a table is the most honest object in this city.

And nobody in that room could say where the line ran, and the man of about thirty-one went down the stair with the box of nails under his arm and did not say what the thing was, and the second of the three things stayed shut on a table where it has been since it came up the river, and the third of them was four lines long and it was going to have to be answered on Friday by about nine people in a room with a date on the door who had four days and no page in four hundred miles that any of them could go and look at.
