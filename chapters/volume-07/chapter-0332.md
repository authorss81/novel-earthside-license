# Chapter 0332 — Eight Of Nineteen, And Nobody Can Say When

A room at the back of a chandlery had the seventeenth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Thursday, and about nine people in it, and the light came down through a skylight in the middle of the floor and stopped in about a foot square, and the cold was out of the glass in that foot square and in the iron of a catch that had gone stiff in a frame nobody had oiled since before the water, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folding knife with a hinge that had gone stiff, a catch, a sheet of paper with nineteen things on it in a woman's own hand and no name at the top of it, and about four feet of tarred twine.

---

The man of about forty-eight came in at about the seventh hour and sat down, which about four people in that room have worked out since is the second morning running that a man who has come out of a notice has sat the moment he came through the door, and that neither of them was asked to and neither of them noticed the other doing it.

He had the knife on the table. It is a folding knife with a hinge that has gone stiff and does not spring, and he opened it and shut it twice with the thumb of the other hand to make the joint take, and then it would hold about nine minutes, and a man who has to do that before he can use a tool has been doing it for years and has never once mentioned it and is not going to mention it in a room he has been written into.

"I will be here on the twenty-fourth as well, if there is one, and I am not going to be asked about that sheet."

Nobody asked him, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of forty-eight has now said the same four things in two rooms in a fortnight and that none of the four was a request and all four of them were rules, and that a person who has decided in advance what will not happen to him in a room can be in a room for nine hours and still be there on the fourth of the next week, and that the four days is the only instrument in this flood with a date on both ends of it.

"**Yes. That is the whole of it and it took me about nine seconds and I have not said the word to anybody else in this city and I am saying it to about nine people in a room with a date on the door, and I would like the room to notice the order of those two things.**"

He said it before anybody had sat down properly, and a man who says yes before a room has finished sitting down has been carrying the yes with him and has not needed a room to make it in, and the four days he has had were not for deciding and were for carrying.

---

Then he got up and did the skylight.

Nobody asked him to and about four people in that room have worked out since that this city has had a window in a room over a market that had not shut for nine years until a man with a stiff knife took the catch off it on the thirteenth, and that the room over a market has had a shut window for about four days now and about four people in that room know why and nobody has ever said it out loud in either room, and that a man who has fixed one has come to a second one and has not asked whether the second one is wanted.

The catch on that frame had not moved since before the water and the frame itself had moved, because the roof had, and that is what about four people in that room had been calling a draught for about nine days without ever having a word for it. It took him about four minutes. He used the knife and his thumbnail and a length of tarred twine and he did not ask anybody for a tool and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who can find a screw in a frame he has never worked on is a man who has done it a great many times and that the second time is always faster than the first and it was.

The light in that floor did not change. It is a foot square of light in the middle of a floor at the seventh hour and it was a foot square of light in the middle of a floor at the fourth hour of the afternoon, and a room which has had a skylight open for a fortnight has not noticed a catch and will not notice a closed one, and the work is real and the room has no way of showing it, and that a man who does work nobody can see is the ordinary condition of every trade in this flood except one.

He came back down and sat in the same place and put the knife in about the place on the table it had been in when he came in, which is a thing about four people noticed and about four did not say anything about.

---

The woman of about thirty-three had the sheet in front of her and had had it since before the light, and a woman who has carried a question about the order of nineteen stones since the forty-eighth day of the Short Month has been carrying it in her coat and not in a book, and a question with no name at the top of it is the only kind in this city that nobody can send for.

Nineteen stones, and the order she has walked them in, and the order is on the sheet in about nine lines and there is no day on it and no name at the top of it and she has been out and looked at them more than once.

"Eleven of them are one stone and eight are another."

"**Eight of them are not the same stone as the other eleven, and I can tell you that off this floor without seeing them, and I would like to say how before I say anything else, because everything else I say about them is worth nothing next to it.**"

He put his hand flat on the table, the way a man does before he starts on the thing he actually does.

"**Stone that is not the same stone does not go up in the same run with stone that is. The colour is the first thing and then it is the tooling — a man who has dressed a face leaves the tooth of the chisel in it, and a man who has split a stone for a wall leaves the grain standing up out of it, and you can put a thumb on a dressed face and know which of the two a man did and which of the two he did it for.**"

The mortar tells you the rest of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has taken old walls down in four towns has never once found nineteen stones of two kinds in a single run, and that he said that in a room with a date on the door in front of about nine people as though it were nothing whatever.

Nobody improved on it, and a man who goes up ladders for a living has just told a room that nineteen stones standing in one row were not put there in one go, and this is the first thing anybody in four hundred miles has said about the stones that is about the stones, and that it is not about the order and it does not give the order and about four of the nine people in that room heard that inside the sentence and about four of them did not.

"Then you have given me the order."

"**I have given you the runs. Nineteen stones standing in a row came there in more than one time and the eight are not the first eight and they are not the last eight, and that is one thing taken off about nine hundred, and one is not the answer, and I am telling you what I know and I am telling you where it stops.**"

He looked at the sheet and did not touch it, the way a man points at a line of somebody else's writing without being asked to.

"**When the eight went up I cannot tell you and I am not going to guess at it in a room. There is a mortar in the joints and a mortar tells you how long it has been mixed and how long it has been standing and it does not tell you a year. A man can put a stone in a gap in an afternoon and a man can put the same stone in the same gap in a week and the wall is the same wall.**"

---

The true thing about a wall, he said afterwards in his own words without being asked again, is that a wall is a wall, and a wall is a hundred years of somebody putting a stone in a gap because that is what was to hand, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who fixes stone for a living and has just been asked about nineteen of them has given the same answer twice in two different voices.

The room was quiet for a while, and a woman who has been right about this room twice without being told has been told this morning that the thing she has been carrying since before the water is not going to be carried out of it, and the man who told her is right about the stone and is right that the year is not in the stone, and that both of those are true at once and that nobody in that room is going to be told either of them is wrong.

"So it is not settled."

"**It is not settled and it is not going to be settled by me and I have not come here to settle it. I have come because there is a sheet on this table with your own hand on it and a day on the door, and because a man who has been in four hundred rooms since the water and been asked nothing in any of them has been asked this once and is going to say the true thing about it,**"

The woman of about thirty-three wrote nothing on her sheet, and a woman who has carried a question for about a month put a pencil on a piece of paper this morning and did not use it, and the not-using is not agreement and is not a decision and is a woman who has decided that eight out of nineteen is not going on a page with nothing at the top of it, because a page with nothing at the top of it can be put down anywhere and a fact about stones that says who put them there cannot.

He did not say anything else about the stones and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in about nine rooms in this city has been asked nothing in any of them and has been offered nothing in any of them, and that the thing he was offered this morning was a question of somebody else's, and that he answered it, and that a man who answers somebody else's question for nothing in a room is the cheapest help anybody in four hundred miles has got and that about four of the nine people in that room will remember that he did it and about four of them will remember what he said.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the back of the room and had not sat down at any point in nine hours, and he had carried the third of these four out on the Sunday and had not read it and had told nobody he had not read it, and this morning the man of about forty-eight said yes before he had sat down and did not say what was on the sheet, and that the two men are a carrier and a person and there is no arrangement between them and neither of them suggested one.

He came forward at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and asked one question, which was whether the catch on the skylight was going to be oiled by somebody or left, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has spent nine hours in a room and the one thing he took out of it was about a catch, and that he is the only person in four hundred miles whose trade is a thing being in the right place by the time it is wanted, and that a catch that will not be oiled is a catch that will be found by somebody else in about a year and paid for by somebody else.

"**Nobody is going to oil it. And I am not asking you to and I am not telling you not to, and I will tell you why there is a difference.**"

He did not look up when he said it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has put a question and taken it back in the same breath, and that the taking back was the answer and that he had not been asked it twice.

"**An oiled catch is a thing somebody did and it goes in a book, and a catch that gets a drop of oil off a hinge because somebody was on a ladder is a thing somebody did and it goes nowhere, and there are about nine of the second kind in this city this week and nobody in four hundred miles can ask for any of them.**"

Nobody improved on that, and a man who has said out loud in a room over a market that a hand is not a page has just told a room that the work of this flood is being kept in hinges and in boots and in a window on a road, and it is not going anywhere, and that this is the second time this month he has said it and both times he has said it to about nine people who will not write it down.

The room let the light go at about the fourth hour and the foot square on the floor went with it, and the catch was where the catch is and the frame was holding, and the sheet with nineteen things on it in a woman's own hand went back inside a coat with no name at the top of it and nothing put under any of the nine lines, and the third of four people who have not left is in a room in this city with a day on its door and has said yes without being asked and has told a woman of about thirty-three the truest thing anybody in four hundred miles knows about nineteen stones, and that the truest thing is that somebody did it in more than one time and nobody can say which time, and that the order is still where it was this morning before he came in.
