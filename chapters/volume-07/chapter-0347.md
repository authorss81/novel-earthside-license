# Chapter 0347 — The Boards Come Off The Wall

The light came in over the wall of the shut gate on the thirty-second day of that month a Friday from the west and did not get to the boards, and the wind was coming round the corner of the gate in the way it comes round that corner, and the cold was out of the boards and out of the wet in the bottom two of them and in the crease of an apron, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a bar of ash in two brackets, a wooden hook over its handle, a barrow with a rope hooked back over the handle where he had put it, and about nine boards standing on end against a wall they had been standing against since about the forty-first day of the Short Month.

---

She put the thirty-second on the post at about the seventh hour and she did not rub anything out, and the twenty-ninth was above it and the twenty-eighth above that, and about four people who live on that lane have worked out since that a keeper who writes a date on a post every morning has a column of them going up the post now that a room in this city would recognise, and that there is nothing under any of them, and that a column of dates with nothing under it is the same shape as the entry that went into a book in this city on Thursday, and that neither of the two is a record and that the woman at the gate has had hers since before the water.

The boards had gone there on about the forty-first day of the Short Month counting the day they arrived, and the arrangement for taking them up was for the forty-ninth, which was a Thursday, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that an arrangement between two people for a Thursday is a thing that does not have to be said out loud again to hold, and that it held for about four weeks after the Thursday and then stopped holding for anybody, and that neither of the two people involved ever found out that it had stopped.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came up the lane at about the ninth hour with the barrow empty and the rope hooked back over the handle, and he did not stop at the gate.

He went past her and up the shut road about nine hundred paces, and he put the barrow down in the middle of the road and stood with his forearms on the top of the handles and looked at nothing in particular to his left, and then he picked it up and came back down the lane and stopped at the boards.

That is the whole of what he did and about four people who have been on that lane since the water have worked out since that a man who stops at a gate on a Friday morning with a barrow and a rope is a man who has come for the thing that is against the wall and not for the gate, and that the gate is in the way of the thing that is against the wall, and that a man does not have to say either of those out loud to anybody for both of them to be true.

He did not ask her anything.

"I am not going to be asked about the boards and I am not going to offer anybody anything about the boards."

She said it to the boards and not to him, and he said the second half of it back to her without turning round, and about four people watching from the four doors on that lane have worked out since that a woman of fifty and a man of thirty-eight have between them used the words not going to be asked on the same morning in two different directions, and that neither of them asked the other one thing, and that this is the sixth time the man with the barrow has gone past that gate without stopping and that this is the first time he has stopped, and that the stopping is the whole of a month of not-stopping being over, and that he did not ask her when the boards were wanted, or who had put them there, or whether she wanted them off, and that she did not tell him, and that the not-telling and the not-asking are the same arrangement and it is the only one either of them has ever had with anybody.

---

The boards came off the wall one at a time and it took him most of the morning.

The bottom two had gone soft at the ends and the wet had got into the grain about a foot up and the whole stack had warped so that the middle of it stood about two inches proud of the wall, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who has come for a stack of boards with a barrow and a rope and no more than that is a man who has not thought about it for a month and that the rope was for the wet end of the stack and not for anything else, and that he had the order wrong — the boards that had been in the middle go on last and the two soft ones go on the bottom, because a stack that has been standing for a month stands again, and that a man who has stood a barrow up against a wall and taken nine boards off it one at a time in the wrong order has not ruined them and has lost about a quarter of an hour.

He got the rope round the two soft ones and put them on the bottom of the barrow and the seven good ones on the top, and he stood there with his hands on the handles and did not lift for a while, and about four people who were watching from the four doors on that lane have worked out since that a man who has loaded a barrow with nine wet boards and stood with his hands on the handles has decided that he is going to do this in about four minutes and that he is going to be wrong about it, and that he was wrong about it by about a quarter of an hour, and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to write any of that down.

"You are going to take them up that road."

The woman of about fifty said it from the post. She had not moved off it in about an hour.

"**They are going up that road on a barrow and there is nowhere else for them to go, and I have not said where, and you are not going to ask me, and I am telling you so that the not-asking is not a surprise to you later on.**"

"I am not going to ask you where they are going and I am not going to ask you who left them and I am not going to ask you whether you want them off your wall, and I have come up this lane four times this month and I have not asked you one thing, and about nine people in this city know that I have not and none of them can say why."

"Then that is the arrangement."

"**That is the arrangement and it was made before either of us said anything on this morning, and I have a barrow and nine boards in it and I have got to get them up a shut road before the light goes off the low ground, and if you want to say one more thing to me this morning you had better say it now.**"

The woman of about fifty put the piece of chalk in the pocket of her apron and did not say anything, and about four people watching from the four doors on that lane have worked out since that a keeper who has not been asked anything by anybody since before the water has told a man with a barrow when to speak, and that he did not use the time, and that the not-using was the answer, and that the two of them have now said more to each other on one morning than either of them has said to anybody in about a fortnight.

---

The light came round the corner of the wall about an hour before the middle of the day and got under the stack for the first time since it went there, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a barrow of boards standing against a shut gate wall for about a month has been standing in a shadow that a man could put his arm into, and that the wall behind it is dry and the colour of the wall is the colour of the wall nine miles up the road above the door of a store, and that nobody in this city has ever washed either of them, and that a man who washes a board every morning is nine miles off and does not know this one is here.

The wall was bare.

That is all it was. It was a piece of a gate wall with the dark shape of nine boards on it and about four inches of grey stone at the bottom where the wet had not got, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a wall with a shape on it is not a record and that a wall without one is not a record either, and that the difference between the two is nothing anybody in four hundred miles can put on a page, and that a man who has been putting shapes on walls on purpose for the whole of this flood has never once been on this lane and would have a great deal to say about it and has not been asked and is not going to be.

---

"Do you want it said anywhere."

It was the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, up the lane from the low ground with nothing in his hands, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that he came up at about the third hour of the afternoon and went past four doors without stopping at any of them, and that this is the second time in about a month that he has come up that lane and that on the first occasion he did the same thing and that a man who has carried five sheets in this flood and has not opened one of them has come up a shut lane to watch a barrow of boards come off a wall and has not said why and is not going to.

"Not to anybody," the man of about thirty-eight said.

"Not to anybody, and there is no form for it, and the only two things anybody in four hundred miles would put on a page about this morning are a day and a number of boards, and there is a day on the post in chalk about a foot off where the two of them were standing, and neither of those is going on anything."

"Was that a decision."

"**That was not a decision anybody made this afternoon. That is just what there is. I came up the lane, I stopped at the boards, and I put nine of them in a barrow, and that is the whole of what I did, and I would like it noticed that I have not said one word this morning about anything that is not a board.**"

Nobody made a decision. That is the finding of the Friday and about four people on that lane have worked out since that nine boards came off a wall because a man came up a road, and that a man came up a road because it is a road he goes up, and that an arrangement for a Thursday held for about four weeks after the Thursday and then did not, and that the whole of what it cost to leave nine boards against a gate wall for a month instead of putting them somewhere else was a quarter of an hour and a piece of rope, and that the quarter of an hour was the man's and the rope was the man's and the wall has been standing there since before the water and is going to be standing there after.

---

The man of about thirty-one was still at the gate when the barrow came back past him.

"You are taking them up the shut road."

"I am taking them up the shut road to a roof about two miles the other way that has had nothing on it since the water."

"**I am taking them up the shut road to a roof about two miles the other way that has had nothing on it since the water, and it is where they have been wanted since about the forty-ninth of the Short Month by two people who are not on this lane and who did not send anybody, and I have not told them they are coming because a hand is not a page.**"

The man of about thirty-one looked up the shut road at the barrow and then down at the nine feet of bare stone at the foot of the wall where the boards had been, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has just looked at a wall, and that a wall with the shape of nine boards on it and about four inches of dry stone at the bottom is the only object in four hundred miles that anybody in this city has made without a page and cannot take back.

"Two people who did not send anybody."

"Two people who did not send anybody and a keeper at a gate who did not ask me, and that is the whole of the arrangement this morning, and there are three people in it and about nine people on this lane who have worked out that there is a fourth, and none of them is right."

The barrow went down the lane and the boards went with it, and the woman of about fifty put the piece of chalk in her apron pocket and the brass key was in the other pocket on its tarred cord and she did not take either of them out, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that the two most durable things on a road that nine households took back on the thirty-ninth day of the Short Month are a date in chalk on a post and a bar of ash in two brackets, and that both of them are in her hands inside her apron and one of them has been in the same pocket for nine years, and that the boards are not either of those and are gone.

The bar of ash went in at the sixth hour and the wind came round the corner and stopped as it always does when it is in, and the thirty-second was on the post and the twenty-ninth was above it, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a gate with nothing against the wall of it is a gate in a lane that nine households gave their days back out of a door about six weeks ago, and that a man with a barrow and a woman at a gate are the only two people in four hundred miles who have ever agreed about a thing on that lane without saying so, and that neither of them has ever mentioned it, and that neither of them is going to be asked.
