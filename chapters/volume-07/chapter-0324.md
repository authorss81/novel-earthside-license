# Chapter 0324 — Six Sentences, And The Sixth Is The One That Costs

A room over a market had the ninth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Wednesday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went along the floor and stopped at the table about the eighth hour, and the cold was out of the glass and in the crease of a sheet of road that had been folded and unfolded nine times in three days, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet with about nine places on it, a lump of chalk carried in one of the folds, a length of new rope over a chair back, and a box with a deed in it.

---

Tamsin Quill put the sheet on the table and turned it so that the fold showed, and did not open ita woman who has walked nine places in three days put a folded sheet on a table the way you put a thing down in front of a dog, and that the reason is that the fold is the part of it anybody can read from across a room.

She folded it long way in half, then in half again across, and the corner that came last went under, and she did it in about four seconds without looking at her hands, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a thing a person does without looking at their hands is a thing they have done since before the water, and that the woman who keeps a public register has watched her do it three times this week and has not asked and is not going to.

"There are nine places on that and eight answers, and the ninth line is empty because nobody came to a door, and I did not go back, and I have not written the word *no answer* on the ninth line for the reason I gave on the road and it is the reason I am going to give in about a minute if anybody makes me."

---

Nobody made her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the woman who has come into this room three mornings this week with a sheet of road in her coat has told the room in advance what she is going to say if she is asked, and that telling a room in advance what you will say under pressure is the one thing in four hundred miles that works and it is the only instrument any of them has that does not need a day on it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said his name, as she says a name when a person in a room is about to be on a page, and then said nothing else about him for the rest of the morning.

He had the pinned sheet in front of him and read the clerk's hand on it from the top down the way he reads everything, twice, and then turned it over and looked at the back of it, which is blank, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who reads a hundred-and-forty-year-old hand and then turns it over to look at the back of it is a man who has run out of the front.

Then he said the thing he had come to say, and he said it in about nine words, because he has been in about two hundred rooms and eleven kitchens this month and has never once been longer.

"**The line cannot be settled here. The only page in four hundred miles that says where it runs is in a town four hundred miles inland and nobody in this city can go and look at it. Somebody has to go and get it, and there are four people in this city who came here to fetch pages, and I have been in this room with all four of them at different times and I am not asking any of them whether they will go.**"

He put his hands in his coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has spent a month reading a hundred-and-forty-year-old clerk's hand has finally put a plan of his own on a table and that the plan is correct, and that the correctness of it is not in dispute and is not going to be argued about this morning.

Nobody improved on it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the seventh time in about ten days that a true thing has been said out loud in that room and had the room not argue with it, and that five of the seven have cost the person who said them something and this one has cost nothing yet and is about to.

---

The man of about fifty-seven took the rope off the chair back and put it over his arm, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in this room five times in about a fortnight has not moved a chair in a week and has now taken his own property off a chair back and put it on his arm, and that the difference is a man getting ready to leave a room and not doing it.

He said nothing. He had said nine sentences in this room a fortnight ago and four on the first day of that month and one on the way down a stair, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been given the whole of the argument out loud in a room with a date on the door has stopped talking in it, and that a man who stops talking in a room is a man who has begun to believe the room, and that nobody in this room is going to tell him he is right and nobody is going to tell him he is wrong.

She looked at him while he did it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the woman who has the floor looked at the only man in the room who has paid for a piece of ground and did not soften anything, and that not softening anything in front of that man is the whole of what the next six sentences are going to cost, and that he knew it before she started and put the rope on his arm so that he would be standing up when she finished.

---

**"A man who has paid for half a holding is not going to be answered by four hundred miles and a winter. The people who are going to pay for the not-answering are nine households on a lane, and they are the ones nobody in this room has ever had to answer to. A road that a man walks once in a good season is a road those nine households did not give back on the thirty-ninth day of that month, and they gave it back to nobody."**

She said the first three of them without stopping, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has walked nine places in three days has the first three sentences ready and that the readiness is the walk and not the room. Nobody in that room had any idea what the thirty-ninth of that month was until she said it, and about four of them had known and had been waiting to see whether she would.

The room did the thing rooms do, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has just spent three sentences on nine households on a lane has about three left and that the rest of them are going to be about a table in this city and not about a road.

**"So the line is settled here, by what is here. What is here is two accounts that disagree and a line that nobody has measured, and neither of those is going to change in a season. And the settlement is that it is not settled, and that is a thing that can be written down with a day on it, and a thing that cannot be written down cannot be argued about, and it has been argued about for forty years."**

She stopped, and about four people in that room have worked out since that six sentences in a room with a date on the door is the whole of what a woman who has been in about nine of these rooms came back with, and that the sixth of them is the one that costs, and that everybody in the room paid it and only one of them had to say it out loud.

---

The man of about forty-three had the deed and the sheet out of the box and both of them flat on the table and had not said a word for about four minutes, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man with nine boxes of other people's papers in front of him sat through a decision about a boundary without once touching the only page in four hundred miles that says where the line runs, and that this is the first time anybody in this city has done that with the page lying on the table.

"What you have said is that we write down that it is not settled."

"**What I have said is that not settled is a thing that can be written down, and that a thing that cannot be written down has been argued about for forty years, and that both of those are the same sentence and you are the only man in this room who has heard the second half of it and I am not going to say it twice.**"

Nobody wrote it down. The woman who keeps a public register was not in that room and about four people in that room have worked out since that the second half of that sentence has now been said in a room with a date on the door and that the person who keeps the register did not hear it, and that the decision of the ninth of that month is therefore a thing that exists in a room and not yet in a book, and that the two of those are different and only one of them is permanent.

---

The man of about fifty-seven stood up with the rope over his arm and said the only thing he said in that room all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been silent in a room for a week and gets up with his property in his hand is a man who is going to say something that costs him and not something that costs anybody else.

"**Then I have lost a season and I have still got a certainty, and I would like both of those entered in whatever this room writes down, and I am not going to say that anybody in this room is wrong, because there is nobody in this room who is wrong and I have checked.**"

He said it to the room and not to the door and did not say he was angry, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the fifth time in about a fortnight that a man who paid money has named a cost out loud before anybody asked him for it, and that on none of the five occasions was anybody in the room able to give him anything, and that the naming is the only thing he has that this city cannot send for.

Nobody argued with him and nobody told him he was wrong, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has just lost a season in a room with about nine people in it and has said out loud that he is not angry is the hardest person in four hundred miles to argue with, and that this room has known that for a fortnight and has not found a way round it and is not going to.

---

The woman who keeps a public register was not in that room and neither was the waxed slate in its case, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the two people who would have written the sixth sentence down were not in it, and that this was not arranged by anybody, and that it is the ordinary way a thing happens in this city, which is that two women of twenty-nine are never in one room and nothing in four hundred miles settles whether they are one woman.

Tamsin Quill put her hand flat on the folded sheet and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has just decided that nobody in this city is going to fetch a page four hundred miles inland has put her hand on the only document in the room with nine answers on it and eight of them came from people, and that the hand is not on the ninth line.

"The ninth line is empty and it is going to stay empty. **And I am not going to ask anybody to go, and I am not going to ask anybody whether they will go, and if anybody in this room has been waiting three days for me to ask them, I would like it entered that I knew I was not going to.**"

She put the chalk in the fold and folded the sheet the way she folds it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a decision about four hundred miles and a season and nine households was made in about six sentences by a woman who has been in three rooms this week. The man who brought the plan did not argue it and nobody argued with him, and he is standing at the back of the room with his hands in his coat, and nobody in that room has asked him one question about it, and that is the fifth morning in a row and she is keeping count of them in a book nobody else can read.
