# Chapter 0339 — He Will Look At It From The End Of It

A room over a shop on the low ground had the twenty-fourth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Thursday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in off the boards below and across the sill of a window with a bread board at the end of it, and the cold was out of the plaster and in the sleeve of a coat that had come eleven miles in the night and had not been off a man since before the light, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a case that was shut, a copy of an entry in a clerk's hand a hundred and forty years old pinned to the corner of a deed, a box with nine of nothing in it, and nine paces of floor.

---

The man of about fifty-two who digs came in at about the seventh hour and sat down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been putting willow in since before the light on eleven miles of open ground came eleven miles in the dark and got here before a man who has been in this city since before the water, and that neither of those two facts is about anybody's character and both of them are about what a person does when a thing is going to be asked of him.

He put his hands flat on the table, the way a man does before he starts on the thing he actually does, and then he took them off, and a man who has put his hands flat on a table and taken them off again before anybody in the room has finished sitting down has decided something about the table and not about the room.

Nobody asked him anything. About four people in that room have worked out since that a room in this city arrived at a rule by itself in about a fortnight and that the rule is that nobody in it is asked anything, and that a man who has walked eleven miles to be in one has been let into it and has not been asked one question in the first four minutes of it, and that about four people in that room have been waiting about a fortnight to find out whether the rule holds for him, and that it does not, because the rule is that nobody asks and not that anybody answers.

Then he said yes.

"**I will go and look at it. I will go on a day and I will stand at the end of it and I will look at it, and I am telling the room what I am going to do before I do it, because a man who has not said what he is going to do has not agreed to anything and has only been in a room.**"

He did not say when and nobody asked him, and a man who has come eleven miles to say yes in a room with a date on the door has said yes and has not given a day, and a day is the only thing anybody in this city has been able to put on anything for a fortnight, and that this man has found the way round all of it by not offering one.

---

Then he gave them the condition and about four people in that room have worked out since that he had it ready before he came in and that he gave the whole of it in one go without a break in it, and that a man who puts a condition on a thing he has already said yes to is a man who has been thinking about the shape of the work rather than the fact of it, and that this is the first person in four hundred miles in about a month who has come into a room and gone straight to the shape of a thing.

"**I will look at it from the end of it and I will not put my hands on it. That is the whole of the condition and I am not going to add to it and I am not going to take any of it off, and I would like it said in this room with a date on the door that I said it before anybody asked me for it and not after.**"

Nobody argued with it and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who digs has just told a room of about nine people that he will not put his hands on a mark in the ground, and that the trade of that man is putting things in holes, and that the only instrument in four hundred miles that would tell anybody anything about a mark in the ground is a rod, and that the man has a rod, and that he is not bringing it, and that nobody in this room asked him about the rod.

The reason is a trade and not a modesty, and it is this. A man whose whole life is putting things in holes is a man people hand holes to, and a man people hand holes to is a man who is not asked what is in the ground, he is asked to make a place for something. And a mark in the ground that was not made by anybody and is about a foot wide and about two inches deep and runs from the near end of a row of trees to a stone nobody put there is not a hole and it is not going to be one, and the trade does not have anything to say about it.

"**I can tell you whether the ground is hard at a place. I can tell you that from a yard off without putting a spade in. I cannot tell you anything about the top of a thing I have not stood at the end of, and I am not going to put my hands on it, and there is nobody in this room I would want to explain that to twice.**"

---

The man of about forty-three had the deed out and the sheet pinned to the corner of it, and the book was on the table shut, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers has had that book shut since the sixth of that month and has not opened it, and that the name is on a page already and it was read out loud once in a room with a date on the door and it took about four seconds, and that a man who read a name off an index in his own hand and put the book back on a shelf and would not say when he would open it again has held to that.

He said the reason out loud, which is what he does.

"**It is on a page. I read it and I said it once and a person wrote it down in their own head and did not put it on anything, and that is the correct way for a name to be in a book, and I am not going to open it again for about nine people.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps other people's papers has decided that the correct use of a name in four hundred miles is once, in daylight, and that he has now said so in a room with a date on the door in front of a man who has come eleven miles, and that the man who came eleven miles is the second person in this city to be told about that name and is the first one to have acted on it.

Adrian read the clerk's hand from the top down, both columns, and put a finger about a finger's width above the last line of the list and did not touch it, and a man who reads a page for a living has read the only page in four hundred miles that says where a line in the ground runs, from the top down, twice, and has not been asked one question about it by anybody, and this is about the eleventh morning running of that and about four of the nine people in this room have stopped finding it remarkable and about four have not.

He said one thing and it was about the shape of the thing and not about the line.

"**You have been asked to look at a mark in the ground and you have said you will not put your hands on it, and there is a rod in this world and it is yours and you are not bringing it, and I want to say one thing in this room and then stop, and it is that nobody in four hundred miles has ever agreed a thing with a person who was going to be asked about it afterwards,**"

---

And about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who said it is the first person anybody in this city has been asked in front of, and that he has put a condition on it before anybody could put one on him, and that a man who is agreed to in front of himself is the cheapest arrangement in four hundred miles and there is not one.

The room accepted the condition and the accepting took no longer than it takes to fold a sheet of road, and a room in this city has spent about a month refusing to ask anybody anything and has just agreed a thing in one breath with a person who volunteered a condition nobody had asked for, and this is the cheapest transaction anybody in this flood has been offered and that nobody in the room has worked out yet who is paying for it.

The finding is not the yes and it is not the condition. The finding is that the only instrument in four hundred miles anybody can think of for a mark in the ground is a man standing at the end of it with his hands behind his back, and that this city has been arguing about two wrong documents for a fortnight and has arrived, on a Thursday morning in a room over a shop, at a man who will tell them what the ground looks like from the end of it and from nowhere else, and that nobody here will be able to check any of it and that every person in this room has known that since the seventh hour and has gone on with the morning anyway.

Nobody was asked whether it would go. The man of about fifty-two who digs put his hands flat on the table a second time and left them there, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who puts his hands on a table in a room he has agreed to come to is a man who has accepted that the room is a place where things are done and not a place where things are agreed, and that this is the first arrangement anybody in this city has made with a man from outside it, and that the arrangement cost him one condition and the city got a man who will look at a thing from the end of it and report nothing that can be checked.

The woman of about thirty-three put the bread board back at the end of the sill at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a room in this city has two dates in chalk on her own door and nothing under either of them, and that a door with two dates on it and nothing underneath is still a notice and not a record, and that the twentieth of that month is the day a sheet came back to this sill with nothing at the top of it and it is four days old, and that the twenty-fourth is the day a man from eleven miles off is going to be in a room, and that the two of those are the whole of what this door has ever been asked to carry.
