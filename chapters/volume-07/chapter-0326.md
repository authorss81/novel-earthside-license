# Chapter 0326 — A Sheet With Nothing At The Top Of It

A room over a market had the eleventh day of that month in chalk on its door, a Friday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in on the north side at about the seventh hour and was off the low ground by the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was out of the glass and in the corner of a piece of paper that had been folded twice by somebody who folds everything twice, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it, a length of new rope over a chair back, a case with a deed in it, and about nine lines of pencil on a single sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it.

---

Nobody in that room had watched the sheet being written, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the first object anybody has put on that table in about a fortnight that did not come out of a box or a coat or a kitchen a mile off, and that it came in at about the seventh hour in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in the room, and that the room did not ask whose hand it was.

It was unsigned. There was nothing at the top of it. There were about nine lines on it in pencil, and the pencil had been pressed hard enough to be felt on the back of the sheet, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a person who writes a thing they mean with a pencil rather than a pen is a person who has decided in advance that this piece of paper is not going to last and is not sorry.

"Read it out."

"**I am going to read it out and I am not going to say whose it is, and I want that said in this room before I start, because the second I say a name at the top of that thing then it is a different object and I have handled about nine of those this month and I am tired of them.**"

---

She read it out in the local language because that is what a sheet gets read for, and it took her about a minute, and it was a question put in the plainest words anybody had heard in that room in about a month, and about four people in that room have worked out since that it did not have a single word in it that a person could have written for somebody else, and that is not a thing anybody can arrange and it is the whole of what the four days bought.

It said what was going to happen in a room on the fifteenth of that month. It said the day. It said that a person was wanted in a room and not a person with a trade, and that anybody might come and that nobody would be asked anything, and it said the last thing twice.

The last thing was that there is nothing at the top of this and that if it is lost it is lost, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a person writing the price of a thing on the thing itself is the oldest habit in four hundred miles and that the second oldest is not doing it, and that this sheet did the first one in nine lines and neither of those is the same as saying what the sheet is for.

---

"It says the fifteenth."

"**It says the fifteenth, and this sheet came into this room on the eleventh, at about the seventh hour, and that is four days, and the four days are the whole instrument and there is nothing else in the world that is worth doing about a person in four days' time.**"

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, who had come in with the sheet and put it down and not let go of it for about a minute, said the part of it that was his, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a question up and down four streets in this city for a month has waited nine days to say a thing he has known since before the water.

"A notice in a hand is not a page. A hand is not a page and I have been telling people that for nine years and nobody has ever wanted to hear it. **A sheet with my hand on it asking a person to do a thing can be argued with by anybody who wants to argue. A sheet with their own hand on it cannot be argued with at all, and that is not a good thing, and it is the only reason this works.**"

He said it to the table and did not say it to anybody, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has spent a month carrying other people's questions up hills has said out loud in a room with a date on the door that the instrument is worse at being argued with and better at working, and that the person who has to live with it being unarguable is the person who wrote it, and that person is not in the room.

---

The sheet had a place on it and not a house, and the place was eight houses in a row with a gap for a cart between two of them out past the last named house, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who walked nine places in three days got no answer at the ninth of them and wrote nothing on the ninth line, and that the ninth place is the place a sheet goes to and not the place a person goes to, and that those are two different facts and only one of them is convenient.

"There is no address on it."

"**There is a place on it and that is all a place is. Eight doors in a row and a gap for a cart, and anybody who stands in the gap can put a thing down and anybody who comes down the row can pick it up, and that is a place and it is not a house, and a house at the end of a lane past two gates is only a house and you cannot leave anything at a house without a name.**"

Nobody improved on that, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the second time in about a month that a room over a market has come to the difference between a place and a house, and that the first time was a woman of about fifty at a shut gate deciding on her own that a sheet on a wall was about nobody, and that she has not been told any of this and is not going to be.

---

The man of about fifty-seven stood at the step at the back with the rope over his arm and had not sat down all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who lost a season on the ninth of that month has been in this room on the tenth and the eleventh and has not asked for anything and has not said anything until this.

"What happens to it if nobody comes."

"**Nothing happens to it. It is nailed to a post in the gap for the cart or it is weighted with a stone or it is put on a door and it is left there, and the room on the fifteenth happens or it does not, and either of those is the answer and neither of them is a failure.**"

"That is not what I asked."

"Then ask it in a room on the fifteenth and you will get a better answer than this one, and I will not say that to you as a way of closing the morning, I am saying it because it is true."

---

She took the sheet down off the table and folded it once and put it inside her coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who folds everything twice folded a sheet that was not hers once, and that the once is the whole of what she is saying about it, and that a person who has kept nine places on one sheet for three days has a rule about folds and the rule does not travel.

The man of about thirty-one went out at about the second hour of the afternoon with it in the inside of his coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has said that a hand is not a page and that the sheet is unarguable is the man who is going to have to find out in about nine days whether the person who wrote it ever read it, and that he did not offer to take it and was not asked to and took it, and that this is the fourth thing this month a man has done in this city that nobody arranged.

It went out on the eleventh of that month at about the second hour of the afternoon and the room it was written for was on the fifteenth, and that is four days, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the whole of what this city has to spend on a person it cannot fetch four hundred miles is four days and a piece of paper with nothing at the top of it, and that the two of those are the same instrument and that the instrument is not a power and is not going to become one, and that four of the nine people in that room have worked out that they are going to spend the four days the way they spend every other day, which is by being in a room with a date on the door and no reason to be in it.
