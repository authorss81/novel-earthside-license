# Chapter 0335 — No Form At All, And A Board Off The Wall

The light came up flat out of the east across about eleven miles of open ground and there was nothing in front of it to stop it, and the cold was out of the north across all of that and not off a shoulder of anything, and the wind came up behind the light and had not stopped finding the gaps between the racks, and what was under about four pairs of hands on the twentieth day of that month was the back of a board off the wall of a hut, a stub of pencil about two fingers long, a spade with the top of its handle worn shiny in one place, and a barrow whose wheel had come off twice.

---

He was in the third row at about the fifth hour and he was not digging because the ground was easy, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who puts things in holes for a living does not choose the fifth hour and does not need to, and that this is the same sentence in the same order as it was on the sixth of that month and that the reason it has not been improved on is that there is nothing in it to improve.

There was a hut at the head of the third row with a stovepipe and a door that has never shut, and inside it against the north wall, standing up on its end in the corner where the wall is thickest, there was a rod. It is a length of ash about four feet long with a flat blade on the end of it, ground down on one side and not the other, and it stood there and it did not stand there any differently, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man who has been told he is going to be asked something in four days' time has not gone and got the only tool in four hundred miles that would tell him anything, and that not getting it is not a decision and is what a man does on the morning after he has heard.

There were nine willow in already and a bundle on the barrow and about a hundred yards of row left, and the light was off the far end of the row about a quarter of an hour before it was off the near end, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has been digging since before the light notices which end of a row the light leaves first, and a man who has been told something does not.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came out along the head of the row at about the seventh hour with nothing in his hands, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has said out loud in a room over a market that a hand is not a page came eleven miles with nothing, and that there is not one page in this city he could have brought, and that a man who carries things for a living walked eleven miles in the wind to stand on ground that is not his carrying nothing at all, which is the only errand in four hundred miles that has no object in it.

He did not go down the row. He stopped where the head of the row is and stood on the head of the row and did not come the nine feet off that another man once stood, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has been asked nothing in any of them knows exactly how close a man wants a stranger to be while he is working and can work that out in about four seconds standing still, and that he got it right.

"Nev Cutler."

The man of about fifty-two who digs went on putting willow in and did not look up, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a name is the only thing anybody has ever said out loud on this ground and it was said once in a room eleven miles off on the sixth of that month in about four seconds, and that the man it belongs to has not been told it was said, and that the two men standing on the head of a row about four hundred yards from each other are the only two people in four hundred miles who have both got it, and that one of them has not said a word.

"I am not going to ask you anything. **I want that said in front of about nine feet of ground before I say anything else, because I have carried four of these this month and every one of them went to a person who did not write it and I am not going to be the one who asks a fifth.**"

---

"**There is a room on the twenty-fourth.**"

He said it about four minutes later and he said it to the ground, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who was not asked anything has worked out in about four minutes that he was being told something, and that he has worked it out from a man standing on the head of a row with nothing in his hands, and that the working out was not difficult and the four minutes were his.

Then he went on putting willow in for about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has been told there is a room on a day has gone on doing his trade for about the time it takes to fill a pail and has not asked a single question, and that the not-asking is not rudeness and is the most expensive thing he has done in a fortnight, and that about four people on those flats have noticed that he put the ninth willow in wrong and took it out and put it in again, which he has not done in about nine years.

"**Four days.**"

"**Four days and there is a date on the room and there is nothing at the top of it and there is no name on it anywhere and that is the whole of what I have got, and I am not going to give you any of it in words because a thing I have said to you is a thing you are carrying for me and there is enough of that in this city.**"

He stopped. He took the spade out of the ground and set it down flat and stood with his hands on the handle.

"**And a question in my own hand is worth more than a question in yours, and the reason for that is nine years old and it is yours and not mine, and I am saying it on a row of racks eleven miles out because nobody in four hundred miles has ever said it in a room where the man it was about was standing nine feet off.**"

Nobody argued with it, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who carries things for a living has said out loud on eleven miles of open ground the thing he said out loud in a room over a market nine days ago, and that the reason he said it in the second place is that the man it was about was standing there, and that saying it to a room and saying it to a person are two different things and only one of them is the reason.

---

Then he sat down on the ground at the head of the row, which about four people on those flats have worked out since is not a thing anybody does at the head of a row of racks, and he had his coat off and out flat in front of him and his hands in it, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man who has been putting things in holes since before the light sat down on the ground to write a piece of paper, and that this is the correct way round and the only way he could do it with a spade and a barrow and a hut with a door that does not shut and no table in it at all.

"**Do not read it.**"

"I am not going to read it and there is nobody in four hundred miles who could not read it and me standing here, and that is the second thing about a hand and not a page that I have never been able to say in a room.**"

---

It took him about as long as nine holes, which is about nine minutes, and there was no form on eleven miles of flats to do it on and about four people on those flats have worked out since that the only page in four hundred miles with a person's name at the top of it is five lines long with nothing under the first line and it is in a city, and that it could have been sent out, and that nobody sent it, and that the reason nobody sent it is that a page with a name at the top of it is a page and this is not one, and that a man on a row of racks with a pencil stub was the right person to be asked and there was nobody to ask him.

The board was off the wall of the hut, about two feet by a foot and a half, planed on one face and rough on the other, and it went under his forearm and he wrote on the rough side, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that a man chose the rough side and not the planed one, and that the reason is that a pencil does not take on planed wood and does take on a sawn face, and that the whole of what he knew about writing he learned from the ends of things.

He wrote nine lines and there was nothing at the top of any of them, and about four people on those flats have worked out since that nine lines is about nine minutes and that nine minutes is about nine holes and that a man who puts things in holes for a living wrote a question to himself in the time it takes him to put nine things in nine holes, and that nobody on those flats has any idea what the nine lines say and that the not-knowing is the entire instrument and that it has been working since the eleventh of that month.

---

It went out on the twentieth day of that month at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and the room it was written for is on the twenty-fourth, and that is four days, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put it inside his coat and walked eleven miles in the last of the light and did not open it, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man who has carried four sheets in this month walked a fifth one eleven miles and did not open it, and that the one he was least entitled to open is the one he carried furthest.

The man of about fifty-two who digs went on putting willow in until the light was off the ground, and the door of the hut was open behind him and the board was leaning against the north wall where it had been, and the rod was where it had been, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the whole of what has happened to that man in this flood is that a man came eleven miles with nothing in his hands and told him there was a room on a day, and that the man wrote nine lines on the rough side of a board about nine minutes later, and that he was not asked anything, and that he did not ask anything, and that the rod is still against the thick wall and it is the only instrument in four hundred miles that could have answered any of it and he did not pick it up, and nobody expected him to, and he is not going to.
