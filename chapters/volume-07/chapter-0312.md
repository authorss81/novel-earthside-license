# Chapter 0312 — The Fifty-Seventh, And A Sheet That Came Down

A barrow of boards was still standing against the wall of a shut gate on the fifty-seventh day of the Short Month, a Friday, and the light came in on the north side of that lane and stopped at the boards and did not get to the gate, and the cold was out of the wind and in the crease of a folded sheet of paper that had been on a wall since the forty-sixth of that month, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a barrow, boards, a stone about the size of a fist, a bar of ash in two brackets, a wooden hook over its handle, and a piece of chalk.

---

She put the fifty-seventh on the post at about the seventh hour and she did not rub anything out, and the fifty-sixth was above it and the fifty-fifth above that, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a keeper who writes a date on a post every morning and has never rubbed one out is doing the one thing on that road that a person can do again tomorrow, and that it is also the one thing that is going to run out first.

The brass key was in the apron pocket on its tarred cord and it had been in the apron pocket every morning for nine years, and the woman of about fifty did not take it out and look at it, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a key somebody does not look at is a key that is only ever going to be looked at by a hand that has learned where it is.

The sheet was on the wall about four feet up on the shaded side, with the stone on the top corner of it, and it had been there since the forty-sixth and the room it was written for had been on the fiftieth, and nobody had come up that lane for it and nobody had sent anybody.

She had said out loud on the day she went up there that she had not read it and was not going to read it, and that reading it would be the beginning of having been asked.

---

The wind came round the corner of the gate at about the second hour of the afternoon and got under the bottom of the fold, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a thing that has been on a wall eleven days has a fold in it that has learned which way the wind goes, and that the fold was made by a woman who watched nine people fold sheets that week and folded hers the other way on purpose, and that the purpose is on this lane now and the paper is on its face in a puddle.

It went off the wall and about a foot and then a hand's width more, and the stone came off it and stayed where it was, and the sheet went down on the wet at the foot of the wall and lay there.

She worked the gate down four inches, put her hand flat on the post and found the draught, and put the bar back in, and it stopped, and she then went and picked the sheet up off the wet stone, and she did it in the order a person does things and not quickly.

The sheet was soaked through one corner and about half dry. She stood with it in both hands and it came apart along the fold as she opened it, and she read it.

There were about nine lines on it in a careful hand and there was nothing at the top of it, and it said what was going to happen in a room on the fiftieth day of that month, and it said the day.

She read the whole of it. She turned it over. The back of that one was blank, and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a sheet with a blank back is a sheet nobody has answered and that there is a second piece of paper in this city with a question written on the back of it in a hand that is not this one, and that the woman of about fifty has not seen it and is not going to see it and did not know until about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the fifty-seventh that it existed.

"Well." She said it to the sheet, and the wind had the rest of it. "**There is no name at the top of it, and there is nothing in this that is about this gate, and I have been right for eleven days and I was right for the wrong reason.**"

She stood in the lane with the wet end of it hanging out of her hand and about four people on that lane have worked out since that the sentence a woman says to a gate at the fourth hour of the afternoon is the sentence she has been carrying, and that what she had been carrying was that a sheet on a wall is a thing that is about her, and that a sheet with no name on it is a thing that is about nobody and could not be about her whatever it said.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came down the lane at about the fifth hour with the barrow empty and the rope hooked over the handle where he had put it back, and he did not stop at the gate.

He put his hand flat on the boards for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and took it off again and went on down the lane, and he did not say anything, and she did not say anything, and nobody was sent and nobody asked and about four people on that lane have worked out since that a man who stood at a wall and said four sentences and was not thanked is the only man in four hundred miles nobody can ask to do a second thing, and that the boards are still against the wall and are going to be against the wall on the fifty-eighth and on the fifty-ninth.

Then she put the sheet back up.

She dried the wet end of it against the inside of her apron for a while first, and she put the fold back in the way it had been, and she put the stone on the top corner, and the stone had not gone anywhere and she did not look for it.

"**I am going to leave it up. That is a decision and I have made it at a gate and not to anybody, and the reason is that a paper with nothing at the top of it cannot be taken off a wall on behalf of a person, and there is nobody to take it off for, and a sheet that has been on a wall for eleven days past the day it was written for is not a sheet anybody is waiting on.**"

She put her hand flat on the post and left it there, and the sentence she had been holding behind that one came out afterwards and flatter, and it was the only thing she said all afternoon that was not a decision.

"**And I have read it, and I have not been asked, and those are two separate facts and I am keeping them apart on purpose.**"

---

The lane did the thing it does at that hour, which is nothing, and about four people who have lived on it have worked out that between the fifth hour and the dark there is no light on that lane at all and the houses do not open and the second house from the bottom puts its curtain across about an hour after the rest, and that all of that is a lane's ordinary habits and is not an agreement and has never been one.

At the fourth hour the boards were against the wall and the sheet was on the wall and the fifty-seventh was on the post and the fifty-sixth was above it and the bar of ash was in its two brackets and the hook was over its handle.

Nobody on that lane was angry with anybody and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that the two most durable objects on a road that nine households took back are a question nobody signed and a barrow of boards nobody took away, and that a keeper of fifty has now read one of them and left it up, and that the leaving-up is the only decision anybody has made about a page on that road in about a month, and that it was made to a post.
