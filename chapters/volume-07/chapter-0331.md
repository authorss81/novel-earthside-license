# Chapter 0331 — Nine Words, And He Does Not Say Them

A room over a shop on the low ground had the sixteenth day of that month in chalk on its door, a Wednesday, and about nine people in it, and the light came in off the boards below through a window somebody had painted shut and somebody else had scraped most of again, and the cold was out of the scrape and in the straw of a bag that had been on a road since the seventh hour, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it, a bread board put down at the end of a sill, a single sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it, and a bag with a piece of chalk and a cloth in the top of it.

---

The man of about twenty-five came in before the light and sat down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in a city since before the water learns to sit the moment he comes through a door, because standing about is what a person does when they have come to be asked something, and he has not been asked anything in this city since the first day of it.

His left boot was mended at the front with a length of cord that is not new, and a boot mended with somebody's spare cord in a town where there has been no shop open for a month is a boot that was mended by the person wearing it, indoors, by a light, and a man who has been in a city since before the water and has not been given anything by anybody has at least mended his own.

Nobody said anything for a while. The bread board went back to the end of the sill, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about thirty-three who keeps a room over a shop and has a board on the sill of it put the board down and not up again, and that putting it down at the end of a meeting is a thing a person does and putting it back at the end of a morning is a thing a person does when the morning has changed.

Then the man of about twenty-five put a sheet on the table face down, and about four people in that room noticed the face down and about four of them had seen the same thing on the same table four days before, and that the two men who did it are not the same man and have never spoken.

"**That is mine and I wrote it and I am not going to have it read out.**"

He put his hand flat on it and left the hand there, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a sheet with a person's own hand on it cannot be argued with by anybody, and that the man who wrote it has just found out that the same rule works on the writer, and that the writer is the only person in four hundred miles who cannot get at it.

Tamsin Quill had the fold of her own sheet showing and did not open it, and a woman who has kept a sheet of road folded the same way since before the water sat four feet off a man who had just used the same idea for a different reason, and neither of them said so and about four of the nine people in that room worked it out separately and got to the same place about it.

"Nobody is going to read it out and nobody is going to ask you about it."

"**I am not going to be asked about it. I said that on the Saturday in the other room and I have not stopped saying it and I would like the room to have heard me say it twice before anybody decides it is a rule.**"

Nobody argued with him, and this is the fifth morning running that somebody in this city has said a thing out loud in a room with a date on its door and had the room not argue with it, and the reason the room does not argue is that it has understood, over about a fortnight, that the people who come into these rooms are not people who have come to be asked anything, and that a room which works that out about itself is a room that cannot do the one thing it was built for.

---

He said yes at about the ninth hour, and it took him about four minutes to get to it and nobody helped him and nobody pushed, and the whole of what went on for those four minutes was a man of about twenty-five turning a piece of paper over and putting his thumb on a line of it and not reading it.

"**Yes. And I want it written down that I said it and not that I was asked, because nobody asked me, and because the difference is the whole of it and I have been in this city a long enough time to know that a thing that was asked can be said to have been agreed to.**"

Nobody wrote it down in that room, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a room which has wanted a sentence entered for a fortnight has had a sentence offered to it in the right shape and has not taken it, and that this is the first time in about a fortnight that a person has stood in a room in this city and asked for a thing not to be entered, and that the woman who asked on Saturday, in a room over a market, put a sheet thirty years old on a table and would not let anybody have it, and that the two of them are not the same and the asking is the same.

"Then what is in it."

"**Nine words, and they say one thing about me, and it is mine, and I am not going to say it here.**"

He did not say anything else about it, and a man who has told a room the length of a thing has given the room the only figure they will get out of him this morning, and nine words is a short thing, and that a person who has been in a room for two hours and has given a number and not the number's contents has been more forthcoming than anybody who has spoken all morning.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had been at the back of the room with his hands in his pockets since before the light and had not said one word, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has carried a question up and down four streets in this city for a month waited nine days to say a thing he had known since before the water, and that this morning he waited two hours, and that a man who carries things for a living is the only person in four hundred miles whose profession is to arrive and say nothing, and that the waiting is the trade and not a way of being polite.

He came forward at about the ninth hour and put his hand on the sheet the man of about twenty-five had left face down, and did not turn it, and a man who has said out loud in a room over a market that a hand is not a page and a sheet with a person's own writing on it cannot be argued with at all put his hand on that sheet this morning without knowing what was in it, and that this is the whole cost of the instrument and it has been standing in this city for a fortnight and nobody has looked at it.

"I carried nine of these out on the Saturday."

"**I took one of them off the post in the gap and I read it and I put it back and I have had it in the inside of my coat for four days, and I want the room to know that I did not put a name at the top of mine and I did not add anything to it, and that I have not told anybody in this city that I read it and I am telling this room.**"

The room did not make a sound, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man of about twenty-one walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth line, and that a man of about twenty-five has spent four days in the inside of a coat with a sheet of his own and has told nobody, and that these are two people doing the same arithmetic about a page in this city and neither of them knows the other exists.

"You had it. All four days."

"**I had it all four days. You went nine places and put nine up and came back and told the room it had gone to nine, and I was one of the nine, and I have been sitting on a chair in a room over a market for four days telling myself a sheet is not the same as a person, and I have had the sheet the whole time and it has not once been the same as a person and it has also not once stopped being mine.**"

Nobody improved on that, and a man who has said that a hand is not a page has just been told what a hand is, by the hand, and the telling cost nothing and took four days, and that the four days were spent on both sides of the same instrument, and that neither of them knew.

---

Tamsin Quill said one thing at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and she had been in the room for nine hours with a fold in her coat and had not used it, and a room which cannot send four hundred miles has about four minutes of work in it on an ordinary morning, and that this one had had about nine hours.

"**Then the four days are two four days and not one, and I would like that said, because we have been counting them off the top of this table since the eleventh and the days a sheet is in a bag and the days it is in a coat are the same days and they are not the same days, and about nine people in this room have spent a fortnight being careful about the first kind and nobody has been careful about the second.**"

She said it to the table and not to the man, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who leads decisions in this city has just said out loud in daylight with a date on the door that the instrument everybody in this flood is proudest of has a half nobody was counting, and that the half is the half where a person has the thing in his own pocket and has to decide what to do about it by himself, and that this is not an argument against four days and she did not make it one.

The man of about twenty-five looked at her for the first time that morning.

"**That is the first useful thing anybody has said in this city about a piece of paper, and it is nine years old, and I have not said it back and I am not going to.**"

Nobody in that room said anything about that, and two people in this city have said out loud, in the same room, on the same morning, that a thing is nine years old and is theirs and is not going to be argued about, and one of them said it to a sheet of road and the other to a person, and that the fracture between them is a month old and is not in this room and is not going to be mended in this room, and that nobody apologised in it and nobody was going to and it was not a fault and nobody in that room has ever thought it was one.

---

He went at about the fifth hour and the cord on the front of his boot had gone further round the toe over four days and had to be pulled round to get the boot off, and a man who has said yes in a room with a date on the door and has not said what he wrote has a boot that is going to have to be mended again before the weather turns, and this is the only part of the morning anybody in that room is certain of.

The bag with the chalk and the cloth in the top of it went out with the man of about thirty-one and it was lighter than it had been that morning by one sheet, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the second of the four people who have not left is in a room in this city with a day on the door and has said yes without being asked, and that four days after a bag of nine went out on a Saturday a man has told a room that he was one of the nine, and that the room had been counting the instrument from the wrong end for a fortnight and a man with a bag in the inside of his coat has corrected it standing up, in nine sentences, without being asked a single thing.
