# Chapter 0305 — A Tin, And A Question In A Woman's Own Words

A sheet of paper with nothing on the top of it was lying on a kitchen table on the fiftieth day of the Short Month, a Friday, and a woman of about sixty-nine had not touched it, and the light came in low at a window about as wide as a hand and went off the flagstones before the second hour of the afternoon, and the cold was in the flags and out of the draught under the door and in the tin, and what was under about two pairs of hands that morning was a tin, a folded sheet thirty years old, a bowl with the lid on it, and a table with one leg shimmed by a piece of split ash.

---

A man of about thirty-one had put the sheet on the table at about the sixth hour and had gone out of the room and had shut the door behind him, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who puts a piece of paper on a table and goes out of the room and shuts the door is a man who has decided that the paper is about to be answered and that he has no business in the room where it is answered, and that there are about nine kitchens in this flood in which a stranger has put a paper down and stayed, and that a man who goes out of the room has been to a great deal more trouble than a man who stays.

She did not touch it for the first hour.

She put the fire in, and she moved the bowl, and she did the things a person does in a kitchen at six in the morning when there is a stranger's paper on the table, and the table with the shimmed leg rocked every time she put her weight on the near side of it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who works around a rocking table for an hour instead of putting a folded piece of ash under the bad leg has decided that the rocking is the table she has, and that this is the same decision a person makes about a person.

He had been standing in the doorway with the box of nails under his arm since the sheet was put down, and he had not come in further, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who delivers a question and then stands in the doorway has already decided he is not in the room the answer gets written in.

"You have not touched it."

"I have not touched it and I am going to touch it and I want to know one thing before I do, and I want you to tell me the difference between two things and I want you to be exact, because I am sixty-nine and I have a right to be exact."

She did not ask it straight off. She put both hands flat on the table on the two sides of the sheet, which stopped the rocking for as long as she kept them there.

"Ask."

"**Am I being asked something, or am I being told something. Those are not the same and a person of my age has had both of them done to her and only one of them lets her keep hold of it.**"

"You are being asked something. It goes to a place and everybody at the place is being asked the same thing in the same words, and the words are not yours, and there is no name at the top of it, and you get four days. It is the fiftieth today. The room is on the fifty-fourth, and the fifty-fourth is a Tuesday."

He counted them on his fingers without looking at them, which is a thing a man does when he has counted the same four about nine times.

"Four days."

"Four days, and you can do whatever you like with them, and I am not going to ask whether the fiftieth suited you."

---

She read the sheet then, all of it, twice, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman of sixty-nine who cannot read fast read a stranger's paper twice anyway, and that the second reading is not for the words and is for the shape of them, and that a person who has lived on a lane for thirty years can tell from the shape of a page whether anybody in the room it came from has ever been up this lane.

"It is written well."

"It is written well and it is written by a person who is careful."

"It is written by a person who has never been up this lane in a cart, because there is nothing up this lane a cart can get down, and it says *the holding*." She put the sheet down flat and put two fingers on the word. "**It does not say whose holding. There are about four of us on this lane and every one of us could be written about and not one of us is named and not one of us has been asked whether the word is the right word, and I am not complaining, I am asking whether that is on purpose.**"

The bowl with the lid on it came to the boil about then and she did not get up for it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman will let a pot go over rather than break a sentence she has decided the wording of.

"It is on purpose."

"Then I will tell you what is on purpose and what is not, and I will do it in four days, because I have a tin on that shelf and there is a thing in it and it is thirty years old, and I am not going to open it for a person who has come into my kitchen at six in the morning and asked me to be exact." She did not look at the shelf. "**And you are not going to ask me what is in the tin, and I would like it said that you did not ask me, and I would like it said in a room and not in my kitchen, and I am not going to ask you for that either.**"

---

He did not ask her. He picked the box of nails up off the flags where he had put it down outside the door, and he looked at the table leg, and he did not ask her.

"**I was not going to ask you what is in the tin, and the reason is that there is a person in this room who is not one of the people the question is about, and a person nobody asks anything is a person who cannot be put in a column.**"

"You have been in nine kitchens."

"Eleven, and about two hundred rooms, and the kitchens are the ones where a person writes something down." He put the box down by the door again, which is what he had come back for. "**I am the one who carries it. I am not one of the four this is about and I am never going to be one of the four, and that is the only reason I am standing in your kitchen, and it is not a reason anybody can put on a page.**"

"**Then say that in a room with a date on the door and not in my kitchen, and I will say the same about you in one, and neither of us will sign it, and in about nine days a clerk will have nothing.**"

The man of about thirty-one said that was the arrangement, and she said it was the arrangement, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that two people agreeing inside about nine days about the price of a sheet of paper is the whole of what a lane has got this month, and that it is not nothing, and that it took about nine years for the woman of about fifty at the gate to arrive at the same sentence with a different set of things in it.

---

She wrote the question at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with her own hand on the back of the sheet that had been left on her table, and she did not sign it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who writes on the back of somebody else's question has decided that her question is an answer to something and not a second question, and that this is the first time in about a month that a thing put into this flood has been answered by a person rather than by a form, and that nobody has entered it anywhere.

"The ground on this lane has been in two pieces since before I came to this house and the paper in that tin is thirty years old and nobody has ever told me a different number and I am not going to start now. What I want written down is that there are two accounts of where the line runs and neither of them has a person in it, and that if somebody comes up this lane to look for a line I want them to come to my door first and I want them to come without a chain."

"That is a question and it has a person in it."

"**It has a person in it and the person is me and I am sixty-nine and I live at the end of this lane, and a question in your own words is worth nine times a question in mine because the words are the ones that have to be lived with, and that is nine years old and it is not mine, it is the man who carries them.**"

Her brother's son came in at about the fifth hour with mud to the knee on him and stood in the doorway and did not come further in, and he is a man of about sixty-three and he farms the half of it, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who stands in a doorway with mud on him in a house where an old woman has a paper on the table has come for a reason and is going to give it in four words.

"The ditch wants water and there is none."

Neither of them looked at the sheet, and the man of about thirty-one was still by the door with the box, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that two people can stand in a kitchen and talk about a ditch and neither of them can look at a piece of paper.

"That is not the question."

"It is not your question and it is the only one going to get answered, aunt, and the channel has had nothing in it since the day the causeway opened and the ground in the middle of that half is not the ground at this end of it, and I am not going to say more than that in a house with a paper on it."

"That is four sentences and every one of them is about a ditch."

"It is four words and two of them are yours and they are the right ones, and I am going to take my coat off and I am going to eat, and there is a thing going to happen to that middle before the season turns and it is not going to happen because anybody writes it down."

---

He said those four words and went out and did not shut the door, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man of sixty-three who comes in out of a wet ditch to say four words and then goes and eats is a man who has decided that a question about ground is worth four words and a bowl of something, and that this is the first person on that lane in about a month who has had an opinion about where the line is and has said it in nine words and left, and that the opinion was about a ditch and not about a line, and that nobody has asked him which of the two he thinks he was talking about.

The four days ran from that morning to the fifty-fourth of that month, and the woman of about sixty-nine kept the sheet in her own hand and did not give it back and did not sign it, and the man of about thirty-one put it inside his coat and did not look at it and did not read it upside down in a cart, which a woman in another lane on that road has said out loud he would not do, and he said so himself, once, and it is on a page.

And at about the eighth hour of that evening he was a quarter of the way down that lane with nothing in his hands, and the light had gone off the low ground behind him, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that he had been thinking about a question in a tin and had not been thinking about the sheet in his coat, and that he said so out loud to the lane, in his own words, and that what he said was that he had been in eleven kitchens this month and that in nine of them somebody had asked him a question and in two of them nobody had, and that he could not remember which two, and that that was the whole of what he knew about it, and that it was enough to be going on with.
