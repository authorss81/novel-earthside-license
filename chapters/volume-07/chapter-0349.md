# Chapter 0349 — A Fold, And A Room With Nothing On The Table

A room over a shop on the low ground had the thirty-fourth day of that month written in chalk on its own door, a Sunday, and about four people in it, and the light came in at about the eighth hour through a window somebody had painted shut and somebody else had scraped most of again and did not get past the middle of the floor, and the cold was out of the scrape and in the fold of a sheet of road a woman had been carrying in the inside of her coat since before the light, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a fold, a bread board put down at the end of a sill, a slate on a shelf in the next room, and about nine paces of floor nobody was standing on.

---

A room with about four people in it and nothing to put on the table is the ordinary condition of a room on a Sunday in the fourth week of a month with no length on it. The four minutes of work a room has on an ordinary day in this flood went somewhere else in about a fortnight, and nobody took it, and nobody knows where it went. A room which has been emptied of work is not a room that has failed at anything.

The register is not in the room. The deed is not in the room. The man of about forty-three was not in the room and the man of about fifty-seven was not in the room and the man of about thirty-four was not in the room. The seven people this city has been putting in rooms since the ninth of that month have all gone somewhere else to be on a Sunday, and none of them was sent, and a room which has about four people in it is the only kind of room this city has left.

Tamsin Quill had the fold showing.

A woman who has folded a sheet the same way since before the water was sitting in a room with the fold turned towards her, and it had been in the inside of her coat since before the light. A person holding a fold like that is waiting for a road to be a road again and not for anything to happen to her, and nobody in that room was going to get either of those out of her on a Sunday morning, and about four of them had stopped trying about a week ago.

---

The man who came in with her had not said a word since the stair.

He has been in about two hundred rooms in this city and has been asked nothing in about nine of them and has said almost nothing in the rest. A man who walked nine miles up the top of a road on the twenty-first day of that month and stopped twice and did not say why has been next to that woman for the length of a morning without asking her for one thing. This is not the same as a man who has stopped minding. The two of them know the difference, and about four of the four people in that room do not and are not going to be told.

"I am not going to ask you for the sheet."

"**I know you are not, and you have not asked me for it since the second day, and I have been carrying it since the fifteenth, and I have noticed every day, and I am not asking you why, and I would like it said in this room that I said I was not asking and that it is Sunday.**"

"Nineteen days is not a Sunday."

He looked at the window with the scrape in it for a while and about four people in that room worked out that a man who has been asked nothing in nine rooms has just been given a count by a woman and has not said one word about it.

"It is a Tuesday and a Sunday and every day in between, and I have counted them, and you have not asked me for one of them, and I want it said in this room that I know what the counting is for and that the counting is mine and not yours."

"What is it for."

"That is the one thing I am not going to say, and I have not said it since the fifteenth and I am not going to say it to a man who has not asked me anything for nineteen days."

The woman of about twenty-one had the ninth line of the sheet of road in front of her and the man of about forty-eight had his knife halfway shut, and about four people in that room worked out at the same moment that three of the four people who came to this city behind a question were in it.

"There are three of us in this room."

"There are three of us in this room and there is a fourth of us who is not in it, and neither of those two sentences is going to get the fourth of us asked, and I want it said on a Sunday that nobody in this room is going to say what she said on the fourteenth and nobody is going to ask her about it and neither of us is going to say that it was a defeat."

"**It is not a defeat and it is not a sulk and it is four words and she said them before anybody asked her, and I have been in three rooms since the fourteenth and not one of them has improved on them, and about four people in this city have worked out that the whole of what is left is a woman who is not coming and a thing nobody is going to soften.**"

Nobody said anything. A woman put a whole nineteen-day thing on a table on a Sunday and the man beside her said nine words, and that the room did not ask either of them a second thing, and that there are two people in this city who have agreed not to mend something between them and neither of them has said the word mend out loud since the first week of the Bare Month and both of them know exactly what is on the table, and that it is still on the table, and that it is going to be on the table on Monday.

The ninth line of that sheet has nothing on it. She did not put the ninth place in the nine and nobody saw her leave it out and everybody in that room understood it inside about four minutes, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one walked nine places in three days and wrote nothing on the ninth line, and that the two of them have been doing the same arithmetic about a page in this city for about a month and that neither of them knows the other exists.

---

The woman of about twenty-one came in at about the ninth hour and sat down and did not stop, which is what she does.

She had the pen with the nib ground narrow in her hand and she has carried it into every room she has gone into since the fifteenth of that month and has not written anything with it in any of them. A person who cuts a pen down herself so that it will make the mark she wants has brought it into a room on a Sunday with nothing on the table and has not put it down, and there is nothing to put it on, and the only sheet in this city with anything on it is her own, and it has nothing at the top of it.

"I have stopped waiting to be told what I am for, and I have been not waiting for about a fortnight, and I want it said in this room that the not-waiting is not a mood and that nobody in here is going to change it by saying something."

Nobody said something.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about twenty-one has said out loud in a room with a date on the door on the fifteenth of that month that she had stopped waiting to be told what she is for, and that nobody argued with it then, and that she has stood in a doorway on the eighteenth of that month with a stick of hazel in her hand belonging to somebody else and said nothing at all, and that the not-saying and the saying are the same decision, and that this time is not a complaint and is the ordinary condition of a person who is on a page and not going anywhere, and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to tell her and that nobody in four hundred miles is going to ask her either, and that the two of those are this city's whole method and that she is the first person in it who has noticed.

---

The man of about forty-eight was in the room with a folding knife in his hand and a sheet of paper in his coat, and he opened the knife twice with his thumb and shut it and the hinge went about halfway and stopped. He came into a room on the seventeenth of that month and said yes before anybody in that room had finished asking and has not been in a room since. He is in this one, and he is on no roll, and a man who farms a holding and has a question behind him and is going nowhere is the exact shape of the four of them.

The woman of about twenty-one and the man of about twenty-five had the ninth line of a sheet of road between them on the table and neither of them had put it there in front of anybody.

"That is the ninth line and there is nothing on it."

"There is nothing on it because I did not put my ninth place in, and everybody in this room saw me leave it out and nobody said one word about it, and you have been sitting on your own sheet for about a fortnight with a thing in it that you have not shown anybody."

"**I have not shown it to anybody and I am not going to show it to you, and I have been in three rooms in a fortnight and in all three of them there is a thing I could say that would make me the person who said the thing, and I have not said it in any of them, and you are the first one in this city who has worked out that I have not.**"

"I have not worked it out. I have watched."

The man of about forty-eight had the knife out by then and was opening and shutting it about halfway with his thumb, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who farms a holding is the only one of the three of them who is doing anything at all with his hands, and that a man with nine words in a coat and a woman with a pen she has cut down are the two people in this room who are carrying the whole of what they came for and not using any of it.

"Watching is working it out. It is the slow way and about nine people in this city do it, and you have been doing it in a room for about a fortnight without saying a word, and that is the whole of what I have been able to look at."

The man of about twenty-five was in the room too and had not opened his coat. There are nine words in the inside of that coat that he wrote himself on the eleventh of that month. He was standing in a room on Sunday with a person beside him when a word came off a slate in a cloth, and he did not say them then and has not said them since. Nobody has asked him. A room which has spent a month not asking anybody is the only room in four hundred miles where nine words can stay in a coat for as long as a man wants them to, and nobody in that room knows what is in the coat, and the man who is not asked is in a better position than anybody who is.

---

The road north of the Marches is shut.

It has been shut since the thirty-ninth day of the Short Month and nobody shut it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a road can be shut for as long as it likes without anybody deciding anything, and that a road which has not been shut by a person is not a decision and is not a punishment and is not an argument, and that it is a road, and that it is going to be a road on Monday, and that there is no form in four hundred miles on which anybody could enter the day it opens and that nobody is going to be told.

Nobody in that room was asked whether anything was going to work, and nobody in that room said anything about whether it was, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a city which has spent six weeks learning not to ask anybody anything has got to the point where it does not even ask itself, and that this is a Sunday it has done that, and that on the two Sundays before this one somebody said out loud that they were not going to be asked, and that this morning nobody said that either, and that the bread board was at the end of the sill and the slate was on a shelf in the next room and the ninth line of a sheet of road had nothing on it and the three of them had a pen and a knife and nine words and a sheet apiece that went nowhere, and that this is what a room looks like when it has stopped asking, and that it is not empty.
