# Chapter 0316 — A Name, In A Room, In Daylight, With A Number On The Door

A room over a market had a number on its door in chalk on the first day of a month this city had not got, a Tuesday, and the number was the three hundred and sixteenth day counted from the water and nothing else, and about nine people were in the room, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and went past the middle of the floor for the first time in about forty days, and what was under about two pairs of hands that morning was a leather bag, a pen, a sheet of paper with a line ruled at the top of it, and a cloth.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had written the number on that door herself at about the seventh hour, in chalk, on the place where the months had gone, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she did it alone and did not tell anybody she was going to, and that the first thing she said in the room was about it, which is her way.

**"There is no form for today and I have written the count of the days instead, and I have written it because a number a person put on a door is a date until somebody puts a better one on it, and I would like that said in this room once, and I would like it said once.**"

She put the chalk down on the table beside the second slate and did not pick it up again, and the second half of what she had to say took the shape of an admission, and she made it flatly and without help from anybody.

"**It is not a form. Nobody in four hundred miles can check it against anything, and if I am wrong about it then every day that goes on after today is wrong on the same count, and that is the cheapest wrong anybody in this city is going to make this month.**"

Nobody argued with her about it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register has chosen a number over a blank space, and that a blank space on a door is the one thing in this flood that nobody in four hundred miles has ever had to explain, and that she has given up the cheapest thing in the world so that a notary can see what a room looks like when it has nothing.

---

Sera Vail came in at about the eighth hour and put the bag on the table and opened it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who left a bag shut on Saturday opened it on a Tuesday, and that the room had a date to work towards and had spent a Sunday and a Monday not looking at it.

She said the cost again in one sentence, because she had said it on Saturday to a room that had not yet run out of anything, and this was the other one. Her name goes at the top of it. The copy of it in every room in four hundred miles that has a table and a clerk's habit. And if the days do not stop where the word on it stops, the paper is wrong, and she is the wrong, and she will be the person somebody writes to about it for as long as the paper lasts.

"**Do it. And I am not going to argue with the cost, because you named it before you were asked and that is the whole of what I have been asking anybody to do for nine years.**"

---

The notary took the sheet out of the bag and put it flat and ruled a line at the top of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who came into a room with a number on the door instead of a month has understood the door in about four seconds and has not asked what the number means, and that this is a thing about a person and not about a notary.

She began.

And the man of about fifty-seven, who had a length of new rope he had not used over his arm and had carried into the room and had not put down, stood up off the wall he had been leaning on.

"**No.**"

The room did not make a sound, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in five rooms in about nine days and has said nine sentences in one of them stood up off a wall in a room with about nine people in it and was not asked to.

He gave the reason in about four sentences and none of them was about the calendar, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been in five rooms in nine days and has said nine sentences in one of them had about four sentences left and spent them on this.

"**I have paid money for half a holding and I cannot show anybody where the half is, and that is the whole of my standing in this room and the only standing in it anybody has paid for. And a word made in a room is a word somebody can be asked to have made, and I am the man who will be asked, because there is nobody else here who could be asked about a boundary and be asked about it honestly.**" He did not take the rope off his arm.

"**I am not angry. I have not been angry for a long time. I would like it said in this room that I have said this out loud in daylight, and that nobody has to do anything about it.**"

Nobody answered him, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is now the third time in about a week that a person in a room in this city has said a true thing out loud and had the room not argue with it, and that a room which does not argue is a room that has agreed the thing is true and has not agreed what to do, and that the man of about fifty-seven has been in every one of those three.

---

She put her hand flat on the sheet and did not pick the pen up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a notary who has been refused does not look at the person who refused her and does not hurry, and that she waited until the light had come off the step at the back and reached the table, and that about four of the nine people in that room have thought since that the whole of what happened on that morning happened inside about a minute of moving light.

"**The Bare Month.**"

She said it once, in the local language, in daylight, in a room with a number on the door, and about nine people in that room heard it, and nobody asked her a second question about it, and about four of them did not because they had worked it out before she had finished saying it, and a word said once in this room is a thing that has to be written down before anybody is allowed to be curious about it.

The man of about fifty-seven asked how long it was, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he is the only person in it with any standing to ask a notary a question about a month, and that he asked it anyway, standing up, on the same morning he had said no to the making of one.

"**I am not going to say. And I want it entered in this room that I was asked, on the first day of it, by a person, in front of about nine people, and that I declined, and that the declining is mine and is not a thing anybody has to talk me out of.**"

The man of about forty-three asked her why, and asked it in the voice a man uses when he has been in the trade for eleven years and has been asked to look at a thing and has found it short.

She has put a number at the head of a month once. She was younger and she was doing it properly and she did it the way the two months in front of her had been done, and the days did not stop where the number stopped, and nobody found that out from the paper. They found it out from the days. She is fifty and she has been a notary for twenty-six years and she is not going to stand in a room and guess at a number again while about nine people watch her do it, and she would rather be asked again in a year by a clerk four hundred miles off than be wrong in front of a room today.

"**Write every day of it that somebody asks me to write. And when a clerk writes to me about a date he cannot place, I will be able to tell him how many there have been, and I will not have told him in advance.**"

---

She wrote the form out in a notary's hand and it was five lines and the first line was the name, and her name went under it, and the sheet went on the table in front of about nine people and nobody turned it over, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the first page in four hundred miles in about a month with a person's name at the top of it in a room, and that the person is a notary of fifty and not a household, and that the two are not the same and only one of them is dangerous.

Then she took the cloth and rubbed out the three hundred and sixteen on the door and wrote the date on the door in the new word, and the woman of about twenty-nine wrote the same thing on the second slate in chalk, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a door in this city has carried a count on it and then a month on it inside about four minutes, and that the difference between the two is not a law and is not a clerk and is a woman with a cloth.

The man of about fifty-seven did not go out of the room then and did not go out of it all morning, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he stayed because he had said no, and a man who has said no in a room in this city cannot leave before somebody has written something down, and that what got written down was the thing he had said no to.

"That is a Tuesday, is it."

"**That is a Tuesday, and yesterday was a Monday, and the day before that was a Sunday, and that is the only part of a month I am willing to put my name under in advance.**"

He looked at the door until the light had gone off it and then put his hand on the rope and left it there and said nothing else, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out since that a man who paid money for half a holding and refused a month out loud in a room with a date on it did not know until that morning that the days of the week were still standing, and that the days of the week were the one instrument in this flood that nobody had to make up, and that it was the one nobody had thought to write down.
