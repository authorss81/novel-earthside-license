# Chapter 0318 — A Man In A Very Good Coat Comes To Say It Is A Problem For Him

About nine people were in a room over a market on the third day of that month, a Thursday, and the chalk on its door was two days old and was a word, and the light came in over a step at the back with no name on it and stopped about the middle of the floor and did not come the rest of the way, and the cold was out of the glass and in the lining of a very good coat, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a box with a deed in it and a sheet pinned to the corner of the deed, a second slate, a form with a notary's name at the top of it and five lines under that, and a hand going into a coat and coming out again with nothing in it.

---

He came in at about the seventh hour and did not sit down, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who stands in a room in a very good coat and does not sit down has decided he is going to be asked to leave and has put the whole of the cost of that in front of the room before anybody does it.

He has nine days of a page in that coat and has not shown anybody the front of it and is not going to today.

"**Then you have not come to be useful. You have come to be a person in the room, and that is a thing this room has not had since Monday, and I would like it said that it has not had it.**" The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not look up from the second slate. "**Say it in your own words and do not make it small.**"

The short shape of it is a thing four people have been saying to each other in about nine kitchens this week, and he has heard it in about four of them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been walking up a road for nine days has been listening to it and has come to a room to be the fifth person who says it.

"**I have come because on Tuesday a woman made a form, and I am the only person in four hundred miles who has already been hurt by that happening, and I would rather say it here than have it said for me in about four months by a man who has never met me.**"

---

The man of about forty-three got the deed and the sheet out of the box and put them on the table flat, and Adrian read the clerk's hand over his shoulder, both columns, from the top down, twice, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who reads a hundred-and-forty-year-old hand at the same speed as a man reading a form two days old is a man who reads, and that the woman of about twenty-nine said his name once and nothing else, and that nobody in that room has asked him one question about the boundary.

"**Then the harm is the number that is not on the form.**" The man of about forty-three had not looked up from the sheet. He had found four spaces in nine boxes where a number should have been and two of them were a hundred and forty years old, and he had told the room about that on Wednesday, and a woman of about thirty-three had been right about it for a fortnight without being told.

"**She has been right about it for about a fortnight, and the day you told us was not the day I was right on, and I would rather be right about this for a fortnight than be told about it on Wednesday.**"

The man of about forty-one in the very good coat put his hand into the coat and took out nothing and put the hand back, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been doing that at intervals all morning is counting something with it, and that what he is counting is how long a room will let a man stand up.

---

A form is for the person who has to fill it in later, and that is the whole of what a form is, and he does not say it to be clever. Tuesday was Tuesday and a woman wrote her name at the top of five lines and the room is pleased with itself and has reason to be. And on the day those five lines become a copy in a room four hundred miles inland, the person holding the copy is going to have a day in a word that has no number on it, and he is going to have to write the number in himself to put it anywhere, and whatever he writes will be wrong, and it will be his handwriting, and he will not be able to tell anybody how he got it.

He turned round to the room and not to anybody in it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been standing for two hours turned round to a room rather than to a person in it because he is not asking anybody for anything and only the room can hear it.

"**A day is not a date. I have known that for about nine days and I have not said it in a room before, because I have been walking up a road with a sheet in a coat and there was nobody to say it to.**" He put his hand in and took it out. "And the whole of my trouble is that a day can be counted and a date cannot, and until Tuesday I could count and could not name, and now I can name and cannot count, and I am the man in this room who has to hold both."

"And you have come to be told to change it."

"**I have come to be the first person in this room to say that a form two days old is going to cost somebody money that is not mine, and I would like that said in daylight with a date on the door, and I am not going to ask for anything, and I am not going to be able to say in about four months that nobody warned me, because I have warned them and I will have done it in a room with about nine people in it.**"

---

Nobody in that room argued with him and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is now the fourth time in about a week that a true thing has been said out loud in that room and gone onto nothing, and that the man who said it is a man nobody in this city can check, which is a sentence the woman of about twenty-nine said in a market about a man with a rope nine days ago and which nobody in the room connected at the time.

The woman of about twenty-nine wrote the third day of that month on the second slate in chalk and put nothing under it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has declined four things out loud in this flood and named the cost of each one before she said it did not decline this, and that the not-declining is not agreement, and that she is a person who would have said no if she thought a no would hold, and that she has not worked out yet whether a no would hold.

Hettis Roke said one thing from the back of the room, and he said it to the wall and not to the man in the coat, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who refused a room a roll on Sunday has been in the room twice this week and has said nine sentences and has not been asked one question.

A number at the bottom of a thing is the only part anybody sends for, and a notary's name at the top of five lines is going to be a number at the bottom of something in about four months, and there is nothing anybody in this room can do about it.

"**I have said one thing and I am going home and I am not going to say it twice, and I would like it entered that I said it once.**"

The man of about forty-one in the very good coat said he would come back when the room had put something on the form, and did not say when, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been on a road for nine days with a sheet in a coat and has not shown the front of it to anybody has just agreed to come back and has not said a day, and that the only person in this city who does that on purpose is a woman of about fifty at a shut gate, and that neither of them has ever met and both of them work in a way that leaves nothing for a clerk.
