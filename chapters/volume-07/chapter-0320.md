# Chapter 0320 — A Tin, And A Sheet That Has No Day On It

On the fifth day of that month, a Saturday, about nine people were in a room over a market and the chalk on its door was a word four days old, and the light came in on the north side and went off the low ground early and did not come back to the table, and the cold was out of the glass and in the seam of a tin, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a tin, a folded sheet of paper thirty years old, a second slate, a form with a notary's name at the top of it and five lines under that, and a length of new rope that had not been used.

---

She came up from the end of the lane at about the eighth hour and nobody had sent for her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-nine who has not been asked anything in about a month got up and came to a room with about nine people in it with a tin in both hands, and that a person who has not been asked anything does not wait to be asked twice.

She put the tin on the table and did not open it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-nine who came a mile with a thing in her hands and then did not open it in front of nine people has decided that the opening is hers and not the room's and is going to happen on her own time and not on anybody else's.

**"Nobody has asked me what is in this and I have said out loud in a kitchen that I would like it said that I was not asked, and I am not going to un-say it, and I am going to open it in this room anyway because I have been sitting on it for about a month and I am sixty-nine and I am tired of it.**"

There was a folded sheet in it. The sheet came out flat and had been folded in eight and unfolded and folded in eight and unfolded about four times in thirty years, and the creases had gone soft, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a paper that has been opened and shut about four times in thirty years is a paper somebody has taken out and looked at and not acted on, thirty years, about nine times.

"**Thirty years old, my hand, and there is nothing at the top of it, and there is no day on it, and there is no name on it, and I have never once in thirty years needed a day on it, and I want that said in this room before anybody picks it up.**"

---

The man of about forty-three read it standing up, both hands behind him, from the top down, and did not sit down and did not put it on the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps nine boxes of other people's papers and has never been asked whether a word in one of them is true read a page in a woman's own hand for about four minutes and did not put it down, and that the reason he did not put it down is that a page on a table is a page in a room.

It is what two households call their own ground and where the water went in the winter of the year it went. It is more use than either of the two things on the other side of this room, and he is not going to say that twice.

"**It is more use and it settles nothing.** And I can put the day of this morning on the outside of it and put the fifth day of that month at the top of the entry, and then in about a year there is a page in a book in this city that says a woman of about sixty-nine saw where the water went thirty years ago, and that is the first time in about a month that a thing has been able to be said that way, and it is the whole of what the form is for."

"And the cost of it."

"**The cost of it is that it is a page and it has your hand on it and a date at the top of it, and a page with a hand on it and a date at the top of it is the thing a clerk can send for. And if somebody four hundred miles off sends for it in four years then it goes, and it does not come back.**"

---

The man of about fifty-seven took his hand off the rope and put it on the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is now the fourth time in about a week that a person in this room has named a cost out loud before anybody asked for one, and that three of the four have been people who were not going to get anything out of the thing they were pricing, and that the room has started doing it without anybody arranging it.

He asked her one question, in front of about nine people, and he said before he asked it that he had never asked her anything, and that he was asking because he was the only man in that room who had paid money for a piece of ground and had not been able to find out what he owned.

"**What do you want done with it.**"

She did not answer him straight off. She put both hands flat on the table on the two sides of the sheet, which is what a woman of about sixty-nine does when a rocking table is not going to be dealt with and there is a question on it.

"**I want it to stay on this table. I want it not to be entered and not to be copied and not to be taken out of this room, and there is a form on this table with a notary's name on it and I have read it upside down from the end of the room and I know what an entry is.**" She lifted one hand off the table and put it down again. "**And if it is ever going to be entered then I am to be asked first, and I am to be told no if the answer is no, and I am not going to be told that it has already been done because I was in the room.**"

Nobody in that room argued with her, and about four people in that room have worked out since that this is the fifth true thing said out loud in that room in about a week that has gone onto nothing, and that a refusal that is not free costs somebody something, and that what it cost here was the only page anybody in that room wanted, and that she gave the reason before she gave the answer, which is the second time in about a month a woman of about sixty-nine has done that in a room.

---

The sheet went down on the table about a foot from the second slate and nobody moved it after that, and the form with the notary's name on it stayed on the other side of the table with five lines under it and nothing on them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a table over a market now has three accounts of where a line runs on it and one of them has a person in it, and that the one with the person in it is the one nobody is allowed to touch, and that a deed and a copy are about a purchase and a Crown clerk, and neither of them has ever once been in a room.

At about the fourth hour she put the tin back on the table empty and squared the sheet's creases with two fingers so that it lay flat, and the woman of about twenty-nine wrote the fifth day of that month on the second slate in chalk and put nothing under it.

The man of about fifty-seven said one sentence on his way to the stair, and he said it to the room and not to the table, and it was about a house four hundred miles off and a book and about forty years, and he said he would have given the whole of what he paid for that paper, and that he was not going to say it again and was not going to be thanked for it.

She went down the lane to the end of it with the tin empty in both hands, and the light was off the low ground behind her, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-nine went home with an empty tin and a paper still on a table in a room over a market, and that the tin had been full for thirty years and was lighter by about four sheets of nothing, and that she did not know what she had done and would not have said so if she had, and that the date on the door of that room is the fifth day of a month with a word on it and no number under the word, and that nothing on it has been entered and a woman of fifty at a shut gate on another road has not been told any of it and will not be.
