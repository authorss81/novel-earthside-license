# Chapter 0662 — A Bucket And A Rag Beside A Trough While A Board Comes Down Off Its Two Nails

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had one hand on the handle of a bucket and the other on a rag beside a trough at the end of that market, and he lifted the bucket off the flags and set it down again a pace nearer the wall. He had washed the wall above that board once in the whole of this flood, on a day that is not one of this run of days, and had not washed it since, and what he had to work with was a bucket, a rag and the water that stood in the trough overnight. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and forty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the light had not come onto the front of that store.

That board is on the outside wall of a store at one end of that market. It carries three sets of figures of this city's own, none of the three rubbed out, and not one of the three carrying a line of words set under it. Nothing on any surface in this city carries a line of words set under a figure. The one line that did is outside this city and is not coming back, and no chapter of the life of anybody in that row puts one under any figure on any surface in it.

He took the rag off the stone lip of the step where it had lain since the evening before, and it was not inside the trough at any hour of that day, and he wrung it out over the bucket and laid it along the lip again. He put his palms flat to the lower edge of that board and looked along the face of it from about the height of his knee, the way he does every morning, and the face was as it had been, and the wall above the top edge of it was as it had been. That wall was washed once in the whole of this flood by the man who washes that board, and by no second person on any day of it, and no hand of his went onto it that morning.

---

On the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of the room over that market there is a date written in chalk, and the three hundred and forty-seventh day of the Bare Month is on it in full. Nothing whatever stands under that date, and the wood below it is bare for the width of a hand, and nobody put anything under it at any hour of that Friday. About four people went past that door before the sixth hour, and about four more went past before the eighth, and nobody took the number of either lot, and nobody said what the bare piece was for.

The room over that market put the two surfaces side by side that morning without anybody meaning to. A woman at a front said the board at the end of the row told a person what was owed and the wall above it told a person nothing, and a man at the next front said the wall told a person the board had been kept and the board told a person nothing about the wall. The three of them, the board and the wall and the room with about nine people in it, did not agree about anything, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can put the three in one order.

---

The washer took the board down off its two nails at the fourth hour and set it on edge against the foot of the wall, and he washed the face of it in two passes and put it back on the nails with the wedge under the near one. He did not wash the wall above it, and no second person put a hand on that wall at any hour of that day.

"**I take it down and I put it back, and the wall stays as it was, and the one morning I put a hand above the top edge is the morning I have washed something nobody asked me to wash.**"

He said it to the bucket and not to the room, and nobody in that row thanked him for saying it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the row with her hands in her sleeves, and she has her second slate and a column on the right of one page with a word in it near the head of it that is not hers, and she looked at the board on its nails and at the wall above it and said nothing about either.

---

He emptied the bucket into the channel and set it beside the trough, and he put the rag back on the stone lip of the step where it lives. A man two stalls along asked him if the board sat level, and he said it did, and the man went back to his own front.

"**It sits level on the wedge, and the figures are where they were, and the wall is where it was, and a person who wants one of them to speak for the other will have to stand here longer than I have stood.**"

He hung the empty bucket on the peg at the end of the row and wiped the rim of it down his leg, and the rag stayed on the lip where it lives, and the board sat level on its two nails with one clean face on it and the wall above that face as untouched as it had been at the fourth hour.
