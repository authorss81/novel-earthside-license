# Chapter 0690 — A Board Put Down On The Ground Face Down At The End Of A Market Row By A Man Who Was Asked Something And Has Not Answered

There was a board standing against the leg of the bench in the room over that market with the face of it turned in to the leg and the bare back of it out to the room, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own right hand flat along the top edge of it. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and seventy-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and about nine people were in that room, four of them near enough to the far end of that bench to have taken all of anything said at that end and four of them not.

Nineteen rates stood on that board in three columns, and the foot of the middle one of those three was a bare patch about two of his fingers wide with nothing in it at all, and he had laid his own hand over it once that morning and taken the hand away again.

That book was open on that bench, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both palms down flat on the wood on either side of it. Five figures stand one under another down the middle of that page with a day entered beside each of the five and none of them struck through, and there is a single word at the head of the column on the right of that page and it is not in her hand. Behind her on that shelf the second slate stood with the day cut across the top of it showing and nothing under that day showing, and it was not picked up and no hand but her own went near that shelf.

The outside of that door at the foot of that stair carried a date in chalk — the three hundred and seventy-fifth day of that month, in full — and under that date the door was bare wood as wide as a hand with nothing whatever on it. Nobody went up or down that stair that morning to look at it.

---

About the middle of that morning the wall along the far side of that room said one thing, and that wall has nothing on it and has had nothing put on it on any morning of this flood by anybody.

> A room is not a record. A record is a page that says the same thing to the person who was in the room and to the person who was not, and a room says its thing once, to whoever is standing in it, and then the room is empty and the thing said in it is on nobody's page. Nine people can hear a thing and be nine people who heard it, and not one of them can show the next person where it was said, and a thing that cannot be shown is not a record and was never going to be one.

"**I have carried this board into four markets for a long while and it has never once been wrong about a rate in any of them, and that is the whole of what I can say about it this morning, and I am not going to improve on it.**"

Four of them said out loud, in that room, that they did not know what it was about, and the rest said nothing at all. Nobody said the thing on that wall was right. Nobody said it was wrong. Nobody in that room repeated one word of it out loud to anybody and nobody took it down onto anything, and nobody thanked the man who trades on a board for having been standing at that end of the room through all of it.

---

Nobody voted and nobody had been asked to. There is no standing a thing over four days ahead of the morning it is put in front of anybody, and nothing about that was said in any mouth in that room, and the room found out what it had been told on the morning it was told it.

The woman at the front of that room asked him whether he agreed with it. She asked him out loud in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room, and she asked him in front of about nine people, and she did not ask him twice.

"**I have carried this board up that stair every morning for a long while and gone down it every evening, and nothing has ever come off it and nothing has ever been put on it, and that is a whole working life and it has not once been a thing anybody in this room could have seen.**"

That went down the room the way the other things went down that room, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said he was right, and the woman who keeps the book did not say a word and did not look up from the page in front of her.

Then about four seconds after she had asked him whether he agreed with it, he did not say anything. It went past four seconds and nobody asked him again and nobody argued with him and nobody said anything against the silence, and the woman at the front of that room put her own hands flat on her own knees and waited, and the man who trades on a board stood at the far end of that room with his own right hand on the top edge of his own board and did not say one word.

Nobody in that room asked him about the bare patch at the foot of the middle of those three columns, and nobody in that room looked down at it, and he was not thanked for not answering, and he did not say what he had thought.

---

At the tenth hour he lifted that board off the floor where it had been standing against the leg of the bench, and carried it out of that room and down that stair and along that row to the front at that end of the market, and set it down on the ground against the leg of a trestle with the face of it to the ground. He left it there and went on up the row without it.
