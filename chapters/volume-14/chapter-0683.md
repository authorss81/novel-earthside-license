# Chapter 0683 — Two Fingers Lifting The Near Edge Of A Register And Letting It Down Again In A Room That Works Out What It Cannot Do

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had two fingers of her right hand under the near edge of the register at the near end of that bench, lifting it enough to take the weight off the wood and letting it down again. It was about the ninth hour on the three hundred and sixty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and about nine people were in the room over that market and the light off the one window had come off the page and up onto the wall behind it.

Five figures go down the middle of that page with a day entered against each and none of them struck, and up at the head of the column on the right of that page there is one word and no other writing on the page anywhere, and the hand that put that word there is not the hand that keeps the book. The second slate was on the shelf behind her, edge-on to the plaster, a day cut across the top of it and the slate bare below that day, and it has never been picked up in this flood and nothing but her own hand has come near that shelf.

The board that had been put into the other man's two hands at that front the day before had gone back up the row to its owner some time in the evening and was under his arm again where it had been. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair at the eighth hour with nothing in his hands and went and stood at the back of that room against the wall, and the piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket stayed where it was.

There was a date in chalk on the outside of that door at the foot of that stair with the three hundred and sixty-eighth day of that month written across it, and under the date the door was bare wood to the width of a hand with nothing on it, and about four people went past it before the light went off the row.

---

The talk in that room had gone round in a circle for about an hour without anybody deciding that it was a circle, which is how a room finds out what it cannot do. A woman at the front wanted to know how anybody would ever tell who had written a thing down, and a man at the back said that a page says the same thing to whoever picks it up, and the woman at the front said that was not the question and did not say what the question was.

The man at the far end of that room came the length of that bench and put his right forefinger down on the paper beside the head of the column on the right of that page, and not in it, and held it there. The woman at the near end of the bench took hold of the near edge of the book with two fingers and moved it along the wood far enough that his finger was standing beside nothing at all. He took his finger off the paper, put his hand down at his own side, and said no word whatever about the book having moved, and about nine people in that room had watched a book move and not one of them had watched whose finger had been on it.

"**There is one thing in this city that can leave a mark you cannot get off again, and it is on me in this room, in a coat, and it has been in that coat the whole of this flood, and that is me telling you where a thing is and not telling you what it is for.**"

Four of them, in that room, said out loud that they did not know what he was on about, and everybody else in it said nothing at all, and nobody thanked him for saying where a thing was, and nobody asked him what it was for.

Nobody got up out of that room and left it, and nobody went to the end of the row and asked a man behind a trestle one question about a coat.

---

By the tenth hour about nine people had been in that room and about four of them had said out loud, in the ordinary way, that if a page has been written in by somebody who is not the person who keeps it, then there is nothing anybody in this city can hold up and point at and say of it, that one. The man whose hand it is is two stalls along and he keeps a stall there, and he keeps a piece of chalk in a pocket, and there is no instrument in this city or in four hundred miles of anything that could put those two facts next to each other and make one of them mean something.

The woman the finding was about kept the book open in front of all of them the whole of that hour. She did not close it and she did not turn the page and she did not put her hand over the column on the right, and when the woman at the front came and stood at her shoulder and looked down at the page she stood still and let her stand there.

"**A room that wants to know which hand wrote a word has to ask the hands, and it is not going to, and I am not going to stand in the middle of it and be the answer to a thing nobody has asked me.**"

It reached the bench and nobody in that room did anything with it and two people behind him went on talking about something else, and nobody in that room said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and the woman at the near end of the bench did not look up.

At the end of that hour he came away from the side of that bench and went the whole length of that room and down that stair, and went back up to his own front two stalls along and stood behind his own trestle with both his hands flat on the top of the wood, and he stayed there while the light went off the store.
