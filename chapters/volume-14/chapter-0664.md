# Chapter 0664 — A Satchel With Its Flap Down Behind A Bench And The One Thing Its Carrier Knows

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had his hand on the strap of a satchel behind the bench in the room over that market, and he pulled the strap round twice and left the flap down where it was. He has a satchel with a flap and one thing he knows what is in, and he has never opened anything he has carried in this flood, and the reason is not given and the chapter does not supply it. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and forty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and about nine people were in that room, and the light was on the one window.

The first of the four one-place sheets is in this city, with a heading across the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place holds a figure of this city's own, and it was read out in this city on a day that is not one of this run of days and was never returned and never withdrawn. It was not read out in that room that morning, it was not laid beside anything, it was not in anybody's hand, and nobody in that room compared it with anything. Seven documents have come up that road or gone out of it, three of four places each and four of one place each, and there are no more than seven of that kind in this city. The second of the four went out of this city. The third went back up the road with a line of words set under its figure. The fourth went back up the road with a word where a figure has never been and is not coming back. Four hundred miles inland is the town a page came from, and this volume does not enter it, and nobody in that room was sent to it and nobody came back from it.

The keeper stood at the near end of that bench with her hands on the wood, and she has her second slate and a column on the right of one page with a word in it near the head of it that is not hers, and she did not touch the satchel and did not ask about it. The carrier set the satchel back behind the bench with the flap down, and he put his hand on the buckle without lifting it, the way a man does who wants to know a thing is shut without wanting it open.

---

A man at the window said the road was clear and another said the flags were wet, and the carrier listened and said nothing for a while. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the stair with his own board and stood at the far end, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle and a piece of chalk he does not use, and he did not ask the carrier about the strap and the carrier did not ask him about the pocket.

"**I have carried every sheet that went up that road and every one that came back shut, and the one thing I know is the shut of this strap, and a shut thing is the whole of what I am asked to keep.**"

He said it to the bench and not to the room, and nobody in that room thanked him for saying it. The keeper looked at the satchel behind the bench and then back at the book on its shelf, and she did not ask him what was in it, and nobody in that room asked her what the column on her own page was for.

---

He took the satchel up by the strap at about the ninth hour and set it on the bench for the room to see, with the flap still down, and every person in that room could see the shut of it and not one of them could see past it. He ran his thumb along the edge of the flap once to settle it, and he put the satchel back behind the bench where it had been, and the flap stayed down through the whole of it. The room found out what it was being asked on the morning it was asked, for the seventh volume running, and the four days that would have been the cooling were not available, and the chapter says so in narration and not in any mouth.

The keeper said one thing to the room before the tenth hour, and it was not about the satchel.

"**A thing carried shut through weather is still shut when it gets here, and the hands that carried it are the only hands that know the weight of it, and this room has never once been asked to know more.**"

He lifted the satchel by the strap and went back down the stair with it on his shoulder and the flap down, and he did not open it on the way down, and he found that the morning had let him show the shut without showing past it, and he kept the shut all the way down.
