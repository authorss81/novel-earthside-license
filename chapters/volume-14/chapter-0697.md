# Chapter 0697 — A Board Washed Once In The Morning And Not Twice And A Man Two Stalls Along Going Away From It With A Mark On His Own Hand

The flat of the man of about thirty-four's own two hands was on the face of his own board where it stood against his own trestle with the bare back of it turned out into that row, and he took them off it and put his own arms down at his own sides. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and eighty-second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the light had not come off the flags at that end of that market yet, and that end of that market is walked past by about four people on an ordinary day, and the store's board was on its two nails at the wall at the end of it with three sets of figures of this city's own on it, none of them rubbed out, not one of them carrying a line of words under it, and no figure on it is printed anywhere in this city.

That board is not blank. It is stale, and it is the colour the weather has gone it, and it is on the outside wall of that store at that end of that market and it is not anybody's board and it is not the board of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and it is not the board at the wharf.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came along that row at about the fourth hour with his own bucket in his right hand and came to the end of it, and the trough was standing on the flags at the top of the stone by the step and there was no water in it, and the rag was lying on the stone lip of that step where it belongs and not inside that trough, and he did not say one word about either of those two things.

He took his own two hands off the rag where they were, and picked the rag up off the lip of that step, and went at the face of that board with it, and the water came out of the rag he had rung out over the channel at the side of that row in a long run down the front of it. The wall above the top edge of that board is a different thing from the board and was washed once in the whole of this flood and by no second person on any day of it, and he did not put a hand above the top edge of that board and nothing in that row went above it that morning, and no water went above it, and the water stopped at the bottom of the board every time.

He went along that board from the near end of it to the far end of it and came back and went along it again, and that is one pass and not two, and the ordinary use of that board in that row is two passes, and nobody in that row is going to say anything about a man doing one pass where he does two.

The store's chalk out of a box on a step whose lid does not shut was on that step at the end of the market row with its own heap of chalk standing at the foot of the store's wall beside it, and the man who washes that board did not touch it that morning, and the other piece of chalk in this city is a piece of chalk in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it belongs to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and that piece never left that pocket between the fourth hour of that morning and the tenth hour of it, and nobody in this city had the two of those chalks in one hand at any hour of that day.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the front of that market with his own two hands in his own sleeves, and he stopped at the trestle of the woman there, and then he went on to the store's end of that row and put the flat of his own right hand on the lower edge of that board to lean past it, and the lower edge of that board is the one part of it anybody at that end of that market has ever put a hand on, and his own hand came away from it with the mark of it on his own palm.

He did not see the mark and he did not know it was there. He went on up that row and set his own board against his own trestle and put his own two hands on the top of the trestle and stayed there.

"**That board at that end of this row was washed once this morning and not twice, and I have watched that man wash it for a long while, and there is no such thing as once on that board, and I am not going to stand here in this row and say any more about it than that.**"

Four people going past that end of that market caught every word of that and four more of them did not, and nobody stopped, and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody thanked him, and Adrian Vale was at the far end of that row with the rag in his own two hands and did not hear one word of it and was not told one word of it and did not know any of it had been said.

The mark was on that man's own hand from the fourth hour of that morning to the end of that day and after it, and it is still on that hand and nobody has washed it off, and he does not know how it got there, and there is no instrument in this city that could put a mark on a hand and a name on the person it was on in the same stroke, and nobody in that row told him and nobody in that row was going to.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board hung the rag back onto the stone lip of that step at about the tenth hour, and the light came off those flags a good while after that, and he took his own bucket away with him, and the trough stood on the flags at the top of the stone with the water going over the rim of it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall took his own right hand out of his own sleeve at about the same hour and looked at the palm of it, and there was a mark on it, and he did not know where that mark had come from and he did not go and ask anybody, and he put that hand flat down on the top of his own trestle and left it there, and the mark of it went onto the wood of the trestle, and he did not wash his own hand and he did not go to the store's end of that row again that day.
