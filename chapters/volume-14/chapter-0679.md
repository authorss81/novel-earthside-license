# Chapter 0679 — Three Turns Of A Rope Round The Near End Of A Plank On A Wet Sill

Adrian Vale had both hands under the plank where it lay along the wet sill of the second row of flats, with his fingers hooked under the lower edge of it to lift it a finger's width off the stone, and the end of a rope was in his right hand and the far end of that rope was round a ring in the sill. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and sixty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and eleven miles of flats went out from that row in both directions with the water coming off them in sheets, and he wanted that second row of flats to be crossed by something a person could walk on.

The trough stood at the wharf end with the rag on the stone lip of the step where it lives, and the bucket was down at the near end of the sill under the low place in the stone where the water out of the joints ran, and Adrian had been carrying water from the trough to that bucket all morning, a full bucket at a time, and emptying it into the channel at the side of the row. The plank was a length of old board and the sill under it was wet stone with a run of silt in the joints along it, and the plank slid on that stone a finger's width every time the wind came across the flats and got under the end of it. The plank was where Adrian had put it and it was going to be somewhere else by the tenth hour.

The salt wharf is at the near end of those eleven miles, and about four people a day walk past that yard in the ordinary course, and the man of about thirty-eight who works there came down that second row at about the sixth hour with his barrow empty and his knife in his belt, and he came to the near end of the sill and stood and looked at the plank and at the silt in the joints and at the ring in the stone with the rope going through it.

"**A board on a wet sill is a board that has not decided yet, and it will decide itself about four times before the light goes and every one of those four times will be a different one.**"

The plank got the first of them and the man crouched at the near end of it did not, and Adrian did not answer him and was not asked anything, and the wharf man went on down the row with his barrow and did not say why he had come down it.

---

Adrian got his fingers under the plank again and lifted the near end a finger's width and worked the silt out from under it with the heel of his own hand, and the water in the joints ran into the bucket and he emptied the bucket into the channel at the side of the row and put it back. Then he took the end of the rope in both hands, made a turn round the near end of the plank, and pulled it until the plank came up off the stone and sat hard against the ring, and he made two more turns on the rope and pulled them down hard and let go of them.

The rope he was using was not new. It had been used before he came by it and it had been used hard, and the lay of it was flat in three places along its length where somebody before him had had a weight on it standing, and there was grit ground into the lay of it, and the ring in that sill had been put there by a person who is not in this city and is not asked about it.

A woman came along that row with a hand-basket on her arm and no hat, and she got to the near end of the sill and looked at the plank and at the rope and at the water standing in the joints on the far side of it, and she stood there and did not say anything to Adrian and did not ask him anything.

She stepped up onto the plank and went along it with the basket on her arm and one hand on the rope, and she came off the plank at the far end onto the stones, and she went up the second row of flats and she did not come down it, and nobody in this city has been told which way she went or whether she came back by another way.

Adrian watched her go until the roofs took her and then he took the bucket and the trough and the rag back to the wharf end and left the plank where it was with the rope round it and the ring in the stone.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came back down that row at about the ninth hour with his barrow in front of him and stopped at the near end of the plank, and he put his own boot on the plank and put his weight on it the way a man does when he wants to know whether a thing is going to stay where it has been put, and the plank went down about the width of a thumbnail and came back and stayed.

"**A plank with a rope on it and a ring in the stone and a woman who went up it and did not come down, and nobody in this city knows which end of that plank she came off at, and I have put my own boot on it and it held and I am not going to say a word about it to either of us.**"

The plank got the second of them and nobody else did, and Adrian was at the far end of that row with his hands in his pockets and did not hear it and was not told about it, and the wharf man took his own boot off the plank and carried his barrow on past it, and then he stopped in the wet and looked back at the near end of the plank, which was still down on the sill.
