# Chapter 0693 — Four People Go Past A Yard In The Ordinary Course And A Boot Comes Down On The Near Edge Of A Board Lying Face Up On A Stack

The flat of the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf's own right hand was on the near edge of that board where it lay face up on the stack of boards in his own yard, and he took the hand off it and put it down at his own side, and the grit across the middle of it where he had put the hand did not move. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and seventy-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. Four people a day go past the open end of that yard in the ordinary course, and the sun comes on to the side of that board at about the eighth hour and not before.

There are two lines of grey grit across the figure on that board and they have not been wiped by anybody at any hour of this flood. The figure itself is his and it is on the side the sun gets to and nobody in this city has ever been shown it and nobody has ever asked him where it came from. The other side of that board is the side the sun does not get to, and there is a figure on that side too, and it is not this figure, and nobody in this city has been shown that one either, and no mouth in that yard has ever said the two of them are the same thing.

About four hundred yards of flags from the market end there is a door at the foot of a stair and somebody has chalked a date on the outside of that door, and the day on it this morning is the three hundred and seventy-eighth day of that month, and the wood under the chalk has been bare for the width of a hand for the whole of this flood, and he walks past that door twice a week and he could not tell anybody what that wood is for.

---

He stood at his own yard door and watched them come past, the way a man stands at a door he has stood at a thousand times, and he did not go out into the road and he did not say one word to any of them.

The first of the four came down the side of that yard at about the fifth hour and looked at the middle of that board and at nothing else on that stack, and went on. The second came past at about the sixth hour and looked along the length of that board from the near end to the far end of it without stopping, and went on. The third came past at about the sixth hour and did not look at that board at all and went on. The fourth came past at about the seventh hour and looked down at the grit and not at what is under the grit, and went on.

Four people went past the open end of that yard in about three hours and not one of them stopped and not one of them said one word, and every one of the four looked at that board and every one of the four looked at a different thing on it, and the four of them were all right.

"**I have been at that door a long while today and I have not asked one of the four of them anything and I am not going to, and I have worked out what the four of them are looking at, and it is four different things, and not one of the four of them is wrong and not one of the four of them agrees with another.**"

That went out across his own yard and not to anybody in it, and there was a dog asleep against the wall of it and nothing else in that yard that can hear a word.

The one line of words that ever went under a figure in this flood went under one on a sheet that left this city before this run of days began, and that sheet has not come back, and there is not a figure in this city with a line of words under it, and there is no surface in this city anybody in it has ever been told they may put one under.

---

At about the eighth hour the sun came on to the side of that board and put a bar of light along the length of it from the far end to the near end, and he saw then that the grit on the near edge of that board was not lying flat. It had been lying flat for as long as he had been coming out to that yard. A piece of it at the near end, about as wide as two fingers, had come away from the wood, and the bare wood was under it, and he did not know how long ago and he was not going to find out.

A woman came past the open end of that yard about that hour with nothing in her two hands, and she had to step up onto the boards of that stack to get round his barrow, which was standing in the mouth of the yard with its handles out into the road the way it stands on an ordinary morning, and her boot came down on the near edge of that board where the piece of the grit had come away, and it went in a little and she got her foot back down onto the stones and went on up the road.

He did not say anything to her. She did not say anything to him. Nobody in that yard said anything about what her boot had done to that board, and the grit that came off that edge went down between the boards of the stack and not anywhere else, and he did not look for it and he did not count it.

"**Somebody chalked a date on the outside of that door, and the date is the three hundred and seventy-eighth day of that month, and under it there is a piece of bare board about as wide as a hand with nothing at all on either of them, and I walk past that door twice a week and have done for a long while, and I have never once been told what I am supposed to make of the wood under the chalk, and I have never once worked it out for myself either.**"

Nobody going past that yard heard him, and about four people went past it between that hour and the tenth hour and not one of them said one word about anything.

---

He went and got both hands on the two handles of that barrow and walked it back into his own yard, the left wheel catching on the edge of a board of the stack on the way, and he stood the two handles down against the wall of that yard and took his hands off them, and the open end of that yard was as wide as it had been before the barrow was standing in the mouth of it, and the board with two lines of grey grit across it was still lying face up on the top of that stack with a piece of its near edge clean where a boot had been, and the figure on it is under the grit and is not printed anywhere in this city and belongs to a man nobody has ever asked.
