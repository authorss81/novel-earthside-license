# Chapter 0667 — A Morning When The Store Board Comes Down And Goes Back Up Without A Word

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had both hands on the lower edge of the store's board at the end of that market, with his fingers spread under it and his thumbs on the face. He had a bucket, a rag, and one wall washed once in the whole of this flood on a day that is not one of this run of days, and not washed since. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and the light was still grey on the front of that store.

The store's board had been on the outside of that wall at one end of the market for longer than this flood, and three sets of figures of this city's own stood on it, and not one of the three had been rubbed out, and not one of the three carried a line of words set under it. The wall above the top edge of it had been washed once in the whole of this flood by the man who washes that board and by nobody else on any day of it, and no hand of his went onto it that morning. The trough stood against the foot of that wall with the night's water still in it, the bucket stood beside the trough on the flags, and the rag lay on the stone lip of the step and was not inside the trough at any hour of that day.

He took the board down off its two nails and set it on edge against the foot of the wall, and he washed the face of it in two passes with water out of the bucket, and the water ran off the face and into the channel. He put the board back on its two nails with the wedge under the near one, and the board sat level, and nothing was decided in that row at any hour of that morning, and the wall said nothing.

---

A thing was said in that row and not on any page, and the row was empty at the end of the morning, and the thing was in nobody's hands. The washer said to the bucket that the face was clean and the wall was as it was, and the person it was said to was the woman at the far front, some way off along the row, doing something else with a crate and not listening. She did not hear it, and she did not need to, and the water went into the channel and the channel took it. A woman set a crate down on the flags further along that row to get her arm out from under it, and it stood over the run of the channel while she stood there, and the washer came out and moved it a hand's width onto the high side of the stone, and she said nothing and he said nothing, and the crate went on down the row when she picked it up.

"**I say it to the bucket and not to the row, and the board is clean and the wall is as it was, and a morning with nothing decided in it is still a morning with a clean board.**"

Nobody in that row thanked him for saying it, and nobody argued with it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the row with her hands in her sleeves, and behind her the second slate stood on its edge on the shelf with its written face to the plaster, and the one word near the head of the column on the right of that page was still not hers, and she looked at the board on its nails and went on past it without stopping.

---

He emptied what was left in the bucket into the channel and set the bucket back beside the trough, and he took the rag off the lip where he had laid it while he washed and put it back on the stone lip of the step. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went past with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned in against his ribs, and the piece of chalk stayed in that inside breast pocket through the whole morning and was not out of it at any hour of that day.

"**The board comes down and the board goes up, and the rag goes back on the lip, and the wall stays out of it, and that is the whole of a Wednesday.**"

He hung the empty bucket on the peg and wiped his hands down his sides, and the rag stayed on the lip, and the board stayed on its nails. Nobody in that row knew that a wet face had been made clean that morning, and the water was still going down the channel after he had gone in.
