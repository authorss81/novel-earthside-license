# Chapter 0695 — One Of About Four People Going Past A Door In The Ordinary Course Reads The Date On It And Does Not Go Up That Stair

There was a board standing against the side of that bench, its face turned in to the wood, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both his own hands resting flat along the top edge of the bare back of it, and he left them there for most of that morning without moving them. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. About nine people were in that room, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at that end of the bench with the book open in front of her and her two hands flat on the wood on either side of it. About four people go past the door at the foot of that stair in the ordinary course of a day.

That door carries a date in chalk, and the day on it this morning is the three hundred and eightieth day of that month, and the wood under the chalk is bare for the width of a hand, and there is nothing whatever on it and nothing else on that door at all, and the two of those facts have been true together for the whole of this flood and are going to go on being true.

About four people go past that door every day. On this day one of them stopped at it, read the date off the chalk, and did not go up that stair. Nobody in this city knows which of the four it was. Nobody asked.

---

Four sheets of one shape have been on a page in this flood. One of the four came up that road and is on a shelf in this city with a figure in its one place. One of the four went out of this city and has not come back. One of the four went back up that road with words standing under the figure in its one place. And one of the four went back up that road with a word in its one place under a heading where no figure has ever stood, and that one is four hundred miles from this city and is not coming back. Seven documents in all in this flood, and four of the seven are sheets of that shape, and the other three have four places ruled on them and none of the three of them is in this city on this day, and no person anywhere has ever had two sheets of that shape in one hand at once or laid one of them down beside another one or said out loud what any two of them are.

That room went on doing what that room does on the ordinary course, and nothing was decided in it and nothing was written, and the wall along the far side of that room said nothing at any hour of that day. There are seven notices in this flood and there is not an eighth of them in it, and nobody in this city was ever told in advance of anything.

At about the fifth hour a man at the back of that room said out loud that the weather had turned in the night and that everybody in that room ought to be glad of it, and about four of them said they were and about four of them said nothing at all, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said nothing and moved his own two hands about a little way along the back of his own board and said nothing else.

"**I have been standing at this end of this bench since the fourth hour waiting for one person in this row to come up that stair, and I have not said that to anybody in this room and I am saying it now, and I would rather one of them came up than it that nothing at all happened this morning.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for saying it and nobody argued with him about it and nobody said he was right, and the woman at that end of the bench with the book in front of her said nothing and was not asked anything, and about four people in that room looked out of the one window of that room at the row below it and about four of them did not.

---

At about the seventh hour the woman at the front of that room stood up off the stool she had been sitting on and said out loud, in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room, that somebody in that row had stopped at that door that morning and read the date off it and gone back on down the row without going up the stair, and that she had seen it from that window about an hour before.

Then about nine people in that room got up off what they were sitting on and went to that one window, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register got up off the bench and went with them, and they stood along the sill of that window in a line and looked down at that row.

There was nobody standing at that door. There were about four people going past that door in the ordinary course and the two sides of that row were where they always are, and the chalk on the outside of that door was a mark a person could read from where they stood in the row, and the wood under it was the width of a hand of nothing.

Nobody in that room said the woman at the front of it was wrong. Nobody in that room said she was right either. About four people in that room said out loud that they had no notion what she was talking about, and about four of them said nothing whatever, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said nothing, and the man of about thirty-four and the man of about thirty-nine were not in that room and are not in that room on any day of this flood together.

"**I have been in this room every morning of this flood and I can tell you what every person in it was doing this morning at the fourth hour and at the seventh hour and at the ninth hour, and I could not tell you who stopped at that door this morning if I were asked in that window, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can be shown the person, and that is the whole of it.**"

That went down that room the way things go down that room, and about four people in that room said they did not know what it was about and about four of them said nothing whatever, and nobody thanked him for it.

---

About the ninth hour a man at that window put his own two hands flat on the sill of it and stayed there, and he did not move for a long while, and he did not say one word to anybody while he stood there, and the people in that row went on going past that door the way they go past it on every day of this flood, and about nine of them came and went while he was standing at that window, and not one of them came up that stair.

He came back in at about the tenth hour, and went to the place where he had been sitting, and sat down in it, and put his own two hands on his own knees, and nobody in that room said one word to him about it and nobody moved anything.
