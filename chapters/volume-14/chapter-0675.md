# Chapter 0675 — A Man With His Hands Flat On The Back Of His Own Board In A Room Where Nobody Is Going To Write Down What He Has Just Said

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put both hands flat on the bare back of his own board where he had leaned it against the side of the bench in the room over that market, and the piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket was where it had been all morning and it did not come out. It was about the eighth hour on the three hundred and sixtieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. Nine people were in that room, and not one of them had been sent.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with the register open in front of her and her two hands on the bench on either side of it, and on the shelf behind the book stood a slate turned to the plaster with a day cut across the top of it and nothing at all under that day. That door, at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, had a date in chalk on the outside of it and the three hundred and sixtieth day of that month was on that date in full, and the wood under the date was bare for the width of a hand and had nothing whatever on it, and no person in this city had ever been told by anybody that they might put anything on that wood or that they might not.

She had wanted to be asked what that piece of board was for before the eighth hour. She did not get asked. She did not ask either, and about nine people were in that room and every one of them had come up that stair for something of their own and not for this.

---

There had been no notice and there was not going to be one, and the four days that would have been the time for a thing to be stood over before anybody acted on it were not available to anybody in that room, and nothing about that was said in any mouth. What a room does when there is no time is the thing it does, and this room had been doing it for a long time, and the stallholder had watched it happen on the morning before and on the one before that.

He took his own hands off the back of his own board and stood up straight and did not raise his voice, and a woman at the front of that room was in the middle of saying that the row had been thin since the light came on and that she could tell it by the way the sound went along the flags, and he waited for her to finish that sentence and did not begin over the top of it.

"**I want it said first that the person this is about is standing at this bench and I am not going to ask her, and that is the cost of it and it is mine to pay and not hers to be asked about.**"

The room got it and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody improved on it, and the woman at the front sat down where she had been standing and the man beside her did not sit down. He waited again after that, and the room waited with him, and about four people in it looked at the door at the back and about four looked at the bench.

---

He said the second thing in the same voice and at the same volume as the first, and he said it into the middle of a room that was not listening for it.

"**A room that says a space is left alone has emptied it.**"

About four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what he was talking about, and about four said nothing at all, and no one of the nine said the sentence back to him, and no one asked him what a room was, and the keeper did not say anything and was not asked anything and kept her two hands where they were on the bench on either side of the open book.

Nobody voted. It was not a thing anybody put to the room to vote on, and the stallholder had not asked for a vote and would not have taken one, and the room found out what it was being told on the morning it was being told it, the way a room does when there is no way to do it any other way.

The thing that was decided in that room on that morning was that the piece of board about as wide as a hand under that date was not to be written in, and that nothing was to be written down about the fact that it had been decided, and that nobody outside that room was to be told. The surface was not touched. The chalk stayed in the coat. The door at the foot of the stair went on carrying a date in chalk and a hand's width of bare wood under it, and at the tenth hour it looked exactly as it had looked at the fourth hour and as it had looked on every morning of this flood, and there was nothing on it anywhere that said a room had ever decided anything about it.

Nobody went out of that room and down that stair and said it to the row below. A man at the far end of that room looked at the door at the back as if he was thinking about the row outside it, and did not get up, and about four people who had said they did not know went on not knowing through to the tenth hour, and the stallholder took his own board from the side of the bench and went out of the room and down the stair with it under his arm.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had been standing at the far end of that room through all of it with his board under his arm, and after the stair had taken the sound of the other man away he turned that board end over end once in his two hands, and put it back under his arm the other way up.
