# Chapter 0688 — Two Hands On Her Own Knees At The Far End Of A Room In Which About Nine People Are Going To Do Nothing At All

The woman of about fifty-two had both hands on her own knees where she was sitting at the far end of the room over that market, and the stool she had stood in the dark end of that room was against the wall behind her with its own seat facing the room. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and seventy-third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and about nine people were in that room and the light off the one window was on the floor and not on anybody.

Nothing in that room had been decided and nothing in it was going to be decided, and every person in it had come up that stair that morning for something of their own and not for the room.

The date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair had the three hundred and seventy-third day of that month on it in full. Below that date the wood of that door was bare for the width of a hand, and there was nothing on it, and there is nothing going to go on it, and no person in this city has ever been told by anybody that they may put a thing on it or that they may not.

---

Seven notices have gone out of this city in this flood and this city has not written one since and is not going to, and the four days that would have been the time to stand a thing over in front of the people it was for are not available to anybody in that room or in this city.

"**I have been in this room every morning of this flood and it does the same thing on all of them, and I have not once gone out of it able to show anybody where anything was settled, because there has not been anything settled in it to show anybody.**"

The woman who keeps the book opened that register at the fourth hour the way she opens it every morning of this flood, at the angle she always opens it to, and put her two hands flat on the bench on either side of it. She did not write in it. She turned no page. She sat with it open in front of her for the whole of that hour with the light off the one window coming across it and going off it again as the cloud came over, and about four people in that room looked at the open page and about four did not, and not one of them asked her what it was for.

About four people in that room said out loud that they did not know what she was talking about and about four of them said nothing, and nobody thanked her for standing up in a room and saying it, and the woman who keeps the book did not look up from the page in front of her.

---

At the sixth hour a man at the back of that room got up off the stool he had been on, carried it round to the near end of that bench and set it down facing the register, and sat down on it, and about nine people watched a man move a stool and not one of them asked him why he had moved it.

A woman at the far end stood up and came down that room and went back up it twice with her own hands behind her back, and about four people got out of her way each time and about four did not, and she sat down on the wall where she had been sitting and did not say why she had walked it.

About the ninth hour a man at the back of that room put his own question out into the middle of it in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room, and it was the question that room had been putting to itself out loud for a fortnight without once getting an answer out of anybody, and he asked it of the room and not of any one person.

Nobody in that room improved on it and nobody said he was right. A woman at the front said she did not know what it was about. A man beside her said the same thing in other words. Two more of them said nothing whatever and went on with what they had in their hands, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register kept her two hands flat on the bench on either side of the open book and said nothing at all and was asked nothing at all.

About four people said out loud that they did not know and about four said nothing, and nobody in that room put the question to any one person in it, and the morning went on. A man at the near end said out loud that whatever it was it had been going on for longer than the four of them had been in that room, and a woman at the far end said that she had been in the room over that market before any of them and it had been doing the same thing then, and nobody argued with either of them and nobody asked either of them how they knew.

The bar of light off the one window came off the open page and went along the top of the bench and reached the near end of it and stayed there, and the woman who keeps the book moved her two hands on the bench a little apart from where they had been and did not close the book and did not say anything, and the man who trades on a board did not move out of the far end of that room and did not say a word.

---

"**That door outside has had a date on it every morning of this flood and a piece of wood under it that nobody has ever been told they may write on, and about four people go past it in the ordinary course and about four of them get something off it, and I am not going to be the one who stands at that door and works out which four.**"

Nobody argued with her about that either, and nobody came and stood at that door with her, and the stool behind her at the far end of that room stood where it had stood since the Saturday before last with the seat of it facing into the room.

She got up off the wall, picked the stool up off the floor by the back of it and carried it the length of that room to the near end, and set it down on the floor under the bench. It went under with a sound, and the room had something under that bench that had not been under it.
