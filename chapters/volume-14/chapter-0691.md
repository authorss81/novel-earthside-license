# Chapter 0691 — Two Hands Flat On Two Open Pages Of A Book While A Woman Says Out Loud What Fifty Days Of Not Being Asked Has Cost Her

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the two open pages of that book where it lay at the near end of that bench, one hand at the head of each page and her fingers spread wide on the paper, and she was not writing anything and she was not turning a page. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and seventy-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and that room was filling up behind her while her hands stayed where they were. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stood at the far end of that bench with his own board leaning against the side of it and the bare back of that board turned out to the room, and he had laid his own two hands flat along the top of it and had not taken them off since he came up.

Behind her on the shelf there was a second slate standing on its long edge with a day cut across the head of it showing and nothing whatever under that day showing. It was not picked up. It was not turned over. It was not written on. No hand but her own had gone near that shelf that morning, and there was nothing else standing on that shelf, and it was not on the same shelf as anything in that room.

The chalk on the outside of that door at the foot of that stair carries the three hundred and seventy-sixth day of this month written out in full, and the wood under that chalk is bare for the width of a hand and has been bare for the whole of this flood, and nobody in this city has ever been told by anybody that they may put a thing on it or that they may not.

---

Nobody in this city puts a thing in front of the people it is about four days before the morning it is about, and there is no eighth notice, and the four days are not available to anybody in that room. Six of the seven documents that have ever gone out of this city went to an office nine hundred miles off and the seventh is on a shelf in this city and has never gone anywhere at all. There is a word in the one place of a sheet that went back up that road and is not coming back, and there is a word at the head of the column on the right of the page in front of her, and neither of those two words has ever been read out in a room, and nobody has ever had both of them in one hand, and nobody has ever put them beside one another and said they were the same kind of thing.

That room was full by the fifth hour and it was not going to settle anything in it, and every person in it had come up that stair that morning for a thing of their own and not for the room.

She took her two hands off the pages, put them flat on the bench on either side of the book, and turned her head a little way round towards the middle of that room.

"**I am going to say the thing that has cost me and I am going to say it first, before anything else in this room this morning, and then nobody in here is going to ask me one question about it, and I would like every one of you to notice that going past as it happens.**"

About four people in that room said out loud, in four different sets of words, that they did not know what she was talking about. The rest of them said nothing whatever. Nobody thanked her for saying it and nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and nobody improved on it, and the man at the far end of that bench did not take his own two hands off the back of his own board and did not say one word from where he was standing.

---

Then that morning went on, and nothing whatever was done about it at any hour of it. A man at the back of that room stood up and asked whether anybody in that room was going to do anything with that day, and about four of them said no in as many ways, and the rest of them went on with whatever they had in their hands. A woman at the front of that room said out loud that she had been in that room every morning of this flood and had not once gone out of it able to show anybody where anything had been settled, and nobody in that room argued with her about that either.

At about the seventh hour a man came up that stair, stopped on the top step, stood there looking round the room at nothing in particular, and went back down it again, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him what he had come up for and nobody saw him go into the row.

A woman with a bundle came up that stair and had to turn her own shoulder sideways to get past that bench, because two people had put their chairs at the near end of it in a way that took half the floor away from the wall. She got past, and stood against the wall at the far end of that room with the bundle on her own knees, and about four people looked at her and the rest of them did not, and nobody moved either of the two chairs.

She put her right hand flat on the page at the head of the column on the right, and moved it away again without touching the word that is there, and set it down on the bench beside the book, and turned the page back the way it had been lying, because it had not been turned.

"**I keep that book and a book is the only thing in this city that can carry a thing out of a room, and I have been sitting at this end of this bench for fifty days with it open in front of me, and not one person in this room has ever asked me what that column on the right of the page is for, and the one word in it is not in my hand, and nobody is going to ask me about it this morning either.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that either. Nobody asked her one question about it. About four of the people in that room went on with what they had in their hands and the rest of them looked at nothing, and the second slate on the shelf behind her stood where it has stood since the day it was put there, with its day across the head of it and nothing under it.

The light came off the one window in that room, went along the top of the bench, came to the near end of the bench where her hands were, and stopped there. She moved her own left hand along the wood a little way from where it had been, and turned the other one over, and looked at the two open pages without reading anything down either of them.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said nothing at all, which is what he had been doing for the whole of the morning, and about four people in that room went on talking about a thing that had nothing to do with the room, and the woman who keeps the book did not go and stand among them.

Nobody asked either of those two men in that room one thing about each other at any hour of that morning, and nobody has ever asked either of them about the other, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who could be shown a page that says what either of them has said to the other man.

---

At about the tenth hour she stood up off that bench, and pushed the book a little way towards the middle of it, and left her own two hands flat on the bench on either side of where it lay, and nobody in that room said a word about that either, and nobody asked her to put it back where it had been.

She went to the back of that room, past the shelf, and put her own two hands on the rail at the top of that stair, and stood there looking down into that row, and she did not go down it.
