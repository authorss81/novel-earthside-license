# Chapter 0658 — A Man Who Went Over A Plank On A Wet Sill And Came Back Over It

Adrian Vale put both hands down onto the top of that wet sill with the plank lying across it and lifted the near end of that plank about a finger's width, and then set it down again with the near end of it in the place he had picked it up from. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month, a Monday, on the second row of eleven miles of flats, in weather, and the light had not come good along the top of that row.

That plank went across at the one place on that row where the ground on the far side stands under water. He squared both ends of it by hand and he did not drive anything under it and he did not tie it to anything, and it was lying where he had put it at the fourth hour of that day and it was lying there at the seventh hour and it was lying there at the tenth.

The trough was standing against the foot of a wall at the near end of that row with the bucket beside it on the ground, and the rag was on the stone lip of the step beside them, and it was not inside the trough at any hour of that day.

---

A man came along that row at about the ninth hour with his own business and got as far as the wet sill and stopped on the near side of it and looked at the water standing on the far side of it. He went over the top of that plank on his own two feet and stood on the far side of it and put his own hand on the top of it to see if it moved. Then he turned about and came back over the top of it the same way and stood on the near side of it again, and he did not go over it a second time and nobody asked him to.

Nobody on that row told him whose hands had laid that plank down and nobody on that row ever told him. He was not thanked for going over it and he was not asked where he had come out of that row for.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the near end of that row with his own bucket in his right hand and the rag over his own shoulder, taking that bucket down to the standing water at the low place, and he stood about ten paces off that sill while that man was standing on the near side of it. He set that bucket down on the boards of that hut and went back out to that sill. He put his own foot on that plank and put his weight on it and felt it move a little under him, and he took his own foot off it again. Then he crouched down and put his own hand flat on the near end of it and pushed it a finger's width towards himself and let it go and watched it settle back where it had been.

---

There is a hut at the near end of that second row with its door standing open and a bench along one wall of it and nothing on that bench at any hour of that morning. The man of about twenty-seven took that bucket in through that open door and set it down on the floor of it and stood with his own hand on the back of the bench and did not sit down on it.

The man who had come over the plank was not in that hut. He was standing about forty feet away from it on the near side of that sill with his own hand on the top of the plank and the water coming through under the far end of it, and he had his back to the open door.

The man of about twenty-seven said it out loud into the middle of that hut, to the empty floor of it and to the open door and not to the man, and the man did not hear one word of it.

"**A plank across a wet sill is not a step and it does not become a step because a person has gone over the top of it twice. I am not going to tell you whose hands put it there and there is nobody on this row who is going to be told, and if it is still lying here tomorrow morning then it will still be lying here and you will still not be told.**"

Not one person on that row improved on that and not one of them said that he was right and not one of them thanked him for saying it, and the thing that had been said in that hut was said in a room and not on a page and it was not in anybody's hands and the man it was said about forty feet away was doing something else.

---

"I have gone over a thing like that twice in my life with my own two hands on both ends of it and both times it was somebody else that put it down, and I am not going to stand here and work out who put this one down for that man, and if he wants to know he can go and ask somebody, and there is nobody on this row he can ask."

He said that out loud from about ten paces off that sill, to the near end of the plank and not to the hut, and the man of about twenty-seven heard one word of it and did not turn about and the man who had come over the plank heard all of it and said nothing back.

A woman at the near end of that row came along it at about the half hour after the ninth hour and stood in the doorway of that hut and wanted to know whether a person could get a bucket of standing water up onto the low place by going over that plank and carrying it. She said so once and nobody in that hut answered her and the man with the bucket said it to the bucket.

"That plank will take a person across and it will not take a person across with a full bucket on top of it, and I am not putting my hand under that bucket to find out."

Not one person on that row improved on that and not one of them thanked him for saying it, and she went on up that row and did not come back to that hut, and the man who had gone over the plank said one thing about a wet morning and a shut room and was not answered by anybody.

---

At about the eleventh hour that hut was empty with its door standing open and the bench along the wall of it with nothing on it, and the man who had gone over the plank went back over the top of it and went on down that row and out of it.

He did not know that plank had been put down by somebody else's two hands at the fourth hour of that morning, and nobody on that row told him and nobody on that row is going to, and a thing laid across a wet sill that nobody knows the age of is the only kind of thing anybody out on eleven miles of flats has any use for.
