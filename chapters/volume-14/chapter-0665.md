# Chapter 0665 — A Board Carried The Length Of A Wharf Yard And Set Down On The Wrong Stack

Adrian Vale had his hands on a board carried the length of a wharf yard and on the handle of a barrow beside it, with the board balanced across his shoulder and the barrow bumping along in front of him. He wanted that board to go back on the stack it came off, and it went back on a stack, and it was a different board on a different stack, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can tell which is which. It was about the eighth hour on the three hundred and fiftieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and about four people a day walk past that yard and not one of them stopped at it that morning.

The man of about thirty-eight at the wharf stood at the side of that yard with his tally-board under his arm and his knife in his belt, and he has a tally-board and a knife and a barrow and a figure on the side the sun gets to. The board at the face of the stack lay face up with two lines of grey grit across the figure on it, and the grit was not counted and was not wiped, and the figure was not printed in that yard at any hour of that day, and the man who owns it was not asked by anybody. Adrian set the board he carried down beside the stack and not on it, because the stack had settled crooked under the morning damp, and the board he set down slid a hand's width along the side of it and came to rest against the wrong stack.

He put his shoulder to the end of the wrong stack to straighten it, and the whole stack gave a finger's width and settled back, and a barrow standing empty at the side of the yard rolled forward off its rest where his foot had touched the handle in passing. The wharf man caught the barrow by the side before it reached the flags, and he had to step out of his place to do it, and the place where he had stood was taken by the end of the board Adrian was easing along.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went past the mouth of that yard with his own board under his arm, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle and a piece of chalk he does not use. He put the flat of his hand over his own inside breast pocket at the mouth of the yard and did not open it, and what did not come out of it was the piece that went in on the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood. He did not say why he kept his hand there, and nobody in that yard asked him, and the hand came away and went back to his side.

Out past the last named house three barrows stand on a road that is shut, and no barrow in this city has anything on it, and the handcart with the coil of rope is not one of the three. Four hundred miles inland is the town a page came from, and nobody in that yard was sent to it and nobody in it spoke of sending anybody. The wharf man ran his empty barrow back to its place and set it down, and he looked at the two stacks and at the board leaning between them, and he said nothing about which of the two it had come off.

"**A board off a stack in the damp sits where it sits, and a yard with two stacks in it has room for a board to lean, and the morning is not the time to lift what the damp has set.**"

He said it to the yard and not to Adrian, and Adrian lifted the board again and set it flat on the top of the nearer stack, and the nearer stack was the wrong one, and nobody in that yard thanked him for the lifting.

---

Adrian picked up the barrow handle he had touched and set the barrow back on its rest with the handles level, and the wharf man watched the handles settle and put his own hand on one of them to feel if it sat true. The board stayed on the wrong stack through all of it, and the grit on the face-up board stayed where it was, and the sun stayed on the side it always gets to. The stallholder went on down the row with his hand off his pocket, and the pocket stayed shut, and the chalk stayed where it was.

"**The damp sets a stack crooked and a hand sets it crooked-er, and by the tenth hour the sun will have set both right, and till then a board leans where it leans.**"

The wharf man put his tally-board back under his arm without having marked anything on it, and Adrian left the yard with his hands empty, and he found that the morning had let him put a board somewhere dry at the cost of putting it in the wrong place, and he kept walking.
