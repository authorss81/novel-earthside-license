# Chapter 0655 — A Hand On The Rim Of A Box Of Chalk And A Thumb's Width Of Grit Under It

Adrian Vale had the flat of his right hand on the rim of the store's box of chalk where it stands on the step above those flags, and he put his thumb down into the gap on the near side of that rim and felt for what was in the gap and then took his thumb out of it and did not do that again. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, at that end of the market, and the front of that store was still in shadow.

There is about a thumb's width of grit under the near side of that rim. Nobody in that row counted it and nobody in that row is going to count it, and the lid of that box lies down on the rim of it and has never shut on any day of this flood, and it was not going to shut on this one.

The store's own chalk is in a heap on those flags with about a third of it gone into the channel. The other chalk in this flood is in the inside breast pocket of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, and it went into that pocket on the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and came out of it once, on the six hundred and thirty-eighth day of this flood, and went back the same afternoon without having been used. There are two pieces of chalk in this city and they are not one chalk, and no hour of that day put both of them in one hand.

The store's board was off its two nails and standing on its lower edge against the foot of that wall with a wet arm across the face of it, and the man who had that board in his hands at that hour was not going to wash it twice. Three sets of figures of this city's own are on the face of it, none of the three rubbed out, and not one of the three carrying a line of words set under it. The stone above the top edge of that board was not washed on that morning and by no second person, and it was washed once in the whole of this flood on a day that is not one of this run of days, by the man who has that board in his own hands every morning of it.

---

There are three things in this city that a person can look at and get three different answers out of, and none of them is a page and none of them is a wall.

The face of that board read down, one set of figures after another from the top of it to the bottom of it, gives one answer. The same face read across, the three sets sitting against each other in lines, gives another. A room with about nine people in it gives a third, and no instrument in four hundred miles is going to put those three in one order.

About nine people were in the room over that market on that Friday morning and not one of them had been sent for anything. A man at the step wanted that board read into that room and the words said out loud with the figures, on the grounds that a set of figures nobody will read out loud is a set of figures only one man has ever looked at. A woman at the far end of that bench wanted that board taken off its nails and put on the table under the one window where a person could come at it and see it while it was being read. Neither of them got anything, and nobody in that room read anything out loud.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was standing at the near end of that bench with his own satchel with a flap behind him and the flap down, and what he wanted out of that morning was a thing on that room's own table that had come up that road and could be set against anything at all. He did not put a hand on that flap and nobody in that room asked him what was in it.

---

A man of about thirty-four came up that stair at about the eighth hour with his own board under his left arm. He leaned that board up against the end of that bench with its bare back out in the room and stood in front of it, and nobody in that room had asked him to bring it up there.

He said it to that bench and not to anybody in that room, and nobody there had asked him for it.

"**That board on the front of that store has three sets of figures on it and not one of the three carries a word under it, and it has been that way every morning of this flood, and there are about nine people in this room who would each read it a different way and not one of you is going to read it out loud this morning because nobody has asked you to and I have not asked you to.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody argued with it and nobody thanked him for saying it, and nobody in that room said one word back to him about it either.

He put his hand flat on the bare back of that board of his own and left it there while he said one more thing to that bench.

"**I have carried a board under that arm out past the last house that has a name on it and back again in this flood and there is a thing out past the last house that has a name on it that I stood in front of and did not touch, and I have not asked her one single thing about that column, and neither of those two is because I am a man who is careful. It is because a man who asks is a man who has to be answered.**"

That did not get one word back from anybody in that room either, and the man of about twenty-seven stood at that bench with his rag over his own shoulder and did not answer it and was not asked to.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came up that stair behind him at about the half hour after the eighth hour with his hands empty and the rag across his shoulder, and he stood at that bench and did not take the rag off his own shoulder.

He said it to the rag and not to the man standing in front of the bench.

"I have washed that board in two passes every morning since the water came and I washed it in one pass this morning, and it is going to want a second one by noon, and it is not going to get it, and nobody in this row has ever asked me how many times I do it and nobody in this row is going to be told."

Not one person in that room made a better thing of that and not one of them thanked him for it, and the two of them stood at that bench with about nine people in that room and about four of those nine had worked out that those two men had said two true things to each other and about four had not, and neither of those two groups said one word to the other about it.

---

Adrian Vale was at that step before the light with the rag over his shoulder and his hand on the rim of that box, and there is a thing at that step he has wanted every morning of this flood and has not got.

He put the flat of his hand on that lid and pushed it down over the rim of that box and let go of it, and it stood up off the rim again the way it stands. He put his hand on it a second time and pushed it down and let go of it, and it stood up off the rim again, and about a thumb's width of grit under the near side of that rim did not move while he did either of those two things. He did not put a stone on it and he did not stand that box on its side and he did not shift the heap of chalk under it, and he put the rag back on the stone lip of that step where it belongs before he stepped back off that step.

Nobody in that row asked him what he was doing at that step and nobody in that row thanked him for anything he did at it.

The man of about twenty-seven came back down that stair at about the ninth hour for his own bucket, which was where he had left it that morning, and he picked it up by the handle in his own right hand and carried it back up the stair with it and did not look at the step on the way.

That board went back up on its two nails at about the half hour after the fourth hour, and the man who washed it stood it up himself with a wet arm across the face of it and put his hand flat on the top edge of it where the wood met the stone, and he did not go above that edge with his hand, and the rag went back on the stone lip of that step.

At about the ninth hour a woman came along that row and put the flat of her hand on the lower edge of that board to lean past it and went on, and it came away from her hand in the same shape it has gone away from every hand that has leaned past it, and the lid of the store's own box of chalk was lying down on the rim of that box at that hour, and it has never shut on any day of this flood and is not going to shut on this one.
