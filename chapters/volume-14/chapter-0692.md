# Chapter 0692 — A Trough Carried Up Out Of Standing Water Onto The Flags And A Rag Put Back On The Same Stone Lip Before The Light Came Off Them

The trough was standing in the low ground at that end of the market with the water going over the rim of it, and Adrian Vale had both hands under the near lip of it with his fingers hooked under the edge, and he lifted. It came up out of that low ground with a foot of water in it and the whole weight of the water going over one side and stopping dead against the other, and the bottom of it was silted through with the grey stuff that has been coming down off the flats since Saturday. It was about the second hour on the three hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and the light had not come off the flags yet, and the rag was not on the stone lip of the step by the store's end of that row, because he had it rolled in his own right hand.

The bucket was standing out in the channel at the side of that row with about a third of water in it, and the flags at the end of that market were wet all the way across from the last night's water, and the step the rag belongs to is the top step of a stone at that end of that row with the box of the store's chalk out of a box on a step whose lid does not shut standing at the back of it and a thumb's width of grit under the near side of that rim that has been there longer than anybody has been keeping anything.

---

He carried the trough up off the low ground and up onto the flags, and set it down on its own base at the top of the stone, and it did not go over, and the water that was in it went over the rim in one long sheet and ran off the flags and into the channel at the side of that row where the water was already going that way anyway. He filled it again out of the channel with the bucket, twice, and the second time the bucket came up heavy and he put it down on the flags with a sound and stood with his own two hands on the handle of it and looked at the trough standing there on the flags where it had not been standing since Saturday.

He worked the rag along the outside of the trough from the near end to the far end and back again and the water in it went grey and then stopped going grey, and he wrung it out over the channel, and he put his own hand flat on the top edge of the stone at the end of the row and pushed the rag along that stone with the heel of his palm until it lay on the lip of that step and stayed there.

That lip is where it lives and where it has been every morning of this flood, and it was not inside that trough at any hour of that day, and nothing in that row put it anywhere else.

Adrian stood at the far end of those flags at about the third hour with his own two hands in his coat pockets and watched the light come off them, and the trough stood on the flags by the step with water in it and the bucket standing beside it with its own mouth up, and the rag lay on the stone lip of the step where the man who washes that board leaves it.

The wall above the top edge of that board is a different thing from the board and was washed once in the whole of this flood and by no second person on any day of it, and Adrian did not put a hand on it that morning or at any other hour of that day, and no water he ran went above the top edge of that board, and the water he ran went along the foot of that wall and stopped at the bottom of the board.

---

At about the fourth hour, before the light had come off those flags, the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the end of that row, and he was there earlier in the week than he has ever been there, and he had his own bucket in one hand and his own two fingers out towards the stone lip of the step as he came up to it.

He stopped. His own two fingers were on the stone lip of that step and the rag was lying on it, and he did not put his hand inside the trough and he did not turn round and look for the trough, and he did not look down at the flags to see whether there was water standing on them, and he did not say one word about where the trough had been or where the water had come from.

The other piece of chalk in this city that could leave a mark is a piece of chalk in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it belongs to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, and that piece stayed inside that pocket at the fourth hour of that morning and was still inside it at the tenth hour of it, and it did not come out of it. No hand in this city had both of those two chalks in it at any hour of that day, and no hand in this city has had both of them in it at any hour of this flood.

"**That rag has been on that lip since before I came round the end of this row and I have not once come round here and found it anywhere else, and I am not going to start asking a row where a thing has been kept in this flood.**"

He said that to the flags and to the water coming off them and not to anybody, and there was nobody else on that end of that row at that hour to say it to, and he went at the trough, and washed his board, and came away off that wall with the rag wet in his own hand, and hung the rag back on the same stone lip of the same step, and it was on that lip by the time the light came off those flags.

He did not ask Adrian anything. Adrian was at the far end of those flags with his own two hands in his coat pockets and had not said a word and was not asked one and had not been standing where the man who washes that board could see him.

---

A woman came up that row at about the sixth hour with a hand-basket on her arm and no hat, and she reached the place where the trough is standing on the flags with water going over the rim of it, and the stone at that end of that row is about as wide as her own two feet side by side, and the trough is standing across it. She stopped, and set the basket down on the high side of the trough on the dry stone, and stepped over into the low ground on the far side of it, and the water that had been going over that rim went into the boot on her own left foot up to over the ankle, and she stood there for a moment with her own weight on the other foot and looked down at it.

She did not say anything to Adrian. She was not going to say anything to Adrian, and she did not know he was at the far end of those flags, and she picked the basket up off the stone and put her arm back through the handle of it and went on up that row with her own boot full of that water and the water going out of it with every step she took on the high side of the stone.

Adrian watched her go as far as the end of that row, and he did not go after her and did not call out to her, and nobody in that row thanked him for anything that morning and nobody told him he was right about anything, and there was nobody in that row waiting for him to be useful to them and he was not useful to any of them.

He came back along the flags at about the ninth hour, and the trough was still standing on the flags at the top of the stone with the bucket beside it, and he took his own right hand out of his coat and put it flat on the near rim of the trough and turned the whole thing over onto its side, and the water that had been standing in it since Saturday went out of it in one go and across the flags and down into the channel at the side of that row, and it lay on its side on the wet stone with its open mouth to the weather.

The man of about twenty-seven came back round the end of that row at about the tenth hour with his own two hands free, and he saw the trough on its side, and he stopped, and he did not say one word about it, and he put both hands on the far rim of it and turned it back over onto its own base and set it down on the flags the way it stands at the end of that row, and he took the bucket up in his own right hand and went off up that row towards the market without looking at the rag on the stone lip above the step.
