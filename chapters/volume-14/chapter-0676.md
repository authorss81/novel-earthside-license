# Chapter 0676 — A Book Picked Up By Two People In Turn And Set Back Down The Wrong Way Up Until A Third Person Turns It Right

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands on the spine of the book as she took it off the bench and stood it on the shelf, and she turned it as she stood it, the way she has turned it every morning of this flood, and put her hands back flat on the bench on either side of the space where it had been. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and sixty-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and about nine people were in that room.

Any run of fifty days has two days in the middle of it, and neither of them is the middle, and this was the second of the two, and no one in that room knew it, and the ninth hour in that room was the same as the ninth hour on any other day of that week. The decision that had been made in that room the day before was not written on anything and had gone out of the room with nobody, and the piece of board about as wide as a hand under the date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room was carrying what it had been carrying every morning of this flood, which was a date and a hand's width of nothing, and about four people went past it before the eighth hour and got the date.

Behind where the book had been, the second slate stood on the shelf with its written face to the plaster, and a day had been cut across the top of it and nothing whatever stood under that day. She wanted the book set back on that shelf the way up it stands, and on Thursday a man in that room had picked it up to look at a figure in the middle of the page and had set it down the other way up, and she had let him, and she had not said a word about it, and she had not said a word about it on any of the mornings before that either.

---

Two people picked that book up between the fourth hour and the seventh hour that Friday. A woman with nothing in her hands picked it up to look at the middle of the page, where five figures stood one under another with a day written in beside each of them, and she set it back on the shelf the wrong way up and went away. Then a man at the far end of that room picked it up, and looked at the same five figures, and set it back down on the shelf the wrong way up and went back to where he had been standing.

The keeper looked at it twice. The one thing that room had and had never said out loud was which way up that book goes on its own shelf, and she had never said it because there had never been any reason to say it, and the reason there was now was that a person had got it wrong and she had watched him do it.

A man who had come in out of the row with his hat in his hands came to the bench and put his own hand flat on the book and turned it round on the shelf without asking anybody whether it wanted turning, and stood it up the way it stands, and put his hat under his arm and went to the far end of that room and sat down.

Nobody in that room said thank you to him for it and nobody said that he had done it right.

"**A book goes on a shelf the one way, and the one way is the way it has gone on that shelf every morning since the shelf was a shelf, and I have kept that one way in my own hands for a very long time and never once had to say it out loud, and I am not going to start saying it out loud because a man got it wrong on Thursday.**"

The book on the shelf got it and the man who had turned it did not, because he had gone to sit down at the far end, and nobody improved on it, and the two people who had got it wrong were not told they had got it wrong.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board went down that stair at about the sixth hour, his own board against his ribs and its face turned in, and the room went on for about an hour after that with about four people in it and then fewer than that, and the thing the keeper had said to the shelf was in nobody's hands and was on no page, and the room it had been said in had about four people in it and the one person it was said to was out in that row with his back to the window at the time and did not hear it.

At about the ninth hour that room was empty except for the woman who keeps the register and a man at the far end who had not moved since the sixth hour. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was not in that room on that Friday and had not been in it since the day before, and nobody in that room said where he was, and nobody went to look.

The keeper took her two hands off the bench, and the room was about to be the same room it had been on Thursday with nothing on it that said what had happened in it, and she had come to the end of the week without the one thing she had wanted and had not said.

"**Two people got that book wrong this morning and neither of them will ever know they got it wrong, and the third one got it right and will never know that either, and the whole of what a shelf is for is that it does not keep the part anybody learns.**"

She said it to the empty bench and not to the man at the far end who had not moved since the sixth hour, and nobody in that room heard it, and the room said nothing back.

He came back up that stair with his board under his arm and came to the near end of that bench, and put his own board down on the floor against the leg of it, and did not set it on the bench.
