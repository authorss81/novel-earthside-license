# Chapter 0687 — Two Hands At Each End Of A Bench In A Room With Nine People In It And A Stool Standing In The Dark End

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had one hand flat on the top of that bench at the end nearest the window and the other flat on the top of it at the end nearest the stair, and she was clearing it. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and seventy-second day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and four people were in that room, and the light off the one window had not yet come across the bench.

The register stood on the shelf behind her with five figures down the middle of the page, a day entered against each, nothing struck through, and the one word at the head of the column on the right of that page where it has been since the day it was put there. The second slate on that same shelf was standing with its face to the wall, a day cut across the top of it and the rest of it bare, and it stayed where it was that morning and her own hand was the only one that went near that shelf.

Against the far wall of that room, in the dark end where the light off the one window does not reach, a stool was standing that had not been standing there before the Saturday, and nobody in that room knew who had put it there or why it was there.

Outside, at the foot of that stair, somebody had chalked a date on that door, the three hundred and seventy-second day of that month in full, and everything below that date was bare wood the width of a hand, and four people went by in the ordinary course of the morning and took the date away with them.

---

There have been seven notices out of this city in this flood and every one of them left on its own morning on its own sheet, and each of them went to an office that reads what it is handed and writes a name against it, and that office is nine hundred miles off. Not one of the seven has come back, and this city has put nothing out since and is not going to put out an eighth.

"**I have cleared this bench because a room with nine people in it needs the whole length of it, and I am not doing it for a reason anybody could put down anywhere, because there is no page in this room and there is not going to be one.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for clearing a bench and nobody improved on it, and about four people went on doing what they had been doing and about four moved their own things off the bench because she had cleared it.

She took the two things that had been standing at the middle of it off and put them on the shelf beside the second slate without touching the slate, and she went along the length of that bench with the cloth in her hand and wiped the wood where the book had stood every morning of this flood, and she did not wipe the middle of it.

---

A sheet of floor paper had been rolled up tight and stood against the leg of that bench at the far end and it had been standing there since before anybody in this city began keeping anything. She got her own hand round the roll and lifted it off the stone where it had been standing against the leg of the bench. She carried it the length of that bench and put it down on the wood and unrolled it along its own length until the roll was gone out of it. She laid it in the position it had been rolled at, which was with the near edge of it short of the front edge of the bench and the far edge of it square with the back of it.

Nothing was written on it. It was not a form and it had never been one, and it had not been near a book all morning and it was not going to be near one after that, and four of the people in that room looked at it and the rest of them did not.

By the eighth hour there were about nine people in that room and the sheet was still lying on that bench where she had put it, and a man at the back of the room put his own hand flat on it to lean past somebody and left a print of his hand on the paper and did not notice he had done it, and the paper carried that print for the rest of that morning and nobody wiped it off, and by the tenth hour it had gone into the paper and would not have come off.

---

"**That is floor paper and it came off this floor and it is going back where it was rolled, and it is not going to carry a single thing on it this morning, and I would rather it carried nothing than carried one thing that was wrong.**"

Nobody in that room asked her what she would have said was right and nobody argued with her about the paper, and the room went on with the morning it had.

A man who had left a thing on a chair in that room came up that stair at about the tenth hour and saw the cleared bench and the sheet lying on it and said nothing whatever about either of them. He took his own thing off the chair, and then he reached out and turned the near edge of that sheet over with the tip of one finger so that the print went under the paper, and he went back down the stair without a word about it.
