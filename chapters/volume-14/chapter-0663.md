# Chapter 0663 — Four People In A Room Saying What A Page With A Word In It Cannot Be Shown To Be

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her hands flat on the bench in the room over that market, with the book on its shelf behind her and the morning light coming off the one window. Her second slate stood behind that book on the same shelf, and on the right of the open page there was a column with a word in it near the head of it that was not hers. It was about the eighth hour on the three hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and about nine people were in that room, and not one of them had been sent for anything.

That book stands on its shelf with its spine to the wall, and the page of it that is open is ruled into three, and the middle one of the three holds five figures put in one under another in the order they were made with a day written in beside each of them, and not one of the five is struck out and no sixth has been entered. The column on the right of that page carries one word near the head of it and nothing anywhere else on that page, and it went in there in a hand that is not the keeper's on a day that is not one of this run of days, and no second word goes into it. Behind that book on the same shelf stands a second slate, up on its edge with its written face turned to the plaster, and a day is cut across the head of it and nothing whatever stands under that day, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on, and the only hand that came near that shelf was the keeper's.

Seven documents have come up that road or gone out of it in this flood, and that is the number of documents and not the number of notices, and a notice is not a document, and the two sevens in this city are two sevens. Three of the seven are of four places each and four of them are of one place each. The first of the four one-place sheets is in this city with a figure in its one place, and it was not read out in that room that morning and was not laid beside anything and was in nobody's hand. The second went out of this city and is not in it. The third went back up the road with a figure in its one place and a line of words set under that figure and is not in it. The fourth went back up the road with a word where a figure has never been and is not coming back. No two of the four were laid beside each other, no two were in one hand, and nobody in that room compared any two of them out loud, and a keeper is not a holder.

---

One word went in the column on the right of that page on a day that is not one of this run of days, and one word went in the one place on a sheet on a day that is not one of this run of days, and the two places are two places and the two hands are two hands and the two days are two days, and neither is printed in that room and neither is spoken in it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stood at the far end of that bench with his own board under his arm, and the face of it was turned in against his ribs, and a piece of chalk he did not use was in his inside breast pocket, where it had been put on the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and had come out once, on the six hundred and thirty-eighth, and had gone back the same afternoon.

A person in that room said out loud that a page written in by somebody else and a page that was always like that are the same page, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that will tell anybody which of the two this one is. Four people in that room meant four different things by whether that surface may be written on, and the first meant it may be because a hand already had, and the second meant it may not be because a hand already had, and the third meant the writing settled nothing, and the fourth meant the settling was the writing, and no two of them were wrong.

"**I keep this book and I did not stop that hand and I was not asked, and a page that has a hand in it besides mine is still the page I keep, and I cannot show you which of the two it is.**"

She said it to the bench and not to him, and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it, and nobody improved on it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair with his board under his arm, and he had nineteen rates on it and one name that was not on it, and he stood at the near end of the bench and listened and did not open his mouth. The room did not ask the keeper what the column is for, and it did not ask the stallholder what he put in it, and it did not ask the tradesman about the space on his own board, and the three not-askings sat in that room like three people.

She laid the flat of her hand on the bench beside the book without touching the shelf, and she looked at the four who had spoken and at the four who had said nothing, and the morning went on.

"**A room can say a thing about a page and the page stays where it was, and the shelf stays where it was, and the only thing that has changed is that nine people heard it, and hearing is not showing.**"

She put her hands back flat on the bench, and the book stayed on its shelf, and the slate stayed on its edge, and she found that the room had said what it could say and the page had kept what it kept, and she kept her hands where they were.
