# Chapter 0681 — Both Hands Of A Man On The Strap Of A Satchel Standing Behind A Bench In A Room With Nothing To Write On

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had both hands on the strap of the satchel at his right shoulder where it came out of the bag, and the two thumbs of those hands were hooked under the flap to hold it flat over what was in it. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and sixty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and about four people were in the room over that market and the light off the one window had not come round onto the bench.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with the register open in front of her and her two hands on the bench on either side of it. Five figures stood one under another in the middle of that page with a day written in beside each of the five and none of them struck out, and one word sat near the head of the column on the right of it and that word is not hers. On the shelf behind her a slate stood turned to the plaster with a day cut across the top of it and nothing at all under that day, and it has stood there every morning of this flood and has never been picked up.

The door at the foot of that stair had a date in chalk on the outside of it and the three hundred and sixty-sixth day of that month was on that date in full, and the wood under it was bare for the width of a hand with nothing whatever on it. About four people went past that door in the ordinary course of that morning and none of them stood at it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm with the face of it turned in against his ribs, and he asked for a line of writing. He asked in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room and he asked twice, and what he asked for was a line that would say what this city was short of, and he said in front of about four people that he did not care what it was written on.

The man with the satchel had come up that stair at the fourth hour with nothing in it and had been standing behind that bench ever since, and what he wanted before the tenth hour was one thing put in it, and there was nothing in that room that anybody was going to be given to put in it.

"**I can carry a thing that is written and I cannot make one, and those two are not the same work and I have only ever had the first of them.**"

The three others in that room heard it and did not take it up, and the woman at the near end of the bench did not look up from the page in front of her, and the man who had asked stood with the board under his arm and did not ask a second thing about it.

The man of about thirty-nine took the board out from under his arm and stood it against the side of that bench with the bare back of it to the room, and then put his right hand out towards the strap of the satchel, and the man with the satchel moved it along the floor out of the reach of that hand without hurrying, and the strap came with it and the hand came away with nothing, and the four people in that room went on watching a strap.

---

About the seventh hour there were about nine people in that room and the thing being asked had not changed and had got no nearer. A woman at the back of the room said that a shortage is a thing you can put in front of somebody in an office and a woman beside her said that no office in this flood has sent anybody anything at all, and neither of them looked at the register, and both of them were right, and about nine people heard the two of them and about four of them went back to what they had been doing.

Four sheets of one shape had been on a page in this flood. One of them was on a shelf in this city at the height of a person's shoulder, with a figure in its one place and its one place ruled under a heading where no figure had ever stood. A second went out of this city on the five hundred and forty-fifth day. A third went back up the road on the five hundred and ninety-fifth with a line of words under its figure. The fourth went back up on the six hundred and forty-fifth with a word where a figure has never been, and it is not in this city and nothing brings it back. Three of those four were somewhere else and the one that was in this city was not written in by anybody that day and was not read out in that room, and the two kinds of empty on the shelf and in the satchel were not compared by anybody in that room, because there was only one of them in the room to compare.

"**I carried the last one of those up the road myself and I did not open it and I am not going to be asked about it by anybody in this room this morning.**"

Nobody asked him about it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood at the far end of that room and looked at the space at the foot of the column between the other two on his own board, which he did not put a finger into, and then he asked the man with the satchel to write the line out for him in front of about nine people, and the man with the satchel said no.

---

Seven documents have gone out of this city in this flood and three of them were of four places and four of them were of one place, and every one of them went to an office nine hundred miles off, and this city has written nothing since and has not said it will. The room found out the thing it had found out that morning in about an hour of the ordinary talk a room talks in, which is that the only person standing in it who had handled every page that had ever gone up that road was the only person standing in it who could not produce one, and nobody in that room thanked anybody for that and nobody said it out loud.

The man with the satchel got his right hand off the strap and put both hands down flat on the top of the bag where it stood behind that bench, and pulled the flap down over the top of it and pressed it flat along its whole length with the heel of his palm, and the satchel stood there behind that bench with the flap down.
