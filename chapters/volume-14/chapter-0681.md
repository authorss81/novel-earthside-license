# Chapter 0681 — Both Hands Of A Man On The Strap Of A Satchel Standing Behind A Bench In A Room With Nothing To Write On

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was standing behind that bench with the strap of the satchel in both hands where it came out over his right shoulder, and the two thumbs of those hands were hooked under the flap to hold it flat. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and sixty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and about four people were in that room with the light off the one window still short of the bench.

At the near end of that bench the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register sat with the register open in front of her, both palms flat on the wood, one on each side of it. Five figures stood down the middle of that page with a day entered against each of them and not one of the five struck through, and up at the head of the column on the right of that page there was a single word in somebody else's hand. On the shelf behind her a second slate stood with its face to the wall and a day cut across the top of it, and there was nothing at all under that day, and in this flood nobody has ever lifted it off that shelf.

Chalk was on the outside of that door at the foot of that stair, and a date was in it — the three hundred and sixty-sixth day of that month, written out in full — and the wood under that date ran bare for the width of a hand. Four people went by that door that morning in the ordinary way and not one of them stood at it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came to the far end of that room with his own board held up flat against his ribs, face in, and he asked for a line of writing. He asked in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room and he asked twice, and what he asked for was a line that would say what this city was short of, and he said in front of about four people that he did not care what it was written on.

The man with the satchel had come up that stair at the fourth hour with nothing in it and had been standing behind that bench ever since, and before the tenth hour he wanted one thing put into it, and there was nothing in that room that anybody was going to be handed over for the purpose.

"**I can carry a thing that is written and I cannot make one, and those two are not the same work and I have only ever had the first of them.**"

The three others in that room heard all of it and none of them took it up, and she kept her eyes on the page in front of her, and the man who had asked it stood there with the board against his ribs and did not ask it a second time.

The man of about thirty-nine took the board out from under his arm and stood it against the side of that bench with the face of it out to the room, and then put his right hand out towards the strap of the satchel, and the man with the satchel moved it along the floor out of the reach of that hand without hurrying, and the strap came with it and the hand came away with nothing, and the four people in that room went on watching a strap.

---

About the seventh hour there were about nine people in that room and the thing being asked had not changed and had got no nearer. A woman at the back of the room said that a shortage is a thing you can put in front of somebody in an office and a woman beside her said that no office in this flood has sent anybody anything at all, and neither of them looked at the register, and both of them were right, and about nine people heard the two of them and about four of them went back to what they had been doing.

There have been four sheets of one shape on a page in this flood. One of them is in this city on a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder, with one place ruled under a heading where no figure has ever stood and a figure standing in that one place. A second went out of this city on the five hundred and forty-fifth day. A third went back up the road on the five hundred and ninety-fifth with a line of words under its figure. The fourth went back up on the six hundred and forty-fifth carrying a word in the one place under the heading, where no figure has ever stood, and it is not in this city and nothing brings it back. Three of those four were somewhere else and the one that was in this city was not written in by anybody that day and was not read out in that room, and the shelf and the satchel were not set beside each other by anybody in that room, because there was only one of the two in it.

"**I carried the last one of those up the road myself and I did not open it and I am not going to be asked about it by anybody in this room this morning.**"

Nobody asked him about it. The man of about thirty-nine was still at the far end of that room with the flat of his own hand on the face of that board, looking down at the bare patch at the foot of the middle of its three columns, and he did not put a finger on it, and then he asked the man with the satchel to write the line out for him in front of about nine people, and the man with the satchel said no.

---

Seven documents have been on a page in this flood, three of them of four places and four of them of one place, and six of the seven went to an office nine hundred miles off, and the seventh is on a shelf in this city and has never gone anywhere. The room found out the thing it had found out that morning in about an hour of the ordinary talk a room talks in, which is that the only person standing in it who had handled every page that had ever gone up that road was the only person standing in it who could not produce one, and nobody in that room thanked anybody for that and nobody said it out loud.

The man with the satchel took both his hands off the strap of the bag and put them behind his own back where the whole of that room could see them, and the flap of the bag settled flat on the top of it without them, and the strap went slack over his shoulder where two thumbs had been holding that flap down since the light came.
