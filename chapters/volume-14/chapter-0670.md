# Chapter 0670 — A Board Standing Face In Against A Trestle While The Row Walks Past Without Counting

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hands on the trestle at his own front and on the edge of his own board where it stood face in against it. The chalk was in his inside breast pocket and had been in it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, with one afternoon in between, the six hundred and thirty-eighth, when a hand went in after it and put it back the same day, and he had not used it at any hour of this flood. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and the row was filling with the morning trade.

His board stood with the bare back of it out in the row, the way it has stood every morning of this flood, and the face of it was turned in where nobody in the row could see it. About four people went past his front before the eighth hour and got nothing off that back but the sight of bare wood, and about four more went past and did not look at it at all, and nobody took the number of either lot, and nobody in that row said what the board had on its face. That is the whole of what went past his front that morning, and the row itself does not add it up.

That board at the end of the market is on the outside wall of a store, and three sets of figures of this city's own stand on it, none of them rubbed out, and not one of the three with a line of words set under it. Nothing on any surface in this city carries a line of words set under a figure, and the one line that did is outside this city and is not coming back. The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came down the row with his bucket, and he had a bucket and a rag and a wall he had washed once in the whole of this flood on a day that is not one of this run of days, and the rag was on the stone lip of the step.

---

A woman at the next front said the row was busy and a man said the row was slow, and the two of them wanted two different things out of the morning and neither got what was wanted. The stallholder lifted his board off the trestle and sighted along the back of it the way a man does when he wants to know if damp has got into the wood, and he set it back face in with the bare back out. One of the people going past stopped for as long as it takes to shift a basket from one arm to the other and looked at the bare back of that board, and the stallholder did not turn the board round while the person was standing there and did not turn it round afterwards either, and the woman at the next front watched him not turn it round and said nothing about that either.

"**A back out in the row tells a person nothing, and a face turned in keeps what it keeps, and the one morning I turn it round is a morning this row has not seen.**"

He said it to the trestle and not to the row, and nobody thanked him for saying it, and nobody asked him what was on the face. The washer put his bucket down by the trough and went on to the store's board, and the keeper looked down from the one window of the room over the market and then drew back from it.

---

He stood at his own front through the eighth hour with his hand resting on the top edge of the trestle, and the row went past him in both directions, and the board stayed where it was. The piece of chalk stayed in his pocket through the whole morning and was not out of it at any hour of that day, and no hand went into that pocket after anything.

"**Four go past and four do not look, and the wood stays bare to all eight, and a stall that keeps its face to itself keeps the only thing it has.**"

He took his hand off the trestle and went inside his own front for a pail of water, leaving the board standing face in where it was, and the row went on past it without him. He was in there long enough for the row to thin out and come back, and he came out with the pail and set it down in the place it had always stood in, and he did not turn the board round to look at it.
