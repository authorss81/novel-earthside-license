# Chapter 0686 — Both Hands Of A Man Flat On The Top Of A Satchel Standing Between His Own Boots On The Bottom Step Of A Stair

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had both hands flat on the top of the satchel where it stood on the bottom step between his own two boots, and it had been behind the bench in that room on the Wednesday and had come back to him since, with the flap of it down flat over nothing at all. It was about the sixth hour on the three hundred and seventy-first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, at the foot of the stair at the back of the room over that market, and about four people went past that door in the ordinary course of that morning and got the date off the chalk on it and did not stop.

The wood under that date is bare for the width of a hand and has been since before anybody in this city can remember, and nothing whatever is on it and nothing has ever been on it in this flood, and no person in this city has been told by anybody that they may put a thing on it.

---

He had come up that stair at the fourth hour with the bag empty and he had been on that step since the light came, and what he wanted before the tenth hour was one thing put in it, and nothing in that row or in that room was going to be given to him.

Twice that morning somebody came down that stair past him and once somebody came up it past him, and each of the three of them had to step out into the row to get by because he was sitting square on the step with the bag against his own shin, and none of the three said anything about it and none of them put a hand on the bag.

A woman carrying a bundle up the row stopped at the foot of the stair and asked him whether he was waiting on somebody, and he said he was not, and she went on up the row and did not ask him anything else and did not come back down it.

"**I have come up this stair twice this week with an empty bag and I am not going down it with an empty bag a third time, and I am not going to say in front of this row what I want put in it.**"

About four people going past that stair heard all of that and about four of them did not, and nobody stopped and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him what he carried.

---

A man came down that stair at about the eighth hour with a case in his hand and stopped on the step below, looked at the satchel for a moment, and went on down into the row and away along it. The man on the bottom step put the flat of his own hand on the top of the bag and turned it a quarter round on the stone. It went round on its base without anything shifting inside it, and the flap came to face the stair, and he turned it back the way it had been and put his hand on the step beside it.

Four sheets of one shape have been on a page in this flood and this man carried the last one of them up the road out of this city on the six hundred and forty-fifth day and it is not in this city and nothing brings it back. One place is ruled under a heading on that sheet where no figure has ever stood and one word went into it, and he knows that, and he has never opened anything he has carried in this flood, not that one and not the three that went before it.

The third of them went back up the road before that one did and had a line of words under the figure in its one place, and none of the three that are gone has come back and none of them is going to, and the four of them have never been in a room together and never been in a hand together.

The first one of the four is in this city. It is on a shelf at the height of a person's shoulder with a figure in its one place, and nobody wrote in it that morning and nobody read it out and it was not laid beside anything.

"**You are all going to want to know why I have not opened one of them, and I am not going to tell you, and I have not told anybody else either, and this row is not going to be the first place it gets told.**"

A man with a bundle went up past him and stopped two steps below and said that anybody who carried for a living would open a thing, and he said yes, and the man went on up and did not ask him why he had not.

Nobody argued with him about that and nobody said anything against it and nobody thanked him for saying it, and about nine people went up and down that stair behind him for the rest of that morning and got the date off the door and went on.

At about the tenth hour he lifted the satchel off the step, carried it out into the row, and set it down on the trestle of a woman with a cloth over it at that end of the market. He took his hand off the top of it and put his own hand flat on the trestle beside it for a moment and then took that off as well, and went back up the stair empty, and the satchel stood on that woman's trestle with the flap down and she had not touched it and did not know what was in it.
