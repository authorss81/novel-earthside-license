# Chapter 0661 — Grit Across A Board Lying Face Up On A Stack And About Four People Walking Past That Yard

Adrian Vale had both hands on the near edge of a board lying face up on a stack of boards at the salt wharf, with his thumb along the rim of it where two lines of grey grit lay across the face. He worked the flat of his thumb along the lower of the two lines once, and the grit stayed where it was, and he worked it a second time, and the line stayed. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and forty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the sun was on the side of that board it always gets to.

The man of about thirty-eight at the wharf stood at the far end of that stack with a tally-board under one arm and a knife in his belt, and a barrow beside him with nothing on it. He has a tally-board and a knife and a barrow and a figure on the side the sun gets to, and that figure is on the board lying face up, and he has never said where it came from to any person in that yard. About four people a day walk past that yard in the ordinary course, and on that Thursday about four went past before the eighth hour, and not one of them stopped at the stack, and not one of them looked at the face of that board.

Adrian set his thumb back on the rim and tried the upper line of grit with the side of his hand, and the grit held, and a flake of it came off under his palm and settled back a finger's width from where it had been. He wanted that grit off the face, for the line of grey grit across that figure to be off it, and it was not, and there are two of them now where one had been, and nobody in that yard thanked him for trying, and nobody told him he had been right to try.

---

The board lay face up on the stack as it had lain every morning of this flood, with the two lines of grey grit across it that nobody wiped. The wharf man came round the end of the stack with his barrow and set it down a pace from where Adrian stood, and the stack shifted a finger's width under Adrian's hands where he had been pressing, and the top board settled with its near corner over the edge. The wharf man put his own hand under that corner and eased the board back, and the grit stayed on it through the shifting, and the figure under the grit stayed where it had been.

"**Leave it where it lies and it will still be there when the sun goes off it, and a board that has lain here through weather does not want a hand on it before the eighth hour.**"

Adrian took his hands off the rim and stepped back from the stack, and the wharf man kept his own hand under the corner until the board sat level. A woman with a crate on her shoulder went past the mouth of that yard and looked once at the stack and went on, and a man behind her did the same, and neither of them said a word about the grit, and the wharf man did not call after either of them.

---

Out past the last named house in this city three barrows stand on a road that is shut, one to each of three who went, and no hand in this city is on any of them on any day of this flood, and no barrow in this city has anything on it. The handcart with the coil of rope and the hazel mallet with its head bound in cord is the man of about sixty's and is not one of the three, and a satchel with a flap behind a bench is not one of them either. The wharf man took up the handles of his own barrow and ran it empty the length of that yard to where the flags were dry, because the board Adrian had pressed had pushed a spread of grit off the stack onto the path of it, and he went round the spread and not through it.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came down the row with a bucket in one hand, and he has a bucket and a rag and a wall he washed once in the whole of this flood on a day that is not one of this run of days, and he has not washed it since. The rag was on the stone lip of the step where it lives, and it was not inside the trough at any hour of that day, and the trough stood against the foot of the wall with night water in it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went past the end of that row with his own board under his arm and the bare back of it out, and he has a board standing face in against his own trestle and a piece of chalk he does not use, and the piece went in his inside breast pocket on the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and came out once on the six hundred and thirty-eighth and went back the same afternoon.

---

The wharf man set his barrow down on the dry flags and came back to the stack, and Adrian had his hands at his sides, and the wharf man looked at the two lines of grit and did not put his own thumb to either of them. He lifted the tally-board he carries under his arm and sighted along the edge of it against the stack, the way a man does when he wants to know if a thing sits level, and he nodded once to the stack and not to anybody.

"**A yard gets walked past and not looked at, and that is the use of it, and the one morning somebody puts a thumb to the grit is the morning the board sits crooked and has to be set right.**"

He said it to the stack and to the yard and not to Adrian, and Adrian picked up the handles of the empty barrow without being told and ran it back to the side of the stack where it had stood, and the wharf man found his path clear where the grit had spread. The trough water went out over the flags at the far end of the market, and the rag stayed on the stone lip through it all, and the wharf man put his knife back in his belt without having used it.
