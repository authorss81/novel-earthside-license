# Chapter 0677 — A Woman With Her Hands On The Back Of Her Own Stool Naming Out Loud What A Room Did On Thursday And Going Out Without It

The woman of about fifty-two had both hands on the back of her own stool where she had stood it out of the light at the far end of the room over that market, and she had a door of her own at the bottom of a row that nobody had ever asked her to open, and she was not going to be asked to open it on that morning either. It was about the fourth hour on the three hundred and sixty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and about four people were in that room and by the seventh hour there were about nine.

She wanted the woman who keeps that book to be able to sit down in that room. For as long as that book has been kept the bench at the near end of that room has been where it is kept and the bench is also where the light comes off the one window, and every person in that room on an ordinary morning stands at the near end of that bench because that is where a person in a room stands when a room has one place in it where anything happens, and the woman who keeps the book has stood at the near end of it through the whole of this flood without sitting down once.

The far end of that room is the end with no light in it. The far end of that room in this weather is the end where a person stands in wet clothes and is looked at. She had moved her own stool there herself that morning before anybody came in, and she had moved it without saying anything to anybody about moving it.

---

At the sixth hour the stallholder came up that stair, his own board under his arm, its face turned in against his ribs, and took up the only position in that room with his back to the plaster in it, the far end where the light does not reach, and said nothing to anybody and asked nobody anything. The register stood open on the bench at the near end with five figures in the middle of the page and a day written in beside each of them, and near the head of the column on the right of that page there was one word in a hand that is not the keeper's and nothing anywhere else on that page, and the only other word that went into a place in this city went four hundred miles up a road in a different hand and is not in this city and does not come back. The second slate stood on the shelf behind the book on its edge with a day cut across the top of it and nothing under that day, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on that day or any day.

The woman of about fifty-two watched the woman who keeps the book stand at the near end of that bench from the sixth hour to the ninth hour with about nine people within reach of her arm, and she watched a man put his own case down at the near end of that bench twice and pick it up again, and she watched two people stand in the aisle behind the woman who keeps the book and talk to each other about nothing at all with their voices at the height of that woman's ear.

"**I moved a stool out of the light at the fourth hour and nobody asked me why I moved it, and I have been the only person in this room doing anything for that woman since the fourth hour and she has not looked at me once, and that is the arrangement and I made half of it.**"

The far wall of that room got it and nobody heard it, and she went on standing with her own back to the plaster.

Out at the back of that room, where a stair comes down to a landing and the landing goes down to the row, that door had the three hundred and sixty-second day of that month on it in chalk and a hand's width of bare wood under the date, and about four people went past that door that morning and one of them stopped as long as it takes to shift a basket to the other arm and then went on up the stair as if the door were a door.

---

Nobody in that room had asked the woman who keeps the book one thing since Thursday, and the woman of about fifty-two stood up off the wall at the far end and put her own stool against the far wall with her foot and came to the front of that room where about nine people could hear her, and nobody had asked her to do that either.

"**On Thursday nine people were stood in this room and a man said a thing out loud about a piece of board, and from Thursday morning to this morning not one person in this room has asked the woman who keeps that book one single question about it, and that is what this room did, and I am not her and it is not mine and I am saying it here so that it has been said.**"

She put it into the room and nobody thanked her for it, and nobody argued with her, and about four people in that room said they did not know what she was talking about and about four said nothing, and the stallholder looked at the floor of that room for the whole of it and did not say a word, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register kept her two hands flat on the bench and did not look up and did not say anything at all.

The woman of about fifty-two went back to the far end of that room and picked her own stool up off the floor and carried it to the door at the back of that room and went out on to the stair and down it and away along the row, and she left the stool standing against the far wall of that room in the dark end where the light does not reach, so that anybody coming in at the tenth hour would find a thing in that room that had not been in it on Thursday and would not know who had put it there.
