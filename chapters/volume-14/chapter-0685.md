# Chapter 0685 — Both Hands Of A Man On The Near End Of A Length Of Cord Laid Along The Top Of Wet Silt That Was Higher

Adrian Vale had both hands on the near end of the length of cord where it lay along the top of the wet silt in the channel along the low side of that row, holding it down where the water came at it from under the stones of the flats. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and seventieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, on eleven miles of flats with the water coming off them in sheets, and the trough the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had left standing a day before in the low ground where the water comes off the stone was still standing in it with the water going over the rim of it and nobody had gone back for it.

The top of that silt was level with the stone of the causeway on the day before and it is not level with it now, and there is no figure for that anywhere in this city and no person in this city has been shown one, and the four counts that were taken on foot on four mornings out of this row are still four and this morning is not the fifth of them.

A man with a barrow comes along that row at his own hour every day and there has never been anything on it, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came along it at the eighth hour with his barrow empty and a knife in a belt at his hip, and he was not asked anything that morning and he asked nothing.

---

He worked from the near end backwards and he got his own two hands into the silt at the edge of the channel and lifted it out a double handful at a time. He laid every handful of it on the high side of the causeway where the water came at it, and the water went along the top of the cord instead of under it. Where the cord crossed a place the silt had been washed hollow he stopped, put the silt back underneath it with his own fingers and pressed it down with the heel of his hand, and went on.

He tied the near end of that cord round a stone at the edge of the causeway and pulled it until it lay straight, and the water came at the place where it crossed and went up over the top of it and along it, and where the cord showed through he pushed the silt down under it with a finger and packed it.

In about an hour he had a heap on the high side of that row that a person could see from the near end of it. The heap was wet through from the bottom up and it was not going to stay where it was put, and the part of it nearest the causeway had already begun to run back into the channel and take its own top with it.

"**That channel had a clean edge to it on the Monday and now it has got a step in it, and nobody has come down here and put a thing on the step, so the step made itself.**"

The man with the barrow said that to the row and not to the man working in it, and the man in the silt did not stop and did not answer and did not know that it had been said, and nobody thanked the man with the barrow for saying it.

---

About the ninth hour the man of about thirty-eight came back along that row with his barrow and found the heap on the high side of it and the cord lying along the top of the silt behind it. He stopped at the heap and looked at it and then at the cord, and he did not ask what it was or who had made it, and he put his own boot on the high side of it and stepped over onto the silt that had not been moved and went on past with his barrow.

A woman came up that row at the tenth hour with nothing in her hands and went round the heap on the low side into the channel, and the cord held where it lay and the water went along the top of it and did not come out on the far side of that heap at all that morning.

"**Whatever you have put there is the first thing on this row anybody has made since the water came off it, and it is going to be here in the morning and I am going to come along this row at my own hour and go round it, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it.**"

Nobody said anything back to him and nobody said he was right, and about four people going along that row that morning said they did not know what the other half of it was about, and he carried his barrow on up the row and did not look at it again that day.

---

Adrian Vale took his own two hands off the cord and put the flat of them on the top of the heap he had made and worked the top of it level with the side of his own boot. He put his own boot flat on it and the silt went down under the sole, and he took the boot off again and the heel of it came up with a grey print of the heap on it, which he looked at and wiped on the stone beside his own foot. He left the heap standing there on the high side of that row where the water comes off the stone, and a person going up that row that morning had to go round it.
