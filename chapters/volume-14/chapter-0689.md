# Chapter 0689 — Two Fingers Of A Man's Own Right Hand Going Into The Dip In The Middle Of A Bench That A Pair Of Elbows Has Worn

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had both hands flat on the bare back of his own board where he had leaned it against the side of the bench in the room over that market, and the piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket and did not come out. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and about nine people were in that room and not one of them had been sent.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of that bench with the register open in front of her and her two hands on the bench on either side of it. Five figures stood one under another in the middle of that page with a day written in beside each of the five and none of them struck out, and one word sat near the head of the column on the right of it and that word is not hers. On the shelf behind her the second slate stood turned to the plaster with a day cut across the top of it and nothing under that day, and it was not picked up that morning.

The door at the foot of that stair carried a date in chalk with the three hundred and seventy-fourth day of that month on it in full, and the wood under that date was bare for the width of a hand with nothing whatever on it. There are two places in this flood where a hand that was not the hand that keeps a page has been at a place on a page, one of them in this room and one of them four hundred miles off on a sheet that is not in this city, and nobody in this room has put one of them beside the other or said one word about either of them.

---

Nobody in that room had asked him anything that morning and nobody was going to, and about four of them were at the far end of that room talking about the row and about four were at the near end not talking about it.

He took his own hands off the back of his own board and stood up straight and did not raise his voice, and a woman at the front of that room was in the middle of a sentence about the row and he waited until she had finished it and did not begin over the top of it.

"**What it has cost me is that there is nothing to point at. I did a thing in this room and the whole of what I did left nothing whatever on anything at all, and I cannot show one person where it was done and I would not be able to show you the room either.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody improved on it and nobody said he was right, and the woman the thing was about was at the near end of that bench with her two hands where they had been and was not asked one thing about any of it.

Nobody wrote it down either. There was no page in that room that was going to carry the fact that a man had stood up in it and named a price, and the four days that would have been the time to put a thing to the people in it before the morning it was put in front of them are not available to anybody in this city, and nothing about that was said in any mouth. The decision he had made in that room on the Thursday before last was in the same place, on nobody's page, and nobody outside that room had been told, and the room had gone on being the same room on every morning since.

---

The bench in that room is not flat. There is a dip in the middle of it with a shine on the bottom of the dip, and it is where a pair of forearms has been put every morning of this flood by whoever stands at that bench, and it was not made by anybody in that room on that morning and no chapter of this flood has ever said whose it is.

He came back to the side of that bench and put his own left hand flat on the top of it a little way off the middle, and stood there looking down at the wood the way a man looks at a thing he has been told about. He put his own hand flat on the near end of the bench and slid it along the wood towards the middle and took it off again, and the near end of the bench was cold where his hand had been and colder where it had not. About four people in that room were still talking about the row behind him and about four had stopped, and the light came off the one window and put a bar of it across the open page and stopped at the middle column and did not reach the column on the right.

"**I have had a piece of chalk in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and I brought it out of it in front of all of you and put it back without using it, and what that cost me is that I am now the man who did that, and there is no road back from being that man to being a man with a piece of chalk in a coat.**"

That went to the bench and about four of them went back to what they had been doing and about four did not, and nobody thanked him for either of the two things he had said and nobody said he was wrong.

At the end of that hour he put two fingers of his own right hand into the dip in the middle of that bench, where the shine was, and left them there long enough for the wood to be warm under them, and took them out again. Nobody in that room saw him do it, and the book was still open in front of her.
