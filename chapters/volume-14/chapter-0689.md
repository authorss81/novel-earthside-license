# Chapter 0689 — Two Fingers Of A Man's Own Right Hand Going Into The Dip In The Middle Of A Bench That A Pair Of Elbows Has Worn

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board leaning against the side of the bench in the room over that market with the bare back of it towards him, and he had both hands flat on that bare back, and the piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket and did not come out. It was about the seventh hour on the three hundred and seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and about nine people were in that room and every one of them was up that stair on their own business and not on anybody else's.

At the near end of that bench the register lay open, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her two hands down on the wood either side of it. Five figures went down the middle of that page with a day entered against each of them and none struck through, and up at the head of the column on the right of it stood one word and that word is not hers. Behind her on the shelf was the second slate, standing face to the plaster with a day cut across the top of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it stayed exactly where it was on that shelf.

A date in chalk was on that door at the foot of that stair, and the three hundred and seventy-fourth day of that month was in it in full, and the wood underneath that date was bare for the width of a hand with nothing whatever on it. There are two places in this flood where a hand that was not the hand that keeps a page has been at a place on a page, one of them in this room and one of them in the one place under a heading on a sheet that is not in this city and is not coming back, and nobody in this room has put one of them beside the other or said one word about either of them.

---

Nobody in that room had asked him anything that morning and nobody was going to, and four of them were at the far end of that room talking about the row and the rest of them were at the near end not talking about it.

He took his own hands off that bare back, stood up straight where he was, and kept his voice at the volume a man keeps it in a room. A woman at the front of that room was in the middle of a sentence about the row and he waited until she had finished it and did not begin over the top of it.

"**What it has cost me is that there is nothing to point at. I did a thing in this room and the whole of what I did left nothing whatever on anything at all, and I cannot show one person where it was done and I would not be able to show you the room either.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody improved on it and nobody said he was right, and the woman the thing was about was at the near end of that bench with her two hands where they had been and was not asked one thing about any of it.

Nobody wrote it down either. There was no page in that room that was going to carry the fact that a man had stood up in it and named a price, and four days before a thing is put to a room is a thing this city no longer has, and nothing about that was said in any mouth. The decision he had made in that room on the Thursday before last was in the same place, on nobody's page, and nobody outside that room had been told, and the room had gone on being the same room on every morning since.

---

The bench in that room is not flat. There is a dip in the middle of it with a shine on the bottom of the dip, and it is where a pair of forearms has been put every morning of this flood by whoever stands at that bench, and it was not made by anybody in that room on that morning, and there is nothing on that bench that says whose it is.

He came back to the side of that bench and put his own left hand flat on the top of it a little way off the middle, and stood there looking down at the wood the way a man looks at a thing he has been told about. He put his own hand flat on the near end of the bench and slid it along the wood towards the middle and took it off again, and the near end of the bench was cold where his hand had been and colder where it had not. About four people in that room were still talking about the row behind him and about four had stopped, and the light came off the one window and put a bar of it across the open page and stopped at the middle column and did not reach the column on the right.

"**I have had a piece of chalk in this pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and I brought it out of it in front of all of you and put it back without using it, and what that cost me is that I am now the man who did that, and there is no road back from being that man to being a man with a piece of chalk in a coat.**"

That went to the bench and about four of them went back to what they had been doing and about four did not, and nobody thanked him for either of the two things he had said and nobody said he was wrong.

At the end of that hour he put two fingers of his own right hand into the dip in the middle of that bench, where the shine was, and left them there long enough for the wood to be warm under them, and took them out again. Nobody in that room saw him do it, and the book was still open in front of her.
