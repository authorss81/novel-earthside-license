# Chapter 0678 — A Piece Of Chalk Taken Out Of An Inside Breast Pocket In Front Of About Nine People And Put Back Without Going Near The Door

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall laid both hands on the bar of the bench in front of him in the room over that market and his hands were empty, and the piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket and had been in it since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood. It was about the eighth hour on the three hundred and sixty-third day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and about nine people were in that room and the light off the one window was on the bench and not on his hands.

The box of the store's chalk stood on a step at the end of the market with the lid of it not shutting, and about a thumb's width of grit has been lying under the near side of that rim since before the man who put it there was keeping anything, and no person in this city has ever counted it and no person has ever asked him to. That door carries a date in chalk and the three hundred and sixty-third day of that month is on it in full, and the wood under that date is bare to a hand's width and has been since before anybody in this city can remember, and no person in this city has been told they may write on that wood and no person in this city has been told they may not, and about four people went past that door before the tenth hour and none of them stopped at it.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board held up flat against the plaster of the far wall with his back to it and the face of it to the plaster, and he had been standing in that position since the seventh hour and had not moved out of it. The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board came up that stair at about the eighth hour with his bucket in his hand and stopped at the top of it with the bucket at his side and did not come into the room.

---

Nobody sent a notice and nobody was going to, and the four days that would have been the time to put a thing to a person before the morning it was put in front of them were not available to anybody in that room. About four people in that room found out what they were being asked on the morning they were asked it. A woman at the front said it first, plainly, in the ordinary voice a person uses in a room, and then a man at the back asked the same thing in the same plain way, and about two others in that room joined them, and the room was told by the stallholder that they were asking him whether he would put a name under the date on that door.

He did not say no. He named the price of it first, in his own mouth, before he answered anything, and he said it quietly and to the bench and not to any one person.

"**A chalk that people can name is a pen.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody improved on it, and about four people in that room said they did not know what he meant by it and about four said nothing.

Then he put his own hand into his own inside breast pocket and brought out a piece of chalk about two fingers long, and he held it in his open hand at the height of the bench with his hand open and his fingers loose, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and the chalk sat in the middle of his palm and he did not close his hand on it and he did not turn towards the door.

---

What happened in that room in the next half minute happened in about nine people's eyes and not in anybody's hands, and what about nine people saw was a piece of chalk in an open hand above a bench.

He had been looking at the hand the whole time that the hand was in the room, and his own board came down about an inch against the plaster where he was standing, and the face of it came off the plaster for about an inch's worth of daylight. He was standing at the top of that stair with his bucket at his side and he saw the face of that board come down, and he looked away from it and went on down the stair with his bucket instead of coming into the room, and about nine people in that room did not see it because about nine people in that room were looking at a piece of chalk in a hand above a bench and not at the walls of that room.

"**I have had this in a pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood and nobody has asked me one thing about what it is for, and I am not going to be asked about it this morning with a piece of it out in my hand in front of all of you, and the answer to your question is in the fact that I am about to put it away again.**"

The bench got it and nobody in that room did, and nobody in that room said he was right and nobody in that room said he was wrong, and about four people went back to what they had been doing and about four did not.

He closed his own hand on the piece of chalk and put it back into his own inside breast pocket, and the chalk went in where it had been, and his hand came out of the pocket empty, and the piece of board under the date on the outside of that door at the foot of the stair was carrying what it had been carrying every morning of this flood.

He picked his own board up from the corner of that room and went out with it under his arm and down the stair, and the washer came up that stair with his bucket afterwards and the two of them passed each other on the stair, and neither of them said anything.
