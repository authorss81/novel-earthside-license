# Chapter 0674 — A Store Board Put Back Up On Its Two Nails Before The Light Comes Round The Corner Of That Store

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board had both hands under the lower edge of it with the board off its two nails and standing on edge against the foot of the wall, and the second nail was in his right hand and the wedge was in his teeth. It was about the sixth hour on the three hundred and fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and he wanted that board back up on its two nails and sitting level before the light came round the corner of that store, and the light came round the corner of that store at the seventh hour whatever anybody wanted.

The board is on the outside wall of that store and it carries three sets of figures of this city's own, one above another, and none of the three has ever been rubbed out, and not one of the three carries a line of words set under it. The wall above the top edge of that board is a different thing from the board: it was washed once in the whole of this flood, on a day that is not one of this run of days, by this man, and by no second person on any day of it, and he was not going near it that morning and no other hand in that row was either. That board is not a blank board. It is a board that has gone the colour the weather has gone it and it says what it has said for years.

He washed the face of it in two passes with the bucket and the rag and wiped the face of it down with the back of his own hand, and put it up on the two nails, and the near nail went into a hole that was not as good as it had been and the wedge went under the lower edge and the board sat where it sat and did not sit level. He took the wedge out and drove it in again with the heel of his hand and it sat level. He took his hands off it and looked at it from about the height of his knee, the way he does every morning, and it was where it had been, and the wall above the top edge of it was where it had been.

Down at the bottom of that stair, on the outside of the door, the date in chalk carried the three hundred and fifty-ninth day of that month in full and about four people went past that door that morning and got a date off it and nothing else, because the board under that date is bare to the width of a hand and has been bare the whole of this flood.

---

About four people were standing in that row at that hour, and they were standing in it because that row is where a market puts the people who are not buying, and by luck or by habit the four of them were standing in the one place the light was going to come round to. A woman with a basket on her arm, a man holding a coil of something by its middle, a man with a sack on his shoulder and one thumb in the strap of it, and a fourth person who had nothing in either hand at all.

The woman with the basket said the board was for what a person owes. The man holding the coil said the board was for what a person should be told. The man with the sack said the board was for neither and was for what a store has decided to put up, and that a store decides that and not a person. The fourth person said the board was for a person to look at, and that everything else said about it was said by people who were not looking at it. They meant four different things by that board and about four others in that row meant nothing at all by it, and not one of the four was wrong.

They went on like that while the washer had his own bucket in the channel and his own rag on the stone lip of the step, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came down the row with his own board under his arm and stopped beside the fourth person and did not put his board down and did not join in what they were saying.

"**Four of you are standing in front of one board and every one of you is talking about a different thing that board is for, and the board says one thing, which is three sets of figures with a day beside them, and if the four of you were right about it then the four of you are wrong about each other.**"

It went to the row and not to the washer, and nobody in that row thanked him for it, and the woman with the basket looked at the board and said nothing, and he did not tell her which of the four she was.

The light came round the corner of that store while the four of them were standing there talking and it came onto the face of that board in one movement, the way light comes onto a thing in a street, and the four of them had about half a minute of it in their eyes before any of them moved.

---

What the four of them found out in that half minute was not on the board. One of them said out loud that a person cannot be shown a surface in this city that anybody ever told them they might write on, and about four others in that row said that they did not know, and about four said nothing. The board had figures on it and a date beside each figure and nothing under any of them, and the door at the foot of that stair had a date on it and a hand's width of bare wood under the date and nothing on that either, and the two surfaces were a few paces apart and they were the same kind of surface and nobody had ever been told anything about either of them by anybody.

He had his hands on his own bucket and had said nothing at all through any of it, and the light was on the face of his own board and his own board was on its two nails with the wedge under the near one and was sitting level, and he looked at the four of them looking at it instead of at him and he put the rag on the stone lip of the step and went up the row to his own work.

"**Four of you have been standing in front of a board that a man takes off its nails and washes and puts back every morning of this flood, and not one of you has ever asked him whether you may, and that is the reason you can stand there and say what it is for, and I have not said a word about any of it, and I am not going to now.**"

The four of them in that row got it and the washer did not, and nobody in that row thanked him for it, and the fourth person said that two of the four of them were talking about things that were not on the board at all, and he did not ask which two.

The stallholder stayed with them for a while and let them go on saying what they said, and then he took his own board off the trestle at his own front, two stalls along, and stood it face out in the row for as long as it takes to wipe the top of the trestle with the sleeve of his coat, and put it back face in.
