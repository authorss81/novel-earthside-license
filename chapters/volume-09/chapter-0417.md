# Chapter 0417 — A Squall Over A Market And A Wall That Washes Itself

A market at the bottom of this city had a squall come across it at about the sixth hour on the hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the squall did the thing that weather does to a market with boards over it, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a stall board with chalk dust in the grain of it, a bucket and a rag, a barrow standing at the end of the stall with nothing in it, and a wall above the outside board of a store that has not been washed for about a month.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall took his board out of the weather himself. He had it off the string and up on edge against the side of his own stall with the chalk face in and a stone on the foot of it, and he did it in about as long as it takes to fill a pail and he did it twice because the first time the wind came round the corner of the market and took the top of it.

The barrow at the end of his stall has been standing at the end of his stall for about a month and there is nothing in it and there is not going to be anything in it, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who keeps a barrow at the end of a stall and never puts anything in it is a man who has kept a thing for the shape of it and not for the use, and that it is not one of the three barrows on the four-hundred-mile road and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road.

He came out of the weather and stood at the end of the store with his hands in his pockets and looked down the market at the man of about twenty-seven, who had a bucket and a rag in one hand and his other arm up over his face.

---

The store's board is on the outside wall at one end of that market and it has not been written on since the two hundred and twenty-fifth day of this flood, and the figures on it are the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day, and a man of about twenty-seven washes it every morning with a bucket and a rag and has washed it every morning since, and about a month ago he decided not to wash the wall above it.

He was not under anything. That was the thing about him in that squall. The man of about thirty-four, who had been under his own board and had put a stone on the foot of it, came down the market and the man of about twenty-seven was standing at the end of the store with his arm over his face and a bucket in his other hand and nothing over the board and nothing over himself either.

"Above your head."

"**I know what is above my head.**"

The rain was coming down the wall in one sheet about as wide as a man and it was going straight over the board and the figures on it, and the man of about twenty-seven did not move for about as long as it takes to count nine, and about four people who came through that market in that weather have worked out since that he was not standing there doing nothing, he was standing there letting it happen to a wall he had decided about a month ago and letting it not happen to a board that had a day in front of it, and that a man who has decided a thing and then has weather decide it for him does not get the credit for it and knows it.

---

The man of about thirty-four put the bucket down in the doorway and put his own coat over the man's arm and went up under the eave and got the board.

He did it with a bucket and a rag and he did it from the top down, and he did it inside the eave where the rain came in at an angle and not on the face, and he took the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day and washed the grit off round them and put the bucket down and stood back, and the man of about twenty-seven stood in the doorway and looked at what he had done and did not say anything about it for about as long as it takes to count nine.

"You did not have to do that."

"**I have been standing under that rain for about a quarter of an hour deciding whether to stand there or to run, and I decided twice, and the first time I decided to stand and the second time I decided to run, and I did not do either, and that is what a person does at the end of a wall in a squall.**"

"It was raining on it."

"**Then do not let it get round that you did it for me, because I did not stand here and ask you to, and if it goes about this market that a man washed a board he did not have to wash I will not be having it, and I have had a bucket and a rag at the end of that wall for about a month and nobody has ever said one word to me about any of it.**"

The man of about twenty-seven stood in the doorway with his hand on the frame of it and did not move while the other of the two took his coat off his arm, and the rain had gone off the wall by then and both of them were standing in the drip off the eave, and the bucket and the rag were on the step between them where either of them could have picked them up.

The wall above the board ran for another hour and a half and stopped when the squall stopped, and about four people in that market have worked out since that the wall the man of about twenty-seven has not washed for a month went clean in a quarter of an hour without him lifting a bucket, and that a thing a person decides not to do for a month turns out to be a thing the sky will do for him on a Friday, and that neither of those two facts is an answer to why he decided it and nobody asked him.

---

Nobody in that market thanked either of them. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has asked in advance, in a lane, not to be thanked, and the man of about twenty-seven said nothing at all and went back to his bucket and his rag and stood there with them after the rain had gone, and about four people in that market have worked out since that a man who is owed something for an hour of a squall and is given nothing for it is going to be owed something for the next hour as well, and that this is the arrangement and he set it himself.

The man of about thirty-four went and turned his own board face out again and put the string on it and wrote the ordinary line on it, and the piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket was where it has been since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, and he did not take it out to do it, and the barrow at the end of the stall stood in the wet with nothing in it and the market came back up off the water the way it does and the day was the hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and nobody in it was any the worse off and one wall was clean.
