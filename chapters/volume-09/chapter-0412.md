# Chapter 0412 — A Space At The Top Of Some Boards About The Length Of A Barrow

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city were wet at the bottom end at about the seventh hour on the ninety-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and the wet came up through the seams about an inch and went down again about the middle of the afternoon, and the light lay along them off the water without moving all morning, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a tally-board, a knife, a stack higher than a man at the top end, a strip of clear board about as long as a cart lying at the bottom of that stack, and a space at the top of the boards about the length of a barrow where nothing was.

---

The man of about thirty-eight worked the boards from about the seventh hour. He had the tally-board under his arm and the knife in his hand and he moved in the same rhythm he has moved in since the winter before the water, and there was no barrow at the top of those boards, and there has not been one since the Sunday morning a man of about twenty-five came up out of the low ground with his hand out and took it off the top of them without one word being said about it on either side.

That was the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood and there were three barrows on the four-hundred-mile road that morning and there are three on it now, and one of them is the one that used to stand at the top of these boards, and it went away with a man who did not open his coat and who has not been in this city since and is not going to be. Nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road. A party of four is four, three of them are on it, the fourth is not on it, and nobody in four hundred miles is going to ask her about anything, and about four people on these boards have worked out since that the reason this city cannot be shown one person on that road is that the road is the only instrument any of the three of them have got.

---

The man of about thirty-nine came down onto the boards at about the eighth hour with his own board under his arm and stood at the bottom of them, the way he has stood at the bottom of things in this city for about a fortnight, and neither of them said one word for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

"You are standing on my boards."

"I have been standing at the bottom of a set of boards for about a fortnight and nobody has asked me for anything, and there is a space at the top of them about the length of a barrow and it has been empty since before you started working this morning."

The man of about thirty-eight turned a board over and did not look up, and the strip of clear board at the bottom of the stack did not move, and it is a piece of board and is not a page and it is on nobody's list of anything.

"**It went down the four-hundred-mile road on the Sunday it went, and it went with a man who is not going to bring it back, and I gave it away in about a quarter of an hour and I did not say why and I am not going to say why now, and I would like it noticed that nobody in this city has asked me about it once, and has not asked me since, and is not going to.**"

Nobody on those boards told him he was right. Nobody asked him why, and about four people who came down onto that wharf have worked out since that a man who has been asked nothing for a fortnight and has been asked one question and answered it and had the answer taken and was not argued with is a man who has been given the only thing anybody in this city has ever given anybody, which is not being asked a second time.

---

He went on working until about the middle of the afternoon. The man of about thirty-nine stood at the bottom of the boards the whole time and did not go up, and the two of them had between them said perhaps nine sentences and none of the nine was about the sheet on a table in a room over a market, and about four people on that wharf have worked out since that two men who are both in a room over a market most mornings and have never once said a word to each other in it can say nine sentences on a set of boards and not have any of them be about the thing that room is about.

"Will you be at the top of them on the day they go."

"**There is no day they go, and there is no form for one, and a sheet of paper with a place for a person in it is not a barrow and it does not need one, and you are asking me a question about a thing I have already answered once this morning in a longer sentence than I am going to use again.**"

He put the knife down flat on the boards and picked it up again and went back to the end he had been at, and the man of about thirty-nine went up off the wharf with his board under his arm, and the space at the top of those boards was about the length of a barrow and was empty and was going to be empty in the afternoon.
