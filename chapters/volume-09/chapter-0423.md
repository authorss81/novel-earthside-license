# Chapter 0423 — One Withie Off The Fence And Put Back On

A shut road out of this city has a fence along the top of that road, and on the hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, the wind came up the same road in pushes that have been up it since before the water and have never once been written down, and the light came up out of the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over it. Two pairs of hands had that morning. A handcart stood in the wet with a hazel mallet in it and a coil of rope on top of the coil, and one withie was lying at the foot of the fence with its binding gone.

---

The man of about sixty had the mallet and the cord and the coil, and the coil has been used and is not new, and he has never told anybody in this city where the wood came from and nobody has ever asked him, and a man who puts a thing up without being sent for it and is never asked about it has already been asked as many times as he is going to be.

He did not knock the withie out. He untied the binding where it had gone, which took him a while, and he cut a new length of cord off the coil with his own knife and bound the withie back to the post it belongs to at the height it was at, and the withie went on the way it goes on, and the fence is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound along it with the same cord and there is not a seventeenth post and there is not a twelfth withy and there was not one missing.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was on the low side at about the tenth hour, and he had not come up the road for that, and nobody knew why he was there and nobody asked, and he had a length of nothing in his hand and nothing over his arm. The only length of rope anybody in this flood talks about is in a room in this city over the back of a chair and has never been used. The coil in that cart is a different rope, and it has been used, and it is not new, and nobody has ever put the two of them in the same sentence.

He watched the whole of the binding. He did not offer a hand and nobody offered him one, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man who is not asked to hold a withy end is a man who has learned what being asked is worth in this flood, and that the not-offering cost the withy about ten minutes and nobody counted them.

---

"**You did not ask me to hold that.**"

"I did not think of it. It is one withy and my hands are colder than the wood is."

"**Then I will say the thing out loud here, on a shut road, to nobody, the way a man says a thing when there is nobody to say it to. I have been in a great many rooms in this city over the last month and not one of them asked me to hold anything.**"

The man of about sixty did not take his hands off the cart handles and did not turn round. A man who has put sixteen posts in the ground without being sent for it does not stop working to be told something by a man who has not asked him a question in a month.

"There was a withy end free."

"**And I have been out here this morning with a man doing a thing two feet from my hands who did not ask me, and both of those are the same arrangement, and I am not going to stand in the wet and decide on a shut road which one of them it is.**"

The man of about sixty got the hazel mallet back into the cart and put the coil of rope on top of the coil, and he did not say that, and he did not agree with it, and he did not ask the other man a single question. Nobody sent him and nobody is going to, and a man who has spent a fortnight with a man at the end of a fence and has not asked him one thing is not going to start on a Thursday in the wet.

---

The man of about fifty-seven went down the road he had come up on and did not stop at the fence and did not look at it going past, and the light came up out of the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over. The wind went on coming up the same road in the same pushes. The road went on down under the fence into the low ground, and at the foot of that road there is a lane with nine houses in it, and that lane is not in this city, and nobody came up that road in this flood who was not already on it.

And there is no page anywhere in four hundred miles with a place in it for that fence, and it is the only thing anybody in this flood has put up out of nothing and been sent for by nobody, and it is still sixteen posts and eleven withies, and the man who built it is not going to be asked about it, and the man standing at the end of it has not been asked where it goes and is not going to be, and nothing in this flood moved today except one withie that came off in the wet and was put back on.
