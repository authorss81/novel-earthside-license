# Chapter 0436 — Nothing On Any Of The Boards For Anybody To Fill In

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and on the hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, the light came up off the river on them in one long sheet and did not move all morning, and it was on the water and not on a wall and there is no hill within about four miles of that wharf. There was a stack at the top end higher than a man, a strip of clear board about as long as a cart lying at the bottom of that stack, a tally-board, a knife with the edge gone blunt on it, and a barrow stood at the top of the boards that had been on them all morning. Nothing on any of it had anything to be filled in. The page with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading was nine steps above a market and it was not on these boards and it was not on any of them.

---

The man of about thirty-eight was working them. He had the tally-board under his arm and the knife in his hand and he was turning boards over at the top end and setting them down flat, and he had been at it since the light came off the water, and he did not lift his head when a woman came down off the step at the end of that wharf and stood at the bottom of his boards, and she had not come down there to be spoken to.

Tamsin Quill stood at the bottom of them for about an hour with a folded sheet of road in the inside of her coat and about nine places on it, and she did not take it out, and nobody on that wharf asked her why she had come down, and the man working the boards did not say one word to her.

She said one thing to him in that hour, and she said it from where she was standing and did not go any nearer him.

"You have not turned one over since I got down here."

"**No. There is no order on and the ones at the top are the ones a man can get to, and I have got to the ones at the top, and it is not your order and it is not mine.**"

Nobody on that wharf asked either of them a second question.

---

There was a stone at the lower end of those boards, about the length of a door and thick as two hands, and it had come off a building when the water came and it was lying in the wet with about four inches of silt under one end of it, and there is no hill within about four miles of that wharf and no crane on it and nobody in this city has ever built one, and it will not roll, because the ground under it is wet to the depth of a hand and there is not six feet of boards clear at that end to roll it into.

A man has been working round that stone for a month. He has cut boards to go past it and he has cut boards to go short of it, and he has a strip of clear board at the bottom of his stack that is the only piece of board on that wharf flat enough to lay over a hole, and nobody has laid it over that stone and he has not laid it there himself.

The man of about thirty-eight said one thing to a man carrying a length of board down that wharf at about the tenth hour, and it was not about the stone.

"**Take it round. Do not come down the middle with that, the middle is soft at the lower end today.**"

The man carrying the board took it round. Nobody on that wharf said thank you.

---

Adrian Vale was on those boards about the seventh hour and he was not asked anything by anybody on them for the whole of that morning, and nobody declined to ask him anything either, and nobody there was waiting for him to be useful. He watched the man at the top end work for about an hour and he watched the woman at the bottom end stand, and then he walked down to the lower end of the wharf and stood by the stone and looked at the four inches of silt under the near end of it, and then he went back up and stood at the top end where he had been.

He did not say one word to anybody about the stone. Nobody on that wharf knew that he had looked at it and nobody has worked out since what he worked out, and the man of about thirty-eight went on turning boards over at the top end with the tally-board under his arm and the knife in his hand, and the strip of clear board lay at the bottom of the stack and did not move, and it is a piece of board and not a page and it is on nobody's list of anything.

---

Nobody wrote anything down on that wharf that morning. The tally-board went under the man's arm at the middle of the day and came out at the fourth hour with the same face to it that it went in with, and the knife stayed blunt, and the barrow stood at the top of the boards all morning with nothing in it and is not one of the three barrows on a road four hundred miles long that nobody in this city can be shown one person on, and the stone at the lower end did not move, and there is no page in four hundred miles with a place in it for a stone at the bottom of a wharf, and the light on that wharf was still on the water at the fourth hour, and the hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month was a Wednesday and nothing was said about that by anybody.
