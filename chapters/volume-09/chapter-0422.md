# Chapter 0422 — The Sheet Goes Out Onto The Step At The Back For An Hour

The hundred and seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door of a room over a market at about the seventh hour on a Wednesday, and the cold had got into the boards of the stair overnight and the stair was colder than the room. A sheet of paper with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and nothing in any of them was on the table, a book was shut on a shelf, and a length of new rope was over the back of a chair with a man in it who had been at the end of that bench on most of the mornings since the page came up the road.

---

They put the sheet out on the step at the back at about the ninth hour because the room had gone cold and the light had got as far as it was going to get. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register carried it out in her own two hands and set it down on the flat of the top step, still folded the way it came, and the crease pressed down the third of the four places lay along the stone where anybody coming up those steps could see it before they were in the room.

She stood beside it with her arms folded and she did not go back in.

Nobody in that market said anything about it for about ten minutes. Then a woman at the market end of the step, who had a basket on her arm and no business in a room with a date on the door, came the length of the step and looked down at it and said one question into the air above it.

"What is the third of the four for."

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not tell her. She said the thing that was true, in the local language, out loud, on a step at the back of a market in daylight with a date in chalk on the door six feet above her head, and she said it once and she did not say it twice and she did not soften it.

"**The third of the four is a person. I do not know what it is for. I have had that page in a room since the Sunday it was read out and I have counted the four places out loud in front of nine people and I have not put one name in the third of them.**"

The woman with the basket stood on the step and waited, and about four people in that market who had come through it that morning worked out since that a person who has asked a question outside is owed something and that a woman who keeps a register in this city does not generally owe anything to a step.

"**And I am not going to start on a step in a market. If you want it written down you will have to be a person and come up nine steps and ask, and I am not going to ask you to do that.**"

---

The woman with the basket stood on the step and looked at the paper for a while longer and then went off down the market with the basket still on her arm, and she did not say thank you and nobody said anything to her, and about four people who had come through that market in that hour worked out since that the first person outside that room to be told anything about that page had been told the truth and had been told nothing else.

From inside the room, from the back, without turning round, one man said one thing to nobody.

"**She has just told a stranger in a market that she does not know, and she has not said one word to this room, and I am not going to be the one who tells this room she is wrong.**"

---

Nobody in that room had told the market anything and the market had worked out where to look anyway. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said that out loud to nobody in particular. A room is not a thing that keeps what is on its table inside itself. The only reason nine people had spent a fortnight in a room with a page on it was that nine people already knew which stair it was up, and a person who has not been in the room does not have to be summoned to look at it, and there is no paper in four hundred miles that says otherwise.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that market that morning for his own work and came up the nine steps at about the eleventh hour with his board under his arm and a piece of bark in his hand, and the bark was off a stall frame at the market end and had nothing to do with the page and he put it on the table and left it there.

"**Four people at the market end of that step and about two more at the corner, and none of them came up, and one of them asked the woman a question out loud on a step with a date on the door over her head, and I am telling you that because nobody else in this room was standing where I was standing.**"

Nobody thanked him. He had said a true thing about where he had been standing, and a man who has been at the back of this room for a fortnight saying true things about other parts of the building is not going to be thanked in it, and the bark stayed on the table where he put it.

---

Adrian Vale came up the nine steps at about the twelfth hour and was not asked why he was in the room and was not asked for anything while he was in it, and he put his hand inside the front of his coat and took out a sheet of his own folded into four and carried it to the window end of the table and stood there with it.

There was a date on the back of it and two names on the front of it and no third, and it had been folded and unfolded a great many more times than the page on the table had, and he read it standing up, and he did not put it down flat and he did not open it out and he did not sit down. He read it for about as long as it takes to count nine and then he folded it the way it had been and put it back inside the front of his coat and buttoned the coat.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of the room and he had not been asked anything that morning and he did not ask anything now, and he had seen the whole of it, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man with a piece of chalk in a pocket that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, watched another man take a page out of a coat and read it and put it back, and said nothing, and that neither of them has ever in this flood been asked to show anybody what he is carrying.

---

The sheet came in off the step at about the second hour of the afternoon and went back onto the table in the same place it had been on since the morning it came up the road, and the four places under its heading were all still empty and the crease was still the only thing on that paper anybody had ever touched. Nobody asked the woman at the market end of the step anything else, and nobody sent for anybody to ask her, and the man of about thirty-nine went back down the nine steps to his own work and about nine people went down after him by the fourth hour with a thing in a market that could not be put back the way it was before somebody in that market had looked at it.
