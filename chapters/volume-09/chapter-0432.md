# Chapter 0432 — Four Lines In The Second Of The Four Places

A Saturday, and the hundred and seventeenth day of the Bare Month went into the chalk on the door of a room over a market at about the seventh hour, and the market underneath was quiet by then because the light had not got off the water. The sheet was lying flat on the table where it had lain every morning, and somebody had picked the lump of chalk up off the edge of that table and put it down again in the middle of it, which is the whole of what changed in that room before anybody said anything, and about four pairs of hands had the morning, and four of the nine people who had been in that room on the Sunday it was read out were in it again.

---

The woman of about fifty-two was the first one in that room to put anything on the page, and she did it at about the eighth hour without being asked and without asking, and she had told the room on a Saturday a fortnight and more before that morning that one of the four lines on that page was going to be hers, and she said that before anybody said it so that nobody in that room could afterwards say she had been asked. She had come up the nine steps that morning without saying one word to anybody on them, and she was not asked where her line went, and she was not asked how she knew it.

She put a mark in the second of the four places. The second of the four is a line, and a line is a thing a person can put down whether or not anybody is standing on either side of it, and about four people in that room watched a woman put one down without appearing to think about where to start.

"**I knew before I came up those steps that I was going to put a mark on that page, and I have said that out loud in this room before, and nobody has asked me since how I knew, and I am not going to be thanked for the mark either.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her.

---

The man of about thirty-six came up the nine steps at about the ninth hour and put a mark in the second of the four places after the first one, and he was in that room for about as long as it takes to do it, and then he went back down the steps and was not in that room again that morning, and nobody in that room said one word to him before or after, and he said one thing to the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register on his way out and it was not about the page.

The two marks were not the same length. About four people in that room had looked at them in the time it took for the second one to dry and had worked out that they were not the same length and had not worked out what to do about it, and neither of the two people who had made them was asked.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room from the seventh hour with his own board under his arm, and he put a mark in the second of the four places at about the tenth hour, and he said nothing at all while he did it. He had kept a hand flat on the face of his own slate through a whole morning in which a woman decided what a column was, and through a morning in which a voice at the bottom of nine steps told another man what he had lost, and he put a mark down in the middle of that page and took his hand off the page and put it back on his own slate, and nobody in that room looked at his face while he did it and nobody in that room has worked out since why.

---

The man of about fifty-seven came up those steps about the eleventh hour with a length of new rope over his arm, which is where he carries it and where it has been, and it has still never been used. He came to the table and he read the fourth mark out loud, in the local language, because it was in front of him and the room had stopped talking, and he read it the way a man reads a line off a page when there is nobody to read it to.

He did not say it was his.

Nobody in that room suggested it. Nobody in that room asked him, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and did not turn round, and the woman of about fifty-two was at the end of the bench and did not look up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that four people drew four lines in one room in one morning and that one of the four read his out loud and did not say it was his, and that there is no way anybody in that room will ever be able to say afterwards which of the four lines is his, including the four of them who drew them.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register asked the only question that room asked that morning, and she asked it standing up in the local language with a date on the door, and it was about the page and not about any of the four marks on it.

"Which of the four of you is going to say so."

Nobody answered her straight away. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm and looked at the four marks and then at the back of the room, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and said it from the back of that room without turning round.

"**I told this room in front of nine people yesterday that I am not one of the four, and I said it before a mark was on that paper, and I am not going to be one of them this morning because there are four of them on it.**"

---

Adrian Vale had been in that room since the seventh hour with his hand on the sheet. He read the first of the four places and the second of them twice, and he read the fourth, and he did not read the third of them, and he did not say one word about anything that happened at that table, and nobody in that room asked him anything and nobody in that room thanked him for being in it. A man who reads pages has no standing whatever in a room where four people have drawn four lines.

---

Nobody in that room wrote anything in the first of the four places or in the fourth of them, and the third of the four places had a crease pressed down the length of it with a bone and nothing at all in it, and it is the third of the four that is a person, and it is empty on purpose because a woman decided that on a Saturday in that room and named the cost out loud before she decided it. The four lines went into the second of the four places, and that page is going to carry them out of this city with no name on any of them, and the day on the door was the hundred and seventeenth day of the Bare Month, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and the four people who drew the four lines went down among them, and not one of them said which of the four was which.
