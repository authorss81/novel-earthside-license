# Chapter 0408 — A Man Working Boards, And A Thing He Is Right About In Four Seconds

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and the light comes up off the river on them in one long sheet and does not move all morning, and it is on the water and not on a wall, and there is no hill within about four miles of that wharf, and what was under about four pairs of hands on the ninety-third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, was a tally-board, a knife, a barrow, a stack higher than a man, and a strip of clear board about as long as a cart lying at the bottom of that stack.

---

The man of about thirty-eight worked the boards from about the seventh hour. He had a tally-board under his arm and a knife in his hand and he moved in a rhythm that had not changed since the winter before the water, and a man who has been doing the same thing on the same boards since before there was a flood does not look at the light and does not need to, because the light on that wharf comes up off the river in one long sheet at about the seventh hour and lies there until the middle of the afternoon and then it goes off the water and comes back on the boards flat, and that has happened every morning of this flood and the man has never once been late for it.

The stack at the top end is higher than a man and the strip of clear board is at the bottom of it and is a piece of board and not a page, and it is on nobody's list of anything and it is not counted.

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine came down onto the boards with his board under his arm and stood at the bottom of them for about an hour, the way he stood at the bottom of things in this city for about a fortnight, and neither of them said one word of anything to the other one.

---

"You are standing on my boards."

The man of about thirty-eight said it without stopping work and without looking down at the ground the other man was standing on.

"**I have been standing at the bottom of a set of boards for about a fortnight and nobody has ever asked me for anything, and you are not going to ask me for anything this morning either, and I am going to stand here until the light comes off the water.**"

"It is up an inch at the seam."

The man of about thirty-eight took the knife off the board and turned it over once and put it down flat, and neither of the two men on those boards looked at the other one while he did it.

"**It is up an inch at the seam and it is up an inch at the seam every morning this week and it does not go down until the middle of the afternoon, and if you have come down here to tell me about it you have come down here for nothing.**"

"I have not come down here for anything."

"**Then stand there for an hour and I will not say another word to you, and that is the arrangement and I have had it with better men than you.**"

He put his right hand on the edge of the boards and looked at the water in the seam for a while, and the man of about thirty-eight went on working, and about four people who came down onto those boards that morning have worked out since that two men who have been in a room with a document from the same office can stand eleven feet apart for an hour and neither of them has to say a thing, and that the standing is the whole of what either of them has come down for.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-nine went back up, and the man of about thirty-eight worked the boards for about as long as it takes to fill a pail and then stopped, and he did not go up the nine steps, and the nine steps are about four minutes' walk from the bottom of those boards and he has been in that room about nine times since the sheet came up the road and he has been up those nine steps about nine times since the sheet came up the road without anybody sending for him and nobody has ever given him a reason for going.

He stood at the bottom of his own boards with the knife in his hand and the tally-board under his arm and said the thing, and it was four seconds, and it was true, and he said it to the water and not to a person.

"A page with a person on it is not a page with a number on it."

Nobody on those boards told him he was right. Nobody told him he was wrong. Nobody went back over it afterwards, and about four people who were on those boards that morning have worked out since that the man who has been putting a number in a column four times a year since the winter before the water is the man who would be asked about a person this time, and that nobody is going to ask him, and that he has a figure and there is another one and neither of them is on any page in this city and neither of them is going into anybody's mouth this morning.

---

He picked the knife up off the boards and went on working, and the strip of clear board lay at the bottom of the stack, and the stack was higher than a man and the tally-board was under his arm and there is a barrow stood at the top of the boards that has been on them all morning and is not one of the three barrows on the road four hundred miles inland and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road, including the three who are on it.

There is no form in four hundred miles for saying that the figure a man sends is not the figure he has. He has been sending a figure four times a year since the winter before the water and the figure he sends is not the figure he has, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what a man of about thirty-eight has been carrying on his own account for years, and there is no column in this city for the difference and there is not going to be one and he has not asked for one.

---

A page with a place for a person in it is lying on a table in a room over a market on this Wednesday morning, in a building with a date in chalk on the door, and about nine people have been in that room and every one of them came up the steps on their own feet. A man who has been filling a column in for nine years was not in that room when it was read out and was not sent for and was not asked, and he is at the bottom of a set of boards at the bottom of this city, and he has been in that room about nine times for no reason anybody has given him, and the reason is that he has been in it.

The third place on that sheet is going to be decided by somebody. It is not going to be decided on those boards and it is not going to be decided by the man with the tally-board, and about four people who came down onto that wharf that morning have worked out since that a thing which has been true out loud in this city more than once has gone nowhere any of the times, and that the third place on a page from an office nine hundred miles off is the third time and there is nobody in this city who has to say anything about it.
