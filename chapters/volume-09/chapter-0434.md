# Chapter 0434 — A Stall Board Brought Up Nine Steps And Put Against A Wall

The hundred and nineteenth day of the Bare Month went up in chalk on a door over a market on a Monday at about the seventh hour, and the light came in low off the low ground and lay along the bench and off the end of the bench, and about nine people were in that room before the light was off the low ground, and lying on that table was a sheet of paper with a heading on it and four places ruled under that heading, and four marks down the second of those four, one above another, and a crease pressed the whole length of the third of them, and nothing whatever in the first of the four, the third of them, or the fourth.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the nine steps about the fourth hour of the afternoon with his own board under his arm and put it against the inside of the wall by the door and sat down at the back of the bench in front of it, and the ordinary line was on that board in the chalk that hangs on its string, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not move and was not the chalk on the board.

Nobody in that room asked him why he had brought a board up nine steps and put it against a wall in a room, and nobody in that room said one word about the board, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has brought a thing into a room and put it down is a man who has come up those steps for something, and that not one of them has worked out what.

---

"You have brought that up nine steps for Friday."

"**I have brought that up nine steps because on Friday I am not going to be at the market, and I have worked out this week that the only place in this city where a man can sit on a Friday and not be spoken to is a room that does not ask anybody anything, and that this is that room, and I am not asking it for anything else.**"

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm and looked at the one against the wall and then at his own and said nothing about either of them, and he had stood at the back of a great many of them in this flood and had never once been asked anything in any of them, and a man who notices a thing and says nothing about it is not doing that for the room.

---

A woman near the step said the practical thing, and she said it to the middle of the room and not to him.

"That board will be against that wall on Friday morning, and so will everything else in here, and there is a clerk somewhere who will get this page and there is nobody in here to tell him whose hand is on any of those four."

"**Then the board is against the wall and the four are on the page and neither of those two things is about the other, and I have brought a board up nine steps and set it down, and I have not asked anybody here to agree with me about that, and I have not told her she is wrong about the other, and those are three things I have done this morning and not one of them is a thing anybody has to answer.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for any of them. He has asked in advance, in a lane, a long way back, that he is not to be thanked for anything he says, and he was not thanked in that room on that Monday, and the board stayed against the inside of the wall by the door all the rest of that afternoon and the nine of them went down the stairs by the fourth hour, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall among the last of them, and he took all nine steps slowly, and out into the market his own board went up on its string at the market end with the ordinary line on it and the cord coiled beside it, and nobody in that market knew that he had carried a second board up those steps that morning, put it against a wall, and brought nothing out of it.
