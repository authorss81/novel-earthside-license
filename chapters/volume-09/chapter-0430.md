# Chapter 0430 — A Voice At The Bottom Of The Stair About A Shut Board

A room over a market had the hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and the light came in low off the low ground and stopped about two feet short of the middle of the table. About four pairs of hands had the morning: a sheet of paper lying flat with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the length of the third of them and nothing in any of the four, a length of new rope over the back of a chair, a book shut on a shelf, and about nine people who had all been in that room before and none of whom was sent.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room at about the eighth hour and stood at the end of the bench nearest the step with his back against the wall, and he said the thing he had been working out since the Saturday, and he said it to the middle of the room in the middle of a cold morning, and about four people heard it and about five did not.

"**That page has been lying on that table every working morning since the morning it came up the road, and no morning has gone past in which nobody in this room looked at it, not once.**"

"Nobody has read it."

"**I am not saying anybody has read it. I am saying it is the only thing anybody in this city owns that gets looked at every morning and has nothing in it, and I sell nineteen rates on a board in four markets and I know the difference between a thing people look at and a thing people buy, and that page has come out the same on both sides of a fortnight, and I do not know what to do with having noticed it.**"

---

Nobody in that room told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and nobody in that room thanked him, and a man who sells nineteen rates on a board in four markets has spent his working life on the difference between a thing people look at and a thing people buy, and he has put the two of them either side of a page of paper in a room and the page has come out the same on both sides, and that a thing which is looked at every morning and has nothing in it is not a thing anybody is waiting for anybody to fill in, because anybody in this city who wanted to fill it in has had a fortnight and a room to do it in.

The sheet lay flat on the table where it had lain since the morning it came up the road, and the four places under its heading were all empty, and the crease pressed down the length of the third of them had not been undone and had not been deepened, and the crease has been looked at all fortnight and not one finger has been put in it.

---

At about the tenth hour a voice came up the bottom of the nine steps and stopped where the stair turns, and it was not one of the nine, and it said one sentence and then it went back down.

"Your board was shut on Monday and it was shut on Tuesday."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket, and the piece of chalk in that pocket did not move, and he did not get up, and he did not say one word back down the stair to the man who had said it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has a stall in a market has not got anybody to leave a message with and that the only instrument in this city for saying a thing to a man who is not in a room is a person who walks up nine steps and says it, and that this one had done that and had gone away again, and that the man it was for had not gone down.

---

Nobody in that room went down after the voice and nobody sent anybody. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book shut on the shelf it lives on and the sheet flat in front of her and the chalk on the edge of the table, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she could have written the day on a slate and put it on the shelf behind the table where anybody coming up those steps would read it, and that there is a slate on that shelf with a date on it and nothing under it and has had for the length of this flood, and that a second one of them is not going to be written either, and that nobody in that room has asked her for one.

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with the rope over the back of his chair and had been asked nothing that morning as he has been asked nothing all month, and nobody in that room looked at him, and the rope was not used.

---

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went down last and went all the way down and out into the market and put his stall board on its string with the ordinary line on it and the cord coiled beside it.

Nobody in that market knew that a man had stood at the top of nine steps about five hours before and been told by a voice at the bottom of a stair that he had lost two mornings. He was not thanked for opening up at all, and there is nobody in this city going to be. The page on the table in the room over that market has nothing in the third of its four places and is going out of this city on a date, and there is not one thing on it that will say afterwards who drew what.
