# Chapter 0438 — The Page Goes Out On Its Date And The Wall Says One Thing

The hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month went into the chalk on the door of a room over a market before the light came off the low ground, and it was a Friday, and by the eighth hour about nine people were in that room and every one of them had been in it before and not one of them had been sent. On that table the sheet lay where it had lain every morning: a heading, four places ruled under it, four marks down the second of those four one above another, a bone crease the whole length of the third of them, and nothing whatever in the first of them, the third of them, or the fourth. A stall board was against the inside of the wall by the door. A book was shut on a shelf and a second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date and nobody in that room went near the shelf.

---

The page went out of the room at about the ninth hour. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register folded it along the two folds that were already in it, put a stone off a doorstep on the top of it, and put it into the hands of a man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, who has carried five sheets of paper in a coat and a return in a satchel and this one, and who has not opened any of them.

"**It goes down to the end of the market and on to the Friday cart and nobody in this city is going to see it after that, and I am not going to say anything else about it in a room with a date on the door.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm and was at the wall end of the bench, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his own hand pressed flat on his coat over the pocket, and about four people in that room had watched a woman hand a page to a man who carries things and had not worked out what any of them were going to do about it.

---

The wall said one thing, and it said it once, and nobody in that room has ever been able to tell anybody where it came from. Nothing happened to the woman against the wall. She did not go the colour of that wall, and nothing else in the room moved at all.

> A line on a page is not a claim until somebody puts their name to it,
> and a page with a line on it and no name on it belongs
> to whoever comes along the road next.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was on his feet before the last of it had finished coming up, and the refusal was out of him in about four seconds.

"**I have got one of those nine miles up that road. I wrote it last, on purpose, in a harder piece of chalk than the rest of it, and it is on a wall and nothing has ever gone over the top of it, and you have just told this room that it is not mine either and that the reason nothing has moved it is that there has been nobody on that road to move it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on what he said. Nobody in that room thanked him for it, and he has asked in advance, in a lane, a long way back, not to be thanked for anything he says, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not move, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the rule on that wall took the only thing he has got in this flood and took it out loud in front of nine people, and the whole of that is his and nobody improved on it and nobody is going to.

"Nine miles is not nothing."

"**It is not nothing, and it is not mine, and I have said the whole of that in one go and I am not going to say it again this morning or any other morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the other thing that morning, and it was said to the middle of the room and not to anybody in it, and it was said flat.

"**Four of them drew those four and there is not one of their names on any of them, and the one place that could have put one there is empty on purpose with the crease still pressed the whole length of it, and anybody who comes along that road next can put a hand on any one of the four and there is nothing on that page to stop them.**"

Nobody in that room told him he was right. Nobody in that room told him he was wrong. Nobody in that room thanked him, and a man who has been asked nothing in this flood for about a month said what he said in a room with a date on the door and the room let him have the whole of it and said nothing, and about four people in that room worked out afterwards that the page was going anyway and that they had all known it before the man at the wall end of the bench had said a word.

---

A woman near the step said the last thing anybody said in that room that morning, and she said it to the table.

"It is a page with four lines on it and no person on it, and it has gone out of this room, and that is all that has happened."

"**Then that is all that has happened, and there is a stall at the market end that goes up at the seventh hour, and I am going to be standing behind it, and that is not a thing any of you have to do anything about.**"

Nobody said thank you on that morning. The sheet went out of this city on the hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month with four marks on it and nothing in the third of its four places, in a satchel with a flap, under a stone off a doorstep, in the hands of a man who carries things for a living and did not open it, and that day is not a silence in this city and the count of them is one lower because of it, and the day on the door was the hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went down first.
