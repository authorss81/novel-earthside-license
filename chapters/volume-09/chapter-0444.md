# Chapter 0444 — Four Stalls In That Market Have Changed Hands Since The Water

The hundred and twenty-ninth day of the Bare Month went into the chalk on the door of a room over a market on a Thursday at about the seventh hour. The light came in over the step at the back and lay along the bench and stopped short of the middle of the table the way it does. About nine people were in that room before the light was off the low ground. The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair that he did not touch and has never used.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had a piece of chalk and a flat board of his own on his knees. He said the thing he had been carrying up those nine steps with, and he said it before anybody had asked him for it, which is a thing he does.

"**Four stalls in that market have changed hands since the water came, and every one of the four new men has paid a day's stallage for a market where nothing is selling, and that is the first thing anybody in this room has found out this week that is not about a page.**"

Nobody thanked him for it, and nobody in that room told him he was right. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the other end of that bench, with the register shut on the shelf behind the table where it lives. A second slate was on that shelf with a date on it and nothing under the date.

---

A woman who has had a stall in that market since before the water said the correction from the step, and she said it without any pleasure in it at all.

"The four."

"**The four have not changed hands. The same four people have been behind the same four boards every morning since the water, and two of them have put their own name on a board that belonged to somebody who has gone up the road, and one of them has had a new front put on and calls that taking it over.**"

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the short half of it, without any heat. What he had been doing all morning was counting boards. The woman who had the stall from the step finished it off, and she finished it off the way a person finishes off a thing that is not about her.

"**You have been counting boards and a board is not a stall, and the man at the end of this market has had the same board on the same string since before the water with a different piece of chalk on it, and if you went down there and asked him he would tell you that himself and he would think you had come to take something.**"

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took the slate off his knee and put it down on the table and left it there. He said the other half of it, and it was not about boards.

"That is a thing I have been wrong about, and I would rather have been wrong about that than about a page. And the part I cannot get round is that somebody four hundred miles off is keeping a list of a market in this city and the list has got four names on it, and that list came out of the same satchel the pages come out of, and I am not saying the two are the same thing because nobody in this room is going to be able to prove it and I am not going to say it."

Nobody in that room agreed with him out loud, and nobody in that room disagreed with him out loud. A man who writes nineteen rates for four markets has decided on his own that he would rather be wrong about a market than right about a page. Nobody in the room told him that was a strange thing to prefer.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back, and one of the four boards in that market is his. He did not say one word about it and nobody asked him.

The length of new rope over the back of a chair at the end of that bench was not the same length of rope as anything on a shut road out of this city, and was not joined to anything. The day on the door was the hundred and twenty-ninth day of the Bare Month, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and nobody in that room had found out anything else.
