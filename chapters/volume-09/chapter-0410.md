# Chapter 0410 — A Barrow Put Down On The Low Side Of A Fence

A shut road out of this city has a fence along the top of that road, and on the ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, the light came up that road from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over, and the wind was up the same road in pushes that have been up it since before the water and have never once been written down, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a barrow with a hurdle of hazel rods in the bottom of it and a handcart coming up the same road behind him with a hazel mallet in it and a coil of rope on top of the coil, and a length of new rope over the back of a chair in a room in this city that is nowhere near this road.

---

The man of about fifty-nine came up that road at about the seventh hour with his barrow, the way he has come up it four times in nine years, and he has walked this road four hundred miles in nine years and has never once got to the end of it and has never once been asked why, and he put the barrow down on the low side at the fence and took his hands off the handles.

The hurdle in the bottom of the barrow is one he made and the bar across the top of it is one he replaced, and both of them were on that barrow when he started up, and there was nothing in the barrow when he started and there is nothing in it now, and there is not going to be.

---

The fence is sixteen willow posts cut in a copse about four miles the other way, and eleven withies bound along it with the same cord that is on the head of the mallet in the cart behind him. Nine of the posts went into the ground on a Saturday, which is the three hundred and forty-eighth day of this flood. Four more went in on the Monday after that, which is the three hundred and fiftieth day, and that made thirteen. The last three went in on the Thursday after that, which is the three hundred and fifty-third day, and there has not been a seventeenth and nobody in four hundred miles has said there is going to be one.

A man of about fifty-seven has gone that fence end to end more than once with his palm flat on every post as he went and got sixteen both times, and he has said the figure out loud to nobody on a shut road, and nobody has ever put the question to him of how he came by it, and nobody is going to. A man of about sixty put it up, was never sent for, and nobody is going to ask him about that either.

There are about four hundred paces between that fence and a mark in a field, and about four feet is a number a man of about sixty has in his mouth and it is not a number he took off anything, and nobody in this flood is going to that field and the mark has no word on any page in the world.

---

A handcart came up the same road behind him with a hazel mallet in it and a coil of rope on top of the coil, and the coil has been used and is not new, and about four people in this city have worked out since that there are two lengths of rope that people talk about in this flood and that they are not the same rope and that neither of them has ever been asked about by anybody.

They had not asked each other anything for about a fortnight and neither of them was going to start.

"Nobody is going to ask you."

"**Nobody is going to ask me, and there are three people on that road, and in nine years of walking it nobody has put one question to me, and I am not going to ask about the fence and nobody is going to ask me about the fence, and I would like it said out loud, here, on a shut road, that a man has watched three people go past him and has not asked them one thing and is not going to.**"

He said it to his barrow. The man of about fifty-nine does not address people, and a man who has walked a road four hundred miles long with nobody on it until the three of them came up it has nobody to address, and the only thing on that road that can hear him is the thing he is holding.

---

The three of them were ten days down that road, measured from the Sunday they went out of this city, which is the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and each of them had a barrow of his own or her own. There are three barrows on that road and none of them is the one standing on the low side of the fence and none of them is the one in the cart behind him. Nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road. Nobody in this city has ever been able to, including the three, and a party of four is four, and the fourth of the four is not on that road and nobody is going to ask her about anything, and nobody on that road counted anything this morning.

He looked down the road for a while. He did not go after them.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was on the low side of that fence at about the eleventh hour, beside the barrow, and he had not stopped at the fence on the way up and he had not looked at it, and he said four things, and he said all four of them to the barrow and not to the man of about fifty-nine, and not one of them was about where the fence is.

"**Sixteen.**"

Nobody asked him what sixteen was and nobody asked him how he knew it and nobody in this city has ever asked that man about a fence.

"**I have not been asked one question in this flood and I am not going to be, and there is a sheet in a room in this city with a place for a person in it, and nobody is going to ask me about that either.**"

"Nobody is going to ask you, and that is not because of anything you have done. It is because there is nothing in four hundred miles that can put a question to a man and get an answer back that anybody can check."

"**I am not moving it and I am not being asked where it is and I am not telling anybody.**"

"**There is a mark in that field and I have not been to it and I am not going to, and a man has a number in his mouth about the gap and it is his number and it is nobody else's.**"

He put his hand flat on the top of the sixteenth post on his way past and did not stop, and the length of new rope was over the back of a chair in a room in this city all morning and nobody went near it and it has still never been used.

---

Then the man of about fifty-nine shouldered his barrow off the low side and went back down the road he had come up on, and he did not halt at the fence and he did not look at it going past, and the light came up the road from the low ground behind the trees and stopped at the fence and did not come over.

Sixteen posts and eleven withies, and the man who built them was not sent, and nobody standing in front of that fence has been asked a single question about any of it, and none of that has changed in fifty days.

And below the fence the road went on down into the low ground, and at the foot of that road there is a lane with nine houses in it, and that lane is not in this city. One of the four lines on a sheet in a room over a market is going to be a woman's, and a household in this city has already said no to a page out loud, in its own kitchen, and nobody in four hundred miles is going to ask that house anything again. Nothing in this flood has moved and something has.
