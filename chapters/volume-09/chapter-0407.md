# Chapter 0407 — A Piece Of Chalk And A Man Who Is Not Going To Use It

A market at the bottom of a city had a stall with a board over it and the light came off the water at about the seventh hour and was gone off the low ground by the ninth, and what was under about four pairs of hands on the ninety-second day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, was a board with a line on it in chalk, a slate of his own, and a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood.

---

He wrote the first line of the morning at about the seventh hour and stopped, and the line he wrote was the ordinary line, and the reason it was the ordinary line is that a man who writes a different one gets asked why, and he has been asked why about five times and has not answered any of the five.

The board was a stall board and it had chalk dust in the grain of it from every morning of this flood, and the piece of chalk in his pocket was a harder one than the one on the string, and it had gone in on the forty-third day of the Short Month and had not been used to write anything anybody could read.

He sold two things before the light came off the water and he did not look up the road at any point in that hour.

---

The store is at one end of that market and its board is on the outside wall of it, and a man of about twenty-seven washes it every morning with a bucket and a rag, and he has washed it every morning since the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood, which is the last day anything was written on it, and the figures on it are the figures of that day and they have been the figures of that day ever since, and he decided about a month ago not to wash the wall above it.

He stopped in front of it with his own board under his arm and did not say anything about the figures and did not say anything about the wall.

"**I have sold on a board every morning of this flood and I have never washed a wall in my life, and a man who does not know what is on a wall should not wash it, and you have not washed it for about a month, and I would like to know what you decided that on.**"

"I decided it on my own. The figures on that board are the figures of the day it was last written on, and there is nobody coming back to write on it, and there is nobody in this city who is going to come and rub them out."

"**Then go on not washing it. I write a line on a board every morning of this flood and I could take those figures off that wall with my sleeve in about four seconds, and I am telling you that a man has thought about it, and nobody is going to do it.**"

He said it flatly, to a man washing a board, on a Tuesday morning, at the end of a market, and about four people in this city who have been in a room with that man in it have worked out since that he says things once and then stops, and that a thing said once to a man washing a board at the end of a market is not going to be said again to anybody in a room with a date on the door.

---

He went up the nine steps at about the eighth hour and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book open on the table with three columns in it, and the third one has been empty on purpose for fifty days and she was not looking at it and neither was anybody else.

The sheet was on the table with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the third of them, and about nine people were in that room and every one of them had come up those steps before, and four people in that room had worked out since Sunday what the third of the four places is, and that nobody in this city has been asked to put themselves on one in their life.

He sat at the back. He had his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and he did not take the chalk out of it, and about four people in that room who have watched a woman not ask him for a piece of chalk have worked out since that a man with a piece of chalk in his pocket is a man everybody in the room looks at, and that nobody is going to look at the second slate on the shelf behind the table in front of him.

---

"You have not written anything on that board."

"**I have not written anything on it since the two hundred and twenty-fifth day of this flood, and the figures on it are the figures of the day before that, and the man who washes it has decided not to wash the wall above it, and that is the whole of what has happened to it and nobody has done it on purpose and that is what has kept it true.**"

"You think a board nobody writes on is worth something."

"**I think it is the only instrument anybody in this city has said out loud has not lied to anybody, and I have not said that in a room with a date on the door before this morning and I am not going to say it twice.**"

Nobody thanked him for it. He has said out loud, in advance, in a lane, that he is not to be thanked, and two of the people in that room had heard him say it more than once and neither of them said a word about it.

---

Then she asked him the thing, and it was not the thing anybody in this city has ever asked him.

"Will you sit at the front tomorrow."

"**I will sit at the back tomorrow, and I will not be the reason anybody starts anything, and there is a page on this table with a person in it and I am not going to be the man who puts a name in it.**"

The chalk stayed in the pocket and the hand stayed flat on the pocket, and he had said it out loud, in a lane, a long time before there was a page on any table in this city, and a man who says that first and then does it is a man who has made himself useless on purpose and told everybody he was going to.

"**I have had this chalk since the forty-third day of the Short Month and I am not going to take it out, and if this room decides to draw something on that page tomorrow I am not going to hand anybody the thing to do it with.**"

Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him again, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who writes a line on a board nine miles up a road, last, in a harder piece of chalk, on purpose, and has refused five times to say whether it works, is a man who has an opinion about what goes at the top of a thing and what goes at the bottom of it, and he did not say what his opinion was and nobody is going to make him.

---

The room went on for about another four hours with the sheet on the table and nothing in it, and about nine people in it did not fill in anything, and the ninth line is not moved and not dated and not read aloud and not taken down and whether it works stays unanswered, and the things this city is going to ask that man about are not this morning and are not going to be this week.

He went down the nine steps at about the third hour of the afternoon with his hands in his pockets and the chalk still in the one on the right. The room over a market was set for the next morning with about nine people in it who were already going to be in it, and the sheet with a place for a person in it was on the table, and nobody had been asked anything, and there was nothing in that room that anybody was going to be able to say afterwards had been decided in it.
