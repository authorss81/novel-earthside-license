# Chapter 0439 — A Table With A Clean Rectangle On It

Nobody was sent for anywhere on the hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month, which was a Saturday, and by the ninth hour about nine people were in a room over a market and had all of them come up nine steps for their own reasons. The sheet was not on the table. There was a clean rectangle on the wood where it had lain, the size of a sheet of paper with a heading on it, and there is no cloth on that table and there is no board on it either, and a stall board was still standing against the inside of the wall by the door where a man had put it on the Monday before, and he had come back for it himself at the end of that afternoon and carried it down the steps, and nobody in that market knew that he had carried a second board up nine steps that morning.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair, and he has not touched it and has never used it and nobody in that room said one word to it on that morning or on any morning since, and he has not been asked anything on that morning either. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back and the piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket, and nobody in that room thanked him for anything he had said the day before and nobody asked him whether he had said it.

Nobody in that room said the word page out loud more than twice that morning, and neither of the two was said to anybody in it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came in about the tenth hour and stood in the doorway for a while and looked at the clean rectangle on the table, and then he went and sat down at the wall end of the bench and put his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand and said nothing at all for about as long as it takes to fill a pail.

"You are looking at the table."

"**I am looking at the table, and I have been in this room every working morning since that page came up the road, and I have looked at that table every one of those mornings, and this is the first morning there has been nothing on it, and I have not got a thing to say about it and I would rather be quiet.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room asked him for more of it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who trades nineteen rates on a board in four markets spent the middle of a Saturday morning saying that he had nothing to say, and that the reason he was in the room at all was that there was nothing in the room to go to.

---

At about the fourth hour somebody's attention went out of the window over the step at the back, because a man with a barrow had gone up the road out of this city at the seventh hour that morning. He has gone up it four times in nine years, with a hurdle of hazel rods in the bottom of the barrow that he made and a bar across the top of it that he replaced, and there was nothing in the barrow and there is not going to be.

About four people who have been up that road before say that he puts the barrow down on the low side at the fence and picks it up again and does not stop at the fence, and that nobody in this city is going to ask him why, and that nobody in that room was going to be told any of it.

---

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour and the room had a clean rectangle on its table for the rest of that day and the whole of the next one, and the second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date and was not picked up and was not turned over and was not written on, and a room in this city that has spent a month of mornings with a page on a table has spent one morning of it without one and nobody in that room found anything to do about it, and the day on the door was the hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month and nothing was said about that day by anybody.
