# Chapter 0435 — A Cart-Track Taken Out On The Low Side Of A Shut Road

A shut road out of this city runs under a fence along the top of it, and on the hundred and twentieth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, the wind came up that road in pushes that have been up it since before the water and have never once been written down, and the light came up out of the low ground behind the trees and stopped at that fence and did not come over. Two pairs of hands had that morning. A handcart stood up above in the wet with a hazel mallet in it and a coil of rope on top of the coil, and about nine yards of the cart-track on the low side had gone soft in the rain and taken its crust off, and a man with a barrow came up the same road behind the cart at about the eleventh hour and did not stop at the fence.

---

The man of about sixty had the mallet and the cord and the coil, and the coil has been used and is not new, and the only length of rope anybody in this flood talks about is over the back of a chair in a room in this city and has still never been used, and those are two ropes and nothing has ever put them in one sentence.

He was not doing anything to the fence. He had a bundle of wedges out of the cart and a length of board and he was packing the wet ground under the boards of the track with the back of the mallet, one at a time, in about nine yards, and the light at the fence did not move and the wind came up the road in the same pushes, and a man who put a fence up out of nothing at the top of a shut road has never once been sent and is not going to be, and what he was doing to the cart-track was not the fence and was not for anybody.

---

The man of about fifty-seven came up that road with his barrow at about the eleventh hour and stopped at the cart the way a man stops at a cart, and there was nothing in his barrow and there is not going to be, and he did not take the end of a wedge and he did not take the end of the board, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man who is not asked to hold a thing is a man who has learned what being asked is worth, and that this time nobody offered and nobody had to be told twice about it.

The man of about sixty did not turn round and did not stop working, and the wedge went under the board and the mallet came down on the back of it, and neither of them said one word to the other, and the man at the end of the barrow said his one thing out loud in the wind on a shut road, to the barrow and not to the man.

"Nobody ever asks me to hold anything."

"**You have not asked me and I am not going to start now, and the wedge goes under the board and the mallet comes down on the back of it, and that is the whole of the arrangement between the two of us, and it has been the whole of it every time the two of us have been on this road together.**"

Nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to count nine. The man of about sixty put the mallet down in the cart and turned round for the first time that morning, and the coil of rope went on top of the coil on its own.

"**You have been saying things to that barrow since the light came and I have not asked you to stop, and I am not going to. I will be up here on the next day the ground is soft and you can say it again.**"

He did not turn back round. He picked the mallet up and went on packing the wet ground under the boards, and the fence along the top of that road is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound along it with the same cord, and nobody has ever measured anything on that road, and the light stopped at the fence and did not come over.

---

The man of about fifty-seven got his barrow up off the low side and went back down the road he had come up on, and going past that fence he did not stop and did not look at it. The man of about sixty finished about nine yards of track and left the mallet in the cart and left the cart where it was, and that road goes on down under the fence into the low ground, and there is a lane of nine houses at the foot of it, and that lane is not in this city, and nothing on that road moved today except nine yards of cart-track a man packed with the back of a mallet, and nobody sent him.
