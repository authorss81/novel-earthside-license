# Chapter 0411 — One Of The Four Lines Is Going To Be Hers

A room over a market had the ninety-sixth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Saturday, and the light was off the low ground by the sixth hour and came up under the eaves at the market end of the room and did not reach the table, and the cold was in the boards of the stair and in the grain of the table, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading, a lump of chalk, a book shut on a shelf, and a length of new rope over the back of a chair with a man in it.

---

The woman of about fifty-two came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour and sat down at the end of the bench that is furthest from the door, and nobody in that room looked up when she came in, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been in a great many of them and been asked one question in none of them does not sit where she can be seen from the door.

The sheet was on the table where it had been since the morning it came up the road. It had a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading, and a crease pressed down the third of the four with a bone, and there was nothing in any of the four of them. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the chalk on the edge of the table and her hands flat on either side of it and was not touching it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket, and there is a piece of chalk in that pocket and it has not been taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, and nobody in that room asked him for it and nobody was going to.

---

"Do you want the sheet moved."

"**No.**"

The woman of about fifty-two did not look at her when she said it, and she said it in about the time it takes to put a hand flat on a table, and the last time she had said anything in a room in this city somebody had written it down on a slate and had put nothing under it.

"Then you can see it from where you are sitting."

"**I can see it from where I am sitting, and I have been able to see it since I came up those steps, and nobody in this room has asked me to look at it and nobody is going to, and I would like it said out loud in here this morning that I read a page nobody gave me.**"

The register-keeper did not say anything at all until the light off the low ground had finished coming up under the eaves. She put her right hand flat on the table on the side of the sheet that is away from the crease, and she did not move the sheet, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who puts her hand down next to a thing instead of on it is a woman who has decided where the thing is and not what is in it.

A page with four places ruled under a heading is four things waiting, and the second of the four is a line, and a page with four places on it does not divide itself. A woman who had been able to count them from the far end of a bench since she came up the steps had counted them before anybody in the room had said one word about them.

---

Nobody asked anybody anything for the next hour and a bit, and the man of about fifty-seven at the end of the bench with the rope over the back of his chair did not look up at any point in it, and a man nobody asks anything learns early that a room which has gone quiet has not finished with him.

Then she said it, and she said it to the middle of the room, and she had not been asked.

"**There are going to be four lines on that page and one of the four is going to be mine, and I have said it out loud before anybody said it, so that nobody in this room can stand there later on and say that I was asked.**"

Nobody answered her. Nobody told her she was right and nobody told her she was wrong, and nobody asked her where her line was, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who says a thing out loud in a room before she has been asked it has not given anybody a question to answer, and that there is no form in four hundred miles on which an answer to nothing can be entered.

"You have not been asked."

"**Nobody has asked me anything at all, and I did not need to be, and I have had a door of my own that nobody has ever asked me to open and I have not opened it for anybody in this flood and I am not going to open it on a Saturday for a sheet of paper.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall moved himself a foot further along the bench at the back without turning round at all, and nobody in that room caught him at it, and nobody in that room has any business whatever knowing what a man of about thirty-four does with his own bench.

---

Nothing was drawn on anything that morning and nothing was written on anything, and the register stayed shut on the shelf it lives on and the chalk stayed on the edge of the table where she had left it, and the four places on the sheet stayed as empty as the four of them had been when the page came up the road in a satchel from a building nine hundred miles off.

About four people in that room have worked out since that what the woman said at the end of the bench changed the arithmetic of the morning and changed nothing on the table, and that four people in a room who have just been told that one of them is going to be on a page are four people in a room who are now going to have to decide whether to ask her, and that nobody is going to ask her, and that the not-asking is the cost and the cost is hers and she put it on the table about as long as it takes to fill a pail before she came up those steps.

She went down the nine steps at about the eleventh hour with her hands in her sleeves and the rope over the back of a chair behind her and the sheet on the table with a crease down the third of the four places and nothing in it, and nobody in that room said one word to her on the way out, and a door at the end of a lane in this city was not knocked on that day and is not going to be.
