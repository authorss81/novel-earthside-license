# Chapter 0405 — Four Places Under A Heading, And The Third Of Them Is A Person

A room over a market had the ninetieth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Sunday, written before anybody came up the nine steps and not written after, and the light came in over the step at the back with no name on it and lay along a bench that nobody was sitting on, and the cold was out of the floor and in the grain of a board, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading, a book with three columns in it, a lump of chalk, a second slate on a shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and about nine people who had all been up that stair before.

---

Nobody was sent. The sheet came up the road in a satchel on Wednesday morning and it was read out on this morning because there was nothing to be read out on any other morning, and there was no room in this city this week with a day set aside for it, and there is not going to be one again.

About nine people came up nine steps between the seventh hour and the ninth. Every one of them had been up those steps for something else. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up and sat at the back. The man of about thirty-nine came up and put his back against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step and put his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand. The man of about fifty-seven came up and sat at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair. The man of about forty-one in a very good coat came up and took the near end of the bench and put a document on his knee.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the chalk down on the edge of the table and did not sit down.

---

"What this is for, before anybody asks me, because nobody in this room is going to get a second morning out of it."

She said it in the local language, standing, the way she says things, and about four people in that room had been in it for that reason for about a month and were not going to get it a second time this morning.

"**It is a form. It is not a return and it is not a notice and nobody in this city sent for it, and it has a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading.**"

The man in the very good coat turned a page of his own document over at the near end of the bench and did not look up. He knew what was being read out and he could not have followed it if he had tried, and the woman reading it knew that he could not follow it, and the two of them were in the same room on purpose and neither of them said so.

"**I am going to read the four of them out loud in the order they are printed on, and I am going to stop at each one long enough for the people at the back to hear it, and I am not going to say one word about what any of them is for.**"

The man in the very good coat had his document open on his knee and was reading it. He read it for about an hour with his head down and he did not look at the sheet on the table, and the two of them were ten feet apart and neither of them was at the other one's table. Two people doing the same work on the same morning from opposite ends of a cold room, and not one word of it passing between them.

---

"The first of the four places is a place."

She stopped. The room was quiet at the back and she waited for it, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a woman who waits at the end of a line for the people at the back is a woman who has decided that the people at the back are the ones she is reading to.

"The second of the four places is a line."

She stopped again.

"The third of the four places is a person."

She stopped, and this time the room did not go back to quiet, because there is no page anybody in this flood has been handed before that has had a place in it that a person could be put into, and about nine people in a cold room found that out at the same time, on a Sunday morning, in a room with a date on the door, and four of them have since worked out that they are not going to be asked about it today and are not going to be told about it today either.

"The fourth of the four places is a day."

She put her finger on the paper beside the fourth one, not in it, and the third one had a crease pressed down it with a bone and nothing at all in it, and the first and the second and the fourth were empty as well and the sheet had come up a road with all four of them empty and it was going to be exactly that way by the time the light went.

---

Adrian Vale was at the shelf behind the table. He was not asked to do anything and nobody in that room knew why he was in it. He read the second slate where it lay on the shelf, without lifting it and without turning it over and without putting a hand on the frame of it, and what was on it was a date and nothing under the date, and he read that and nothing else, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man standing at a shelf reading a slate that nobody has touched in fifty days is a man who has been given the least he could be given and has taken it.

Nobody in that room asked him what the third of the four places was for. Nobody in that room asked the man of about fifty-seven where anything was. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-four who kept a stall what a line was, and he has never been asked that by anybody in this city, and he was at the back of the room with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and a piece of chalk in that pocket that he has not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight, and four people in that room knew the day the chalk went in. Not one of them said it out loud.

---

She read the heading again at about the fourth hour, and then she said the thing that costs her, and she said the cost first, in her own mouth, standing, with the chalk still on the edge of the table where she had left it.

"**The cost of not saying what the third place is for today is that about four people in this room are going to leave here this afternoon still not knowing, and I am not going to pretend that is a small thing, and I am not going to be the one who fixes it in a week.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall shifted along the bench at the back and did not turn round, and nobody in that room saw him do it and nobody in that room was going to ask him about it.

"**I am not going to say what that place is for until I have found out what it is for. The reason is that the last time a column was read out loud in this room the whole of what was in it came out at once, about nine things at once, and one of the things that came out was about a man who had never been asked anything by anybody.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her. Nobody argued with her either. A woman who reads a form out in the order it is printed on is not a woman who is going to improve on it later when somebody objects, and a room which has just heard the third of four places named and has not answered it has been told something about itself. That is what four of the nine worked out and none of them said it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine said nothing at all that morning. He stayed against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand, and his right hand did not move off the face of it from the seventh hour to the fourth, and about four people in that room have watched a man keep a hand on the face of a slate through a whole morning of being told a thing and have worked out since that the hand is not on the slate.

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat said one thing, and he said it in his own language, and nobody in that room understood one word of it and nobody asked him to say it again, and the woman who keeps a public register looked at him for about as long as it takes to count nine and then went on with the date on the door, and a page that has been read out in a room where one person cannot follow it is a page that was read out for the eight.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said one thing at the back of the room, and it was about the light, and it was true.

"It will be off this bench by the fourth hour."

"**Then we will be on the step at the back by the fifth, and the step has no name on it, and about nine people have been standing on it through every morning of this flood without it once being on anything.**"

He did not ask to be answered and she did not say anything else, and a man who sells on a board for a living knows what the light does on a bench better than anybody in a cold room and said so, and a man who gives a room a fact it can use is not a man who has been asked a question.

Nobody filled anything in. The sheet is not filled in and it is not sent and it did not go back anywhere and it did not come back today, and it went down onto the table flat with a crease down its third column and nothing in it, and the date on the door was the ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and about nine people went down nine steps in the light with a thing they had heard read out and no instrument in four hundred miles to answer it with.
