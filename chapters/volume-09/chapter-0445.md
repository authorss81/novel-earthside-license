# Chapter 0445 — What Is A Line For, Asked In A Room And Answered By Nobody

A room over a market had the hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month on its door in chalk at about the seventh hour on a Friday, and the light came in over the step at the back the way it comes in, and about nine people were in that room and had all of them been in it before, and a woman of about sixty-nine came up the nine steps with a tin in both hands and stood at the end of the bench and did not sit down.

---

Nobody in that room asked her what she had brought the tin for, and nobody in that room has ever asked her what she carries it for, and a person who is not sent for is a person who came, and those are the only two kinds of coming there is in this flood. The register was on its shelf and a second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of the bench with his own hand shut flat over the inside breast pocket of his coat.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who is not sent for is one of about nine people who came, and that in this flood that is also the only kind of asking there is.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, at the back of that bench, said one ordinary thing to the room before she asked it, and it was about the door and it was about the cold and it was about nothing at all.

"**There is a draft on this bench every morning of this flood and I have never once said so out loud in this room, and I am saying it now because there is a woman standing at the end of this bench with a tin in her hands and I would like her to stand somewhere warmer and I would like nobody in this room to make anything of that.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for it, and a woman near the step said that the light on the flags had moved about a foot since the seventh hour.

"I will tell you what I have done about it, which is nothing, and I have been doing nothing about it since the seventh hour and I expect to go on doing nothing about it until the light goes off it."

---

She waited until the room had gone quiet in the ordinary way, and then she said it to the room and not to anybody in it, and she said it in the local language, and it took her about as long as it takes to say it.

"**What is a line for, on a page, when the only thing anybody will use it for is to move it.**"

Nobody in that room answered it.

About four of the nine people in that room had an answer and about four of them said nothing, and nobody in that room put either group in front of the other one, and nobody in that room thanked the woman who asked it, and nobody told her it was a good question, and nobody in that room said one word about it again for the rest of that morning.

---

A woman near the step said something else entirely, about a man on the boards at the bottom of the street who had been standing at the head of his own boards since before the light, and it was a fair thing to say and it had nothing to do with what had just been asked, and by then two other people in that room had said something else as well, and neither of those two things had anything to do with what had just been asked either. A man at the far end asked the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register whether the shelf behind that table had been there when the room was taken, and she told him it had, and he said that was all he wanted.

---

Nobody answered her. The tin stayed in her two hands and the tin was empty and had been empty on the last two occasions on which she carried it up those steps, and the man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair and was not asked anything and did not offer anything, and a page that has a place in it for a person went out of this city and came back and was lying on the table with a heading on it, and nobody in that room used any of it to answer the question.

At about the middle of the morning she went back down the nine steps with the tin in both hands and nobody went after her and nobody said anything to her on the way down, and the market went on under her the whole time. The day on the door was the hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and the question she asked went out of that room with her, and it is on no page in four hundred miles, and about four people who came through that market afterwards have worked out since that a woman of about sixty-nine said one thing at the top of those nine steps and nothing after it.
