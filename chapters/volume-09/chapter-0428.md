# Chapter 0428 — A Man Is Asked What A Line Is

In chalk on the door of a room over a market there was the hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, put there at about the seventh hour on a Tuesday, and the light came in over the step at the back, lay along a bench, got about halfway to the table and stopped. A sheet of paper was lying flat on that table with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the length of the third of them and nothing in any of the four. About four pairs of hands had the morning. A length of new rope was over the back of a chair with a man in it, there was a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket that nobody in that room had asked for, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the nine steps and sat down at the back.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up at about the ninth hour and asked a question, and she asked it in the local language, in daylight, with a date on the door, in front of about nine people, and she asked it once and she did not ask it twice.

"**What a line is. There is one of them in the second of these four places, and four people in this city are going to be asked to put one on this page, and nobody in this city has ever asked a man that. I am asking it. You are the man I am asking.**"

---

Nobody answered for him. The man of about thirty-nine at the wall end of the bench had his own slate flat on his knee and the man of about fifty-seven at the end of the same bench had the rope over the back of his chair and the woman who reads forms out loud for a living had just put a question in a room with a date on the door that was not on the table, and of the two men in that room who could have answered it she had picked one of them out loud, and nobody in that room put a hand up either way.

He answered from the back without turning round, and he answered in the local language, and it was five words, and he said them at the speed a man says a thing he has been carrying and has not put down.

"**Put down, and left there.**"

---

Nobody in that room thanked him. Nobody in that room said anything else about it. Nobody asked him a second question and nobody said the thing back to him, and nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong. A man who has said one thing out loud in a room in this city has spent the only thing he had, and there is no form in four hundred miles on which to enter it. It is in the heads of about nine people in one room and on no page anywhere.

He has asked out loud, in a lane, a long way back, that he is not to be thanked for anything he says, and he was not thanked, and four of the people in that room had heard him say that more than once and not one of them said a word about it.

---

Nobody in that room asked him for it a second time, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said so from the back without turning round, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been asked nothing all month did not ask a second question. The woman who reads forms out loud gave the reason anyway, once, in her own mouth, standing up, and nobody in that room improved on it.

"You are not going to be asked that again, and I am not going to be asked for the reason twice either."

"**I asked it because that page has four places under the heading and one of the four of them is a line, and there are four people in this city who are going to be asked to put one on it, and nobody in this city has ever asked anybody what one is.**"

Then she sat down, and the chalk stayed on the edge of the table where it had been all morning, and the third of the four places under the heading had a crease pressed down it with a bone and nothing at all in it, and the crease is still the only thing on that paper anybody in this city has ever touched.

---

Adrian Vale had been in that room since the seventh hour with his hand on the sheet on the table, and he had read the first of the four places and the second of them twice, and he had read the fourth, and he had not read the third of them, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the one place on that page he did not read that morning is the one with a person in it, and that he read it as a person and not as an empty square.

He did not say one word about what the man at the back of the room had said. He did not tell him what he had said, he did not tell anybody it was the name of anything, and nobody in that room asked him anything about it, and nobody in that room thanked him for being in the room, and a man who reads pages has no standing whatever in a room where something has just been said, and the arrangement between the two of them has not changed since either of them came up those steps.

---

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall went down last and went slowly, and the piece of chalk was in his inside breast pocket where it has been since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, and nobody in that market below said one word to him about it, and the door at the top of the stair had the hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month on it in chalk and nothing above it, and nothing was written on anything in that room that day, and the man of about fifty-seven went down the stairs without anybody saying good morning to him and without anybody saying good morning to anybody else.
