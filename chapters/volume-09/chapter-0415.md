# Chapter 0415 — A Man With A Document He Cannot Read Out Loud To Anybody

A room over a market had the hundredth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Wednesday, and the door was shut for the first hour it has been shut on a working morning in this flood, and the light came in under the eaves at the market end and stopped short of the middle of the floor, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a document inside the front of a very good coat, a sheet of paper on a table with a crease pressed down the third of the four places under its heading, a lump of chalk, and a book shut on a shelf.

---

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour and put his hat on the bench and did not take his coat off, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who does not take his coat off in a room is a man who came into it for the length of a document and not for the morning.

He said out loud, in his own language, that he does not have the local one. He has said it once in this city and he said it once this morning because nobody had said it for him and a man who has said a thing once in a room learns not to say it twice, and every one of them in that room is going to go on not arranging for it, and that is now a permanent fact about a man who sits in their rooms.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the sheet flat on the table in front of her and the chalk on the edge of the table and did not get up.

"You told us that in this room before."

"**I told you that in this room before, and nobody has done anything about it since, and I am not going to say it a third time, and I would like it noticed that I have said it twice and not once.**"

He looked at her for about as long as it takes to count nine, and then he went into the front of the coat with two fingers and brought out a document that somebody a long way from here had creased in four and had carried it folded like that ever since, and he laid it across his own knee and kept a palm flat on the whole of it.

---

The office that sent this sheet up the road sent another one down it in the same quarter, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the one that went out had a person's name at the top of it, correctly, and that this one has a place in it that a person could be put, and that neither of the two of them has anywhere on it to say who the office is actually asking about.

Nobody in that room said that out loud. Nobody said it in a mouth at all, and a thing four of them have worked out is not a thing anybody can hand to the one man in the room who would be the natural person to hand it to, because he cannot follow one word of the language it would have to be said in, and because a room that has worked out a thing and cannot give it to anybody is a room that has to go on carrying it by itself.

He read the document. It took him about as long as it takes to count nine and he read it with his head down and his hat on the bench beside his knee, and the woman who reads forms out loud for a living sat about ten feet from him and did not look at his knee once, and the two of them were in the same room on purpose and neither of them said so.

He shut the document and put it back inside the front of the coat and buttoned the coat.

"You have been in most of these rooms."

"**I have been in most of these rooms, and I have not been in one in which anything was decided, and nobody in this room has asked me where I have been, and I am not going to tell you, and that is not a thing I am keeping from you. It is that I was not there.**"

Nobody in that room asked him to say it again. Nobody asked him to say it in the local language and nobody in that room offered to find somebody who had it, and the day on the door was the hundredth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and it was a day like any other and nothing was said about it by anybody.

---

He went down the nine steps at about the fourth hour with the coat buttoned and the document in it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the whole of what a man in a very good coat is in this flood is a page he cannot read in a language he does not have in a room where nine people have been talking about a third column for a week, and that nobody in that room has ever once put him down and nobody in that room is going to try, and that nobody in that room is going to ask him anything about it again.

The sheet stayed on the table with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the third of them and nothing in any of the four, and the door was shut for an hour and a half and then it was not, and the hundredth day of the Bare Month went the way the ninety-ninth had gone and the ninety-eighth before it.
