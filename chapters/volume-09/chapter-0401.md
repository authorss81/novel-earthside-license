# Chapter 0401 — A Second Sheet Comes Up The Road In A Satchel

A room over a market had the eighty-sixth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the sixth hour, a Wednesday, and the light was on the water at one end of the market and off the low ground early at the other, and the cold was in the shutters and in the boards of the stair, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a satchel with a flap, a lump of chalk, a book shut on a shelf, and a sheet folded twice with a bone that had come up the road in somebody else's writing and was on a table in that room with its face down.

---

He came up the nine steps at the pace a man comes up nine steps with something in a satchel he is not going to hand over on a stair, and he put his hand flat on the door frame to stop it swinging back behind him, and he did not knock.

The room held two people. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book out of the shelf it lives on and shut again and standing beside it, and the second slate was on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and the chalk was on the edge of the table where she had left it after writing on the door.

He put the satchel on the table. He undid the flap. He took out a sheet and put the sheet on the table and shut the flap and put his hand flat on the satchel afterwards as if the satchel were the thing that had been risked.

Nobody in that room watched him turn it over. About four people in this city have watched him do this same thing with something somebody else wrote, and not one of them has ever seen him open one, and a man who carries things for a living is the only person in this city who can be shown to have been in seven places and cannot be shown to have been anywhere.

---

"You have brought it up the road."

"**I have brought it up the road, and it came in the satchel the return came in on the three hundred and fifty-first day of this flood, and it came off the same round, and the hand at the top of it is not a hand anybody in this city writes.**"

She did not look at the satchel. She looked at the sheet, face down, and there was a bone mark down each of two creases and a third line pressed across the paper that was not a fold in half.

"Read me the heading."

"**I am not going to open it.**"

"You have carried it up a road for that."

He did not look at her when she said it. He looked at the flap of the satchel, which was shut, and which he had shut himself about a quarter of an hour ago in a cold market with a sheet in it.

"**I have carried it up a road because that is what I do with things, and I have said out loud in a room in this flood what I do with the ones I carry and why, and I said it twice, and I am not going to say it a third time to two people in a room with the door shut.**"

"You said it in a room and there were nine of us."

He put his thumb back on the third line pressed across the paper and took it off again, and the bone marks on the two creases it had been folded on had been pressed hard enough to crack the surface, and neither of the two people in that room asked him how many times he had put a thumb on that line.

"**There were nine of you in that room and there is one of you now, and the answer does not improve on the number.**"

---

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a pail. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living looked at the third line pressed across the paper for the whole of that, and he did not open the fold along it, and the bone marks on the two creases the sheet had actually been folded on had been pressed hard enough to crack the surface of the paper, and they had been pressed that way a long way from here.

"There is a crease on it that is not one of the two it was folded on."

"**There is.**"

"What is under it."

"**I do not know, and I am not going to find out on your stair, and if you want to know what is under it you are going to have to open it yourself, and you are the one who keeps the book.**"

He said the last part without any weight at all, the way a man says a thing he has thought about for a month and has never once had an occasion to say. About four people in this city have worked out since that the price of no name at the top of a thing is not only that nobody can do anything for you, it is that the man holding the thing is the one person nobody is allowed to ask anything, and he had just said out loud that he would rather she did the opening than told him what was under a fold.

---

And the two of them found out what they were being asked on the morning they were being asked it, and neither of them had been sent anything about it in advance, and the reason is that there is no sheet of paper in this city that is going to go out and say a day is coming.

The four days are the cooling, and the cooling is the time a room has in which to find out what it is being asked. A sheet goes to a place and not to a house, four days out, unsigned, with a day on it and a place on it and nothing at the top of it, and a person who gets one has four days to ask somebody what it means before the room is full. There is not going to be an eighth of those in this flood, because a man working boards at the bottom of this city said out loud on a day in the last month, in a room with a date on the door, that the last one was the last one, and a room that has been given a sheet to read out on the morning it arrives is a room that has been given the reading and not the cooling, and this city got its rooms full seven times and is not going to get them full an eighth time, and so the two of them in this room are two people who came up nine steps because they were already in the building.

---

She turned the sheet over at about the eighth hour and read the heading and did not read anything under it, and about four people in this city could have told her what the heading meant, because a heading is a thing anybody with a book can read in about a second, and the heading on that sheet was in a hand that nobody in this city writes and it had four places ruled under it and the four of them were printed in an order that somebody in another building had chosen.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living watched her read it and then he took his hand off the satchel.

"I have carried five of these in a coat and a return in a satchel on the three hundred and fifty-first day of this flood and this one, and I have not opened one of the seven of them, and I am not going to open this one either, and you can put that on the table or you can leave it off, and I would rather you left it off."

"I am not going to write it in the book."

"**No, and you are not, and that is the arrangement and I have carried seven things and the arrangement has not changed once.**"

She put the chalk down on the edge of the table. She did not put the sheet in the satchel and she did not give it back to him, and a man who carries things for a living cannot be given a thing he has already handed over, which is the whole of what he has.

"Go and do something else."

"**I have three walls before the light goes and I have not been to any of them.**"

---

He went down the nine steps with the satchel shut and the flap down and the sheet on a table in a room over a market with its heading read and its four places unread, and the date on the door was the eighty-sixth day of the Bare Month and it was a Wednesday and it was written before he arrived and it was not written for him.
