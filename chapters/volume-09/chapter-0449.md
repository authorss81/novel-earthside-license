# Chapter 0449 — A Chair Is Moved And The Rope Goes Onto The Floor

The hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month was a Tuesday, and it was in chalk on the door of a room over a market before the light came off the low ground. About nine people were in that room by the eighth hour. The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with the length of new rope over the back of his chair, where it has been every morning of this flood, and where it has never once been used.

---

A woman at the far end of that bench got up to let somebody past her. She took hold of that chair by the back of it and moved it about a foot out along the bench, so that the person behind her could get down. The length of new rope came off the back of the chair and went onto the floorboards. The room went on talking about something else for about as long as it takes to count nine.

Nobody in that room said one word about it. Nobody asked him anything, and nobody told him anything. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his own hand shut flat over the inside breast pocket of his coat, and he said nothing at all about it. The register was on its shelf, and a second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date.

---

The man of about fifty-seven bent down and picked the length of new rope up off the floor, and put it back over the back of his chair. He did it in about the time it takes to pick up a thing and put it down again. He did not say anything while he was doing it, and he did not say anything after it. Nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody in that room has ever thanked him for anything.

A woman at the far end of that bench said the practical thing about the cold coming in under the door, and she said it to the middle of the room. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall answered her from the back of the bench without turning round.

"There is a cold on that bench this morning that was not on it yesterday, and it is coming in under the door and not out of the window, and one of us ought to do something about it and none of us is going to."

"**It has been coming in under that door every morning since the water came and this is the first one that has got as far as the middle of the floor, and I am not going to say that to her, because if I say it she will stop saying it, and she is the only person in this room who says it out loud.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what the rope is for, and nobody said the word fence in a sentence that had anything to do with it. About four people in that room have worked out since that a chair can be moved in a room, and that a thing on the back of it can fall on the floor and go back where it was, and that the person whose thing it is is the only one in that room who has to get down off a bench to deal with it. That is all of what that is.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said one thing that was not about the rope, which was that the fourth hour comes earlier on a Tuesday than it does on the other days of the week, and that he has never once worked out why. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not open the book, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said nothing else at all.

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour. The length of new rope was over the back of a chair at the end of that bench and had been used for nothing at all, and the day on the door was the hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, and nobody in that room said anything back to it.
