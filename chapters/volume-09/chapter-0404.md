# Chapter 0404 — Two Women Of Twenty-Nine And Neither Of Them In The Room

A kitchen at the end of a lane had the eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month coming in grey through one window at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, a Saturday, and the cold was in the flagstones and in the handle of the door, and what was under about four pairs of hands that evening was a folded sheet of road with about nine places on it, folded the same way every time, a knife with the edge gone, and a tin that was not in that room and was not opened and has not been opened in this flood.

---

She did not take the sheet out of the apron. She has not taken it out since the Tuesday she put a page on a step in front of one person and folded it the same way she has folded it every time since, and the fourth crease has gone soft in the middle where her thumb goes, and the only part of that page anybody in four hundred miles is losing is the crease.

The road on it is not nine hundred miles long. It is a road out of a market town, past two gates, along nine places of which she has put down four, and against four of those four there is a mark, and the mark is a turn and not a name, and there is no name on that sheet anywhere and there never has been.

A page came up a road in this city yesterday in a satchel and it has a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading, and one of those four places is a place a person could be put into, and it is going to be read out tomorrow morning in a room over a market, in the local language, in daylight, with a date on the door, and Tamsin Quill was not in that room yesterday and has not been asked to be in it tomorrow and there is no instrument in this flood that can ask anybody to come to anything.

---

She said it out loud to the kitchen table, and there was nobody in the kitchen.

"**A page that says where a line runs is the plainest claim in this flood.**"

The tin was on a shelf in another room and it was not brought out and it was not opened. It is not hers to open for this and she did not open it, and about four people in this city have known for a long time that there is a sheet thirty years old in a tin somewhere in this flood and that nobody in four hundred miles has ever been asked to see it and that the asking is not coming from anybody in a kitchen.

"I said that to a table."

She put her hand flat on the front of the apron, over the fold, and left it there.

"**I said it out loud because a thing you know and do not say is a thing you have chosen, and I chose it out loud in a room with a date on the door, and I am not going to un-choose it in a kitchen to make an afternoon easier.**"

Nobody in that kitchen was going to answer her, and nobody was going to forgive her for it either, and nobody was going to say that it had not happened, and the withdrawal was paid on the seventeenth day of the Short Month at the sixth hour with no apology and no refused apology and no forgiveness asked for and no forgiveness given, and it is not one of the things an afternoon is allowed to put back.

There is a man in this city who went out of a room at the sixth hour on that day and has not come back into this kitchen, and she is not going to go and find him, and there is no form in four hundred miles on which either of them could be made to, and the fifth thing that was never asked for is not going to be asked for tonight.

---

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and by the sixth hour the light that comes up off the river on them has gone flat and come off the water and is lying along them like something laid down.

She went down onto them at about the sixth hour and stood at the bottom of them for about an hour. The man of about thirty-eight was working them with a tally-board under his arm and a knife in his hand, and there is a stack at the top end higher than a man and a strip of clear board about as long as a cart at the bottom of it, and he did not look up when a woman came down onto the bottom of his boards, and she had known that he would not, and she had come anyway.

Four sentences, in an hour, and none of them was about the sheet that is going to be read out in the morning.

---

"You have not been up there."

"**I have not been up there and I have not been asked to and there is nothing in this flood that can make anybody be asked.**"

"That is not what I asked you."

"**Then I have answered the question that was in your mouth, and you can put the one you meant on the table when you have it.**"

He worked the boards. She stood at the bottom of them with her hand on the front of her apron and the four places with a turn against them nine miles away from here and not in this room.

"Your road is not his road."

"**My road is not the road that page is asking about, and I know that, and I have known it since the page was read out, and I have said so out loud in a kitchen tonight, and it is the one thing about that page I am certain of and it is also the one thing about it that is no use to anybody.**"

He did not look up. About four people who came down onto those boards in that hour have worked out since that a woman standing at the bottom of a set of boards with her hand on an apron and a man working them with a knife are two people who have both said out loud, in two different rooms, on two different days, that they know something about a page and cannot get it to where it would do any good.

---

She went up off the boards at about the eighth hour with the sheet still folded the same way in the front of the apron, and the crease in the fourth fold of it is soft, and there is no copy of it, and she did not give it to anybody and she is not going to give it to anybody.

In a room over a market there was a date on the door in chalk for the ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and about nine people were going to be in it in the morning, and every one of them had come up those nine steps before.
