# Chapter 0425 — The Third Of The Four Places Is Going To Be Left Empty

The hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month went into the chalk on the door of a room over a market from the sixth hour, and it was a Saturday, and the market underneath was already going by the time the light came off the low ground. A sheet of paper lay flat on the table in that room with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the length of the third of them and nothing in any of the four. A second slate was on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it, and a length of new rope was over the back of a chair with a man in it, and about four pairs of hands had the morning.

---

About nine people came up those nine steps between the seventh hour and the ninth, and every one of them had been up that stair for something else, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up and sat at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket, and the man of about fifty-seven came up and sat at the end of the bench, and the man of about thirty-nine came up and put his back against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step with his own slate flat on his knee.

The page had been read out in this room on the ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and nobody had been sent for that morning and nobody had been sent for this one, and every morning between the two had been a morning this room was in and nobody in it had gone down nine steps to ask anybody anything, and that is why it came to a decision in one sitting and did not come to one at all for a fortnight.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up at about the ninth hour, and she had the sheet flat in front of her and the chalk on the edge of the table and she did not touch either, and she said the first thing first.

"**The third of these four places is a person. A page with a person on it finds them.**"

She stopped there. She had said two things and the second one of them was the whole of what a page with a person in it does, and four of the nine in that cold room had known it since a Sunday morning and had watched the other five find it out at the same time, and nobody in that room said she was right and nobody said she was wrong.

---

Then she said the cost, and she said it before the decision, and she said it standing up in the same voice she had used to read four places out loud in front of nine people.

"**The cost of leaving it empty is that a line with nobody's name on it will be easier to move than one with a name on it, and that whoever is standing where one of them runs is the one who finds out last. That is not me and it is not anybody at this table and I am not going to name one of you while I am saying it.**"

Nobody answered that. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not turn round and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not move, and nobody in that room looked at the man at the end of the bench while it was being said, and the length of new rope stayed over the back of the chair it was on and it was not used and it has still never been used.

---

Then she decided, and she said it in one sentence, and there was no vote and nobody had asked for one.

"**Both of those are true. I have just said the true thing about a person on a page and I have just said the true thing about a person not on one. I am leaving the third place empty.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her. Nobody in that room told her she was right and nobody told her she was wrong, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the reason she was going to leave the third of the four empty was not that it was the smaller of the two, and about four of them are not going to forgive her for it, and about four of them are not going to blame her for it, and she is thanked by nobody either way and did not ask to be.

---

A woman at the far end of the bench asked a practical question about a quarter of an hour later, and it was a fair one, and the room turned to the man of about thirty-nine to ask it to, because he is the one person in that room who writes on a board for a living.

"How does a person put a line on a page that has not got a person in it."

The man of about thirty-nine said nothing at all. His right hand stayed flat on the face of his own slate where it had been since the seventh hour and his eyes went to the sheet on the table and stayed there, and about four people in that room have watched a man keep a hand flat on the face of his own slate through a whole morning in which he was told a thing and asked one and did not answer it, and he was not asked it again.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the only thing he said that morning, and he said it to the back of his own bench and not to her.

"**Then there is nothing for me to do in this room and there has not been for a fortnight, and I would like it said that I am not the reason there is, and I am not going to be, and I have a piece of chalk in this pocket and it is going to stay in this pocket.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either. He has said out loud, in a lane, a long way back, that he is not to be thanked, and his stall stood shut at the market end for the whole of that Saturday because he was up nine steps in it, and about nine people who came to that market for what he sells found a shuttered board and went on down the market, and nobody in that room heard about any of it and nobody came down the steps to tell him.

---

Nobody in that room wrote anything on anything that day. The second slate stayed on the shelf behind the table with a date on it and nothing under it and was not picked up and was not turned over, and the four places under the heading stayed as empty as the four of them had been when the page came up the road, and the crease pressed down the length of the third of them is still the only thing on that paper anybody in this city has ever touched.

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour in the light, and the day on the door was the hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month, and nothing was said about that day by anybody, and the third of the four places on a sheet in this city was going to be left empty, and the woman who left it empty went down the stairs last and did not say one word to anybody on the way past.
