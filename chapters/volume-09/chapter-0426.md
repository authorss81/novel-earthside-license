# Chapter 0426 — A Stall Out In The Cold With Nobody At It

A market at the bottom of this city had the hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month over it, a Sunday, and the cold came off the water in a way that had got into the flagstones by the seventh hour and stayed in them, and the light lay along the market from the water and did not move all morning. A stall board went up on its string with the ordinary line on it in chalk, a barrow stood at the end of that stall with nothing in it and has had nothing in it for about a month, and a handful of people came through that market in the first hour and none of them stopped at it.

---

He set the board up himself and put the string out on the board beside it and stood behind the two of them with his hands in his sleeves, and he had his own piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket where it has been since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, and he did not take it out to write the line on the board, because the line on the board is a different piece of chalk on a string and has been since before the water.

Nobody came. That is the whole of it. People came through that market in the first hour and more of them in the second and not one of them stopped, and the day before, nine steps above them, in a room with a date on the door, somebody had decided something out loud that nine people heard, and not one person in that market knew a thing about it. Nobody in that market had been kept out of anything. There was nothing to have been kept out of.

---

The man of about twenty-seven came past at about the ninth hour with his bucket in one hand and his rag over the other shoulder and no intention of stopping, and he stopped.

"Saturday is my morning."

"**I know it is your morning. You have been in this market on a Saturday morning since before the water and I have had this stall on this spot since before the water, and there has not been one Saturday morning in this flood when either of us was here and the other one was not.**"

---

"You are stood there with a board up and nothing on it."

"**There is string on it and I have not had a customer since the water came, and you have a bucket and you are not going to put it down, and neither of us is going to stand here and say what that is, and you are welcome to go on past.**"

The man of about twenty-seven went on past. He did not say anything to anybody about that afterwards, and nobody in that market thanked either of them for it, and about four people who had come through it have worked out since that two men who have had the same ten feet of that market for the whole of this flood had stopped on a Sunday morning for the length of about a sentence and had neither of them put a bucket down or picked up a piece of string, and that this is the whole of what either of them has got, and that it is not nothing, and that neither of them would want it called that.

---

The market went on. The stall board stood on its string with the ordinary line on it in chalk that nobody read, and the barrow stood at the end of the stall with nothing in it, and the flagstones got the light off the water and gave it back, and about nine people went up nine steps above that market that morning and got down again by the fourth hour.

And not one hand in that market was sent for, and there is nothing in four hundred miles with a place in it for a person to complain on about a room over a market deciding something on a Saturday, and the woman who kept that register was nine steps above thirty people who had bought cord off a man that week and had not been told one thing about a page, and none of them is going to be told, and there is no paper coming.
