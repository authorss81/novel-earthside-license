# Chapter 0413 — The Page Came Out Of A Fold On The Third Of The Four Places

A room over a market had the ninety-eighth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door from about the sixth hour, a Monday, and the shutters at the market end of it stood open and the noise of the market came up through the floor the way it comes up through a floor on a morning when there is nothing under it to be doing, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet of paper lying open on a table, a lump of chalk on the edge of that table, a piece of chalk in a man's own inside breast pocket, and two creases pressed into that sheet with a bone.

---

The sheet was not folded. It had been taken out of a satchel and put on a table and it was lying open, and the four places ruled under its heading were all there to be read, and the crease pressed down the third of the four had broken the surface of the paper the way a crease does when it has been pressed hard enough and often enough to be a mark rather than a bend.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had it flat in front of her and her hands either side of it, and she had been in a great many rooms in this flood in which a page arrived and somebody opened it, and about four people in that room have worked out since that she had never once in her life had a page arrive with a line already on it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour and sat down at the back and put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket, and he looked at the page for a while before he said anything at all, and he had been in that room every day that week and had not been asked for the chalk in his pocket once.

---

"**That page was folded twice.**"

"It is lying open on the table."

"**I am not saying it is folded now, I am saying it was folded twice, and I have had it in front of me since Sunday without opening or touching it, and I am not asking for anything, and one of the two folds is the one down the third of the four places and the other one is along the top, which means somebody nine hundred miles off put that sheet together so that the third of the four places fell on a fold.**"

Nobody answered him. The register-keeper had her right hand flat on the table on the side of the paper away from the crease and she did not put it on the crease, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has been reading forms out loud for nine years looked at a crease the way a person looks at a thing they are not going to touch, and did not put a finger on it.

"A crease is a crease. It is not a line. It goes when a page is opened."

"**It is not a fold, because the page is open, and it is not going to go, and a crease pressed with a bone hard enough to break the surface of a paper is a line that somebody drew without a pen on it, and it is the only line on that page and it is not in the second of the four places.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his other hand on the back of his own chair and left it there, and the piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket did not move, and nobody in that room thanked him for any part of it and nobody argued with him either, and a man who has asked in advance, in a lane, not to be thanked is not thanked.

---

Nobody in that room worked out how a line gets onto a page in four hundred miles, and about four people in it have worked out that nobody in it is going to be worked out by anybody this week either, and that a page which arrives with one mark on it that nobody meant as one is a page a room can no longer be sure it is the first thing that has happened to it that week.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said one more thing and it was the whole of what he had come up the nine steps for.

"**If that crease is the third of the four places then whoever folded that page has already told a clerk nine hundred miles off where the third of the four places is, and it is not a name and it is not a person and it is the inside of a fold, and I am not going to draw the second one and nobody in this room should let me.**"

The register-keeper put the lump of chalk down a half inch further from the edge of the table than it had been, and nobody in that room saw her do it, and a woman who has kept the third column of a book empty on purpose for fifty days knows exactly what a column is and is not going to be the one who marks one.

The sheet lay open on the table with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the third of them and nothing in any of the four, and nobody in that room opened it, folded it, wrote on it or turned it over, and the day on the door was the ninety-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour with a thing they had heard worked out and nothing on any page in this city to put it in.
