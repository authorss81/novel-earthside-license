# Chapter 0403 — A Man In A Very Good Coat, And The Same Office

A room over a market had the eighty-eighth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Friday, and a sheet was on a table in it with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading, and the light came in over the step at the back and lay along a bench nobody was sitting on, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a document inside a coat, a book with three columns open in it, a length of new rope over the back of a chair with a man in it who nobody was going to ask a question, and about four people who had come up nine steps before anybody had written anything on a door for them to come to.

---

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat came up the nine steps at about the seventh hour and put his hat on the bench and did not take his coat off, and the coat is the reason there is a document in this room at all, because a page that arrives in this city from nine hundred miles off arrives inside a coat or it arrives in a satchel, and this office has used both.

He said it out loud when he came in, the way he said it out loud in this room on a day in the last month, and nobody in that room had arranged for him to have the local language since and nobody was going to, and the sentence he said was short, and he has said it once in this city, and he is not going to say it a second time in a room with a date on the door.

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair and did not look up when a man in a very good coat came into a room he was in, and about four people in this city have worked out since that a man nobody asks anything learns early not to look up at anybody who walks in.

---

"You have read that page."

"**I have read the four places under the heading and I have not read anything else and nobody is going to ask me what is on it, and nobody is going to ask you either, and I have been in a room in this city before where a column was read out and a man was asked a question about it in the same hour.**"

Nobody in that room asked him anything. The sheet lay on the table with a crease pressed down its third column and nothing at all in it, and the woman who keeps a public register had the book open in front of her and did not turn a page of it, and about four people in that room had worked out since that a page that has a place for a person in it is the first one anybody in this city has been handed, and that the person who hands it over is the person who cannot read it, and that these two facts are the reason the room is quiet.

The man in the very good coat did not look at the sheet again. He put two fingers inside the front of the coat and took out a document that was folded in four and had been folded in four a long way from here, and he put it on his own knee and left his hand flat on it.

---

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city are the only flat thing for about four streets, and the light comes up off the river on them in one long sheet and does not move all morning, and there is no hill within about four miles of that wharf, so a man standing on them can see anybody coming along them for a long way before that person can see him.

The man of about thirty-eight was working them. He had a tally-board under his arm and a knife in his hand and a stack at the top end of it that is higher than a man, and a strip of clear board about as long as a cart lying at the bottom of that stack, and the strip is a piece of board and not a page and it is not on anybody's list of anything.

The man in the very good coat came down onto the boards at about the eighth hour with his document in his hand and stood at the bottom of them and did not go up, and the man working them did not look up, and a man who works boards at the bottom of this city and does not look up has decided that there is nothing in this morning that is going to be asked of him, and there is not.

---

"This is the round that came your page up."

"**I have worked these boards since the winter before the water and I have never once asked anybody what was in a satchel, and there is a man standing at the bottom of them with a document in his hand this morning, and I am not going to start.**"

"It goes out four times in a year and it is in a satchel for about twenty-two days on its way up, and there is no office in it that will read your page before it goes and then tell you what it read."

"**Twenty-two days is how long a thing is on a road. I have had boards off a barrow that were on it longer than that and they came out the same as they went in.**"

The wind came up off the river on the boards and went away again and came back, which is what the wind does there, and neither of the two men on those boards changed what he was doing because of it, and about four people who have been in a room with a column in it have worked out since that two men who have both said a true thing out loud in this city can stand eleven feet apart and neither of them has to hear the other.

"Your page is folded twice with a bone before it gets here. It comes up a road and it has been folded with a bone and the creases are pressed hard enough to break the surface of the paper."

"**The bone is a bone. I have cut a fence out of willow with a knife and it did not make the fence any straighter than it was going to be anyway.**"

"You have two of them on the same round."

The man of about thirty-eight turned a board over at that point and did not look up, and the strip of clear board at the bottom of the stack did not move and is a piece of board and not a page and is on nobody's list of anything.

"**I have put a figure in a column four times a year since the winter before the water, and the figure I send is not the figure I have, and neither of those two numbers is on a page in this city, and that is the whole of what I have got to say to anybody this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-eight worked the boards for about two hours with the man in the very good coat standing at the bottom of them the whole time, and about four people who came down onto those boards that morning have worked out since that the two of them are the only two people in this city who have been in a room with a document from that office, and that neither of them has ever been asked what a column is for, and that they have been standing eleven feet apart for two hours without one word of it being aimed at the other one.

A man who fills a column in four times a year out of what he is given does not turn round at a man who brings the round. A man who brings the round does not describe the room where anything got decided, because he has not been in it, and there is no version of this flood in which a man in a very good coat nine hundred miles off has been in a room where anything got decided, and he was not going to start now by putting it into a conversation on a wharf in the middle of a morning.

---

The man in the very good coat took his hand off the document on his knee and opened it and read it, and it took him about as long as it takes to count nine, and then he shut it and put it back inside the front of the coat and buttoned the coat, and he did not tell anybody what was in it and nobody asked him, and the man working the boards did not look up when it happened.

Then he went back up to the room over the market, because that is the room the round comes into, and the sheet was still on the table with a crease down its third column and nothing in it, and the man of about fifty-seven was still at the end of the bench with the rope over the back of his chair.

"You will be in the room."

"**I will be at the near end of the bench, and there will be about nine people in it and every one of them will have come up those nine steps before, and I do not have the local language and I said so out loud in that room once already, and it will be read out in the local language and I will not understand one word of it.**"

The sheet was going to be read out on a Sunday, in a room that had not been warned, by a woman who had said out loud that she would stop at each of the four places long enough for the people at the back to hear it, and the wind came up off the river on the boards and went away again and the strip of clear board lay at the bottom of the stack and was not a page and was on nobody's list.
