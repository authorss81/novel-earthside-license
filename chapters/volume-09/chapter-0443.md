# Chapter 0443 — A Second Line In A Book That Has Kept Its Third Column Empty

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came up the nine steps on the hundred and twenty-eighth day of the Bare Month with both hands under the front of her coat. She took a book off a shelf and put it down on the table in front of her and opened it. A sheet of paper with a heading on it was lying on the same table and was not looked at by anybody in that room that morning, and the day was in chalk on the door six feet behind her.

---

She had the pen out and the cap in her left hand, which is the way she keeps it and has kept it for nine years. She turned back to a ruled line and then went back one more, because a register that has had a line ruled and nothing in it is not the same thing as a register that has had a line ruled and something in it. She said that out loud, standing up in the middle of the room, before she touched the page.

"**A book with a day in it and nothing under the day is the only thing in four hundred miles that a man four hundred miles off cannot ask this city to produce, and that is the whole of what it is for.**"

A man at the far end of the bench said the practical half of that out loud, because somebody always does.

"Then what goes in the second column."

"**Nothing, and no name in the third one either, and I am going to say both of those out loud on a Wednesday morning before the pen goes near the page, and I would like nobody in this room to answer either of them.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her, and nobody told her she was right, and nobody told her she was wrong. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of the bench with his own hand not on his pocket for once. The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair that nobody said one word about.

A second slate lay on the shelf behind that table with a day on it and nothing under the day. No hand in that room went near it. It did not get turned over, and it did not get written on.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a book for nine years said out loud what a book is for before she opened it, and that nobody in that room had been in one when it was said.

A woman near the step put the other half of it out loud. It was the far end of the room talking, and it has been noticed in this flood that it is always the practical half that gets said.

"Then a man four hundred miles off reads that line and sees a day and nothing under it, and he has to write a return about it, and what does he write."

---

She ruled the line herself with the back of the pen, and it is not a long line. Then she wrote the day in the first column and read it back before she wrote anything else, which is a thing she does, and then she put the cap back on the pen.

"**This morning is the hundred and twenty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and the day in that line is the hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and that is five days counted back from this morning and not counted forward, and the day in that line is the morning that page went out of this city, and there is nothing under it.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was quiet at the back of that bench for about as long as it takes to fill a pail. Then the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the plain question, and then the man at the back of the bench said the one thing he said that morning. He said it from where he was sitting and not to anybody in the room.

"Then somebody is going to hand it in and somebody is going to want to know what was under the day."

"**There is going to be nothing under the day, and there is nobody in this city that can fill it in, and I am not going to ask you anything about it, and I would like it said that I noticed that nobody in this room is going to ask you about it either.**"

Nobody in that room said thank you to him for that. He has asked in advance, in a lane, a long way back, not to be thanked for anything he says, and he was not thanked. The pen was on the table with the cap back on it, and the column under that day was empty, and it stayed empty.

---

It is the third time anything has gone into that book since the water came. Two of the three have a day in the first column and nothing under the day. The third column of that book has been empty on purpose the whole of the time and is empty on purpose now.

Nobody in that room has asked the woman who keeps it why, and the reason is not that she would not say.

The book went shut on its shelf, and the shelf was behind the table where it has been since before the water. The day on the door was the hundred and twenty-eighth day of the Bare Month, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and nothing in that room said anything about the day itself.
