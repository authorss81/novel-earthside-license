# Chapter 0442 — It Is Going Again By The Time He Gets To The End Of It

A shut road out of this city runs under a fence along the top of it. The ground on the low side of that fence has been soft since before the water and is soft now. On the hundred and twenty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, a handcart stood up above in the wet with a hazel mallet in it whose head is bound in cord and a coil of rope on top of the coil. There was a bundle of wedges out of the cart and a length of board, and one pair of hands on about nine yards of cart-track that had gone soft in the night and taken its crust off.

---

He packed it with the back of the mallet, one board at a time, the way he had packed the last stretch. The light came up out of the low ground behind the trees and stopped at that fence and did not come over. The wind came up the road in the same pushes it has been coming up in since before the water and has never once been written down anywhere.

He was not doing anything to the fence. Nobody in four hundred miles has ever sent him up that road and nobody is going to. What he was doing to the cart-track was not for anybody.

---

About halfway along the nine yards the rain came across the low ground sideways. It did not stop for the length of a board being packed. Where he had already been, the crust came off it again behind him, in the same order he had done it in, so that by the time he was at the far end of the nine yards the first of them was going again and he could hear it.

He did not stop. He put the mallet down in the cart and turned round and looked at the stretch he had done, and he did not go back to it. Then he picked the mallet up again and carried on at the far end where the ground was firmer. That is what a man does when the rain has come. It is not a repair, and it does not get told about.

About four people on that road have worked out since that a man who packs a soft track with the back of a mallet, and then does not go back to the part he has already done, has decided something about his own work and is not going to say what.

---

The man of about fifty-seven came up that road at about the eleventh hour with his barrow. He pulled it up alongside the cart and set the handles down, which is what a man does at a cart. The barrow is empty and it is going to stay empty.

There were wedges in that cart and a length of board, and the man with the barrow put his hands in his sleeves instead of taking either of them. He said his one thing out loud in the rain on a shut road, to the barrow and not to the man.

"That is the third time you have done those nine yards."

The man of about sixty did not turn round and did not stop packing. The wedge went under the board and the mallet came down on the back of it, and he answered it without stopping.

"**It is, and the other two were not in rain like this, and I am not going to argue with you about it in the wet, and the arrangement between the two of us is the same as it has been every time the two of us have been on this road together.**"

Nobody said anything back to him. The man of about sixty worked a wedge in and a wedge out, and neither of them looked at the other. After a while the man with the barrow shouldered it again and took it back down the way he had brought it up. He did not break stride at the fence, and he did not put his head on one side to look at it.

---

The fence along the top of that road is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord, and nobody has ever measured anything on that road. The light stopped at that fence and did not come over it. The man of about sixty finished about nine yards of track, left the mallet in the cart and left the cart where it was, and the first third of those nine yards was soft again by the time he had finished the last of it.

That is the whole of what moved on that road today. The road itself goes on under the fence into the low ground, and a lane of nine houses stands at the foot of it out of sight. None of the nine is in this city.
