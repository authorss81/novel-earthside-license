# Chapter 0446 — He Stops At That Stall For The Second Time In This Flood

A market at the bottom of this city on the hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, had the light coming off the water along the flags by the seventh hour and lying there. A stall board was up on its string with the ordinary line on it in chalk, and a length of good cord coiled beside it. A barrow stood at the end of that stall with nothing in it, and has had nothing in it since the water came. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was behind it with his hands in his sleeves, because the cold was still in it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came through that market at about the ninth hour the way he comes through it every morning, and he stopped at the end of that stall. About four people who came through that market before the tenth hour have worked out since that a man who has not stopped at the end of that stall in this flood stopped at the end of that stall on a Saturday morning and stood there.

He put his own board down on the flags. He did not have any question for anybody. He had stopped at that stall once in this flood, on a Wednesday, and he had not been back to it since, and nobody in that market had asked him about either of those two things.

---

"Do you want to know why the cord is not cheaper."

"**I have never once been given a reason for a price on that board, and I have not asked you for one in this flood and I am not asking now, and I have come to that stall twice and I can stand at it without saying anything, and a man who has stood at a thing twice is a man who is not going to be able to stop a third time.**"

Nobody in that market heard either of them say a word about a page, and nobody in that market thanked either of them. The cord went over the counter and the money went into the man's own hand, and there was no measure said out loud in either direction, which is how that stall has always done it.

---

A woman buying a length of that cord on the same morning asked the man of about thirty-nine who he was, and he told her. She asked whether anybody four hundred miles inland was selling cord at that price too, and he said that he did not know, and that the last time anybody in that market had gone that far to look at anybody else was before the water, and that they had not come back with anything anybody here could use.

Four hundred miles is how far that page came from. It is a place a page came from, and not a place anybody in that market has ever gone to. Nobody in that market said a word about the three who went the other way on that road with a barrow each, and nobody knows where they are, and nobody in that market is going to be told.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board picked his own board up off the flags and went on through the market, and he did not stop again. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put the cord away, and the barrow at the end of that stall stayed empty. The day was the hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month. Nobody in that market had anything to say about it.
