# Chapter 0448 — The High Side Of A Shut Road, And A Barrow That Does Not Stop

The hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month was a Monday, and the wind came up a shut road out of this city in the same pushes it has been coming up in since before the water and has never once been written down. A handcart stood on the high side of that road above the low ground, with a hazel mallet in it whose head is bound in cord and a coil of rope on top of the coil. The high side of that road was the one part of it that had not gone soft again. There was one pair of hands on about nine yards of cart-track, and no other.

---

A man with a barrow came up that road at the seventh hour with a hurdle of hazel rods in the bottom of it that he made, and a bar across the top of it that he replaced. There was nothing else in the barrow, and there is not going to be. He went up past the handcart and past the fence along the top of that road and did not stop at either of them. Nobody on that road spoke to him, nobody has ever spoken to him on it, and he has not asked anybody to.

---

The man of about sixty packed the high side of the track with the back of the mallet, one board at a time, and the light came up out of the low ground behind the trees and stopped at that fence and did not come over.

The man of about fifty-nine came back down the road at about the ninth hour with the same barrow and the same hurdle in it and the same bar across the top of it. He stopped on the low side of the fence for about as long as it takes to turn a bar over, picked the barrow up again, and went on down. Nobody on that road asked him why he had stopped, and nobody on that road has ever asked him why he stops there.

The man with the barrow had gone down past the cart before anybody said anything, and the man of about sixty said it to his back and not to the top of the road.

"You are not going to carry a wedge down there and I am not going to hand you one, and the two of us have had that morning and neither of us is going to be the one who says so out loud, and I would rather the ground took it than have either of us standing in it saying it."

---

The man of about sixty rested the head of the mallet on the top of a board and looked down the road after the barrow for about as long as it takes to count nine, and then he said the other thing.

"**He is not the first one to have gone up that road today and not the first one not to come back down it by the middle of the day, and I do not know what any of them went up for and I am not going to put a second bar in that barrow to find out, and the high side will hold and that is the whole of what I have got out of it.**"

---

Nothing on that road was measured, and nothing on it was looked at twice. The withies on that fence are bound with the same cord the posts were set with. A man who puts a barrow down on the low side of a fence and picks it up again has been doing it on the same stretch of road for long enough that it is not a habit, and it is a stop.

The man of about sixty finished about nine yards of track, took the mallet out of the wind, put it back in the cart and left the cart where it was. The high side of that shut road held what he had done to it, and the light stopped at that fence and did not come over it.

Past the fence that road keeps on down through the trees into the low ground, and at the bottom of it there are nine houses in a lane. None of those nine houses is in this city.
