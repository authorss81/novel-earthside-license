# Chapter 0421 — There Is No Way In This Flood To Ask Anybody Anything First

A room over a market had the hundred and sixth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Tuesday, and the light came in over the step at the back and stopped about two feet short of the middle of the table. There was a sheet of paper lying flat on that table with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the length of the third of them, a length of new rope over the back of a chair with a man in it, and a lump of chalk on the edge of the table that had been on the edge of the table for a week.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the nine steps with his own board under his arm and put his back against the wall at the end of the bench nearest the step. He had been in that room on most of the mornings since the page came up the road and had said nothing at the back of any of them.

He said it at about the eighth hour, and he said it to the middle of the room, and he said it to nobody in particular.

"**We have had that page on that table since the morning it came up the road, and four of us have been in this room every working morning since, and not one of us has gone down those nine steps and put one question to one man.**"

Nobody thanked him. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her hand flat on the table on the side of the paper that is away from the crease and she did not move it. A man who has said a true thing out loud in a room in this city is not thanked for it and is not argued with, and this one was neither.

"You are going to want the rest of that said out loud."

"**There is no rest of it. There is the reason. A sheet goes out of this city four times a year and comes back with things in it that were not written on it, and that has happened twice in a month, and I have never had a page for asking, and there is no form in four hundred miles that puts a question to a man.**"

---

Nobody in that room thanked him for that either. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket, and the piece of chalk in that pocket did not move, and he had asked in advance, in a lane, not to be thanked, and four of the people in that room had heard him say it more than once.

He said one thing without turning round.

"You are being kind about him."

"**I am not being kind about him. He is not being spared. He is not being reached, and those are two different things, and about four of us have been calling it the first one since Sunday.**"

The man of about thirty-nine did not look round at him and neither did anybody else. The room had a man at the back of it who had never been asked anything and a man at the end of the same bench who had never been asked anything, and nobody had ever noticed that those were the same arrangement until the morning somebody said both halves of it out loud.

The man of about fifty-seven at the far end of that bench did not look up, and nobody in that room looked at him while the two of them were saying it, and the rope stayed over the back of the chair it was on and it was not used.

---

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up and put her finger on the paper beside the third of the four places and not in it, and about four people in that room have watched a woman keep a column empty on purpose for a month and have not once seen her put a finger on somebody else's one.

"**Then say the rest of it. The third of these four places is a person, and a person is the one thing a sheet of paper can ask for, and this room has not asked one person for anything with it.**"

Nobody in that room answered her. A question put to a room in daylight on a Tuesday does not get an answer on the Tuesday, and this one had been in the room since a Sunday and had not got one, and the woman who reads forms out loud for a living had known what the third of the four places was for from the morning she read it and had said nothing about it to anybody, including the man at the back of the bench.

"The page does not ask you."

"**The page does not ask anybody. I have looked at it every morning for a fortnight, and if it had asked me one I would not have answered it.**"

---

She took her finger off the paper and put her hand flat on the table on the side of the crease. The lump of chalk was still on the edge of the table where it had been since the morning the page came up the road, and nobody in that room had picked it up on any morning since, and picking it up is not a thing anybody in that room is going to be the first one to do.

Then she said the thing that had been sitting under all of the rest of it, and she said it to the room.

"**I have told this room every morning since the Sunday it was read out that I am not the one who is going to decide, and I said it first so that nobody could say afterwards I had been put in a chair.**"

Nobody said anything to that.

"**I have said it so many mornings that it has gone flat. I am going to stop saying it on Saturday morning, and on the Saturday I am going to say the other thing instead.**"

About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who puts a day on a thing out loud in a room with a date on the door has done the same thing a page does, and that nobody had asked her to, and that a person who says a day out loud in this city is not thanked for it either.

---

The sheet lay flat on the table with the four places under its heading all empty, and the crease pressed down the length of the third of them is still the only thing on that paper anybody in this city has ever touched. The length of new rope was over the back of the chair at the end of the bench and it has still never been used, and the man at the end of that bench went down the nine steps at the fourth hour with his hands in his sleeves and nobody in that room said one word to him on the way past, and a door at the end of a lane in this city was not knocked on that day and is not going to be.

Nobody wrote anything on anything that morning. Nothing in that room had changed except that two men had said out loud, on the same morning, in front of each other, that the reason nobody in this city asks anybody anything is that there is no instrument for it, and about four of the nine who were in that room have worked out since that that is a true thing and that it is not going to make anybody go and ask anybody, because a true thing about why you have not done a thing is not a thing that does it.
