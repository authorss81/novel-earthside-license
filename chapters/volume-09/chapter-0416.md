# Chapter 0416 — Two Of The Four Places Can Be Answered By Anybody And One Of Them Cannot

A room over a market had the hundred and first day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Thursday, and the cold had got into the boards of the stair overnight and the stair was colder than the room, and what was under about four pairs of hands that morning was a sheet of paper lying open on a table with four places ruled under a heading on it, a length of new rope over the back of a chair with a man in it, a book shut on a shelf, and a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket that nobody in that room had asked for.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the sheet flat in front of her and had not touched it, and she had been in that room every morning for about a week with a page on a table that has a place for a person in it and has not been told by anybody what a person on a page is for, and on the hundred and first day of the Bare Month she worked out the arithmetic of it out loud in front of about nine people and named no day.

"**Of the four places, the first is a place and anybody in this room can write it. The fourth is a day and anybody in this room can write that too.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and did not take the chalk out of it, and he had been asked for that piece of chalk in about a dozen rooms that week and had not been asked for it once.

"The second one is a line."

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went on standing, and the man at the back of the room had not been asked to say anything by anybody and had said it, and a room in which a man nobody is going to ask anything in finishes the sentence of somebody who is in the middle of one, and always has.

"**A line is a thing a person can put down whether or not anybody is standing on either side of it, and that makes three of the four, and every one of the three can be answered by the office or by this room or by a man with a pen, and I am not saying what any of them is for, and I am saying that it is a count and I have made it.**"

Nobody answered her. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has said for a week that she is not the one who is going to decide has spent a morning counting the four places out loud instead, and that counting is the one thing a person can do in a room where deciding is not hers to do, and that the two of those are not the same and the difference between them is the whole of what a week of this has been.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and he answered it from where he sat without turning round.

"**You have made it sound like three easy and one hard, and you have told this room which three, and you have not said one word about what any of them is for, and I have a piece of chalk in this pocket that has not been out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood, and I am not going to be the one that puts a mark on any of them.**"

---

The man of about fifty-seven at the end of the bench had the length of new rope over the back of his chair and had been in that room for about two hours by then and had not been asked anything, and about four people in that room have worked out since that he was in it on the day a woman said one of the four lines was going to be hers and was in it on the day a man worked out that a page came out of a fold, and that nobody has put either of those two things in front of him and that this is the only arrangement anybody in this room has for him and it is not going to be changed this week.

Nobody in that room told the register-keeper she was right about her count and nobody told her she was wrong about it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was not thanked for anything he said and has not been thanked in any of the days he has been in that room, and a man who says in a lane that he is not to be thanked is not thanked.

"**Then it is three that this room can do and one that it cannot, and I have been in this room a week saying I would not say what it was for, and the reason I would not say it is that the one of the four that this room cannot do is the one that the fold is pressed down.**"

She put her finger on the paper beside the third of the four places and not in it, and the crease pressed down the length of that place with a bone had broken the surface of the paper and had been there since before it came up the road, and a woman who has been careful for fifty days about a column of her own has just put her finger beside a column on somebody else's page without touching it, and that is not the same decision twice.

---

The fence along the top of that shut road out of this city is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound along it with the same cord, and it was finished before this week and nothing has been added to it and nothing has been taken off it, and the length of new rope over the back of the chair at the end of that bench has still never been used and was not used on that Thursday and is not going to be used, and about four people who have been in that room have worked out since that there is a man in this city standing in front of a thing he cannot move and has not tried to move, and that nobody in that room has ever asked him where it is and nobody is going to.

Nobody in that room wrote anything on anything that morning. The four places on the sheet stayed as empty as the four of them had been when the page came up the road, and the crease stayed where it was, and the chalk stayed in a pocket, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour with a count of three and one in their heads and no form in four hundred miles on which to enter it.
