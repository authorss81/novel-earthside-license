# Chapter 0437 — Who Is Going To Take It Down To The Road

The hundred and twenty-second day of the Bare Month was a Thursday and the chalk on the door of a room over a market was put there before the light, and by the ninth hour about nine people were in that room and had been in it on most of the mornings since a page came up the road. There was a sheet of paper lying flat on that table, heading at the top, the four places ruled under it, four marks down the second of those four one above another, a bone crease the whole length of the third of them, and nothing at all in the first of the four or the third of them or the fourth. A stall board was against the inside of the wall by the door where it had been since the Monday before.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had been in that room since the seventh hour with his own board under his arm and at about the tenth hour he asked the question, and he asked it plainly, and he asked it in the local language, and nobody in that room had asked it before, and it was a fair question.

"Who is going to take it out of this room."

Nobody in that room had said anything about the day it goes out. It goes on the hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month, which is the next morning, and about four people in that room had known that for a fortnight and about five of them had not, and the man who asked it was the only man in that room who had ever put a question in it that was about getting a thing done rather than about the thing.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the answer standing up, and she said it the way she says things in that room, which is once.

"**I am. He carries things for a living and he carries this in a satchel and he does not open what he carries, and he will take it down to the end of the market and put it on a cart that goes out on the Friday round, and I will be the one who put it in his hand.**"

Nobody in that room said thank you, and nobody in that room took her side or took the other one, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the woman who left the third of the four places empty on the Saturday before last is going to be the one person in this city that a page like that can be said to have come from, and that there is nothing on the paper to say afterwards whose hand any of the four marks on it belongs to, and that she is the one who is going to hand it over knowing that.

"You understand that is not the same as sending it."

"**It is not the same as sending it and it is going to be the same as sending it by the time it has been on a cart for a week, and I have thought about that on every working morning since the Saturday and I have not found the thing to do about it, and I am not going to find it in front of nine people on a Thursday.**"

---

A woman near the step said the last thing anybody said in that room that morning, and she said it to the middle of the room and not to the woman who keeps the register.

"Then somebody in this room should be the one who hands it over, and it should be somebody nobody is going to ask about afterwards."

"I am not going to be handed to anybody, and I am not going to hand this to anybody either, and those are one decision and not two, and I made it out loud on the Saturday and nobody in that room has asked me about it since."

Nobody in that room thanked her for that either. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back with his hand not on his pocket for once, and the piece of chalk in that pocket did not move, and he said one thing and it was the last thing said in that room on that Thursday.

"**Then I will go down those nine steps first and open my own board up, because a man who has a stall at the market end is going to be standing there on Friday when that satchel goes past, and I am not going to be asked anything about it, and I am not going to say one word about it, and that is the last thing I have.**"

He has asked in advance, in a lane, a long way back, that he is not to be thanked, and nobody in that room thanked him, and the chalk had not left that pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and the four marks on that page were still the only writing on it and the third of the four places was still empty on purpose, and the day on the door was the hundred and twenty-second day of the Bare Month, and the page went out of that room in the morning of the next day.
