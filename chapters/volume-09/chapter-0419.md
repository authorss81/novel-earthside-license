# Chapter 0419 — The Page Came Up The Road And Has Been On A Table Every Morning Since

A room over a market had the hundred and fourth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Sunday, and the shutters at the window end were shut against the wind and the noise of the market came up through the floor in a slow pulse all morning, and what was under about four pairs of hands that day was a sheet of paper lying open on a table, a document inside the front of a very good coat, a piece of chalk in an inside breast pocket, and a hat on a bench that nobody had picked up.

---

The sheet has been on that table since the morning it came up the road in a satchel from a building nine hundred miles off, and about nine people have been in that room with it on most of the mornings since, and the woman who keeps the book has read the four places under the heading out loud once, in the local language, in daylight, standing up, stopping at each of them long enough for the people at the back to hear it.

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat was in the room again on the hundred and fourth day of the Bare Month, at the near end of the bench, with a document folded in four on his knee and his hat beside him, and he has been in that room on most of the mornings since the sheet came up the road, and he has not read one word of the page on the table.

He has no local language. He said so out loud in that room once, and about four people in that room have worked out since Sunday morning that the reason is not that he is quiet and the reason is not that he has not been looking, and that the two of them have been at the same table for a week and the four of them have been talking about a column in front of a man who cannot follow one word of it, and that none of the four worked it out before the morning they all worked it out at once.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it, and he said it at the back of the room without turning round, and nobody in that room had asked him to.

"**You have been sitting ten feet from that page every morning this week and you have not read one word of it, and four of us have been talking about the third of the four places in front of you.**"

"We worked it out about an hour ago and none of us said anything for about forty minutes."

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said that to the table and not to anybody in it, and a woman who has kept a column empty on purpose for fifty days was the first one in that room to admit that the room had been wrong, and nobody in that room thanked her for it either, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not answer her, and said the next thing himself, at the back of the room, and not to her.

"**We all worked it out at the same moment and nobody in this room is going to arrange for you to have the language, and nobody is going to say it to you, and that is what this room is and I am the one who has said it out loud.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him. Nobody in that room argued with him either, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her hand flat on the table on the side of the paper that is away from the crease and did not look up, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who has kept a column empty on purpose for fifty days knows exactly what it costs to have discovered a thing about a page a week too late, and that she is not going to say so in front of a man who cannot follow it.

The man in the very good coat looked at the man at the back of the room for about as long as it takes to count nine and then he said one thing in his own language, and it was short, and nobody in that room asked him to say it again and nobody in that room asked anybody else what it was.

---

He read the document on his knee for the best part of an hour. He did not look at the sheet on the table once in that hour, and he did not describe the room to anybody, and he did not tell anybody where he had been before he came up the nine steps, and he has not been in a room in this city in which anything got decided and he is not going to be, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man who cannot read the page is the only man in it who has not been in a room where anything got decided, and that the two of those facts are the same fact and that it took a week for anybody in that room to notice it.

Nobody in that room did anything about it. Nobody went for anybody who had the language and nobody offered to read the four places out loud again in a second language for the sake of one man at the end of a bench, and a room which has worked out that it has been talking in front of a man for a week does not get to fix it by talking in front of him again.

The sheet went down onto the table flat with a crease pressed down the third of the four places and nothing in any of the four of them, and it is going to be answered by about nine people who can all read it, and the man it came from sat at the near end of the bench and did not read a word of the answer.
