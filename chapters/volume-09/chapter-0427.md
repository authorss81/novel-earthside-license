# Chapter 0427 — A Tally-Board On A Table Face Up, And Two Men On One Stair

A room over a market had the hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the eighth hour, a Monday, and by then the light had come round off the low ground and onto the middle of the table and was the only warm thing in the room. About four pairs of hands had the morning, and there was a tally-board and a knife with the edge gone blunt on it, and a sheet of paper lying flat with a heading on it and four places ruled under the heading and a crease pressed down the length of the third of them and nothing in any of the four. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had not come up those nine steps, and four people in that room had noticed that by the ninth hour and none of them said it out loud.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up the nine steps before the light was off the low ground, with the tally-board under his arm and the knife in his hand, because he had carried the knife up with him, and nobody in that room has ever worked out why, and he sat down at the end of the bench and put the tally-board on his knees with the face of it up.

He had not done that before. He turns it, in rooms, so that the face of it is away from whoever is in the room, and a man who turns a thing he keeps to the side in a room over a month is a man who does not want to be thanked for what is on it, and that on the hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month he put it down on a table in a room with a date on the door with the face of it up, and he did not read from it, and nobody in that room asked him what was on it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room that morning with his own slate flat on his knee under his right hand, and he looked at the face of the tally-board for a while and then said one thing about it, and he said it to the middle of the room.

"You have never turned it face up in here before."

"That is the first time in this flood you have put one of those down with the numbers showing in a room full of people who cannot read them."

"**I know it is. I have worked out this morning why I turn it over, and it is not modesty, it is that a figure I have said out loud is a figure somebody is going to ask me where I got, and the one I send is not the one I have, and there is no page in this city that has both of them on it, and I have stopped turning it over.**"

---

Nobody in that room asked him for the second figure and nobody in that room asked him where the first one came from, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who has been putting one number into a column four times a year for nine years without anybody ever asking him what the column was for has worked out by himself in one morning that the safest thing he can do with a page is stop managing what it shows, and that this is the whole of what has happened in this room today and it is not a decision anybody took.

Then he put his hand flat on the face of the tally-board and left it there, and the knife stayed on the table beside it where he had put it down, and a man who puts a hand flat on a page and says nothing about it is doing what a woman who keeps a column empty on purpose is doing, and that neither of them has ever explained it to the other and neither of them is going to.

---

He went down the nine steps at about the third hour of the afternoon with the tally-board under his arm and the knife in his hand, and the stair was colder than the room had been, and the knife was still blunt, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was coming up those same steps at the same time with his hands in his pockets.

They passed each other on the stair and neither of them said one word, and about four people in this city have worked out since that two men who have both been in a great many rooms in this flood and have never once been asked anything by anybody met on nine steps on a Monday afternoon and said nothing, and that one of them was going up to a room where a third of four places was going to be left empty and one of them was going down off the boards at the bottom of this city, and that the stair is the only place in this flood beyond the reach of a room, and that neither of them has ever said so and neither of them was going to.

The sheet lay flat on the table where it had lain since the morning it came up the road, and nobody in that room wrote anything on it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of it by the fourth hour with his hand on his own inside breast pocket and nobody asked him where he had been.
