# Chapter 0406 — A Room That Has Just Heard A Person And Has Not Answered

A board in a market had nineteen rates on it and the light was off the low ground early, and the boards under a man's feet were wet at the bottom end where the water comes up through the seams, and what was under about four pairs of hands on the ninety-first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, was a board, a slate of his own, and a sheet of paper with four places ruled under a heading and a crease pressed down the third of them.

---

He set his own board up at about the sixth hour and wrote nothing on it, because the rates on it are the rates he has written every morning for nine years and they are the same rates, and a man who writes nineteen rates on a board has to stand in front of it to sell anything at all, and standing in front of it is the whole of what he did for about an hour and a bit.

Then he took the slate off his hip and put it under his arm and went up the nine steps into the room over the market, and about four people in that room had worked out since Sunday that the third place on the sheet on that table is the first place anybody in four hundred miles has ever been handed where a person goes, and that the man who trades on the Open Hand board is the only person in this city who has ever done the other half of that by hand and has never said so to anybody.

He wanted two things and he only got half of one of them. He wanted to be somewhere else, and he was not. He wanted to stand where he could hear the third place read again, and it was not read again, and he stayed in the room for the whole of the morning, and a man who is right about a column in this city gets one turn at being right in and this was not going to be it, and he knew that on the stair.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book open on the table with three columns in it and the third one empty on purpose for fifty days, and she had the sheet in front of her and was not looking at it.

"I have not looked at it since I sat down."

"**Then do not look at it now and do not look at it this afternoon either, and I am not going to be one of the people who explains a page to a man who has his own hand over his own slate.**"

"That is a position."

"**It is not a position. You have been at the back of every room this city has had for about a fortnight and nobody has asked you one question in any of them, and I have not asked you one and I am not going to, and I would like it said out loud in this room that the woman who reads forms out loud is not going to start on a man holding a slate.**"

He had his right hand flat over the face of his own slate the whole time he was in that room, and his right hand did not move off it once, and he has not shown anybody what is on the face of it and was not going to this morning, and about four people in this city who have been in a room with that slate have worked out since that the man holding it is not protecting a figure.

---

Somebody said the thing about the office. It was somebody at the far end of the bench and it was not said to him and it was not said to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and it was not said to anybody.

"Two pages off the same round in a fortnight."

"**Two pages off the same round in a fortnight, and they went on different days, and they are not the same page, and the difference between the two of them is not the business of this room, and anybody in this room who wants to know the difference can go and stand in a corridor with a man in a very good coat.**"

A woman at the far end of the bench had her hands in her lap for the whole of the morning and nobody in that room said one word to her, and a household in this city was asked once for its days and said no out loud, in its own kitchen, and nobody has asked it since and nobody is going to, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the whole of what a sheet with a place for a person in it does is wait for somebody, and that this room is the part of it that is waiting.

---

Then he said it, and it was four words, and it was true, and he said it to the middle of the room the way he says everything, and he did not say it to the woman who read the sheet out on Sunday and he did not say it to the sheet.

"A page can be shown a person."

Nobody answered him. Nobody in that room told him he was right and nobody in that room told him he was wrong, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the man at the back with his hand on his slate has been at the back of every room in this city for about a fortnight saying things that are correct into a room that is not going to say so, and that this is the arrangement and it is not going to be thanked and it is not going to be argued with and it is the only arrangement on offer.

He put the slate back on his hip and went down the nine steps, and the wet was still coming up through the seams at the bottom end of his own board, and he stood in front of it and sold two things before the light came off the water, and he did not write anything new on it and he did not rub anything off it, and a board nobody has written on since the two hundred and twenty-fifth day of this flood is not his and is not in that market and is not his to sell off.

---

There is no form in four hundred miles for saying how a person answers a page that has a place for a person in it, and a page like that one cannot be answered by anybody at all except the person it is about, and the room over a market found that out in the middle of a morning with a book open on the table and a third column in it that has been empty on purpose for fifty days, and about four people in that room have worked out since that the only instrument this world has for doing anything about a page is a person saying a thing out loud in a room in daylight with a date on the door, and that is not an instrument anybody can fill a column in from.
