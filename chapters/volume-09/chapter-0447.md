# Chapter 0447 — What Is Done To A Page While It Is Waiting

The hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month was a Sunday, and it went into the chalk on the door of a room over a market at about the seventh hour. About nine people were in that room by the eighth, and all of them had been in it before. The sheet was lying flat on the table with a heading on it and four places ruled under that heading. The third of those four places was empty and has a crease pressed down the length of it. The first of them and the fourth of them were empty. The room had nothing to do that morning except wait.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the wall end of the bench with his own board under his arm. He said the thing that the morning was made of, and he said it to the middle of the table and not to anybody in it.

"**That page went out on a Friday and came back on a Sunday and it is lying on that table now, and there is a stretch of time between those two Sundays that nobody in this city knows the length of.**"

He put his own board down against the leg of the bench, which is not a thing he does, and went on with the rest of it.

"**And every day of that stretch this room is a room with a page on a table in it, and there is not one thing in this flood that a room can do with a page on a table except wait.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that, and nobody asked him what he wanted to be true instead. The register was on the shelf where it lives, and a second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date. About four people in that room have worked out since that the sheet has been on that table on every working morning since the Sunday it came back, and has not been folded once in any of them.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the half of it that is hers, and she said it standing, which is a thing she does.

"I am not going to fold it again until it is going out again, and it is not going to be folded by a person who has not been asked either, and I want that said in this room this morning because there is no way in the world of asking anybody to say it, and so somebody has to say it out loud on an ordinary Sunday to about nine people."

"**Then it does not get folded and I do not get asked, and those are the two things and I have said them and I am not going to say either of them again.**"

Nobody in that room thanked the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall for that. He put his hand out in a lane a long way back and said two words about not being thanked for anything he says, and he has not been thanked in this room for any of it since. The piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not move, and that chalk is not the chalk on any board in this city.

---

Adrian Vale was in that room from the seventh hour with his hand on the sheet. He read the first of the four places and the second of them and the fourth. He did not read the third of them. After a while he said one sentence, out loud, in the local language, to the page and not to anybody in the room.

"**There is nothing at the top of it.**"

A woman at the far end of the bench said those five words back to him as a question, and he did not answer it. Nobody took it up, and nobody asked him a second question. The page has a heading on it, and the heading is the only thing printed on it, and it is not the name of anybody in this city or of anybody in the office the page came from.

---

About nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour, and the page stayed on the table where it was and was not touched. Nothing was said about that day by anybody. The day on the door was the hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month.
