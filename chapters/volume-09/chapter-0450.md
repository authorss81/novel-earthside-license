# Chapter 0450 — Three Barrows On A Road Out Of This City

A room over a market had a date in chalk on its door on the hundred and thirty-fifth day of the Bare Month, and the date was above the table because the table is under the door, and about nine people were in that room by the fourth hour of the morning on a Wednesday and had all of them been in it before, and a sheet of paper with a heading on it was lying on that table with a crease pressed down the length of the third of its four places and the first of them and the third of them and the fourth of them all empty.

---

Nobody in that room was sent for and nobody in that room was waiting to be sent for, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of the bench with his own hand flat on his own inside breast pocket and the piece of chalk in it did not move, and the man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench with a length of new rope over the back of his chair, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his slate on his knee and said nothing that morning at all.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up at about the ninth hour and put her hand flat on the table beside the sheet and did not touch it, and said the one ordinary thing she said.

"**It is lying there and it is not going anywhere today, and the third of these four places is empty and it is empty on purpose, and nobody in this room is going to put anything in it, and I am not going to ask anybody to.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her and nobody in that room told her she was right and nobody in that room told her she was wrong, and the register was on its shelf behind the table where it has been since before the water with its third column empty, and a second slate was on that shelf with a date on it and nothing under the date and was not picked up and was not turned over and was not written on. About four people in that room have worked out since that a woman who keeps a public register put her hand down flat on the table beside a page and did not touch it, and that nobody in that room has seen her do that before.

---

The morning went on the way a morning goes on in that room, and the light came in over the step at the back and lay along the bench and stopped short of the middle of the table, and the four places under the heading stayed as they were, and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour and the sheet lay where it was and nobody said one word about the hundred and thirty-fifth day of the Bare Month or about what it is or what comes after it.

A woman near the step said the one ordinary thing she said that morning, and she said it to nobody in particular and it was not about anything on that table.

"You could put a hand on that page at four o'clock this afternoon and the shape of it would still be there, and that is all any of us have got out of it."

"**Whoever comes up these steps next will be able to see where that page has been lying, and that is the only thing anybody has got out of the last stretch of mornings, and it is not a thing you can put anywhere.**"

Nobody in that room agreed with her out loud and nobody in that room disagreed with her out loud, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the back of that bench and did not turn round.

And a road out of this city with three barrows on it is a road that goes away under a fence along the top of it and out past the last named house, and there is nobody in this city who can be shown one person on that road, and nobody in that room went down nine steps that day to ask, and the door behind them had a day on it in chalk and nothing above it.
