# Chapter 0424 — What Do You Sell

A room over a market had the hundred and ninth day of the Bare Month in chalk on its door at about the seventh hour, a Friday, and the shutters at the market end of it stood open and the noise of the market came up through the floor. About nine people were in the room before the light reached the stair and not one of them had been sent. The sheet was lying flat on the table with a crease pressed down the length of the third of its four places and nothing in any of them, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the nine steps and sat down at the back where he has sat down at the back every morning since before that page arrived.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up at about the eighth hour and asked a question, and it was not about the table, and she asked it in the local language, in daylight, with a date on the door, and she asked it once.

"**What do you sell.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not turn round, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not move, and the room at the market end went on being as loud as it was.

"**Two things. Whatever came in on the boards behind me yesterday that I have not sold by the fourth hour, and about a cord of good string. The string is the trade.**"

"And the rest of it."

"**The rest of it is what a stall is when there is nothing on the boards, which is most mornings, and I do not write a different line on the board about it any more, because a man who writes a different one gets asked why and I have been asked why about five times and I have not answered any of the five.**"

Nobody thanked him. He has said out loud, in a lane, a long way back, that he is not to be thanked, and four of the people in that room had heard him say it more than once and not one of them said a word about it, and the register-keeper wrote nothing down, because there is nowhere on that table to write down what a man sells.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of the bench and had been at the end of the bench on most of the mornings since the page came up, and nobody in that room had asked him a question this morning either, and the length of new rope was over the back of his chair where it had been every morning and it has still never been used, and a woman who asks a question that is not on the table turns out to have had one in her and not to have spent it for a fortnight, and the reason is not that she was saving it.

She asked a second one at about the ninth hour, and it was also not about the table, and she asked it to the back of the room and not to anybody in particular.

"And when you have sold all of it, at the fourth hour, what do you do with the rest of the day."

"**I wash the board and I put the string up and I sit at the back of a room over a market until the light goes off it, and I have done that on most mornings since before the water, and I have never once in this flood been asked a question about any part of it, and you have now asked me two, and I am not going to make anything out of that in front of anybody.**"

---

Nobody in that room said thank you and nobody in that room said he was right, and about four people in that room have worked out since that a man who keeps a stall has been sitting in it for a fortnight waiting to be asked what he does and not what he thinks, and that a woman who reads forms out loud for a living spent two questions of a morning on the first of those and not one on the third of the four places under the heading on the table in front of her, and that she is not going to be thanked for that either and has not asked to be.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room that morning and had a slate of his own flat on his knee under his right hand, and he did not look up at any point in either of those answers, and the two men at that bench are the only two in it who have been asked nothing all month and the only two who have been asked nothing this morning, and one of them has asked for it and the other one has not, and neither of those is going to be put in front of the other one this morning.

---

The sheet stayed flat on the table with the four places under its heading all empty and the crease down the length of the third of them, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall washed his board that afternoon in the market below and put the ordinary line on it and did not take the chalk out of his own inside breast pocket to do it, and the day on the door was the hundred and ninth day of the Bare Month and about nine people went down nine steps by the fourth hour with two questions in the room that had nothing to do with a page and a day on the door that has a thing coming on it, and nobody in that room told anybody else what either of those two things was.
