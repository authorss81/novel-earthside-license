# Chapter 0908 — Carried The Length Of The Room And Back

A hand went flat along the front edge of the second slate where it stood on the shelf behind the book, and the fingers came off the wood of it and went under, and the slate came off the shelf with the dust standing up off the wood behind it in a rectangle of its own.

It was Saturday, the five hundred and ninety-third day of the Bare Month. The light came on the row early and hard with the sky clear over the roofs, and the frost had not been in the shade at the fifth hour the way it had been in the week before, and there was no wind anywhere in the market. The register stood on the shelf behind the bench at the height of a person's shoulder in front of the second slate, where she had put it back on the Friday afternoon, and the bench in front of it was bare from end to end. Out of its two sockets the bar lay along the top step at the back of the store.

At the leg of the table the man of about twenty-seven had the bucket on the stone and the rag thrown over the rim. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along was sitting in the middle of the bench with his own board flat across his knees and the bare back of it turned out against his chest. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took the far end of the bench with both his own hands flat on the wood of it. The woman of about fifty-two had the wall side of the bench and her hands at the sides of her dress. At the near wall the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the satchel tucked against his side and the flap of it hanging away from him. At the near end of the bench the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her own hands in her lap. That room was full and nearly every body in it was off the bench.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the slate off the shelf at about the fourth hour and carried it the length of the room with both hands under it and set it down on the table with the cloth on it, and she turned it over there, and she stood it on its edge on the cloth and looked at the back of it for a while with her own hands flat on either side of it, and then she turned it back the way it had been and carried it the length of the room again and set it down on the same shelf in the same place and the same way up as it had stood. The dust on the wood of the shelf went down into the rectangle where the slate had been lying and stood pale against the pale all round it. No other hand in the room went near it at any hour of that day.

**"I have had a hand on the slate and I have turned it over and put it back the way it stood,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the shelf, **"and I am not going to say one word to anybody about it, and I would take it as a kindness if nobody in this room asked me one."**

**"Nobody is going to ask you one,"** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, at the middle of the bench, **"and I have not looked at it once this flood and I am not going to start this morning."**

**"Then it will go on standing there,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and put the flat of her own hand on the front edge of the shelf where her own hand had been at the fourth hour and left it there.

Nothing whatever was done about it at any hour of the afternoon. The stallholder went out to his own trestle at about the eighth hour and came back in at about the ninth with his own hand still flat on the face of his own board. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board kept both his own hands flat on the wood of the far end of that bench and looked out of the window. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket down and up the stair and the water stood flat in it every time. The carrier turned about from the near wall, looked at the flags inside the room instead, and left the flap of the satchel hanging where it was. The woman of about fifty-two looked at the shelf once and said nothing at any hour of the day. The keeper put her own hand flat on the front edge of the register where it stood on the shelf and left it there for a while and then took it off.

---

About the tenth hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the register off the shelf and put it down on the bench at the near end of it and turned it with its edge to the front the way the dust had it, and took her own hand off it and left it. By the light's going there were two rectangles standing in the dust of the room where two things had been standing, one of them on the shelf and one of them on the bench, and the one on the bench was longer than the one on the shelf by about the length of a hand.
