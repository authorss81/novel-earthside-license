# Chapter 0947 — The Space Where A Figure Stood

A thumb went along the foot of the middle column of a board that stood face out in its own row and stopped at the end of that column, and the thumb came off and went back to the top of it, and the man it belonged to went on standing there.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month. The morning came up hard and blue with the dew standing on everything that had not got the sun on it. The sun came over the roofs at the far end of that market a good deal later than it had on the day before, and came along the row with the shade of the trestles in front of it going up the walking spaces as it came. The store's end of that row was wet through where the cart had gone out over the flags, and had dried pale in the middle of the walking spaces, and was still black in the joints. Two trestles along, the trestle of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood with nothing at it. His own board was face in against it with its bare back turned out to the row behind his own shoulder.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood at his own trestle with his own board standing face out on it in the height where a man walks, and his own cloth over his own shoulder. The face of that board carried nineteen rates in three columns and the space about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two. He was there before the light was properly on the flags, and he was alone with it for the better part of a quarter of an hour, the rest of that row taking a while to come down on a morning when the sun was late.

---

What went through him while he stood there was the cloth. He put it on the face of his own board at the top of the top column and brought it down over the figures to the foot of the middle column, and the cloth stopped there the way it stops there every morning of that flood. The middle column is cut a little proud of the face, and the cloth cannot get past a thing that stands up out of the face in front of it. Then he lifted the cloth off it, folded it over his own shoulder, and stood looking at the face of his own board with the cloth off.

The space was bare. There was nothing standing in it, and nothing had ever been scraped off it, and nothing had been rubbed out of it, and nothing had been wiped over it. A figure had been standing in that space since a Monday earlier in that month in a man's own hand. It was not standing in it now, and nobody in that row had seen it go, and nobody was going to claim to have. The reason nobody had seen it go is that nobody in that row stands at that board and looks at it, which is the plainest thing about a board with a face out in a row.

What he saw instead, when he had stood looking at it long enough for the light to come across the walking space and up onto the face of it, was that the wood inside that space was a different colour from the wood either side of it. It was paler than the wood round it, and paler than the foot of the middle column that came down to the head of it. It was not the paleness of a thing that has been rubbed, because there was no rub on it at all and the edges of the space were as square as they had ever been. The wood of the whole of that face had been under a wet cloth every morning of that flood, and had been dried and wiped and dried again a hundred times and more. The wood inside that space had not been under anything at all, because a cloth that comes down a face and stops at a column cannot get into the foot of it. That space had not been wet since the board went out.

He did not put his hand on it. He did not ask himself aloud what a space about two fingers wide at the foot of a column is for, and nobody in that row asked him, and nobody came and stood beside him at that board. He put the cloth back on the face of his own board at the top of the top column and brought it down to the foot of the middle column and stopped. Then he lifted it off and folded it over his own shoulder again. The figure was not put back, and nothing was written anywhere about it.

---

**"I have wiped that face every morning since the spring, and the cloth comes down to the end of that middle column and stops there, and it has stopped there every one of those mornings. It has never once been into that space at the foot of it. The wood in that space is the only wood on that board that has not had a wet cloth on it in as long as that board has stood out in this row."** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, at his own trestle with the cloth over his own shoulder and the face of the board out to the row.

He said it once and said nothing else that day, and the row went on along the walking spaces, and some of it stopped at that board and some of it did not. Nobody standing at the board asked him a thing about the space at the foot of it, and the two of them carried on. The stallholder came back to his own trestle and stood at it with his own board face in against the wood and his own thumb along the foot of it, and stayed there. The shaving that had been lying on the stone at the foot of the tradesman's own trestle since an earlier morning was not on the stone any more, and nobody in that row saw it go.

---

The sun came along the whole row and stopped at the near end of the walking space, and the face of that board had gone out of the light by the time it got there. The dew had come off it and gone, and the whole of the wood of that face had dried pale and gone the colour of the wood two trestles along. The space about two fingers wide at the foot of the middle column was the only part of that board still holding the damp of that morning in it, and it stood out against the wood either side of it as a square of darker wood the size of a thumb joint. His own trestle had not been moved out of its place, and the board had not been touched that day by anything except a wet cloth that could not get to it.
