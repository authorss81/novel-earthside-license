# Chapter 0915 — Eleven Miles Of Flats And No Load

A hand came under the near end of a barrow and got hold of the shaft where it came down out of the frame, and the barrow went up over a ridge in the ground that had not been there the day before and came down hard on the far side of it, and the hand stayed on the shaft where it was.

It was Saturday, the six hundredth day of the Bare Month. The wind had got up in the night off the flats and was still coming across them in long pushes, and the water that came up out of the ground in the yard had gone into the same cracks in the flags it had gone into before and had not gone anywhere else. Eleven miles of flats lay between this yard and the last house on the other side of them, and the wind came the whole length of them without anything in the way of it. The store door at the back of the yard stood open a hand's breadth across and had stood that way since before the flood, and nothing had gone through it that morning. On the top of the stack of boards at the near end of the yard the tally-board lay face up with two lines of grey grit lying across it.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf stood at the handles of the barrow with the knife in his belt. At the mouth of the yard the man of about sixty stood with the handcart behind him and the hazel mallet lying in the bed of it with the head of the mallet bound in cord, and a coil of rope used and dark with the cold lying beside the mallet, and he had not come out with a barrow and he was not going to. Adrian Vale had his own hand on the shaft of the barrow at the near end and his own shoulder against the frame of it.

---

The barrow had gone out over the flats before the light with a man on the handles and another walking beside it, and it had come back along the eleven miles about the ninth hour without the load it had gone out for, and the man who had gone with it had said one sentence about it at the mouth of the yard and had gone on to the stack. There was a stack of boards at the near end of that yard standing on ground that had come up wet under it, and the work was to get the stack off that ground and onto the barrow, and the ground on the far side of the ridge in the yard was worse than the ground the stack was standing on.

Adrian Vale had wanted the stack off the wet ground and not put down in it anywhere on the far side of that ridge, and it was not what he got. He had the near end up off the ground with his own hand under the shaft and his shoulder against the frame, and the near wheel of the barrow came round into the ridge and the barrow went up onto it and over, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf got the far side of it over without stopping, which he would not have done with the near end down, and neither of them said one word to the other while it was going up. Then the boards came off the stack onto the frame a board at a time, and the stack got lower, and the bottom four boards stood in the wet with their feet going down into it as he got to them.

**"That ridge was not in this yard on Monday and it is going to be here all week,"** said the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf, at the handles, **"and there is water standing on both sides of it and the far side is the worse of the two."**

**"Then the far side is where a stack ends up,"** said the man of about sixty, at the mouth of the yard with his own hand on the shaft of the handcart, **"and a handcart will not cross a ridge with a stack on it, and mine is empty and it will stay empty."**

**"I know your cart is empty, and I have known since before the light,"** said the man of about thirty-eight, going at the stack again.

Adrian Vale stayed at the near end with his hand under the shaft and lifted while the boards went on and off the frame and the barrow went back and forward over the ridge twice more, and on the third time across the barrow went down into the soft on the far side up to the axle with the load on it, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf took four boards off the frame and set them down on the wet ground on the far side of the ridge to get the barrow back out, and the four boards went into the ground as he set them and the water came up round the near ends of them while he was still holding them. The man at the handles got the barrow out and set it down on the high dry part of the flags and Adrian Vale took his own hand out from under the shaft, and the stack was four boards shorter and every one of those four boards was lying in the wet where nothing else was, and he got no thank you and nobody told him he was right, and not one man in that yard was waiting for him to be useful and he was not useful to them. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf went at the stack again about the tenth hour and took the four boards off the wet ground one at a time and stood them on edge on the high dry part of the flags with the others, and the water came up out of the ground under them again within the hour and stood along their bottom edges.

---

At the light's going the barrow stood where it had been left on the near side of the ridge with its near wheel in the place where the ground was soft, and the wheel had gone down into it to about the depth of a hand's breadth in the hour it had stood there, and there was water standing in the rut the wheel had made with a skin on it about as thick as a leaf and the water in it was not moving at all.
