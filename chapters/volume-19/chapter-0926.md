# Chapter 0926 — The Morning After Standing Empty

A hand lay flat on the bare back of a board where it stood face in against a trestle, resting there, and the hand did not go into the coat and did not come out with anything.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month. The night had gone damp and the flags along the row were dark with it past the fifth hour. In the room over the market the second slate stood on the bench where it had been set the day before, and the register stood open beside it with six strokes in the column that holds figures. On the shelf behind the bench the dust held the shape of what had stood there. The chalk date stood on the wood of the door at the foot of the stair, with the bare piece of board under it empty, and on the step below the box of chalk stood with the lid standing off it at one corner. The trough beyond the door had ice standing in it, and the bucket stood at the leg of the table with the rag folded over the rim.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood at his own trestle with his own hand on the bare back of his own board. The man of about twenty-seven had the bucket on the stone at the leg of the table with the rag folded over the rim and the trough beyond the door with ice standing in it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her own hand on the head of her own page. That room was full and most of what was in it went on with what it had in its own hands, and the light lay along the bench from the far end to about the middle of it.

---

The stallholder stood alone at his own trestle for a few minutes with his own board in front of him, and what went through him while he looked at it was the morning before with the slate coming off the shelf and going onto the bench and the room hearing it and going on, and his own board standing face in through all of it with the rate at the foot wanting its day, and the piece of chalk in his own pocket lying where it lay, and the trough with ice in it beyond the door that nobody had fetched a heat for, and the box of chalk on the step with the lid off at one corner that nobody had shut. The board stayed where it stood and the chalk stayed where it was, and the thing in front of him was untouched at the end of it. He thought of carrying the board up the stair and set the thought aside, and he thought of wiping the bare back of it and left the cloth where it was.

**"The bare back of mine will stand out till the damp goes off,"** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, with his own hand still on the wood, **"and the foot of it will go on wanting its day past the light."**

**"The ice in the trough will stand till it goes off on its own,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the leg of the table with his own hand on the rim of the bucket, **"and I am not for carrying a bucket up a stair for the sake of a board."**

**"Then carry nothing up it, and leave the ice where it is,"** said the man of about thirty-four, and took his own hand off the board and put it at his side.

Nobody in that room waited for anybody to be useful at any hour of the day, and the woman who had spoken the day before went on with the head of her own page and was thanked by nobody at any hour of it. About four people a day went past the foot of that stair on their own business, and the satchel of the man who carries things for a living hung against his ribs with the flap down, and the slate stood on the bench while the room used it and would go back on the shelf when the room left it. The keeper turned a leaf and squared it against the fold, and the leaf stood up off the paper at the head where the damp had cockled it.

---

At about the tenth hour the keeper squared the leaf against the fold and left the dry cloth along the head of it, and the cloth had taken up nothing off the page where it lay, and the stallholder wiped his own hand on his own coat and set the board a little straighter against the trestle, and she put her own hand flat on the wood of the bench beside the book and left it there.
