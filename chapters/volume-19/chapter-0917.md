# Chapter 0917 — The Chalk Stayed Where It Was

A hand came up off the bare back of the stallholder's own board and went to the outside of a coat and stopped on the cloth over the pocket, and the hand stayed on the outside of the coat where it had stopped, and the chalk in the pocket did not come out of it.

It was Monday, the six hundred and second day of the Bare Month. The day came up clear and cold with the light coming over the roofs before it was properly light, and the frost was in the shade of the row and along the stone lip of the step at the store's end until about the seventh hour, and there was ice standing in the trough at the top of that step with the crack in its near end going thread-thin underneath it and no heat fetched for it by anybody. Along the top step at the back of the store the bar lay out of its two sockets with the frost standing on the whole length of it. Outside the door at the foot of that stair the day in chalk stood white on the wood and the bare piece of board under it stood clear of the wood by the width of a finger with nothing on it whatever, and on the step below the door the box of chalk had its lid standing off it at one corner and the two sticks inside it lying one on top of the other.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had the middle of the bench with his own board on edge across his knees and the bare back of it turned out against his chest. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the near end, where his own board stood propped against the end of the bench with its face turned into the room and a cloth over his own shoulder. The woman of about fifty-two kept the far wall with her hands at the sides of her dress and the stool standing against the wood at a foot's reach from her. The man of about twenty-seven had the bucket standing on the stone beside the leg of the table with the rag folded over the rim. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the near wall with the satchel held against his ribs. At the far end of the bench the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the register open in front of her. That room was full and most of what was in it was on its feet, and nobody came up that stair at the back of the store between the light and the light's going who had not come up it before.

---

The hand stayed on the outside of the coat for the length of about a minute and came off it and went back onto the bare back of the stallholder's own board, and the rate at the foot of that board wanted its day and did not get it. The pocket on the inside breast of that coat has a piece of chalk in it that has been there a long time and has been taken out and put back more than once, and it was not taken out that morning.

**"The face of this board has been out in the row for seventy-five days and I have wiped it once a week and it has needed it twice a week this month,"** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, at the near end of the bench, **"and there is a cloth on my shoulder and no water in this room and a bucket standing at the leg of that table and nobody in it."**

**"There is a bucket standing at the leg of that table,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the leg of the table, **"and it belongs to me and I fill it at the far end of the trough and bring it up, and I am not carrying it up to a room over a market for the sake of a board."**

**"Then the board stays as it is,"** said the man of about thirty-nine, and put the cloth back over his own shoulder and left it there.

The morning went on the way the morning before it had gone. The keeper turned a leaf about the eighth hour and squared it against the fold and went on with the page under her own hand. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board went down the stair and out to his own row and stood in front of his own trestle for about the length of a minute and came back up and took the near end of the bench again. The woman of about fifty-two came off the wall and looked at the head of the open page for the length of a minute without touching it and went back to the wall and left her hands at the sides of her dress. The man of about thirty-one gave the width of that wall to nobody and looked at the sill for a while instead. The stallholder got his own thumb along the foot of his own board and left it there all morning. Nobody thanked anybody for anything at any hour of that day and nobody in that room was waiting for anybody to be useful.

---

About the tenth hour a puff of wind came down between the buildings at the back of the store and went along the step under the door and the box of chalk moved on the step about the width of a thumbnail, and the lid of it came down on its near corner and went back off it again, and the two sticks inside the box were lying one across the other where they had not been lying one across the other at the light, and neither of them had been out of the box at any hour of that morning.
