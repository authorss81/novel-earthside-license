# Chapter 0927 — A Chair Standing In The Way

A hand went to the back of a chair where a length of new rope lay over it, and the hand rested on the wood of the chair and not on the rope, and the rope did not move.

It was Thursday, the six hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month. The night had gone cold and the ground out past the last named house had gone hard on the top of it and wet underneath. Inside the house the chair stood in the way of a door that was opened twice a day, and the length of new rope lay over the back of it where it had been left. The handcart stood at the side of the road with the hazel mallet bound in cord in the bed of it and the coil of rope that had been used lying dark beside it. The frost had settled along the top of the fence and stayed on the shaded side of the posts past the fifth hour.

The man of about sixty had his own hand on the back of the chair. Adrian Vale stood beside the chair with his own hand on the wood below the rope. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stood at the side of the room with the satchel against his ribs and the flap of it down. That house was small and the door stood shut with the chair against the swing of it, and the light came in low through the doorway and lay along the floor to the foot of the chair.

---

The work was the same work it had been the day before, getting the chair clear of the door without cutting what lay over it, and it did not come out. The man of about sixty put his own hand to the back of the chair and felt the wood under the rope and left the rope where it was, and Adrian Vale put his own hand to the same wood lower down and left the rope where it was, and the door stayed where it was with the chair in the way of it. The handcart stayed where it stood with the mallet in the bed of it, and the coil that had been used stayed beside the mallet. Adrian Vale shifted his own weight and tried the chair against the swing of the door and set it back where it had been, and the rope settled a little along the wood and stayed where it lay. What went through him while he stood was the morning with the lid of the box of chalk on the step and the trough with ice in it beyond the door, and he set that aside and went on with the chair.

**"The back of it wants shifting and the rope wants leaving where it lies,"** said the man of about sixty, with his own hand still on the wood, **"and the door will go as far as the chair lets it and no further."**

**"I can carry to the step and back and I know what is in my own satchel,"** said the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, at the side of the room, **"and I have nothing in it for a chair."**

**"Then carry nothing for it, and leave the chair where it stands,"** said the man of about sixty, and took his own hand off the wood.

Adrian Vale had wanted the rope cut because it was in the way of a door that was opened twice a day, and he did not lift it and he did not ask the man who had put it there one thing about it, and the rope was not cut at any hour of the day and the chair was not shifted. He got no thank you and nobody told him he was right, and nobody in that house was waiting for him to be useful and he was not useful to them. The man who had put the rope there was asked nothing at any hour of the day. The carrier shifted the satchel against his ribs and left the flap down, and the door stayed shut with the chair against it.

---

At the light's going the man of about sixty set the handcart with its nose out of the wind and left the hazel mallet in the bed of it, and the carrier went back along the road with the satchel against his ribs and the flap down, and Adrian Vale took his own hand off the back of the chair and wiped it on his own coat, and the chair stood where it had stood with the rope over the back of it.
