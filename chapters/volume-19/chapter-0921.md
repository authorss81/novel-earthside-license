# Chapter 0921 — Chalk Lid And A Word Said Aloud

A hand went to the lid of the box of chalk on the step below the door at the foot of the stair and settled it a little flatter where it stood off at one corner, and the lid stood off again as the hand came away, and no stick came out of the box.

It was Friday, the six hundred and sixth day of the Bare Month. The night had gone cold and still and the frost had settled along the walking spaces of the row and stayed on the shaded side of the flags past the fifth hour. At the top of the market step the trough had ice standing in it and nobody had broken the ice. Along the top step of the stair the bar lay out of its two sockets with the frost standing along the whole length of it. Outside the door at the foot of that stair the date stood in chalk on the wood and the bare piece of board under the date had nothing on it whatever, and the box stood on the step below with the lid standing off it at one corner where the hand had left it.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board propped against the near end of the bench with the face of it out to the room and the cloth folded over his own shoulder. The face carried nineteen rates in three columns with a space about two fingers wide at the foot of the middle column. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the book open on the bench at the far end with her own hand flat on the page and six strokes standing in the column that holds figures with a day against each. Adrian Vale stood on the step below the door with his own hand near the box of chalk. The room over the market was full and most of what was in it was on its feet, and the light lay along the bench from the far end to about the middle of it and stopped.

---

**"What it has cost me to say this out loud in this room this morning is that I have traded nineteen rates off this board in four markets for about nine years and I have never had one word to say about how any morning in that row is ordered, and saying a word for ordering it puts my own mouth into a place where every man who walks past that row will hear that I said it and will bring his own trouble with a rate to me before he brings it to anybody else, and I keep no book and I answer for no line in one, and I have stood out in that row with an empty place at the foot of my own board that I have not filled and have not asked anybody to fill, and from this morning I will be a man who said it and cannot take it back off the air of this room."**

He said it into the middle of the floor and not to anybody in it, and he said it once, and he did not say it again at any hour of that day.

**"The cloth wants washing and the face wants wiping and there is no water in this room,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the far end of the bench without lifting her own hand off the page, **"and the head of this leaf is still damp from the cover and wants the dry end of a cloth twice today."**

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of the bench with his own board face out and the cloth in his own hand going over the top of it. She was at the far end of the bench with her own shoulder to the window and her own face toward the middle of the room, about nine feet off him, going on with the head of the page while he spoke, and he did not turn his head while she was speaking and he did not stop the cloth while she was speaking either, and what he said was in no hand and on no page.

**"There is a word in this world for a body that decides about a morning, and the word for it is council, and I am saying it out loud in this room this morning, and I am not saying it to anybody in it."**

Nothing else stood from either of the two things at any hour past the light. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went on with the damp head of her own leaf and squared it against the fold. Adrian Vale asked in the ordinary way of that stair whether anybody would fetch a heat for the trough at the top of the step, and the man of about twenty-seven said the bucket was standing with ice in it and left it where it stood, and the heat was not fetched by any hand at any hour of the day. The tradesman wiped the top of his own board and stopped at the foot of the middle column and did not go over the space there. Not one thank you came his way for the first thing or the second, and not one mouth in that room improved on one word of what he had said.

About four people a day went past the foot of that stair on their own business while the light was on the flags, and the door stood open on its latch the whole of it, and not one word of what had been said in that room came out past the bottom step.

---

Adrian Vale put his own hand back to the lid of the box on the step and found the corner standing off again where he had left it, and the man of about twenty-seven came down with the bucket and set it on the stone and went up again with nothing in it, and the ice in the trough stayed where it was past the tenth hour.
