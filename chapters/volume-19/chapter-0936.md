# Chapter 0936 — The Barrow Out Over The Flats

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came over the flats with the satchel held up against his own ribs and the flap of it hanging away from his own side, and on the crest of the rise the flap came up once off his coat and went down again and nothing came out of it, and eleven miles of ground went under his own boots without his putting a hand on any of it.

It was Saturday, the six hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month. The sun was up before the light and had been at the crust of the flats since about the fourth hour, and the crust had gone hard on top and was still wet underneath it, so that a heel went through and came up dark. The yard at the near end of the flats stood with the stack of boards in it and the tally-board face up on the top of the stack with two lines of grey grit lying across the face of it, and the barrow stood beside the stack with the knife lying in the bed of it. The store door stood open on its hinges at the side of the yard. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had his own hand on the top board of the stack. The man of about sixty stood at the mouth of the yard with his own hands on the shafts of the handcart and the hazel mallet bound in cord lying in the bed of it.

---

They took nine boards off the top of that stack, which had been standing with its foot in the wet where the water came up out of the ground under the flags, and set them down square on the flags, and the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf squared the ends of them with the flat of his own hand before they went on the barrow, and then they went on one at a time, and the man of about sixty took each of them off his own shoulder with the flat of his own hand and laid it flat and not on its edge, because a board on its edge goes over sideways on ground that is wet underneath a hard skin.

Then the barrow went out across the flats with the nine on it and came back with the nine on it. The near wheel went through the crust at gap after gap all the way across and came up wet to the hub, and each time it went through the man at the handles felt it and said nothing, and the carrier walked at the near wheel and put his own boot flat down on the crust ahead of it and held it with his own weight while the wheel came through the next gap and went on. They brought the nine back into the yard and stood them on edge against the wall under the eave with the top of them out of the wet, and the stack was nine boards shorter than it had been at the light and the ground at its foot was left standing in the open where it had been.

The ground they had come across showed the whole of it. Every place where that near wheel had gone through stood with a rim of the crust broken up round it and the broken pieces drying pale in the sun, and the rims stood up out of the wet like the edges of a set of shallow cups, and they ran in a straight line from the door of that yard out to the far side of the flats and back.

**"The ground takes a wheel through it at gap after gap and it will go on doing it all day, and every board of the load is off the wet and up on its edge against that wall and that is the whole of what came of it, and I want the crust to dry before I move anything else across it, and I am not going to be told to hurry it."** said the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf, at the door with the knife back in his belt.

The carrier stood in the mouth of the yard with the satchel against his ribs and the flap of it down, and he had carried it eleven miles over ground that gave way under a wheel at gap after gap, and he did not open it at any hour of that day, and no mouth in that yard asked him why he opens nothing he carries and he did not offer a word of reason.

---

The line of broken crust had dried all the way out by the light's going, so that the pale rims of the ruts stood hard in the sun and had gone white at their edges, and a person could walk that line from the stack to the store door with a heel on every rim and never once go through.
