# Chapter 0948 — A Rate That Still Wants Its Day

His own thumb went along the foot of a board that stood face in against its own trestle and stopped on a rate that had nothing standing against it, and the thumb went back to the end of that foot and did the same thing again.

It was Thursday, the six hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month. The night had gone hard and clear and the frost had come off the roofs and off the trestles before the light and left a white rim on the tops of the boards in that row, and the sun came over the far end of that market with the shade of the awning in front of it and lay along the walking spaces from the far end toward the store's end. The trough at the top of the market step stood with the crack in its near end quiet and no water coming out of it. The step below the door at the foot of that stair stood bare, and the box of chalk stood on the table inside that room with its lid off at one corner as it always stands.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own trestle with his own board face in against the wood and the bare back of it out to the row behind his own shoulder. On the other side of that walking space the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own trestle with his own board standing out on it face out and the cloth over his own shoulder. The woman of about fifty-two came down out of that room and along the row to the store's end of it and went on to the end of the flags and stood there with the wind at the back of her dress. The row went by on its own business and some of it stopped at one board or another and some of it went along without stopping at anything.

---

On an earlier morning in that same week a thing was said out loud at the far end of the bench in the room over that market, and it was said once, and it was not said into the middle of that floor.

**"The damp has got into the bare back of my own board overnight and come out of it in a dark line along the foot, and it will not come off with a dry cloth and I will not have it out with a wet one, and the face of it stays face in against the trestle and the rate at the foot of it goes on wanting its day."** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, at the far end of that bench with his own board against his own hip.

Nobody in that room had said anything back to him about it and nobody had asked him about it then or afterwards, and nobody had said that it was a good thing to have said or a bad one, and no day was put against that rate and nothing was marked against it, and the rate went on standing on the foot of that board with nothing against it.

On that Thursday morning the same thing was said a second time in that row in different words by a different man, and it was said once, and the man who said it said it into the walking space and not to anybody in it.

**"A rate at the foot of that board has stood there with no day against it for as long as I have traded on this row, and there is no reason anybody in this market could give me that I would act on, and I am not going to put one there this morning, and I am saying so out loud in the open because a thing said out loud in the open is a thing nobody has to believe and a thing written on a board is a thing everybody has to believe whether they mean it or not."** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, at his own trestle with the cloth in his own hand and the face of his own board out to the row.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along did not turn round. He went on with his own thumb along the foot of his own board and stopped where he had stopped, and he did not say a word back and he did not ask the other man a question about it, and he did not say afterwards that he had heard it before. Nobody in that row put one of the two things beside the other, and nobody improved on either of them, and nobody in that row said what either of them had settled, and the rate at the foot of that board went on standing there with nothing against it and no day was entered against it and nothing was entered in either of the books in that room above.

The woman of about fifty-two stood at the end of the flags with the wind off the flats going at the back of her dress and did not turn round and did not say one word about what either of the two men had said. The man of about twenty-seven went down to the low place in the stone at the side of the yard and came back up the step again with the rag over the rim of it and said nothing about either of the two men. The tradesman took the cloth off his own shoulder, put it on at the top of the top column of his own board and brought it down, and it stopped at the foot of the middle one and did not go over the space, and he lifted it off and folded it over his own shoulder again and went on standing at his own trestle, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along went on standing at his own with his own thumb where it had been all morning.

---

By the light's going the shade had come back along that row from the store's end of it and the two boards had gone over from the sun into it within about as long as it takes to walk between them. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along took his own thumb off the foot of his own board at last and looked at it before he put his hand down, because the tip of it had gone white and the white had gone hard and cracked, and a flake of the skin of it had come away and was stuck to the wood of that board at the foot of it, and he left it there and did not put his own hand back on the wood.
