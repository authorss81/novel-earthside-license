# Chapter 0923 — Willow Posts Along A Shut Road

A hand went along the top of a willow post where it came out of the ground, and the hand felt round the joint of it, and came off it, and the post stood where it stood.

It was Sunday, the six hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month. The night had gone hard with a clear sky and the ground along the top of the shut road had gone hard on the top of it and stayed wet underneath, so that a heel went through the crust and came up dark. Out past the last named house the fence stood along the top of that road with sixteen willow posts and eleven withies along the top of it. The handcart stood at the side of the road with the hazel mallet bound in cord lying in the bed of it and the coil of rope used and dark with the cold beside the mallet. The grass stood up through the metalling at the far end where nothing went any more, and the wind moved along the top of the fence and stopped.

The man of about sixty had his own hand on the top of the post at the near end of the fence. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood further along the road with his own board under his arm and the cloth over his own shoulder and the face of the board turned in against his side. That road was shut and had been shut since before this flood, and the light lay along it from the houses to the bend and stopped. The carrier's satchel hung against his ribs where he had left it at the side of the road, and he did not open it at any hour of the morning.

---

He went the length of the fence the way he had gone it the day before, putting his own hand to the head of one post and then to the next, feeling round the joint where the wood went into the ground, and the withies along the top of it held where they held and gave where they gave, and he put no withy on and took none off, and he moved no post. The ground gave under his own heel at the third post and held again at the next, and the handcart stayed where it stood with the mallet in the bed of it, and the coil of rope stayed beside the mallet where it was. He stopped at the middle of the fence and looked back toward the houses and then went on again, and the wood was cold under his hand and the cord round the withies was stiff with the frost. What went through him while he went was the same work the day before had left, and he went on with it because it was his to go on with.

**"The top is hard and the foot is wet and it will stand another day,"** said the man of about sixty, with his own hand off the post and at his side, **"and the withies want leaving where they are till the ground dries."**

**"My own board wants the sun on it and it is getting none on this road,"** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, further along with the board under his arm, **"and I am for the row before the light goes off the flags."**

**"Then go on to the row, and leave the fence to stand,"** said the man of about sixty, and put his own hand to the next post along.

The tradesman went back along the road toward the row with the board under his arm and the cloth over his shoulder, and the man of about sixty went on to the far end of the fence and came back again the same way, and the fence was sixteen willow posts and eleven withies when he left it and it was the same when he came back to it. He lifted the shafts of the handcart and set it a little out of the rut and put it down again, and the mallet shifted in the bed of it and lay still. Inside the house out past the last named house the chair stood with nothing touched on it, and he did not go inside that house at any hour of the day.

---

At the light's going he put his own hand on the last post along and the cold of it came off his palm the way it had come off it four times that day, and he left the fence standing the way he had found it, and he turned the handcart about on the ground so that its nose stood out of the wind with the cord still round the head of the mallet where the cord had been all day, and the coil of rope lay dark in the bed of it where he had put it that morning. Then he went up toward the houses with his own hands in his own pockets and the road behind him with nothing on it that he had put there.
