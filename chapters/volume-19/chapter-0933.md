# Chapter 0933 — The Gate Standing Shut One Morning

Rain came off the flats in a line that could be seen coming, and a hand went down into the two worn places in the stone at the foot of the gate and the water that had been standing in them went over the knuckles and up the inside of the wrist.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and eighteenth day of the Bare Month. The rain had been at it since before light without stopping, and the standing water in the ruts of the shut road had come up over the top of them and gone level with the flats on both sides, so that the road was a strip of moving water with the grass standing up out of it in the middle. At the near end of the shut length the grass stood in the mud with its roots still holding, and at the far end the last of the metalling lay flat under the water with the grass up through it. The gate stood open on its latch, where it has stood open since before this flood, with the water running through the opening and out across the flats on the far side. Adrian Vale had been at the gate since before the light. The man of about sixty stood at the side of the road a good way back with the handcart in the shafts and the hazel mallet bound in cord lying in the bed of it.

---

He went along the shut length and back twice before the light was properly on, which is about nine hundred paces each way, and he came back to the gate with the water coming off him and the grass-seed stuck to the wet of his own coat along the front of it. He got down onto the stone on his knees and put his hands into the two hollows and the water in them stood about the depth of a knuckle, the same as it had on the Sunday morning when a different man had knelt in the same place and put his own hands in the same two hollows and wiped them on the front of his own coat.

The far side of the gate was under. The strip of metalling went out from the foot of the stone and went under the water and did not come up again on that side, and past it the water was level and open to a line in the east that he could not see through the rain. He stood up and looked at it and looked back down the road he had walked, and there were people on it already, going along in ones and twos on their own business with the water coming up past their boots.

---

He took the gate with his own hand and drew it across the opening and put the latch down, and the latch went as easy as it had always gone and the gate came to against its own jamb with the sound a gate makes when nothing is in the way of it, and he put his own hand flat on the iron of it and left it there. He did it because there was no ground on the far side to stand on and a person who went through that opening in this water would be going out into nothing that would hold him up, and he had been at that gate since before the light and had seen the water come up over the metalling while he was on it. He said nothing to anybody about it. He had no reason to give to anybody and he had nobody standing near him to give it to.

People had been going along that road and out through the opening in ones and twos while it stood open, and then he shut it, and the road carried them up towards him after that, and it did not go through the opening any more, and the people who came to it stopped.

**"The cart will not take the water at this end of the road and I can stand here till midday with it,"** said the man of about sixty, at the shafts a good way back with the rain running off the brim of his own hat into the bed of the cart, **"and that is my own business and it wants doing before the light goes, and I can hear the gate behind me and I am not turning round about it."**

**"Then stand there till midday with it,"** said Adrian Vale, at the gate with his own hand still on the iron, **"and I am not moving off here either."**

The man of about sixty stood there with his own hands on the shafts until the light began to go, and he did not come past him and he did not ask him one thing about it, and he did not say to him that he was wrong and he did not say to him that he was right.

---

They came along the road in ones and twos for the rest of that morning and every one of them that came to the shut gate stopped at it. Some stood a long time in the water with the rain going down the fronts of them and their faces turned away down the road. Some put what they were carrying down on the wet ground at the side of the road and stood beside it. Some went on up the shut length to where the water was deeper and turned round and came back and stood at the gate again. Nobody went through. Adrian Vale did not move off the gate and did not lift his hand off the iron of it and did not open it at any hour of that morning, and no mouth on that road said one word to him about it, and nobody came up to him and thanked him for it. When the light began to go off the flats the people standing at the gate picked up what they had put down and went back up the shut length on the side they had come from, and some of them went slowly, and one of them looked back at the gate as he went.

---

At the light's going he got his hands back into the two places in the stone, and the water that had been standing in them all that day went over the top of them and ran out over the foot of the gate and in again at the other side of each. Both of them took his hands the way a shallow cup takes a hand. The near one was deeper than the far one by the depth of a thumb, as it had been on the Sunday morning, and he had his own hands in them and the water going over them, and the stone was not worn where his hands went. It was worn where the water went.
