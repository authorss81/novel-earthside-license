# Chapter 0935 — One Thing Said And Said Back

The folded sheet of road came up the stair in front of a woman who did not put it down, and a hand that was not hers went to the near corner of it and stopped a finger short of the paper and stayed there.

It was Friday, the six hundred and twentieth day of the Bare Month. The flags had dried pale in the middle of the walking spaces and had not dried at all in the joints, and the daylight came in under that awning, lay along the bench and crossed the flags inside the room. Out on the top step of that stair the bar lay where it has lain every morning of this flood, and the sun was on one end of it. There was a day in chalk on the outside of that door at the foot of the stair, and the board under it stood clear of the wood with nothing on it. The second book lay at the far end of the bench with its own cover slab across the leaf where it had settled the day before, and the register lay open beside it with the dry cloth laid along the head of the leaf.

Tamsin Quill came up the stair at about the tenth hour with the sheet of road in her own hand and about nine places marked on it, and she was in the room before the rest of it had finished what it was doing at the foot of the stair. Adrian Vale had the near end of the bench with his own hand flat on the wood about a finger's width from the corner of that sheet where it lay folded along the end of the wood, and his own shoulder to the window. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood at the other end of the same bench with his own board under his own arm and the cloth over his own shoulder. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat on the middle of the bench with his own board across his own knees and the bare back of it turned out. The bucket stood at the leg of the table with the rag over its rim and the man of about twenty-seven not far off it. Against the near wall the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stood with the satchel held up under his own arm and the flap hanging away from his own side. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register kept the far end of that bench with the second book in front of her. The woman of about fifty-two kept the far wall with her own hands down the sides of her dress. There was standing room in that room for very little else.

---

**"I have come up this stair to say one thing, and I am going to say it once, and then I am going to sit down where I sat last time and go on with the rest of the morning. Neither name goes in a column of either book. Not yours and not mine. Not while either of us is standing here and not afterwards, and I am not asking you to promise it and I am not asking anybody in this room to hold me to it, and there is nothing on that bench this morning that could hold either of us to anything, and I would rather have said it out loud in a room with a day chalked on the outside of that door than said it nowhere at all."**

She said it once and she did not say it again at any hour of that day.

She had the sheet of road lying folded along the end of that bench with the marked places turned away from the light, and she did not open it out, and after a while she took it up onto her own knees and kept it there. The keeper went on with the head of her own page and put her own hand flat on it again. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along looked at the back of his own board and said nothing. The man of about twenty-seven took the bucket down that stair and brought it back up with the water standing flat in it. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came off the near wall and turned about and looked out at the flags in the room and then turned about again and looked at the stair.

Adrian Vale had wanted one thing of that morning and it was not the thing he got. His own hand had gone to the corner of that sheet because there were about nine places marked on it and every one of them was a place a person could be found in, and there was one thing about Tamsin Quill that morning he had no use for unless he knew which of them she stood in tonight, and she had said something else. She had said a thing that would be in his own head on every morning after this one and that he could not put down anywhere and could not do one single thing with.

**"Neither of us goes in a column of either of them, and I am not going to ask you to hold to it either, and I am not going to make you stand in a doorway and choose, and I have not come up this stair to ask you for anything, and I am not going to say another word about it in front of them."** said Adrian Vale, at the near end of the bench with his own hand still on the wood.

Nobody in that room compared the two things that had been said, and nobody put them side by side, and nobody asked either of them where the other had got to. The man who came up that stair on an earlier morning of this flood had put his own name into the second book in his own hand with a day against it and had named out loud, in his own words and into the middle of that floor, what it had cost him to be a person who was in no book at all, and nobody in that room had ever set one of those against the other, and nobody did now. The line in the second book did not change, and neither of those two names went in it that day, and the two of them did not speak again at any hour of the rest of that morning, and neither of them looked at the other one at any hour of it. What went on between them was what goes on between two people in a room, which is that they were in the same room and each of them went on with what he or she had come up that stair carrying.

---

The bench held a dry rectangle about a hand wide at the light's going, where the sheet of road had lain across it all day with the damp of the floor coming up into the wood on every side of it, and inside that rectangle the wood was still pale and dry. By then the sheet was folded away inside a coat and the dry place had begun to go at one corner, where the damp had got under the edge of it and was standing in the grain.
