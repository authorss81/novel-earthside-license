# Chapter 0930 — Grit Lines On A Tally Board

A hand went under a tally-board where it lay face up on the top of a stack, and the hand lifted it off the stack and turned it round in the air and set it back down face up where it was, and the two lines of grey grit did not break under it.

It was Sunday, the six hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month. The night had gone still and the light came across eleven miles of flats and lay along the yard at the near end of them. The stack of boards stood at the near end of the yard with the tally-board face up on the top of it and two lines of grey grit lying across it. The barrow stood beside the stack with the knife lying in the bed of it. The date stood chalked on the wood of the door at the foot of the stair, and under it the bare piece of board carried nothing whatever. In the room over the market the second slate stood on the shelf behind the bench. The ground in the yard was hard on the top of it and wet underneath, and the flags at the side of it showed the damp coming up through the seams.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had his own hand on the tally-board where it lay. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood at the side of the yard with the book shut under her arm. That room over the market was full and the yard held what it held, and the light lay along the stack from the near end to the far end of it. The handcart stood at the mouth of the yard with the hazel mallet in the bed of it, and the man of about sixty stood behind it with his own hands at his sides.

---

**"What it has cost me to say this out loud in this room this morning is that I have kept a tally-board face up on the top of a stack for a hundred days with two lines of grey grit across it and I have not turned it over once, and saying a word for what binds a record puts my own mouth into a place where every man in this yard will hear that I said it and will bring his own trouble with a tally to me before he brings it to anybody else, and I keep no book and I answer for no line in one, and from this morning I will be a man who said it and cannot take it back off the air of this yard."**

He said it into the middle of the yard and not to anybody in it, and he said it once, and he did not say it again at any hour of that day.

**"The head of my own page wants the dry cloth and the leaf will not lie flat,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the side of the yard with the book under her arm, **"and I am for the bench before the light goes off the flags."**

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf was at the stack with the date in chalk on the outside of the door behind him and the tally-board face up in front of him, and she was a little way off him going on with what she had in her own hands while he spoke, and what he said was in no hand and on no page. The barrow stood between the stack and the store door with the knife in the bed of it, and the stack stood where it had stood since before the light.

**"There is a word in this world for the thing that binds a record to a place, and the word for it is seal, and I am saying it here in the daylight with the date behind me, and I will not say it again today."**

The yard went on with what it had in its own hands at every hour of that day. He carried the tally-board from the stack to the barrow and back to the stack face up, and he did not turn it over at any hour of it and he did not count the two lines of grey grit as figures. The barrow stayed where it stood with the knife in the bed of it, and the stack stayed where it stood with the tally-board on the top of it. Not one thank you came his way for the first thing or the second, and not one mouth in that yard improved on one word of what he had said.

---

The wharf man set the knife back in his belt and left the barrow standing beside the stack with the tally-board face up on the top of it, and the keeper went back toward the stair with the book under her arm, and the handcart stayed at the mouth of the yard with the mallet in the bed of it, and he looked after her along the yard and said one thing and stopped.
