# Chapter 0910 — An Hour Of It Going Round The Room

Two hands took the weight off a board that had been propped against the near end of the bench and the board went over, and the hands were on it before it reached the flags, and it lay on the stone of the step with its wet face up and the water running off it toward the low place at the side of the yard.

It was Monday, the five hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month. The water had come back up out of the seams of the flags in the night again and the row was dark from end to end, and the light came on it late and grey. Two boards stood against the near end of the bench at the back of the store, and they had stood there since the middle of the week with one of them leaning on the other and both of their faces turned out to the room. The bar lay along the top step out of its two sockets. The bare piece of board under the day chalked on the outside of that door had nothing on it whatever and nothing went under it.

Against the near wall the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was standing with the satchel under his arm and the flap of it down. The woman of about fifty-two stood against the far wall of the room with both hands hanging at her sides. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had it open in front of her at the far end of the bench with her own hand flat on the page. The man of about twenty-seven was at the leg of the table with the bucket and the rag beside him. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own board lying edgeways across his knees at the middle of the bench and the bare back of it turned out. At the near end the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own hands on the board that had gone over and had set it down on the step and left it lying there. The room over the market was full and it had been full since before the light.

---

The question went into the room about the eighth hour in the ordinary way a thing goes into a room while people are in the middle of what they have in their own hands.

**"Does anybody in this room know who went through the gate at the far end of the shut road on the Friday the rain was on it,"** said the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, with his face turned into the room, **"not from the row, not from a stall, from anywhere at all."**

**"I was at my own trestle from the light on that Friday and I did not see it done,"** said the stallholder, and put his own hand along the foot of his own board.

The question went round the room for the better part of an hour and it did not get past the room. Some of the people in it said they did not know, and some of them went on with what they had in their own hands, and the stallholder said twice more that he had been at his own trestle from the light, and the second time he said it in fewer words than the first. The man of about twenty-seven said he had been on the step at the store's end with a bucket in his hand and had not been up the row at all, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said he had been in the room all day and had not gone past the near end of the bench, and the woman of about fifty-two said she had been up the stair every morning of the week and had not gone up the row. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not asked and kept her own hand flat on the page, and turned a leaf, and squared it against the fold. The carrier had walked out along that shut road on the Sunday morning and had got down on his knees on the wet stone at the foot of the gate and had put his own hands into the two worn places in it, and he did not say one word about the Sunday morning and nobody in the room asked him one word about the Sunday morning.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living put his own hand flat on the wood of the sill in front of him where the satchel was standing and said one thing and stopped.

**"Then that is the whole of what I have,"** said the carrier, and left the satchel where it was standing and did not say anything else at any hour of the day.

---

Before the light went off the row somebody had put the fallen board back up against the near end of the bench on top of the other one, and the two of them stood leaning there with the foot of the top one out from the wood by about the width of a hand, and the water coming off that awning had run back under the top one and come out again at the other side of it and made a dark band up the lower edge of both of them about as deep as two fingers.
