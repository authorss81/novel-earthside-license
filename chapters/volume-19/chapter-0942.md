# Chapter 0942 — Nineteen Rates And A Space

A cloth went over the face of a board in that row from the top of the top column down to the foot of it and stopped there, and a thumb went in beside the figure standing in the space under the middle column and came out again without touching anything.

It was Friday, the six hundred and twenty-seventh day of the Bare Month. The night had gone hard and blue with no cloud in it and the sun had not been over the roofs before the light, so that the flags of the row were dry through and cold and the shadows of the trestles lay across the walking spaces at an angle from the far end of the market to the store's end of it. The trough at the top of the market step stood with the crack in its near end gone quiet and no water coming out of it, and the stone lip of that step had a rim of white on it where the frost had been and gone. The step below the door at the foot of the stair stood bare, and the box of chalk stood on a table inside that room with its lid off at one corner.

A board stood face out at its own trestle in that row with nineteen rates in three columns cut into the face of it and a space about two fingers wide at the foot of the column between the other two. A figure was standing in that space. It had been put there in a man's own hand on an earlier Monday and had not been asked for by anybody since. At that trestle the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the cloth in his own hand and the face of that board out to the row. Two trestles along, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood at his own trestle with his own board face in against it and the bare back of it turned out into the row behind his shoulder. The man of about twenty-seven was at the top of the market step with his bucket on the stone and the rag over the rim of it.

---

About four people a day went along that row on their own business while the light was on the flags, and about four people a day got a thing off the face of that board and about four people a day did not. None of them stood in front of it. What the face of it said to them was a column of figures going down from the top and three columns side by side, and one space at the foot of the middle one with nothing said about what the space was for. The people who stopped at it stopped for as long as it takes to read three columns, and then went on with their morning.

That row talked about the board the way a row talks about a board. What it wanted was the sun. It was standing out in the open with its face to the light and it was getting it, and the board two trestles along was standing face in against its trestle and getting none. One of those two things was said out loud on that row more than once that morning, and neither of them was said about a figure.

What was said about the board was that it wanted wiping at the foot and that the cloth did not go there. It was said that the cloth had caught on a splinter at that foot on an earlier morning and pulled a shaving off the face of it about the length of a finger, and that the shaving was still lying on the stone at the foot of that trestle where he had put it down. About four people a day took a rate off the face of that board and did not stop at it, and about four people a day did not take one. Not one of the two kinds said anything out loud about it.

**"There is a figure standing at the foot of the middle column of that board, and it has been standing there since the Monday it went in. Nobody in this row has asked what it is and nobody in this row has been told. I have stood at that trestle every morning the row has been going and I have not once stopped at that board to look at the space under the middle column. I am not going to stand here and tell anybody what a figure in a space is for. I do not know. A man who stands in the open and explains a thing he has not been told is a man who has decided that he knows it."** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, to the row at the height of a man's chest, with his own board face in against his own trestle and his own thumb along the foot of it.

The tradesman went on with the cloth. The cloth went from the top of the top column down to the foot of it and stopped where the middle column came to an end, and it did not go over the space. It went back to the top and came down again. The man who had said it did not ask him anything about it, and he did not answer the man who had said it, and the row went on along it.

Nothing in that row was done about the figure in that space that day. The figure was not taken off the board, nothing was put back into that space, and nothing was written anywhere about it. The man who had put it there was not asked for it by anybody in that row. The man who had spoken about it was not asked a second thing, and he did not speak about it again. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket down to the low place in the stone at the side of the yard and back up the step again with the rag over the rim of it, and said nothing about either board.

---

As the sun came round toward the far end of the row the shade went back down the walking space between the trestles a foot at a time, and the face of that board went over from the light to the shade as the light left it. The middle of the three columns stood about the thickness of a finger proud of the face, with the hard edges it had been cut to, and it threw a shadow of its own across the foot of itself. That shadow came across the space before it came across the wood either side of the space. By the time the rest of that face was in the shade, the figure standing in the space under the middle column was the last thing on that board that could be seen at all. It went into the shade with the face, and the cloth went over the top of it and stopped at the foot of the top column. The shaving the cloth had pulled off the face of that board was still lying on the stone at the foot of the trestle with its wet face turned up to the sky.
