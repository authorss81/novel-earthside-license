# Chapter 0536 — A Distance And A Place Nobody Here Has Stood In

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the sheet with the figure in it off the shelf behind the bench at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and carried it in both hands to the near end of the table under the one window and set it down on the wood with its heading to the wall, and then she carried it back up onto that shelf and put it in the same place on that shelf it had been in, at the height of a person's shoulder, with the front of it to the room. The two hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and there was no name on that door. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

The other sheet was lying on that table the whole morning at the far end of it with its heading to the wall and its one place ruled under that heading showing nothing whatever in it. It was turned face up at about the ninth hour and it stayed face up, and the length of a table was between the two of them at every hour of that morning, and nobody in that room moved either of them a hand's width towards the other and nobody in that room said one word out loud about how the two of them stand in relation to one another or about one of them being the other one coming back.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the shelf.

"Four documents have come down that road and there are two of those things in this room."

"**Four, and three of them have four places ruled under a heading and one of them has a single place, and the single place on that one is empty and the single place on that other one is not, and I am not going to lay the two of them down together so that a room can count the places on them, and if a room in this city wants that it can have it over my body.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it, and nobody in that room did either of those two things to see what would happen.

---

A woman at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the window and not to the room.

"There is a road out past the last named house and it is a long one."

"**That road is four hundred miles long and it is a measure of a distance and not a place anybody in this city has stood in, and every document that has come down it has come inward on it, and not one thing has ever gone out of this city on it, and I have not got a figure for what that road is for and I am not going to invent one in a room in daylight.**"

Nobody in that room asked her what the road is for. Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her, and about four people in that room had worked out that the sheet on the table came up that road and about four had not, and the two of them who had worked it out did not say so to each other.

The fence runs along the top of that road from the corner by the last named house to the corner where the road turns, and it is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on them with the same cord, and it was put up by the man of about sixty and nobody took a cord to it that morning and nobody took a cord to the corner in it and nobody has ever taken a cord to it. A party of four set out down that road with a barrow each for three of them on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road and nobody in that room said one word about it.

---

At about the tenth hour a man in that room said out loud that he had stood at the near gate that morning and had looked up that road and could not see the top of it, and about four people said nothing and about four said that they had not looked. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he looked out of that window at the top of that road for about as long as it takes a man to count nine and then looked back down at the face of his own board, and the space about two fingers wide where a name had been in his own hand about nine years ago is not filled and was not filled and nobody in that room asked him about it.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench with the satchel with the flap on the bench beside him and his hands on his knees and the strap of it over the back of the bench, and nobody asked him what was in it and he did not tell anybody and nobody in that room said a word about the sheet on the table being put into it or not being put into it.

That road is four hundred miles long and it goes out past the last named house and it does not come back into this city anywhere, and nobody in this city has ever stood at the end of it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board looked up it this morning for about as long as it takes a man to count nine and then looked back down at the face of his own board, and the space about two fingers wide where a name had been in his own hand about nine years ago is not filled. The two sheets are in this room and they have not been laid side by side and one of them has a figure in its one place and the other has nothing whatever in it.
