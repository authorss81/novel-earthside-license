# Chapter 0543 — A Knot He Was Shown And A Different One Twice

Adrian Vale came up that stair at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and twenty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, with a length of string in his own two hands, and he put the near end of that string on the near corner of the table under the one window and let the rest of it lie along the wood. The date on that door was in chalk and there is no name on it and there never has been one. About nine people were in that room by that hour and every one of them had come up on their own two feet, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was already at the far end of that bench with the satchel with the flap on the seat beside him and the flap down and nothing in it.

---

That table has a folded sheet on it. There is a heading at the top of that sheet and one place ruled under that heading, and there is one place only and there has never been a second one, and a figure of this city is in that one place and there is nothing else on that sheet anywhere. That figure went out of this city two days ago in a satchel with a flap and it is not in this room and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back, and nobody in that room said one word this morning about what that figure was a figure of, because there is no place on that sheet for it.

The bone was still lying where it had been put down on the day that sheet was folded and had not been moved since, and the string Adrian Vale had put down lay along the wood on the near corner of the table with the far end of it still loose and going over the edge.

---

Two stalls along in that row the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall keeps a stall with a trestle in front of it, and his own board stands face in against that trestle with the bare back of it out in the row, and the board is held to that trestle with a length of string that he ties and unties as the weather gets at it. He has tied that knot in front of people at that trestle every morning of this flood and he has done it with his own two hands in about as long as it takes a man to walk from that trestle to the end of that row and back.

Nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall what his knot is, and nobody in that room asked Adrian Vale where he had got the knot he was working at, and he did not say, and he worked at it at the near corner of that table for about as long as it takes a man to walk from that window to the far wall of that room and back.

---

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the string.

"Somebody is going to have to open that knot."

"**A knot is not a thing you open, it is a thing two people made and neither of them learned it off the other one, and I have untied things on this road for nine years that I made myself and I do not know how to undo that one, and that sheet came down that road four hundred miles and it is going back up it and there is not one person in this room who can be shown who tied that string.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. Nobody in that room said one word about the four hundred miles except that one, and about four people in that room had worked out that a sheet came down that road and about four had not, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall kept his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not turn round from that window.

---

Adrian Vale undid his own knot with the nails of his left hand and did it again, and it came out the same different one a second time, and he left the loop of that string lying on the near corner of that table over the folded sheet, so that the string went across the wood and across the near edge of the sheet and over the side of it and down.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living got up off that bench and reached for the strap of the satchel, and the loop of that string was lying across the strap where the strap comes over a shoulder. He lifted the loop off it with two fingers and it was over the strap again where he had it, and then he took the whole of it in his hand and worked at it with the flat of his thumb nail while the string went slack and then tight again and then slack, and he did not say the word knot while he did it.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"You took a while at that."

"**I did not ask him whose hands it was and I did not ask you either and I am not going to ask anybody in this room, and a thing a man has to work at with a nail is a thing a man does not have to work at, and I have been carrying things for nine years and I have never had to think about one.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked him, and nobody in that room said that he was right. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board looked down at that string once and then out of the window and said nothing at all, and about four people in that room had watched a man work at a knot and about four had not, and not one of the two of them said one word to the other half.

That knot is in a string across the near corner of a folded sheet on a table under one window, and two men in that room have had their hands on that knot this morning and neither of them has said one word to the other about it.
