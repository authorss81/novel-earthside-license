# Chapter 0521 — Two Figures On A Step And A String Nobody Claimed

The store's board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put both hands flat on the face of it and turned it over once and laid it down across the mouth of the trough with the figures up and the wet side to the air. The trough was standing against that wall on its own spot and it was full to its stone lip. He worked the rag across that board from the bottom left-hand corner to the top right-hand one and did the second half of it the same way, and the water came off it grey and went over the stone lip and along the flags.

He put the rag on the stone lip of that step, where it goes every morning, and carried the bucket to the channel and back and carried it again and tipped the second one in as well, and then he carried the board up and put it on its two nails again. There are three sets of figures on it and not one of the three has been rubbed out, and the top of that board is where his washing stops every morning, and the wall above that line is still the colour it was the morning before the water came.

---

At the near end of eleven miles of flats a woman put a tin down on a stack of boards under a tarred cloth and went up the row with a slate under her arm, and the weather came in off the low ground in the middle of the morning and stayed in. She marked the shut doorways one at a time with the flat of her finger while she was still standing in front of each one. She came back down about the fifth hour and she wrote one figure on the face of that slate that had nothing on it, and she wrote the day beside it, and she did not rub out the three that were on the other faces of it.

Those four figures are of the same kind of thing. They were counted on the same eleven miles by the same person on four mornings, and they do not agree with one another, and nothing about that has been said out loud by anybody in this city.

Adrian Vale took the store's chalk out of the box on that step, and the box has a lid that does not shut and that chalk is not his, and he put a mark on each of the four corners of the top step with it and then laid a string along the front edge of that step and weighted the near end of the string with a stone so that it lay straight, and he took the string up again and laid it along the back edge of the same step and weighted that end with the same stone. The top of that step is worn down at the front lip by a hand's width of depth where the water has been going over it, and the front edge of that step comes out shorter than the back edge of it by the depth of that wear. The two did not come out the same.

He wrote both of them on the stone of that step with the stub of that chalk, the first one low down and the second one above it, and he wrote the day beside each of them, and then he put that stub back in the box on that step, and there was more of that chalk in the box than he took out of it. He put the string back across the top of that step and put the stone on it and went off up the row and did not stand there to see whether it stayed.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that row at about the sixth hour with his barrow behind him, and the front wheel of that barrow went over the string on that step and the string came up on the wheel and round it twice and took the stone off the step with it. He stopped the barrow in the middle of that row and took a knife out and cut the string off the wheel in two places and put the two pieces of it and the stone down on the flags, and the stone went off the edge of those flags into the channel at the near end of the row and stayed there.

A woman standing at the far end of that row said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"That string was not on your barrow this morning."

"**It was not on my barrow this morning and it is not mine and it is in that channel now along with a stone, and there is chalk on the top of that step that I did not put there, and I am not going to ask anybody about it because I have got a barrow to get up that row with.**"

Nobody at that end of the market asked him about the string. Nobody asked about the chalk on that step. About four people at that end of the market had seen a man cut a string off his own wheel and about four had not, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the step with his back to the row and did not turn round.

---

About the eighth hour she came down that row with the tin in her two hands and the slate under her arm and she stepped up onto the top step of that store to get past a channel of water that had come off the flags and gone along the length of them. The flat of her left boot went down onto the lower of the two figures and the chalk came off the stone onto the sole of the boot, and the upper of the two was still on the step when she had gone, and she did not look down at the step and she did not stop and she carried it the length of that row to the near gate on the sole of her boot.

Not one person in that row said a word to her about the morning or about the board.

A woman at a stall two along said it out loud, to the flags.

"There is writing on that step."

"**There is writing on that step and it is not on my board and I have not put it there and I am not going to ask who has, and the water off that board goes over the top of it every morning and always has.**"

The trough was against that wall on its own spot with the bucket standing in it and the rag on the stone lip, and the board was on its two nails above them with all three sets of figures on it, and the wall above the top of that board is as dirty at the eighth hour as it is at the fourth hour on any morning of this flood.

The top step of that store is where it was at the fourth hour and the string is not on it, and a woman has carried one of the two figures off it eleven miles of flats on the sole of a boot and put the tin down on a stack of boards under a tarred cloth, and the other one is on the stone of that step in the chalk where the stone is worn down, and nobody in this city has been told whose hands laid that string there.
