# Chapter 0505 — Four Hours On A Table And One Sentence About A Page

The hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the back of the room over the market, a Tuesday, and the door was shut from the fourth hour of the morning. About nine people were in that room by the middle of it and none of them had been sent for anything. A sheet of paper was lying on the table under the window with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and the one place was empty. It had come down a road four hundred miles long in a satchel with a flap and it was on that table and it was going to stay on that table until the end of the day, and nothing in that room was going to be done about it before then.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living sat at the far end of that bench with the satchel between his two knees and his hands on it, and it was the satchel he had carried up that stair and it was on his knees. He was not asked what he had carried. Everything he has carried up that road in this flood is still shut — a return, a sheet that came up on the four hundred and first day of it, a sheet on the four hundred and fifty-first, and the sheets that went out to places — and he has not opened one of them, and nobody in this flood has ever put the question to him, and nobody in that room put it to him on that morning.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board standing face in against his own trestle a foot further along the row than it had been standing on Friday, with the bare back of it out in that row, and he was not trading off it that morning and his hands were in his pockets. The piece of chalk of his own was in his inside breast pocket and did not come out of it.

---

At about the fifth hour a man at the bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"There were seven of them went out in this flood."

"**I know how many went out. What I want to know is what this one is, and there is nobody in this room who is going to be able to tell me, and that is the arrangement and it is not an accident somebody made this morning.**"

A woman near the step said the thing that is said to him and not to him.

"Then nobody is going to thank you for saying it."

"**I have asked in advance not to be thanked, and I am asking now, and I have said what is wrong with a page in about as many words as it takes and I would rather somebody in this room took the sheet and looked at it than stood here being pleased with me about it.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody improved on it either, and about four of the nine people in that room had been looking at the one empty place on that sheet since the fourth hour and about four of them had not, and not one of the two of them said a word to the other about it.

---

The four hours went. The light came along the flags and reached the near side of the bench and then went up the wall behind that table and stopped under the window, and the sheet lay on the wood through all of it with its one place empty and the window behind it. A man at the near end of the bench put his thumb on the edge of the table beside it and left it there about as long as it takes a man to count nine and took it off again. A woman at the far end got up twice and went to the window and came back and sat down. The man of about thirty-four stood at the window with his back to the table and his hands in his pockets and looked out of it at the end of the row, and in the row there was a handcart standing with its bar down, and in that cart there was a coil of rope that had been used and a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, and neither the rope nor the mallet was in anybody's hand and the man they belong to was not in that room.

Nobody in that room wrote anything down that day. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one what he had carried. The door stayed shut.

---

At about the eighth hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register picked that sheet up off the table with both hands and turned it so that the heading went to the wall and the one empty place under it came out facing the room, and carried it across and put it on the shelf above where the register stands, and came back and shut the door behind her. The man of about thirty-one sat on with the satchel between his knees and did not get up to carry it, and he is not the man who carried it to the shelf.

There were four hours between the fourth hour of the morning and the eighth hour of the day on the hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month, and a sheet of paper with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading lay on a table in a room over that market for every one of them, and about nine people stood in that room through all four of them, and not one of the two of them who had looked at that one place had put anything in it.
