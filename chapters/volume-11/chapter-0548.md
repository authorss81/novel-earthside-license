# Chapter 0548 — One Man At A Window And A Question The Room Did Not Hear

The man of about fifty-nine who walks the four-hundred-mile road came up that stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, with a hurdle he made in one hand and a bar he replaced under his arm, and he put the hurdle up against the wall by the door at the foot of that stair and stood at the window with the bar still under his arm. The date was in chalk on that door and there is no name on it and there never has been one. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

He has walked that road four times in nine years. It is four hundred miles from this city to a town that has a public register in it, and it is a measure of a distance and it is not a place anybody in this room has stood at the end of, and he has never once walked it for anybody and nobody in this city has ever asked him to and he has not gone down it since three people went down it with a barrow each on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and nobody in that room asked him about those three and nobody in that room is going to.

Out of that window the store's board hangs on its two nails on the wall of the market below it, and there are three sets of figures on it and none of the three has been rubbed out. The left-hand set of them is the account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and it came down that road in an office's own hand, and the other two were put on in chalk in this city and the two of them do not agree with one another. The stone above the top edge of that board is the colour it has been the whole of this flood and nothing has gone over it since the morning it was washed.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the side of that room at about the middle of that morning, past the back of the bench, and stopped beside the man of about fifty-nine at that window, and she said it to him and not to the room and not to the window, quietly, so that it went as far as the two of them and stopped there.

"**A word in a count, what is it for, when the only thing anybody is going to use it for is making two numbers into the same number.**"

He did not answer it. The room did not hear it.

---

Nobody in that room thanked her for asking it and nobody in that room told her it was a good question and nobody in that room told her it was a bad one. Nobody in that room heard one word of it, and about half of them went on with what they had been doing, and about half of them stopped for a moment and then went on. The man of about fifty-nine who walks that road stood with his hand on the sill and the bar under his arm and did not look at her and did not look at the room, and he stood there long past the point where a man standing at a window either says something or does not, and then he put his thumb on the sill and took it off again and said nothing whatever.

Nobody in that room has been told what he was thinking at the time and nobody in this city has an instrument for being told it. This question has been asked in this room before, on another morning, by another person, to another man, and it was not answered then either, and nobody in that room this morning knew that it had been asked before and about four of them would not have believed it.

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table she came back to.

"You have come back to that table and you have not said what you asked him."

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said the thing that is said, to the table, when she got back to it.

"**I asked that man a question at that window and he did not answer it and I am not going to say it again in this room, and I am not going to say it better than it was said the last time it was said in this room either, because it was said to a man and not to a room then and it is to a man and not to a room now.**"

Nobody in that room asked her what she had asked him and nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for it.

---

The register was on its shelf behind that bench all morning with its spine to the wall and its cover shut and it was not opened, and the second slate on that shelf stood on its edge with its face in to the wall with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf and hers did not go near it. No figure was put on the board outside that window at any hour of that morning and nobody in that row said which of the three sets of figures on it is the one anybody should be reading.

At about the eleventh hour the man of about fifty-nine who walks that road took his bar from under his arm, put it against the wall beside the hurdle he made, and went down that stair and out along the flags of that row and up the road at the near gate. Nobody in that row asked him what the bar was for and nobody in that row asked him where he was going and nobody in that room said one word about where he was going.

The man of about fifty-nine who walks that road is at that window with his thumb on the sill and his back to the room, and the question he did not answer is in the mouth of one person in this city and in nobody else's.
