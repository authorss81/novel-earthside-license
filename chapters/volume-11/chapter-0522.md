# Chapter 0522 — Four On A Slate In The Order They Were Made And A Barrow Going Up The Road

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the working slate down on the table under the one window at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and turned it over once and laid it on the table with the one face of it that takes a figure facing up, and the register itself stayed on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut. Behind the register a second slate stands on its edge with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Neither of them was picked up. Neither of them was turned over. Nothing was written on either of them. About nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

She set four figures out on that face one under another, and she set them in the order they were made in, the first of them at the head of the slate and the last of them at the foot of it, and she wrote the day beside each one in the same hand as the figure. There was a hand's width of clean slate above the first of them and nothing was put in it, and none of the four was rubbed out and none of the four was a correction of any of the other three.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"They are not the same and you have put them a hand's width apart."

"**They are not the same and they are not going to be made the same, and the order they are in is the order they were made in, and the order is the only thing about those four that anybody in this room can be sure of this morning.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. Nobody in that room said one word about what the four of them are a count of, and nobody asked her, and nobody in that room chose one of the four of them, and nobody in that room said which one of them was going anywhere. About four people in that room had worked out that the four of them were made by one person on four mornings and about four had not, and not one of those four said a word to her or to each other.

The chalk she wrote them with was the stub off the shelf and she put it down on the table when she had done the fourth one, and the four of them stood on the face of that slate in a column with a hand's width between each of them and the day beside each of them, and the face of that slate was a working face and that slate goes in and out of that room on a barrow.

A woman at the end of that bench nearest the door said the thing that is said, to the table, later than the first of them and not louder.

"That slate goes in and out of this room on a barrow."

"**It does, and it goes up that road and comes back down it, and nobody has ever written down what it is for going up and nothing is going to be written down about it this morning.**"

Nobody in that room asked her what that slate goes up that road for, and she did not say, and about four people in that room had worked out that a slate that goes up a road and comes back is a different thing from a slate that lies on a table and about four had not.

---

At about the ninth hour a woman took that slate off that table and carried it down that stair and out along the row of flags to the near gate, and pushed it up the shut road out past the last named house on the bed of a barrow with the day written on it face up. That road is four hundred miles long and it is a measure of a distance and not a place anybody in this city has stood, and the fence runs along the top of it from the corner by the last named house to the corner where the road turns, and it is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on them with the same cord, and nobody measured the fence and nobody measured the corner in it.

The barrow stopped at the top of that road and stood there about as long as a man stands at a gate, and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road. A party of four set out and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and three went down it with a barrow each on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and the three of them were one hundred and thirty-one days out from this city at that moment. Nobody counted anything on that barrow and nobody wrote anything on it, and it came back down the same road with the slate still face up on the bed of it and the four of them in the order they were in when it went up.

---

That slate has been up the shut road out past the last named house and back down it again and it came back the same way up, and along the top of that road there are sixteen willow posts and eleven withies and a corner in it that nobody has taken a cord to, and nobody in this city has been shown what is at the far end of that road, and on the face of that slate, face up on a table under a window in a room over a market in this city, there are four figures of the same kind of thing standing one under another in the order they were made in with a hand's width of clean slate above the first of them and nothing in it.
