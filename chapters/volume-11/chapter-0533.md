# Chapter 0533 — A Chair Up A Stair And A Rope Nobody Lifted

He came up out of that row about the ninth hour carrying a chair by both of its ends, on the two hundred and eighteenth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. He carried it the length of that room and set it down against the wall at the foot of the bench and took his two hands off it, and the chair stood where he had put it and nobody sat in it and nobody asked him where it had come from. The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of that stair, and nobody has ever put a name under it, and the nine people or thereabouts in that room had every one of them come up on their own account.

---

Behind the bench the register stood on its shelf with its cover shut and its spine to the wall, and it stayed on that shelf all morning. The second slate stood on its edge on the same shelf with its face in to the wall, a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and the shelf itself is on two brackets and the back bracket is the higher one. Nobody picked that slate up, nobody turned it over and nothing was written on it, and nobody went near that shelf at all except the keeper's, who did not either. The ledge under the second slate keeps its straight break in the dust with a soft edge on one side of it and a hard edge on the other.

At the end of that bench the man of about fifty-seven was sitting with his hands flat on the table in front of him and a length of new rope over the back of his chair, and that rope has been over the back of that chair every morning of this flood and it was over the back of it that morning, and no hand in that room lifted it and nobody in that room said one word about it. Nobody asked that man anything at all. He was not asked where the fence is and he was not told anything about it and he was not looking at the rope.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and its face turned out into the room. Nineteen rates stand on that board in three columns, the rows running across, and at the foot of the middle column there is bare board about as wide as two fingers and nothing whatever in it. Nobody asked him about that space. Nobody asked him what had been in it.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the shelf behind him.

"You have been at this end of this bench nine years and I have been at this end of it nine years."

"**I have counted your prices out loud across a market row for nine years and you have never once asked me what a rate is, and I have never once asked you what is on that space on your board, and neither of us has ever asked the other anything at all, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it in front of anybody.**"

Not one person in that room improved on that, and not one of them thanked him for it, and the two of them went on sitting at the two ends of that bench with the whole of the room in between them. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the far end of that bench with his own piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, and he did not say one word back to that, and nobody in that room asked either of the two of them anything else.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the window with the satchel with the flap on the bench beside him and the strap of it over the back of that bench and his own hands on his knees, and nobody in that room asked him what was in that satchel and he did not put his hands in it. The chair that was carried up that stair stood against that wall with nothing on it and nothing under it and nobody sitting in it, and the man who had carried it up did not look at it again for the rest of that morning.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the bench.

"You have all been in this room nine years and not one of you knows what the other ones are doing."

"**Somebody in this room has to be first at something, and it is not going to be me, and the man at this end of the bench has not asked me a question in nine years and neither have I asked him one, and there is no instrument in this city that puts two people in a room four days before either of them says anything.**"

Nobody thanked her for that and nobody improved on it, and nobody in that room said that the two men at the ends of that bench had been in the same market for nine years.

The chair is standing against the wall at the foot of that bench with nothing on it and nobody in it, and the length of new rope is over the back of another chair at the other end of that same bench and has not moved all morning, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board is still sitting at the near end of that bench with his board under his arm and the space about two fingers wide on the face of it not filled, and nobody in that room asked him about it.
