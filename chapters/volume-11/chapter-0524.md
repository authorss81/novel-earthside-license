# Chapter 0524 — A Room Finding Out On The Morning It Is Being Asked And A Box On A Step

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the sheet with the empty place down on the table under the one window at about the sixth hour on the two hundred and ninth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and put the flat of her hand on the wood beside it and left it there, and about nine people came up that stair at the back of that room after that and none of them had been sent for anything. The sheet has a heading at the top of it and under that heading there is one place ruled, and there is one place only, and the one place is empty, and it was empty when that sheet came up the road and it is empty now. Nobody in that room put a hand in it and nobody in that room said one word about what the figure that is going in it is a figure of.

---

This is the fourth time a room in this city has found out what it was being asked on the morning it was being asked it. Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and every one of them was written four days before the room it was for, and there has been no eighth one, and nobody in that room was going to be told anything in advance of anything, and the room did what a room does when nobody has told it anything, which is to work it out while it is happening and to sound as though it had known.

Out of the window at the back of that room the store's board is on its two nails on the wall of the market below it, and the account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood is on the left of that board and a figure of this city's own in chalk is in the middle of it and a second figure of this city's own is on the right of that, and none of the three has been rubbed out and the two in chalk do not agree with each other, and the wall above the top of that board is the colour it is every morning of this flood because the washing of that wall stops where the board stops. About four people at that window had looked down at that board and about four had not, and not one of the nine said which of the three is the one anybody should be reading.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Nobody told us what this is for."

"**Nobody has told this room anything and this room is working it out on the morning of it, and a room that has not been told cannot be told, and I have been in this market nine years and there has never once been a day off for it.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said it with his own board still face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row and the bare back of it out in the row, and he came down the flags to the step where that store's chalk stands in its box with a lid that does not shut and about a thumb's length of it showing, and he did not put his hand over that box and he did not stop at it. The piece of chalk of his own was in his own inside breast pocket and had not been out of that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood is the day that piece of chalk went into it, and it was not out of it that morning.

---

At about the ninth hour the two hands that may carry that sheet carried it down that stair and out along the row of flags and back up it again, and it went down with the one place under the heading showing nothing whatever in it and it came back up with the one place under the heading showing nothing whatever in it. About four people in that room had counted the places on that sheet twice by that hour and had got the same answer both times, and about four had not counted them at all, and no hand went into the one place under that heading and there is no second place under that heading and there has never been one.

The box on that step has a thumb's length of the store's chalk showing in it and the lid of it does not shut, and the piece in the inside breast pocket of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has been in that pocket since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and nobody in that row used either of them and nobody in that row asked that man where a form goes after it has been read out, because nobody in that row has ever asked him anything.

A woman at the near end of that bench said it out loud, to the step and not to the man.

"There is chalk in this city and none of it is going to be used this morning."

"**There is a piece of my own in this pocket and there is a thumb's length of another kind in that box on that step, and neither of them is going to be used this morning and nobody in this room is going to ask me for mine.**"

Nobody asked him for it and nobody improved on him and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not raise her eyes from the one place under that heading when it was said, and nobody in that room said a word about where a form goes after it has been read out.

The one place under that heading was still empty at the eleventh hour of that morning, and about nine people had gone down that stair at the back of that room knowing what they were being asked and about four of them had worked out the other half of it, and the box is still on that step with the lid of it standing open off it and a thumb's length of the store's chalk showing in it, and nobody in that market put a hand in that box.
