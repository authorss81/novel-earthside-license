# Chapter 0534 — The First Question And One Answer That Goes Nowhere

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and he put it against the bench with the face of it to the wall, which is the way he puts it down when he is not selling anything. The two hundred and nineteenth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair. There is no name on that door and there has never been one. The light came in at the one window of that room and not at the door, it was a good light, and there were about nine people in that room by that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair about a quarter of an hour after he had and stood at the near end of that bench with his hands in his pockets. His own board was two stalls along in the row with its face in against his own trestle and the bare back of it out in that row, and he had come up without it. The man of about thirty-nine did not look round when that door opened and neither did anybody else in that room, and about four people in that room noticed that the man of about thirty-nine had put his board down face in and about four did not.

There are nineteen rates on the face of that board in three columns down it with the rows going across, and along the bottom of the middle column of it there is a space about two fingers wide with nothing whatever in it. A name was in that space in his own hand about nine years ago and he took it off himself and he has never said what it was to anybody, and nobody in this city has ever asked him what he calls a thing he has never said.

---

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the man who came up without a board.

"Nobody has ever asked you anything about that board."

"**Nobody has, and nobody is going to, and that is not what I came up here about this morning, so I would rather somebody asked me the thing they actually wanted than that we went round it for a quarter of an hour first.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stood where he was with his hands in his pockets and looked at the middle column of that board and did not say one word for about as long as it takes a man to count nine, and nobody in that room said anything at all while he did it, and nobody in that room had any idea what was going to be asked.

He took his hands out of his pockets and put one of them flat on the edge of the bench near that board and he said it once, to the man and not to the room, and it was the first question either of those two men had ever asked the other, and he said in the same breath that he would rather nobody thanked him for it.

"**What do you call a thing you have never said.**"

Nobody in that room thanked him for asking it. Nobody improved on the question and nobody answered it for him and nobody in that room said one word about it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not look up from the window when he asked it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood up off that bench and turned round so that he was facing the man who had asked it, and he did not look at the board while he said it and he did not touch it, and he said it out loud once, in his own mouth, in the local language, to a man and not to a room, and it was four words long.

"**What a man takes and never says.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying it. Nobody improved on it and nobody asked him a second question about it and nobody asked him to say it again in a different way, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not repeat it and did not write it down and did not say that he had heard it before and did not say one word about it for the rest of that morning, and about four people in that room heard the whole of it and about four heard the first half of it and not one of the nine of them said what it was for.

The two of them did not discuss it. The two of them did not stand in that room and work out what it was about, and nobody in that room asked either of them a second question, and about four people in that room worked out that the two men at the two ends of that bench had said something to each other and about four worked out nothing at all.

---

At about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took his board up off the bench where it was standing with its face in to the wall, turned it over, and put it back under his arm with the face of it out, and went down that stair and out into the row, and nobody in that room said one word about his going and nobody said he had said anything before he went. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stayed where he was with his hands in his pockets for the rest of that morning, and the register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and it did not come off that shelf.

The board with the nineteen rates on it went back down that stair under his arm and is the same board that came up it, and a room of about nine people is sitting in there in the light off the one window, and two men at the two ends of that bench have said one thing to each other and have not said one word to each other since, and the space about two fingers wide on the face of that board is still not filled.
