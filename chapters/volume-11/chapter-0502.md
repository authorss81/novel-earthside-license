# Chapter 0502 — A Register On A Table And A Board Under An Arm

The man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's board had the bucket standing in the trough at one end of that market at about the ninth hour on the hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, with the rag over the stone lip of it, and the board on its two nails above him with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out. The wall above that board was not washed. Two stalls along, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board standing face in against his own trestle with the bare back of it out in the row, and there is a place on the front of his coat about the size of a hand where a piece of chalk of his own has been going in and out of an inside breast pocket for the whole of this flood, and it is lighter than the cloth around it. That piece of chalk had not been out of that pocket since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and it did not come out of it on the hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the stair at the back of the room over that market with both hands under the register. The hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her and there was no name on that door. She had it by the spine and the fore-edge, the way a person carries a thing that is not theirs to spill, and she set it on the table with the cover up and the spine away from her. Behind the bench the shelf it had come off stood with nothing on it except the second slate, and the head of that slate had a day scratched across it and nothing whatever under that day.

She opened it on the table. Three columns ran down every page of it. The one on the right was empty from the top of the page to the bottom and had been empty on purpose since before the water came. The one in the middle had four lines in it. Three of them had a day in them and nothing under the day. The fourth had a day in it and a figure under the day, and the figure was of this city's own and it was not the office's, and it had gone in on the day the register-keeper says out loud in that room whenever anybody in that room wants to hear it.

About four people were in that room at the ninth hour and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room. There were nineteen rates on the face of it in his own hand, and they had been on that face in four markets for nine years. There was a space on it about two fingers wide where a name had been in that same hand about nine years ago, and he had taken it off himself, and he has never said so to anybody, and the space has not been filled.

He stood at the near end of the bench with his thumb along the top rail of that board and said the four sentences, and he said them to the middle of the room and not to the register.

"**I have not opened that book in nine years and I am not opening it this morning, and a man who has not opened a book in nine years has no business saying what is in it.**"

A woman at the far end of the bench said the thing that is said to him and not to the register.

"Then do not say what is in it."

"**I will tell you the one thing I know about it, and it is that there is a day in every line of it and there is a figure under one line out of four, and I have worked that out from the door of this room and not from inside it.**"

"**There is a space on this board about two fingers wide and there is not a name in it, and I took it off myself, and I am telling you that I took it off and not what it was.**"

A man at the step said the thing that is said.

"You could fill it in again."

"**That is four sentences and there is no fifth one, and nobody in this room has asked me a question and I would like it kept at that.**"

Nobody in that room asked him a question. A woman at the far end of the bench put her hand flat on the table and left it there, and about four of the people in that room had looked at the space on that board that morning and about four of them had not, and not one of the two groups said a word about it to the other.

---

The register-keeper wrote nothing in that book that morning. She put her hand on the fourth line with the day in it and left it there for a while, and then she shut the cover, and turned the spine towards her and lifted the book off the table with both hands and carried it back to the shelf behind the bench. She set it down on that shelf with its spine to the wall in the place it had been standing in. The second slate was behind where it had been and in front of where it had been, and it had not moved, and the day across the head of it was the day it had always had across the head of it, and nothing whatever stood under that day. Her own hands came off that shelf and went to her sides. No other hand in that room went near it, and nothing on it was picked up and nothing on it was turned over and nothing was put down on it.

A man at the near end of the bench put both his hands out in the air in the direction of that shelf and brought them back to the table and put them flat on it, and said nothing about it, and nobody said anything to him about it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine went back down that stair with the board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the row as he came off the bottom step. He went along the end of the market and past the trough where the bucket was standing in the water, and the man who kept the bucket did not turn round and did not look up. Two stalls along the man of about thirty-four put the flat of his own hand on the top of the bare back of his own board and kept it there until the other one was past the end of the row, and the piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket did not come out of it.

Not one person at either end of that row used his name that morning, and the space on his board about two fingers wide went out of that market with him in it, and he has not said what was in it to anybody since.
