# Chapter 0506 — The Reader Says What It Is For

The hundred and ninety-first day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the back of the room over the market, a Wednesday, and there was no name on that door. About nine people were in that room by about the ninth hour and none of them had been sent for anything, and there was no notice in this flood that had told any of them to come, and there was no instrument in four hundred miles that could have given that room four days' warning of what it was being asked on the morning it was being asked it.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up that stair at about the ninth hour with the satchel with the flap under his arm and put it on the table under the window with the flap towards the wall, and undid the strap of it, and did not open it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had a bone in her hand and the register was on the shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and behind it the second slate lay with its face in to the wall and a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

---

She took the sheet out of the satchel herself and unfolded it on that table and put the bone down on the long crease in it and pressed the bone along the whole length of the fold with the heel of her hand, and the sheet came out of itself and lay flat. There is a heading at the top of it. Under that heading there is one place ruled, and there is one place only, and the one place is empty.

She said what it was for before anybody in that room had said anything, and she said it once, in her own mouth, to that room.

"**It is for one figure of this city. One figure, in the one place under that heading, and it is to be sent back, and I am saying what it is for before anybody in this room asks me what it is for.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"What is it a figure of."

"**I am not going to say what it is a figure of, because the name of the thing is not on the page and I have had it in my own hands twice now and it is not on the page, and a name that is not on a page is not a thing I am going to supply to a room out of my own head.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. She put her hand flat on the table beside the sheet and turned it, so that the heading stood at the far side of that table with its back to the window and the one place ruled under it lay in the middle of the table where the people on the low side of the bench could see into it.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and the nineteen rates on it were on the face of it, and the space about two fingers wide where a name had been in his own hand about nine years ago was not filled and had not been filled. He said the four lines, and he said them to the table.

"**I have looked at that one place from this bench and I could not tell you what it is a figure of, and I have carried a board with nineteen rates on it under my arm for nine years and I do not know what a place is for either.**"

"**What I do know is that a person in this room can get up off that bench and look into that one place, and I am not going to say that the people who did it and the people who did not are two different kinds of person.**"

"**I would put it away and take it out again in a week and see whether it reads the same at the end of that week, and I am not going to be the man who says to do it.**"

A woman near the step said the thing that is said to the bench and not to him.

"Nobody has said that to you in nine years either."

"**Nobody has said that to me in nine years, and you have said it in one morning, and I am not going to improve on what you have just done for me.**"

Nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-nine anything and nobody improved on him, and the nineteen rates on his board were still on the face of it and the space about two fingers wide was still not filled when he sat down.

---

Nobody in that room was asked to do anything that morning. A woman at the far end of that bench came up to the table at about the second hour of the day and stood over the one place with her hand held out above it and did not put it in, and she held it there for about as long as it takes a man to count nine and then took it away and sat down. A man at the near end of the bench put his own finger on the ruled line at the near end of that place and ran it along to the far end of it to see where it stopped, and it stopped where the ruling stopped, and he took his hand off it. No hand went into the one place under that heading and nothing was put under it, and there is no second place under that heading and there never has been, and about four people in that room had counted the places on that sheet twice by the middle of the day and had got the same answer both times.

The sheet on the shelf above the register, which has a heading and one place under it and a figure in that one place, was not brought down off that shelf and was not put on that table, and the sheet on the table was not carried over to that shelf and put beside it, and nobody in that room laid the two of them side by side or said one word out loud about how the two of them were the same shape or not.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall stood at the window with his hands in his pockets and the piece of chalk of his own in his own inside breast pocket, and he did not come away from that window. The man of about thirty-one sat at the far end of the bench with his hands on the satchel and did not put his hands in it. Behind the bench the second slate with its day across the head of it and nothing under that day was not picked up and not turned over and not written on, and no hand but the register-keeper's went anywhere near that shelf, and she did not go near it either.

---

The bone that put the fold down in that sheet is still lying on the table where she left it, and the one place under the heading on that sheet is still empty, and about nine people went down the stair at the back of that room after it with nothing in their hands, and the woman who read it out said what it was for once and did not say it again.
