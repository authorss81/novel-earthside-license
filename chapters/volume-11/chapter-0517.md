# Chapter 0517 — A Plank Two Thirds Of The Way And A Woman Going Back Over It

The weather came in off the low ground before the light on the two hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and it came in hard and it did not let up all morning. There is a channel of wet silt the length of those flats, and the water in it stood about nine feet across where it crosses from the first row to the second, and nobody in this city has ever taken a cord to it and nobody in this city is going to. Adrian Vale got the near end of a plank off the ground against the end wall of the first row at about the second hour and put his shoulder under it and went into the channel with it.

---

The water was at his chest by the fourth step and it was not cold. He got the plank under him and went on along the bottom of it with his two hands on the wood and the plank across his body, and the plank's far end went down under the silt at about the third of the way across and stopped. He got his left hand flat under that far end and lifted it and pushed, and the plank came up out of the silt and went forward a hand's width and stopped again, and he did it five times, and each time the bottom of the channel gave under it and the plank went back.

The second row of the flats is at the far side of that channel and there is a sill along the bottom of it where the water has cut down through the mud of a hundred years, and that sill on that morning was under about a foot of water and moving. He put the far end of the plank against that sill at about the seventh hour and it stayed there about as long as it takes a man to count nine, and then the whole channel came down on the plank from the side he was standing on and turned it. He was no further forward than the morning before.

---

He got himself out of that channel at about the eighth hour with his hands on the flags at the near side and his coat full of water and the plank lying across two thirds of the width of it, the far end of it under and the near end of it up on the flags where he had pulled it from. He did not lift it out. He left it where it was, because a plank across a channel two thirds of the way is not a thing anybody can use from either side, and it is not a route, and nobody in this city was going to be told by him that it was one. He lay on the flags at the near side with his back to the stack of boards until about the ninth hour and got up and went and stood at the end of the row with his hands in his pockets and watched the water go over that plank and under it.

---

Nobody asked him for it and nobody knew whose hands had been on it. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came along the row at about the ninth hour with his barrow behind him and got as far as the near side of that channel, and a barrow cannot go into that water with a wheel on it, and he stood there a while and then he put the shafts of that barrow up and got the front wheel of it up onto the near end of that plank and went across the two thirds of it standing on the wheel with the barrow coming over behind him, and he came back the same way.

"**That plank was not there on Thursday and I have been standing at this end of the row since the sixth hour with a barrow I cannot take across, so somebody has done me a service this morning and it was not done for me.**"

A woman at the near end of that row said the thing that is said, to the water.

"It is somebody's plank."

"**It is laid across two thirds of that channel and it is not going to reach the far side, and I have got a barrow to get to the end of that row with, and I am not going to stand here working out whose it is.**"

He did not look at the plank and he did not look down at the flags on the near side, and nobody in that row said whose hands had been on it.

---

She came along the row about the tenth hour with the slate under her arm and the tin in her two hands, and she went over the two thirds of that plank with her back to the water and she came back over it, and going back the near end of it had gone a hand's width from where it had been and she put her foot on the end of it and shoved it back to the stone and took her foot off it and went on up the row.

The plank is lying across about two thirds of the width of that channel where there was nothing to lie across a week ago, and it has about a foot of the wet sill of the second row under its far end and no more of that sill than it had before he started, and there is a woman going back up eleven miles of flats in worse weather with a slate under her arm and nothing on it yet.
