# Chapter 0503 — A Form That Came Up The Road And Stayed

The hundred and eighty-eighth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the back of the room over the market, a Sunday, and there was no name on that door. About four people were in that room by about the tenth hour and none of them had been sent for anything. The register stood on the shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and behind it the second slate lay with its face in to the wall, a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Under that shelf there is a chest of drawers with a lock on the top one that has not been turned since before the water came, and the key of it hangs on a nail behind the bench where a person has to reach over the bench to get at it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register got the key off the nail and unlocked the top drawer with it and shut the drawer and put the key back on the nail. In the top drawer there was a folded sheet of paper and a piece of cord and nothing else. She took the sheet out and shut the drawer on the cord and did not lock it, and carried the sheet in both hands and put it on the table under the window.

There was a heading at the top of it. Under that heading there was one place ruled, and there was one place only, and there was a figure in that one place, and there was nothing else on that sheet anywhere. It had come down a road four hundred miles long in a satchel with a flap, folded twice with a bone, and the crease across the middle of it is the crease it came out of that satchel in.

She said what she had found out loud before anybody in that room had looked at it, and she said it in about nine words.

"**It came up the road on a Thursday and it has not gone anywhere, and there is a figure in its one place and it is still in this city.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"Then it wants sending back."

"**I am not going to say whether it wants sending back, because nobody in this flood has ever said one word to me about what happens to a page after a person has read it out, and I am not going to invent an answer in a room with about four people in it.**"

Nobody improved on that. She turned the sheet over once and turned it back, and put her two hands flat on the table on either side of it and left them there. A woman at the near end of that table got up out of her seat first. She came round the end of the table, stood over the sheet with her two hands behind her back, and looked down at the heading and the one place and the figure in it for about as long as it takes a man to count nine. A man at the far end got up after her and stood on the other side of that table and put his own thumb on the edge of the place the figure was in and took it off again, and sat down. About four people in that room had been up off that bench and looking at that sheet by then, and about four had not, and the register-keeper had not moved from the head of the table.

---

The register was on the shelf behind the bench the whole of that morning with the cover shut, and it was not opened, and the sheet on the table was not put down beside it and was not held up beside it and was not compared with it by anybody in that room. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his own coat on and the piece of chalk of his own in his own inside breast pocket, and he said two sentences.

"**That page has a heading and one place under it and the one place has a figure in it, and I have been looking at a wall at the bottom of that stair that has three sets of figures on it and a heading for none of them.**"

"**I am not going to say another word about either of those things, and I would be obliged if nobody in this room put them beside each other this morning.**"

Nobody did. The man of about thirty-four put his hand flat on the sill of that window and left it there and looked out of it at the end of the market for a while, and the register-keeper stood at the table with the sheet in front of her and her own hands on either side of it, and about four people in that room had worked out that the two of them were not being put side by side on purpose and not one of those four said so.

---

The sheet went back into the top drawer of that chest, under the cord, and the drawer was shut and not locked, and the key went back on its nail behind the bench. Then the register-keeper took the register off the shelf with both hands, and put it down on the bench, and put the second slate down on the table where the sheet had been, and she did not turn the slate over and did not put anything on it and did not pick it up again, and she put the register back on the shelf with its spine to the wall.

The sheet was not in the drawer by the end of that morning. It was on the shelf, in the space above where the register stood, at the height of a person's shoulder, with the heading at the top and the one place under it and the figure in that one place, and the two long creases of the bone fold across it.

A man at the door said the thing that is said.

"Anybody coming up that stair can see that from the bottom step."

"**Yes, and that is where I have put it, and I am not going to say one word to you about why.**"

Nobody asked her why. The door at the back of that room carries a day in chalk and no name, and there is a shelf above a book on it with a sheet on that shelf that came up a road four hundred miles long, and from the bottom of that stair the sheet is the first thing in that room anybody can see, and the book is under it and shut.
