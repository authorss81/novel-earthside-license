# Chapter 0542 — A Wall Washed Once And The Figures Left Alone

The store's board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and twenty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and went down into the trough that stands against that wall on its own spot, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put both hands flat on the face of it and turned it over once and laid it across the mouth of that trough with the wet side to the air. He worked the rag across it from the bottom left-hand corner to the top right-hand one and did the second half of it the same way, and the water came off it grey and went over the stone lip and along the flags, and all three sets of figures came out of the wet as clean as they went into it.

---

The trough was full to within a finger of its stone lip. He put the rag on the stone lip of the step, where it goes every morning, and he filled the bucket at the channel and came back and tipped it into the trough, and the water in it stood a finger over the lip and stayed there for as long as it takes to carry a bucket to a channel and back. He did not carry a fourth one. He put the bucket down on the flags inside the step instead of standing it in the trough, which is not where it goes, and it stood there the rest of that morning with its handle out over the stone.

Nothing in that row asked him about anything. Nothing in that row asked him about the bucket. About four people at that end of that market were watching him at that hour and about four were not, and not one of the two groups said one word to the other group about it, and nothing was entered anywhere in that row that morning and nothing went out of that market.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board did not put that board back on its nails straight away. He stood at the foot of that wall and looked up at it, and the top of that board is where his washing stops every morning of this flood, and the whole of the wall above that line has been the colour it was the morning before the water came. He put his left hand flat on the stone at the top edge of that board and took it up the wall with him, palm and fingers together, the whole width of his hand, and he went up the stone with it about as high as a man can reach and stopped, and he took it down a hand's width and did that again, and the water came off that stone grey the way it came off the board and went down the face of it and over the stone lip of the step into the trough.

He did the top of that wall first and came down it a hand's width at a time, the full width of it across, and the rag came down the stone ahead of his hand and he did not hurry it. When he got down to the top edge of that board he stopped with the rag in his hand about the thickness of two fingers above the figures, and he put the rag down on the stone lip of the step without touching that board and without touching the figures, and he turned round and picked the board up off the mouth of the trough and put it on its two nails.

---

Nobody in that row asked him why. Nobody in that row asked him whether that wall had been washed before and nobody thanked him for washing it, and the rest of that row went on doing what they were doing. About four people in that row looked up at that wall after he had come down off it and about four did not, and not one of the two of them said one word to the other half about what he had done.

A man at the end of that row said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"There has not been a morning in this flood when nothing was entered anywhere and nothing went out of anywhere."

A woman at a stall two along said it to the flags, to him and not to the row.

"**There has not, and there is one this morning, and the whole of what has happened in it is that a board came off two nails and went back on them and a man went up a wall he has not gone up before and came down it again and did not touch three sets of figures that would have come off under his hand, and I am not going to tell anybody what any of that is worth.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. Nobody in that row asked the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board a second question about that wall, and nobody has ever asked him one, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall kept his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket two stalls along and said nothing at all about any of it.

A woman at a stall further along said the thing that is said a second time, to the trough.

"That wall is clean and that board is clean and nothing in this row is clean."

The woman at the stall two along said it again, and said it once.

"**That board was never dirty and that wall was, and there is a difference and it is the difference between a thing somebody does to get a figure off a stone and a thing a man does once in a flood with no figure on it at all, and I have watched the whole of it from two stalls along and I have not said one word to him.**"

Nobody improved on that either, and nobody in that row said that she was right, and about four people in that row have worked out that the stone above that board is not the colour it was and about four have not.

---

The light came off the flags at the end of that row at about the ninth hour and went up the face of that wall about as high as it goes on any morning of this flood and stopped there, and the water was still coming off the top of that wall in two places at the ninth hour where the stone has cracks in it, and it was running down the face of that stone into the trough and over the stone lip of the step onto the flags and along them toward the channel at the near end of that row.

That trough has stood against that wall on its own spot through the whole of this flood and has been filled and dipped into every morning and has never once been over its lip, and it was over its lip at the fourth hour and it was over its lip at the ninth hour, and the rag is on the stone lip of the step where it goes every morning, and the stone above the top of that board is not the colour it was the morning before the water came.
