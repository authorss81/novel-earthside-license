# Chapter 0515 — The Second Morning And A Figure That Does Not Come Out The Same

She was at the near gate before the light with the tin in her two hands and the slate under her arm on the two hundredth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and she put the tin down on the sill of the store's step where the store's chalk was in its box, and the slate she carried was not the store's and had a figure on it and the day beside that figure and a second face to it that had not been on it a week ago. The channel had less in it than it had on the Monday and the weather came off the low ground flat rather than in gusts, and she went up the outside of eleven miles of flats and marked the shut doorways the way she marks them, one at a time, with the flat of her finger while she is still standing in front of each one.

She came back down about the fifth hour and stood at the near gate for about as long as it takes a man to count nine before she wrote anything, and then she wrote one figure on the second face of that slate and wrote the day beside it, and she did not rub out the figure on the other face and she did not rub out the day beside that one either. The two figures are of the same kind of thing. They were counted on the same eleven miles by the same person on two mornings four days apart, and they do not agree with each other, and nothing about that is a surprise to anybody who has looked at them and nothing about that has been said out loud by anybody who has.

---

At about the seventh hour the register-keeper came into that room from the far end of it and stood at the side of the table, and the working slate was put down under the window with its two faces up, and the register itself stayed on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and did not come off that shelf that morning.

"**Two of them on one slate and I am going to write the second one under the first one and neither of them is going to be struck out, because a figure that does not agree with the one above it is a figure somebody walked to get and not a figure somebody got wrong.**"

She wrote it with the stub off the shelf and she wrote the day beside it in the same hand and she put the chalk down on the table when she had done, and the two figures stood one under the other with about the width of two fingers between them.

A man at the near end of the bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"You do not know that."

"**I have written it on a slate that goes in and out of this room on a barrow, and the man who carries that barrow is not going to be asked, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it and I have said it.**"

Nobody in that room said one word about the difference between the two figures and nobody in that room was asked anything that morning. Behind the bench the second slate stood on its edge on the shelf with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on, and no hand but the keeper's went near that shelf all day and hers did not go near it either.

---

There is a barrow that comes down the shut road out past the last named house and there is one that goes back up it, and the one that went back up it on that evening had come down it empty in the morning, and there is nothing in this city on the bed of it, and it is not one of the three that went down that road with a barrow each on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood and are one hundred and twenty-four days out from this city at that moment. Nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road. A party of four set out and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and nobody in this city has counted a day since she said so, and nobody is going to.

The barrow went up that road empty with its shafts up and nobody on this side of the last named house watched it go, and there was nothing on the bed of it when it came down that morning either.

---

The two figures stand one under the other on the face of a working slate in a room over a market with about the width of two fingers between them and the day beside each of them in the same hand, and neither of them has been read out in that room and neither of them has been sent anywhere, and the barrow that came down the road that morning is going back up it with nothing on the bed of it.
