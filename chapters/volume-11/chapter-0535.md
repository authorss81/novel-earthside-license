# Chapter 0535 — Nineteen Boards And A Gap At The Fifth Of Them

Adrian Vale got the top board off a stack against the wall at the end of that market at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and twentieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and stood it on its edge on the flags, and then he went back and got the next one, and he did that nineteen times, and the stack went down nineteen boards in about as long as it takes a man to walk from that wall to the end of that row and back. The store's board came off its two nails about the same time at the other end of that row and a man washed it and put it back on them with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and the wall above the top of that board was not washed.

---

He set them in a row along that wall on the flags, the long edge of each one down, with the ends of them all against the wall, and he went along that row and counted it. The gap in it was at the fifth place, and the board that should have gone there was short by about a hand's width, and every other one of the eighteen was the length of the next. He took that fifth board out of its place in the stack-numbering and stood it in that gap, and the row was even, and the fifth board was the short one, and it was short by about a hand's width at the end of it, and he did not take it out of the row and he did not write anything on it and he did not say anything about it.

A man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf came up that row about the sixth hour with a barrow behind him and a tally-board under his arm, and the front wheel of that barrow could not get past a row of nineteen boards lying along the flags in front of a trough, and he put the shafts up and stopped. He stood there a while. He came back about as long as a man stands at a gate, picked the whole row of nineteen up off the flags in two arms, carried it about four paces along the wall and stood it up on end against that wall with the ends of them all down, and the row of nineteen went up the wall in one piece and about a hand's width of daylight showed at the foot of the fifth board of them because that one is short.

A woman standing at the near gate said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"Nineteen of them were not lying along that wall this morning."

"**There were no boards lying along that wall at the sixth hour and there are nineteen of them standing up against it now, and one of them is short, and I have a barrow to get up this row with and I am not going to stand in the middle of a market working out whose they were.**"

Nobody at that end of that market asked him about it and nobody told him. Nobody thanked him for moving the boards and nobody said one word to him about the short one, and about four people in that row saw a man lift nineteen boards off the flags and about four did not.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row with the bare back of it out in the row, and the store's chalk was in its box on the step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of it showing, and the piece of chalk of his own was in his own inside breast pocket where it has been since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and neither of those two chalks was used that morning. Nobody in that row asked that man for his chalk. Nobody in that row has ever asked him for it.

The man of about sixty was at the near gate with the handcart standing behind him and the shafts down in the wet, and there is a coil of rope over the side of the cart and a hazel mallet in the bottom of it with its head bound in cord, and that cart is not one of the three barrows that went down a road four hundred miles long with a man on the wheel of each of them, and it did not go down that road, and nobody in that row asked him anything at all. Nobody in that market has asked him why he put the fence where he put it and nobody is going to.

---

Adrian Vale was six rows up with his back to that row for the rest of that morning. He did not go back to the wall and he did not look at the nineteen boards again and he did not go and get a hand's width of anything to make the fifth one the length of the other eighteen, and nobody in that market knew his hands had been on that stack at all. Nobody thanked him for the row and nobody told him the row was even, and no person in that row was waiting for him to do anything.

The trough is against that wall on its own spot with the bucket standing in it and the rag on the stone lip of the step where the rag goes every morning, and nineteen boards are standing up on end against that wall with about a hand's width of daylight at the foot of the fifth one of them, and the board with the three sets of figures on it is on its two nails above all of that and the wall above the top of it is the colour it was the morning before the water came.
