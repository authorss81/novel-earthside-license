# Chapter 0513 — The Top Of A Fence And A Man Who Does Not Go Through

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the shut road out past the last named house at about the ninth hour on the hundred and ninety-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, with the strap of his satchel in his right hand and the satchel itself on his shoulder, and he got as far as the top of that road and stopped there with his hand on the strap.

---

The fence runs along the top of that road from the corner by the last named house to the corner where the road turns, and it is sixteen willow posts with eleven withies bound on them with the same cord, and it was put up by the man of about sixty, who is not with his handcart this morning. It has a corner in it where the ground drops and the two posts at that corner are not the same distance from each other as the two posts either side of them, and nobody has measured that and nobody is going to. The gap a person would have to put a leg through to get past that corner is a thing that the people at that last named house have argued about since the water came and not one of them has taken a cord to it.

The man of about sixty was standing at the third post from that corner with the hazel mallet in his hand and a coil of rope over his shoulder, and he had not been asked to be there and he did not offer.

"**Sixteen posts and eleven withies and the ground under the corner one is not what it was two years ago, and that is everything I have got to say about it in front of anybody.**"

A woman at the last named house said the thing that is said, to the road.

"It was a good fence before the water."

"**It was a good fence before the water and it is a fence now, and I did not ask anybody to put me on this road and nobody asked me why I put it here, and I would rather it stayed like that between the two of us.**"

---

The carrier stood with his hand on the strap and did not put the satchel down and did not go through. That road is four hundred miles long and it goes out past that house and does not come back into this city anywhere. A carrier goes up it on the round because the round says he goes up it on the round, and there is no gate in it, and nobody in this city has said the road is shut and nobody has said it is open either.

"**I have gone up this road on the round for nine years and there has never been a gate in it in nine years and there has been a fence across it since before the water came up, and I am standing here on the round with an empty satchel and I am not going through, and I would rather that somebody wrote down that I stood here than that they wrote down that I went through.**"

A woman at the last named house said the thing that is said, to the road.

"There is nothing up there for you."

"**There is nothing up there for me and there was nothing up there for me the other nine years, and a man who has carried nothing up a road for nine years can stand at the top of it in the rain without anybody making a speech about it.**"

---

The sheet that came up this road in that satchel on the last round is in this city. It is on a table in a room over a market at the end of that row of flags, with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there is nothing whatever in that one place, and it has been in this city since a Monday and it will have that same one place under that same heading on any morning in this flood that anybody looks at it.

Nobody on that road said a word about the sheet. Nobody in this city has said out loud that a sheet comes up four hundred miles on a round and goes back down the same road, and nobody in this city has been told where a sheet goes after it has been read out.

---

Nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road. Three went down it with a barrow each, and the day they went is the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and a party of four set out and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and none of that was said out loud at the top of that fence this morning, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living took his hand off the strap of the satchel and put it flat on the bar of it instead and stood there while the light came off the top of that road.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living is off that road before the light has gone off the top of it, and the man of about sixty is still standing at the third post with the mallet in his hand and the coil of rope at the foot of it. The fence runs along the top of that road from one corner to the other with a corner in it where nobody has measured anything, and nobody in this city has said to either of those two men where that road is shut or who shut it.
