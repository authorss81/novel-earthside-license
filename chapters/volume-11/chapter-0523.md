# Chapter 0523 — An Empty Tin On A Table And A Man Who Noticed And Said Nothing

The man of about sixty was standing at the near gate of that market at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, with the handcart standing behind him and the shafts down in the wet, and a coil of rope over the side of the cart and a hazel mallet in the bottom of it with its head bound in cord. He had not been sent for anything and he was not going anywhere with it. The stair at the back of the room over that market goes up off the end of that row and it is nine steps from the flags to the door of that room, and the two hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the bottom of that door and there was no name on it.

---

She came up those nine steps with a tin in her two hands and there was nothing in the tin, and she did not stop on any of them. She came into that room where about nine people were and none of them had been sent for anything, and she put the tin down on the table under the one window and took her hands off it and put her two hands down at her sides, and she did not sit down and she did not ask anything.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was standing at the foot of that stair with his hand flat on the top rail of the back of a chair he had brought up with him, and his own board was face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row with the bare back of it out in the row, and he had come up that stair without it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was sitting at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and there are nineteen rates on it in three columns down it with the rows going across. Along the bottom of the middle column of that board there is a space about two fingers wide with nothing whatever in it. A name was in that space in his own hand about nine years ago. He took it off himself. Nobody in that room has asked him about it and he has not said what it was to anybody.

A woman in the room said the thing that is said, to the table and not to her.

"Are you going to stand there all morning."

"**I have asked three questions in this flood. One of them was about a person and one of them was about a fence and one of them was about a line, and I am not going to ask a fourth one, and I have not asked one this morning, and I would rather somebody in this room noticed that than somebody in this room asked me about it.**"

Nobody asked her about it. Nobody improved on what she had said and nobody thanked her for it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall took his hand off the rail of that chair and said nothing at all, and the tin stood on that table with nothing in it and the lid of it lying beside it on the wood.

---

She has asked three questions in this flood and she has not asked a fourth of them, and the three of them are not going to be argued about in that room, and there is no fourth of them in it this morning. Nobody in that room put a fourth question to her and nobody in that room told her she had not asked one, and about four people in that room had noticed that she had come up that stair and about four had not.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board noticed. He was at the near end of that bench with the board under his arm and he looked at the door when she came in and then at the tin on the table and then at the middle of the room, where the questions in that room go when they are asked, and he put his thumb along the edge of his own board and took it off again. He did not say one word about any of it to anybody in that room, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not look at him and did not say one word either, and not one of the other seven people in that room noticed that either of the two of them had said nothing.

---

The tin stood on that table until the end of that morning and nobody put anything in it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall carried that chair back down that stair and put it against the wall at the foot of it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took his board from under his arm and stood it face in against that bench with the face of it to the wall, which is not the way he carries it and is the way he puts it down when he is not selling anything, and then he sat down at the near end of that bench with it standing there and said nothing for the rest of that morning.

The tin is on the table under that window with the lid of it off and nothing whatever under the lid, and about nine people went down the stair at the back of that room after that without anything in their hands, and the woman who brought the tin up those steps did not ask the fourth question, and the man who noticed that she had not asked it has not said a word about it and has not said it to anybody in that room and is not going to.
