# Chapter 0526 — A Tin Scrubbed On A Sill And Two Middle Days And Neither Of Them The Middle

The tarred cloth came off the stack of boards at the near end of eleven miles of flats at about the fifth hour on the two hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and a woman put a tin down on the boards with water standing in the bottom of it, and she turned it over onto the stone sill at the foot of the first building and pushed it along the sill with the flat of her hand while the water in it went out over the edge in a sheet, and she got her two hands into it and scrubbed the inside of it about the way she had got the two hands of it in, and the grey that came off it went down the sill in a run and off the end of the sill into the channel. She filled it at the channel, and set it on the sill upside down, and left it there until the water had stopped coming out of it, and then she turned it over and put the water out of it again and stood it on the boards with the bottom of it wet. She got the flat of her hand round the inside of the bottom of it and worked the last of the grey out of it over the end of the sill, and she did that twice, and the second run of grey went down after the first one into the channel.

She has walked the outside of those eleven miles of flats on four mornings and this was not one of them. There is no fifth morning of that count in this city, and nobody went up that row with a slate on this morning, and nothing came off a slate this morning anywhere in this city.

---

She came down the length of that row with the empty tin in her two hands and went up the stair at the back of the room over that market and into that room, where about nine people were and none of them had been sent for anything, and she put the tin down on the table under the one window and it left a ring of water on the wood about the width of her two hands and she did not wipe the ring out and she did not put anything in the tin. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row with the bare back of it out in the row, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the far end of that bench with his own board under his arm, and neither of them said anything to her and neither of them said anything to anybody else about her.

A man at the window said the thing that is said, to the table.

"You could have left that at the near end."

"**It is not mine to say where a woman puts a tin and it is not mine to ask her, and if it leaves a mark on that table then the mark is on the table and not on me.**"

Nobody in that room asked her one question. Nobody asked her where she had been that morning or what she had put her hands in or what she had done with the eleven miles, and about four people in that room had worked out that a tin does not come up that stair for nothing and about four had not, and not one of the nine said a word about it to her or to each other.

A man at the middle of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"That ring will be there tomorrow."

"**It will be there tomorrow and the day after and nobody in this room is going to wipe it out, and a mark that somebody left in a room is the only thing in that room anybody can point at in a week without having to explain it.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm the whole of the time he said it and did not put it down.

---

A run of fifty days has two days in the middle of it. This is the second of the two of them, and it is no more the middle of anything than the day before it was, and the two of them are one day apart and neither of them is in the middle of this city or of this flood or of a morning that anybody in that room was having. On the day before it a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register named the cost of something out loud in that room before she did it, and on this day nobody in that room had anything to do at all, and the wall said nothing on this day as it says nothing on every day of this flood that is not the one day, and the register stood on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and the second slate behind it with a day across the head of it and nothing under that day.

The tin is on the table under that window with a wet ring round the bottom of it on the wood and nothing whatever in it, and about nine people were in that room and not one of them asked the woman who brought it anything, and the second of the two middle days of this range went out of that room with the ring still wet under the tin.
