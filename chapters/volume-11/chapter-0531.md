# Chapter 0531 — A Sheet Named And One Of The Same Shape Not Named

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of her right hand on the sheet lying on the table under the one window and turned it over so that the ruled place on it came up to the light. The date on that morning was chalked on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room, the two hundred and sixteenth day of the Bare Month, and no name has ever been written under it. There were about nine people in that room by the ninth hour and every one of them was there of their own going. The register had not come off its shelf behind the bench, where it stood with its spine to the wall and its cover shut, and standing on its edge behind the register was a second slate with its face in to the wall, a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Nobody picked it up. Nobody turned it over. Nothing was written on it. No hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and hers did not go near it that morning either.

---

The sheet she turned over has a heading at the top of it, and under that heading there is one place ruled, and there is one place only, and the one place is empty. It came up that road in a satchel with a flap on the quarterly round, and the two hands that may carry it are the hands that took it out of that satchel and unfolded it on that table, and the one place under that heading was empty then and it is empty now. There is no second place under that heading and there has never been one, and nothing in this city has ever been put in it.

There is a second sheet in this city of the same shape as that one. It has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and a figure in that one place, and it was read out to a room on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of this flood, and it was never returned and it was never withdrawn, and it is standing on the shelf behind that bench at the height of a person's shoulder. Nobody in that room brought it down this morning and nobody in that room said one word about it. Nobody in that room gave it a name.

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and every one of them was written four days before the room it was for, and there has been no eighth one, and that is a fact about this room and about that door and nobody in that room said it out loud this morning. Four documents have come down that road in this flood and three of the four have four places ruled under a heading and one of the four has a single place with a figure in it, and the four of them have never been joined together and never been compared out loud in this room. The road they came down is four hundred miles long, and it is a measure of a distance and not a place anybody in this city has stood, and nobody was sent up it this morning and nobody came down it.

A party of four set out down that road with a barrow each for three of them, and the day they went was the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and the three of them were one hundred and forty days out from this city at that moment, and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road.

---

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Nobody has said what that is for today."

"**It is for one figure of this city, in the one place under that heading, and it goes back down that road filled, and I am telling this room what it is for before anybody in it asks me, and the name of the thing it is a figure of is not on that sheet and it is not coming out of my head.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody in that room thanked her for saying it. She put the flat of her hand down on the wood beside the ruled place and left it there, and the hand did not go into the place.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the bench behind him rather than to the table.

"There is another one of them on that shelf."

"**There is another one of them on that shelf and there is a figure in the one place on it, and I am not going to bring it down, and it is not this sheet coming back, and I am not going to stand in this room and lay the two of them side by side so that people can look at them.**"

Nobody improved on that either and nobody asked her why the two of them are not the same document, and about four people in that room had worked out on their own that there are two of them in this city and about four had not, and the two of them who had worked it out did not say so to each other.

---

At about the tenth hour the two hands that may carry that sheet carried it down the stair at the back of that room and out along the flags of the row and back up that stair again. It went down with the one place under its heading showing nothing whatever in it, and it came back up with the one place under its heading showing nothing whatever in it, and the fold in it was pressed out with a bone on the way up and there is no bone in this room that has been used on any other sheet. The flags on that stair are worn pale in the middle of each tread and darker at the nosing, and by the time those two hands came back up the second time the sun had moved off the row and onto the stones at the far end of it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his own board face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row and the bare back of it out in that row, and he did not come away from that window. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room, and nobody in that room asked him anything, and the nineteen rates were still on the face of it and the space about two fingers wide where a name had been in his own hand about nine years ago was not filled.

The sheet is face up on the table under that window with one place ruled under its heading and nothing whatever in it, and the other one is standing on the shelf behind that bench with a figure in its one place, and a table's width of that room is between the two of them, and nobody in that room has laid them beside each other and nobody in that room said a word about the second of them.
