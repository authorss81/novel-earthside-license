# Chapter 0520 — Five Lines' Worth Of Room In A Column And A Rope Nobody Looks At

The register came off its shelf behind the bench at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and the two hundred and fifth was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair and there was no name on that door. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took it down in her own two hands and put it on the table under the one window with the cover facing the wall and opened it at the page it is always opened at, and no other hand in that room went near it. There were about nine people in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

The register has three columns. The one on the left holds days and the one in the middle holds figures and the one on the right is empty from the top of the page to the bottom and has been empty for every day of this flood, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register has never given a reason for that out loud and nobody in this city has asked her for one.

She put her finger on the head of the middle column and ran it down the ruled lines to the foot of them and then back up the left-hand margin to the head of the page, and she did it twice, and the second time she stopped a little higher than the first and said the one word out loud.

"**Five.**"

A man at the window said the thing that is said, to the table.

"Five what."

"**Five lines' worth of room in the column that holds the figures, and three lines' worth of room in the column that is empty, and I have not written anything in either of them this morning and neither of you is going to make me.**"

Nobody in that room decided anything about the five and nobody asked her what the five was going to be used for, and about four people in that room had worked out how many mornings it would take to fill a column of that book and about four had not, and not one of them said so to her or to each other.

---

The sheet with the empty place was lying on the same table at about the tenth hour with its heading to the wall and its one place ruled under that heading showing nothing whatever in it, and there is one place under that heading and there has never been a second one under it. It is not on the shelf this morning and it is not on the bench and it is not in the register, and nothing has been written under its heading and no hand has gone near that one place.

The four documents that have come down that road this flood are four documents, and three of the four have four places ruled under a heading and one of the four has a single place with a figure in it, and that one is on the shelf behind the bench at the height of a person's shoulder and was not brought down this morning. The two sheets in this city that have a heading and a single place under it were in this room on the same day and stood a full table apart from one another, one at each end of it with nothing laid against it and nothing moved off it, and nobody laid them side by side and nobody said one word out loud about how the two of them stand in relation to one another. That road is four hundred miles long and it is a measure of a distance and not a place anybody in this city has stood, and nobody was sent up it this morning and nobody came down it.

---

The man of about fifty-seven was at the end of that bench with his hands flat on the table in front of him and a length of new rope over the back of his chair, and it has been over the back of that chair every morning of this flood and it was over the back of it this morning. No hand in that room lifted it and no mouth in that room said one word about it and nobody asked that man anything at all. He was not asked where the fence is and he was not told anything about it and he was not looking at the rope.

---

About the eleventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register turned the sheet with the empty place face down on that table with the writing side against the wood, and she put the register back on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and its cover shut, and she was the only pair of hands in that room that touched either of those two things.

The sheet is lying face down on the table under the one window with its one place against the wood, and the rope is over the back of that chair and has not moved all morning, and nobody in that room looked at it once.
