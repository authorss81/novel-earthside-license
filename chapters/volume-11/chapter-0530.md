# Chapter 0530 — Four Figures Written Above One And No Room Left In That Column

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the register off its shelf behind the bench at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and carried it to the table under the one window and opened it at the page it is always opened at and stood it on its spine with the two covers holding it open, and she put the working slate down beside it with the four of them face up on the one face of it, and she uncorked the ink and put the pen into the bottle and brought it out and ran the nib on the back of her hand, and the ink was stiff and she worked it round twice before it took. About nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything, and the window at the back of that room was open because the room was warm, and out of that window the store's board is on its two nails on the wall of the market below it with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out.

---

She wrote them one under another in the column that holds figures and she wrote them in the order they were made in with the newest of the four at the head of that column and the oldest of the four at the foot of them, and she wrote the day beside each one in the same hand as the figure, and none of the four was struck out and none of the four was a correction of any of the other three. There was one figure in that column already and it was at the foot of it with a day of its own written beside it, and she wrote the four above that one, and the last of the four went in on the line directly above the figure that was already in there, and that column of that book was full when her hand came off the page, and there is no more room in it for anything at all. The pen went across four lines and then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put it down on the table and corked the ink and put the flat of her hand on the wood for a moment with the four of them under it and then took the hand off again, and nobody in that room said one word about the four of them and nobody in that room said one word about the one that was already in that column.

A man at the end of that bench by the shelf said the thing that is said, to the table.

"That column is full."

"**There is no room left in that column for a sixth figure of this city's own and there is not going to be one made, and the column on the right of that book is empty and it is going to stay empty.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his own piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, where it has been since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and the store's chalk was in its box on that step with the lid of it open and about a thumb's length of it showing, and nobody in that market used either of those two chalks that day and nobody asked that man for his.

---

About nine people looked at the middle column of that register between the seventh hour and the eleventh hour and about four of them counted the five figures down it and about four did not, and not one of the nine said a word to any of the others about what those four figures are a count of.

A woman at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the register and not to the table.

"You have written them in the order they were made."

"**I have watched a woman write for nine years the way a man writes a price, and a price goes where the next price goes, and that is the whole of what I know about an order and I am not going to say more of it than that.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and the nineteen rates were still on the face of his board and the space about two fingers wide in the middle column of it was still not filled when he sat down at the near end of that bench with it under his arm. The second slate stands on the shelf behind that bench on its edge with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and on that morning nobody picked it up, nobody turned it over, and nobody wrote on it, and the only hand in that room that went near that shelf was the keeper's, and it went near it exactly once, at the eleventh hour, when she put the register back on it with its spine to the wall and the cover shut.

The register is on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and there are five figures standing in one column of it and that column is full from the head of it to the foot of it, and the column on the right of them is empty from the top of the page to the bottom, and nobody in this city has been told which of those four figures is the one that is going anywhere.
