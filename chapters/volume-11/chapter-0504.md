# Chapter 0504 — A Satchel On The Table And A Corner Of A Sheet

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the road at about the seventh hour on the hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, with a satchel with a flap under his arm, and he came into that market at the end where the store's board is. The board was on its two nails above the trough with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and the wall above it was not washed. He stopped in front of it with the strap of the satchel in his hand and looked at all three sets of figures for about as long as it takes a man to count nine, and went on up the row and did not say anything about any of them.

---

He had come up that road four hundred miles with that satchel on the quarterly round and he had not opened it on the road and he did not open it at the end of that market. The flap of it was closed and the strap of it was round twice, and it had a weight in it that was not the weight of a sheet of paper, and he put his hand flat on the outside of it once and took his hand off it.

He went up the stair at the back of the room over that market with it and stopped on the step with the satchel against his leg.

---

The hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind him and there was no name on it. About four people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything. The register was on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and above it on the same shelf, at the height of a person's shoulder, there was a sheet of paper with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading, and there was a figure in that one place.

The man of about thirty-one put the satchel on the table with the flap up. He undid the strap and took his hand off it and said the four words.

"**It is not for me. That is all.**"

Nobody asked him what was in it. He said nothing else about it and he did not open it, and the flap stayed where he had put it, and nobody in that room put a hand on the strap of it.

---

Adrian Vale had the flap in his left hand and the strap in his right, and he took them both off the satchel and set the satchel down on the table with the flap towards the window. Then he opened it and got the sheet out with both hands and carried it to the middle of the table, and he put the near corner down first and walked the far end of it round so that the heading stood at the far side of that table with its back to the window and the one place ruled under it lying in the middle of the table facing the low side of the room, where anybody sitting on that bench could see it.

There is one place ruled under that heading and the one place is empty. There is no figure in it and no ink in it and no pencil in it and nothing whatever in the one place, and the ruling of it is as clean as the ruling of the other one on the shelf above the register, and there is no second place under that heading and there never has been.

He wanted that sheet to reach that table with its one place still showing empty, and it did, and he did not ask what the sheet was for and he did not ask who it had come from, and the reason he did not ask is that a man who asks what he is carrying is a man who has opened it. He said nothing at all to anybody in that room and nobody said anything to him.

---

About four people in that room saw that one place was empty before anybody in that room said one word about the sheet. A woman at the near end of the bench got up off the bench, came round the end of the table, stood over the one place and looked down into it, and went back and sat down again. Two men at the far end of that bench stood up and looked at it and stayed standing. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his hand on the sill and the piece of chalk of his own in his own inside breast pocket, and he looked at the one place and said nothing at all about it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came in from the far end of that room and stood at the head of the table and put her hand flat on the wood at the end of it and left it there, and she did not pick the sheet up and she did not turn it over and she did not put it down beside the one on the shelf.

"Not opened."

"**Not opened, and not going to be opened by me, and I am not going to say one word to anybody about whether it ought to have been.**"

---

The sheet lay on that table through the rest of the morning with its one place empty and the window behind it. The satchel with the flap went back over the man's own shoulder at about the middle of the day and the strap of it went round twice, and he went down the stair at the back of that room and out through the door at the bottom of it into the middle of that market. He was carrying less than he had come up that stair with and nobody at the bottom of that stair asked him what had been in it.
