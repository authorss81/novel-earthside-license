# Chapter 0544 — Seven Sheets And A Space About Two Fingers Wide

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the register off its own shelf behind the bench at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and twenty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and carried it in her own two hands the length of that room and stood it open on the table under the one window at the page it is always opened at, with the two covers holding it, and she put her hand flat on the wood beside it and left it there. The date was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and no name has ever been written under it. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

Behind her on that shelf the second slate stands on its edge with its face in to the wall. A day is scratched across the head of it and there is nothing whatever under that day, and it has been like that every morning of this flood, and it did not move this morning and nothing was written on it and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf at any hour.

Five figures stand in the column of that book that holds figures, one under another in the order they were made in, and the day stands beside each of them in her own hand. That column is ruled into five lines and every one of the five lines has a figure in it and there is nothing else that can go into it. The column on the right of that page is empty from the top of the page to the bottom and it has been empty every day of this flood and nobody in that room has ever said one word about it.

---

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood and every one of them was written four days before the room it was for, and there has been no eighth one, and that is a fact about this room and about that door and not one person in that room said it out loud that morning. Four days is the time a room has to find out what it is being asked, and this room finds out what it is being asked on the morning it is asked it, and nobody in that room wrote a notice that morning and nobody in that room was sent for anything.

The book lay open on that table all morning and nobody read out of it. Nobody in that room put a finger on that page and nobody in that room wrote in it, and the keeper stood at the head of that table with her hand on the wood for about as long as it takes a man to walk to the end of that row and back, and then she shut it.

---

A fence stands along the top of the road that goes out past the last named house, from the corner by that house round to the corner where that road turns. It is put up on sixteen willow posts with eleven withies tied on to them with one cord, and the man of about sixty put it there and nobody sent him. Out of that window in this room about four people can see the top of that fence and about four cannot, and nobody in this room has taken a cord to any part of it and nobody in this room has measured that fence or the corner in it, and that road is four hundred miles long and it is a measure of a distance and not a place anybody in this city has stood.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room. Nineteen rates stand on that board in three columns with the rows going across, and along the bottom of the middle column of it there is bare board about as wide as two fingers and nothing whatever in it, and a name was in that space in his own hand about nine years ago and he took it off himself.

A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the board.

"Nine years and nobody has asked you what was in that space."

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board looked at the bare space at the bottom of the middle column of his own board for about as long as it takes a man to count nine and then said it, and he said it once.

"**Nobody has asked me in nine years and I have not said it once, and I am saying now that nobody has asked rather than that nobody can, and there is a difference between those two and nobody in this room is going to be told which of them it is.**"

Nobody in that room asked him. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody in that room said one word about what had been in that space, and about four people in that room had worked out that there was a space on that board at all and about four had not, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window two stalls along his own row with his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not come away from it.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the bench.

"Nine years and you have never said it out loud in a room."

"**I have said it once now and I am not going to say it again and it has not changed one thing in this room, and I have a board with nineteen rates on it and two fingers of bare board in it and that is what I have got, and nobody in this room is going to be told what is under the two fingers and I am not going to be asked.**"

Nobody in that room asked him and nobody improved on him the second time and nobody thanked him the second time, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board put his thumb along the top rail of his own board and took it off again and said nothing else for the rest of that morning.

---

Near the eleventh hour the keeper closed the covers of that register on her own two hands, took it off that table, walked it round the end of the bench and set it down again on its own shelf behind that bench with the spine of it to the wall. She went to that shelf once in that morning. Nothing else in that room came near it, and the second slate on that shelf did not move a finger's width all morning, and nobody in that room wrote anything anywhere.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board is still at that end of that bench with his board under his arm, and he has said two things this morning and both of them were about a space about two fingers wide on the face of it, and he is not going to say a third.
