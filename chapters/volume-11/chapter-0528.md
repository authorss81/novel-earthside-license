# Chapter 0528 — Eleven Miles Out And Eleven Miles Back With The Same Kit And Nothing Counted

The store's board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board laid it down flat on the flags at the foot of the wall with the figures up and the wet side to the air. He worked the rag across it from the bottom left-hand corner to the top right-hand one and the water came off it grey and went over the stone lip of the step and along the flags, and he put the rag on the stone lip and the bucket in the trough and carried the bucket to the channel and back and then again. At the fourth hour the board went back up on its two nails with all three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, the trough was against that wall on its own spot with the bucket standing in it, and the wall above the top of that board was not washed.

At about the fifth hour he took the trough off its spot against that wall and got it up on his shoulder, and he took the bucket in his right hand and the rag over the rail of it with his left, and he went out along that row of flags and out through the near gate and up the length of eleven miles of flats in weather that had come in off the low ground before the light and had not let up all day.

---

At the near end of those flats he set that trough down on the bank of the channel where the ground is hard, and he filled it out of the channel with the bucket, three goes and a part, and he stood the bucket in the trough with about a hand's depth of water in the bottom of it, and he put the rag out on the flat stones at the near end of the first building to dry where the wind came off them, and he stood there with his hand on the rim of that trough and looked up the length of that row of flats at about the seventh hour and did not go up it.

A woman at the near end of those flats said the thing that is said, to the water.

"You have brought that trough a long way for water."

"**The water at the end of that market has gone the colour of that cloth and there is one stretch of it in this city I would take a trough to, and nobody asked me to and I am not stopping here to say why.**"

Nobody at the near end of that row asked him why he had come, and nobody thanked him for coming, and about four people at that end of that row saw a man carry a trough a long way and about four did not, and he put the rag in his coat and picked the bucket up and got the trough back on his shoulder and went down eleven miles of flats the same road he had come up.

---

The trough was not what went out of that market at the fifth hour. The water in it went out over the eleven miles in two places where he had to set it down to get his hands round it again, and when he got the trough on his shoulder at the near end of that market there was a stone in the bottom of it about the size of his two fists put there by nobody, and he did not put his hand into the water to find out where that stone had come from and he did not say one word about it to anybody in that row.

He set the trough down against that wall on its own spot under that board and the bucket into it, and the rag went on the stone lip of that step where it goes every morning, and the water in that trough was about a hand's depth under the stone and the stone sat on the bottom of it and did not go under the lip. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board face in against his own trestle two stalls along in the row and the store's chalk was in its box on that step with the lid of it open and a thumb's length of it showing, and the piece of chalk of his own was in his own inside breast pocket where it has been since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and neither of those two chalks was used that day.

A woman two stalls along said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"He has been gone since the fifth hour."

"**He has been gone since the fifth hour with a trough on his shoulder and a bucket in his hand, and I watched him go out of that row and I am not going to ask him and you are not going to ask him either.**"

Nobody in that market asked the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board where he had been or why, and nobody thanked him, and nobody counted anything that day. Nobody went up eleven miles of flats with a slate and nobody came down that row with a figure of anything, and the man who brought the kit back was not asked about it by anybody in that market and did not stop to say anything about it.

The trough is against that wall under that board where it has been since the seventh week of this flood, and the rag is on the stone lip, and there is a stone in the bottom of that trough that nobody in that market put there, and nobody in that market has picked it up.
