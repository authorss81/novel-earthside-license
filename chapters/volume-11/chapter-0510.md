# Chapter 0510 — Two Counts Of One Room And A Window

The hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the back of the room over that market, a Sunday, and about nine people were in that room by about the ninth hour and none of them had been sent for anything. There is one window in that room and the light comes in at it and not at the door, and there is a bench and a table under the window and a shelf behind the bench with a book on it and two sheets of paper standing on their edges on that same shelf. One of those sheets has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under the heading with a figure in that one place. The other has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under the heading with nothing whatever in it.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took both of those sheets off that shelf, one in each hand, and put them on the table under the window. She put the one with the figure in it at the far end of that table with its heading to the wall, and she put the one with the empty place in it at the near end of the same table with its heading to the wall, and there was the length of a table between them, and they were not laid beside each other, and no person in that room put them side by side or said one word out loud about how the two of them were the same document or about one of them being the other one coming back.

She looked at the one with the figure in it for about as long as it takes to count nine. Then she went to the far end of that table and put her two fingers on the ruled line under the heading on that sheet and ran them along it to the end of it, and came back to the near end and did the same thing on the other one, and did not carry the two of them to each other to do it. Then she went to the door and counted the room out loud.

"**Nine.**"

A woman at the step said the thing that is said.

"There are eight of us here and you."

"**There are nine of us here. I have counted the two of us at this end separately and I am not going to do it the way that makes the number come out smaller.**"

---

A man at the bench said the thing that is said to the table.

"There were seven of them went out in this flood."

"**Seven, and every one of them went out because a man had days in front of him to write them in, and there is not one of the two of us in this room who could have written one of them on the morning it was wanted.**"

There was no eighth notice in that room and there was not going to be one, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that could put four days between a thing and the people it is about, and nobody in that room was asked to do anything at all that morning.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his hands in his pockets and his back to the table, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of the bench with his own board under his arm and the face of it out into the room, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of that bench with the satchel with the flap on the floor at his feet. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one what was in it. Nobody in that room asked anybody anything about either of the two sheets on that table, and about four people in that room had worked out which count the woman of about twenty-nine was doing and about four of them had not, and the two of them did not say so to each other.

At about the middle of that morning the man of about thirty-one got up off the bench, picked the satchel up off the floor, and went down the stair at the back of that room, and the door at the bottom of it shut behind him.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine counted that room out loud again at about the tenth hour, standing in the same place in the doorway with her hand on the frame of it, and she counted longer the second time and she got a different figure.

"**Eight.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"You had nine an hour ago."

"**I had nine an hour ago, and a man went down that stair with a satchel and he did not go through the room to do it, and that is the whole of the difference between the two numbers and I am not going to make anything more of it than that.**"

Nobody in that room said which of the two numbers was the count. The light came off the flags and went up the wall behind that table and stopped under the window, and the two sheets lay at the two ends of that table with the length of the table between them, and the one with nothing in it was still showing its one place with nothing in it, and nobody in that room had been told what it is a count of.
