# Chapter 0539 — A Column Read Down And A Road With Three On It

Nobody took the register off its shelf behind the bench on the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, until about the tenth hour, and when the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took it off she took it down in her own two hands and stood it open on the table under the one window at the page it is always opened at, the two covers holding it, and she was the only person in that room who touched it from one hour to the next. The two hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and there was no name on that door. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything. On that shelf behind the register there is a second slate, standing on its edge with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and nothing was written on it, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and hers did not go near it that morning either.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up that stair at about the quarter past the tenth hour and stood at the far end of that bench with his hands in his pockets, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was already at the near end of it with his own board under his arm, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other when the second one came in, and neither of them said one word to the other for the rest of that morning, and nobody in that room asked either of them about it. About four people in that room had noticed that and about four had not.

There are five figures in the column of that book that holds figures, one under another in the order they were made in, with the day beside each of them, and the newest of the four of them is at the head of that column and the oldest of the four of them is at the foot of those, and one older figure stands below that with a day of its own written beside it. That column is full from the head of it to the foot of it and there is no room left in it for anything at all, and the column on the right of that page is empty from the top of the page to the bottom and nobody in that room said one word about it and nobody in that room has ever said one word about it.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the book.

"Read them down and I will read the days after you."

"**You read the figures down and she reads the days after you and nobody in this room needs to be told what order a column of a book goes in, because I have watched nine people in this room find it out on their own in a morning, and I have not heard one of them get it wrong.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board did not read down that column and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not read the days after anybody, and both of them sat down where they were.

A woman at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the window.

"Three went down that road and this city has not had word of any of them."

"**Three went down a road four hundred miles long with a barrow each on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood, and they are one hundred and forty-eight days out from this city at this moment, and a party of four set out and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and there is not one person on that road that anybody in this city can be shown, and there is no instrument in this city that puts a person on a road or takes one off it.**"

Nobody in that room counted anything out loud. Nobody in that room asked him how long it was since the three of them went and nobody in that room said the number back at him, and about four people in that room had worked out that nobody could be shown one person on that road and about four had not.

---

The two of them at the ends of that bench did not say one word to each other from the time the second of them came in until the end of that morning. Nobody in that room mentioned the ninth hour of the day before and nobody in that room asked either of the two of them what had been said in that room on the day before, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board has nineteen rates on the face of his board and the space about two fingers wide along the bottom of the middle column of it is not filled and was not filled and nobody asked him about it.

About the eleventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register closed that register and carried it back to its shelf behind the bench and put it down with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and she was the only pair of hands in that room that touched it, and she went near that shelf exactly once.

Two men at the two ends of that bench did not say one word to each other from one hour to the next, and the man at the near end of it is looking at a window and not at anybody in that room, and the man at the far end of it has his hands in his pockets and is looking at the table, and three barrows went down a road four hundred miles long on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood and nobody in this city has seen one of them since, and a book is shut on the shelf behind that bench with five figures standing one under another in one column of it.
