# Chapter 0540 — A Rule On A Wall And A Sheet Shut On A Table

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up that stair at about the ninth hour with the satchel with the flap under his arm, set it down on the bench at the far end of that room, and took his hands off the strap of it, and the flap of it was down. The two hundred and twenty-fifth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there was no name on that door. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything, and the sheet with the figure in it was lying face up on the table under the one window with the writing side up and nobody had asked for it and nobody in that room had put a hand in the one place under its heading.

---

Out of the window at the back of that room the store's board hangs on its two nails on the wall of the market below it, carrying the three sets of figures it has carried since that store was shut. The old account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood is on the left of it with the office's own hand still under it, and this city's own figures in chalk are to the right of that, the first of them a hand's width clear of the old ones and the second of them clear of the first, and the two of them do not agree. Above the top edge of that board the stone goes on up in the colour it keeps. The washing of it stops at the top of the board and has stopped there every morning of this flood, and the man who washes that board is not in that room this morning and is in that market.

The wall above the top of that board said one thing that morning and it came about the middle of it. Nothing in this flood has ever put anything on that stone and nobody had been near it to make it do that, and what came out of it came out of the store's own stone, the stone the old figures are chalked on. Nine or thereabouts were standing in that row at the time, and about four of them looked at that wall afterwards and the rest of them did not.

> A figure that goes out of a place on a form does not stop being a figure of that place,
> and the place it left has no instrument for being told what it was asked for,
> and a form with one place under a heading and no place under the heading for the person who counted it
> will be filled right and will be shown to nobody.

---

A woman at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the wall outside the window and not to the room.

"That wall has said a thing and nobody in this room is going to be asked anything about it."

"**Nobody in this room is going to be asked anything about it and nobody in this room is going to be told anything about it, and the man who washed that board every morning since the seventh week of this flood is not in this room this morning and I am not going to stand here and speak for him.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that and nobody thanked her for saying it, and nobody in that room asked the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board anything about it, because he was not in it, and the market below went on.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was standing at the end of that bench with his own hand on the back of it when that came, and he refused it in about four seconds. It was a handful of words. Nobody improved on any of them, nobody thanked him for them and nobody argued with them, and he has not asked to be thanked and did not.

"**That is a rule about a form and not about me, and I have carried five notices, a return, a sheet on the four hundred and first day of this flood and a sheet on the four hundred and fifty-first day of this flood without opening one of them, and this is the fifth thing I have carried that I know what is in, and the rule does not say one word about the man who carries it and that is why it is going to be true.**"

Nobody in that market asked him what was in it. Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room said a word against it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not look up from the table when he said it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall kept his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket and did not say one word.

---

Nobody in that market said the thing on that wall out loud again. About four people at that end of that market read that board afterwards and about four did not, and not one of the nine of them can say which of the three sets of figures on that board is the one anybody should be reading, and the wall did not say anything else that day. The store's chalk was in its box on the step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of it showing, and the piece of chalk of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has not come out of the inside breast pocket of his coat since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and neither that piece nor the one in the box has been used on any morning of this flood.

At about the eleventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register folded that sheet twice along the creases that came up that road in it, and pressed the bone along the whole length of each fold with the heel of her hand, and set the bone down on that table, and put the flat of her hand on the folded sheet for a moment and then took the hand off it. That sheet now has a heading and one place under that heading with a figure in it and nothing else on it anywhere, and it was not opened again that day by anybody in that room, and the satchel with the flap was still on the bench at the far end of that room with the flap of it down and nothing in it.

The sheet is shut on the table under the one window with its two folds in it and the bone lying beside it, and the satchel is on a bench at the far end of that room with the flap down, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living is sitting at that end of that bench with his hands on his knees and has said his handful of words once and is not going to say them again.
