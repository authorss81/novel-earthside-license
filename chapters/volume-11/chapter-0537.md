# Chapter 0537 — A Book Open At A Column And A Wall With One Figure On It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the register open on the table under the one window by the ninth hour on the two hundred and twenty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. She had taken it off its own shelf behind the bench in her two hands and stood it on its spine at the page it is always opened at with the two covers holding it open, and no other hand in that room went near it at any hour. That day was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and there was no name on that door. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything. The second slate stood on its edge on that shelf behind the register, its face to the wall, a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. It was not picked up. It was not turned over. Nothing was written on it. No hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and hers did not go near it that morning either.

---

There are five figures in the column that holds figures and they are in the order they were made in with the newest of them at the head of that column and the oldest of the four of them at the foot of those, and the day stands beside each of them in her hand. There is one more figure at the foot of that column, older than any of the four, with a day of its own written beside it, and that column of that book is ruled into five lines and all five of them have a figure in them and there is no room left in it for anything at all. The column on the right of that page is empty from the top of the page to the bottom and it has been empty every day of this flood and nobody in that room said one word about it.

Out of the window at the back of that room the store's board is on its two nails on the wall of the market below it, and there are three sets of figures on it and none of the three has been rubbed out, and one of them is an account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood that came down a road four hundred miles long with an office's own hand on it, and the other two are figures of this city's own in chalk that were made in this city and that do not agree with one another, and the wall above the top of that board is the colour it is every morning of this flood.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the book.

"That is a column of five figures and there is a figure on a wall outside that window."

"**That is a column of five figures in a book and that is a figure on a wall, and a wall is read across and a book is read down, and this room has both of them in it at the same time on the same morning, and nobody in this room is going to tell me which of the two a person is meant to be using, and I am not going to ask.**"

Nobody said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody in that room asked him which one he thought a person should use, and he did not offer one.

A woman at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the window and not to the book.

"Four people in this room have looked at that board and at that book this morning and the rest have not."

"**About four have and about four have not, and I have watched which is which and I am not going to say it out loud in front of the other four, because the day I say it the other four start looking and then it is a thing people look at instead of a thing people count.**"

Nobody in that room said which four. Nobody in that room looked up at the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board to see whether he was one of the four, and he was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the nineteen rates on the face of it, and he did not look up.

---

The register was read down. A man at the near end of that bench put his finger on the head of that column and went down it to the foot of it reading the figures out loud, and a woman at the far end of the same bench read the days beside them after him, going down the same column at the same pace, and the two of them did not agree about a single day and neither of them said so, and nobody in that room asked them to go down it again. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at the window with his own piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket, where it has been since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and he did not come away from that window.

About the eleventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register closed that register and carried it back to its shelf behind the bench and put it down with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and she was the only pair of hands in that room that touched it, and she went near that shelf exactly once.

The book is on its shelf behind that bench with five figures standing in one column of it and that column full from the head of it to the foot of it and the column beside them empty from the top of the page to the bottom, and outside the window below it there is a figure on a wall that about four people in that room have worked out is of the same kind of thing as the figure at the head of that column and about four have not, and not one of the nine of them said so out loud this morning.
