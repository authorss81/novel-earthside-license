# Chapter 0532 — Four Figures And A Word Nobody Had Needed Before

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the working slate out from under the table at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and seventeenth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and carried it the length of that table and set it down on the wood with the one face of it that takes a figure turned up, and the two hundred and seventeenth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair at the back of that room and there was no name on that door and no second person in this city has ever put one there. About nine people were in that room by that hour and none of them had been sent for anything. The register stood on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and behind the register a second slate stood on its edge with its face in to the wall and a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Neither of them was picked up. Neither of them was turned over. Nothing was written on either of them, and no hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and hers did not go near it that morning either.

---

Four figures of one kind of thing were standing on the face of that slate, one under another, in the order they had been made in, with the day beside each of them in the same hand as the figure, and there was a hand's width of clean slate above the first of them and nothing whatever put into it. None of the four had been rubbed out and none of the four was a correction of any of the other three. A woman at the far end of that bench came and stood at the head of that slate and read all four of them out loud, the first one and then the second and then the third and then the fourth, reading the day beside each of them as she went, at the pace a person reads a price off a board, and she said nothing at all after the fourth one and went back and sat down where she had been.

Nobody in that room asked her what the four of them were a count of. Nobody in that room said the four of them out loud together, and nobody worked out loud which of them was the larger, and about four people in that room stood with their hands behind their backs and about four did not.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the slate.

"Those four do not agree and not one of them has been struck out."

"**They came off the same eleven miles in the same week in the same weather by the same person, and the first of them and the last of them are about a fifth apart, and it is not the weather, and it is not a knock, and there is no error in any one of the four of them, and I have not found one and I have looked.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for saying it, and he has said in that market that he would rather nobody did.

A woman sitting near the step said the thing that is said, to the table and not to the slate.

"Then what is a person going to do with four of them."

"**A second figure of the same kind of thing, standing above the first one, that nobody is going to be made to agree with it, is a counterpart, and I have carried a board with nineteen rates on it for nine years without a word for one of those, and I am not going to leave this room without one this morning.**"

Nobody said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him where he had got the word from, and he put the flat of his own hand on the edge of that slate for about as long as it takes a man to count nine and then took the hand off it, and he did not say the word again for the rest of that day and he has not said it since.

---

At about the tenth hour the four of them were still on that face of that slate in the order they had been made in, and a woman at the far end of that bench counted them down with the flat of her finger while she was still standing in front of each one, and the number of them is four and there is no fifth morning of that count in this city and there is not going to be one. About four people in that room said out loud that a fifth part is not a small thing to stand between two numbers and about four said nothing at all, and not one of the nine of them said what the four of them were a count of.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and the nineteen rates on the face of it, and he did not look at that slate and nobody in that room asked him to. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the far end of the same bench with the satchel with the flap on the floor at his feet and his hands on his knees, and nobody in that room asked him what was in it.

The four of them stand on one face of a working slate under the one window with a hand's width of clean slate above the first of them and nothing whatever in it, and the first of them and the last of them are about a fifth apart, and this is the first morning in this flood on which anybody in this city has said out loud why, and nobody in that room has said the word a second time.
