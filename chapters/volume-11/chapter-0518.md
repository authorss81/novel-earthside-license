# Chapter 0518 — One Surface Face Up On A Table And A Space About Two Fingers Wide

The two hundred and third day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of the stair on a Monday and there was no name on that door, and about the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was coming up that stair with his own board under his arm, and he put it down flat on the table under the window with the face of it up and the long edge of it square with the long edge of the table, and he took his hands off it and put them in his pockets. There are nineteen rates on that board and they are written in three columns down it with the rows going across, and they are the rates he has traded in four markets for nine years and he has never had them written any other way. About the width of two fingers along the bottom of the middle column there is a space with nothing in it, and a name was in that space in his own hand about nine years ago, and he took it off himself, and he has not said what it was to anybody and nobody in that room has asked him.

---

The register stood on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and its cover shut, and there are about nine people in that room and none of them had been sent for anything. A woman at the near end of the bench read that board across, going along the rows from the left, and a man at the far end of that bench read it down, going along the columns from the top, and they came to different ends of it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register read neither of them and stood at the side of that table with her hand on the wood and looked at the order of things on it.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said the four seconds' worth.

"**That board is read across and every book in this city is read down, and whoever has been taking the head of a column first every time for nine years has never been wrong about a figure and has been wrong about which end he started from.**"

Nobody in that room said that he was right. A man at the far end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the board and not to him.

"Nineteen rates is a lot of writing for one board."

"**It has been nineteen rates for nine years and it will be nineteen rates on this board in nine years, and nobody in this room has ever once offered me a second board to put them on.**"

He sat down at the near end of that bench after that and did not say one word more. A man at the far end put his finger on the top rate of that board and counted four rates down it and did not say anything, and the woman at the near end went back along the top row and counted them the other way and did not say anything either. About four people in that room had worked out that both of those surfaces carry figures of this city's own and about four of them had not.

---

Seven sheets went out of this city in this flood. Every one of them was a sheet with a heading and four places ruled under the heading and no name at the top of it, and every one of them was written four days before the room it was for, and there has been no eighth, and nothing in this flood has put an eighth one into a hand. Nobody in that room was asked to do anything on that Monday and nobody in that room was told what to bring to it.

About the eleventh hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board took his board up off that table and put it back under his arm with the face of it out into the room as it had been when he came up that stair.

The two of them counted the same nineteen rates in two directions on that table and came to different ends of the board, and the man who owns it is sitting at the near end of that bench with it under his arm and has not been told by anybody in that room that he was right, and the nineteen rates are on the face of it where they have been for nine years.
