# Chapter 0501 — The Board Washed Twice

The store's board was on its two nails above the trough at one end of that market on the hundred and eighty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and the trough was full to its stone lip. The account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood was on the left of that board and had been on the left of it every morning since the water came. A figure of this city's own, in chalk, was in the middle of it a hand's width clear of the account. A second figure of this city's own was on the right of that, a further hand's width clear of the first, and the two of them in chalk did not agree with each other. None of the three had been rubbed out and there was no place on that board that had ever been wiped. The account on the left of it had come down a road four hundred miles long with an office's own hand on it, and the two in chalk had been made in this city. The wall above the top of that board was the colour it had been all flood, and the washing of that wall stopped where the board stopped.

---

Adrian Vale had his two hands under the near edge of it. He got his fingers behind the stone lip of the trough and lifted, and the board came off the two nails with the sound a thing makes when it has been up on the same two nails for a long time, and he caught the far end of it in the crook of his arm before it went down on the flags. He turned it over once and laid it across the mouth of the trough with the figures up and the wet side to the air, and the water in the trough went over the lip on the near side of it and off it again on the far side. He set the bucket down in the wet on the flags and picked the rag up off the lip and wrung it out once, and the water off it went into the trough and the trough took it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board standing face in against his own trestle, two stalls along, with the bare back of it out in the row, and a piece of chalk of his own in his own inside breast pocket that did not come out. He came out from behind the trestle about the middle of that and stopped with one hand on the top rail of his own board.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board said the four sentences he had come out to say, and he said them to the stone and not to the row.

"**There are three of them on it and no two of them agree with each other, and I wash all three.**"

A woman at a stall two along called out to the flags and not to him.

"You have washed that board more often this week than you have washed anything."

"**The one on the left came down a road with a hand on it that was not in this city, and the two in chalk on the right of it were made in this city, and I could not tell you what any one of the three of them is a count of and I am not going to try this morning.**"

"**Nobody in this market has ever asked me which of the three a person ought to be reading, and I am not going to stand here and tell them which of the three I would read, because I have never read any of them.**"

A man further down the row said the thing that is said.

"Then what is it for."

"**It is a board. It comes down at the fourth hour and it goes up at the fourth hour and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it.**"

Nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody disagreed with him out loud. A woman at a stall put her hand flat on the top of her own board and left it there, and a man further down the row turned a barrow about a foot and set it down again, and about four people at that end of the market had read that board that morning as they came through and not one of the nine who came through it said which of the three was the one anybody should be reading.

---

The first pass went from the bottom left-hand corner to the top right-hand one and took about as long as it takes a man to count nine, and the water on that board came off it grey. He laid the rag on the wet stone, turned it over, laid it back on the same stone and took it up again, and did the second half of the board the same way. He put the board down flat on the flags with its face up and put the whole weight of his hand on the middle of it and looked at the three sets of figures through the water that was still standing on them, and then he did it again.

What he wanted from the second pass was that it would be the last thing he did before the light came off the flags, because the light on the wall above that board was the only hour of the day in which he did not have to look at what was on it. He had got the second pass and he had got the order of it, and the reason he wanted that hour was the whole of the reason, and the whole of the thinking behind it took him the length of putting a coat on.

He wrung the rag out a second time. The water off it went over the stone lip of the trough and along the flags under the trestle two stalls along, and it stood in a band about the width of a hand along the length of that trestle and stayed there. The leg of the trestle stood in it to the depth of a finger.

Nobody asked Adrian Vale what he was doing and nobody spoke to him at all that morning. There was no instrument in four hundred miles that would ever have told anybody that the man who washed that board every morning and the man who carried the water down the flags at one end of the same market had been within a hand's width of each other before the light was properly on.

---

The man of about thirty-four came out from behind his own trestle and got both hands under the frame of it and lifted it off the flags and set it down a foot further along the row, on dry ones. He did it in one movement and did not hurry it, and the bare back of his own board came a foot further out into that row with it. He stood looking at the band of water on the flags where the trestle had been and at the wet leg of it, and then he put his hand flat on the top of the bare back of his own board and left it there.

Nobody said anything to him about it. The trestle going a foot along that row was seen by about four people at that end of the market and worked out by every one of them to be the water under it, and not one of them said a word about it to him or to each other, and the man who had washed the board was at the trough with his back to the row and did not turn round.

---

At about the fourth hour the board went back up on its two nails with all three sets of figures on it, none of them rubbed out, and what washing went on below it stopped at the top of the board where the wall begins, and the wall above that board is as dirty now as it was at the fourth hour on any morning of this flood. The trough under it was fuller at the fourth hour than it had been at the fourth hour on any morning since the water came, and about four people standing along that row heard the water go over the stone lip of it and looked up, and went on down the row.
