# Chapter 0545 — A Sheet Out Of A City In A Satchel With A Flap

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour on the two hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, with the satchel with the flap under his arm and the flap down, and he stopped on the step with the satchel against his leg and waited there while the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the folded sheet up off the table under the one window with both hands and carried it to the near end of that table and set it down. The two hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there is no name on it. About nine people were in that room by that hour and not one of them had been sent for anything.

---

She opened the flap of that satchel and put the sheet in flat, the two folds in it going in the way the satchel was hung, and there was a length of string round the near corner of that sheet with a knot in it that nobody in that room undid that morning and nobody in that room asked about. She put her two hands flat on the top of it for a moment and then took them off. She folded the flap down over it and put the strap round twice and did not put the buckle on the strap and did not say one word about the buckle.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living took that satchel off the table with his left hand under it and his right hand on the strap and nobody in that room asked him what was in it and nobody in that room asked him to open it and he did not open it and nobody in that room is going to ask him to open it. That sheet has a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under that heading and a figure of this city in that one place and nothing else on it anywhere, and there is no place on it under that heading for the person who made that figure and there never was one.

---

A man at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the table.

"You know what is in that and you have never opened one in your life."

"**I know what is in that and I have carried five notices and a return and a sheet on the four hundred and first day of this flood and a sheet on the four hundred and fifty-first day of this flood without opening one of them, and I am carrying this one down that stair and out along that row, and there is nobody in this city who is going to be told what is in it by me.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him, and about four people in that room had worked out what is in that satchel and about four had not, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not say one word about it and put the bone down on that table where it goes.

A woman near the step said the thing that is said, to the satchel.

"You could put it in your own bag and carry it down that row yourself and not say a word about it."

"**I am going to put it in this one and take it down that row and I am not going to say a word about it, and if anybody in this city is going to open that sheet it will not be me and it will not be this morning, and that is the last thing I have got to say about it and I have said it.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it, and about four people in that room had worked out that he knows what is in that satchel and about four had not.

Four documents have come down that road in this flood and three of them have four places ruled under a heading and one of them has a single place with a figure in it, and the four of them have never been joined together and never been laid side by side and never been compared out loud in this room, and that sheet that is in that satchel is a fifth document and a different shape from the first one of its shape that is standing in this city on a shelf behind that bench.

---

He went down that stair and out along the flags of that row with the satchel under his arm and the strap round it twice, and at the near gate he turned up the road and did not stop at the gate and did not say anything to anybody there, and nobody in that row asked him what was in that satchel as he went past, and the flap of it stayed down the whole of the length of that row.

At the far end of that bench the man of about fifty-seven was sitting with his hands flat on the table in front of him. Over the back of his chair there is a length of new rope, and it has not been used, and no hand in that room lifted it this morning, and nobody in that room said one word about it and nobody in that room asked that man anything at all.

What has been achieved is that a figure of this city is outside this city, and what has been lost is that a figure of this city is outside this city, and that is the same sentence twice and it is the whole of it. No document was defeated by it and nobody was rescued by it and nobody was defeated by it and nothing was settled by it and nothing in this flood has an instrument for bringing a figure back, and nobody in that room said either half of that sentence out loud.

That satchel went out along the flags of that row with the strap round it twice and nobody in this city opened it and nobody in this city is going to, and the table under that one window has nothing on it at all.
