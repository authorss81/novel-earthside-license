# Chapter 0529 — A Wall Read Across And A Book Read Down And A Man Standing At The Head Of A Column

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register took the register off its shelf behind the bench at about the eighth hour on the two hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and carried it to the table under the one window and opened it at the page it is always opened at and stood it up on its spine and let the two covers hold it open, and she put a pen down on that table beside it and an ink bottle beside the pen, and no other hand in that room went near that register at any hour of that morning. Behind the register on that shelf there is a second slate standing on its edge with a day scratched across the head of it and nothing under that day. It was not picked up. It was not turned over. Nothing was written on it. No hand in that room but the keeper's went near that shelf, and hers did not go near it either. About nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

Adrian Vale stood at the shoulder of that book at the head of the table with his left hand on the shoulder of it and his right hand on the pen, and he did not look down into the open page at any hour of that morning, and nobody in that room saw him look down into it, and the ink stood open on the table about a hand's width from his wrist and the five lines' worth of room in the middle column of that book were empty.

---

Out of the window at the back of that room the store's board is on its two nails on the wall of the market below, and there are three sets of figures on it and none of the three has been rubbed out, and one of them is an account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood that came down a road four hundred miles long with an office's own hand on it, and the other two are figures of this city's own in chalk that were made in this city and that do not agree with each other, and the wall above the top of that board is the colour it is every morning of this flood. A wall of figures is read across. A book of figures is read down. There is no instrument in this city that will put those two things in the same order and there never has been one.

A woman sitting at the near end of that bench said the thing that is said, to the window.

"There is a book open on that table and there is a wall outside that window and nobody in this room has said which of the two a person is meant to be reading."

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at the back of that room at about the ninth hour with his own board under his arm and he got as far as that doorway and stopped in it, because that table is across the width of that room and there is nowhere for a man to stand between it and the wall but where that man was standing, and Adrian Vale did not move from the head of that table with his hands on the pen and the ink, and nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-nine to come further in and he did not push past anybody to get there, and he stood in that doorway about as long as a man stands at a gate and said his four seconds' worth from where he was standing.

"**That board is read across and that book is read down, and a figure of this city's own goes on one of them the way it goes on the other one, and there is no instrument in this city that will ever tell anybody which of the two a person is meant to be using, and I am not going to be the man who says use that one.**"

---

Nobody in that room said that he was right and nobody in that room said a word against it either, and he came the rest of the way into that room at the tenth hour and put his board against the bench with the face of it to the wall and sat down at the far end of it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register wrote nothing in that book all day and nothing in the column on the right of it either, and the middle column of that book has five lines' worth of room in it and nothing whatever written in it, and at about the eleventh hour she closed the register and put the pen down on the table beside it and carried it back to its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut.

Adrian Vale took his hands off that book and off the pen and off the ink at the tenth hour and moved out of the way of that doorway, and nobody in that room said one word to him about it and nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room was waiting for him to do anything at all. There is no place on that sheet on the table for the person who made any figure, and the middle column of that book holds one figure that was entered on an earlier day of this flood, and the five lines' worth of room above that one figure are empty, and the wall outside that window has three sets of figures on it that nobody in that room has said is the one anybody should be reading.

The register is on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and the second slate behind it with a day across the head of it and nothing under that day, and the pen is lying on the table under that window with the nib of it wet and the bottle of ink standing open beside it and not stoppered, and nothing was written on the page of that book with either of them, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came into that room at the tenth hour and only after a man at that table moved out of his way, and nobody in that room said a word about it.
