# Chapter 0527 — A Slate A Hand's Width Further Back And A Hand On The Ledge Under It

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up the stair at the back of that room at about the seventh hour on the two hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, with the satchel with the flap under his arm, and he put the satchel down on the bench at the far end of the room and took his hands off the strap of it. The two hundred and twelfth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door at the foot of that stair and there was no name on that door, and about nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything. Behind the bench there is a shelf on two brackets, and the register stands on that shelf with its spine to the wall and the cover shut, and behind the register a second slate stands on its edge on the same shelf with its face in to the wall, and there is a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

---

A man near the door said the thing that is said, to the shelf and not to anybody in the room.

"It is further back than it was yesterday."

"**It is the only thing in this room that moves and it moves because those two brackets are not the same height, and I have got a satchel on that bench and I have not opened it in nine years and I am not going to open it in this room.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it. Nobody in that room asked him to put anything under that slate and nobody in that room asked him not to, and the satchel stayed shut on that bench with the flap of it down, and it was not picked up and not opened by anybody in that room at any hour of that morning, and there is a flat thing about the length of a hand in the bottom of it and he did not know that and did not look and was not told.

He got down onto the floor of that room with his back to the bench and put his left hand flat on the ledge under that second slate and got the fingers of it over the front edge of the shelf, and that was all of it. On the ledge under that slate there is a straight break in the dust with a soft edge on one side of it and a hard edge on the other, and the soft edge is the way it comes, and that break has been on that ledge since the shelf went in on those brackets before the water came. The second slate walked back down the slope of that shelf, which is what it does every time a hand goes near it, and it came up against the wall behind it and it did not stop for his hand, and the dust on the ledge went out from under the slate in a line about the length of a hand and stopped where his wrist was.

He wanted something flat to go under the far edge of that slate. He did not have one within reach and he did not go and get one and he did not send anybody to get one and he did not put anything under it. He held that shelf where it was for about as long as a man holds a door and then he took his hands off it, and he got the grit that was on that ledge into the two lines of his palms doing it.

---

The second slate was a hand's width further back on that shelf when he stood up than it had been when he came into that room, and it is still standing on its edge with its face in to the wall, and there is nothing under it. The register stood on that shelf with its spine to the wall and the cover shut and it did not come off that shelf that morning, and no hand in that room but the register-keeper's went near that shelf and hers did not go near it either, and nobody in that room said one word about what is on that slate and nobody in that room asked anybody anything about it.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and had not moved off that end of it for the rest of that morning, and the two grey lines in the palms of the man who carried a thing up that stair and put his hands on a ledge and took them off again are the only mark that morning made on him, and the slate is a hand's width further back on its shelf than it was at the seventh hour, and there is nothing under it.
