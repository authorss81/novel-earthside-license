# Chapter 0546 — Wet Flags And A Bucket Standing In The Middle Of A Row

At about the fourth hour on the two hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, the man of about twenty-seven who washes that store's board took it off its two nails above the trough, put it down into the water, took it out again with all three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and hung it back on those nails. The rag went on the stone lip of the step and the bucket went into the trough and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board took his hands off it and stood at the foot of that wall for a while with his head back, and the wall above the top of that board is the colour it keeps and nothing went over it that morning.

---

The flags at that end of that row were wet to the near gate at the third hour. They had been wet at the fourth hour the day before and they had been wet at the fourth hour the day before that, and the water comes off that row and along the length of it and stands in the hollow of the flags at the end nearest the channel and does not go off them, and about four people in that row work at those flags with something flat and about four do not.

Adrian Vale was on those flags before the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was. He had the rag in his right hand and his left hand flat on the rim of the trough, and he worked the rag along the top of the flags from the store towards the near gate, pushing the standing water ahead of him in a long flat sheet about the width of a board, and the water went along the row ahead of his hand and off the end of it into the channel at the near end of that row. He got to the near gate and worked back the other way, and the flags behind him were wetter than the flags in front of him had been.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was out on those flags at about the third hour with his bucket in his hand before Adrian Vale was at the end of them, and he did not put anything on those flags and he did not take anything off them and he did not use them for anything at all, and he went up the row with his bucket and did not come back down it.

The flags were not dry at the fourth hour. The board went down into that trough at the fourth hour and came back up on its nails at the fourth hour with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and the flags under it were wet, and about four people in that row had watched a man push water off the flags and about four had not.

---

The man of about sixty was at the near end of that row at about the sixth hour with the handcart behind him and its bar down, and in the bottom of that cart there is a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord and there is a coil of rope over the side of it, and that cart is not one of the three barrows that went down a road four hundred miles long with a man on the wheel of each of them, and it did not go down that road and nobody in that row has ever asked him what it is doing in that market.

He came up that row at the sixth hour with the cart and Adrian Vale's bucket was standing in the middle of that row on the flags where the water had been pushed off them, and the front wheel of that cart could not get past it. He took the shafts up in both hands, lifted the front of that cart over the bucket, set it down again on the far side of it, and went on up that row that way with one wheel off the ground for about as long as it takes a man to count nine.

A woman at a stall two along said the thing that is said, to the flags.

"There was no water coming down that row yesterday."

"**There is a bucket standing in the middle of that row and there is a channel at the end of it taking more than it took yesterday, and I have got a cart to get up this row with and I am not going to stand in the middle of a market working out whose water it is, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it.**"

Nobody in that row asked him about it. Nobody in that row said one word to him about a bucket standing in the middle of a row that had been in a trough four hours before, and nobody in that row knew whose hands had put it there, and the man of about sixty did not ask and did not say anything else about it, and about four people in that row saw a man lift a cart over a bucket and about four did not.

---

He came back down that row at about the seventh hour with the cart and stopped at the near gate with the shafts down in the wet, and the mallet was still in the bottom of it and the rope was still over the side of it, and nobody in that row has asked him about any of that and nobody is going to.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was at the step with his back to the row and his hands in the bucket and did not turn round at any hour of that morning, and nobody in that row could have told anybody what was under a boot at the near gate that morning.

---

The bucket went back into the trough at about the ninth hour and the trough was on its own spot against that wall the whole morning and did not move, and the rag went on the stone lip of the step and did not go in the trough at any hour of that day. No figure was put on that board at any hour of that day and no mouth in that row said which of the three sets of figures on it is the one anybody should be reading, and the stone above the top of that board was not washed and no second person put a hand on it.

The channel at the near end of that row is carrying more water than it was carrying at the fourth hour and the flags from the step to the near gate are wet, and there is a handcart two rows up that row with one wheel of it off the ground, and a man at the near gate who has not said to anybody where that water came from.
