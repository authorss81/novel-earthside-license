# Chapter 0550 — A Board Washed Clean And A Road With Three Barrows On It

The store's board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour on the two hundred and thirty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and went into the trough against that wall, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put both hands flat on the face of it and turned it over once and laid it across the mouth of that trough with the wet side to the air and worked the rag across it from the bottom left-hand corner to the top right-hand one and did the second half of it the same way. He put the rag on the stone lip of the step and carried the bucket to the channel and back and back again and then carried the board up and put it on its two nails. There are three sets of figures on it and not one of the three has been rubbed out, and the top of that board is where his washing stops and the stone above that line is the colour it has been the whole of this flood.

---

The left-hand set of figures on that board is the account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood and it came down that road four hundred miles in an office's own hand, and it is the one anybody at one end of that market can walk past and read. The other two sets were put on in chalk in this city, the first of them on a day this city knows and the second of them on a later one, and the two of them do not agree with one another and neither of them has ever been rubbed out, and about four people a day walk past the end of that row and read one of them.

Over that market there is a room, and in that room, on a shelf behind a bench, there is a book. Five figures stand in the column of it that holds figures, one under another in the order they were made in, with the day beside each one of them. That column has five lines ruled into it and there is not room in it for a sixth. Beside it, on the right of that page, another column runs the whole height of the page with nothing in it at all from the top to the bottom. That book was not opened on this morning and no hand but the keeper's has been near that shelf, and behind that register on the same shelf a second slate stands on its edge with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the window of that room at about the ninth hour with his own board under his arm and the face of it turned out into the room. Nineteen rates stand on that board in three columns and along the bottom of the middle column of it there is bare board about as wide as two fingers and nothing whatever in it. He looked out of that window down at that board on its two nails and then out along the road past the near gate, and then he said it, and he said it once.

A woman who had come up that stair behind him said the thing that is said, to his back.

"Which of those two is the one a person is meant to be reading."

"**A wall of figures is read across and a book of figures is read down, and one of the two of those came four hundred miles to this city and the other one was made in this city on a morning by a person, and they are of one kind of thing and they are not the same thing, and there is nothing in four hundred miles that will turn one of them round and stand it beside the other one.**"

Nobody in that room asked him which of the two a person should be using a second time and he did not offer one. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody in that room said that he was right, and about four people in that room had worked out that those two figures are of one kind of thing and about four had not.

---

Along the top of the road that goes out past the last named house there is a fence put up on sixteen willow posts with eleven withies tied on to them with one cord, from the corner by that house round to the corner where that road turns, and nobody has measured it and nobody has taken a cord to it and the corner in it has not been measured either. Three barrows went down that road with a man on the wheel of each of them on the three hundred and ninety-first day of this flood and they are one hundred and fifty-nine days out from this city at this morning, and a party of four set out down it and one of the four said no before anybody asked her, and nobody in this city can be shown one person on that road.

The light came off the flags at the end of that row at about the ninth hour and went up the face of that wall to the height it goes to on any morning of this flood and stopped there, and a date was in chalk on a door at the top of that stair with no name under it, and a board was on its two nails and a book was on its shelf and a fence was along the top of that road, and three barrows were a hundred and fifty-nine days out on that road from the day they went down it.
