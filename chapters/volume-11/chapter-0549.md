# Chapter 0549 — A Slate On A Sill And A Lump Of Chalk That Is Not His

Adrian Vale came up the flags at the end of that market at about the fifth hour on the two hundred and thirty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, with a slate under his own arm and nothing written on it, and he carried it the length of that row and set it down on the sill of that store face up with the stone of that sill under the four corners of it and took his two hands off it. The store's board was on its two nails above the trough with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out, and the wall above the top edge of that board was not washed, and the store's chalk was in its box on that step with the lid of it standing open and about a thumb's length of it showing.

The board came off its two nails at about the fourth hour and went into the trough and came out of the wet with three sets of figures on it and none of the three rubbed out and went back up on the two nails, and the rag went on the stone lip of the step and the bucket went into the trough, and the man of about twenty-seven who washes that board put his hands flat on the face of that board and turned it over once and did not look up at the wall above the top of it. Nobody in that row put a figure on that board at any hour of that morning and nobody in that row said which of the three sets of figures on it is the one anybody should be reading, and the store's chalk is in its box with the lid standing open and has not been put to anything today.

---

There are four figures of one kind of thing in a book in a room over this market, one under another in the order they were made in with the day standing beside each of them, and the order of them is the same order every time anybody reads them and no figure off any of the four is written on this slate and no fifth morning of that count exists in this city.

Adrian Vale turned that slate once on that sill so that its long edge went along the sill and then took the lump of chalk out of that box on that step. That chalk is not his and it is the store's chalk and it is a stub with about a thumb's length of it showing in a box with a lid that does not shut. He held it in his own hand for about as long as it takes a man to walk to the end of that row and back, and he put it back in that box with about a thumb's length of it showing again, and he left the lid of that box standing open where it goes.

He did not write anything on that slate. There is one piece of chalk in this flood that a man writes a figure of this city with in his own hand, and it has been in the inside breast pocket of the coat of the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall since the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, which is the forty-third day of the Short Month, and it is not going to be taken out of that pocket today, and the man it belongs to is two stalls along with his own hand flat over that pocket.

---

A woman with a stall in that row came along that sill with the foot of her own board in her two hands at about the seventh hour to put her board up on it where it goes every morning, and there was a slate on that sill that had not been there the day before, and she put the foot of her own board down on the face of that slate to hold it where it was, because the wind at that end of that row comes along it and takes light things off a sill, and she did not ask whose slate it was and nobody in that row told her.

A woman with a stall in that row said the thing that is said, to the sill.

"That slate was not on there yesterday and there is nothing on it."

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at the lump of chalk in its box on the step and then at his own hand flat over his own inside breast pocket, and said it once.

"**That is not mine either, and there is a lump of chalk in a box on that step that is not mine and I have used it once on a step in this flood and it went grey, and the piece I would write those four figures with is in this pocket and it is not coming out of it.**"

Somebody at the end of that row said the thing that is said, to the sill.

"You could write them in the box chalk and nobody would know."

"**I could and I am not going to, and I would rather a slate stood on that sill all morning with nothing on it than have those four figures on it in a hand that is not the one they were made in.**"

Nobody in that row said that he was right. Nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him and nobody in that row asked him about either of those two chalks, and about four people in that row had looked at the slate on that sill and about four had not.

---

A slate is standing face up on the sill of that store with nothing on it and the foot of a woman's board is on it, and a box of chalk is on the step below with the lid open, and two stalls along a man has kept his own hand flat over his own pocket all morning.
