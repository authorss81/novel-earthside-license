# Chapter 1007 — Two Hands On That Bench And Neither Letting Go

The man who came up that stair came up it at about the fifth hour with his own coat open and one sleeve of it wet to the shoulder and the other sleeve dry, and he went to the window at the side of that room and stood at it with his own two hands hanging at his own sides and looked out of it at nothing at all.

It was Sunday, the six hundred and ninety-second day of the Bare Month. There was a fog on eleven miles of flats that came up to the roof line at the far end of that market and did not go off it until about the ninth hour, and the light came in under that awning about the eighth hour and lay down the length of that bench and stopped about the middle of the wood. The bar lay along the top step of that stair and not in its two sockets, and the trough on the flags beside it had ice in it about the depth of a thumb and nothing had been fetched for it, and the rag lay folded on the stone lip of that step.

On the outside of that door at the foot of that stair the day stood out in chalk with about a finger's width of that writing gone down into the groove between the two planks, and under that day the bare piece of board stood clear of the near plank by the width of a thumb and carried the one mark that is on it.

---

The keeper had the far end of that bench from the fifth hour with her own register open in front of her and her own hand flat on the page of it, and she went down the column that holds figures and came back up it.

There are six strokes in that column and a day standing against each of the six and not one of the six struck out, and the seventh place at the foot of it is bare wood of the colour of the six above it. The second book lay open beside that register with its cover slab drawn back off the leaf under it, and the crack along the whole length of the column of that leaf has filled and gone hard and stands a hair proud of the stone, and the dry cloth is lying in it and stayed in it all morning and no hand lifted any part of it out.

That sheet lay on that bench a hand's width in from its right edge, square with the wood since the middle of the week before, with six lines on it in two blocks of three and the space under the lower block bare from there to the near edge of it.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up at the fifth hour with his own board under his arm and put it down on the near end of that bench with its face out and its foot toward the door, and he stood at that end of it with his own two hands on the two ends of that board.

The woman of about fifty-two stood at the far end of that bench past both books with her own shoulder to the wood behind the far end of it and her own hands at the sides of her dress. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the near wall with the satchel up against his own ribs and the flap of it hanging down.

---

At about the ninth hour the man who came up that stair came off that window and went down that room and put his own two hands flat on the wood of that bench at the near end of it.

At the far end of that same bench the keeper had her own hand flat on the wood at the same moment, and neither of them took it off, and neither of them put a foot on that bench to get nearer to the other one, and they did not speak to each other at all that day.

That bench is about eleven feet long and it was moved about a foot along toward that wall in an earlier week and had not been put back, and the two of them were at the two ends of it with about nine feet of that wood between their hands.

The two books lay open side by side in the middle of it.

One of them carries a version of a day in a hand that is the keeper's own and one of them carries a version of the same day in a hand that is not the keeper's, and the two of them have been open side by side on that wood since before the water came and have never been compared and no mouth in that room has ever said which of the two of them is the one that happened.

Nobody in that room put the two of them side by side on that morning with the intention of comparing them, and nobody in that room came to that bench and read one and then the other, and the woman of about fifty-two standing at the far end of it past both of them did not turn her own head down at either. There is no third version of that day in this city. There are those two and there is nothing anybody has ever put together to set them beside each other with.

What is between those two hands on that bench is a piece of wood with two books open on it and a sheet lying beside them with six lines on it and a stool standing against the wall along the far side of that room with nobody sitting on it. It does what a thing does on a bench. It stays where it was put and it holds still while the two ends of it are held at both ends and it does not move of its own accord.

---

The morning went on.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came down the face of his own board with the cloth at about the tenth hour, and the cloth came off the face at the foot of the middle column, and he folded the cloth back over his own shoulder. The keeper went down her own column and came back up it.

About four people a day went past the foot of that stair on their own business with the light on the flags, and the fog stood off that row until the ninth hour and then came off it in about half an hour, and the walking spaces of that row came pale along their whole length as the light got to them. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket up that stair four times with the water standing flat in it.

At about the middle of the afternoon the man who came up that stair took his own two hands off that bench and went back to the window at the side of that room and stood at it again with his own hands hanging at his own sides until the light had gone off the flags.

The keeper's own hand came off that wood at the near end of the far end of that bench at about the same time and she put her own hand flat on the page of that register and went down the column that holds figures again.

Nobody said anything about any part of that morning and nobody in that room asked anybody else for anything at any hour of it.

---

The second of those two books lay open on that bench where it has lain, and what was under it was the wood of that bench, and the wood under it stood a shade darker than the wood either side of it along the whole length of that book, and the edge of that book had not moved off the place it was put.
