# Chapter 1005 — Said Out Loud And The Cloth Went Down

About nine people were in that room in daylight on that morning with the date in chalk on the outside of the door behind them, and the first thing that happened in it was that the keeper told them what it cost her, and no mouth in that room said a word back to her about any part of it.

It was Friday, the six hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month. The morning was close and grey and the light came in under that awning about the sixth hour and lay along that bench as far as the middle of the wood, and the wet stood in the joints of the flags inside that room about the depth of a grain of rice and had come up that stair on the feet of about nine people and had been walked into the dust along the walking space at the foot of the bench.

The keeper had the far end of that bench with her own register open in front of her and the second book lying open beside it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the near end of it with his own board face out on the wood and the cloth over his own shoulder. The woman of about fifty-two stood at the far end of that bench past both books with her own shoulder to the wood behind the far end of it and her own hands at the sides of her dress, and that stool stood against the wall along the far side of that room where she had set it down in her own two hands. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the near wall with the satchel up against his own ribs and the flap of it hanging down. The rest of them stood along the walls and went on talking about what they had come up that stair about.

She said the cost first, in her own mouth, in her own words, before anything else that morning.

**"I am going to read the six days out loud in this room this morning, one after another, the six days that stand against the six figures in this column, and everybody in here is going to hear me read them. It costs me that when I read a day out loud in front of people it stops being a day against a figure and it starts being a thing nine people are carrying about, and I have no way in this room of standing in front of any of them afterwards and saying it is not so. I know that and I am going to do it anyway, and I am not going to tell anybody in this room that they are right about it, and nobody has to do anything about it," ** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the far end of that bench with her own hand flat on the wood and the two books open in front of her and the date on the outside of that door behind her own shoulder.

Nobody in that room said a word back to her about any part of it. Nobody argued with her and nobody improved on one word of it and nobody thanked her for saying it, and nobody asked her afterwards what she meant by it.

Then she read the six days out loud, one after another, from the head of that column to the foot of it, and it took about as long as it takes to say a thing twice.

The seventh place at the foot of that column is bare wood of the colour of the six above it, and she did not read a seventh day, because there is not one.

---

At about the middle of that morning the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board said a thing out loud in that room.

He said it in the middle of his own work and the way a person says a thing that has been said to him. He was at the near end of that bench with his own two hands on the two ends of his own board, and he had the near end of that bench with his own board lying face out on the wood in front of him, and he said it into the middle of that room about the width of the space at the foot of the middle column of that board.

What he said was a thing that had been said in that room on an earlier day. It came back into that room in words his own mouth had not used, and he said it as he would have said anything else he had been told, at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary course of that morning.

He did not say where he had got it. Nobody in that room asked him where he had got it. Nobody said a word to him about it at all, and nobody asked him to say it again and nobody asked him to explain it and nobody asked him whether he had got it right.

About nine feet off from him at the far end of that bench the keeper had her own hand on the wood and the register open in front of her, and she went down the column that holds figures and came back up it while he was talking, and she did not stop with her hand where it was and she did not look up off the page, and she went on with what her hands were doing from the head of that column to the foot of it and back up it again.

The woman of about fifty-two stood at the far end of that bench and did not turn her own head about at it. The carrier turned about off that near wall once and looked at the flags and turned about again. Three of the people along that wall went on with what they were saying to each other across the middle of that room without breaking it off. Nobody interrupted him. Nobody said one word that improved on one word he had said.

That room was not surprised and it was not prepared, and it did not do one thing about it. It went on with the middle of that morning exactly as it had been going on with the middle of the morning before, and the light came along that bench a finger's width further while he was talking, and the wet in the joints of the flags inside that room stood where it had stood.

Nobody in that room said a word about what had just been said, and nobody in that room said a word about it afterwards either.

---

What went out of that room in that man's mouth went down that stair and along that row and out past the store's end of it.

There is no leaf on either of those two books with a scratch of it on it. There is no board in that market and no chalk anywhere in this city that carries one word of it. Nothing went on the sheet that lies on that bench, and nothing went on the outside of that door, and nothing went into the grit at the store's end of that row, and the register and the second book were not written on at any hour of that day by any hand, and the second book was not picked up and not turned over and not closed. The seventh place at the foot of that column stayed bare wood of the colour of the six above it.

The two books on that bench went on carrying two accounts of one day and nobody in that room compared them and nobody in that room said which of them is the one that happened.

---

At about the seventh hour the woman of about sixty-nine with her tin came up that stair with the tin under her right arm and her own stick in her own hand and came the whole way up into that room and stood about two feet short of the near end of that bench, and she stayed there about as long as it takes to say a thing twice, and then she went back down that stair and along that row and off out of it.

The tin was not opened and nothing came out of it and nobody in that room put anything to her. She has not said anything out loud in that room since the morning she came up that stair with her own hand on the wood and told the keeper in her own words what she meant to put to a man.

The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket up that stair four times with the water standing flat in it, and the ice in that trough at the top of that step was whole about the depth of a thumb and nothing had been fetched for it and nothing had been broken in it. The bar lay along the top step of that stair and not in its two sockets.

About four people a day went past the foot of that stair while the light was on the flags, and not one word of anything said in that room came past the bottom step of it.

---

At about the middle of the afternoon that man took the cloth off his own shoulder and came down the face of his own board with it from the top of the top column, and the cloth came off the face at the foot of the middle column and stopped there, and he folded the cloth back over his own shoulder and stood looking down at the space at the foot of that middle column with his own two hands on the two ends of that board.

Nobody in that room had said a word about what he had said at the middle of that morning, and nobody in that room said one now.
