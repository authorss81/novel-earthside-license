# Chapter 1000 — A Sixth Line Drawn Before The Light

There was a line in the grit at the store's end of that row that had not been standing there at the light on the morning before. It was about the length of a hand, and it was a foot to one side of the other line at that end of the row.

It was Sunday, the six hundred and eighty-fifth day of the Bare Month, and the thousandth morning of that flood. The night had been hard and clear for the fifteenth morning running and the sun was up before the light and came over the roof line at the far end of that market about the fourth hour. The air went thin along the ground early, so that the light got in under the awning at the store's end and lay the whole length of the walking spaces before that row had finished waking. Four hundred yards of flags ran from the store's end of that row to where that row gave out into the side of a yard.

There are two lines in the grit at the store's end of that row and four going up from them at four joints toward the far end of that market. The newest of the two at that end of the row was made before the light that morning, and the stick came up out of the grit with a ridge of it standing along the stroke and a hollow at the near end of it. Nothing had walked on any part of that stroke since it was made. Nobody has asked the woman who made them what any of them are for. Nobody asked her that morning and nobody asked her on any morning before it.

The trough at the store's end of that row had ice in it about the depth of a thumb, and the thread of water out of the crack in its near end came over the lip of the step and stopped in the joint of the flags a foot short of the walking space, where it has stopped every morning of the week. The rag was where it lies every morning, folded on the stone lip of that step. The bar was on the top step of that stair and not in its two sockets, and the two of them were empty in the far side of the door below.

At the far end of that market a board stood on the outside wall on its one nail above its step, and the hole of a second nail was empty above it. The wood around the lip of that hole had gone soft a whole finger's width from the hole all the way round, and pale dust lay in the gap behind the bottom edge of that board about the depth of a grain of rice, along the whole length of it.

---

The woman of about sixty-nine with her tin came along that row at about the seventh hour with the tin under her right arm and her own stick in her own hand.

She went the length of that row and stopped at every joint along it and stood over each of them with the end of the stick held down against the stone and her own thumb on the top of it, and she lifted it and carried on. She stood over the two lines at the store's end of that row about as long as it takes to look at a row of trestles from end to end and be doing nothing else while you do it, and she made none of them.

She came back down that row at about the middle of the morning and went past the store's end of it out to where the flags give out into the side of that yard, and she stood there a while looking at the ground on the far side of it. Then she came back along them and went up that row again with the tin under her arm.

She offered nothing to anybody in that row, and she went on her own way along the walking spaces and not the joints.

---

About four people a day went along that row on their own business with the light on it.

Two of them looked down at the grit at the store's end of that row as they went past and could have said afterwards that there were two strokes in it at that end and one of them was standing up along its whole length. Two of them went along that row with their own eyes on the walking spaces of it and could not have said what was in the joints at the store's end of it.

Nobody stopped. A woman with a basket on her own arm went up the near walking space and looked down at that end of the row as she went by it and went on up the row. A man with a coil over his own shoulder went up the far side and did not look down at anything. A boy came down the middle of the flags with his own hands in his pockets and put a foot on the walking space and went on and could not have said afterwards what was chalked on the board at the far end of that market. A woman going down that row stopped about as long as it takes to read a thing twice, and looked up at the face of a board on a trestle two along, and went on down that row.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own board face in against the wood of his own trestle at the store's end of that row, with the bare back of it turned out into the row behind his own shoulder.

The near corner of the walking space at the foot of that trestle was standing up on its edge about the depth of two fingers with the pale damp packed earth under it, and the foot of that trestle was still gone into the flag it went down into.

At the trestle two along stood the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board with his own board face out in front of him and the cloth over his own shoulder. He came down the face of that board with the cloth, and the cloth came off the face at the foot of the middle column, and he folded it back over his own shoulder and looked up that row once and went on with what he was doing.

The man of about twenty-seven had that step to himself all morning, and the bucket stood on the stone beside his own foot with the rag over the rim of it. He filled it and carried it up that stair four times with the water standing flat in it, and on the last of those four he stayed on the flags with his own hand on the outside of the rim of that trough and looked at the ice in it.

On the outside of that door a day stood in chalk with about a finger's width of that writing gone down into the groove between the two planks. Under the day the bare board stood clear of the near plank by the width of a thumb and carried the one mark that is on it.

---

The shade of that awning came off the walking spaces at the store's end of that row at about the middle of the afternoon and went up onto the wall of that store above the doors. The light went along the walking spaces of that row and off the flags at the far end of it and did not stop anywhere on the way.
