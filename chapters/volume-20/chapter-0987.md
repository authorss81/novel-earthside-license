# Chapter 0987 — The Chalk Stays And One Thing Gets Asked

The inside breast pocket of that coat had a piece of chalk in it and the pocket was buttoned, and it had been buttoned that morning before he came out, and the chalk had not come out of it.

It was Monday, the six hundred and seventy-second day of the Bare Month. The sun got up over the far end of that market before the light did and was along that row by the fourth hour, walking spaces pale and hard and the joints of them still black, the way they had stood for a week.

The ice in the trough at the top of that step was whole about the depth of a thumb, and the bar lay out of its two sockets on the top step of that stair. At the far end of that market the board on the outside wall stood on its one nail above its step with the hole of a second nail empty above it. The wood around the lip of that hole had gone soft a whole finger's width from the hole all the way round after the rain of the week before, and there was nothing in it and nothing went into it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along went at the near foot of his own trestle again at about the ninth hour, on ground that had been dry for three days.

He went along the walking space with his own hand flat at the foot of that trestle and worked at the earth round it with his own thumbnail first, and then with the edge of his own board, which is what he wanted to try on the wet ground and could not. The foot of that trestle has gone down into its flag a little at a time and the ground has come up round it while it was doing that. About half an inch of that foot is under the top of the flag, with the earth of the walking space packed against the sides of it.

He took the wood off the sides of that foot with the flat of his own board. About an inch of it came away on the near side and about half an inch on the other, in shavings the length of a finger. The trestle stood exactly as it had stood before, and the foot of it was still gone into the flag it went down into.

He got up off the walking space and stood looking at the near corner of that flag. It has been standing up on its edge about the depth of two fingers since the ninth day of that flood, with nothing under it holding it up, and there is pale damp packed earth under it. The foot of that trestle is still in the flag it went into.

His own hand went flat against the front of his own coat over that pocket at about the ninth hour, and it came off again. It went there again at about the tenth hour and stayed a while and came off. It went there a third time while he was kneeling on the walking space and it came off and it went there a fourth time in the middle of the afternoon.

The chalk was a stub about the length of a thumb with the point of it gone round, and it had been in that pocket since before the water came, and it did not come out of that pocket on that day. He had the hand for it and he had the chalk to do it with, and the board with the space at the foot of its middle column was two stalls along and belonged to another man, and nobody at that trestle had asked him to write on it.

---

The heap of shavings at the foot of that walking space stood there for the rest of that day. They were the pale colour of the inside of that trestle where it had not been wet all that flood, and they lay on the grit in a crescent about the length of a hand, and nobody walked on any of them.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down that stair at about the eighth hour and went to the store's end of that row. She stood at the top of that step under the awning with her own hands at the sides of her dress.

She stood there about as long as it takes to look at a row of trestles from end to end and be doing nothing else while you do it, and then she went along the walking space past the four trestles with nothing at them and came back and stood where she had been.

He got his own hand at the foot of that trestle and looked up at her along four hundred yards of flags and put one thing to her, out loud, in that row, standing up.

He put it to her about whether anybody was going to come along that row and look at the near foot of his own trestle, and whether anybody had ever come and looked at one.

She answered him. It was answered in the ordinary course of a row with the trestles going on about their own business on either side of them, and the answering took about as long as the asking, and nobody in that row stopped what he was doing while it was put. What she said went into that row with the rest of what that row was saying that hour and it went nowhere else, and nothing on any surface in this city holds one word of what either of them said to the other.

Nobody in that row thanked either of them, and nobody told either of them that what they had said was right. He went back to the near foot of his own trestle and took a fifth shaving off the near side of it with the flat of his own board, and put the shavings in a heap at the foot of the walking space and left them there.

She was still standing at the top of that step when he came back to it. She looked down the row at the place where the trestle was and said one ordinary thing about the shavings. He told her they would be under the walking space by morning and would not be under it tonight, and neither of them said anything else about it.

She stood at the top of that step a while longer with her own hands at the sides of her dress, and then she went back up that stair. The sheet was on the wood of that bench at the far end of that room with three lines on it and nothing added to it, and those three lines stood where they had stood since the morning they were written.

The man of about twenty-seven came along that row about two hours later with the bucket in his own hand and stopped at the top of that step and filled it, and neither of them said one word to the other about anything at all.

---

The seam of that inside breast pocket stood a little out from the cloth where his own hand had been in it, along about the length of two fingers. The button of it was still buttoned, and the chalk was still in it, and the pocket had been buttoned that morning before he came out and it was buttoned at the going of the light.
