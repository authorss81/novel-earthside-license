# Chapter 0974 — Two Hands On One Board And Neither Letting Go

It had rained in the night and stopped a little after the light, and the flags of that row were wet through and the shade of the trestles came off them in a slow curl where the sun got at the wet.

It was Tuesday, the six hundred and fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month. Four hundred yards of flags ran from the store's end of the row to where the row gave out into the side of a yard, and there was not a dry joint in the whole length of them. The air came off eleven miles of flats behind that rain and stood close along the walking spaces and then thinned about the ninth hour, and by the middle of the morning the walking spaces had begun to go pale and hard from the under side while the joints of them were still black. The light came along that row in one long stripe and stopped in a stripe at the top of the step where the trough stood. The ice in that trough was whole about the depth of a thumb, the crack in its near end was quiet, and the rag lay folded on the stone lip of that step.

The board on that outside wall stood on its one nail above the step with the hole of a second nail empty above it, and the water from the night was standing in that hole.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along came up that row at about the light with his own board under his own arm and the chalk in his inside breast pocket, where it was. He went along to the far end of that market and put his own board face in against the wood of his own trestle and left it there. He came the length of the row again and stopped at the bottom step and looked at the face of that board on the wall.

He worked at it for the better part of an hour with both his own hands and neither of them let go.

The board stands off the wall about the thickness of a card along its top edge and lies close along the bottom of it. He put the flat of his own left hand on the face of it about the middle of the big row at the top. Then he got his right hand down into the gap between the bottom edge of that board and the wall behind it, and his right forearm went down his own side to get there and his own knee came up on the stone of that step to hold it. Then he pulled.

That board did what a board does between a hand on its face and a hand under its foot. It gave about the fraction of an inch, and the wood at the top of it came away from the wall by that same fraction and the nail it stands on came a little further out of that wall at the head, and then it stopped and the two of his hands had it where it was. He held it there and he did not put it back and he did not shake it, and it stood with his own hands on it at that fraction of an inch off the wall for about as long as it takes to get a good edge on a thing with a blade.

What he was doing was not getting that board down. What he was doing was finding out what the nail in it had to say. The nail had been drawing out of that wall at the head for long enough that the board had begun to take a line of shadow about the width of a card away with it. A nail that draws out slowly draws out slower in the wet than in the dry, and it draws out faster at the top of the day than at the bottom of it. He had it off the wall a fraction with his own hands twice that morning and let it back where it stood both times. He could feel through the face of the board whether it was going any faster, and it was not going any faster.

**"I have got it off that wall about as far as a nail lets a board go and I have had it there twice and let it back twice, and the nail is where it was. It has been drawing out at the head of it since before the water came and it is going at the rate it was going a month ago and I have found that out. There is nothing in this row that will stop that nail and there is nothing in this row that will hurry it either, and I am not going to stand in the wet all morning pulling at a board to find out a thing I have found out," ** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, at the bottom step with his own right forearm down his own side and his own left hand flat on the face of that board.

---

Above that board there is a second hole in that wall, standing full of the rain that fell in the night.

That hole goes back into the wall about the depth of two fingers, and there is nothing at the back of it to let the water out at. It has been in the rain since before the light and is still standing in it, with the sun not yet on that wall. What comes out of a wall along the line it went in leaves a hole longer than it is wide, and that one was not longer than it was wide. Inside it the wood had gone hard and dark and a burr stands turned down on the near lip about the depth of a grain of rice, and since the rain of a week ago the wood all round the lip of it has gone soft about the width of a nail. Water standing in wood that has gone soft gets into the soft part of it and does not come back out of it. The rim of that hole will stand there for a year with the grain of it up in a ring round the opening, like the end of a stick that has been chewed.

He looked at that hole with his own hands still on the board under it and he had nothing to put in it. There is one piece of chalk in that row and it is in the inside breast pocket of the coat he has on, and it has been in there all that flood. He put his own hand flat against the outside of that pocket once while he worked and it stayed shut. Nobody went up that row and offered him another piece, and nobody came at that wall with a hammer.

The sun came round on the wall in the middle of the afternoon and lay on the face of that board. The water in that hole stood a while with the sun on it and then went down over the better part of an hour, slowly, without running out anywhere. By the time the light had begun to go there was nothing standing in it but the dark of the wood inside it.

---

That board was standing on its one nail when the light went and it stood off the wall about the thickness of a card at the top edge of it, and it had taken a line of shadow about the width of a card away from the wall with the nail.

The bottom edge of that board lay close along the wall behind it where it has lain for years, and there was dust in the gap behind it about the depth of a grain of rice. That dust had not been there at the light. It was pale, and it lay along the whole length of that bottom edge in a line, and it was the only thing that morning had got into a place where no hand had put it. The soft ring round the lip of the empty hole above it stood damp in the shade of the board's own shadow, darker than the wood the length of a finger from the hole all the way round.
