# Chapter 0955 — Two Hands Short Of The Book

The flat of a hand went down on the wood of that bench at the far end of it and stayed about an inch from a book that was lying open on it with its leaf showing.

It was Thursday, the six hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month. The light came in under the awning at the store's end of that market and lay along that bench and stopped about the middle of it, where the shadow of the cover slab on the second book came up to meet it. The bar lay along the top step along the edge of it, and the two sockets it belongs to were on the far side of the door below and had nothing in them. There was the day chalked on the outside of that door with the bare board under it carrying one mark. There was a box of chalk on the table with its lid off at a corner of it.

The keeper had that bench at its far end, with her register open in front of her and the dry cloth along the head of her own leaf. The second book lay on the wood in the middle of it, its cover slab drawn back off the leaf under it and that leaf lying flat. The man who came up that stair had the near end of the same bench with his own hands on the wood and one sleeve of his coat wet to the shoulder and the other dry. The woman of about fifty-two had the far wall and the stool standing against the wood where she had put it down. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat at the far end of the bench past the man who came up that stair, with his own board across his knees. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stood at the near wall with the satchel against his ribs.

---

They came at it from opposite ends of that bench on the same morning and neither of them touched it.

The keeper's hand went onto the wood at the far end and stopped an inch short of the near edge of that book and stayed there through most of the hour. The man who came up that stair put his own hand down on the wood at the near end at about the same time, and stopped about the same way short of the far edge of it. Neither of them went over the inch. Neither of them took his own hand off the wood while the other of them was doing it.

**"There is a crack down the length of the column in that leaf and there is a different stone in it, and it is paler than the stone round it and harder, and it stands a hair proud of the surface. Whoever filled it did it well and did it long ago, and there is nothing whatever in this room that would tell this morning when it was done," ** He said it at the near end of that bench with his own hand flat on the wood and one sleeve of his coat wet to the shoulder.

**"I have not looked at that crack twice since it came onto this bench, and I have been at this end of it every morning of that week, and I have not looked at it because there is nothing on that page I want off it. I am not going to have anybody's hand on that book this morning," ** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the far end with her own hand flat on the wood and the dry cloth along the head of her own leaf.

---

They did not touch it. At about the ninth hour he took his own hand off the wood at the near end of that bench and went down that stair with his coat open, and came back up and put his hand down in the same place. She went on at her own page and did not look across at the second book.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along went along the foot of his own board with his own thumb twice and came back to the top of it. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living turned about off the near wall and looked at the flags inside the room and then turned about again. The woman of about fifty-two kept her own wall. One of the two leaves on that bench was left exactly as it had been standing when that hour began.

Which of the two came nearer to that book was not settled that morning and was not settled by anybody in that room.

---

The shelf behind that bench stood at the height of a person's shoulder. The register had been kept on it and the second book had been kept on it before the second book came out to the bench, and there was a clean-edged place in the wood of that shelf where the second book stood, with a sharp edge round three sides of it, and the register's own dust-mark in front of that. Nothing went onto that shelf that morning and nothing came off it, and no hand in that room went near that shelf at all.

---

By the light's going the light had come far enough along that bench to get under the slab of that second book and lie on the leaf at an angle that none of them had seen on it before. And the filled crack showed. It ran the whole length of the column in that leaf, from the near edge of it to the far edge, straight, and about as deep as a thumbnail. The fill in it had taken the colour of the stone round it, so that there was nothing in the colour to tell anybody that anything had ever been done to that book. But the fill stood a hair proud of the surface it sat in. Where the light came across the leaf at that angle it caught along the whole length of the crack at once and put a bright line across that column from the near edge to the far edge. It was the only bright thing on that leaf, and it went out of it again with the light. The register lay open beside it with the dry cloth along the head of its own leaf and the two of them lay there side by side and neither of them was shut.
