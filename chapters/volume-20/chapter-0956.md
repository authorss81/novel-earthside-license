# Chapter 0956 — Chalk Held Out Over The Stone

A hand held a piece of chalk out over the flags at the low place in the stone at the side of that yard, level with the ground and about a foot above it, and held it there, and the chalk did not touch the stone.

It was Friday, the six hundred and forty-first day of the Bare Month. The wind had come up off the flats in the night and had gone into the north-east by the light and was still going, and the light came over the roofs sideways and did not lie down anywhere on that row. The trough at the top of that step had ice in it, and out of a crack in the stone at its near end a thread of water was running thin across the flags. The rag lay folded on the stone lip of that step. The bucket stood at the leg of the table inside the room and the rag was not on it that morning.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had come up that stair with his own hand in his own pocket and had gone down it again and had come to the bottom step of that stair. The man of about twenty-seven stood at the side of that yard at the low place in the stone with the bucket on the flags beside his own foot. The woman of about fifty-two was on the strip of ground at the foot of that stair with her own hands at the sides of her dress and not doing anything with them.

---

About four people a day go past the foot of that stair on their own business while the light is on the flags. That is what it is. It is not a figure that anybody in that room has ever said out loud in the whole of this flood, and it is not going to be one this morning, and nobody in that room has ever taken it and given it to anybody else.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living takes one stroke against each of them. He has been doing it since before this flood and he does it with a piece of chalk out of his own pocket. What he does with the strokes when he has finished them is his own business. They are not on the ground in front of that stair. They are not on the door, and not under the date on the outside of it, and not in either of the two books on the bench at the top of that stair.

He stood at the bottom of that step with his arm down at his side and the chalk in the fist of that hand and he did not take it out.

**"I have had that chalk in my hand since the light came up on the roofs and it has not touched anything, and it is not going to. I take one against them and I put it in my pocket and I take another out, and I have done that since before the water and I am not doing it this morning because my hand is cold and I would lose the end of it. That is all I am going to say about it to anybody."** said the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, at the bottom of that stair with his own hand shut at his side.

**"That low place at the side of that yard took the bucket in to the depth of two fingers and cut a ring of stone for itself about the width of a nail deep, and the water in the bucket and the water in the hole stand level whichever of the two I lift. I am not going to argue with a stone ring about it. I have been down to it twice this morning and I have not broken the ice in that trough and nobody has offered to."** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the side of that yard with the bucket on the flags beside his own foot.

---

Nobody at the foot of that stair was sent for and nobody was asked anything. The people that went past went past on their own business with their own eyes on the flags and the doors of that row, and one of them lifted a hand about the height of a hat in the way people do to a person they know well enough for that and nothing more, and went on.

Nobody in that room counted that strip of ground and nobody in that room measured it and nobody put a name to it or a figure on it, and the strip of it where the paving gives out has not been cleared or paved over or named in any book.

---

By the light's going he had gone back up that stair, and the piece of chalk had gone into the pocket on the near side of his own coat. The flat of his own thumb came away from the holding of it grey along the whole of the last joint. The end of the chalk has gone round and soft in the heat of a hand shut round it all day, and it has left its own colour there. That was the only mark the day made on anything, and it came off a stick of chalk into the skin of a thumb and not onto the stone at the low place in that yard, and nobody in that row saw it being made, and he did not show it to anybody and did not say that it was there. The trough at the top of that step still had its ice in it and its crack running its thread across the flags, and the chalk box inside the room on the table stood with its lid off at one corner and the two sticks in it lying one across the other.
