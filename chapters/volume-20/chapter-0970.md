# Chapter 0970 — The Handcart And A Coil Of Rope

Two hands took hold of a handcart at two points on opposite sides of its bed and neither of them let go, and the thing between those two hands went on standing in the rut it had stood in for eleven years.

It was Friday, the six hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month. The ground out past the last named house had taken two days of rain and had gone hard on the top of it since, and the carts and the barrows and the feet of everything stood on a crust with soft ground an inch or two under the crust. The sun was out and there was no wind and the whole of that stretch of country had a hard shine on it that made every rut look deeper than it was until a man put his own heel in one of them.

The man of about sixty had that handcart out in the open past the last house, with the shaft up and the coil of rope lying on the bed of it and a hazel mallet across the bed of it with its head bound in cord. The fence along the top of that shut road was not measured that day and no post of it was moved. The new rope over the back of a chair in that house was lying on the chair where it has been lying and no hand went near it and nothing was lifted off that chair.

---

**"That wheel is in a rut that has been going in since before I was out here and I have got the shaft up and the whole of the bed of it is standing over the wheel and I cannot get it out on my own, because it is not the weight of it. It is that the ground under that rut has gone soft to the depth of a foot and it has not gone soft evenly, and I have taken the weight off it twice and it has come back into the rut both times."** said the man of about sixty, at the shaft of that handcart with both his own hands on the wood of it and the bed of it standing over one wheel.

He said it once to the open air. Nobody came. There was nobody on that road at that hour except the two of them and a crow on the top rail of the fence going along it.

That crow was the only thing that came up that road that day. Two children came past the end of the fence about the fourth hour and stopped and looked down the road and did not go any further along it, and a man with a barrow went past in the afternoon and looked at the cart and went on his own way up the road.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was asked nothing that day and the man of about sixty was asked nothing that day and neither of those two facts has anything to do with the other of them.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came out past the last named house at about the tenth hour with nothing in his hands and found the man of about sixty standing at the shaft of his own cart and not getting anywhere with it.

He did not ask what was in the cart, because there was nothing in the cart. He put his own hands on the bed of that handcart at the far end of it, on the rail, about as wide as his own two hands together. He and the man of about sixty each took hold of that bed from their own side of it and pulled, and neither of them let go for about as long as it takes to walk the length of a room.

The cart did what a cart does when four hands are on it and the ground under one wheel has gone soft. The wheel came up out of the rut about a hand's width, and the crust along the near side of the rut broke and went in. The wheel went back down into the place it had been and stopped, and the bed of that cart stood level between them with the coil of rope on it and the mallet across it and nothing shifted.

**"Hold it there and do not let go of it and do not put your own weight down on that side of it, because the second time you put weight on it, that crust goes and you will have that bed over on its side in the rut and the two of us will not get it out of it without taking the mallet off it first, and I am not taking the mallet off it."** The man of about sixty said it at the shaft of that handcart with both his own hands on the wood of it.

They took the weight off it and did it again, and the wheel came up out of that rut about a hand's width and held, and the cart stood on hard ground with one wheel in a rut that is now shallower and wider than it was at the light.

Nothing else about that cart was touched that day. The coil of rope stayed lying on the bed of it and was not uncoiled and was not measured, and the length of new rope over the back of a chair in that house was where it has been lying since before this flood, and nobody came out of that house to look at the wheel that had come up out of a rut.

---

By the light's going that handcart was standing in the road past the last named house with its shaft up and the coil of rope lying on the bed of it and the hazel mallet lying across that bed with its head bound in cord.

The cord on that mallet has gone round the head of it enough times to lie flat against itself in a band. Where the binding begins there is the mark of the beginning of it on the hazel, a paler line about the length of a finger across the wood, and the binding has not been undone and was not undone that day. The hollow where that wheel had been standing at the light had water standing in it about the depth of a thumb, because the crust that had been broken along the near side of it had gone in, and it was the only standing water in that road.
