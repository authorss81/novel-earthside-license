# Chapter 0964 — The Gate Open And Two Worn Places

A hand went down onto the stone sill at the near end of that gate and lay flat along it and felt for the two hollows in it, and found both of them, and one of them was deeper than it had been at the light.

It was Saturday, the six hundred and forty-ninth day of the Bare Month. The morning came up with the wind gone round to the south-west and the light coming along that shut road from the side and lying in one long stripe down the middle of it. That road has been shut since before this flood. It runs out past the last named house and along the top of a fence of sixteen willow posts and eleven withies, and it comes to an end at a gate, and the gate has stood open at the near end of it every morning of that flood and stood open the morning before that. The bar that goes across that road is not in the gate and is not in the road and is not standing at either end of it or lying along the top of the wall beside it. Nothing has been put across that gate and nothing has been taken off it.

The fence along the top of that road was not measured that day and no post of it was moved, and the new rope over the back of a chair in the house beyond it was lying on the chair where it has been lying, and no hand went near it.

---

About four people a day come along that road past that gate. That is what that is, and it has not been reduced and has not been given to anybody and has not been used as a count of anybody.

Two of the four did something about it. A man going out with a basket over his arm came as far as the gate, put his own heel down into the near of the two hollows in that sill and pushed forward once with his leg, and the gate moved about the width of a hand along the hinge side of it and came back to where it had been. He looked at the far post of that gate on his way past and then he went on up the road and did not look at anything else on it.

A woman came the other way with her own hands full and put two fingers on the near post of that gate about the height of her own shoulder and left them there while she decided what she was doing, and then she took her hands off it and went on down the road without having done anything.

Two of the four did not. A man went up that road without looking at the gate at all and a boy went down it and stopped about twenty yards short of it and stood there until somebody else came and then went on with them. The gate stood open through all of it and nobody in that road closed it and nobody put anything across it.

---

The two hollows in that sill are at the foot of the two posts and they are about the width of a heel each and they have been worn into the stone there by the gate coming and going on its hinge for longer than anything standing at that gate is old. The near one of them is on the side the hinge is on and it goes deeper than the far one because the near post takes the whole of the weight of that leaf every time it comes back.

The stone of that sill is not laid in one piece. Two flat slabs run across the gate mouth side by side with a joint between them about the width of a thumbnail, and the two hollows are one in each slab. The two of them have been worn into the two of them separately, and they are not the same depth and they are not quite the same length. In the far one there is a ridge of grit standing along its far side where the grit of that road comes in under the gate in a wind off the flats and cannot get through the gap at that joint. That ridge had been swept out of the near one about twice in the last week by somebody who swept the sill and did not sweep the far side of it, and the far one had filled again both times.

That gate has two leaves and only one of them is a gate. The leaf on the hinge side is the gate. The other one is a frame of three uprights and two rails that was put up at some time to close the other half of that road mouth, and it does not close anything. There is nothing on the other half of that road mouth for it to come against, and it has stood a finger's breadth clear of the near post ever since it was put there.

**"That sill has got two hollows in it the width of a boot each and the near one is the deep one, and that gate has been coming back on that hinge since before there was a fence along the top of this road. It has been standing open all this flood and it stood open the morning before the water came, and there is not one bar across this road and there is not one mark on that gate saying anything about anybody being let through it or kept out of it."** said the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf, at the near post of that gate with his own hand off the barrow.

He said it into the road and not to anybody, and nobody in that road answered him, and nobody told him that what he had said was right, or that it was wrong.

He stood at that gate a while after that with his own hand on the top of the near post. There is nothing written on that post and there is nothing cut into it, and there is no staple in it and no socket in it and no notch in it. The wood at the height of a man's shoulder on the inside of it is the same colour as the wood at the height of a man's ankle. There is no mark on that gate of the kind of mark a gate has when it is shut, and there is no mark on the two posts of it of the kind of mark that gate posts have when something is put across a gate, and nobody in that road has said a word about that to anybody.

---

By the light's going the gate stood open exactly where it had stood at the light and the two hollows in that sill had the light gone out of them and stood there in the shade of the two posts.

Where that gate had gone once and come back on the morning, along the hinge side of it, there was an arc about the width of a hand where the paint had come off the iron and the rust under it had broken and stood bright. It was the only clean place on that hinge. Every other part of that iron was the colour it had been standing for years, and that arc was where a foot went into a hollow in a sill and pushed and the leaf came about as far as its own length of slack would let it and no further.
