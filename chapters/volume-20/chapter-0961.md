# Chapter 0961 — Nine People In A Yard Before The Light

Both hands of a man went down flat on the top board of that stack and pressed on it, and the stack took the weight and made a small noise at the near end of it and did not move.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and forty-sixth day of the Bare Month. There had been no sun before the light and the air over eleven miles of flats stood white and close until about the seventh hour, and then it thinned along the ground and let the light come in under the ridge of that wharf from the side. The yard was dry to the grit in it and the grit stood up in grains about the depth of a nail in the walking part of it, and the boards in that stack had gone pale on the top of them where the dew had come off them at the going of the day before. A tally-board lay face up on the top of that stack with two lines of grey grit lying across the face of it from one edge to the other. The store door at that wharf stood a hand's breadth across and had stood that far across since before anybody in that yard could remember.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had that stack for his own and his barrow standing behind him with the shafts up, and his knife in his belt. The man of about twenty-seven had the side of that yard, with his bucket on the flags beside his own foot and his rag folded over the rim of it. He had come out to that yard that morning because there was water standing in the low place in the stone at the side of it and he wanted to see what it was doing. The step he keeps at the store's end of a market row was left standing with ice in the trough on it and the thread of water coming out of the crack in the near end of that trough and going across those flags, and nothing had been fetched for that trough and nothing had been broken in it.

---

About nine people came into that yard before the light was properly on it and went on doing what they had come for. About four of the people in it answered something that was said to them, and about four of the people in it said nothing at all.

It came out of the ordinary work, the way most of it comes out of that kind of yard. A man asked about the width of a board and got an answer and did not ask again. A woman asked whether the barrow was going out and got told it was not and went and stood somewhere else. Two men said nothing to anybody and stood at the end of that stack with their own arms along the top of it, and one of them was there an hour and the other of them was gone in ten minutes.

Then the man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf asked the man of about twenty-seven a thing, standing at the side of that yard with his own hands off the barrow.

He asked him about the trough at the top of that step. He asked him why nobody has fetched a heat for it. He said it the way a man says a thing he has been meaning to say since the day before and has had the same person in front of him for the whole of two mornings.

The man of about twenty-seven answered him. It was answered in the ordinary course of a yard with about nine people standing in it and the answering took about as long as the asking. There was no keeping of it by anybody. It went into that yard with the rest of what that yard was saying that hour and it went nowhere else. Nothing on any surface in this city holds one word of what either of those two men said to each other that morning, and there is no leaf on either of the two books on the bench at the top of that stair in a room over a market that has ever held a scratch of it.

Nobody in that yard stopped what they were doing while it was put, and nobody thanked either man for it, or told either of them that what they had said was right.

---

The man of about twenty-seven went on with his own work after that, and his work came out about as well as that kind of work comes out.

He put the rag down on the wet stone at the side of that yard and wiped the face of the low place where the water was standing against it. The stone under the rag came dry in about the time it takes to say something twice, and a clean band about the width of a hand stood along that face of the stone where the rag had been. Then he lifted the rag and the wet came back up that face of the stone behind it inside about a quarter of an hour, and by the time the light had got properly along that yard the clean band was gone and the stone was standing wet across the whole of the face of it again. He did it a second time lower down, and it did the same.

**"There is water standing against that face of that stone about the depth of a thumb and it is not going to go down on its own, and you can take the face of it clean with a rag and it will be back on it before you have got your bucket filled. I have done that face twice this morning and I am not going to do it a third time, and I am not going to tell anybody in this yard that I have done it twice."** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the side of that yard with the rag in his own hand and the bucket at his own foot.

He said it into the walking part of that yard rather than to anybody, and the man of about thirty-eight heard it and said nothing back, and neither of them said anything about it again.

The bucket went into the low place in the stone and came up with about half of itself in water in it and the hole was not emptied by it. The man of about thirty-eight stood at the store door at that wharf for a while with his own shoulder near the frame of it and did not go through it, and that door stood a hand's breadth across while he stood there and after he had gone along the row.

---

By the light's going the face of that tally-board had not been turned over and the two lines of grey grit across it had not been counted by anybody in that yard and had not been counted by anybody in this city.

The store door at that wharf stood a hand's breadth across at the going of the light exactly as it had stood at the coming of it. The air does not move through a gap that narrow, and the boards of that door have gone dark along the foot of them in a band about the depth of two fingers where the damp of that wharf has been standing against the inside of them, and nobody in that yard has ever given anybody a reason for it. That board lying face up on the top of that stack was still lying face up on the top of it, and nothing had been written on it that day and nothing had been taken off it.
