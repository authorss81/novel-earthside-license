# Chapter 0992 — The Boards Going Up Along That Row

The first of that row to put anything up on that morning was the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, and he put his own board up face in against the wood of his own trestle and turned the bare back of it out into the row behind his own shoulder.

It was Saturday, the six hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month. The sun was not up before the light and the air over eleven miles of flats stood white and close over that row until about the eighth hour. Then it thinned along the ground, and the light came in under the ridge at the far end of that market at once and came along that row with the shade of the trestles going ahead of it. That row is four hundred yards of flags from the store's end of it to where it gives out into the side of a yard.

The board on the outside wall at the far end of that market stood on its one nail above its step with the hole of a second nail empty above it, and the wood around the lip of that hole had gone soft about the width of a nail from the hole all the way round. There was pale dust in the gap behind the bottom edge of that board about the depth of a grain of rice, and it lay there along the whole length of that bottom edge. Nobody went at that wall with a hammer.

At the store's end of that row the trough stood on the flags at the top of that step with ice in it about the depth of a thumb. The thread of water out of the crack in its near end came over the lip of that step and stopped in the joint of the flags a foot short of the walking space, where it had stopped every morning of the week. The bar lay out of its two sockets on the top step of that stair.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the row from the store's end of it at about the sixth hour with his own board under his arm and the cloth over his own shoulder, and put the board up on his own trestle two along with its face out to the walking space.

There are nineteen rates on the face of that board and there have been nineteen rates on it for as long as anybody in that row can remember. He stood in front of it and came down the face of it with the cloth from the top of the top column. The cloth stopped at the foot of the middle column, where the middle of that board stands about the depth of a thumb deeper than the two ends of it. He folded the cloth back over his own shoulder and stood with his own hands down his own sides looking at the face of it.

**"I have had this board up eleven days and I have wiped it every morning of them and the face of it is as clean as it was on the first morning, and there is not one person in this row who has ever come and stood in front of it. A board in a row is a thing a person has to stop at to get anything off it, and the whole of the difference between a board that is read and a board that is not is whether anybody stops," ** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, at his own trestle two along with the cloth over his own shoulder and his own board face out in front of him.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along was at his own trestle with his own hand flat on the top rail of it and his own board turned in against the wood.

**"I put mine out with the face of it to the wall every morning and I have done it every morning for eleven days, and the reason I do it is that I have never known one reason to put it out. There is nothing on the face of it that anybody would come and read, and if I turned it round it would be the same board with a blank back to it facing this row, and that is not worth the two minutes it takes to turn it," ** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, at his own trestle with the bare back of his own board turned out into the row.

Neither of them said one word to the other about what the other one had said, and neither of them improved on it.

---

About four people a day went along that row on their own business while the light was on it.

Two of them looked down at the face of that board as they went past and could have said afterwards what was written in the top line of it. Two of them went past with their own eyes on the walking spaces of that row and could not have said what was standing at the far end of that market on that wall.

Nobody stopped. A woman with a basket on her own arm went up the near walking space and read the top line of that board without breaking her step. A man with a coil over his own shoulder went up the far side and did not look at it. A boy came down the middle of the flags with his own hands in his pockets and went by it and could not have said afterwards what was chalked on the board at the far end of that market. A woman going up the row with a basket stopped about as long as it takes to read a thing twice, and looked at the foot of the middle column of that board and not at the top of it, and went on up the row and out at the store's end of it.

---

The man of about twenty-seven had the step at the store's end of that row all morning, with the bucket on the stone beside his own foot and the rag folded over the rim of it. He filled the bucket once and left it standing there full. He carried it up that stair four times with the water standing flat in it. On the last of those four he stood on the flags at the top of that step with his own hand on the outside of the rim of that trough and looked at the ice in it, and nothing had been fetched for that trough that morning and nothing was broken in it.

The woman of about sixty-nine with her tin came along that row at about the seventh hour with the tin under her arm and her own stick in her own hand. She went up the row and stood at a dozen joints with the end of the stick held down against the stone and her own thumb on the top of it, and she lifted it and carried on, and she made none of them.

She went past the store's end of that row and came back down it and went out past the store's end again with the tin under her arm. Nobody in that row put anything to her about the joints of that row, and she offered nothing to anybody in that row, and she went on her own way along the walking spaces and not the joints.

---

About the ninth hour the light came over the roof line at the far end of that market and came along that row and lay on the face of that board at the trestle two along.

It lay along the whole length of it and it took the wood of the top column and the wood of the two beside it and the wood of the middle of it, and every one of the nineteen rates on that face was readable from the far end of that row and from the near end of it. The people who went past in that hour went past.
