# Chapter 0968 — The Cloth Will Not Go Into The Gap

Two fingers went into the gap at the head of a leaf and pushed the end of a dry cloth down after them and stopped, and the cloth stayed where it was, and the fingers came out again.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and fifty-third day of the Bare Month. The sun came over the roof line at the far end of that market before the light and came along that row and in under the awning at the store's end by the fourth hour, and the flags were drying from the under side and the walking spaces had gone pale and hard already while the joints of them were still black. The bar lay along the top step of that stair out of its two sockets and the two sockets on the far side of the door below stood empty and had stood empty since before this flood. There was the day in chalk on the outside of that door and the bare board under it carrying its one mark.

---

Nobody came up that stair and stayed that morning.

The tradesman came up at about the fifth hour with his own board under his arm and the cloth over his own shoulder, and stood at the near end of that bench with the face of that board out to the door. He went along it with the cloth from the top of the top column down to the foot of the middle column, and took it down that stair again to his own trestle. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along came up at about the ninth hour and sat down in the middle of that bench with his own board across his knees. He went along the foot of it with his own thumb and did not say one word about the gap at the head of anybody's leaf. The woman of about fifty-two kept her own far wall and the stool standing a foot further out from that wall than it had stood two days before. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the near wall that morning with the satchel against his ribs and the flap of it away from his own side. About four people a day went past the foot of that stair while the light was on the flags.

The two books lay open side by side on that bench the whole of it and nobody in that room compared one leaf with the other of them and nothing whatever was done about either of them.

---

The keeper worked at the gap at the head of her own leaf for about half an hour of that morning and it did not come.

She put the end of that dry cloth down into it twice with her own fingers behind the cloth, and twice the cloth stood up off the leaves and came back out with the fold of it bent over on itself. The gap at the head of that leaf is not a gap between two things that have come apart. It is a gap between a page that has swollen and a spine that has not, and it closes at the bottom of it and stands open at the top of it, and the two of those are at the same place and only one of them will take cloth.

She tried the corner of her own sleeve and that did not go in either. She said one word under her own breath at it that nobody in that room heard, and then she squared the register against the fold and put her hand flat on the column that holds figures and went on with that column.

**"I am not going to get that cloth into that gap today and I am not going to get it in tomorrow. I have been at it three mornings and I have got it in twice for about as far as the width of my own nail and it has stood back up off the leaves both times. There is nothing in this room that puts it right and there is nobody coming up that stair with the thing that puts it right, and I am going to go on with the page I have got."** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, at the far end of that bench with her own hand flat on the page in front of her.

She said it once. Nobody in that room offered her anything and nobody in that room said that she had better leave it, and she went on with her own page.

What she went on with was the column that holds figures, from the head of it to the foot of it and back up again, with a day standing against each of the strokes in it and none of the strokes struck out. There are six of them in that column and there have been six of them in it since an earlier morning of that flood, and the seventh did not go into that column that day, and the second book lay open beside it with a leaf showing and the two of those two leaves were not compared.

---

By the light's going the box of chalk on that table was standing where it had stood at the light with its lid off at one corner and the two sticks inside it lying one across the other.

At the corner the lid stood off at, the wood of the box underneath it had gone pale in a crescent about the width of two fingers and about the length of a thumb. It is not a mark that anybody made. It is where that lid has been standing off that corner of that box every morning of that flood. The air has been getting at the wood inside the box and at the wood under the lip of that lid, and the dust of that room has not been able to get at either of them, and the two of them have gone a different colour from the rest of that box.
