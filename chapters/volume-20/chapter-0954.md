# Chapter 0954 — One Nail And The Hole Above It

A hand went flat on the face of a board that stood on one nail above a step at the far end of that market, and came down the writing on it from the top of it to the foot of it, and came away at the foot of it with the grit of the wood standing on the flat of it.

It was Wednesday, the six hundred and thirty-ninth day of the Bare Month. The sun had been on the roofs by the fourth hour and the wall behind that board had begun to give off the warmth it had taken in the day before, so that the air stood against it in a slow curl. The flags at that end of the market were dry to the grit in them and pale in the middle of the walking spaces, and the light came along the row and stopped in a stripe at the top of that step where the trough stood. The trough had ice in it, and the crack in its near end was quiet that morning, and the rag lay folded over the stone lip of the step.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had come up that row at the light with his own board under his arm and his own chalk in his inside breast pocket, where it was, and had stopped at the far end of that market. The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had come out from the wharf at the fourth hour across eleven miles of flats and was standing in the mouth of that row with the barrow standing behind him and the knife in his belt.

---

The board on the outside wall was in two sizes of writing, a big row at the top of it and six smaller rows under that, and the top row had been gone over with a wet cloth at some time long enough ago that it had gone pale while the six under it had not. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along put the flat of his own hand on the face of it and came down it. The grit of the wood came off on his hand and stood on it. He went down it twice, and then took his own hand off and looked at the grit on it.

He did not write on that board and no other hand went near it that morning. Nobody had written on it for long enough that the nail it stood on had begun to draw out of the wall a little at the head and take the top of the board with it. The board stood off the wall about the thickness of a card at its top edge and lay close along the bottom of it.

**"The face of that thing is not worth a wet cloth and it is not worth a dry one either, and the top row on it has been washed and the six under it have not, and whoever washed it washed it with something that was not water, because you cannot take writing off that wood with water and leave it standing pale. I am not going to say that wall has been washed twice and I am not going to say it has been washed once by anybody."** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, at that far end with his own hand down the side of his own coat.

**"There is a hole in that wall above the board it stands on and it is not a hole that has lost a nail. I have been in among these boards since before the light, and I have been looking at that wall for eleven years from the other side of eleven miles of ground. I know what a hole that has lost a nail looks like on a wall. That is not it."** The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf said it at the mouth of that row with his own hands off the barrow.

---

Neither of them was asked anything by anybody in that row about the board or about the hole. About four people a day came along that walking space on their own business. Some of them read the face of that board from the bottom step and some of them did not look at it at all. Not one of them stopped at it. A man going up the row with a barrow across his shoulder went past it and looked at the top of it and kept going. A woman with a bundle on her head went past it the other way and did not look.

The man of about thirty-eight went back down his eleven miles at about the ninth hour with the barrow and the knife in his belt, and the board on that wall stayed where it was on its one nail, and nobody in that row went up and put a second nail in the hole above it.

---

By the light's going the board on that wall had drawn another fraction out at the head of the nail and had taken another shade of the daylight with it, so that there was a line of shadow at the top edge of it about the width of a card. And the hole above it had gone on being a hole. It is not the round hole a nail leaves, because a nail comes out of a wall along the line it went in and leaves a hole longer than it is wide, and that hole was not longer than it was wide. The wood inside it had gone hard and dark and there was a burr turned down on the near lip of it about the depth of a grain of rice, and the lip had split very slightly along the grain for about the length of the burr. That is what a hole does when something has been in it a long time and taken a long time to come out of it. Nobody in that row knew what had been in it, and nothing went into it from any hand in that row. The chalk in the inside breast pocket of the man who keeps a stall two stalls along came down that row again with him at the light and did not come out of that pocket.
