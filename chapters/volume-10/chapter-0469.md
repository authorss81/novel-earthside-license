# Chapter 0469 — A Bar He Put In And A Hurdle He Hung On It

The man of about fifty-nine was on a box at the near gate with both hands on a hurdle at about the fourth hour on the hundred and fifty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, cutting the lashing on it with the blade of a clasp knife that was not sharp enough for the job and working at it anyway. The hurdle had come off the gate and was lying across his knees and the gate was standing open behind him with the bar he had put in nine years ago going into the socket at the bottom. There were about four people standing under that gate where he could see them and the rest of them were out of sight past the turn, and no two of them were saying anything to each other.

---

He got the lashing off in three pieces and did them again in three, and nobody stood and watched him do it, and nobody asked him what the hurdle was for or where it had come from or how long it had been hanging there. There is a bar in that gate that he put in himself after the one that was in it rotted through at the socket, and he is nobody's man, and there is nothing in this city that has his name at the top of it.

"**I have walked that road four times in nine years. It is four hundred miles and I have never once walked it for anybody, and I have never once been asked to, and I would like it kept on the record that I have never been asked to and I would like the record left blank anyway.**"

---

A man under the gate said the thing that is said under that gate.

"You could go again in the spring."

"**There is no spring in this and there is a road that is shut, and I have been to the end of it and back four times, and I know what is on it and what is not on it, and the part that is not on it is the part I have not got to yet, and I am a man of about fifty-nine and I have not been asked about any of that and I am not going to be.**"

Nobody under that gate improved on it. A man on the far side of that gate went back to the end of the lashing he had been holding and did not say anything, and two of the others found something to do with their hands, and whatever it was that the three of them had understood about the road on that gate was understood to nobody else, and the man of about fifty-nine did not know it and went on with the lashing.

He got the hurdle back up onto its hooks at about the sixth hour and pushed it over until the top rail sat against the rail above it, and the gate stood the way it had stood every morning of this flood, and the bar he had put in was still the bar in that gate. He came down off the box, untied the short length of spare hazel he keeps for that gate and put it inside his coat, and went along the row to one end of that market because that is where the hurdle came from and he had to know whether its frame was still standing. On the way back he stopped for a while at the stall where the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall has his board on the string, and he did not say a word to the man or about the man or about anything at all, and then he went back to the gate and put his hand on the bar he had put in himself.

---

The day was the hundred and fifty-fourth day of the Bare Month. Nobody under that gate has been paid, and no number of days has been said out loud under that gate this flood. The thirty-nine men under it are a figure that a person in this city knows and a person nine hundred miles off does not, and the road is shut and does not open. Nobody asked the man of about fifty-nine about anybody, and he did not talk about anybody. The market went on being a market, and a man stood at the end of a row of boards and was not thanked for the bar and was not asked about the four times he had walked.
