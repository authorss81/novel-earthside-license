# Chapter 0496 — A Plank Across The Wet Silt

The eleven miles of flats were under weather on the hundred and eighty-first day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and the channel that had gone dry in this flood was still dry. There was about nine feet of wet silt in the bottom of it from the last rain to come into it, and it runs across those flats at about the middle, and a barrow cannot get up the side of it. Adrian Vale had a plank off a stack at the near end of it in his two hands. He laid it across from the firm lip on this side to the firm lip on the other, and put his hand flat on the middle of it and left it there. The middle of it went down about the width of a hand where it lay on the silt, and the two ends did not move at all.

---

Nobody out on those flats asked Adrian Vale what he was doing. Nobody out there stopped and looked at him, and there was nobody standing about on that ground waiting to see whether he would be any use to anybody. He was not reading anything and there was nothing on those flats for a man to read. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up to the near lip with a barrow and a register-keeper's working slate face up on the barrow under a fold of sacking. He got the front wheel onto the plank and came across it with one hand on the handle and one hand on the sacking. Adrian Vale kept his hand on the middle of the plank the whole way, and the wheel went over and the slate did not go anywhere.

He said the thing that is said, in his own mouth, to nobody in particular, after he had got the barrow up the far side.

"**I have carried four things up that road in a year and I have not opened one of them, and this is the fifth, and it is not in a satchel this time.**"

A man with a spade on the far bank said the thing that is said.

"Not opened it either."

"**There is a slate under that sacking that is going to come back down that road with something on it that is not the same as what went up it. I am not going to look at it here and I am not going to look at it on the way either, because it is not mine to look at and it is not mine to be the first man to say.**"

The man with the spade went back to it and did not stop and did not answer him and was not asked anything else.

---

The man of about thirty-one put his hand on the sacking on the barrow where it had been all that morning and left it there while the barrow went up the far side. Behind him, out on the near bank, there was a man in a wet coat who had not been in the barrow and had not been asked to be in the barrow, going back along the lip towards where the boards were stacked. Adrian Vale had picked the plank up off the silt and put it back on the stack, and had put his hand flat on the top board to see whether it was steady, and it was.

Nobody out on those flats has said the name of the man who laid a plank across that channel and picked it up again. The slate on that barrow went along a line of doorways that are the same as every other line of doorways, and not one figure about those eleven miles is written down here, and every one of them that has been taken on them has been taken on foot.

---

That plank is on the stack at the near end of that channel and it is not the shortest one on that stack, and there is nothing on it to say what it was laid across. On the other side of that channel the ground is pressed flat in a line about the width of a wheel, where a barrow has gone over the same firm lip and up the same bank more than once. Nobody in this city has counted how many times.
