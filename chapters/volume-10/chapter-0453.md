# Chapter 0453 — She Is Asked Something She Can Answer And Then She Goes On Working

The weather came in off the low ground at about the seventh hour on the hundred and thirty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and got into that market from the open end and went along it in one push, and the woman who walked a market on two feet was at the third stall from the end with a slate under her arm that had nothing written on it and her hands in the front of her coat. There were about nine people in that market before the light was properly on the boards. Adrian Vale had both hands on the barrow of stall boards at the head of it and his shoulder against the frame of it, and nobody spoke to him and nobody asked him anything.

---

The boards come off that market in an order and the order is the order they have always come off in, and about four people in that city know the order and none of them has ever written it down. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board on the string in front of him with a stone on the foot of the string, and the man of about twenty-seven was at his trough at the end of the market with the store's board already back up on its wall behind him and nothing being looked at.

The man of about thirty-four put his hand flat on his own board and asked her, and it was the plainest question anybody asked anybody in that market that morning, and there is one way this flood has of putting a thing to a person and it is to ask them in daylight where they are already standing.

"Which one comes down first when it is wet like this."

"**The one with the iron foot on the low corner goes first, and the one with the long side to the weather goes second, and the man at the far end always waits and his board is the one that will be standing there at noon with nothing on it and a string still on it.**"

That is the order. It is the order they have always come down in, and she said all of it without stopping in the middle of it and without looking at anybody while she said it.

---

Nobody in that market said anything about how she knew it. Nobody asked her where she had learned it and nobody asked her how long she had been coming round that market, and nobody said she was good at it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at his own board on the string and then at the low corner of it, where there is an iron foot, and he said nothing.

"Then that is the order and I will take my board down in that order, and I have got nine boards and not eight, and the man at the far end has been doing it wrong since before you came."

"He has not been doing it wrong. He has been waiting, and there is a difference and you know there is a difference, because you have been standing at that stall every morning of this flood watching him wait."

She had the slate under her arm again and her hands in the front of her coat again before she had finished the second sentence, and she went back down the market to the third stall from the end and took hold of the first board on the low side of it, and the morning went on being worked.

---

The barrow went up that market twice with Adrian Vale's hands on it and nobody said one word to him at either end of it. He put the boards down where four different people pointed and he did not ask which of two places a board was wanted in, and nobody told him he was doing it well, and nobody in that market was waiting for him to be useful at anything.

The man of about twenty-seven washed that store's board down at about the seventh hour with the water off the low ground and put the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood back up on the wall above the trough. The market came past the end of that wall the whole morning. About four people in it saw the board go up and nobody in it stopped, because that board has said the same thing every morning of this flood, and a board that says the same thing is not a thing anybody walks round a market to look at.

At about the ninth hour the rain came across the low ground sideways and the woman who walked a market went on taking the boards down in that order, and the day was the hundred and thirty-eighth day of the Bare Month, and nobody in that market asked her one question about anything else.
