# Chapter 0456 — A Length Of New Rope Goes Off The Back Of His Chair And Nobody Says One Word About It

The man of about fifty-seven had both hands on a length of new rope at about the tenth hour on the hundred and forty-first day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, lifting it off the back of his own chair to put it back over the back of his own chair, and he has never cut it and has never once used it for anything at all. It came in his own bundle with him and it has never been used, and it is the only new thing in that room. The sheet with the one place ruled under the heading was lying on the table with the figure in it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the register was at the other end of the bench, and about nine people were in that room.

---

He read the figure out loud because it was in front of him and there was nothing else to do with his voice on a morning like that. He did not say it was his and nobody asked him about it.

"**One thousand three hundred and eight.**"

"You have read that out once and I am not going to thank you for it, and I am not going to say you were right either, because a man who has read a figure off a piece of paper has not been anywhere."

"**I have not been anywhere. I have been in this room every morning since before the water came down and I have read that figure off a piece of paper and I do not know what it is a count of and neither do you.**"

Nobody in that room said a word back to him. He is right about most of what he says and nobody in that room has ever asked him about any of it, and the figure on the table is not his and he did not say it was and nobody said it was.

---

The rope went off the back of that chair at about the eleventh hour, when a woman at the far end of the bench stood up and turned round to look at the light on the flags, and it came down onto the floorboards of that room and lay there. The room went on talking about something else for about as long as it takes to count nine, and nobody in that room said one word about it, and nobody asked him anything and nobody told him anything. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register looked once at the rope on the floor and then went back to the book on the shelf behind the table, which was shut.

The man of about fifty-seven bent down and picked it up and put it back over the back of his own chair, and he did it in about the time it takes to pick a thing up and put it down again, and he did not say anything while he was doing it and he did not say anything after it, and nobody in that room thanked him for it, and nobody in that room has ever thanked him for anything.

About four people in that room have worked out since that a chair can be moved in a room and a thing on the back of it can come off it and go back where it was, and that the person whose thing it is is the only one in that room who has to get down off a bench to deal with it, and that the room can be entirely full of people and still not say one word about any of it. That is all of what that is.

---

Nobody in that room asked him where anything was. The fence along the top of a shut road out of this city is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord, and a man of about sixty put it up and nobody sent him, and that fence is not in this city and it is not on the way to anywhere anybody in that room goes. He said a thing about the half he had paid money for, and he said it flat, and about four of the nine people in that room have worked out since that he is right about most of it.

"**I paid money for a half and I have never once been able to show anybody where it is, and the man farming the other half of it would tell you the same about his side, and neither of the two of us has ever been asked.**"

"Then you have both been left alone about it, and neither of you is going to be asked in this flood, because there is nothing left in four hundred miles that can put a question to either of you in front of a morning like this one."

Nobody improved on that and nobody argued with it. Whether that fence stands between the right two things is not a thing that room knew that morning, and it is not a thing that room is going to find out this flood, and nobody in that room is going to be asked about it. The rope stayed over the back of his chair. The date on the door was the hundred and forty-first day of the Bare Month, and about nine people went down the stair at the back by the fourth hour, and nobody in that room said anything back to the day.
