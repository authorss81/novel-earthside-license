# Chapter 0466 — Eleven Miles Of Flats And A Number That Is A Number Of Places

Adrian Vale had a slate in his left hand and a hazel stick in his right at about the sixth hour on the hundred and fifty-first day of the Bare Month, a Friday, at the near end of eleven miles of flats. The wind was out of the north across open ground with nothing on it to come up against, and there is no hill anywhere on those flats and there has not been one since before the water. The stick was for the ground under your feet and the slate was for the number, and he had neither of them from anybody: the slate was on a bench in the room over a market and the stick was off a gate he walked past.

---

He started at the gate and went east, and he put a stroke on the slate for every place there was anybody at. He did not knock and he did not call out and he did not stop, and a man who does not stop does not have to be answered, and that is why he was the one walking. There is a figure on a sheet in a room over a market with a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under the heading, and it came up the four-hundred-mile road in a satchel in somebody else's week, and it is the only figure about this city that has come from anywhere at all. He had been in this city the whole of this flood and had never once been asked to put a mark on anything, and he had a slate under his arm and a stick in his hand and nobody in this city knew what he was going to do with either.

"**A door is a thing and a person is a thing and I have been putting down one mark for both of them since the sixth hour, and I do not know yet which of the two I have been counting.**"

By the ninth hour he had the length of the first two miles. The ground was soft in the places the water had stood and firm in the places it had not, and there was no way to tell from the walking which was which until you had put your weight on it. There is a channel across those flats that takes its water out of a gate, and it has been dry since the hundred and ninety-sixth day of this flood. There is wet silt in the bottom of it and the silt has not been out of it in a long time, and it is the same silt in the bottom of that channel on every one of those eleven miles.

He stopped once, at a place where a man of about sixty-four was cutting a ditch that was not his, about nine hundred paces off a ditch that somebody else was cutting. He did not stop either. The man of about sixty-four said four words to the ground and not to anybody.

"Not his and not paid."

"**I did not ask you whose it was, and I am not going to, and I will not be back down here tomorrow with a slate if you do not want me on these flats.**"

---

Nobody asked Adrian Vale what he was doing on those flats and nobody followed him along them and nobody was standing at the end of them waiting to be shown what was on the slate. He went the whole length and came back at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the cold out of the north in his face and the slate in his hand, and it had a number of strokes on it that came to about four hundred and forty. There was a man at the bottom of that city who sends about four hundred and forty up a road four times a year. Nobody in that city has said out loud whether those are the same figure, and nobody in that city is going to be in a position to say it this side of a long while.

What was on the slate was a count of places. This city keeps a count of people. Those are two different kinds of number about the same eleven miles of ground, and the difference between them is not a mistake anybody has made. The number on the slate cannot be turned into the number anybody else keeps without going to every one of those places on foot, one at a time, and standing in it long enough to find out how many people are in it. There is nobody in this city who has offered to do that, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can do it for them.

"**There are places out there with nobody in them and places with three in them and I have counted the same thing in both and they are the same mark.**"

---

He said that to the flats and to the stick and to nobody else. About four people on that market had seen a man go out along the flats in the morning with a slate, and had seen him come back at the fourth hour, and about four of them have worked out since what was on it. Not one of those four people is going to go and look.

That was the first of three mornings and a count of eleven miles of flats cannot be done in one of them, whatever anybody in this city would like it to be. The slate went on the bench in the room over a market with the marks on it and the cold still on his hands, and the register on its shelf behind the bench was not opened, and the second slate behind it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on. Nobody in that city can check a number of marks on a slate against anything at all, and there is no form in four hundred miles on which anybody could write down that they had failed to.
