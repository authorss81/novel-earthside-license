# Chapter 0454 — A Page Lies On A Table And Nobody Can Tell How Long It Has Been Lying There

The satchel went out of the room over the market at about the ninth hour on the hundred and thirty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in the hands of the woman who kept a public register, and came back into that room in three other hands in the course of the morning, and came back to the table in the fourth. The flap was shut every time. Nobody in that room opened it. Adrian Vale had his hands on the satchel on its way round and read nothing on it and nothing in the room, and the woman who walked a market was in that room and did not speak to him and he did not speak to her, and neither of them looked at the other one doing it.

---

The third hand it went into was a hand that did not know what to do with it, and that hand carried it a few paces and put it down on the sill, and then picked it up again and carried it a few paces the other way and put it back on the table, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board watched that happen and said one sentence about it.

"**That is a page learning what a market does to a piece of paper, and it is doing it in a room where nobody will open it.**"

"It has not learned anything. A page on a table does not learn. It sits there and gets looked at and nobody can tell from it how long it has been looked at."

Nobody in that room said a word back to him. He has been right about a page three times over and nobody has ever once told him so, and nobody improved on what he said and nobody thanked him for it, and he has asked in advance, a long way back, in a lane, not to be thanked for anything he says.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own hand flat on his own inside breast pocket for most of that morning, and there is a piece of chalk in that pocket and it has not been taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is day two hundred and ninety-eight of this flood. He did not take it out. He did not put his hand near the box of chalk on the step at the end of that market either, and the piece in his pocket and the piece in that box are not the same piece of chalk and have not been all this flood, and neither of the two of them was in his hand at any point that morning.

He said the thing that was wrong with a page, and it took him about as long as it takes to fill a pail, and he said it to the table.

"**Nobody can tell from a page how long it has been lying there.**"

The room went on exactly as it was. Nobody said that was true and nobody said it was not true and nobody asked him a second question about it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board looked at the satchel on the table and then at the man of about thirty-four and did not say anything, and the satchel stayed shut.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not say what a page is for. There is a thing about nine miles up that road that he wrote last, in a harder piece of chalk, on purpose, and he has refused five times to say whether it works, and nobody in that room connected the two things, and neither of them was mentioned at the same time by anybody, and the man himself was the only one in that room who could have connected them and did not.

Then the satchel came back to the table in the fourth hand, which was the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and she put it down on the wood and squared the flap with two fingers, and then she put her hand flat on the table beside it and left it there, and nobody in that room asked her why, and the hand stayed on the wood until the light came off the flags and past the end of it.

About nine people went down the stair at the back of that room by the fourth hour. The register went on its shelf with its third column empty on purpose, and a second slate was on that shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on. The day on the door was the hundred and thirty-ninth day of the Bare Month, and a page lay on a table in the dark, and nobody in that building could tell how long it had been lying there.
