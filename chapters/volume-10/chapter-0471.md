# Chapter 0471 — The Wind Comes Through The Store Door

The wind came up off the low ground behind the row of stalls at about the seventh hour on the hundred and fifty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and went straight in through the open door of the store at the near end of that market, and the boards lying in a barrow there shifted against one another along their own grain every time it came round the corner of the building. Adrian Vale had both hands on the frame of that barrow and had had them on it for as long as the wind had been doing it. The trough was against the wall under the store's board with the bucket standing in it and the rag on the stone lip beside the bucket, and the board itself was up on its two nails with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it anywhere, and the wall above it stood over the top of the board and was not washed.

---

The wind got under the store door and went across the flags and up the row, and about two stalls along a string that had been holding a board on a stone at the foot of it went slack at the top. The board came off the string and hit the barrow Adrian Vale was standing at, and he put his hip into the frame to hold the barrow and got both hands under the board and laid it flat on the boards already in the barrow. The string went over and hung down against the stall post with its stone still on the foot of it, sitting on the flags, where the stone had not been since before the water came.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had a piece of chalk in his own inside breast pocket that he had not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and his hand went to that pocket about once a minute and came back out again with nothing in it. He looked at the board lying in the barrow and at the string hanging behind it and did not pick either of them up.

A woman with a stall two along from him said the thing that is said.

"You are going to have to say them now."

---

He said what he said, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody argued with him either, and about four people at that end of that market heard all of it and went on with what they were doing.

"**I am going to have to say them to whoever walks past this stall, and I have said prices out loud in this market every morning of my life, and I have never once had a man write one of them down afterwards, and that is not the same animal as a board and I am not going to stand here and let anybody tell me it is.**"

The rag was on the stone lip because the man who washed the board had put it there, and the man of about thirty-four looked at the rag and at the trough and at the water standing in the trough and did not say one word about any of them. He got his board up off the barrow with two hands and carried it the length of his own stall and put it down on the trestle under the awning, face up, where anybody coming along the row could read it if they stood and looked down at it.

---

The first man who stopped at that stall was told the price out loud and bought. The second was told the price out loud and did not buy and stood there afterwards and asked what it was last week, and the man of about thirty-four told him that as well, and the woman at the stall two along turned and said the same figure over her shoulder to a man going past with a basket, and the man with the basket stopped and said it back to her to see whether she had it right.

Nobody in that market had noticed any of that happen, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had come the length of the row with his own board under his arm and had gone on past without stopping, which is what he does at that end of that market.

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the end of the row with his hand on the flap of a satchel, and he said it to the wind rather than to anybody.

"**The road out of here is shut and there are three barrows on it four hundred miles up, and I have been up and down that road four times in a year with things I have not opened, and not one man in this market has ever been out to that gate and looked, and there is no instrument in this city that will send anybody to look.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody improved on it. The wind came round the corner of the store again and the open door banged on its own hinge and the water in the trough went over onto the flags and stood there.

---

The barrow went back down the row with the boards in it and Adrian Vale's hands on the frame of it, and the man of about thirty-four's board was not on it, and the string stayed hanging down the stall post with the stone sitting on the flags at the bottom of it. The stone had been put under that string to hold a board off the ground and it was still doing the job it had been put there to do, and there was nothing on the other end of it, and the wind was still coming through the door of the store.
