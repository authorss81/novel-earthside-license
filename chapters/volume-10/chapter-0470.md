# Chapter 0470 — A Question Asked Out Loud In A Room And Nobody Answers It

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her hand flat on the one-place sheet on the table at about the ninth hour on the hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, in the room over a market, with the heading at the top and one place ruled under the heading and a figure in that one place and nothing else on it anywhere. The hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her. About nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his board under his arm and he put it down against the leg of the table to get his hand free, which is a thing a man does in that room and which about four people in it noticed and none of them mentioned.

"**Put it on the board.**"

He said it plainly and to the room and not to her. He meant the board on the outside wall at one end of that market, the one that has the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it. He meant chalk, and he meant the open. He meant that a man who walks the length of that market in the ordinary course would be able to stop and read it without anybody asking him to.

Nobody in that room answered him.

---

It went quiet in that room the way a room goes quiet when about nine people have all worked out the same thing at the same moment and none of them is going to be the one who says it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at his own hands. The woman of about fifty-two, who was on the bench again and who had said the true thing about this paper in that room on a Tuesday morning, sat perfectly still and said nothing at all, and nobody turned round to look at her when it went quiet, and that was a kindness and not an accident.

The register-keeper said one sentence, and she said it to the man of about thirty-nine and not to the room.

"**There is no instrument in four hundred miles for asking a room to be asked anything in advance of anything.**"

---

He stood against the leg of the table with his hand still on his board and did not argue with her, and nobody in that room argued with her either, and about four people in that room had worked out since that she had answered a different question than the one he had asked and had answered it correctly, and not one of those four people was going to say so. A man at the back said the thing that is said.

"Then it is not for saying out loud in here."

"**It is not for saying out loud in here, and it is not for saying out loud anywhere, and I have not said it anywhere, and I would like everybody in this room to go home this evening knowing that there is one question in this city that has been asked out loud in daylight with a date on the door and that nobody has answered, including me, and including you.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody in that room improved on that. The man of about thirty-nine picked his own board up off the leg of the table and carried it out into that market himself and did not look at anybody on his way to the stair. The sheet went off the table in one pair of hands at about the eleventh hour and went down the stair and out into that market, and it was not opened on the way, and it stays on this side of the water where it is.

The board on the outside wall at one end of that market was up on its two nails with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it, and the man of about twenty-seven had gone in through the door of the store and shut it behind him, and the wall above that board that he does not wash stood over the top of it. The day was the hundred and fifty-fifth day of the Bare Month, and a question was on a table in a room in this city with nothing at all on a wall about it, and nobody answered it, and nobody said out loud that nobody had.
