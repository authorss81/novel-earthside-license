# Chapter 0490 — A Figure Chalked On The Board

The box on the step beside the store's door had its lid off at about the ninth hour on the hundred and seventy-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the store's own chalk in her right hand. Her left hand was flat on the stone beside the trough, and the board was on its two nails above her with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood still on it exactly where they had been. She had come down out of the room over a market and across that market with nothing in her other hand. About nine people were at that end of the market by the time she put the chalk on the stone. Two stalls along, the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was at his own board with his hand going into his own inside breast pocket and coming out again with nothing in it, which is what that hand has done about once a minute for the whole of this flood.

---

She put the chalk on the board on the right of the old figures and a hand's width clear of them. She wrote one figure, and there was a stroke in the second place of it, and she wrote it the way a person writes a thing they have looked at a great many times and not in a hurry. She did not rub anything out. The figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day were on that board when she started and they were on that board when she finished, and there is no place on that board that has been wiped. The wall above the board was not washed, and the washing of it stopped where the board stopped.

About four people at that end of the market read that board that morning, and about five of the nine who were standing at that end of it did not. Everybody who read it worked out that the two sets of figures on it do not agree, and not one of the nine of them can say which set of the two is right, and the reason none of them can is a word, and the word is not on the board. A man at the end of the row said the thing that is said.

"Which one is the new one."

A woman at a stall two along said the thing that is said to him and not to the board.

"**They are both the new one, because they do not agree, and if that wall could answer that it would be a different kind of wall.**"

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came down that row with his own board under his arm, and stopped at the board on the wall, and read it. He said the thing that is said, and nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him.

"**That figure is a count of places and there is not one person standing in this market it is a count of. Every person standing in this market is going to read it as though it were a count of them, and that is what a public figure does, and it does it in about a morning, and I have watched it do it four times.**"

Nobody said that he was right and nobody said that he was wrong, and he did not take his own board down. He went on down that row with it under his arm and did not stop at the end of it the way he does.

---

The wall said one thing at about the middle of that morning. It came out of the stone on the outside of that store, the same stone the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day are chalked on. About nine people were standing in that market at the time, and about four of them looked at the wall afterwards and about five did not. The store was shut and there was nothing behind it but a room with a barrow in it.

> A figure on a board is not a figure anybody has checked until somebody who counted it stands under it,
> and a board with a figure on it and nobody under it is a board anybody can put a different figure on.

---

The man of about twenty-seven who washes that board was standing at the trough with the rag in his hand when that came, and the refusal was out of him in about four seconds. It was a handful of words, and nobody improved on any of them and nobody thanked him for them, and he has asked in advance not to be thanked and has not been.

"**I have washed those figures every morning since the seventh week of this flood, and I have not asked anybody to check them, and nobody else has either. The reason they have stood up on that wall is not me and it is not my washing, it is that I have not washed the wall above them.**"

The market went on. Two stalls along, the man who keeps a stall kept his hand flat on his own coat over that pocket and did not take anything out of it and did not say one word. The woman who had written the figure put the chalk back in the box on the step and shut the lid, and the lid came down on it the way any lid comes down on a thing that has no key to it. About four people at that end of that market read that board again after she had done it, and it had not changed.

The board was taken down at the fourth hour and washed, and the man who washed it washed every inch of both sets of figures and stopped at the top of the board where the wall begins. Both of them came back up out of the wet exactly as clean as they went on, and the word that says which of them is right is not on the board and is not in anybody's mouth.
