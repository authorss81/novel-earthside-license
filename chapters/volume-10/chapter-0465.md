# Chapter 0465 — Sixteen Posts And Eleven Withies And A Mallet With Its Head Bound In Cord

The man of about sixty had one hand on the shaft of a handcart and the other under the near end of it at about the ninth hour on the hundred and fiftieth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, at one end of that market. He brought the cart in off the flags the way a man brings a cart in when he is not stopping for long and does not intend to be asked where he has been. A hazel mallet was lying in the bed of it with its head bound in cord, and a coil of rope was over the side and stayed over the side, and there was no salt in that cart and there was no wood in it either.

---

He stood it against a wall where a wall would hold it and took his hands off it, and about four people at that end of that market looked at the cart and not at the man, because a cart stood against a wall in this flood about as often as a board came down off a wall.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board on the string in front of him and a stone on the foot of the string to keep the string from going slack, and he looked at the mallet and said the thing that is said.

"You are not selling anything."

"**I am not selling anything and I have not come to sell anything and I have not come to be asked what is in the cart either, and if you want to know what is in the cart it is a mallet and a rope and I could not sell you either of them.**"

The man of about thirty-four said that was a fair answer and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him, and the man of about sixty took the mallet out of the cart and turned it over in his hands the way a man turns over a thing he has been using.

---

"**That is a hazel handle and I bound the head of it with cord because hazel splits and a split head is a head that goes past your fingers at the exact moment you are hitting something with it, and I bound it nine years ago and it has not split, and that is the whole of what I know about anything.**"

A woman with a stall two along said the thing that is said at that end of that market on a Thursday morning.

"You are a long way from a fence to be talking about a mallet."

Nobody improved on that either. The man of about sixty put the mallet back in the bed of the cart and stood with his hand on the shaft of it, and he said the count out loud because he had cut the wood and nobody else in this city had, and he said it the way a man gives a figure he is not asking anybody to do anything with.

"**It is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies and the withies are bound with the same cord, and the eleventh withy is the one that took me the longest because the wood was in the wet, and it is about four feet and I did not measure it and neither did anybody else, and that is the whole of what I know about that as well.**"

---

Nobody asked him why he put it up. Nobody in that market asked him why he put it up. Nobody in that market asked him whose it was, and he did not say, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not say. About four people at that end of that market had worked out since that a man who has cut sixteen posts and eleven withies and is standing in a market with the mallet he bound is a man who has been somewhere that nobody in that market has been. Not one of those four is going to say a word about it to anybody.

He took the cart handle up again at about the eleventh hour and went back out the way he had come, and the coil of rope went over the side of the cart and never came off it and had been on that cart a long time. The cart went out through the end of that market with the mallet lying in the bed of it and nobody walked beside it, and whether the fence stands between the right two things is not a thing anybody at that end of that market was asked and is not a thing anybody at that end of that market knows.
