# Chapter 0489 — The Shut Road, On A Sunday

There is a fence at the top of the shut road out of this city, and it is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound with the same cord. On the hundred and seventy-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, the man of about sixty was standing at the ninth post of it with his hand flat on the cord where it came over the top of the wood. The road at that point is a lane, and a handcart stands in the lane with its bar down, and the cart is his. In the cart there is a coil of rope that has been used and is not new, and a hazel mallet with its head bound in cord, and the mallet was not in his hand and the rope was not in his hand and he had not touched either of them. The weather on that road came off the low ground behind the fence and went away again the way it does, and there was nothing on it at all that anybody in this city could have been shown.

---

He went down the line with his hand on the cord and he counted the posts as he went, because that is what a man does on a line of posts. The eleventh withy had come loose at the top where it turns over the ninth post, and about a hand's length of it was hanging. He put his thumb under the loose turn and lifted it, and he looked at the place on the cord where the withy had been sitting, and at the place on the wood where the withy had been sitting. Both of them were worn. He let the withy go back down and it hung where it had been hanging. He went back to the ninth post then and did that again with the other hand, and made the same motion twice more, and then he took his hands off the fence entirely and stood in front of it with his arms at his sides.

He said it out loud, in his own mouth, to the fence, and there was nobody on that road to hear it.

"**Sixteen willow posts and eleven withies, and the withies are bound with the same cord, and I put it up with a handcart and nobody sent me.**"

He put his thumb under the loose turn of the eleventh withy again and lifted it and let it go, and it swung twice and hung.

"**There is one of the eleven come loose at the top of the ninth post, and I have looked at it four times, and I have not bound it up again and I am not going to. There is nobody within four hundred miles of this lane who could come and tell me which two of those posts it is that this argument is about.**"

Nobody said anything to him about that, on that road or anywhere else. The only other man in this city who has ever said those numbers out loud is a man who paid for half of a holding about forty years ago, and he was not asked about them and has not said them again. The two of them have never seen one another, and neither of them has ever given those figures to anybody who was going to write them down.

He put the bar up under the wheel of that handcart and shouldered it back down the lane a foot or two until the wheel came up against the bar. The cart stayed where it was with the bar against it, and the loose withy hung where it had been hanging.

Two men in this city have said that fence out loud without being asked, and they have both said sixteen and eleven, and not one person in four hundred miles can be shown where any of the sixteen is standing. The eleventh withy was still hanging the way it hangs and the cart was barred in the lane behind it.
