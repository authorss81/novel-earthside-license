# Chapter 0492 — A Door Shut On A Wednesday

The door at the back of the room over a market shut on the hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, without anything having been done to it. It was not the frame and it was not a lock and nobody carried it anywhere. The damp had gone out of the bottom rail the way it goes. The hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the face of it and there was no name on that door, and the door was shut. About nine people were in that room by the middle of the morning, and none of them had been sent for anything and none of them had been told to come.

---

She came up the stair at the back of that room about the tenth hour and shut the door behind her with the back of her hand. She stood with her hand on it for a moment. The light in that room came in at the one window and not at the door any more, and everybody in it looked at the door and then at what they had been doing.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put the flat of her hand on the table and said the thing that is said, to the room.

"**This door being shut is not an instrument and I am not going to pretend to you that it is one. It is a door and the wet has gone out of it.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"Then what is it for."

"**There is no sheet going out on this and there is not going to be one. The seven that went out in this flood went out because a man had days to write them in before the day they were for, and there are not that many left in anything I know of and there are not going to be any more.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it. A man further down that bench leaned over and put his own hand flat on the back of the chair in front of him and then took it off again, and said it to the door and not to her.

"You have shut us out of the light."

"**I have shut you out of the light and the light is coming in at a window, and what a room can do is have a day on its door and be here on the day. Anybody who wants a person in this room has to bring the person themselves. I have made that plain in here twice and I am making it plain a third time and I am not going to keep making it.**"

---

They came up the stair anyway. About four of them, at about the middle of that morning and after it. The hundred and seventy-seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door and the door was open when each of them got to the top of that stair, and shut behind them. And the light came in across the back of that room and lay along the flags. They had all of them been past the store that morning and had all of them stood in front of that wall with its figures on it. Not one of them had a slate, and not one of them had a sheet, and there was nothing in that room for a figure to go into.

The woman who keeps the register said one sentence to the table.

"**A figure anybody can read is a figure that stays up after the morning it was written on, and a figure that stays up after the morning it was written on wants somebody with a place to put it.**"

A woman at the back said the thing that is said to her and not to the room.

"And we have not got one."

"**This room has one book in it and the book has a day in it and nothing under the day, and I am not writing a figure into that book because nobody has put a day under it. Go down the stair and put it on a wall like the one at the bottom of it.**"

A man at the door said the thing that is said to her and not to the room.

"That is a poor answer to give anybody."

"**That is the whole of the answer there is and I have given it out four times this morning, and I would rather give it out five times than write one thing down that a person in this flood is going to come back and say somebody chose.**"

---

Nobody in that room wrote a figure down that morning and nobody asked the woman who keeps the register to write one down, and the only thing the room did with a number all day was to have the day on its door in chalk. Behind the bench the shelf stood where it stands, and the register was shut on it with its spine to the wall, and nobody opened it that morning. Behind the register another slate lay face in against the wall. A day was scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever stood under that day. Nothing in that room lifted it, nothing turned its face over, and no mark of any kind was put on it, and no hand but hers came near that shelf. The one-place sheet was not on the table and was not named out loud by anybody. It had come down a road four hundred miles long in a satchel with a flap on it and had been read out in that room in the daylight. There was a heading at the top of it, one ruled place under the heading, and a figure written in that one place and nothing else anywhere on the sheet. There was nobody in four hundred miles who had come back up that road for a second one.

By the middle of the afternoon that room was about nine people again and the door was shut. The steps at the back of it had been up and down about four times each way, and the treads of the four of them going down had grit on them that the four of them coming up had not put there.

---

The light in that room comes in at the one window, and it lay along the flags the whole of that morning with the door shut. Every one of the people who came up that stair put a hand on the back of the door as they came through it and shut it behind them, and about nine of them were still in there at the end of it with the light coming in past them from the side, and the door had been shut every minute of it.
