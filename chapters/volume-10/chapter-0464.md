# Chapter 0464 — A Slate On A Trestle And A Man Who Is Not The One Counting

The store's board came down off its two nails into Adrian Vale's hands at about the seventh hour on the hundred and forty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. He stood it on its edge on the flags with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood facing out into that market. The trough was against the wall under it with the bucket standing in the trough and the rag on the stone lip. The man of about twenty-seven came out of the store, looked at the board standing where he would have stood it, went past him, got the bucket out and filled it at the standpipe, came back, and started on the board with the rag and did not say one word to him about any of it.

---

The light was flat and coming in off the low ground and there was no wind on that market that morning, which is the kind of morning a thing comes up in the open on and nobody stops for. About four people came through the end of it before the board was back on its nails. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had his own board on the string in front of him with a stone on the foot of the string, and he did not look at the store's board at all, because a board that says the same thing it said yesterday is the one thing in this flood that nobody stops for.

A man at a trestle two stalls down had a slate propped against a basket and a stub beside it, and he had been putting marks on it since before the light was on the flags, and the marks were his own and were about his own stall and his own morning and were nothing else whatever. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came along the row, stopped, and read the slate from where he was standing without touching it.

"**That is a count of how many times you have had somebody at that stall this morning, and you have got it in a place where anybody can walk up and put their own morning on top of it, and I have watched men do that for nine years and I have never once seen a man do it twice and get the same number.**"

---

Nobody thanked him. The man with the slate took a look at his own marks and put the stub down beside the basket. The man of about thirty-nine went on down the row with his own board under his arm. Nobody in that market improved on what he had said and nobody argued with him either, and the man at the trestle said the thing that is said at that end of that market.

"It is only my stall."

"**It is only your stall this morning, and it is a figure with nobody under it, and I am not saying a word about your stall, and I have said a word about figures and there is no harm done, and I would like it understood that the two of those are not the same sentence.**"

The trough was running where it had been running for two or three weeks because the flags under the far end of it had been out of true since the water came, and the man of about twenty-seven came out of his store with the board up and asked the one question he asked anybody that morning, and he asked it in the local language and he asked it plainly.

"Move the trough along the wall a hand's width."

---

Adrian Vale moved it along the wall a hand's width with his foot under the end of it and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards to see whether it had stopped. It had stopped. He said one sentence about it and the man of about twenty-seven went back inside without answering him, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said that it wanted doing, and nobody in that market looked round at him when he said it, and about four people in that market looked at the man who had moved a trough instead of at the board.

Nobody asked Adrian Vale a single question about a figure, and nobody thanked him for the trough, and nobody in that market is going to. He got the bucket out of the trough and filled it at the standpipe and carried it back and put it in the trough and put the rag on the stone lip, which is where the rag had been, and he did not wash the board because it had already been washed that morning by the man it belongs to.

The board went back up onto its two nails with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it, and the wall above it that he does not wash stood over the top of it, and the trough stood a hand's width along the wall from where it had stood since the water came, and nobody said anything back to the board.
