# Chapter 0483 — The Back Of The Sheet

It was the hundred and sixty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and about the tenth hour the one-place sheet was lying face up on a table in the room over a market, where it had been lying since the morning it was read out. It came down the four hundred mile road in a satchel with a flap, and it was folded twice with a bone, and the crease across the middle of it is the crease it came out of that satchel in. A heading, one ruled place under it, and a figure in that one place, and nothing else on the front of it. It was lying face up on a table in that room at about the tenth hour, and the carrier had his hand on the edge of it, and the date was on the door in chalk behind him. About nine people were in that room and had not been sent for anything.

---

He put two fingers on the near corner of it and turned it over, once, the way a man turns over a page he has read. The back of it was not blank. There was a figure on the back of it, written in the top corner with a hand that was not the hand the front of it was written in, and he put the two sides of that sheet level with each other on the table and looked at the two figures side by side for a while and did not say anything. Then he went round the table and looked at the back of it from the woman's side of the table, and came back, and put his two hands flat on the table on either side of it and left them there. The figure on the front was a good deal longer than the figure on the back, and the strokes of the two were not made the same way. There was nothing whatever on either side of that sheet except a figure, and no day on either side of it, and no name, and no place, and nothing at all that said what either of the two was a count of.

He said it once, in his own mouth, to the room, and he did not say it again for the rest of that morning.

"**There is a figure on the back of it as well, and the two of them are not the same figure, and they are the same kind of thing. There is not one false word in that, and I have never in my life had to say the same about two numbers.**"

A woman at the back said the thing that is said.

"Two things are not the same."

He turned the sheet a quarter of a turn on the table so that both figures stood the same way up, and put his hand flat on the table again.

"**Two numbers are not the same, and I have said so out loud in this room on a Monday morning, and I am not going to un-say it because somebody has come at it from a different direction. I am not going to work out in front of about nine people what a person is supposed to do with a thing like that.**"

A woman at the back said the thing that is said.

"How do you know they are the same kind of thing."

"**I know because it came off that round with the others and because a person read it out in this room on the morning it came, and there is nothing on either side of it that says so.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"You cannot know that."

"**I have carried things up and down that road four times in a year and I have not opened one of them, and this was one of them and I did not open it. I turned it over this morning because there is going to be a figure on a wall in this market and I did not want to be the only man in this city who had not looked at both sides of a thing.**"

Nobody thanked him for turning a sheet over. Nobody argued with him about it, and nobody asked him who had written the other side, and he did not tell anybody who had written the other side. The man of about forty-one in a very good coat was not in that room and was not spoken of, and the room did not go looking for anybody, and the road did not go anywhere on that Monday morning.

---

The register-keeper said one sentence and she said it to the table.

"Then the two of them are the same kind of thing, and the only person in four hundred miles who can say which of the two that office means is that office. There is no day written on either side of that page to tell me which of the two went out first, and I am not going to put either of them in a book and I am not going to be the one who chooses between them."

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"Then the office is wrong."

"Then the office has said one thing twice and the two of the things it said are not the same, and a man who has said one thing twice differently is not a man who has been caught out, and I have watched four hundred miles of people say one thing twice differently for the whole of this flood without one of them noticing."

Nobody improved on that. Behind the bench the shelf stood where it stands, and the register was on it shut, and the slate behind it lay with its face turned in to the wall and a day across the head of it and nothing under that day. Nothing lifted it that morning and nothing turned it over and nothing was put on it, and no hand in that room but hers went anywhere near that shelf.

---

That sheet went back onto the table it came off with the side that had been under it turned down again, and it lay there for the rest of that morning and for the day after that, and the two figures on the two sides of it stayed exactly where they were. The woman who keeps the register did not ask anybody to write either of them down and nobody in that room offered to.
