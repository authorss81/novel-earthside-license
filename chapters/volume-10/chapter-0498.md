# Chapter 0498 — A Woman At The Back Of The Room

On the Tuesday the hundred and eighty-third day of the Bare Month was on the door of the room over a market in chalk, and about nine people were in that room at about the eleventh hour and none of them had been sent for anything. The register was on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and it was not opened, and behind the register there was a slate with a day across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day, and its face was to the wall. A woman came up the stair at the back of that room with a slate of her own under her arm and no tin in her hands. She did not stop on the step, and she stood at the back with her back to the wall where she has stood before, and nobody in that room asked her one thing about anything at all.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was at the near end of that bench with his own board under his arm and his thumb along the top rail of it. The face of that board was out and the nineteen rates were on it, and the space where a name had been in his own hand in about the ninth year of this flood was not filled and had not been filled. About four people in that room had looked at the face of that board and not one of them had looked at the space. He turned the board about as far as a hand's width towards himself, looked down the length of the writing on it, and put it back the way it was. Nobody in that room asked him about the space and he did not offer it.

A man at the bench said the thing that is said to the room.

"Nobody at that end of the market has rubbed that figure out."

"**Nobody at that end of the market has rubbed it out and nobody is going to, and it was on that board before anybody in this room was in this room. I have watched what a figure on a wall does to a person and I have said so out loud in a market and nobody thanked me for it, and I am not going to say it again in here.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked him for it.

---

She came down the side of that room at about the middle of that morning, past the back of the bench, and stopped beside the man of about thirty-nine. She put her slate under her other arm, and said it to him and not to the room and not to the wall, at a volume that carried about as far as the two of them and no further.

"**What is a word for, in a count, when the only thing anybody will use it for is to make two numbers the same.**"

He did not answer it and the room did not hear it. He stood with his hand along the top rail of his own board and he did not look at her and he did not look at the room. After about as long as it takes a man to decide whether a person is going to say anything else, he put his thumb on the space on that board where a name is not and took his hand off it again.

Nobody in that room thanked her for asking it and nobody told her it was a good question and nobody told her it was a bad one. About four of the nine people in that room had a thing they could have said to a woman at the back of it and did not say it then and did not say it after. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register turned her head once towards the two of them and then put her hand flat on the table and left it there.

---

A woman at the back of that room asked a question of one man and got nothing back from him. There is nobody in four hundred miles who can say what he was thinking at the time, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can say what was in her head before she asked it. She is not on a roll in this city and there is no place in anything this city has written down for a person of hers to be put.
