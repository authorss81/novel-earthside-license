# Chapter 0455 — One Place Under A Heading And The One Place Is A Figure

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the sheet in both hands at about the eighth hour on the hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday, and she had it by the two top corners the way you hold a thing you are going to read out in a room, and the hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her. There were about nine people in that room and none of them had been sent for. She held it up so that the people at the back could see there was something on it, and then she did not read it, and she said what it was for first.

"**It came up the road on the quarterly round on Thursday and it has been in this city since Thursday, and it is the office's way of saying how much of this city there is, and I am going to say that out loud before anybody in this room asks me what it is for, and I would like nobody to improve on it and nobody to thank me for it.**"

---

The man of about forty-one in a very good coat was against the wall by the step with a document inside the coat and no local language, and he had come up those stairs in this flood and had said so at the top of them, and he said one thing that morning and he said it in the local language as badly as he says everything, and he did not describe that room to anybody.

"Nobody sent anybody to be told what that is."

"**Then nobody has ever sent anybody to be told what anything is, and that has been the arrangement in this flood since before the water came down, and this morning is the first morning in a long time that a thing has come up the road at all.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that either. A man who carries a document inside his coat and cannot speak the language of the room he is standing in is not a man anybody in that room is going to be asked to explain a page to, and nobody tried. The name of the thing that figure is a figure of is not written on the paper and is not written on the back of it either, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register has had it in her own hands long enough to know that.

---

Then she read it out, and it took her about as long as it takes to turn a bar over.

"**A heading at the top. Under the heading there is one place ruled, and there is one place only, and in that one place there is a figure, and the figure is one thousand three hundred and eight.**"

She stopped there. She stopped at the one place and she stood with the sheet held up and she waited until the people at the back of that room had the figure, and about nine people were in that room and the people at the back heard it the second time because a woman near the step said it out loud to the wall.

"There is nothing else on it. I can see the heading and I can see the one place and I can see the figure in it and there is no place for a person on it and no place for a day on it, and I have been looking at it from the back of that room and I can see all of that from here."

---

About four people in that room asked her the same question in three different ways and got the same answer. The answer was that the figure came from nine hundred miles off with the office's own hand on it, and that nobody in this city had any way of putting a second figure beside it to see whether the two were about the same thing or about two different things. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the register said that out loud herself before anybody had put it to her in those words.

"**I keep this city's own figure and it is not the figure on that paper, and I am not going to read mine out this morning, and I want it said in this room that nobody in this room knows yet whether the two of them are a count of the same thing or a count of two different things, and I do not know either, and the two of those are not the same as not knowing the figure.**"

Nobody in that room agreed with her out loud and nobody disagreed with her out loud. The sheet went back onto the table with the heading at the top and the one place under it and the figure in that one place, and they were still turning over the two sentences she had said at the end of it after the room had gone quiet, and none of them was going to say either of those sentences out loud.

The date on the door was the hundred and fortieth day of the Bare Month, and the man of about thirty-one who had carried the sheet up the road that morning was not in that room and was not asked anything by anybody in it, and the second slate on the shelf behind the register was not picked up and not turned over and not written on.
