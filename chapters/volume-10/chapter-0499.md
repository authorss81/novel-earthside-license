# Chapter 0499 — A Sheet Turned Face Down

The hundred and eighty-third day of the Bare Month was wiped off the door of the room over a market on the Wednesday with the sleeve of a coat. The hundred and eighty-fourth day of the Bare Month was put on the same door in the same place, and the door took it, and both of them are on that door. There was no name on that door then and there is no name on it now. About nine people were in that room by about the tenth hour and none of them had been sent for anything, and one of them had not come up the stair in this flood before the morning a figure went on a wall at the end of that market.

---

She said the thing that is said, to the table, with her hand flat on it.

"**I am not going to be the one who says a name of a room on a door, and I am not going to be the one who writes a person's business down because they brought it in here.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"You cannot stop him bringing it in."

"**I cannot stop him bringing it in and I am not going to try. If this room has not got an instrument for what happens to a thing that arrives out of a market, then this room is going to have to go on not having one and say so out loud, which I have now said, and I will not be saying it again this morning.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it.

---

The man at the far end of that bench had had it in his coat since the middle of the morning. He got it out and put it flat on the table with the writing on it against the wood, and turned his hand over it and took his hand away. There it was: a piece of paper about the size of a man's two hands together, with a figure on it in that man's own hand and a stroke in the second place of it. The figure was the figure on the wall at the end of that market, because he had stood in front of that wall and copied it off it with a piece of chalk and a slate and nothing else. He pushed it about a foot away from him and went and stood at the window with his back to the table.

"It is only a figure."

"**It is only a figure in your hand, and it is a figure somebody has already put on a wall, and there is no heading on it and there is no place on it for the person who wrote it.**"

A woman at the back said the thing that is said to the window.

"You have read one out in this room before."

"**I have read a sheet out in this room in the daylight with the day on the door and about nine people in front of me, and that is the whole of what I know how to do with a number. A man can carry a number up a stair on a piece of paper, and I am not going to stop him, and I am not going to look at it.**"

Nobody turned it over. About four of the nine people in that room knew what was on it, and about four of them had come up that stair that morning without knowing. Not one of the nine of them put a hand out towards it, and the man at the window stayed at the window with his back to the table for the rest of that morning and did not turn round.

---

Behind the bench the shelf stood where it stands, and the register stood on that shelf shut with its spine to the wall, and it was not opened that day. The second slate was behind it with its face to the wall, and the head of it carried a day and nothing whatever under that day. No hand but hers went near that shelf, and nothing was picked up off it, and nothing was turned over, and nothing was put down on it. The paper on the table was not a document of this flood. It had no heading on it and no places ruled under one, and it was nobody's answer to anything, and it belonged to nobody at that table. It was the only thing in that room that anybody had brought in out of a market.

There are two days on that door one above the other and the top one is the only one anybody in that room can read, and underneath it there is a set of marks that has been wiped once and not scrubbed. It is on no form in this flood and it is not on any of the seven sheets that went out, and nobody has ever put a heading over it.
