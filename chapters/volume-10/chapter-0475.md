# Chapter 0475 — She Named The Cost Before She Named The Figure

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the head of the bench in the room over a market at about the ninth hour on the hundred and sixtieth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and the date was in chalk on the door behind her, and about nine people were in that room. A sheet had been read out in that same room on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day, on a Monday, and nobody had been summoned to that morning or to this one, and those two mornings are the only two this city has held on the matter.

---

The board the man of about thirty-four had carried up that stair on the Saturday was still standing against the wall of that room where he had stood it, with the face of it turned out and his own prices on it and nothing else on it, and it had stood there through one night and nobody had come up that stair for it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was in that room with his own board under his arm and had it under his arm the way he had carried it out of that room on the Saturday, with the face of it against his coat. The register stood on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and was not open, and the second slate was on that shelf behind it lying face in against the wall with a date on that face and nothing whatever under the date, and nobody in that room went near either of them.

---

The keeper took her hands off the head of the bench and put them down at her sides and said the cost first, and she said it before she said the thing, and it is not the way that room is used to beginning with anything.

"**A figure nobody can read is a figure nobody can be asked about.**"

Nobody in that room said the thing that is said.

"That is what it costs and it is mine and not this room's. There were four days that are the cooling and I have not had one of them, and there is no way to ask anybody anything before anything, and nobody has asked me and nobody is going to, and I am not going to spend a morning waiting for it."

"The two figures on that board do not match and I am not going to stand in front of this room and tell any of you they do. One of them is a count of places and the other is a count of people, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that turns a count of places into a count of people except a person going to every place on foot, and nobody in this room has been anywhere."

A man near the step said the thing that is said, to the door.

"That is not a reason to put it up."

"**It is not the smaller of the two that is the reason and I am not going to stand here and let this room think it is.**"

"**A figure anybody can read is a figure that stays up after the morning it was written on, and that is the whole of the reason, and both of those are true at once and I am going to have it written anyway, and I have not come up that stair to be talked out of it and I would not have come up it to be talked into it.**"

---

Nobody in that room improved on that. About four people in it had their hands on the back of the bench in front of them and about four had them on their knees, and the two of those groups had been sitting that way since before she stood up, and the room waited.

"**What I have decided is that this city's own figure goes on the store's board in chalk beside the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day, and that the two sets of figures stand side by side and that neither one of them is rubbed out.**"

"**There is no vote in it and I am not taking one, and there is no column in that book for what I have just said and I have not put it in one.**"

The man of about thirty-nine took his own board out from under his arm and held it against his ribs with both arms and said the thing that is said, to her.

"You have not asked one person in this room."

"I have not asked one person in this room and I have told all nine of them, and you are the ninth, and that is the same instrument and it is the only one there is in four hundred miles and I have said so twice this morning."

Nobody thanked her for saying that. Nobody in that room improved on it and nobody argued with it and nobody came at her afterwards with a second question about it.

---

About four people in that room had worked out since the Saturday that the reason she was going to have it written was not that it was the smaller of the two, and about four had not, and about four of that room were not going to forgive her for it, and about four were not going to blame her, and nobody in that room said which of those two groups of four was which, and she is thanked by nobody either way. The board the man of about thirty-four had carried up was still standing against the wall of that room at the end of that morning with the face of it turned out and nothing on it but his own prices, and the man of about thirty-nine took his board out of that room and down that stair and did not say one word on the stair about any of it, and the register stood on its shelf behind that bench with its spine to the wall and every column in it as it had been that morning, and the second slate was where the keeper's own hand had left it on the Friday with a date across the head of it and nothing whatever under that date, and the date in chalk on that door was still there with no name under it, and nothing was written on any surface in that city that morning, and the figure was not on the board at that end of that market and was not put on it that morning, and that is what it cost and she had said so before she said anything else.
