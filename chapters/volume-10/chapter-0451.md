# Chapter 0451 — The Board Goes Down Off The Wall And Comes Back Up With The Same Figures On It

The man of about twenty-seven had both hands under the store's board at about the seventh hour on the hundred and thirty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and he lifted it off its two nails and stood it on its edge on the flags with the chalk figures on it facing out into the market. He has done that every morning since the seventh week of this flood. The figures under them were put there in chalk by the man whose store it is and they are the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood, and they are the last account this city has of that day, and the board has not been written on since the two hundred and twenty-fifth day of this flood.

---

There is a trough against that wall under where the board hangs, and a bucket standing in the trough, and a rag on the stone lip of it. He filled the bucket at the standpipe at the end of that market and carried it back in his right hand with the board still standing on its edge where he had left it, and nobody in that market watched him do any of it. About four people came through the end of that market before the light was properly on the flags and none of them looked at the board, because a board that says the same thing it said yesterday is the one thing in this flood that nobody stops for.

Then the board went down into the trough face up, and the water came over the figures, and he worked the rag across them from the left to the right and back the way you do it, and he did not hurry at it. The chalk came off the stone in the bottom of the trough and not off the board. The figures came up again under the water darker than they had been, and they were the same figures they had been that morning.

---

The wall above the board is the part he does not do. It stands about two hand-spans above the nails and then there is a course of stones, and above that the roof line, and the wall is grey and has been grey all this flood. He came to a decision about the top of it about a month ago and he has not said the decision out loud to anybody. He stands on the step to reach the board. He does not stand on the step to reach the wall.

A man selling nothing in particular two stalls along asked him about it from where he was standing, and it was an ordinary question and it was the fourth one anybody had asked him that month.

"You are not going to do the top of it."

"**I am not going to do the top of it, and I have not done it on any morning since the seventh week of this flood, and it is not going to start because somebody asked me about it in front of a market, and the man at the far end has watched me not do it and can say so himself.**"

"Then the top of it is only a wall."

"**It is only a wall.**"

That was four sentences. Nobody in that market agreed with him and nobody in that market disagreed with him out loud, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls down had his own board on the string in front of him and a stone on the foot of it to keep the string from going slack, and he said nothing at all about the wall, and nobody asked him.

---

The board went back up onto its two nails at about the eighth hour with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it. It is not blank. A blank board is a board nobody has had anything to say on, and this one has had a thing said on it and said again every morning since, and a thing said every morning for this long stops being the same thing and starts being what a board is for.

Nobody in that market was waiting to be told what goes on that board next and nobody in that market was told. The man of about twenty-seven put the rag on the stone and the bucket back into the trough and went in through the door of the store, and the board stood on the outside wall at one end of that market with the figures on it and the wall above it that he does not wash, and the market went on being a market.
