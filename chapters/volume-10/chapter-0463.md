# Chapter 0463 — One Of The Two Is A Count Of Places And The Other Is A Count Of People

The one-place sheet was face up on the table in the room over a market at about the ninth hour on the hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, and the woman of about fifty-two had both hands on the edge of that table and neither of them was on the paper. There was a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under the heading and a figure in that one place, and there was nothing else on it, and a crease ran across it that was not the crease it went out of that room with, and it had been read out in that room on the Monday of the week before by the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register and had not been read out since. The hundred and forty-eighth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her. There were about nine people in that room and there were four benches.

---

Somebody carried a stool up the stair at the back of that room and put it against the wall, and about four people sat down on it in the rest of the morning, and nobody thanked the man who carried it, and he went out again.

The woman of about fifty-two did not sit down on any of the five. She had come into that room with nothing at all in her two hands and she kept them on the table, and she had a door at the back of the half she holds and nobody in that room asked her to open it or to account for it.

She waited until the room had finished shifting on the stool. Then she said the thing, and she said it once and did not say it again, and nobody in that room asked her where she had got it from.

"**That paper is a count of places. What this city keeps is a count of people. Those are two numbers about the same ground and they are not going to match, and neither one of them is wrong, and the whole of the difference between them is a unit, and a unit is a decision somebody has made about what a thing is, and nobody in this room was asked about that decision and everybody in this room is living inside it.**"

---

Nobody improved on it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his own board under his arm and he looked at the sheet on the table and then at the woman who had said it, and he said the thing that is said at that end of that market on a Tuesday.

"That is a great deal to have come out of a woman with her hands on a table."

"**It came out of a woman with her hands on a table who has been asked for a figure before and did not have one, and I am not going to be thanked for it. I would like the paper to stay on that table where it is. A paper on a table in a room is a paper that about nine people know about. A paper on a wall is a paper that everybody knows about and only about four of them can do anything with.**"

Nobody said anything for a while. The light came over the top of the step at the back of that room and got as far as the middle of the table and stopped there, and it was on the paper and on nobody's hands.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said that a figure about places was a figure about places wherever it happened to be written, and that went round the room and nobody stopped it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put both hands flat on the table on either side of the sheet and looked at the one place ruled under the heading. She did not say one word about the difference between the two figures, and she did not say the sentence again, and she did not agree with it out loud and she did not disagree with it out loud.

About four people in that room had worked out since that the woman of about fifty-two was not going to be thanked for it by anybody and had said so before she started. About four people in that room had worked out since that she was not wrong. Not one of those four people was going to say so to her or to anybody else.

---

Nobody asked her to show anybody anything. The sheet stayed on the table with its heading at the top and its one place under it and the figure in that one place. It was not returned and it was not withdrawn. The register was on its shelf behind the table and was not opened, and the second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date, and it was not picked up and not turned over and not written on.

The two figures were on a page nine hundred miles off and on the inside edge of a tally-board at the bottom of this city, and a man at the bottom of this city sends one of them up a road four times a year and does not say the other one out loud. Nobody in this city has an instrument for checking a reason, and a woman with her hands on a table gave one out loud in a room with a date on the door, and the sheet was on that table with a crease in it that was not the crease it went out of that room with, and nobody in that room said one word about the crease.
