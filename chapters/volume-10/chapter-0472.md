# Chapter 0472 — A Method That Has Nobody To Do The First Part

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had both hands flat on the edge of a table in the room over a market at about the tenth hour on the hundred and fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the one-place sheet was lying on that table in front of her in one pair of hands that had carried it in and gone straight back down the stair again without stopping anywhere else. There was a heading at the top of it and one place ruled under the heading and a figure in that one place, and the crease across the middle of it was the crease it had gone out of this room with. The hundred and fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her, and about nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

A man at the back of that room, who had come up on his own and had given his name to nobody, put his hands up in front of his chest and worked something out on the fingers of his left hand with the thumb of his right. He said it out loud as he went, the way a man says a thing when he has decided to be useful in a room and has not been asked to be useful in it.

He got as far as the first part of it. He held up his first finger and said that the first part was knowing where a man was to start, and that there were eleven miles of flats out past the near gate, and that a man could start at the two ends of them or in the middle. Two men starting at the two ends would meet in the middle and would have the same figure. One man starting in the middle would have half of a figure and would have to be trusted for the other half. Then he put his hand down and said that was the first part and that was as far as he had got.

The register-keeper did not look up from the table.

"**You have got as far as a place where a man would have to stand, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that will tell a room which end to send him to, and the two ends of those flats are the same ground and neither of them is nearer to anything.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was standing at the end of the bench with his hands hanging at his sides and had not sat down. He said it to the room and not to the man who had been working on his fingers, and he said it once.

"**I would go and make the other one. I would go this week and I would take a slate and I would put a mark on it for every door I came to, and I would not ask anybody for anything while I was out there, and I have said that out loud now and I am not going to say it a second time in this room.**"

Nobody in that room accepted it. Nobody in that room refused it either, and nobody asked him why the first figure on that table could not simply be added to a number like his and made into something that answered it. The man at the back of the room sat down on the end of the bench and put his hands on his knees and looked at the flags.

The man of about thirty-four stayed where he was for the rest of that morning and did not say one word more about it, and nobody in that room brought it up again, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not write one word of it in anything, because the register was on its shelf behind the bench and was not opened.

---

A man at the foot of the stair asked the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living when the road came round again, and he answered him straight, and did not soften it, and did not say that he had not been asked the question before.

"There is no day. There is no day any more than there was a week ago and there will be no day next week, and I carry what comes and I put it on a table and I go back down, and that is the whole of what I know about it and it has been the whole of what I know about it since the water came."

He said one more thing to the room before he went, and he said it about himself and not about the road.

"**Seven sheets have gone up that road in this flood as notices, and there is no eighth one, and I carried five of the seven on my own back before any of you were keeping anything in this room, and I have not opened one of them and I am not going to open one tonight.**"

The register was on its shelf and the second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date, and nobody in that room went near that shelf, and the one-place sheet stayed on the table it was on.

---

A method is the easiest thing in the world to make at the back of a room with your hands up, and the first part of a method is the part with the ground in it, and there was nobody in that room on that Thursday who had been out on the ground.
