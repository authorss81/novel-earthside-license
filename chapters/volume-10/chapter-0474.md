# Chapter 0474 — Two Men With Boards In One Room

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall came up the stair at the back of the room over a market at about the tenth hour on the hundred and fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, with his own board under his arm, and about nine people were in that room and about four of them looked at the board and one of those four looked at the man and not at the board. The hundred and fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind him. He had not brought it up there before and nobody had asked him to bring it up there.

---

He stood it against the wall on the far side of the room with the face of it turned out, the way it stands on its string at the end of the row, and he took his hand off it and put his hand at his side. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was on the other side of that room with his own board under his arm and looked at it and did not go over to it, and nobody in that room said one word about either board. The register stood on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall and was not opened. The second slate was lying on the shelf behind that register with the face of it turned in against the wall, a date on that face and nothing whatever under the date.

The man of about thirty-nine said it plainly and to the room and not to the man who had come up the stair.

"**I have been carrying a board in four markets for nine years and I have never once put it in a room, and I have never once seen another man's board in a room, and I do not know what a room does with two of them and I am not going to find out by standing here looking at them.**"

---

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody improved on it. A woman at the back of that room said the thing that is said.

"You could put them side by side and see."

"**I could put them side by side and see and there is nothing in this city that would come of the seeing, and I have said what I have to say about boards and I am not going to say a second thing about them in a room with a date on the door.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall did not look at the man of about thirty-nine when that was said, and he did not look at the board either. He sat down on the end of the bench, put his hands on his knees, and stayed there with his hands on his knees for the rest of that morning while the light came over the back step and went along the flags and stopped somewhere in the middle of the floor.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said one sentence to the room and it was not about either board.

"Two things leaned against a wall in this room are two things nobody is going to write on, and the fact that they are in here this morning is the only reason anybody in this city is going to see them together, and I would like it noticed that neither of the men who brought one of them in has said a word about the other one's, and that is the first thing either of them has ever done in nine years that I have not had a reason to be careful about."

The man of about thirty-four said nothing at all to that. The man of about thirty-nine took his own board out from under his arm, put it under his arm the other way round so that the face of it was against his coat, and went down the stair and out into that market. He did not come back up that stair that day, and nobody in that room remarked on that he had gone or on when he would be back.

---

The board the man of about thirty-four had carried up was still standing against the wall of that room at the end of that morning with its face out and nothing on it but his own prices, and it was the only thing in that room that had ever come off a string and gone up a stair instead.
