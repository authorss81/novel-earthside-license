# Chapter 0452 — Four Hours With A Satchel On A Table And Nobody Opens It

The man of about thirty-one put the satchel down on the table in the room over the market at about the tenth hour on the hundred and thirty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday, and kept his hand flat on the flap of it for a moment, and then took his hand off it and went back down the stair at the back of that room. The flap has a brass stud in it and the stud has been polished by somebody's thumb over a long time. There is a sheet inside that satchel. Nobody in that room has touched the flap since the man of about thirty-one took his hand off it, and he was not thanked for carrying it up those stairs, and he did not open it, and he is the fourth thing in this flood to have come up a road inside something he has not opened.

---

The room had about nine people in it by the tenth hour and every one of them had been in it before. The register was on its shelf behind the table where it has been since before the water, and a second slate was on that shelf behind the register with a date on it and nothing under the date, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the register looked at the satchel once, the way you look at a thing that has arrived, and then she did not look at it again for the rest of the morning, and her hands were in the front of her coat.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came in with his board under his arm and put it against the leg of the bench and sat down. He had a slate on his knee. He waited until the room had gone quiet in the ordinary way, which it does about twice a morning, and then he said the thing that was in that room.

"**That came up the road on Thursday and it has been in this city since Thursday, and it is going to sit on that table until somebody in this room decides what it is, and nobody is going to be told what it is. Is that what it came up the road for.**"

Nobody thanked him for asking. He has asked a question in that room more times than anybody else has and nobody in that room has ever thanked him for asking one, and nobody in that room has ever improved on it either, and nobody told him he was wrong.

---

The four hours are the whole of that morning. The light came in over the step at the back and lay along the bench and came off the flags about the second hour, and the satchel stayed where the man of about thirty-one had put it, and the flap stayed shut, and the brass stud on the flap went from dull to bright and back to dull and back to bright as the light moved across the table.

A woman near the step said a thing about a barrow that had gone past under the window, and it was not about the satchel. A man at the far end of the bench said a thing about the cold coming in under the door, and it was not about the satchel. About four people in that room worked out in the first hour that a thing which has come up a road cannot be opened by the people it has come to see, because there is nothing in four hundred miles that lets a person in this city send a word up that road asking whether it may be opened. That same room came to it again in the third hour and came to something worse, which is that there is not going to be any way of asking anybody anything at all about anything from here on. Neither group said a word about either of those two things out loud.

---

A man who had been in the room since the seventh hour said the practical thing, to the middle of the table.

"Then it is not a satchel, it is a table with a thing on it."

"**It is a table with a thing on it and I have been trading off a board off a string for nine years and I know exactly what a thing on a table does to the people who come in under the door after it, and that is all I am going to say about it this morning.**"

Nobody answered him. About four people in that room had an answer and about four of them said nothing, and nobody put either group in front of the other one.

The day on the door was the hundred and thirty-seventh day of the Bare Month, and the satchel was still on the table with the flap shut when about nine people went down the stair at the back, and the room was locked by the woman who keeps the register, and the register went on its shelf with the second slate behind it, and the shelf was not touched again that day by anybody.
