# Chapter 0493 — A Third Line In The Register

The hundred and seventy-eighth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door of the room over a market on the Thursday, and about nine people were in that room by the ninth hour and none of them had been sent for anything. The bench had been cleared along its length and the table had been drawn up to it. The register was still on its shelf behind the bench with its spine to the wall, and behind the register there was another slate with its face turned in to the wall, and a day scratched across the head of it, and nothing whatever under that day. No hand in that room but one went near that shelf on that morning, and it was the hand of the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register. It went near that shelf and came away again three times before she was ready.

---

She said it out loud before the pen went near the page, and she said it to the room and not to the shelf, and she said it once.

"**The day in this line is the four hundred and ninetieth day of this flood, which is the morning that figure went up on that wall at the end of this market, and I am writing this line on the four hundred and ninety-third day of this flood. Those two days are three days apart, and I am naming both of them out loud in this room.**"

A man at the bench said the thing that is said.

"Nobody in this room knows which of those two days that book is a record of."

"**Then they can come down the stair and stand behind me and look at it, and I am not going to have a page in this book with two days on it and nothing saying which of the two is which. That is not a record of anything.**"

A man further down that bench said the thing that is said.

"You could put the second of them in as well."

"**I could put the second of them in as well and I am not going to. The second of them is the morning I am standing in, and a book that holds the day a thing happened and the day it was written down has four places in it and this one has three.**"

She put her hand flat on the table beside the pen and did not pick it up, and the light came across the table and along her forearm.

"**One of those three is going to stay empty and it is not the one on the right, and it has been empty on purpose since before any of us were in this room, and there is nothing anybody has said to me that would change that today.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody thanked her for it.

---

The two lines already in that book were put in at two different times and in two different rooms, and the ink on the first of them had gone brown at the edges and the ink on the second had not. The woman who keeps the register had looked at both of them a good many times and had never said so out loud. She got the register off the shelf herself. She put it on the table with the spine up and the cover open away from her, and turned it round so that the page was the right way up for her and not for anybody else. She put her own working slate face up on the bench beside it, with two figures on one face of it and neither of them rubbed out, and nobody at that table could see the working slate from where they sat. Each of those two lines had a day in the first column of it and nothing whatever under the day. She put the pen in the first column of the third line and wrote the day in it. She took her time over it the way a person does over a thing they have looked at a great many times, and then she put the pen down and said the second of the two sentences.

"**Under that day goes the figure, and this is the first time anything has gone in that column in this flood. It goes in on its own, with nobody's name on it, and the third of these columns stays empty.**"

A man at the far end of the bench put both hands out towards the shelf and stopped them in the air about where the register had been standing. He brought them back to the table and put them flat on it, and he said nothing about it and nobody said anything to him about it.

---

She wrote the figure under the day. The pen made a noise on that page that the room had not heard out of it before, because nothing else has ever been written under a day in that book. About four of the nine people in that room had worked out what the two of them on one line say that neither of them said on its own, and not one of them said so. The third column stayed empty, and she did not put the pen into it and she did not look at it. The figure under the day was this city's own and was not the office's, and the two figures on the working slate on the bench beside it were of the same kind of thing as the one in the book and were not the same figure as it.

"**There is no column in this book for the two of them together. A day, and a figure under a day, and a person in the same line with them, are three things, and this book has a place for the third of them and it is going to keep having a place for it while I am the one who keeps this book, and nobody in this room is going to be asked about that today.**"

Nobody said a word to that either. She put the pen down on the table and shut the cover. She carried the register back to the shelf with both hands and put it back on it with its spine to the wall in the place it had been standing in. The slate behind it had not moved and had not been moved, and its head still carried its day with nothing under it. She did not touch it, and nobody in that room came near that shelf after her hands came off it.

---

That book has three lines in it now and two of them have nothing under the day and the third of them has a figure. The column on the right of all three of them is empty from the top of the page to the bottom, and the room in which it was written has about nine people in it most mornings who have not been sent for anything. The two days are in it, the one the thing happened on and the one it was written down on, and only one of those two has ever been on this page as a number anybody in this room could not do the other one from, and that one is on the left.
