# Chapter 0467 — Four Things He Has Carried Up And Down A Road And Has Not Opened

The man of about thirty-one had the flap of a satchel under his right hand on the table in the room over a market at about the tenth hour on the hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and the flap was down and his hand was flat on it and nothing in that room was going to be on the table with the flap up on a Saturday. The hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind him and there were about nine people in that room and none of them had been sent for it.

---

The satchel had come up that road on the quarterly round in the same week as a sheet with a heading and one place ruled under it, and the sheet had been read out in that room on the Monday of the week before, and the satchel had been put on a table and gone round the room in four hands and come back, and it was on the table on that Saturday with its flap down. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the bench and the register was on its shelf behind the bench and was not opened, and the second slate was on the shelf behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date.

A man at the foot of that stair, who had come up without knocking and had come up on his own, said the thing that is said at the end of a stair on a Saturday.

"What is in it."

"**There is a thing in it that has been read out to nine people in this room and to four people in a market, and I have not opened it, and I am going to tell you exactly what I have and have not done with it, and I would like nobody in this room to say thank you for that.**"

---

He said it in four sentences and he did not say it again for the rest of the morning. About four people in that room had worked out since that he was not being clever and was not being proud of it either. The light came over the top of the stair and lay on the satchel and on his hand and on nothing else anybody in that room was holding, and it moved about a hand's width across the flags over the course of the morning the way it does.

"**I carried five notices up that road once, in a coat, on my own back, before any of you were keeping this register. I have never opened one of those five.**"

The man at the back said the word.

"Notices."

"**A return in a satchel like this one, and I have never opened that either. The last sheet that came up that road, and I have not opened that one. On the day after this one came up I carried this satchel to this table, and I have not opened it.**"

A man at the back said the thing that is said.

"So you are a man who carries things and does not know what is in them."

"**I am a man who carries things and does not know what is in them, and I have been that long enough that I have stopped minding. I will tell you the one thing I mind.**"

Nobody said anything. He waited about as long as it takes to turn a bar over and went on.

"**It is that a thing in a flap on a table is a thing four people know about, and a thing in a flap on a man's back is a thing nobody knows about. There is a difference between those two and it is not a small one, and I have not been asked about it once this flood.**"

---

Nobody in that room asked him about the difference. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register said one sentence, and she said it to the room and not to him. The sentence was that a thing carried is a thing somebody else decides what happens to, and that a thing on a table is a thing that nine people in a room can be wrong about together. She said that the second of those is worth more and costs more, and that she was not going to say what it costs this morning.

Nobody thanked the man of about thirty-one for four sentences and nobody thanked the woman of about twenty-nine for one. About four people in that room had worked out since that those two people had said the same thing from opposite ends of it and had not heard each other do it, and not one of those four was going to say so.

The man of about thirty-one did not answer her. He put the satchel under his arm with the flap still down and went down the stair and out into that market on a Saturday. He stood at one end of it for a while with the satchel against his hip the way a man stands who is not waiting for anybody, and about four people at that end of that market did not stop.

The satchel went back into the room over the market at about the eleventh hour and was not opened then either, and it went back against the hip of the man who had carried it up that road and had never looked inside it. The sheet stayed on the table it was on with its heading at the top and its one place under it and the crease still in it, and it stays on this side of the water where it is.
