# Chapter 0457 — He Is Right About It In About Four Seconds And Nobody Says So

The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf had the flat of a knife with a blunt edge on the face of his own tally-board at about the seventh hour on the hundred and forty-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. He was doing it with the board on his knee at the foot of a stack higher than a man, at the bottom of this city, where the light comes off the water and lies along the boards and does not get onto a wall at all. A barrow stood at the end of the wharf boards with the handles turned in. The knife is not for cutting anything. It has never once been for cutting anything.

---

He sends about four hundred and forty in the return that goes up the road on the quarterly round, and he has been sending that figure four times a year since the winter before the water. On the tally-board he keeps in the inside of his coat there is a second set of notches cut further along the edge than the notches he has sent. The second set is not the first set, and there is no figure on those boards that anybody in this city could write down.

He worked the blunt edge along the wood a notch at a time and he did it the way a man does a thing he has done a great many times, and about four people on those wharf boards have worked out since that he is not cutting that board for the return and has not been for a long time.

Then somebody who had come down the wharf steps told him what had been read out in a room over a market two mornings before, which was a heading and one place ruled under the heading and a figure in that one place, and the man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf put the knife down on the boards and worked the rest of it out in about four seconds.

"**That is a count of this city. It is not a count of a road or a market or a ward and it is not a count of anything that anybody in this city keeps a tally of, and there is no name on it anywhere on the paper.**"

A man shifting a board at the other end of the wharf said the thing that is always said at the bottom of that street.

"You are not going to send that one up."

"**I am going to send about four hundred and forty up and I have been sending about four hundred and forty up four times a year since the winter before the water, and what I have on my own coat is mine and is not going on a strip of clear board for anybody.**"

Nobody on those wharf boards thanked him. Nobody argued with him either. Nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and the people standing near the foot of that stack had each got as far as the first half of what he had said and would not get past it without him finishing it, and he did not finish it.

---

He picked the knife up again and went on with the notches. There is no eighth notice in this flood and there is not going to be one, and it is not going to be him who carries a sheet up a road on a morning and says what it is for.

It was worked out on a wharf at the bottom of this city on a Wednesday morning by a man nobody asked, and it is not going to be reported to a room, and the reason it is not going to be reported to a room is not that anybody has decided it shall not be. There is no instrument in this flood to report anything to anybody in advance of anything, and by the time a thing gets to a room in this city it is a morning, and the room is about nine people who were already in it, and that is the whole of what there is.

---

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was not on that wharf and was not told. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was not told either and did not ask, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was not told and was not going to be, and none of the three of them has any way of being told that would not arrive too late to be worth anything to anybody. The light came off the water and lay along the boards and did not get onto a wall, and about four hundred and forty went on a strip of clear board about as long as a cart, and the day was the hundred and forty-second day of the Bare Month.
