# Chapter 0468 — A Number Said Out Loud In A Room And Not Written Down

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her own slate in both hands at about the tenth hour on the hundred and fifty-third day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, in the room over a market, and she was putting it on the shelf behind the register and taking it off again in the same hand, in about the time it takes to do either. The register was on that shelf with its spine to the wall and it was not open, and the second slate was behind it with a date on it and nothing under the date, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on. The hundred and fifty-third day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door. About nine people were in that room and no hand in it but hers went near that shelf.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall had a piece of chalk in his inside breast pocket that he had not taken out since the forty-third day of the Short Month, which is the two hundred and ninety-eighth day of this flood, and his hand went to that pocket about once a minute and came back out again without anything in it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board was against the wall with his board under his arm. The woman of about fifty-two was on the bench with her hands in her lap and was not asked anything by anybody in that room on that Sunday.

The man of about thirty-nine said his number out loud because he had it and had said it out loud to himself on a great many mornings.

"**Two hundred and six this week off that board and I know the figure to the one because I have been doing it nine years, and I am telling this room it because I would like somebody in this room to have a number in their head that is right.**"

---

Nobody thanked him for it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put her slate down flat on the bench and said one thing, and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody asked her where she had got it from.

"**I am not going to write that down. A number in my hand is a number anybody can carry to anybody.**"

The man of about thirty-nine stood against that wall for a while with his board under his arm and then said the thing that is said.

"That is a poor reason for keeping a register."

"**It is the only reason there is, and I have had it for nine years, and if I write a man's private figure down one day because he was pleased with himself the day before then I am a woman with a page and not a woman with a room, and there is a difference between those and everybody in this flood has learned it the hard way and I have learned it twice.**"

---

Nobody in that room asked her why the third column of that register is empty, and nobody in that room is going to. Two lines were in that book, put in on days of this flood that she had named when she put them in, and each of them had a day in it and nothing under the day, and she did not read either of them out and she did not open the book to look at them. The woman of about fifty-two on the bench did not look at the shelf either. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall turned his board over on its string and looked at the back of it, which is a thing a man does with a board when a room has been talking for an hour.

The slate went onto the shelf and came off it again in the same hand. The second slate behind the register was where it has been since a room in this city said one word out loud once, and the word went on that slate in chalk with the date beside it and no name at the top of the line. The day was the hundred and fifty-third day of the Bare Month, and about nine people in that room had heard a figure that was right. About four of them had already begun to work out what it would be worth to the man who had said it if it were ever on a wall somewhere.
