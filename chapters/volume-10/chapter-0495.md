# Chapter 0495 — A Board Turned Face In

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall took his board down off the string at about the ninth hour on the hundred and eightieth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and he did not hang it up again. The string was on its post and the stone was on the foot of the string, and the string had been on that post and the stone on the foot of it every morning of this flood, and there was nothing on the string at the end of it. He turned the board over once in his two hands, so that the face of it went into the back of his own trestle and the back of it came out into the row. The back of it was bare board, and had been bare board the whole time anybody in that market had been reading the other side of it.

---

Above the trough the store's board was still on its two nails and it had both its sets of figures on it. The account of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood was on the left of it, and a figure of this city's own in chalk was on the right of them a hand's width clear, and neither one had been moved since the morning the right-hand one went up. The wall over the top of that board was not washed. The trough was full and the bucket was standing in it up to its middle and the rag was on the stone lip. The water was not going over the lip, and nobody at that end of the market had been near any of the three of them that morning except the man who keeps the bucket.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that is said, to his own trestle and not to the row.

"**A board on a string is the only thing at this end of this market a person can read from the far end of it, and I have had it out here since before the water came. I have taken it down this morning because a figure went up on a wall at this end of this market, and everything anybody walks past out here can read now.**"

The woman at the stall two along from him had her hand flat on the top of her own board when she said it, and she said it to the row and not to him.

"Twelve people have asked me this morning what that figure is."

"**Then twelve people have asked you what a figure is, and I have no answer to that at this end of the row. My nineteen are on a piece of wood, and there is not one person in this market who does not already know what I charge, and I would rather nobody knew the price of my board than had to stand in the row explaining it.**"

Nobody thanked him for it and nobody argued with him about it. About four people in that row had looked for the face of that board and gone on with what they were doing, and not one of them said a word about the stone on the foot of the string.

---

He pulled the string up off the post and coiled it and put it behind the trestle where the boxes were. The post stood in the flags with nothing on it and the stone lay beside it on the flags and not on the foot of anything. A man at the far end of that row came down it about the middle of the morning and looked at the post and looked at the bare back of that board and went on down the row without stopping, and it is not a thing that man does.

His own piece of chalk was in his own inside breast pocket the whole of that morning. He put his hand flat on it twice through the front of his coat, where it was, and it did not come out. There is a place on the front of that coat about the size of a hand where the chalk has been going in and out of that pocket for the whole of this flood, and it is lighter than the cloth around it.

---

That post at that end of that market has a string on it and a stone at the foot of it and nothing at the end of the string, and the row walks past the bare back of a board that a person used to be able to read from the far end of it. The lighter patch on the front of that man's coat is the only account anybody keeps of the reason.
