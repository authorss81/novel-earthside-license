# Chapter 0482 — A Door That Will Not Shut

The door at the back of the room over a market would not shut on the hundred and sixty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Sunday, and it was not the frame and it was not a lock. That door has never carried a lock, and it takes a day in chalk and no name, and what had happened to it was the damp coming up the stair overnight and swelling the bottom rail until the door stood a hand's width proud of its own frame. Adrian Vale had his hands on the edge of it and his shoulder against the wall of the stair, and he pushed it, and it came back on him. He pushed it again with his weight behind it and it came back on him again. The hundred and sixty-seventh day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the face of it, and about nine people were in that room, and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

He went down to the bottom step and got the end of a bar off a leaning pole and came back up. He worked the end of it into the gap where the door had swollen against the jamb, and the door came in about half of what it had been standing out by. That was enough to let a man through one at a time and not enough to shut. He then prised a piece of slate out of the top step with the bar, and it came up whole and about the size of a hand, and he set it on the flags at the foot of the door where the door would have to lift itself over it. He stood back and looked at what he had done. The door stood open a finger's width on its own with the slate under it. Nobody in that room asked him what he was doing, and nobody in that room stopped talking about whatever it was they had been talking about, and about four of the nine looked at the open door once and went on with what they were doing.

Behind the bench there was a shelf, and the register stood on it with its spine to the wall, and it was not opened. Behind the register another slate lay face in against the wall, with a day scratched across the head of it and nothing whatever under that day. Her hands stayed off it the whole of that morning and its face was not turned over, and no mark of any kind was put on it. No hand in that room but hers went near that shelf.

The register-keeper said the thing that is said, and she said it to the room and not to the man at the door.

"**A room in this city is a thing a door makes and a date makes. A door that stands open on its own with a piece of a step under it is not a room any more, it is the end of a market with a bench in it.**"

A woman at the back said the thing that is said.

"Then it is not a room."

"**Then it is not a room, and about four people came up those stairs this morning who have not come up them before the water came, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles for a room that a person can walk into. I am not going to be the one who sends them back down, and I am not going to write their names down either.**"

Nobody thanked her for that and nobody improved on it, and about four of the nine in that room had worked out what it meant to be a room you can walk into, and not one of them said so. A man at the bench got up and put his hand flat on the face of the door and pushed, and it went about a finger's width on the slate and came back. He sat down again, and the light came in over the back step and went along the flags the way it does.

---

A woman came up that stair at about the tenth hour with a slate of her own under her arm and a tin in her other hand, and she did not stop on the step to wipe her boots. She went in past the door that was standing open on a piece of slate and stood at the back of that room with her back to the wall. Nobody in that room asked her one question about anything at all, and she did not wait to be asked one, and she gave her name to nobody. She stayed until the light had gone most of the way across the flags and then went down the stair again with the tin still in her hand. A woman at the bench said the thing that is said to nobody in particular.

"She has been before."

"**She has been before and she has not been asked anything by any of us this morning, and there is a difference between a person walking into a room and a person being sent for, and I am not going to be the one who makes the two of them the same by writing a thing down.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

---

The room over a market held about nine people on the hundred and sixty-seventh day of the Bare Month and it held about fourteen by the time that woman had gone, and the five that made the difference came up a stair through a doorway a man had held out of its own frame with a piece of the top step. One of the five stood at the back with a slate of her own under the arm for the whole of the morning, and the woman who keeps the register did not write it down and did not ask her for it.
