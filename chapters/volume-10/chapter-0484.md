# Chapter 0484 — A Man At The End Of A Lane

There was a lane past two gates with about nine households in it, and a wall at the top of the lane with nine boards gone off it. On the hundred and sixty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, a man was going up that lane with a stick in his hand putting a notch on the frame of every door he came to. The notches he was cutting were the same cut on every frame, and they were not anybody's tally, and they were not on any page. He had started at the gate end, and he was a third of the way up when Tamsin Quill came out onto the bottom step of that lane and stood on it with the fold of the sheet of road showing in the inside of her coat and about nine places on it. She did not take it out.

---

She stood on that step for as long as it took him to get to the fourth frame and cut his notch in it. He came on up the lane past her and did not stop and did not look at her. She said the thing that is said, and she said it once, in her own mouth, to his back and to the lane and to nobody else. Nobody in that lane improved on it and nobody asked her where she had got it from.

"**A figure that says how many of something there are is the plainest instrument of that claim that this flood has produced. It is plainer than a notice and plainer than a name, and I have been on a step about those Marches long enough to tell you the difference between the two. The figure is the one that counts and the name is the one that finds people.**"

He did not answer her. He went on up the lane with the stick and cut the fifth notch, and the sixth, and the seventh, and she came off the step and went up the lane after him at a distance of about ten paces. She did not say anything else, and there was nobody else in that lane to hear the first of it twice.

The man of about twenty-seven who washes the store's board was not in that lane and was not spoken of. The room over a market was not in that lane, and the woman who keeps a register in that room was not in it either, and nobody who came up that lane that morning knew what either of the two women in this flood of them was called.

A woman at a door said the thing that is said to the man with the stick and not to Tamsin Quill.

"Are you doing the whole of it or just this side."

"**The whole of it, and it is not a count of anything that anybody has asked me for, and I am doing it because a man came down this lane with a stick and told me to and did not tell me what for, and I have cut seven notches and there are two frames left and I am going to do those two as well unless a frame says otherwise.**"

He went on up the lane and cut the eighth notch in the eighth frame and stood in front of the ninth.

---

He got to the last frame in that lane and stopped, because that frame had already got something on it. Low down, where a child could reach it and where a grown person stooping to put a bucket under a step would put a hand, there was a name cut into the wood with a knife. The letters had gone white in the grain of it and had been there long enough for that. He turned the stick over in his hand and looked at the name and then at the space above it. There was room above it for a notch, and he did not cut the notch above it. He got down on one knee in the wet at the foot of that frame and put the end of the stick into the ground and dragged it, and then he drew the same stroke in the mud in front of that door where the water runs off the lane. He did it in one stroke the way he had done all the others, and he got up and looked at it and went on down the lane. The water that comes down that lane after rain took that stroke before he had gone the length of two houses, and he did not come back to look at it, and he has not been up that lane since.

That lane had nine door-frames on it that morning and eight notches cut in them, and a ninth stroke in the mud in front of the ninth house that was gone before the morning was out of it, and a name cut low into the ninth frame where anybody stooping for a bucket would put a hand on it. The man who had stopped in front of it walked back down that lane past a step with nobody standing on it and did not look at it going past.
