# Chapter 0462 — Two Men In One Room And Neither Of Them Is Asked

The man of about fifty-seven had his hands flat on the bench in front of him at about the tenth hour on the hundred and forty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in the room over a market, and the length of new rope was over the back of his chair where he had put it when he came in. It was where it had been every morning of this flood. Nobody in that room said one word about the rope, and nobody asked him about it, and it was not cut and it was not taken up, and it was on the back of that chair when the room emptied. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had a slate and a cloth in front of her at the bench and she was doing nothing at all with either of them.

---

A man came in out of a ditch with the mud to the knee and put his hand on the frame of the door and left it there.

"The ground wants doing."

He said that and stood in the doorway for a while and then went out again, and nobody in that room asked him a second thing, and he did not wait to be asked one. He farms the half of the holding the other man paid for, and he is about six years the older, and that is the whole of what this city can produce about the two of them, and it is not produced on anything.

Then the man of about fifty-seven said what he said, and he was right about most of it, and nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody disagreed with him out loud.

"**I paid for the half about forty years ago. It is sixteen willow posts and eleven withies and the withies are bound with the same cord, and a man came up the shut road with a handcart and cut them, and he was not sent, and I have never asked him why he did it and I am not going to.**"

---

A woman at the back of that room said the thing that is said in that room on a Monday.

"Then somebody has put it up and nobody knows whose it is."

"**Then somebody has put it up and nobody knows whose it is, and I have had about forty years to get used to that, and what I have not got used to is that nobody in four hundred miles has ever walked up that road to look at it, including me.**"

Nobody improved on that. A man who has been right about most of what he says in a room where nobody is going to act on any of it is a man the room has learned how to leave alone, and about four people in that room had worked that out a long time before this Monday.

The register-keeper wiped her slate with the cloth and set the cloth down and did not write anything on it, and she said the one thing she said that morning, and she said it to the room and not to him.

"**A thing that nobody has been asked about is the only kind of thing in this city that has not been decided by somebody for us, and I have a shelf behind the table in this room with a date on it and nothing under the date, and I know exactly what that is worth and I am not going to tell you what it is worth this morning.**"

---

The man of about fifty-seven did not answer her and she did not ask him anything. He sat with his hands where they were until the light went off the middle of the floor. Then he got up and went out of that room with his own two hands, and the rope stayed on the back of his chair where it had been all morning, and nobody in that room said one word to him about that either. At the stair he stopped and looked back at about four people in that room and did not say anything, and nobody said anything to him, and the man of about sixty-three was not there any more and had not been asked where he had gone.

The date on that door was the hundred and forty-seventh day of the Bare Month. The board at the far end of that market had been washed that morning and had the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it. Whether the fence stands between the right two things is not a thing anybody in that room has been asked, and it is not a thing anybody in that room is going to be told.
