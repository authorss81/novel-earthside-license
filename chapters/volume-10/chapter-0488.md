# Chapter 0488 — A Plank Against A Shed Door

The boards of a salt wharf at the bottom of this city were being run down a chute at about the seventh hour on the hundred and seventy-third day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and the weather came up off the water behind them. The light on that wharf was on the water and not on a wall, and a stack at the far end of it was higher than a man. Adrian Vale had both hands under the end of a plank that was going faster than the man above the chute wanted it to. He had it, and the man at the top of the chute had hold of the other end of it, and the chute was wet and there was nothing on that wharf to stop a thing once it started. There is a shed on those boards with a door in it, and that door was standing open with its frame square to the run of the chute and its middle rail about where a plank of that length arrives.

---

He got his right hand off the end of the plank and his left hand under the frame of that door at the middle rail and lifted. The frame came up off its foot blocks about as far as a man's hand is thick, and the plank went into the gap and stopped there. The man at the top of the chute let go of his end of it and stood on the chute with both arms out and did not come down. Nobody on those boards asked Adrian Vale what he was doing. Nobody on those boards stopped and looked at him either, and there was nobody standing about on that wharf waiting to see whether he would be any use to anybody. The man of about thirty-eight at the wharf, who had a tally-board against his leg with about four hundred and forty on it and a knife with a blunt edge lying on the boards beside the chute, walked to the head of the stack and looked down the run of the chute at the plank and at the door, and came back.

He said the thing that is said, in his own mouth, to nobody in particular.

"**That is a wet morning and there is no more of that coming down today. A plank into a door frame is a thing I have seen hold and a thing I have seen not hold, and I am not going to say which this is until about the fourth hour, and I am not going to say anything to the man who put it there.**"

A man at the foot of the stack said the thing that is said.

"It will hold."

"**Then it will hold, and I have watched about nine of them hold on a morning like this one and about the same again not hold, and a man who tells you which before the fourth hour is telling you what he would like.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody argued with him. The man of about thirty-eight put the tally-board down on the boards against the stack and put the knife on top of it. He got a bar and a length of cord off the shed wall and went along the row past the barrow that was standing at the end of the wharf with nothing at all in it.

---

They took the door off its hinges at about the fourth hour, the two of them. Adrian Vale took the weight of it on his shoulder while the other one got the pins out, and they carried it across about six boards and set it down on the other side of the opening and put it back on. The door went crooked and stayed crooked and would shut only if you pulled it. That shed has not shut since. There is a strip of clear board about as long as a cart leaning against the stack at the other end of those boards, and there is a man who has to walk round the outside of that shed now to get at the boards he wants. He walks round it and says nothing about it, and he has walked round it every morning since.

Nobody on those boards has said the name of the man who put a plank under that frame, and the tally-board still has about four hundred and forty on it, and the barrow at the end of the wharf is still standing there with nothing in it at all. The man of about thirty-eight has not taken that board down and put it up again, and that is the only number on those boards that has not moved.
