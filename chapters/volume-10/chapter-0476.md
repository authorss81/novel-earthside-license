# Chapter 0476 — The Rag Comes Back Off Her Board

Adrian Vale had both hands on a wet rag at about the seventh hour on the hundred and sixty-first day of the Bare Month, a Monday, in that market, and the rag was not his. It had not been on the stone lip where it belongs. The trough was against the wall under the store's board with the bucket standing in it, and above the trough the board was still on its two nails with the old figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else, and the wall over the top of it was not washed. A wet rag does not blow from a stone lip into a market, and somebody had taken it off that lip and used it, and it was lying on the corner of a stall board two along from the store with the water running off it onto the flags.

---

He took the rag off that board with one hand at each end and carried it to the trough and wrung it out over the water in the trough with both hands, and the water went up over the stone lip and came down again, and he shook it once and put it on the stone lip and left it there with the wet end hanging over the side. The woman whose board it had been on watched him do all of that and did not say one word while he was doing it, and about four people at that end of that market watched him do it and none of them said anything either, and a man at the end of the row went on with what he was doing to his own board.

Then she picked up a stub of chalk out of the box on the step beside the store's door and stood with it in her hand and did not put anything down with it, and she said what she said, out loud, in that market, to nobody in particular.

"**A figure on a board in the open can be taken off the board by the man who owns the board, and I have taken about forty of them off in nine years, and a figure on a wall in chalk cannot be taken off by anybody at all, and I am not saying that is a bad thing and I am not saying it is a good thing, I am saying that the difference between those two is my whole trade and that it is about to stop being mine to have.**"

---

Nobody thanked her. Nobody in that market improved on what she said and nobody asked her where she had heard it, because everybody at that end of that market had heard it, and it had been said out loud on the Sunday in the room over the market, and nobody had asked her anything about it and nobody had answered her and she had not said it a second time since. She stood with the stub in her hand and then she put the stub back in the box on the step and shut the lid of the box, and the lid shut the way a lid shuts on a box that is not locked.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came the length of that row with his own board under his arm and did not stop and did not look at the wall. Adrian Vale got the bucket out of the trough and carried it up the flags to the standpipe and filled it, and the water came out of the standpipe the colour of the sky, and he carried the full bucket back down the row with it in his left hand and set it in the trough and the level of the water in the trough came up over the stone lip and stood on it and stopped there. He put his hand flat on the rag on the stone lip to see whether it was wet enough, and it was.

---

A man at the end of the row said the thing that is said at that end of that market on a Monday, and he said it to the flags.

"You have just taken a thing off her board."

"**I have put a wet rag back where it was standing, and I did not take it off her, and if a thing can be taken off a board by anybody who puts a hand on it then it was never on her board, and I am not going to argue that with you in a market and I would like to go and fill the bucket again.**"

The man at the end of the row said nothing further about it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall was two stalls along with his own board up on its string, and he looked at the stone lip and at the rag on it and looked away again, and nobody in that market said one word to him about it.

---

The chalk in that box on the step belongs to the store and it is the chalk that would go on that wall, and the lid of the box was shut, and the board on the wall above the trough was still on its two nails with the old figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else, and it was still the only instrument in that market that had not said anything at all.
