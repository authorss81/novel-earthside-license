# Chapter 0459 — Two Empty Tins Come Up Nine Steps And Nobody In That Room Says One Word About Either Of Them

The stair at the back of the room over a market is nine steps and it has been nine steps since before the water came down, and two women came up it at about the eleventh hour on the hundred and forty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, at a quarter of an hour apart. The first of them was the woman of about sixty-nine, who had a tin in her two hands. The second of them was the woman who walked a market on two feet, who had a tin in her two hands as well. There were about nine people in that room and every one of them had been in it before.

---

Both tins were empty. The tin of the woman of about sixty-nine has been empty on every morning she has carried it up those steps, and it is empty this morning. The tin of the woman who walked a market on two feet has been empty on the two mornings before this one on which she carried a tin up those steps, and it is empty this morning. The two tins are not each other's tin and the two women are not each other's woman, and about four people in that room can see both of them at the same time.

Nobody in that room said one word about either of the tins. Nobody asked the woman of about sixty-nine what she had brought it for, because nobody in this city has ever asked her that, and nobody asked the woman who walked a market what hers was for, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register looked at the two of them and then went back to the shelf behind the table where the register is.

---

The woman who walked a market on two feet put her tin down on the table beside the sheet, on its side, and said the one thing that was being said in that room, and it was the only thing being said, and it was about stopping.

"**I have stopped, and nobody in this room is going to ask me about it, and I would like the rest of you to go on doing exactly what you were doing this morning, because there is nothing in this room that needs me to have started again.**"

Nobody asked her. A woman near the step said the thing that is said in that room most mornings, which is about the door.

"That door has not shut properly since the water came and there is a wind coming in under it that gets as far as the end of this table every morning and nobody in this room is going to do anything about it."

"**You have been saying that since the water came and I have been listening to you say it every morning of this flood, and I have not been listening to anything else.**"

Neither of those was an answer to the thing she had said a moment before, and neither of them was a question.

---

The woman of about sixty-nine stood at the end of the bench where she usually stands, with her empty tin in her two hands, and she did not ask anything. She has asked a question in this building three times, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, and each time it was a different thing, and each time she was answered by nobody, and she did not put a fourth thing into that room on this Friday morning. About four people in that room had been waiting for her to say something since she came to the top of those nine steps, and they were still waiting at the fourth hour, and nobody in that room said a word to her about not saying anything.

Nobody remarked on any of it. A woman who is not asked a question is a woman who was not asked, and the reason is an instrument and not a decision about her: there is nothing in this flood that carries a question to a person before the morning, and so the asking has to happen in a room about nine people were already in, and on this morning it did not.

---

The tin of the woman who walked a market on two feet stayed on its side on the table where she had put it and nobody moved it. The register went back on its shelf behind the table and the third column of it was empty on purpose and is empty on purpose. Behind it on the same shelf there is a second slate with a date on it and nothing at all under the date. Nobody in that room picked it up or turned it over or wrote on it, and the only hand that went near that shelf in the whole of that morning was the hand of the woman who keeps the register.

The date on the door was the hundred and forty-fourth day of the Bare Month, and about nine people went down those nine steps by the fourth hour, and one of the two tins was still on the table when the light went off it, and nobody had said a word about either of them from the beginning of that morning to the end of it.
