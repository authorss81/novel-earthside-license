# Chapter 0473 — A Column Empty On Purpose And A Column Empty

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her own working slate in both hands at about the ninth hour on the hundred and fifty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Friday, in the room over a market. She turned it over once in front of everybody, which she did not do for anybody, and set it down on the table in front of the bench. She put her hand flat on the back of it so that the whole of that room could see that there was nothing written on it. The hundred and fifty-eighth day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind her, and about nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything.

---

Behind the bench there was a shelf. On that shelf there was the register, with its spine to the wall, and it was not open, and no hand in that room but hers went near it. Behind the register on the same shelf there was a second slate with a date on it and nothing under the date, and she did not pick it up, and she did not turn it over, and nothing was written on it. In the register there were two lines and each of them had a day in it and nothing under the day, and she did not open the book to look at either of them and she did not read either of them out. She reached up behind the bench and put two fingers on the spine of the book and pushed it a finger's width further back along the shelf so that it sat square against the wall, and took her hand away again, and the book did not open and nothing on that shelf moved except the book.

A man at the back of that room asked the question that gets asked about a column in a room like this one, and he asked it of her and not of the rest of them.

"If nobody ever writes in it, what is it for."

---

She said it once and she did not say it again for the rest of that morning, and nobody thanked her for it and nobody argued with it and nobody asked her where she had got it from.

"**A column that is empty on purpose and a column that is empty because there is nothing to put in it are two different things, and this room has been treating them as one, and the reason nobody has found that out is that I have never once had a figure to put in that column, and on the morning I have one, the second column stops being a thing I am protecting and becomes a thing I am filling.**"

The woman of about fifty-two took her hands out of her lap and laid them flat on the bench on either side of her and left them there. The man at the back of the room got his hand up as though he were going to ask a second thing and then put it down again, and the slate lay on the table with her own hand flat on the back of it, and not one person in that room said one word to the woman who had just said the thing, and none of them was going to.

---

The woman of about fifty-two was at the far end of the bench with her hands where they were and was not asked anything by anybody in that room on that Friday, which is the ordinary condition of her in that room and which nobody in it has ever thought to remark on. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood against the wall with his own board under his arm and said the thing that is said in that room.

"You have never had a figure for that column because nobody has ever brought you one."

"**Nobody has ever brought me one and I have not asked anybody for one, and asking is the half of this I have never been able to do, because the only people who could bring me a figure are people I would have to ask, and there is no instrument in this flood for asking anybody anything in advance of anything at all.**"

She took her hand off the slate, wiped the face of it with the flat of her dress, turned it over and set it back down on the table on the other side of the bench from where it had been, and left it there, and the register stayed shut on the shelf behind her with a date on a slate behind it and nothing under either date. Nothing was written in anything that morning and nothing went anywhere that anything else could be read from.

---

There were two slates in that room on that Friday and they were four feet apart. One of them was lying face up on a table where anybody in that room could walk round it and look at it, and the other one was lying on a shelf behind a bench with its face against the wall, and the difference between those two things was not a difference about what was on them at all.
