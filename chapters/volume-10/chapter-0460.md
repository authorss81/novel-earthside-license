# Chapter 0460 — A Page Goes Round A Market In Four Hands And Nobody Says A Word About How It Comes Back

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the sheet flat in both hands in the open at one end of that market at about the eighth hour on the hundred and forty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday, and he was holding it out for the people at that end of the market to see. It came out of the room over the market folded twice with a bone, and there is a heading on it and one place ruled under the heading and a figure in that one place. The morning was an ordinary morning at that end of that market and the stall boards were up on their strings and a stone was on the foot of one string to keep it from going slack, and the trough was against the wall at the far end with the bucket in it.

---

It went into four hands. It went to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and it went to the woman who walked a market on two feet, and it went to a woman who has had a stall in that market since before the water, and the fourth was a man at a trestle who was eating and did not get up. Each of the four had it in his or her own two hands for about as long as it takes to read a thing off a board, and three of the four gave it back flat, and the fourth of them gave it back folded, and it came back to the man of about thirty-nine with a crease in it that is not the crease it went out with.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall turned it over once and looked at the fold and put it down on his own board and said nothing about the fold, and nobody in that market said anything about the fold, and nobody in that market said anything about who had folded it or whether it had been folded properly, and nobody asked anybody anything about it.

---

"**It has been read out to nine people in a room, and it has been read out to four people in a market, and that is the whole of the difference between the two mornings, and not one of the four of them is going to be able to repeat it to anybody this afternoon.**"

A man at the next trestle said the thing that is said at that end of that market on a Saturday.

"**You could stand there all morning with that and nobody here is going to write a figure on anything because of it.**"

"I am not asking anybody to write a figure on anything. I am holding a piece of paper in the open at one end of a market, which is what four people do with a piece of paper when four people are all that there is."

Nobody thanked him for that. Nobody in that market improved on it and nobody in that market argued with it, and the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board put the sheet down flat on the trestle and kept his own hand on the corner of it the way he keeps his own hand on the corner of his own board.

The woman who had a stall since before the water looked at the figure in the one place for a while and then asked the market what it was a count of. She asked it out loud, in the local language, in daylight, standing at her own stall on an ordinary Saturday morning with her own hands empty.

Nobody in that market answered her. A man at the next trestle said something about the price of cord. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at his own board on the string. The man of about twenty-seven at his trough was working at the far end of that market and did not turn round, and the woman who walked a market on two feet had a slate under her arm and nothing written on it and looked at the flags. Nobody in that market answered her, and nobody told her the answer was not hers to have, and nobody in that market has said one word about that question since.

---

About four people at that end of the market had the figure in front of them that morning and about four of them did not, and about four of the four looked at it and could not have said afterwards what it was underneath the heading, and none of them is going to be able to say so to anybody this afternoon either.

The sheet went back into the room over the market at about the tenth hour in the same hands it had come out in, and nobody opened it and nobody unfolded it, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register put it on the table with the crease in it and did not say one word about the crease.

Then the store's board at the far end of that market went up onto its two nails with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and nothing else on it, and the man of about twenty-seven put the rag on the stone and the bucket back in the trough, and the day was the hundred and forty-fifth day of the Bare Month, and nobody said anything back to it.
