# Chapter 0458 — He Is Handed A Rag And Nobody Teaches Him Anything

The man of about twenty-seven had the store's board off its two nails and down on its edge on the flags at about the seventh hour on the hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month, a Thursday, and the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood were facing out into the market in the ordinary way, and the trough was against the wall under it with the bucket standing in it and the rag on the stone lip. Adrian Vale was standing at the end of that trough with his hands at his sides. He has not washed a board in this city before and nobody in that market had said so to him.

---

The man of about twenty-seven went into the store and came out and put the rag in Adrian Vale's hands without a word and went back in. He did not show him which way to hold it and he did not tell him how wet to get it and he did not stand and watch him do it, and he did not stop him. He had decided a long time ago what he was going to do about that board and he had not decided anything at all about the man holding the rag, and there was a difference between those two things which nobody in that market was going to be told about.

Adrian Vale took the rag in his right hand and got the bucket out of the trough and filled it at the standpipe at the end of that market and carried it back and put the board down into the trough face up, and the water came over the figures.

---

He worked it from the left to the right and back the way it is done, and he did it twice over, and the second time he did it slower than the first. There was a number standing up out of the wood under the water where the chalk had been, and he put his hand flat on the board beside it, and he said one thing out loud in the local language to the trough and not to anybody in that market.

"**There is a number under the water.**"

A woman buying something at the stall two along said the thing that is said in that market every morning of this flood, and she said it to the man at the stall and not to the man in the trough.

"Are we doing the boards up again or are we not."

"We are, and we are doing them in the order and not in the order you would do them in."

Nobody in that market took any of it up. Nobody asked Adrian Vale a second question about the number under the water and nobody asked him a first question about anything else, and the man of about twenty-seven came out of his store with a broom in his hand and went past him and swept the flags at the end of the trough and said not one word to him about the rag or the board or the number, and went back in.

About four people had come through the end of that market by that hour and about four of them looked at the man in the trough instead of at the board. About four of them have worked out since that a man who has spent a long time in rooms with pages in them spent that morning on his knees in a trough with a rag. There is not one of those four people who is going to say a word about it to anybody.

---

Nobody asked him what the number was a count of. Nobody in that market knows and nobody in that market is going to know this morning, and the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood are the last account this city has of that day and they have not been looked at by anybody in that market since the water came and they were not looked at this morning either.

He drained the trough and put the bucket back in it and put the rag on the stone lip, the way it had been, and he got the board up off the flags and stood it against the wall ready for the two nails. He was not asked to do that either and he did it. The man of about twenty-seven came out and took the other end of it, and they put it up on its two nails together, and the two of them did not say one word to each other while it was going on. Afterwards the man of about twenty-seven went in and shut the door behind him, and the board stood on the outside wall with the figures of the two hundred and twenty-fourth day of this flood on it and the wall above it that he does not wash.

The day was the hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month, and the market went on being a market, and a man stood in a trough and then got out of it, and nobody gave him anything and he asked for nothing.
