# Chapter 0478 — He Looks At The Rope And Nobody Asks

The man of about fifty-seven came into the room over a market at about the ninth hour on the hundred and sixty-third day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday, and sat down at the end of the bench and put his hands flat on it in front of him. A length of new rope was over the back of his chair, and it had been over the back of that chair every morning of this flood. The hundred and sixty-third day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind him, and nobody in that room said one word to him about the rope, and nobody asked him anything at all, and it was not cut and it was not taken up and it did not fall off the chair that morning or any other morning.

---

He gave the count out because he is the man who paid for the half of it, and not because anybody in that room had asked for a count, and nobody thanked him for giving it.

"**Sixteen willow posts and eleven withies, and the withies are bound with the same cord, and a man cut them with a handcart he brought up the shut road and nobody sent him, and in about forty years not one person in this city has come out and asked me where the whole of it stands, and I have not been asked today either and I had stopped waiting for it.**"

Nobody in that room improved on that. Nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody disagreed with him out loud, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register did not say one word about the posts or the withies or the road, and the register was on its shelf behind the bench and was not opened, and the second slate was on the shelf behind that register with the date on it and nothing whatever under the date.

Then he said the other thing, and he said it once and he did not say it again for the rest of that morning.

"**The only thing anybody has ever done about a piece of ground in this city is put a number on something, and I have had a piece of ground for about forty years with two men on it who have never agreed about it, and nobody has ever put a number on anything at all about it, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to guess.**"

---

A man at the back of that room said the thing that is said.

"Somebody would have to write it down."

"**Somebody would have to write it down and somebody would have to stand under it, and there is no more of either of those in this flood than there was when I paid for the half, and I have not been asked where it is and I have not said, and neither of those is going to change this morning.**"

Nobody thanked him for either of those sentences and nobody argued with him, and nobody in that room or outside it was in a position to say that he was right about any of it, and the two men at the bench went on with what they had been doing.

He took his hands off the bench and pushed them into the pockets of his coat and left them there, and the rope did not move and nothing in that room touched it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register turned her own slate over on the bench, wiped the face of it with the flat of her dress, and turned it back the way it had been, and did not write one word on it. He did not get up out of that chair until the light had gone off the middle of the floor, and he got up by pushing back on the bench with both hands, and the rope stayed over the back of the chair the whole of the time.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair at about the eleventh hour with his own board under his arm and stood at the back of that room for the rest of the morning. He looked at the man of about fifty-seven and he looked at the length of new rope over the back of that man's chair, and he did not ask him where the fence is, and he did not ask him what the rope was for, and he did not ask him anything at all, and neither of those two men said one word to the other.

---

The rope was over the back of that chair all morning and it was over the back of that chair when the man got up and put his coat on and went out and left it there, and it was the one thing in that room that nobody had any business looking at twice, and the one person who had looked at it had spent the whole of the rest of that morning looking at the flags.
