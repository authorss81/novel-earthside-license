# Chapter 0477 — A Man Who Knocks At Doors For A Living

At about the tenth hour on the hundred and sixty-second day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday, the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was part of the way up the stair at the back of the room over a market with a satchel under his arm, and he came the rest of the way and put the satchel on the bench and took his hand off the flap of it and left it lying there. He had come up that stair on a great many mornings over a great many years and he came up it the way he always came up it, and this morning he stopped part of the way and stood on the step with the satchel against his leg and looked at the whole of that room from where he was standing for about as long as it takes a man to come in at the bottom. Then he came the rest of the way up and set the satchel down on the bench with the flap of it lying flat against the underside of the bench top, and the people in that room went on with what they were doing. The hundred and sixty-second day of the Bare Month was in chalk on the door behind him, and about nine people were in that room and none of them had been sent for anything, and nobody said one word to him about the satchel being on the bench.

---

He put both hands flat on the top of the bench on either side of it and left them there, which is not a thing a man does with a satchel he intends to pick up again in a hurry. A woman at the bench said the thing that is said.

"You have brought that in from the road."

"**I have brought that in from the road and I have put it on your bench and I have not opened it and I am not going to open it, and I have carried five notices up that road on my own back, and a return in a satchel, and a sheet that came on the round, and the one that came on the round after it.**"

"Six."

"**Six, and I have not opened one of the six, and I am not going to stand here and be praised for that, because a man who has not opened a thing has not done anything at all, and a bench in a room is not a thing anybody would call having done something.**"

Nobody in that room praised him for it. The register was on its shelf behind the bench and was not opened, and the second slate was on the shelf behind the register with a date on it and nothing under the date, and it was not picked up and it was not turned over and it was not written on.

---

He said the other thing a little after that, and he said it to the room and not to the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and he said it once.

"**I have knocked at more doors in this flood than anybody in this city and nobody has ever asked me a single thing while I was doing it, and the reason nobody asks is not that I am not any use, it is that a man who knocks has already come to the door, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can go out to a door to ask whether it would be all right.**"

The register-keeper said one sentence and she said it to him and not to the room, and she did not say thank you and she did not say she was sorry.

"Then you are the only man in this city who has been to the outside of a door in this flood and has nothing to show for it, and I have a shelf behind this bench that has the same trouble, and I am not going to compare the two of them in front of about nine people."

Nobody compared the two of them. Nobody in that room asked him what he knocked on, and nobody asked him how many, and nobody asked him what he had found out, and the satchel lay on the bench with the flap down for the whole of that morning while the light came over the back step and went along the flags and reached the near side of it.

---

He picked the satchel up at about the eleventh hour, and he did it without hurrying and without any of that room knowing he was going to, and he went down that stair with it under his arm and the flap of it lying flat against the side of it, and out through the door at the bottom into the middle of that market on a Tuesday. The bench where it had been had nothing on it at all and had not had anything on it, as far as anybody in that room was able to tell, before he came up or after he went.
