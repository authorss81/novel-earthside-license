# Chapter 0900 — Rain On The Stone At The Gate

The rain came down straight on the road at the far end of the shut one, and the gate at the end of it stood open on wet stone with water running off the bars onto the road.

It was Friday, the five hundred and eighty-fifth day of the Bare Month. The road had been shut since before the flood, with grass grown up between the ruts and the ruts full of water, and at the far end of it the gate stood open with nobody minding it. About nine ordinary people waited under it with ordinary things, sacks and baskets and bundles tied with cord, and a child among them with a bundle of seed husks in her arms. Adrian Vale stood on the near side of the gate with his hands empty at his sides and rain on his shoulders. On the far side Tamsin Quill stood with a book under her arm and rain dark on the cover of it.

---

Two people had a hand on the gate, and the stone at the foot of it was worn in two places where hands have gone. The rain ran off the bars and off the hands on them alike. The ordinary people shifted their sacks and baskets under the shelter of the bars and looked through to the far side where the road went on.

**"Are the next people through players,"** said the child, with the bundle in her arms.

**"They are people coming through on a road folk at both ends have agreed to keep open."**

Adrian Vale said it in one sentence, to the middle of those waiting, and he did not look away from the child when he said it. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was right. Tamsin Quill shifted the book under her arm and did not open it. The two hands stayed on the gate where they were.

**"Will the road stay open if the rain keeps on,"** said one of those waiting with a sack on his shoulder.

**"It will stay open,"** said Tamsin Quill, from the far side.

The rain went on over the road and the gate and the stone. The ordinary people moved forward by ones and twos through the opening, with their sacks and baskets held clear of the bars, and the child went through with her bundle held up under her chin to keep it dry. Adrian Vale stepped aside to give the width of the gate to those going through, and then walked toward the far side where the woman with the book waited.

---

When the last of them had gone through the gate stood open as it had stood, with rain on the bars and on the stone. The worn places in the stone held clear water, and the water ran out of them down the road toward the shut length behind.
