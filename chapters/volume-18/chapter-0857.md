# Chapter 0857 — Four Times Down And Four Times Up

The flat of a hand went onto the bottom step of the stair as the man of about twenty-seven went down it in the wet, and the stair was running, and there was water standing along the bottom step where the stone dipped and the water had come to a stop against the wall.

It was raining by the fourth hour on Thursday, the five hundred and forty-second day of the Bare Month, and it came on the flags of the market row and into the stairwell past the door standing open on its latch. The date in chalk stood on the outside of the door, and the board under the date still standing out from the wood. The rain had got into the dust along the top step and darkened it in a band across the middle, and the iron lay on the wet stone beside its own two empty sockets with none of the grit coming off it. The trough at the top of the market step had taken the ice out of it over three days without a hand at it and was running a foot of water along its length, and the man of about twenty-seven could fill his bucket out of it.

He went up the row with the bucket against his hip and the rag across its rim, and filled it at the top of the step and carried it back with a hand at the rim, and the water came over and ran down the front of his coat and left a line on the flags behind him going up the row. He set it down by the leg of the table with the water in it standing a finger's breadth off level, and went down the stair again and came up with it again.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stood at the window with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down and watched the man of about twenty-seven go down past him and come up again with the bucket.

**"You will have the stair slippery by the middle of the day."**

**"I have had it slippery since the rain came on,"** the man of about twenty-seven said, and went down it again.

**"Then put the rag on the step and not in the bucket."**

**"The rag is for the sill,"** the man of about twenty-seven said, and set the bucket by the leg of the table, and the room went on with the page and the boards and the rain going along the row.

---

He went down the stair four times that morning and up it four times, and the bucket went along the market row four times and the water came over its rim on every one of them, and nobody in the room timed him and nobody counted the journeys. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register turned a leaf and squared it and put her hand back on the page, and the bench was wet through at the near end and dry at the far one. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress did not sit down at the side of the bench. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along came up out of the row at about the eighth hour with his own board face in and put it against the bench leg and stood at the middle of the bench. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood at the far end with the board propped at the leg of it and his own hand flat on the face.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one went down the stair with his satchel under his arm and came back up with the same satchel and the flap of it down, and went down again at about the tenth hour and did not come back up.

---

The rain had eased to a mist by the time the light had gone off the bench wood, and along the flags of the row the water was going back into the ground in patches, and the dry patches and the wet ones moved on up the row away from the store's end at the speed a person walks.
