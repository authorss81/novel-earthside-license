# Chapter 0860 — The Wheel Dropped In And Went Straight On

Two hands went onto the handles of a barrow and the near wheel dropped into a track in the ground that was not there on Saturday morning, and the wheel did not turn in it and came out of it on the far side and went on with the load on top of it.

It was Sunday, the five hundred and forty-fifth day of the Bare Month, and the rain was coming back across eleven miles of flats from the west with the wind behind it, and it had not come far enough yet to wet the ground through. The track was two lines pressed into the ground close together and going on along the flat in the direction of the market row, and the ground on either side of it was unbroken, and no cart had taken it and no wheel but this one had been in it.

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf had the load on his barrow and it was not boards that morning. It was sacking over something round and heavy, and the sacking was lashed at the top with a coil of cord. He had his tally-board under his arm and the knife in his belt where it stayed. He came off the yard and out on to the flats and went along them with the barrow, and he went through the track twice before he stopped looking at it and once more afterwards without stopping at all.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the side of the road with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down, and he fell in with the barrow and went along beside it for a while, and the wind came off the flats into their faces and the rain came on after it and went along the flats beside them.

**"You are going the whole of it with the wheel in the same line."**

**"It is where the wheel goes."**

**"That is not a road."**

**"It is not a road,"** the man of about thirty-eight said, **"and nothing on wheels has been in it."**

They went on up the flats with the satchel under the one man's arm and the barrow handles under the other's two hands, and the load shifted once on the sacking and the man of about thirty-eight stopped and took it up a hand's breadth and lashed it again, and the two of them went on and came to the flags at the store's end of the market row with the rain behind them.

---

At the store's end they got the load off the barrow and carried it in past the awning on two hands a side, and the near end of the sacking went under the boards standing in the rows and the far end of it stood out into the yard, and nobody in that yard asked what was in it or where it was going. The man of about thirty-eight put his tally-board face up on the top row with the two lines of grey grit lying across it and did not turn it over.

The man of about thirty-one took the satchel off his shoulder and set it against the wall of the store with the flap down where he had put it, and stood in the rain at the step of the store with his hands empty and the water coming off the awning onto the flags in a line.
