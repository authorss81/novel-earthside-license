# Chapter 0872 — The Shape Of A Thing Nobody Had

The flat of the right hand of the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went down the column holding figures, from the head of it to the foot of it and off the foot of it into the margin, and she stopped there with the hand flat.

It was Friday, the five hundred and fifty-seventh day of the Bare Month, and the light came in under the awning at the store's end and lay along it from the near end to about the middle of the room. The door at the bottom of the stair stood open on its latch. Somebody had chalked a day on the outside of it, and the chalk had gone grey in the weather, and under it the bare piece of board about as wide as a hand stood clear of the wood at its near end. Five strokes stood down the column in front of her with a day against each of them, and none of the five was struck, and there was no sixth of them entered, and the page had not been shut since the middle of the week.

Behind the page the woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress stood in the shade of the shelf and did not sit down on anything. The man of about twenty-seven had his bucket by the leg of the table and had already carried it down and up once. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living kept the one window, with the satchel under his arm and its flap hanging down off the side of it. Against the far wall on the side of the table the man with the one wet sleeve stood with his hands empty at his sides. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along was in the middle of that bench, his own board across his knees and his own thumb going along the foot of it, where a rate has wanted a day against it from the middle of the week onward and has still not got one, and at the far end of the bench the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood with his own hand on the face of his own board, the board leaning on the leg of the bench.

---

The first hour went along the bench the way it goes, and what came out of it was the rate at the foot of the man of about thirty-four's own board.

**"I would like to know how a day gets into the column,"** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, and put his thumb on the rate and left it there. **"I have had my thumb on it all week and this is the first time I have said it out loud."**

**"I have not seen one go in,"** said the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living, at the one window, without taking his hand off the strap of the satchel. **"I have seen the page open and I have seen it shut and it is all I have seen it do."**

The question went back along the bench and came along it again, and the mouths it came back with gave the next mouth nothing it could use. The man of about twenty-seven said nothing about it and put his bucket down and took it up again. Nobody asked the woman of about fifty-two, and her hands stayed at the sides of her dress. The man of about thirty-nine said nothing through the whole of the hour, and his own hand stayed on the face of his own board where it had been.

**"It would want a thing said out loud in a room,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, **"and then the same thing written down by somebody else, and then the second one read back out again so the first one could hear it read back. That is the shape of it. I have never seen it done and there is no such thing in this city, and not one person in this room has one in a pocket, and it is not lying on a shelf."**

The hand of the woman of about twenty-nine went back onto the page and stayed where it was. The shape of the thing went out of the room in the mouths it had been said in and went into nothing else: it was not written down, it was not put on a surface, and nobody asked her where she had got it from or asked her to say it again.

**"Then a day goes in when one of us says it and another one writes it,"** said the man of about thirty-four, with his thumb still on the rate.

**"Or it goes in the way it has been going in,"** she said, and turned a leaf and squared it against the fold and put her hand back onto it.

---

The rate at the foot of that board wanted its day still. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket down the stair and up it and set it by the leg of the table with the water in it standing flat. The man of about thirty-one went down once with the satchel and came up once with it, the flap hanging, and nobody asked him what was down there or why he had gone. The man of about thirty-nine put his board down face in against the leg of the bench at about the ninth hour and stood with his hand flat on the back of it and looked out of the one window at the far end of the row, and nobody in the room asked him what he was looking at.

The light came off the bench wood before the light went off the row, and the room went on round its page and its boards and the bucket standing at the table leg, and not one of the three of them went any nearer to a day against a rate.

At about the tenth hour the keeper squared the page against the fold with two fingers of her right hand, and the grey of the cut edge of the paper came off on the flat of her thumb and lay there in a crescent, and she turned the thumb over and looked at the crescent and then put the hand back onto the page with the grey of it showing.
