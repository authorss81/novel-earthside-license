# Chapter 0867 — A Wet Coat Over The Back Of A Chair

The hand of the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went into the pocket of her own apron and stayed there a moment, and came out again with nothing in it, and went straight back onto the page of the open register and turned no leaf.

It was Sunday, the five hundred and fifty-second day of the Bare Month, and a mist had come off the flats in the night and had not cleared by the light and was standing in the row off the flags. Outside, at the foot of the stair, the chalk on the door carried a day and the board under the chalk stood off the wood at its near end. The bar lay along the top step out of its two sockets and nothing lay under it.

The coat was over the back of the chair at the far side of the room. One sleeve of it was wet to the shoulder and the other sleeve was dry, and the wet sleeve stood up off the wood where the weight of it was, and the dry one had begun to come up under itself where the air had got at it.

---

The man with the one wet sleeve was at the one window in his shirtsleeves and had been there since before the light. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress was in her place at the side of the bench and did not sit down. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat at the middle of that bench with his board across his knees and his thumb along the foot of it, and at the far end of it the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood with his board leaning on the leg of the bench and his hand flat on the back of it. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the near end with the satchel under his arm and the flap of it down. The man of about twenty-seven came up the stair at about the sixth hour with his bucket and went on with his own work.

**"That row is not going to ask him one thing,"** said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along, to the board across his knees.

**"It is not going to ask him one thing,"** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board.

**"Then it wants nothing off him,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and neither of the two men looked round the room while she said it, and nobody took it up and nobody said anything about it, and she turned no leaf and put her hand where it had been.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board went down the stair and came back up with his board still under his arm and stood at the far end of the bench again. The man of about thirty-one went down the stair once and came up once with the flap of the satchel down, and nobody asked him anything and he said nothing. At about the ninth hour the keeper's hand went into the apron pocket and stopped there a while and came out and went onto the page again, and the man at the window had not turned round while she did it and had not looked at the coat on the chair at any hour of the morning. Nobody asked the man at the window one thing about Saturday, about the stair, about the row or about the coat, and he asked nobody for anything at any hour of the day, and he did not thank anybody for anything.

The light did not come into the room at all. It stood off the sill in a flat grey and the wall along the far side was the same colour at the tenth hour as it had been at the fourth.

---

The chair at the far side of the room had a coat over the back of it which had not been on the chair the day before, and the dust on the seat of it was showing plain and unbroken from the front edge to the back, and nobody in the room had sat in it, and the grey light of the morning lay across the dust and showed every grain of it standing up off the wood.
