# Chapter 0865 — The Wall Board Stood On One Nail

The rag came off the rim of the bucket and went into the hand of the man of about twenty-seven, and he stood at the step under the board on the outside wall and looked up at it. The three sets of figures on it were showing at the height of his own chest.

It was Friday, the five hundred and fiftieth day of the Bare Month, and the sun was coming in low under the awning at the store's end and had not yet reached the wall. The board was on two nails above the step, as it had been, and one of the two nails was in it and the hole of the other stood empty above it, round and clean inside, with a ring of old grit lying in the bottom where the head of the nail had not been for long. The board hung off the nail it had and sat a hand lower in the wall than the figures on it wanted, and the lowest of the three sets came out at the height where a man walks. He had carried the bucket past the step on every morning for as long as he had been doing it, and he had always had to go up on the step to read them. He read them now standing on the flags.

---

He put the bucket down at the foot of the step and got the end of the bucket handle up under the lower edge of the board and lifted. The board came off the wall about the thickness of a coin and the nail in it held it. He let it come back against the stone, and the nail took it again without marking it.

**"It wants lifting off the nail, and it is not coming off with a bucket,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, and let the handle down and picked the bucket up off the step.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register came down the stair and stood at the step and had to go up on the step to read the figures, and read them from the bottom of the lowest set to the top of the highest and did not read them again.

**"It was higher than that when I last stood here,"** she said, from the step, with her hand on the frame of the stair door behind her.

**"It was high enough to want the step,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, and put the handle of the bucket down the back of his coat where it belonged and picked the bucket up.

---

The register stood open on the bench upstairs and could be read down the column that holds figures, where five strokes stood with a day against each of them and none struck and no sixth entered. He had read the column down every morning of the week and had never once read it across. The board on the outside wall could be read across from where a man walks, and he had never tried to read it down. The hole in the stone above it could be read in neither direction from anywhere at all, and he stood under it with the bucket in his hand and looked up into it, and the keeper came out and stood at his shoulder and looked up into it as well.

Neither of them put a finger in it. Neither of them said when the hole had been empty, and the water coming off the awning ran down the stone either side of it and never through it.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along came along the row and read the figures standing on the flags without going up on the step, and went on to his own trestle and said nothing about it to anybody.

---

The sun came onto the wall below the board about the tenth hour and did not reach the hole above it, and the shadow of the board's own lower edge lay across the lowest of the three sets and stayed there while the sun came on up the wall above the board and did not touch the bottom of the hole.
