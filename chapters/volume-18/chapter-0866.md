# Chapter 0866 — The Man With One Wet Sleeve

The flat of Adrian Vale's right hand was on the sill of the one window and the sill was wet at the corner where Monday's rain had got in behind it, and his left hand hung at his side and stayed there, and he had come up the stair before the light for no reason he could have put to anybody if he had been asked for one.

It was Saturday, the five hundred and fifty-first day of the Bare Month, and the weather had gone round to the north and the morning came up the stairwell in a hard white light with the door standing open on its latch at the bottom of it. A date in chalk stood on the outside of the door and could be read from inside the room through the opening, and under it the bare piece of board stood off the wood at its near end, as it has stood all this week. Along the top step above it the bar lay where it lay on every morning of this flood, out of its two sockets, with nothing under it.

He had wanted to be in the room when whoever came up the stair came up it. He was in it, and he had been in it since before the light, and he was going to find out nothing about who came up it, and the finding out was not what he had come for either.

The room was full and most of it was standing. The register was open on the bench in front of the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register with her hand flat on the page. The board of the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood propped against the far leg of the bench and the flat of his hand lay on the face of it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own board across his knees at the middle. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress was against the wall at the side of the bench. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was at the window with the satchel under his arm and the flap of it down.

---

The first hour of the morning went along the bench the way it always goes, and what came out of the morning was the trough at the top of the market step, which had a crack in the near end of it, and nobody had done anything about that trough since a mouth in this room said it out loud.

**"You have not done anything to that trough."**

**"I have not,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, who had the bucket by the leg of the table and the rag over the rim of it, **"and I have not said I would."**

The man of about thirty-one said nothing at all about the trough and had not been in the room before the light. The woman of about fifty-two was not asked it. The man of about thirty-nine said nothing about the trough at any hour of it, and the woman of about twenty-nine turned a leaf and squared it against the fold and kept her hand on the page. Nobody settled whether the trough wanted doing.

**"A crack in a trough wants nothing done to it until the water wants in,"** said the stallholder, to the board across his knees, and nobody took it up after him.

---

At about the fifth hour a man came up the stair.

He came up the open length of the steps and stopped at the top of them, and one sleeve of his coat was wet to the shoulder and the other sleeve was dry, and he stood on the top step with his arms at his sides and looked into the room the way a man looks at a room he has not been in. Then he came the rest of the way up and came into the room and stood against the far wall on the side of the table, and the light from the one window was on him, and the sleeve of his coat which was dry to the shoulder was dry inside the room as well.

The room made nothing of him. The woman of about fifty-two turned her stool two inches along the wall out of his way and left it there and did not look at him again, and the man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket out of the room and back again and set it down by the leg of the table, and the man of about thirty-nine kept his hand where it was on his board. Nobody in that room asked him anything, and nobody thanked him, and nobody had stood up when he came to the top of the steps and nobody had gone out of the room and nobody had sent anybody, and nobody in there had been waiting for him.

Adrian Vale said one thing.

**"He has come up the other side of that door."**

He said it flatly and into the middle of the room and not to anybody in particular, and nothing came of it, and nobody answered him and nobody turned round, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register went on with the page under her hand.

---

The stallholder got his board off his knees and tried to stand it against the near end of the bench, and could not, because Adrian Vale was standing at the end with his hands at his sides and there was not room for a board and a man together. He carried it the length of the room instead. He stood it against the shelf at the far end, where the dust showed plain where it had been leaning, and the board went over once and stood with the bare back of it to the wall, and he came back the length of the room to the bench and took his seat and put his hand flat on his own knee where he had not put it that morning. Adrian Vale knew what the carrying had cost him and did not turn round to see it done, and nobody in that room said anything about it and nobody said anything to Adrian Vale, and Adrian Vale did not move.

The man against the far wall did not speak for the rest of the morning. He did not ask the room for anything and he did not thank the room for anything. He stood with his hands at his sides, and at about the seventh hour he turned his head and looked out of the one window at the row going up past it, and he stood there while the room went on round the page and the boards and the bucket standing where it had stood all morning, and nobody in that room said one word to him at any hour of it and nobody said one word about him.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along carried his board back from the far end of the room to the near end of the bench and set it down across his knees again, and the light had gone off the top edge of it by the time he had done it. He had carried it the length of the room and back inside three hours, and the shelf at the far end showed the edge of it pressed into its own dust a finger's depth in from the front of the shelf, and there was a mark in that dust which had not been there that morning.
