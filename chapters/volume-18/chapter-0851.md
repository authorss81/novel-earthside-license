# Chapter 0851 — The Board Under The Date Stood Off The Door

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register set both hands flat on the spine of the register and pushed it back along the shelf behind the bench until the back of it stood against the wall, and the second slate behind it stood where it stood and did not move, and she took her hands off the book and left them hanging at her sides.

Friday came in cold and dry, on the five hundred and thirty-sixth day of the Bare Month. A date stood in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair, and under it a bare piece of board about as wide as a hand had nothing on it whatever. The door stood open on its latch and the light came up the open length of the steps and stopped short. The iron lay along the top step out of its two sockets, where it had lain on every morning of the flood, and the room above the market had gone on every morning of the flood without one of its people looking down the stair.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat down at the middle of the bench with his own board across his knees and the bare back of it turned out into the row behind his shoulder. She took the near end and squared the page with two fingers. At the far end the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood with his board propped against the leg of it and the flat of his right hand on the face. The light lay along the middle of the bench wood and did not reach either end. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress was against the wall at the side of the bench, and the man of about twenty-seven set his bucket down by the leg of the table with the rag over its rim.

---

Three things in the room could be read, and they did not agree about anything. The page in front of the keeper could be read down the column that holds figures, where five strokes stood with a day against each of them and none struck and no sixth entered, and the column on the right of the page carried a word near the head of it that was not in her hand. The door could be read across, and what it said was one day and nothing under it. The iron on the top step said nothing at all, and had said nothing for every morning anybody could remember.

**"You have put the page back the wrong way up."**

**"It goes in the way it comes out,"** she said, and turned it round.

**"It does not,"** he said, and kept his hand on the face of his board.

**"It does to me,"** she said, and left her hands flat on the page.

The talk went on about a load which had waited out the wet and could go now, and about the trough at the top of the market step holding since the day before, and about the flags drying in patches from the store's end back. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket out to the step and brought it up full and set it down, and the water in it went round once and stood flat. At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-four turned his board over on his knee and turned it back again, and went on with the grain of it under his thumb.

---

At about the eighth hour the keeper stood up, squared the page with her thumbnail, and left the register open on the bench where it lay with the book standing open to the air of the room. The man of about thirty-nine kept his hand on the face of his board and looked out of the one window at the far end of the row, and the man of about thirty-four went on at the middle of the bench with his own board across his knees.

At about the tenth hour he put his board face down on the bench to keep the light off it, came across to the sill and looked down past it, and stayed there with his hand on the frame.

**"That board is not lying flat on the door."**

**"It has been that board the whole time,"** the keeper said, without looking up from the page.

He came away from the sill and stood at the near end of the bench with his hands hanging, and the room went on with the page and the buckets and the light going off the bench wood. At the middle of it the man of about thirty-four lifted his own board off his knees, looked at the near end of the wall as though for something not standing on it, and put the board down again. The keeper shut the register on the mark of her own thumb and put it back on the shelf with the second slate standing behind it, and the two of them on the shelf went into one shape with the book in front of the slate.

---

In the last of the light a man came up the market row and looked at the outside of the door as he went past and went on up the row without stopping. The board under the date was standing off the wood at its near end with a finger's width behind it, where a hand writing the date above it would have brushed the top edge on the way out, and the dust in the edge had gone hard and pale and had a skin on it.
