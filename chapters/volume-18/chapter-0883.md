# Chapter 0883 — A Second Person On The Step Below

The light came up the stairwell from the open door at the foot of it through about a hand's depth of mist, and some way up from the bottom there was a person standing on the step below the top one with his hands at his sides, and he had not come up out of the mist and he had not gone down into it.

It was Tuesday, the five hundred and sixty-eighth day of the Bare Month, and there was a mist off the flats which came up the market row at about the fourth hour and came into the stairwell with the air and stood in it, and it did not burn off until the light had got up over the roofs at the far end of the market. The woman of about fifty-two kept her hands at the sides of her dress and did not go to the door. The man of about thirty-four stood in the doorway on the sill side looking up the row. The man of about thirty-one stood with the satchel under his arm, looking at the flags outside the window and not at the stair. The man of about thirty-nine had his own hand flat on the wood of the far end of the bench. Otto Marsh was against the far wall on the side of the table with his hands behind him where he had put them.

---

At the near end of the bench the keeper went her own hand down the column holding figures and came off the foot of it into the margin.

**"A column of figures goes down and only down,"** she said, **"and there is no way to read across one of these and get anything at all out of it."**

**"It is all of them at the height a man walks,"** said the stallholder, from the window, **"and I read it off the flags twice this week and I get the same off it every time."**

**"You get the same,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the leg of the table with the bucket beside him, and put his hand down into the water in it.

The page went on under the keeper's hand and the board on the outside wall went on carrying its figures at the height of a man's chest, and neither of them changed at any hour because a person had been looked at in between them. A page is read down and a wall board is read across, and a person standing on a step with his hands at his sides is read neither way, and there was no way in the room to read him at all.

About the seventh hour the man of about thirty-nine got his hand off the far end of the bench, went down two steps of the stair and stood on the third one looking down into the grey at the person below him for as long as it took to see what there was there to see, and came back up and stood at the far end of the bench again with his own hand flat on the wood where it had been.

**"He is not coming up,"** said the man of about thirty-nine, to the room.

Nobody asked him how he knew it, and nobody put a question to the one on the step, and the man of about thirty-nine said his one sentence and went and put his own hand flat on the wood of the end of the bench again. Otto Marsh did not turn round from the wall and did not go down the stair and did not say a word about the person standing below him, and the man of about thirty-nine did not say it a second time either.

The mist came into the room a hand's depth off the flags by the middle of the morning and lay along the floor of it in a low grey, and the light off the row came through the open door and stopped where the grey was thickest. The keeper went to the head of her column, moved a leaf and squared what was under her hand against the fold. The man of about twenty-seven took the bucket down and up the stair and stood it by the table leg with the water standing flat in it. The man of about thirty-four came off the row carrying his own board on its edge with the bare back of it turned out, and nobody in the room told him there was anybody on the step below, and nobody in the room had been told anything about it either.

The mist had gone off the flags by about the ninth hour and the light was coming in under the awning across the walking spaces of the row, and the person on the step below the top one was standing where he had been standing since before the light, in the same place and the same way, his hands still at his sides. No mouth in that room dated his coming or said how long he had been there, and no mouth in that room asked anybody anything about it.

The man of about thirty-one went down the stair at about the ninth hour with the satchel under his arm and came up it again at about the tenth hour with the flap of it down. He went past a person on the step below and neither of them said anything, and he did not tell the room about it and nobody in the room asked him.

---

The mist came back up the stairwell from the flats about the tenth hour and stood in it as high as the fourth step, and the light coming through the open door at the bottom laid a slab of it across the flags as far as the leg of the table. There was a skin on the water standing in the bucket where the slab reached it, and the skin broke where the light came onto it and went together again in one piece further along.
