# Chapter 0887 — Read From The Flags, And The Same Again

A hand went down flat on the stone of the market step and came up with the frost standing in the lines of it, and the frost on the step was about the depth of a coin at the fourth hour where the awning kept the sun off it, and where a boot had been the stones under it were pale and the stones either side of them were white.

It was Saturday, the five hundred and seventy-second day of the Bare Month, and the night had gone hard again with no cloud in it, and by the fifth hour the light came in under the awning at the store's end of the market and lay along the bench from end to end, the same light Friday's had come in and the same shadow off the edge of the awning. The frost had gone off the walking spaces of the row by then and there was water standing in the low place in the stone at the side of the yard with a skin on it. The boards on the trestles stood with their bare backs out where they had stood all this flood, and the one face standing out among them was standing out the same way it had stood out on Friday. At the store's end of the row the door stood open on its latch and there was a day chalked on the outside of it, and the chalk had gone the colour of the stone round it.

---

The board on the outside wall at the far end of the market stood above a step at the end of the row. It was on a nail and the hole above that nail was empty. Its figures came out at the height where a man walks, no line of words stood under any of them, and anybody going along that row could read all of it off the wall without going up on the step in front of it.

**"I read it off the flags yesterday and I read it off the flags this morning,"** said the stallholder, standing out at the end of the row with his own board under his arm, **"and it is the same off it both times, and the figures come out at the height a man walks at."**

**"It is on a nail and the hole above it is empty,"** said the keeper, who had come down the stair behind him to the door, **"and it has been on the nail long enough that the hole has gone its colour."**

**"There is nothing under any of the figures,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the step with the bucket in his hand, **"and a man can read all of it standing on the flags where he is stood."**

About four people went by the row on their own business while the light was on it, and some of them stopped in front of the board on the wall and stood there while they read it, and some of them went along without stopping at it, and the ones who stopped did not stop long. Some of them stopped at the face standing out in the row as well and stood clear of the walking space while they stood there, and then went on up the row. The stallholder read the board off the flags once more before he went up the stair, and put his own hand flat on the wall under the figures and came away with the stone cold on the palm of it, and said nothing to anybody about what was written there.

Nothing whatever was done about the face standing out in the row on that Saturday at any hour of the day. The man of about thirty-nine came in at about the ninth hour and stood at the far end of the bench and looked down the row at his own trestle out of the one window for a while, and did not go down, and did not turn his board, and said nothing to anybody about it, and the keeper went over a leaf at the near end of the bench and drew what was under her hand square against the fold, and the face out in the row stood out in the open the same way it had stood out on Friday.

---

The frost went off the face of the board on the wall in the middle of the morning when the sun came round onto it, and the empty hole above the nail took none of the frost at any hour of the day and had none in it to take. By the light's going there was a rim of white standing round the edge of that hole where the frost had been lying when the sun came onto the wall, and it was the only thing on that side of the market which had not gone the colour of the stone by then.
