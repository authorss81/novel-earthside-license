# Chapter 0884 — One Round Trip Where Two Had Been

The woman of about fifty-two was at the foot of the stair before the light was on the flags, with her hands at the sides of her dress, and the stone at the bottom step was wet through where she was standing and had been wet through for some time before she came to it. Above her head the door stood open on its latch, and a day in chalk on the outside of it was going soft in the weather.

It was Wednesday, the five hundred and sixty-ninth day of the Bare Month, and a thin sleet came across the flats out of the east, not enough to wet a coat and enough to be in the face. It went along the row and came off the edge of the awning at the store's end in a line of small drops which hung on the wood and fell when the wind moved it. In the room the keeper sat at the near end of the bench with the register open on the wood in front of her, and five strokes stood in the column with a day against each of them and none struck. The man of about thirty-four had his own board propped against the leg of the bench with the bare back of it out. The man of about twenty-seven had his bucket on the stone of the step with the rag hanging across the rim. Otto Marsh stood on the far side of the table with his hands in front of him where he could see them.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was on the step at about the sixth hour and came up with the satchel under his arm, the flap of it hanging, and put the satchel down on the sill of the one window, took his own hand off it and went and stood at the far end of the bench with his hands hanging at his sides. He had gone up the stair twice on Friday morning with the flap down both times. He came up it once on this one, and he stayed up.

**"There is a man used to go down that stair and up it again in one morning,"** said the stallholder, **"and there is no going down it at all today."**

**"There is no going down it at all today,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, at the leg of the table, **"and there is water coming out of the near end of the trough and it would want somebody at the step with a pail under it."**

**"There is not on the page that he goes down the stair,"** said the keeper, without lifting her hand, **"and there is not on the page that he does not. This column has had nothing in it to do with him at any hour."**

The satchel was not opened at any hour of that morning. No reason for it was given and nobody in that room asked him for one, and the room's ordinary talk went on under it with the flap hanging the whole time.

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one anything. He was where he had put himself with his hands hanging and the satchel on the sill behind him, and at about the ninth hour he went and stood in front of the window and looked out of it at the sleet going along the row, and at about the tenth hour he was still standing there, and no mouth in the room had said one word to him between the sixth hour and the light going off.

The woman of about fifty-two stayed at the foot of the stair the whole of the morning and did not go down it, and the light off the row came through the open door behind her and went up the steps and stopped at about the fourth one, and she said nothing about the trip which had not come. Otto Marsh looked at the sill and at the satchel standing on it once and then looked at the flags and did not look at either of them again. The man of about thirty-nine went down the row at about the eighth hour and came back up at the ninth and put his own hand flat on the wood of the far end of the bench, and said nothing to anybody about what was out there in the row.

---

The dust on the sill had been going pale and loose in the shape of a satchel for as long as anybody had been setting one down on it. On this morning there was one shape in it, and the shape was the shape of the satchel standing on the wood above it. Underneath the newer shape, where the older one had been standing before it, there was a second shape of the same width and a little longer, and it had been set down nearer the middle of the sill, and the two of them had been pressed into the wood by two hands on the same morning while the satchel was still standing on the sill and its flap was hanging.
