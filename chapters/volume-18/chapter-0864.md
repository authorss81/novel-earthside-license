# Chapter 0864 — Somebody Made A Sound On That Stair

A hand went flat on the strap of a satchel under an arm and turned the strap a quarter round so the buckle lay flat against his side, and the flap was down where it always was, and the man carrying it went down the stair and the door at the foot stood open on its latch with the light coming up the steps past him.

It was Thursday, the five hundred and fifty-ninth day of the Bare Month, and there was no wind at all and the stairwell kept everything done in it. The dust on the top step lay undisturbed along the wood and the bar lay along the step beside its own two sockets with nothing under it.

Up above, the room stood full. The register lay open on the bench with the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register behind it and her hand flat on the page. At the far end of the bench the board of the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood propped against the leg of it under his own hand. At the middle of the bench sat the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along with his own board across his knees. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress kept her place against the side of the bench in the shade.

---

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had gone down before the light and had not come up. The man of about twenty-seven went down and came up with the bucket and the rag and set it by the leg of the table, and the room went on, and at about the fifth hour something happened on the stair which only one man in the room was sure he had heard.

**"There was somebody on the top step of that stair a minute ago,"** said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board, from the far end of the bench, without taking the hand off the back of his own board.

**"I heard nothing,"** said the stallholder, and did not lift his face off the board across his knees.

**"You would not hear it where you are sitting,"** said the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register, without taking her hand off the page, **"the stair gives a sound back twice, and the second time is louder than the first."**

Nobody got up. The woman of about fifty-two did not turn her head towards the stairwell door, and the man of about thirty-nine said nothing further about it and put his hand back where it had been on the back of his board, and nobody said where the man of about thirty-one was, and nobody asked.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came back up with the satchel under his arm and the flap of it down. He came into the room and stood at the far side of the bench and did not say where he had been, and nobody asked him. The strap was squared where he had turned it. He stayed at the far side of the bench for the rest of the morning and did not go down again.

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board put his hand flat on the back of his own board and said the flag at the near end of the room had come up loose where the water came under the sill.

**"It will come up again with the next lot of water,"** said the stallholder, with his thumb along the foot of the board across his knees, **"and it will come up under the sill and not against it, and the difference between the two is one nobody in this room is going to see."**

At about the ninth hour the man of about twenty-seven went down the stair and came back up with the bucket, and the water stood flat in it.

---

That stair did not make the sound again that day. The door at its foot had been swinging in and out on its latch in the draught off the flags all day, and at about the tenth hour it stood still against its jamb with the latch not down and the light lay along the top of it in one long strip.
