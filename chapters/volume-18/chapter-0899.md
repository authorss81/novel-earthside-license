# Chapter 0899 — Two Boards Off Onto The Flags

Adrian Vale had both hands on the near end of a stack of boards on a barrow at the side of the yard, and the wood was wet through the top board and damp along the edges of the ones under it, and he leaned his weight on it to keep it from shifting while the barrow stood.

It was Thursday, the five hundred and eighty-fourth day of the Bare Month. The morning came up grey with rain hanging off, and the flags at the side of the yard were dark with two hollows in them, one with its crack gone deeper and one with pale grit and nothing broken in it. The man of about thirty-eight at the salt wharf stood at the handles with the tally-board face up on the frame under two lines of grey grit and the knife in his belt. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood at the mouth of the yard with his own board under his arm. The man of about twenty-seven stood by with the bucket in his hand. Otto Marsh stood against the wall of the store his hands kept behind him, one sleeve of his coat wet to the shoulder and the other sleeve dry. He kept off the wet crown.

---

Adrian Vale had wanted to have the stack dry by the fourth hour, and it was not. The rain in the night had gone through the cover and into the top boards, and the cover lay folded back over the far end while the near end stood open to the sky. He kept his hands where they were while the man at the handles eased the barrow forward a half length to clear the hollows, and the near wheel came round the far side of both of them and neither came near it.

**"Ease her forward and I will take the top two off before they wet the ones under,"** said the man of about thirty-eight, at the handles.

**"Ease her,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, with the bucket in his hand.

The man at the handles pushed forward, and Adrian Vale took his hands off the near end to give the width of the frame room, and the two top boards slid with the push and came off the stack onto the flags with a flat sound. He put his hands back on the new top board and held it while the barrow settled. Nobody thanked him, nobody told him he was right, and nobody in the yard was waiting for him to be useful and he was not useful to them.

The two boards lay on the flags with the wet faces up, and the water stood on them in a thin sheet. The man of about thirty-four came forward from the mouth of the yard, looked at the boards on the stone and at the stack on the frame, and said nothing about either. Otto Marsh stayed against the wall of the store and said nothing. The man of about thirty-eight wiped his hands on his coat, lifted the tally-board off the frame, set it face down on the dry crown beside the hollows, and left it there.

**"They will dry on the stone if the rain holds off,"** said the man of about twenty-seven.

**"They will dry,"** said the man of about thirty-four.

---

When the barrow went on up the yard toward the row the two boards stayed where they had fallen. The thin sheet of water on the upper one drew back from the edges toward the middle, and by the light going the middle held a dull shine while the edges had gone the colour of dry wood.
