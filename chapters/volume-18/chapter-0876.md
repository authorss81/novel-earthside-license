# Chapter 0876 — Three Surfaces And What You Could Read From Where

A hand went down the column of figures on the open page of the register from the head of it to the foot of it, and came back up the same way, and went off the top of it into the margin above the head stroke and stopped there.

It was Tuesday, the five hundred and sixty-first day of the Bare Month, and it was a close morning with the light coming in flat and no shadow standing anywhere in that room, and the flags of the row had gone the colour of ash. The door at the bottom of the stair stood open on its latch. A day was in chalk on the outside of it and a bare piece of board about as wide as a hand stood under the chalk, as it stands. Nothing whatever was done about the board standing out in the row on Monday, at no hour of the day, and by the light's going nothing about it had been altered since it was put there.

---

Three things could be read that morning and they were read three different ways, and the room worked it out without anybody sitting down to do it. The keeper sat at the near end of the bench and hers was the first of them. The stallholder had read one of the others off the flags at some point in that week and was in the middle of the bench now. The man of about thirty-nine stood at the far end of it with his own hand flat on the face of his own board, which was not in the room and was out in his own row with its face standing out into it. At the side of the bench the woman of about fifty-two stood with her hands at the sides of her dress, and the man of about twenty-seven had the bucket by the leg of the table, and the man of about thirty-one stood at the one window with his satchel held up under his arm and the flap of it hanging. The man with the one wet sleeve stood on the far side of the table with his face turned into the room.

The page in front of the keeper was read down and only down. Five strokes stood in the column holding figures with a day against each of them, and there was no way to read across that column and get anything out of it, because there was nothing beside any stroke of it to read across to. The board on the outside wall at the far end of the market was read across, and the three sets of figures on it came out at the height where a man walks now, the one nail it hangs on being the only nail in it, and a man going along the row could take all three of them in without going up on the step.

**"You can read it from where you stand on the flags,"** said the stallholder, wiping his hands down the front of his coat and not looking at anybody while he said it. **"I read it from there yesterday and every stroke of it came out."**

**"You can,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, with the bucket by the leg of the table. **"I read it from there this morning. I cannot read it from up here, and I have been in this room for a week."**

---

He said one thing about the face of his own board, standing where he had been standing.

**"It is out because it is out,"** he said, to his own hand on it. **"Nobody has to read it and nobody has to do anything with it."**

The page went on under the keeper's hand. The woman of about fifty-two said nothing. The man of about thirty-one went down that stair at about the ninth hour, the satchel up under his arm, and was back up it at about the tenth hour with the flap of it still down, and nobody asked him anything. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket down and up twice and the water in it stood flat every time.

The third of them was a surface and not a page, and it was the face of the board out in that row, and it came out of the one window as a pale rectangle standing up between two trestles with the light flat on it. It was the same size from up there and the same shape as the bare back of it had been from up there the day before that, and at the distance of the window nothing written on it could be told from anything else written on it. The man of about thirty-nine looked out of the window at it at about the tenth hour, and the man of about thirty-one looked out of the window at it at about the same hour from the far end of the room, and neither of them said anything about it and neither of them was asked.

The light on the face of that board went off the top of it first, the way light goes off a thing standing up in the open, and the pale rectangle it had been from up the stair became a grey one, and then it was the same colour as the trestles standing on either side of it.
