# Chapter 0877 — What A Face Out In That Row Meant

Two hands came down onto the stone of the market step with a bucket set down between them, and the water in the trough at the top of the step went over the rim and ran along the stone and stopped against the leg of a trestle.

It was Wednesday, the five hundred and sixty-second day of the Bare Month, and the wind had come round to the south-west and had been pushing at the back of the row all morning, and the edges of the boards standing on the trestles had been moving in it since the light. The trestles stood in a line with a walking space between them and the flags went up between the walking spaces, and the boards on them stood face in against the trestles as they had stood all this flood, and at one trestle in the middle of the row a board stood with its face out into the space.

At his own trestle, two stalls along, the stallholder stood with his board propped face in against the leg of it and the bare back of it turned out into the row behind his shoulder. The man of about twenty-seven had the bucket on the stone of the step and the rag over the rim of it. At the store's end of that row a date stood in chalk on the outside of a door at the foot of a stair, with a bare piece of board under the chalk about as wide as a hand, and not one person in the row went near that door.

---

**"There are people stopping at the trestle,"** said the stallholder, at about the seventh hour, without turning round, with a board in his hand.

**"There are people stopping,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, who had come along the row with the bucket and gone past both of them and was on his way back, **"and there are people going up the row as they went up it before, and the two are not the same people."**

Nobody in the row said the face was new. The man it belonged to came along the row at about the eighth hour with his own hand at the side of it and stood at his own trestle in front of it and went on doing what he went to a trestle to do, and nobody followed him up the row to look at it a second time and nobody said a word to him about it.

About four people a day went past the row on their own business, and nobody went up the stair at the store's end of it. Some of them stopped in front of the face that was standing out and stood well clear of the walking space while they stood there, and some of them did not stop at all, and the two were not the same people going by. The stallholder read it once from his own trestle on his way past and went on laying boards.

**"People standing in a walking space is the whole of what it is,"** said the stallholder, with his own thumb along the foot of his own board where the rate wanted its day. **"It is not a rate and it is not a day against anything and it does not go in a column."**

**"It is not any of those,"** said the man of about twenty-seven, and took his bucket on up the row to the trough and filled it and set it down again with the water standing flat.

---

By the ninth hour the walking spaces of the row had got to be a thing people stepped round, and nobody said it out loud either. The stallholder took his own board down off the leg of his own trestle a while later and stood it against the leg with the face of it in and the bare back of it out, and nobody asked him to do the other thing with it and nobody said anything to him about it.

The wind came off the flags at about the tenth hour and went along the row from the store's end, and where people had been standing in front of that board the flags were trodden through to the stone and the stone was wet again and shining where it had been dry, and behind it every other board in that row went on showing its back.
