# Chapter 0858 — He Squared The Strap Where It Had Cut

A hand went onto the strap of a satchel under an arm and pulled the strap round once and squared it where it had cut into the coat at the shoulder, and the hand came off it and the flap of the satchel was not lifted.

The light came in at the one window and lay along the middle of the bench wood and did not get to either end of it, and the flags below were drying in long patches from the store's end back. The stairwell under the door standing open on its latch smelled of wet stone going warm. The date in chalk stood on the outside of the door, and the rain had left the stone round it darker than the rest of it. Along the top step the iron lay out of its two sockets, with the grit of the step gone pale along the underside of it.

It was Friday, the five hundred and forty-third day of the Bare Month. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat at the middle with his own board across his knees and the bare back of it turned out into the room. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had her register open on the bench at the near end, with the second slate standing behind it on the shelf behind the bench. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress stood where the light came off the sill and stayed standing. At the far end the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had the flat of his hand on the face of his board.

---

He went up the stair and came down it and went along the market row and came back up, and the flap of the satchel was down on all four journeys. He set the satchel down at the far end of the bench at about the fifth hour and stood beside it looking out of the one window, and he picked it up again at about the sixth and squared the strap where it had cut and went down the stair with it and came back up with it. Nobody in the room said a word to him about the satchel at any hour of that morning.

**"The flags will take the day off them."**

**"They will take more than today off them,"** the keeper said, and turned a leaf.

**"Then nobody will walk on them in boots."**

**"The row walks on them in boots,"** the keeper said, and squared the page against the fold.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about twenty-seven went down with the bucket and filled it and brought it up again, and the water in it stood flat. The man of about thirty-four turned his own board over on his knee and said the rate at the foot of it still wanted a day against it, and the keeper said the page had not been shut since the middle of the week, and the man of about thirty-nine said a rate on his own board would want a day against it as well, and put the flat of his hand back on the face of it.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living went up the stair and down it and along the market row and back up, and the strap was squared where it had cut before he came through the door, and nobody in the room marked any of the four journeys at the time.

**"The flags will be dry enough to walk on by tomorrow,"** the keeper said, and she stopped there and turned the leaf over and put the flat of her hand on it, and the light went off the bench wood and up onto the wall above the shelf.

---

By the tenth hour the strap had cut through the cloth of the coat at the shoulder and there was a thin place in it where the light came through, and he did not turn that shoulder toward the window.
