# Chapter 0890 — Said Back In Another's Own Words

The door at the foot of the stair stood open on its latch with the chalk date on the outside of it at the height of a man's shoulder, and Adrian Vale came up the steps and into the room over the market with nothing in his hands at all, and the light off the row came in under the awning and up behind him and lay along the bench from the near end of it to the far end.

It was Tuesday, the five hundred and seventy-fifth day of the Bare Month. The cold had come in behind the night and there was no wind and no cloud, and the light came white and hard through the one window and put a hard edge on the sill. The room was full and most of it was standing. Otto Marsh stood on the far side of the table with his hands behind him, one sleeve of his coat wet to the shoulder and the other sleeve dry. At the near end of the bench the keeper had the register open on the wood in front of her with the flat of her hand down on the page, and the second slate stood behind her on the shelf with the face of it clean. The man of about thirty-four had his own board lying across his knees with the bare back of it out and the flat of his own hand along the foot of it where a rate wanted a day. The man of about twenty-seven had the bucket standing by the leg of the table with the rag across the rim of it. The woman of about fifty-two had her hands at the sides of her dress at the wall side of the bench. The man of about thirty-nine was at the far end of the bench with a knot in the wood of it under his own thumb.

---

At about the eighth hour he came off the floor and said the cost of it out loud first, in his own mouth, into the middle of the room, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of the stair behind him and the light on his face.

**"—"**

Nobody improved on one word of it, nobody said one word back to him about it, and nobody asked him anything else about it then or afterwards at any hour of the day. The keeper turned a leaf and squared it against the fold and put her hand back onto the page. The man of about twenty-seven carried the bucket out of the room and down the stair, filled it at the far end of the trough and brought it up and set it down beside the table leg with the water standing flat in it. The man of about thirty-four took his own hand off the face of his own board and put the hand on his own knee and left the board propped where it was. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living had the window with the satchel under his arm and the flap of it down, and nobody asked him anything.

A while after that he said the other thing, once, and it was the only other thing he said in the room.

**"—"**

The room went on round it. The man of about thirty-nine kept his thumb on the knot in the wood of the far end of the bench and did not turn round and said no word about it, and the woman of about fifty-two looked at the shelf and said no word about it, and the man of about thirty-four took his own hand off the foot of his own board and put both his hands flat on the top of his knees and left them there.

The dust in the light along the top of that bench went up and turned over in it where he was standing, and the man of about thirty-four's own knees and his own hands flat on the top of them and the knot under the far end of the bench all stayed where they were while it did.

Then Otto Marsh came off the wall on the side of the table, and he came about nine feet and stood on the far side of the bench with his face turned into the room, and he said it back, in his own words, on the same morning.

**"—"**

Nobody thanked either of them. Nobody agreed with either of them and nobody argued with either of them and nobody bettered a word of what either of them had said, and no mouth in the room said a word about where a man had got anything from on the morning he said it. The man who had said it first stood where he had said it and did not look at the man who had said it back, and Otto Marsh put his hands behind him again and went and stood against the wall he had come off, and nothing whatever was done about any of it at any hour of the morning.

The keeper turned a leaf and squared it against the fold. The bucket went down the stair and up it again, and the water stood flat in it every time. The man of about thirty-four took his own hand off his own knee at about the tenth hour, and the cloth of his trousers at that knee had a dark place on it about the size of a hand where it had been wet through and had not dried, and the sun came back onto the row and went along the bench and found it, and the damp in it had begun to come up out of the cloth in a pale ring at the edge of it.

At about the tenth hour the light came off the row and went along the flags and up the bench to the wall along the far side of the room, and after that hour there was no way in this city of putting a question to him, and not one mouth in the room put one there after it, and nobody in that room has said one word about it since, and no mouth in this city can say what became of the asking.
