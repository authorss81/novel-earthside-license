# Chapter 0801 — A Bar On The Top Step

The man of about thirty-nine came up that stair with his board under his arm and set his right hand flat on the iron lying across the top step, and found it colder than the wood and wider than his own two fingers past his own boot.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The light came in at the one window and stopped short of the bench wood. Down at the bottom of that stair the door stood a hand's width off its frame with its date in chalk showing white on the outer face, and nobody at the top of that stair had put it that way.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end with both hands empty. The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket by the window, and the woman of about fifty-two waited near the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress.

---

He stood where he was with his hand on the iron a while. Then he took his boot off the top step, went down two, and stood on the one below it, where the whole length of that stair and the open door at the bottom of it were in front of him at once. Two people went past on the paving below and neither of them looked up.

He came back up and stood the board against the near leg with its face turned in, and took his hand off the iron and put it in his coat. He came down the bench to the near end beside the keeper's page and stood there with his back to the far end of that room.

**"Was it shut on Friday?"**

The keeper kept her eyes on the page.

**"I do not know. I do not come down it till the tenth hour."**

He said nothing to that. He stood at the near end a while longer with his hands in his coat, and the bench went on behind him about the rain and a trestle, and about four of the people along it did not turn round.

---

At about the tenth hour the keeper shut the book and carried it the length of the room to the shelf behind the bench, ahead of the dark shape that had never been picked up. The date on the outside of that door stood where it had stood all morning, whole and unrubbed, with clean wood beneath it and no thumb anywhere near it.
