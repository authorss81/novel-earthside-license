# Chapter 0812 — The One Who Had Not Come

The keeper put both hands flat on the open page at the near end of the bench and looked down the length of that room toward the far end, where the man of about thirty-one usually stood.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday. The light came in at the one window and lay across the bench wood. The top step of that stair was in view from the near end of the bench, with the iron lying across it, and the door at the bottom stood a hand's width off its frame.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-nine came up with his board under his arm. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of the bench with her hands at the sides of her dress.

The talk along the bench was of a trestle leg that wanted wedging and of rain coming back before evening, and the keeper turned her leaf through it without looking up.

---

At about the fifth hour the keeper looked at the far end again and said it to the middle of that floor, in the ordinary course, and nobody had asked her for it.

**"He has not come up this morning."**

The man of about thirty-four turned his hollow over without looking up off it.

**"He may have gone past below and not come up."**

Nobody said anything to that. The talk started again about a trestle at the far end of the market row, and about four seconds went by, and nobody in that room put a morning to it or said when he had last been up it.

---

The keeper turned the leaf back and went on with the page as if nothing had been said.
