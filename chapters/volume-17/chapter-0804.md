# Chapter 0804 — Nine Words About A Bar

The man of about twenty-seven came up that stair with his bucket in one hand and his rag over his shoulder and set the bucket down under the window, and stayed standing beside it, which he did not usually do.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. The room was quiet enough that a man could be heard from the near end to the far one. The bar lay on the top step and the door at the bottom of the stair stood a hand's width off its frame, and both of those had been so since the middle of the week.

The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end. The woman of about fifty-two was at the side of the bench.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about twenty-seven took his hand off the side of the bucket and looked down the open length of that stair.

**"I have never had that bar in my hands."**

Nobody answered him. About four of the people along that bench had been in this room on the Wednesday morning when the same man had stood in it and said that the door shut from the inside, and none of them said anything now either.

The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four turned his hollow over and set it down. The man of about thirty-one looked at the top of that stair and then at the far wall. Nobody asked him a second question and nobody improved on what he had said, and the talk came back in about four seconds on a trestle at the far end of the market row.

---

The rag was spread on the stone lip at the top of the market step and left lying damp there, and the water standing in the trough beside it had not been drawn off at any hour of that day.
