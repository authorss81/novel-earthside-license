# Chapter 0824 — Wiped Round And Not Over

The man of about thirty-nine put the flat of his right hand on the stone of that wall below the lowest of the three sets of figures on the board above it, and the stone was cold where it had been wet and dry where it had not.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and ninth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The board was on the outside wall at the far end of that market on two nails above a step, and the three sets of figures were written on it in the board's own chalk, and no line of words stood under any one of them. Somebody had gone over that stone with a wet cloth that morning. They had done it round each of the three sets and stopped short of every one of them, and each set stood in a field of stone that had been washed and dried paler than the stone round it, and the figures themselves were untouched and standing out of that pale field darker than anything on the board.

He ran two fingers round the edge of the lowest of the pale fields and the grit of it came off on his fingers.

---

The man of about thirty-four came along that market past the store's end with his own board face in and his hands in his coat, and stopped, and put his head on one side and looked at that wall.

**"Somebody has been at that board with a wet cloth."**

**"Round them,"** the man of about thirty-four said.

**"Not over them."**

Nobody asked either of them who had done it and nobody went back into the store to ask. The row went past that step all morning on its own business, and two people looked at that board as they went by and neither of them said anything about the pale fields round the figures, and no line of words was put under any of the three that day and none was taken out from under any of them.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-four said that whoever had done it would have had to be up before the light, and the man of about thirty-nine said nothing to that at all, and neither of them asked the other where he had been at the fourth hour.

The cloth had been gone over the stone at the sides of the figures and had not been gone over the figures, and whatever was on the cloth had gone into the board where the cloth had been dragged at the corners of the work, and by the tenth hour the grit had all of it dried into the low places of the chalk.

---

By the tenth hour the wiped hollows on that board had gone pale all round their edges and the figures in them stood up off the stone of it like writing that had been left in a clean place on purpose.
