# Chapter 0818 — Two Men At The End

The man of about thirty-four put one hand on the bare back of his own board and stood at the back of his own trestle, and the man of about thirty-nine stood beside him with his board under his arm, and both of them looked down the row.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and third day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. The row was quiet at that end of the market and the flags were dry. The bar lay on the top step of a stair at the back of the market behind them.

The man of about thirty-nine shifted his board under his arm and said the rain had held off since the middle of the week. The man of about thirty-four said it would come back before evening. They talked of a rate that wanted a day against it and of a trestle leg that wanted wedging, and neither of them turned round toward the stair.

A person came down the row with a message and neither man stopped them, and a barrow went past them toward the far end with a squeaking wheel, and both men watched it go without a word.

---

At about the seventh hour a person went past with a basket and both men moved aside, and after they had gone they stood where they had stood, and the thing nearest to both of them stayed unsaid between them.

**"That leg will want doing before the rain,"** the man of about thirty-four said.

**"It is the store's business and not mine,"** the man of about thirty-nine said, and neither asked the other anything at all.

---

One trestle stood empty at the end of the row till the light went, and nobody set a board on it.
