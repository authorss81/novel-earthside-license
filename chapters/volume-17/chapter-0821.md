# Chapter 0821 — A Stool Where Nobody Can Get At It

The woman of about fifty-two came up that stair and put both hands under the seat of the stool that stood at the side of that bench, and lifted it off the flags and turned it round with its back to the wall.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and sixth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light came in at the one window and lay across the bench wood and did not reach the corner she was carrying it to. The shelf above that corner comes down to the height of a person's shoulder, and the table with the cloth on it stands against the wall in front of it, and between the two of them there was room for a stool and not much else.

She set it down and pushed it back with her heel until the top of it touched the plaster, and took her hands off it. To get at it a person would have to move that table out from the wall and stand on the bench to reach past the shelf, and nobody in that room moved the table. The keeper was at the near end with the register on the shelf behind her. The man of about thirty-four was at the far end with his hollow on his knee. The man of about thirty-nine came up with his board under his arm and stood it against the near leg.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-nine looked down the length of that bench and then at the corner, and said a thing into the middle of that floor, and nobody had asked him for it.

**"There was room at that end this morning."**

**"There was,"** the woman of about fifty-two said, without turning round.

Nobody improved on that and nobody argued with it. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four turned his hollow over and set it down on his knee the other way up, and the talk came back in about four seconds on a rate that wanted settling and a trestle leg at the far end of that market, and nobody said one word about where a stool had gone.

At about the seventh hour a man came up that stair and stopped just inside that door and looked along the bench for somewhere to sit, and the far end of it was full, and he put his hand flat on the wood at the near end for a moment and took it off again and went back down. He did not go into the corner and he did not say anything about the corner. Nobody asked him what he had come up for.

The light came off the bench wood before the ninth hour and went onto the wall above the shelf, and the corner with the stool in it went into the shade of the shelf and stayed there.

---

The flags at the side of that bench kept the damp shape of where that stool had stood, and at the tenth hour the shape was still on them.
