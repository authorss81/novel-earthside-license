# Chapter 0820 — A Fortnight And Nobody Asking

The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket down on the flags at the foot of that stair and put his rag over his shoulder, and looked at the two sockets in the frame of the door.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and fifth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. The door at the foot of that stair stood a hand's width off its frame and the light came in off the flags. The bar lay on the top step above, and the sockets stood empty below it with the dust lying in them where the iron had been.

He picked up his bucket and went on up the paving with it. At the top of the market step he set it down again beside the trough, and the keeper came up behind him with the register under her arm.

---

At about the sixth hour in the room above, the woman of about fifty-two looked down the open length of that stair toward the empty sockets at the bottom of it.

**"Those have stood empty a fortnight now, and nobody has asked about them."**

The keeper turned a leaf and said nothing. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end with his hands on the strap of his satchel and said nothing either, and the talk came back on a rate before about four seconds had gone by.

Nobody asked what was to be done with them. Nobody said whose business they were. The morning went on round the two empty places at the bottom of the stair as it had gone on round them every morning since the iron came up.

---

Nobody came down that stair after the ninth hour, and the room went on till the tenth.
