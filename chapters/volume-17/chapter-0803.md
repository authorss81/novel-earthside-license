# Chapter 0803 — Before The Light Is On The Flags

The woman of about fifty-two came down that stair and put her right hand flat on the outer face of that door, low down, at about the height of her own hip, and left it there while she looked at the two sockets in the frame.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The rain had stopped in the night and the flags were wet with it and the light had not got on them yet. The bar lay at the top of that stair above her, where it could not be seen from where she stood.

She put her fingers through into the far side of that door and touched the inside of the sockets, one and then the other, and took her hand out again and wiped it down the front of her dress. She stood there a while with her hands back at the sides of her dress.

---

About four people went past on the paving, in twos and singly, and stepped around the bottom step without being asked to. She moved a pace for the first of them and a second pace for the second, and the second pace took her out onto the flags clear of the step where anybody coming up could see her standing there in the open with the door behind her and nothing in her hands.

The man of about thirty-eight came up the paving with a barrow and stopped short of her and set his hands down on the handles. She looked at him and he looked at her, and neither of them said anything at all, and after a moment he lifted his barrow again and went on past her and up the row.

She stood another while. Then she went up that stair behind him and into the room and took her place at the side of the bench, and nobody in that room said one word to her about the hour she had spent at the bottom of it.

---

Her own hand was washed in the trough at the top of the market step at about the sixth hour, and the chalk came off it, and the lines of her palm stayed white in the creases of it for the rest of that morning, and she put that hand in her apron pocket and did not take it out again.
