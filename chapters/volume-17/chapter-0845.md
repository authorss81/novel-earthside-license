# Chapter 0845 — She Asked Him And He Answered

The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress set them on the wood of the bench to hold herself still, and the wood was warm from the sun through the one window. She kept them there a moment, then folded them again.

Saturday lay over the market with a pale sky, and the flags below were drying. The room above it stood full, with people along the wall behind the bench and the shelf above them, and the light on the middle of the bench wood. It was the five hundred and thirtieth day of the Bare Month, with the date in chalk outside the door at the foot of the stair. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end of the bench. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his board against his knee. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat at the far end. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stood at the far end with his satchel under his arm and the flap down.

She looked at him across the bench and asked him the one thing she had to ask.

**"Tell me the morning you first saw the iron off the door."**

He shifted the strap on his shoulder and looked at the floor a while before he spoke, and his voice stayed level all the way through what he gave them. When it was done nobody asked him for any of it again.

---

The keeper kept her hand flat on the page all through. The man of about thirty-four lifted his eyes from his board and then dropped them again. The man of about thirty-nine turned his board a little on his knee and said nothing.

**"The paving was wet under the awning that morning."**

**"It was,"** the keeper said, and squared the page.

**"The load went in late."**

He nodded once and said nothing more, and the room went on with the page and the boards and the light moving off the bench wood. The woman of about fifty-two took her hands off the bench wood and let them fall again. The man of about thirty-four stood his board against the bench leg and left it there.

---

By the tenth hour a stool at the side of the bench that had stood facing the wall was found turned a little toward the room, with the dust gone off one foot of it where a boot had touched it going past.
