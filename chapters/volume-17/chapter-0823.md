# Chapter 0823 — The Far End Of The Bench

The man of about thirty-one put his left hand on the far end of that bench and his right hand on the strap of his satchel and turned the bench end for end, so that the worn place came to the near end and the far end of it stood against the wall with nothing on it at all.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and eighth day of the Bare Month, a Friday. The light came in at the one window and lay on the middle of that bench and had not got to either end of it. The keeper had the register on the shelf behind her and both her hands flat on the wood on either side of it. The man of about thirty-four was where he always was at that end with his own board face in against the bench and the bare back of it turned out. The man of about thirty-nine was at the far end with his board under his arm and the space at the foot of the middle column of it showing white.

He took his hands off that bench and stood back from it and looked at it and went and stood at the far end of it again, which put him into the part of that room the light had not got to.

---

Nobody put their hand on that bench at the near end for the rest of that morning, and nobody said one word to the man of about thirty-one about turning it end for end, and he went on standing at the far end of it with his satchel under his arm.

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-four said a thing at the near end about a trestle leg at the far end of that market, and the man of about thirty-one said another thing at the far end of that bench about the same leg, and the two of them held them both without either of them giving the other one.

**"That leg is the store's to wedge."**

**"It is not the store's to leave either."**

About four seconds went by and the keeper turned a leaf and neither of them said one word more about it, and the talk came back on a rate that wanted a day against it and could not get one.

A thing said at the near end of that bench at about the seventh hour did not carry to the far end of it, and the man of about thirty-nine said it again himself at the far end of that bench in his own words before anybody asked him to, and what he said the second time was not what he had said the first time, and nobody at the near end told him he had it wrong.

---

The worn place in that bench was at the near end of it that morning where the light was on the wood, and it was darker than the wood round it and darker than it had been anywhere on that bench before.
