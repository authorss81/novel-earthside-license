# Chapter 0807 — Anybody Who Wants To Be In It

The man of about thirty-nine turned his board over at the far end of the bench and put his right hand flat on the near edge of the face of it and held it there while he looked down the length of that room at nothing in particular.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-second day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light was on the bench wood at the near end and had not got to the far end. The bar lay on the top step of that stair and the door at the bottom of it stood a hand's width off its frame.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of that bench.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-nine came down the bench to the near end, stopped beside the keeper's page, and said a thing to the middle of that floor in the ordinary course of a morning, and nobody had asked him for it.

**"Anybody who wants to be in the room when that bar is settled should be in the room."**

He went back to the far end and turned his board over again and stood the face of it against the far leg with his hands in his coat.

**"I am not going to say it twice."**

Nobody answered him. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow up off the board and set it down again. About four seconds went by and the talk started again about a trestle at the far end of the market row, and it did not come back to that bench.

Nobody had been sent for, and there had not been time given to anybody to find out what they were being asked about, and that room was not going to be given any.

---

Nothing came of that sentence before the light went, and the blank against the rate on that board was the same blank it had been on the first morning of that flood.
