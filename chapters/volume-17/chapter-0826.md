# Chapter 0826 — It Went On As It Had The Morning Before

The keeper put the back of her right hand flat on the sill of that one window and drew it along the wood toward her, and the wet came off the sill onto her hand, and she put that hand down on the cloth on the table and left it there for the length of a breath.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and eleventh day of the Bare Month, a Monday. The light came in at that window and lay across the bench wood and stopped short of the near end. The register stood on the shelf behind the bench where it stood every morning of that flood, and the second slate stood behind the register on that same shelf, and the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder carried the first one-place form and had carried nothing else at any hour of that flood.

That room went on exactly as it had gone on the morning before, and it went on that way for the whole of the day, and nothing in it was different at any hour of it. The man of about thirty-nine came up a little after her with his board under his arm and stood it against the leg of the bench at the near end with the face of it turned in. At the far end the man of about thirty-four turned his hollow over and set it down on his knee with his hands in his coat. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress. The man of about thirty-one went up and down that stair on his own business with the flap of his satchel down.

---

Nobody said one word to anybody about what had been said in that room the morning before, and nobody asked the man of about thirty-nine whether he was sure of it, and he was asked nothing else either. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four had a rate on the face of his own board that wanted a day put against it, and he looked at the blank where the day would go, and his hand stayed in his coat where it had been all morning.

At about the eighth hour the woman of about fifty-two said that the light had come round further than it had the day before, and the keeper said it had not come round any further at all, and neither of them said one word about anything else and the first of them did not say it again.

The talk went on about the weather and a trestle at the far end of that market and nothing else that anybody in that room thought worth saying out loud, and nothing whatever was done about the thing that had been said in that room on the Sunday.

---

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one went down that stair and out through the door at the foot of it, and the date in chalk on the outside of that door was wet enough to take the print of a thumb where somebody had gone over the last of it, and it had been done that morning by a hand nobody in that room had seen.
