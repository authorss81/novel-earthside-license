# Chapter 0839 — A Stool Out Again And Rocked Once

The man of about thirty-four laid his right hand flat along the top of that bench and looked down the length of it at the stool standing at that side with the seat of it turned into the room and the dust of the flags standing round its legs.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twenty-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. The corner behind the near leg of that bench was empty, and the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder came down over it and the table with the cloth on it stood against the wall in front of it, and that stool was not in the corner and had not been in the corner when that room had filled up. The light came in at the one window and lay along the middle of that bench, and neither end of it was in that light.

Nobody in that room said one word about that stool being where it was.

---

At the near end the keeper put both hands flat on the wood of that bench on either side of her page and said that there was a stool at that side again.

**"There is a stool at that side again."**

**"There was one at that side before."**

**"Before it went into the corner."**

**"It is not in the corner,"** the man of about thirty-four said, and took his hand off the top of that bench.

Nobody asked who had put it there and nobody asked the woman of about fifty-two anything about it, and she stood there at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress and did not say one word about where it had been or where it was. The man of about thirty-nine did not remark on it. That room had a place to sit at the side of that bench and nobody sat on it that morning, and at about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-one stood up at the far end of that bench with his satchel under his arm and shifted his weight and put the toe of his boot against the lower rail of that stool to turn it out of the way of that bench leg.

The stool rocked once across the flags and came down again with the seat of it facing the wall instead of the room.

Nobody said anything about that either. Nobody put the stool back and nobody set it right and nobody asked the man of about thirty-one why he had turned it, and at about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-four came past that stool on his way to the window and went round it where it stood.

---

The seat of that stool had gone pale along its front edge where the light came along the bench wood and had not gone pale at the back of it at all, and it was standing a foot further out from that bench than the woman of about fifty-two had set it down at the fourth hour.
