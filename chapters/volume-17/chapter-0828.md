# Chapter 0828 — Nobody In That Room Asked Him

The man of about thirty-nine came up that stair with his board under his arm and stood it against the leg of the bench at the near end and turned his right hand over and looked at the palm of it and wiped it on his coat and put it back on the face of the board.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and thirteenth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light was on the near end of that bench and got a little further along the wood than it had the morning before, and the keeper was at the near end with the register on the shelf behind her, and the woman of about fifty-two was at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress, and the man of about thirty-four was at the far end with the chalk in his inside breast pocket and both his hands on his hollow.

---

He was in that room the whole of that morning and nobody in it asked him whether he agreed with what he had said in it on the Sunday. At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-one came down the bench from the far end of it with his satchel under his arm and began a sentence with his hand on the strap of it, and turned it into a different sentence before it had finished.

**"Then when somebody comes up that stair on a morning—"**

**"That sill wants the water off it,"** the man of about thirty-four said.

**"Or the page goes."**

**"The sill,"** the man of about thirty-one said, and went back to the far end of that bench and stood there, and nobody at the near end of that room said one word to him about it, and the man of about thirty-nine did not turn round and never knew that sentence had been started at all.

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-nine said one thing into the middle of that floor.

**"The window lets the weather in at the bottom and there is nobody in this room that will ever say so."**

Nobody answered him and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him for the Sunday either.

He turned that board over twice in the course of that morning and set it down with its face the same way round both times, and nobody in that room said one word to him about either time.

---

The rain had been over about an hour when the water came back into that sill at about the ninth hour, and it stood in the wood there till the tenth, and he had been the only one in that room who had said anything about where the weather came in.
