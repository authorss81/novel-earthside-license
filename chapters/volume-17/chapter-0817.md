# Chapter 0817 — A Stack Carried In Dry

The man of about thirty-eight put both hands under the near end of a length of board and lifted it off the pile by the store door, and carried it across the yard with the rain running off his sleeves.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and second day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The wind came in off the eleven miles of flats and drove the rain sideways across the yard. The stack by the store door stood in the weather with the water standing on the top board of it.

The man of about thirty-one came out from under the awning and took the far end of the length, and the two of them carried it in together and laid it down under the awning where the boards already in were dry.

---

Board by board they worked through the morning. The man of about thirty-eight steadied his barrow against the wall with one hand and carried with the other, and the pile by the door went down and the rows under the awning went up, and nobody in that yard said one word about stopping.

**"The next one wants turning end for end,"** the man of about thirty-one said, with his hands on the far end of it.

**"Leave it as it lies and it will go in,"** the man of about thirty-eight said, and it went in.

By the ninth hour the pile by the door was gone and the stack stood under the awning out of the weather, and both men stood under it with the water dripping off their coats onto dry wood.

---

The road out to the flats shone all the way to the bend, and nobody went down it again that day.
