# Chapter 0840 — He Said What It Had Cost Him

The man of about thirty-one came up that stair with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down, and put the flat of his left hand on the wood of that bench at the far end of it and left it there, and the strap of that satchel stayed over his shoulder.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twenty-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. On the outside of the door at the foot of that stair a date stood in chalk, and under that date a piece of board about as wide as a hand had nothing on it whatever. The man of about thirty-nine stood at the near end of that bench with his board propped against that bench leg. The man of about twenty-seven was by the table with his bucket at his feet and the rag over his shoulder. The keeper was at the near end of that bench with the register on the shelf behind her. At the side of that bench stood the woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress. At the far end of it the man of about thirty-four sat with his own board propped against his knee, and nobody had asked him to bring it.

He went down that stair once in the middle of that morning and came up again with the same thing under his arm and stood at the far end of that bench with his hand on the strap of it, and nobody in that room asked him what he had gone down for.

The keeper drew the page flat under her hand, turned it back, and ran one finger down the fold to square it. The man of about twenty-seven took his bucket up off the flags, carried it out to the step, set it down outside and came back and stood against the wall. The man of about thirty-nine turned his own board round on the leg of that bench and set the bare back of it out into that room again, and nobody at either end of that bench said anything about any of that.

---

At about the seventh hour he took his hand off the strap of that satchel and said it out loud into the middle of that floor, and nobody in that room had asked him for it.

**"I have carried a thing up that stair every morning of this flood and I have gone past the foot of it every morning of this flood, and I could not tell anybody on which morning that door began to stand open."**

Nobody thanked him for saying it. Nobody improved on one word of it. Nobody asked him a second thing about it, and nobody asked him what he thought ought to be done about it, and nobody in that room looked at the door at the foot of that stair while he was saying it.

Nobody bettered one word of it at any hour after that either. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-nine said one flat thing into the middle of that floor about the trough at the top of that market step having a crack in the near end of it, and nobody answered him about that, and nobody took either of those two things any further than the middle of that floor.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one put his hand back on the strap of that satchel and went down that stair and out into that market, and did not come up it again at any hour of that day.

---

At the tenth hour his hand was off the strap of that satchel for the first time in that day, and the skin of the palm of it was wet to the first joint out of the trough at the top of that market step, and the flap of that satchel was still down.
