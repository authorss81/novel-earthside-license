# Chapter 0850 — The Dust Beside It Showed No Break

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stopped at the foot of the stair with his satchel under his arm and the flap down, and laid his free hand on the newel to steady himself before going up. The paving was dry, and the light lay thin along the wall of the stairwell.

Thursday had set fair from the fourth hour. The room above the market stood empty at first, with the bench bare and the one window shut, and then the bench took its people again. The five hundred and thirty-fifth day of the Bare Month was in chalk outside the door below, with the bare board under it empty. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register opened the page and squared it. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board set his board against the bench leg. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress took her place at the side of the bench.

---

The morning went on with talk of a trough mended at the top of the market step and of a load that had waited out the rain and could go now.

**"The trough holds since yesterday."**

**"It will want watching."**

**"It will,"** she said, and turned a leaf.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one went down the stair on his own business and did not come back up for a long while. The stairwell below lay in half light, with the bottom step dry and the dust lying thick along the upper steps, and the room went on above it with the page and the boards and the light going off the bench wood.

He came back at last with his coat buttoned to the throat and the water standing in the shoulders of it and the satchel under his arm with the flap down where it had been all the morning. The man of about thirty-four looked at the wet on that coat and then at the face of his own board. Nobody asked him where he had been and he did not say, and he undid the coat at the top step where the iron lay along the stone.

---

He put his hand on the iron where it lay, lifted it a hand's breadth off the stone, and set it down again in the same place. His palm left no print on it, and the dust on either side of it showed no break to tell whether the iron had lain there untouched or had been lifted and set down the same way many times before. He took his hand off it, picked up the strap of his satchel where it had slipped, and went up into the room with the dust of the step white along the edge of his palm.
