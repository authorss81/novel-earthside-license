# Chapter 0846 — His Hand On The Iron In The Rain

Adrian Vale stopped at the head of the stair with rain on his coat and put his right hand on the iron where it lay along the top step, to lift it clear of the wet and from under feet going up and down. Water stood in beads along the top of it, and grit stuck along the underside where it met the stone.

Sunday had come in with rain driving over the store's end and the stairwell running. The five hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month was on the door below in chalk, and the stair ran wet down past him to the bottom step. He tried the weight of the iron with his wrist, got it a little way off the stone, and found he had no purchase standing as he was with the wall at his back and the drop below.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf came up behind him with his barrow shafts in his hands, saw him crouched over the step, and stopped on the paving below.

**"You will want your feet under you for the like of it."**

**"I have it,"** Adrian said, without looking round.

**"Then I will hold back a while."**

The barrow could not come past a man kneeling on the top step with both hands on the iron, so the man stood back on the paving below with the rain running off the load and waited. Adrian shifted his grip, tried again to slide the iron toward the wall where feet would miss it, and the wet took it off him. It went down again on to the stone with a dull sound, in the same place and at the same angle, with the beads of water broken and running. He wiped his palm on his coat and tried the edge once more with his fingers, and left it where it was.

He got his hands clear, stood up out of the way, and nodded the man on. The man brought the barrow up past him with one wheel close to the wall, and the near edge of the load brushed Adrian's sleeve and left a dark print on it. Nobody in the stair thanked him for what he had done with the iron, and nobody told him he had been right to touch it, and nobody there had been waiting for him to be of use.

---

He went down the stair empty-handed into the rain, with the water running off his coat on to the paving. The man with the barrow went on up the market with his load and did not look back. The prints of the barrow wheels ran on past the foot of the stair toward the store's end, filling slowly with rain behind him.
