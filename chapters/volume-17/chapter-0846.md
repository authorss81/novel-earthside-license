# Chapter 0846 — The Iron Would Not Move For Him

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf came up the stair behind his barrow until the near wheel came against the iron, and put his right hand flat along the top of it to keep the load from going forward over the step.

Sunday had come in with the rain driving over the store's end and the stairwell running. The five hundred and thirty-first day of the Bare Month was on the door below in chalk, and the stair ran wet down past him to the bottom step. The bar lay along the top step where it had lain since the flood, with the water standing in beads along the top of it and the grit of the step stuck along the underside where the iron met the stone.

He got the end of his barrow shaft under the near end of it and put his weight on the shaft, and the iron came up as far as the width of two fingers and stopped there, and it stayed up only while his weight was on it. He moved his hands to the end that stood nearest the wall and pulled at it, and that end would not move at all. The water came off the top of the iron and ran down over his knuckles into his sleeve, and he took his hands off it and stood with them hanging while the barrow leaned against him in the wet.

The door at the head of that stair stood open on its latch, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at it with one hand on the edge of it and the register under her other arm, keeping the rain off the corner of the page.

**"You will not get a wheel past that,"** she said.

**"No,"** he said.

**"Then somebody wants two hands on it,"** she said.

**"It would want a hand at each end of it,"** he said.

---

He put the shaft back under the wheel and turned the barrow side on to the stair and went up with the near wheel in the wet gutter along the wall and the load going over close above the iron, and it passed without touching it. The keeper stood at the head of that stair until the far wheel was past the step and then went back to her page, and nobody in that stairwell asked him one word about the iron he had had his hands on, or about the boards, or about where he meant to put them.

The barrow went on up the market with the rain running off the ends of the boards, and the man went with it without looking back at the stair. The two wheel prints ran on past the foot of the stair toward the store's end and filled slowly with the rain behind him.
