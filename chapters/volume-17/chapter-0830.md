# Chapter 0830 — Sixteen Posts And Eleven Withies

The man of about thirty-four put the flat of his right hand on the bare back of his own board where it stood face in against his knee and turned the whole thing a quarter round on his knee, and out past the last named house of that city the road went down away from the top of it with nobody on any part of it.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and fifteenth day of the Bare Month, a Friday. He had come out past that last named house in the ordinary course with his board under his arm, and nobody had asked him where he was going, and the wind at the top of that road came up the rise and took the hat off him and put it back on him. The fence stood along the top of that shut road, and it was sixteen willow posts and eleven withies bound on with the same cord, and it had not been measured and no post on it had ever been moved.

A handcart stood in the yard by the road with a hazel mallet leaning against the wall of it and a coil of rope beside the cart that had been used, and nobody was at the door of that house.

---

A man came out of that house with his sleeves turned up and stood at the top of that road with his hand on the top rail of his own gate.

**"That wood is mine to cut if anybody wants it cut. Nobody goes down that road."**

**"It is not mine to give away,"** the man of about thirty-four said.

**"I am not asking you to give it away. I am asking whether anybody wants it."**

Nobody wanted it. The man of about thirty-four said nothing at all to that and the man at the gate stood with his hand on his own gate and looked down that road, and the two of them stood at the top of it a while longer with the wind off the shut road going past them and neither of them saying one word about the other having said anything.

Nobody joined them and nobody settled it and neither of them thanked the other for anything. The man of about thirty-four turned his board round in his hands and put the face of it in against his own side and went back up that market with the bare back of it turned out into the wind, and the man at the gate went in and shut his door.

---

By the tenth hour the sun had come over the far side of that land and lay along the top of that shut road from one end of it to the other, and the sixteen posts stood in it with their cord on them, and there was nobody on the road at all.
