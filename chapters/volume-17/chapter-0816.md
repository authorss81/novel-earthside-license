# Chapter 0816 — Where The Bar Is Lying

The man of about thirty-four put both hands on the bare back of his own board where it stood face in against his trestle, and squared it against the leg with his shoulder.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and first day of the Bare Month, a Friday. The row was filling on both sides of that walk and the flags were damp underfoot. The bar lay on the top step of a stair at the back of that market and no one in that row had seen it that morning.

A man came down the row with an empty barrow and stopped at the trestle, and looked up toward the back of the market where the stair went up behind the store's end of it.

**"Where is it lying these days?"**

The man of about thirty-four did not take his hands off the board.

**"On the top step."**

The man with the barrow nodded and went on up the paving with it. The man of about thirty-four turned his board a hand's width and stood it flat, and put his hands back in his coat.

Another barrow came down the row with a load of boards and had to wait while a trestle was squared, and the man behind it stood with his hands on the handles till the way was clear. The row filled round them and the talk went on over their heads about the weather and the morning's work.

---

At the far end of the row two men talked it over between them, whether the door below wanted shutting or wanted leaving, and neither of them went up to look, and by the tenth hour the row had gone on to other things.

---

The awning dripped at one corner all afternoon and nothing was set under it.
