# Chapter 0836 — He Looked At That Door And Went On

The man of about thirty-one came along that market row with a coil of rope under his arm and put the toe of his boot on the bottom step of that stair and stopped there, and the rope came down off his hip and hung against the flags.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twenty-first day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. It had been raining in the night and the flags were wet and the strip of ground at the foot of that stair was dark along its whole width and had a channel cut down the middle of it where the water had gone. The date in chalk stood on the outside of that door and the bare piece of board under the date had nothing on it and the weather had not put anything on it either, and the light came in past the door and lay along the steps of that stair.

He looked at the outside of that door. He looked at the chalk and the board under it and he looked up the open length of that stair to the top step, where the iron lay across it with the wiping still standing in the dust of the wood, and he stood looking at all three of those things until a woman coming the other way along that row had to step wide of him in the wet.

---

Then he picked the coil up off the flags and went on up that market row and did not stop and did not come up that stair.

A man at the store's end of that row put his hand on the man of about thirty-one's arm as he went past and asked him whether he would take a crate up to that store's end before the middle of the day.

**"Not this morning."**

**"It is not the middle of the day that is asking."**

**"It can ask me again,"** the man of about thirty-one said, and went on up the row with the coil of rope under his arm, and the man who had asked him stood at the trestle and watched him go and said nothing else about it.

He has gone past the foot of that stair every morning of this flood and he was asked nothing about it then and he was asked nothing about it on that Thursday. Nobody in that market row knew that he had looked, and nobody on that strip of ground knew it either, and he did not tell anybody and he did not go back and look a second time.

The rain came on again a little after the seventh hour and went along that row and into the channel at the foot of that stair and down the flags at the store's end, and the marks of that morning's boots went soft at their edges and most of them went away by degrees.

---

The light lay along the wet flags of that market row from the foot of that stair to the far end of it, and there was one line of prints going up the middle of those flags that did not come back that day.
