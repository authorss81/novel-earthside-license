# Chapter 0838 — The Room Carried On And Took No Notice

The keeper set the register down square on the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder with the flat of her hand on the spine of it and pushed it back to the wall behind that bench, and the second slate behind it did not move, and she took her hand off the book.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. That morning in that room was the morning before it over again. The bench, the shelf, the register, the one window with its sill, the ordinary work that went on in that room: all of it was where it had stood the day before, and nothing in that room was different at any hour of that day.

The man of about thirty-nine came up that stair and stood his board against the near leg of that bench with the face of it out and put the flat of his right hand on the face of it. At the far end of that bench the man of about thirty-four had his own board propped against his knee. The woman of about fifty-two was still at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress. Twice that morning the man of about thirty-one came up and went down again on his own business with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down.

---

Nobody in that room said one word about the wall along the far side of it at any hour of that day. Nobody asked anybody whether they thought it right and nobody asked anybody whether they thought it wrong, and nobody at the near end of that bench said anything about it to anybody at the far end of it.

There was a rate on the face of the man of about thirty-four's own board that wanted a day against it, and he looked at the blank where that day would go, and his hand stayed in his coat all morning and never went into his inside breast pocket. The keeper turned a leaf. The talk went on about the weather and about the trough at the top of that market step and about a trestle at the far end of that market row.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the face of that board, turned it over once, took it up off the floor where it stood against that bench leg, and went down that stair, out under that door and away along that market row, and did not come back up at any hour of that day. Nobody said good day to him and nobody asked him why he was going out at that hour and nobody said that he would be back.

---

That board was gone off the leg of that bench, and the place on the flags where its foot had stood that morning held the print of it in the dust, and the dust was standing up in the shape of the leg where nothing had been standing there the day before.
