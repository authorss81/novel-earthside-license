# Chapter 0838 — The Room Carried On And Took No Notice

The keeper set the register down square on the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder with the flat of her hand on the spine of it and pushed it back to the wall behind the bench, and the second slate behind it did not move, and she took her hand off the book.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twenty-third day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The morning in that room was the morning before over again. The bench, the shelf, the register, the one window with its sill, the ordinary work that went on there: all of it stood where it had stood the day before, and nothing was different at any hour of the day.

The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up the stair and stood his board against the near leg of the bench with the face of it out and put the flat of his right hand on the face. At the far end the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along had his own board propped against his knee. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress was still at the side of the bench. Twice that morning the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living came up and went down again on his own business with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down.

Nobody in that room said one word about the wall along the far side of it at any hour of that day. Nobody asked anybody whether they thought it right, and nobody asked anybody whether they thought it wrong, and nobody at the near end of the bench said anything about it to anybody at the far end of it.

---

There was a rate on the face of the man of about thirty-four's own board that wanted a day against it, and he looked at the blank where the day would go, and his hand stayed in his coat all morning and never went into his inside breast pocket.

**"It wants a day against it."**

**"It can want,"** the keeper said, and turned a leaf.

**"It has wanted since Friday."**

**"Then it will want on Monday."**

He took that the way a man takes a thing that has been said at him and not argued with, and he turned his own board over on his knee and turned it back. The man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the face of his board and looked along the bench at him.

**"The face of that one is wet through at the near edge."**

**"It is wet at the near edge on a morning like this,"** the man of about thirty-four said, **"and it is dry at the foot of it, and I am not going to set a day against a rate in a hand that will not take it."**

**"Then do not set it."**

**"I have not set it."**

The keeper turned a leaf. The talk went on about the weather and about the trough at the top of that market step and about a trestle at the far end of the row, and the rate went on wanting a day and the hand stayed in the coat.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the face of his board, turned it over once, took it up off the floor where it stood against the leg of the bench, and went down the stair, out under the door and away along that market row, and did not come back up at any hour of that day. Nobody said good day to him and nobody asked him why he was going out at that hour and nobody said that he would be back.

---

That board was gone off the leg of the bench, and the place on the flags where its foot had stood that morning held the print of it in the dust, and the dust was standing up in the shape of the leg where nothing had been standing there the day before.
