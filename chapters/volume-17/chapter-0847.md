# Chapter 0847 — Rain All The Way Down The Stairwell

The man of about twenty-seven curled both hands round the handle of the bucket to keep it from spilling on the stair, and went up sideways with the water lapping at the rim. His sleeve was wet to the elbow before he reached the top.

Monday stood grey over the market from the fourth hour on. Rain came straight down into the stairwell past the door at the foot of it, ran along the wall, and shone on the bottom step where the stone dipped. Above it the iron lay along the top step where it always lay, dark with wet. The five hundred and thirty-second day of the Bare Month was in chalk outside. In the room above, the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register had the register shut on the shelf, the man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board had his board under the dripping of the one window, and the woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress stood back from the bench to keep her skirt clear.

He set the bucket by the table leg with a hand's depth slopped over the side, wiped his face with his sleeve, and stood dripping.

**"The bottom step is under water."**

**"Leave the door on the latch for the air,"** she said.

**"It will only let more rain in,"** he said, and shut it to a hand's width.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along pushed his board further under the bench to keep it dry, and the man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living moved his satchel from under his arm to across his chest where the flap would stay shut. The keeper wrung out the rag over the bucket and laid it along the sill where the drip was coming in. Talk went on a while about a trough running over at the top of the market step and about a load covered with sacking that had held and one that had not. Nobody went down again till the middle of the day, and nobody touched the iron above.

At about the ninth hour the rain eased for a breath and then came on harder, and water found a new line down the stairwell wall and ran where it had not run all morning.
