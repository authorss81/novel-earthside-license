# Chapter 0832 — A Hollow That Had Dried Hard

The man of about thirty-eight put the near wheel of his barrow into the hollow at the side of that yard and stopped, and the wheel did not sink, and the flags under it rang like a board.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and seventeenth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. The wind had dropped in the night and the hollow at the side of that yard had been dry for several mornings, and the grit of the stone had come up out of the bottom of it and made a skin across it like ground, with the edges of that skin gone down into the flags on either side and the middle of it sound. The low place in the stone at the side of that yard was dry as well, and the awning along that store had been dripping off the row of boards under it all night.

He wanted the loaded barrow down that yard and in under that awning, and the dry crown on the high side of the run of flags was the wrong line for a load, because the ground beyond the far side of that yard was soft.

---

He took the load down the hollow.

The skin went down under the near wheel first and it went with a noise like a board breaking and not like stone, and the barrow dropped into that hollow with its load on it and went over on its side with the boards off it and the boards into the hollow from four sides at once. The man of about thirty-eight got both his hands clear, stood up out of the water that came at him round his boots, and stood looking down at the barrow for the length of a breath before he said anything.

**"That is not ground."**

Nobody in that yard said anything back to him. He got hold of the barrow under the axle and got it up off its side and stood it on its legs again, and he carried the boards up out of that hollow one at a time and stacked them along the dry crown with their ends squared, and the water stood in that hollow where it had stood before with the grit of the stone coming into it from four sides in a skin.

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-one went across that market row with a crate on his shoulder and past the foot of the stair at the back of the store's end of it and did not stop there.

Up at the near end of that bench the keeper turned a leaf and then said that the man of about thirty-one had not come up.

**"He has not come up."**

**"He goes past that door every morning and has never once come up it."**

**"Then that is not the same thing,"** the keeper said, and turned a leaf.

Nobody put a morning to it and nobody went down that stair to look at anything and nobody said out loud that there was no way of settling it.

---

By the tenth hour the piece of the broken skin was lying up on the dry crown of that run of flags where the wheel had come out of it, and nobody had picked it up.
