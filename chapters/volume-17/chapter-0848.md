# Chapter 0848 — She Spoke To Him At The Bench End

The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress came and stood at the near end of the bench while the light from the one window lay across the middle of it, and she kept her hands where they were. The bench was full from end to end, and the shelf above was dusty in the sun.

It was Tuesday, the five hundred and thirty-third day of the Bare Month, with the date in chalk outside the door at the foot of the stair and daylight coming up the stairwell with it. The room stood full, with people standing behind the bench. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a public register was at the near end with her page open. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along sat at the near end of the bench with his board across his knees. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board stood behind him with his own board under his arm.

She bent a little toward him, so her words went to him and not to the room, and asked him the thing she had carried up the stair with her. He looked up at her, looked down again at the face of his board, and said nothing at all. Nobody in the room thanked him for saying nothing, and nobody took the asking up for him.

---

The keeper drew her page flat and squared the edge of it against the fold. The man of about thirty-nine shifted his weight and looked out of the window.

**"The sun is on the bench at last."**

**"It will not stay long,"** he said, still looking at his board.

**"It is enough to warm the wood."**

Talk went on after a while about a load to be shifted before the middle of the day and about sacking that had held through the rain and sacking that had not, and nobody in the room came back to what had passed at the near end of the bench. The man of about thirty-four kept his hand out of his coat all through, and his board stayed across his knees with the face of it to the light. The keeper turned a leaf and found the damp going out of the edge of the page.

By the tenth hour the light had gone off the bench wood and up the wall above the shelf, and the near end where the two of them had sat stood in shade with the dust showing plain across it.
