# Chapter 0833 — The Door Stood Shut And Nobody Said

The man of about twenty-seven put the flat of his right hand on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair and pushed, and the door did not give and did not move a finger's width.

It was before the light was on the flags, on the five hundred and eighteenth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. That door had stood a hand's width off its frame for as long as anybody in that room had been going up and down it, and the iron lay across the top step of that stair above, and the light came up the open length of the steps and stopped short of the bottom one and stopped at the door.

He set his bucket down on the flags at the foot of that stair, got his shoulder against the outside of the door and put his weight on it, and got it back off its jamb a hand's width, and he went through it and up that stair with the bucket in his hand and the rag over his shoulder, and stood at the near end of that bench with the bucket at his feet.

---

Nobody in that room said anything about that door being shut. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four stood at the far end of that bench with his own board against his knee and his hands in his coat. The man of about thirty-nine came up and stood his board against the near leg of that bench and put the flat of his hand on the face of it. The woman of about fifty-two stood for a while at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress, and nobody asked her where she had been.

That morning had one thing out of it that was not there the day before, and one person noticed. The man of about thirty-one was not in that room at any hour of that day, and the keeper said so at about the sixth hour, and the man of about thirty-nine said that the man of about thirty-one went past that door and did not come up it, and that nobody had ever asked him about that one way or the other.

Nobody asked the keeper a second thing about it. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one anything when he went past that market row later with a thing under his arm, and nobody put a morning to his not coming up.

By the ninth hour that door stood a hand's width off its frame, which is the way it stood on any ordinary morning, and nobody in that room remarked on that either. The man of about twenty-seven carried his bucket down to the trough at the top of that market step, filled it, and carried it back and set it down at the near end of that bench, and nobody in that room asked him why he had filled it, and he was asked nothing else at any hour of that day.

---

That bucket stood on the flags at the foot of that stair at the tenth hour with a hand's depth of water in it that had not been in it at the fourth hour, and the water was flat and had the grey of the sky lying in it.
