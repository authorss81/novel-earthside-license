# Chapter 0833 — The Door Stood Shut And Nobody Said

The man of about twenty-seven put the flat of his right hand on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair and pushed, and the door did not give and did not move a finger's width.

It was before the light was on the flags, on the five hundred and eighteenth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. That door had stood a hand's width off its frame for as long as anybody in that room had been going up and down it. The iron lay across the top step above, and the light came up the open length of the steps and stopped short of the bottom one and stopped at the door.

He stood with his palm flat on it a while, the way a man stands at a gate that is latched from the far side, and then he took his hand off and looked at the hand as though the hand had been asked a question. He set his bucket down on the flags, got his shoulder against the door and put his weight on it, and got it back off its jamb a hand's width. Then he went through it and up the stair with the bucket in his hand and the rag over his shoulder, and stood at the near end of the bench with the bucket at his feet, breathing.

---

Nobody in that room said anything about the door being shut. The keeper turned a leaf. At the far end of the bench the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along stood with his own board propped against his knee and his hands in his coat. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up, stood his board against the near leg of the bench and put the flat of his own hand on the face of it. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress stood for a while at the side of the bench, and nobody asked her where she had been.

He unhooked the rag from his shoulder, folded it once and put it on the bench beside him, which is not where it goes, and the keeper looked at the rag and then at the page.

**"Your trough is running over at the top of the step."**

**"It runs over when it is full,"** he said. **"The low place at the side of the yard is dry. It has not held anything since the wind dropped."**

The keeper turned the leaf back and squared it against the fold and said nothing about the low place at the side of the yard. He took the rag off the bench and put it over his shoulder again and stood on with the bucket at his feet. At about the fifth hour the man of about thirty-four took one hand out of his coat, turned his own board round on his knee and put it back the other way up.

**"This face will not take anything in this damp."**

**"It is not yours to turn on that bench,"** the man of about thirty-nine said, and left his own hand where it was on the face of his board.

**"It is my board."**

Nothing came of that either. The man of about thirty-four put his hand back into his coat, and the man of about thirty-nine took his off the face of his board and turned it a quarter round on the leg of the bench and put it back with the face of it out. Neither of them said one word more about it, and nobody thanked either of them for saying anything at all.

---

That morning had one thing out of it that was not there the day before, and one person noticed. The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living was not in that room at any hour of that day. The keeper said so at about the sixth hour, looking at the far end of the bench and then at the door below, and the man of about thirty-nine said that he went past that door and did not come up it, and that nobody had ever asked him about that one way or the other.

Nobody asked the keeper a second thing about it. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one anything when he went past that market row later with a thing under his arm, and nobody put a morning to his not coming up.

By the ninth hour the door at the foot of the stair stood a hand's width off its frame again, which is the way it stands on any ordinary morning, and nobody in that room remarked on that either. The man of about twenty-seven carried his bucket down to the trough at the top of that market step, filled it, and carried it back up and set it down at the near end of the bench. The man of about thirty-four moved his own board off the bench to make the room for it, and did not say that he had done so, and the man of about twenty-seven was asked nothing at any hour of that day.

---

That bucket stood on the flags at the foot of the stair at the tenth hour with a hand's depth of water in it that had not been in it at the fourth hour, and the water was flat, and the grey of the sky was lying in it.
