# Chapter 0815 — Five Strokes And No Sixth

The keeper laid her right hand flat on the open page on the bench and held it there, so the page would not turn in the draught from the stair.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundredth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The light came in at the one window and lay full across the bench wood. The bar lay on the top step of that stair and the door at the bottom of it stood a hand's width off its frame.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-nine came up with his board under his arm and stood at the near end. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of the bench.

---

At about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-nine came down the bench and stood beside the open page, and looked at the column that held the strokes. There were five strokes down the page with a day set against each of them, in the order they had been made in, and nothing entered after them and nothing taken out.

He looked at the empty place below the last of them a while. The keeper took her hand off the page and turned to the near end. The woman of about fifty-two looked at the page from the side of the bench and said nothing at all.

Nobody said it out loud. Nobody took up a pen and nobody reached for the shelf behind the bench, and the talk came back on a rate that wanted settling before the rain.

---

There was chalk dust on the keeper's cuff at the tenth hour where there had been none at the fourth.
