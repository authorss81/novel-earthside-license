# Chapter 0835 — A Boot Mark And No Morning For It

The man of about thirty-one who carries things for a living stopped with the toe of his own boot on the top step of that stair and looked down at the dust along the outside edge of the step where it went up to meet the wall, and there was a mark of a boot in it.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twentieth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light came in past the door at the foot of the stair and lay along the steps all the way up, and the iron lay across the top step with the dust of the wiping still in a smear across the wood. The dust on the outside edge of that top step was a different colour from the dust in the middle of it and had been blown about more than once. The mark was not a size of anything. There was nothing on it to tell.

He stood on the step and looked at the mark for about as long as it takes to look at a thing twice, and he went up.

---

He put his satchel down at the far end of the bench and stood there with his hand on the strap, looking at nothing at all for the length of four or five breaths. The keeper at the near end of the bench looked up off her page at him and then went back to it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall two stalls along came in with the wet of the row still on the sleeve of his coat and took his board against his knee at the far end of the bench, and said nothing. The woman of about fifty-two with her hands at the sides of her dress stood at the side of the bench and said nothing either. The man of about thirty-nine who trades on a board came up and stood his own board against the near leg of the bench with his hand flat on the face of it.

At about the seventh hour the keeper said what he had been looking at.

**"What is on that step?"**

**"A mark of a boot, at the wall side of it."**

**"Not a size of what?"**

**"Of anything,"** the man of about thirty-one said. **"It is a mark and there is nothing on it."**

The man of about thirty-nine went down the stair four steps and stood on the one below the top and looked at the dust on the outside edge of it for a while with his board under his arm. Then he came back up and put the dust off his own knee.

**"It is not a size,"** he said. **"There is a wind off that market and this is the step it comes over first, and whatever made that will not be on it at all by the morning."**

**"It is not my business what made it,"** the man of about thirty-four said, **"and I am not going to make it mine."**

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one to go down and put his own boot beside the mark, and nobody else in that room asked him anything about it, and nobody asked anybody whether they had seen anything on that step in any other morning. Nobody went down that stair a second time. Nobody said anything at all out loud about whether it could be put to a morning, and the man of about thirty-nine did not say it again and neither did anybody else. The man of about thirty-one put his hand back on the strap of his satchel and stayed at the far end of the bench, and the flap of it was down.

---

The dust on the outside edge of that top step was lifted a little by the wind at about the ninth hour and settled again a foot further in, and the mark was fainter at one edge than it had been at the seventh hour. The man of about thirty-four came past it on his way to the window and went up the middle of the stair where the dust had not been moved at all. The light came further up the open length of the steps as the morning went on and took the wall side of the top step out of the light altogether, and the smear of grit across the iron on that step went pale and then went out of sight under the light.

By the tenth hour the toe of that mark was gone and the wind had taken the outside of it off the stone, and all that was left on that step was the heel of it, an inch and something across, sitting in the dust with nothing at all in front of it.
