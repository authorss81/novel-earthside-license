# Chapter 0835 — A Boot Mark And No Morning For It

The man of about thirty-one stopped with the toe of his boot on the top step of that stair and looked down at the dust along the outside edge of that step where it went up to meet the wall, and there was a mark of a boot in it.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and twentieth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light came in past the door at the foot of that stair and lay along the steps all the way up, and the iron lay across the top step of it with the dust of the wiping still in a smear across the wood, and the dust on the outside edge of that top step was a different colour from the dust on the middle of it and had been blown about more than once.

He stood on that step and looked at that mark for about as long as it takes to look at a thing twice.

---

He went up. He put his satchel down at the far end of that bench and stood at the far end of it with his hand on the strap and looked at nothing at all for the length of four or five breaths, and the keeper at the near end of that bench looked up off her page at him and then went back to it.

At about the seventh hour she said what he was looking at.

**"What is on that step?"**

**"A mark of a boot, at the wall side of it."**

**"Not a size of what?"**

**"Of anything,"** the man of about thirty-one said. **"It is a mark and there is nothing on it."**

The man of about thirty-nine went down that stair four steps and stood on the step below that one and looked at the dust on the outside edge of the top step for a while with his board under his arm, and then he came back up.

**"It is not a size,"** he said. **"There is a wind off that market and this is the step it comes over first, and whatever made that will not be on it at all by the morning."**

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one to go down and put his own boot beside it, and nobody else in that room asked him anything about it at all, and nobody asked anybody whether they had seen anything on that step in any other morning. Nobody went down that stair a second time. Nobody said anything at all out loud about whether it could be put to a morning, and the man of about thirty-nine did not say it again and neither did anybody else.

The dust on the outside edge of that top step was lifted a little by the wind at about the ninth hour and settled again a foot further in, and the mark was fainter at one edge than it had been at the seventh hour.

---

By the tenth hour the toe of that mark was gone and the wind had taken the outside of it off the stone altogether, and all that was left on that step was the heel of it, an inch and something across, sitting in the dust with nothing at all in front of it.
