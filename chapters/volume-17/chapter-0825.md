# Chapter 0825 — Nobody Agreed With It

The man of about thirty-nine came up that stair with his board under his arm and stood it against the leg of the bench at the near end and put the flat of his right hand on the face of it, and the keeper at that near end moved her own hands off the wood to make room for it.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and tenth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. On the outside of the door at the foot of that stair there was a date in chalk and a bare piece of board under the date about as wide as a hand, and nothing on the board. The light was on the bench wood from the near end to about the middle of the room and had not got to the far end of it.

The bench was full from end to end and the people who had nowhere to sit stood along the wall behind it where the shelf came down. The woman of about fifty-two stood at the side of that bench with her hands at the sides of her dress. The man of about thirty-four was at the far end with his chalk in his inside breast pocket and both his hands in his coat. The man of about twenty-seven was by the door with his bucket at his feet and his rag over his shoulder, and he was asked nothing. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end of that bench with his satchel under his arm and the flap of it down.

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-nine took his hand off the face of that board and stood up straight, and said the cost of it out loud before he did the thing, into the middle of that floor, and nobody had asked him for it.

**"I have put a day against every rate I have ever traded and there is nothing on that board for what I am about to say, and when I have said it there will be no day against it and nobody will ever know that I said it at all."**

Nobody improved on one word of that. Nobody asked him why it had to be him that said it, and nobody thanked him for saying it first.

He put the flat of his right hand back on the face of the board and said the other thing, in his own mouth, in the middle of that day, with a date in chalk on the outside of the door at the foot of that stair behind him.

**"—"**

That was the whole of what he said about it. Nobody in that room agreed with it and nobody argued with it and nobody bettered one word of it, and nobody asked him afterwards whether he was sure of it, and nobody thanked him for it. The woman of about fifty-two did not turn round. The man of about thirty-four kept both his hands in his coat. The man of about thirty-one put his hand on the strap of his satchel and left it there.

It was done faster than a thing of that weight ought to be done. The four days a summoned room would have spent finding out what it was being asked were not available, and nobody in that room had been given any part of a morning instead of them, and there was nobody else in this city that anybody in that room knew of who could have been asked.

---

Nothing at all was done about it at any hour of that day. People went on talking about a rate and a trestle leg and the weather, and the keeper turned a leaf, and the man of about thirty-one went down that stair at about the ninth hour with his satchel under his arm and went out into that market, and the man of about thirty-eight went down behind him with his barrow and stood a while at the bottom with his hands on the handles and nobody asked him what he was waiting for.

At the tenth hour the man of about thirty-nine had his hand off the face of that board and in his coat, and the board stood face in against the leg of the bench with the flat of it in shadow, and there was nothing on that table with the cloth on it and nothing on the shelf at the height of a person's shoulder that a morning could be set down on.
