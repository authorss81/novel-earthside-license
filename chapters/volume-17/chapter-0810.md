# Chapter 0810 — A Trough Emptied By Hand

The man of about thirty-four set his board up face in against his own trestle and then put one hand on the near end of the trough on the flags and one on the far end and tipped it, and stood there and let it go.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-fifth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The row was full by then and the flags were damp underfoot and drying at the edges. The water in that trough had stood in it since the middle of the week with a skin on it, and about four people a day went past the bottom of that step.

The man of about twenty-seven came along the row with his bucket half full and went over the wet flags in front of that step without stopping, and nobody in that row said one word to the man of about thirty-four about what he was doing. At the far end of that walk the man of about thirty-one stood at the wall with both hands empty and said the wind had come round to the north again and would be at that door by evening, and the man of about thirty-nine said it had come round to the north on Tuesday and he would not put money on the evening of it.

**"It came round on Tuesday and it will come round again before the evening,"** the man of about thirty-one said. **"That is twice you have got it right and once you have not."**

**"You are keeping the wrong morning."**

---

The water went across the flags in a broad sheet and found the dip under the near trestle and went down into it, and after that it came up over the edge of the trough and spread out flat and thinned and was gone by the time it had reached the far side of that walk. The trough stood on dry wood with its rim dark all round and its feet standing in the middle of a wet place that was wider than the trough itself.

He set it down square on the flags, the way he set everything down, and wiped his hands down the front of his coat and went back to his own board and stood at the back of his own trestle with his hands in his coat again.

---

The trough stood dry at the tenth hour and the wet edge round its feet had dried away except where the water had gone under the trestle, and the rim of it was still dark all round where the level had been.
