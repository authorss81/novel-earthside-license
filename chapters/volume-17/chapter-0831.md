# Chapter 0831 — The Door Swung Back Onto The Load

Adrian Vale put the flat of his right hand on the face of the store door at the wharf and pushed it back against its jamb, and the wind took it off him again and shut it a hand's width, and stopped.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and sixteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The yard lay at the near end of eleven miles of flats. The wind came over the wall, dropped into the yard and had hold of that door every time he let go of it. An awning ran along the side of the store, and the boards under it stood in rows with their ends squared off against the wall, and a tally-board lay face up on the top row with two lines of grey grit across it. There was nothing on that wharf to hold a door open with but the door itself.

He wanted it propped, so a stack could be carried in out of the weather and stand dry under the awning instead of standing in the rain coming over the flats. He had been at it a while and the wind had beaten him twice, and there was no bar on that wharf, no chock, and nothing loose in the yard to put a foot under.

He tried it with his shoulder in the opening, side-on with his back to the jamb and his feet braced on the flags. He got the door back and held it for about as long as a man can hold a thing and be in the way of the whole of a doorway at the same time. He got it back a second time with his heel against the bottom edge and stood in the opening with his hand flat on the face of it, and the door stood where he had put it for the length of a dozen breaths. Then it came to again in the wind and pushed his hand off it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight at that salt wharf came into the yard at about the fifth hour with his barrow and a stack of boards on it, and set the near wheel down against the step, and found Adrian Vale in the opening of the door with his shoulder against it.

**"That door wants holding,"** he said.

**"I have it."**

**"Then I will come past you with it."**

The barrow would not go through an opening with a man standing in it, whichever way round it was tried. The man put his shoulder against the near end of the stack and pushed it at the gap. The near end of it went into the jamb and stopped there, with the rain coming over the flats into the ends of the boards and standing on the top of the load.

He drew it back and set it down and stood with both his hands on the handles and said nothing at all. He did not ask the man in that doorway to move out of it, and he did not ask him to go and find something to prop it with. Adrian stood in the opening a while longer with his hand on the face of the door. He was the reason that stack was standing in the rain and not under the awning, and nothing was said about that either. The water was running along the flags at the side of the yard where the paving dips, and the near end of the load was standing in it.

Then he came out of the opening to let the man of about thirty-eight by with it. The wind took the door and swung it back against the near end of the load behind him, and the edge of it went across the two boards at the top and put both of them off the stack on to the flags. The near end of one of them went down into the rain standing against the wall of the store and lay there in it. The other lay up on the dry crown with its end in the air.

The man of about thirty-eight picked both of them off the flags. He stood the wet one on its end where the water running along the wall would drain off it, and then put it back on the barrow with that end at the near bottom of the load, where it would not be the first thing anybody put a hand on. He got the door open himself with his knee, one hand high on the face of it and his hip against the panels, and it went back far enough for the barrow to come through with the stack on it. He carried the whole of that stack in past Adrian Vale on his own and set it down under the awning with the rows squared off round it.

He came back out and set the barrow on its legs and put the tally-board straight on the top of the load where it had been, face up, with its two lines of grey grit across it. Adrian got his own hands clear of the jamb and stood aside to let him pass. Nobody in that yard thanked him for what he had done with that door, and nobody told him he had been right about it, and nobody there had been waiting for him to be useful. He had held a door open that morning and the stack had still gone into the water, and the two of them were still strangers at the end of it.

---

The near end of that stack stood black and dripping on to dry boards under the awning, and there was a wet ring on the board underneath it where the water had come off the end of the one that had been down in the water against that wall.
