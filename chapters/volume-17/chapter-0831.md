# Chapter 0831 — The Door Swung Back Onto The Load

Adrian Vale put the flat of his right hand on the face of that store door at the wharf and pushed it back against its jamb, and the wind took it off him again and shut it a hand's width and stopped.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and sixteenth day of the Bare Month, a Saturday. The yard lay at the near end of eleven miles of flats and the wind came over the wall of that wharf and dropped into the yard and had hold of that door every time he let go of it. An awning ran along the side of that store and the boards under it were stacked in rows with their ends squared off against the wall. There was nothing on that wharf to hold a door open with but the door itself.

He wanted that door propped, so a stack could be carried in out of the weather and stand dry under that awning instead of standing in the rain coming over the flats.

He tried it with his shoulder in the opening, side-on with his back to the jamb and his feet braced on the flags, and he got the door back and held it for about as long as a man can hold a thing and be in the way of the whole of a doorway at the same time. He got it back a second time with his heel against the bottom edge of it and stood in the opening with his hand flat on the face, and the door stood where he had put it for the length of a dozen breaths and then came to again in the wind and pushed his hand off it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came into that yard at about the fifth hour with his barrow and a stack of boards on it and put the near wheel down against the step, and Adrian Vale was in the opening of that door with his shoulder against it.

**"That door wants holding,"** the man of about thirty-eight said.

**"I have it,"** Adrian Vale said.

**"Then I will come past you with it."**

The barrow would not go through an opening with a man standing in it, whichever way round it was tried. The man of about thirty-eight put his shoulder against the near end of that stack and pushed it at the gap, and the near end of it went into the jamb and stopped there with the rain coming over the flats into the ends of the boards and standing on the top board of the stack. He drew it back and stood with both his hands on the handles and said nothing at all, and he did not ask the man in that doorway to move out of it and he did not ask him to go and find something to prop it with.

Adrian Vale stood in that opening a while longer with his hand on the face of the door. He was the reason that stack was standing in the rain and not under the awning, and nothing was said about that either.

Then he came out of the opening to let the man of about thirty-eight by with it, and the wind took that door and swung it back against the near end of the load behind him, and the edge of it went across the two boards at the top and put both of them off the stack onto the flags at the side of that yard. The near end of one of them went down into the rain that was standing against the wall of that store and lay there in it, and the other lay up on the dry crown with its end in the air.

The man of about thirty-eight picked both of them off the flags. He stood the wet one on its end where the water was running along the wall to drain off it, and then he put it back on the barrow with that end at the near bottom of the load where it would not be the first thing anybody put a hand on. He got that door open himself with his knee, one hand high on the face of it and his hip against the panels, and it went back far enough for the barrow to come through with the stack on it, and he carried the whole of that stack in past Adrian Vale on his own and set it down under that awning with the rows squared off round it.

Not one person in that yard thanked Adrian Vale for anything he had done with that door, and nobody said a word to him about having been right about it, and nobody there had been waiting for him to be useful and he was not useful to them.

---

The near end of that stack stood black and dripping on to dry boards under the awning, and there was a wet ring on the top of the board underneath it where the water had come off the end of the one that had been down in the water against that wall.
