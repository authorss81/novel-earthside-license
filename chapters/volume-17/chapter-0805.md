# Chapter 0805 — Put Back And Not Left There

The iron on the top step of that stair was cold through a sleeve when a hand came down on it at about the fifth hour, and it went down the stair end first and had to be turned twice in the turn, because the stair was narrower than the length of it.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninetieth day of the Bare Month, a Monday. The rain had stopped in the night and the flags were wet at the edges and drying in the middle. The light came off the flags at the bottom of that stair and up the steps as far as the door and stopped there.

The room filled in the ordinary way. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end with both hands empty. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of that bench.

The man of about thirty-four put his hollow down and turned it over.

**"That far trestle wants a leg under it before the rain comes back."**

**"The leg is the store's business and not mine,"** the man of about thirty-one said, at the far end, without turning round. **"You may tell them so and I will not thank you for the walk."**

Nobody said anything else about it.

---

The bar went into two sockets on the far side of that door and turned, and the door came to against its own weight and stood there with nothing holding it but the iron. For the better part of two hours the light on the flags at the bottom of that stair went no further than the step, because the door was shut against it, and about four people a day went past on that paving and could not see in.

At about the seventh hour a hand went to the sockets again and the iron came out and went up the stair and lay down across the top step, and the door swung a hand's width off its frame and stayed there, and the light came back in across the bottom four steps.

Nobody in that room said one word about either of those two things. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end with his hands open and did not say that he had done it, and nobody asked him.

---

The light went off the top of that stair before it went off anything else in that room, and the boards there were dry, and had been wet at their edges since the rain stopped.
