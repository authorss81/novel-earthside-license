# Chapter 0811 — A Quarter Of An Hour Below

Somebody stood at the foot of that stair with both hands in a coat and did not go up, and stayed there while the paving filled and emptied twice over.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-sixth day of the Bare Month, a Sunday. The light lay flat on the flags and stopped short of the bottom step. The bar lay on the top step above, where it could not be seen from the paving, and the door at the bottom of the stair stood a hand's width off its frame.

About four people a day went past on that paving, in twos and singly, and each of them stepped round the bottom step without being asked to. The one in the coat moved a pace for the first of them and stood back against the wall for the next, and neither time went up.

The rain had left the flags shining and the water standing in the low places of them. A barrow went past with wet wood and a man behind it with his head down, and neither of them slowed at the stair.

---

The room above went on with its morning. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. Talk came down the open stair about a trestle at the far end of the market row, and nobody at the top of it looked down.

A quarter of an hour went by at the foot of that stair. Then the one in the coat took both hands out of the coat, wiped the rain off the shoulders of it, and went on up the row toward the far end of the market, and did not look back at the door.

---

The rain came back before the tenth hour and took the footprints off the flags.
