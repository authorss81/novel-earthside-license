# Chapter 0822 — The Barrow Came Down The Hollow

Adrian Vale knelt at the side of that yard with both hands in the water standing in the hollow of the flags, and turned his palms over under it and let it run off the ends of his fingers into the same place.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and seventh day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The yard was at the near end of eleven miles of flats and the wind off them came over the wall and dropped into that yard and pushed the standing water to the low side of the hollow and held it there. The low place in the stone at the side of the yard had a bucket standing in it and the flags ran downhill from the step toward the hollow and could not get any lower than the hollow.

He wanted that hollow dry before a barrow came down it, so the boards a man carries through that yard would not go into the water at the near end. He got both hands in as far as they would go and scooped the water out into his palms and put it outside the ring of that hollow, and it came back into the same place behind his hands. He worked his fingers into the joint where two flags met and brought out a fistful of grit and a cake of mortar with stone in it, and the water came up into the hollow again and stood exactly where it had stood at the fourth hour.

---

The man of about thirty-eight came in at the yard from the flats with his barrow and his load of boards on it, and saw the stranger down in the flags and did not stop.

**"There is no drain at that end of that yard,"** he said.

**"Is there one at the wall?"** Adrian said.

**"Not that anybody has ever told me of."**

The barrow came on down the line of flags with the near wheel running wide of the hollow, and Adrian was in the flags at the near side of it with his hand still wet, and the man of about thirty-eight had to swing the barrow wide over the dry crown of that run of stone to get round him. The wide wheel went down into the hollow and stopped there, and the near end of the load went over with it and stood in the water, and the boards took the water off their own faces and put it back into the same hollow from four sides at once.

The man stood with the handles up and let it be what it was for the length of a breath, and the water went off the boards in a stream and filled the hollow to a level it had not been at, and Adrian got his hands out of it and stood up on the flags beside the barrow with the water running off the cuffs of his coat. Nobody said anything. The man of about thirty-eight took the weight again and drew the barrow up out of the hollow onto the crown, and the near end of that load stayed wet where it had been standing, and he went on up the yard with it and did not look back at the step.

Nobody in that yard thanked Adrian Vale for anything and nobody told him he was right about the hollow, and nobody in that yard was waiting for him to be useful.

---

The water in that hollow stood deeper at the tenth hour than it had stood at the fourth, and there was a skin of the grit of the stone lying across the top of it where his hands had been working.
