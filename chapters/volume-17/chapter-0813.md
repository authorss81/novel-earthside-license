# Chapter 0813 — What It Has Cost Her

The woman of about fifty-two came up that stair with rain on the shoulders of her dress and both hands empty, and took her place at the side of the bench.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-eighth day of the Bare Month, a Tuesday. The light came in at the one window and stopped short of the bench wood. The bar lay on the top step of that stair and the door at the bottom of it stood a hand's width off its frame.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about thirty-one stood at the far end with his satchel under his arm.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of about fifty-two looked down the open length of that stair and said it to the middle of that floor, before she said anything else that morning, and nobody had asked her for it.

**"I have stood at the bottom of that stair in the wet to see whether the iron was in the door, with nothing to carry and nobody to tell, and I would rather have spent those mornings at my own bench."**

Nobody answered her. The keeper kept her eyes on the page. The man of about thirty-nine stood at the near end with his hands in his coat and did not turn round, and nobody in that room thanked her or bettered one word of it, and the talk came back in about four seconds on a trestle at the far end of the market row.

Nothing in that room was done about it at any hour of that day. She stood at the side of the bench with her hands at the sides of her dress till the tenth hour, and nobody asked her a second question.

---

The hollow stood on the board at the far end with its open side up till the tenth hour, and nobody swept it out.
