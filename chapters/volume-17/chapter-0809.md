# Chapter 0809 — A Lid That Does Not Shut

Adrian Vale put his left hand flat on the lid of the box of chalk at the top of the market step and his right thumb under its edge and lifted, and the lid came up about as far as it had ever come up and stopped on the same pin it had stopped on before.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-fourth day of the Bare Month, a Friday. The step was dry where the sun had been on it and wet at the near corner. The row was filling, and the water in the trough at the top of that step was flat and had not been drawn off.

He wanted that lid shut, so the chalk in it would not be walked off that step by a boot heel at any hour of any day.

He tried it a second way, with the box tipped forward on its near edge and the lid pressed down flat, and the lid came down to within a finger of the rim and sat there and did not go the rest of the way, and the pin that had always stopped it stopped it again.

The man of about thirty-four came past on the row with his board face in and his hands in his coat, and Adrian asked him, from the step, whether that lid had ever shut.

**"Not in my time,"** the man of about thirty-four said, and went on up the paving without stopping.

---

At about the fifth hour a man came along that row with a bucket full in one hand and had to turn out into the trestles to get past a stranger crouched on the step. He set the bucket down on the flags, went round the step on the wide side, came back for it, and carried it on. Two of the flags in front of that step were wet before the eighth hour that were dry at the fourth.

He said nothing about it. Nobody in that row said anything to Adrian Vale about the box, and nobody told him the lid had never shut, and nobody stood and waited to see whether he could shut it.

---

By the eighth hour there was a skin on the standing water in that trough, and a man going back along the row put his heel into one of the two wet flags and did not look down at it and went on.
