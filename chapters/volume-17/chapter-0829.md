# Chapter 0829 — Two Of Them On That Bench

The keeper put both hands flat on the open page at the near end of that bench and looked down the length of the room at the two men who were standing at the far end of it, and then at the stair, and then at the two men again.

It was the fourth hour on the five hundred and fourteenth day of the Bare Month, a Thursday. The light came in at the one window and lay across the bench wood and reached the far end of it, and the door at the foot of that stair stood open with the light coming up it. The man of about thirty-four was at the far end of that bench with his own board against his knee. The man of about thirty-nine was at the near end with his board under his arm. The man of about thirty-one went up and down that stair three times that morning with his satchel under his arm and nobody in that room watched him do any of it.

---

**"Somebody went up that stair and I did not see who it was."**

**"Somebody went up and came down again,"** the keeper said.

**"It is the same to me either way."**

Nobody asked her how she knew that it had come down again. Nobody asked anybody else anything about it either, and the talk came back in about four seconds on the weather and a trestle leg at the far end of that market.

The man of about thirty-four and the man of about thirty-nine were both at that bench at the fourth hour and both at it at the seventh hour, and one of the two of them went up that stair and came down again between the two, and by the seventh hour that room had the two of them standing at its far end and no way whatever of saying which one of them had gone up.

Nobody tried. Nobody went down that stair to look at the step, and nobody asked the man of about thirty-one whether he had seen anybody go up, and nobody said out loud that it could not be settled.

At about the ninth hour the keeper looked at the far end of that bench again for a while and said nothing at all about it, and turned a leaf.

---

At the tenth hour those two men were both standing at the far end of that bench in the light, and neither of them had anything to say about where he had been between the fourth hour and the seventh, and nobody in that room asked either of them.
