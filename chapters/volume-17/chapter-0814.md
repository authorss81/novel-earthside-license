# Chapter 0814 — The Book On The Bench

The keeper put both hands under the register on the shelf behind the bench and carried it the length of that room, and set it down square on the bench wood.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and ninety-ninth day of the Bare Month, a Wednesday. The light came in at the one window and lay across the bench. The door at the foot of that stair stood a hand's width off its frame and the bar was on the top step above it.

The man of about thirty-four was at the bench with his hollow. The man of about thirty-nine came up with his board under his arm and stood the face of it against the near leg. The woman of about fifty-two waited at the side of the bench with her hands at the sides of her dress.

---

She opened the book in the middle of the bench where anybody along it could see the page, and left it open, and went back to the near end. The man of about thirty-four glanced at the page and back at his hollow. The man of about thirty-nine looked at it from the near end and said nothing about it.

The page lay flat and the leaves stayed where she had put them, and the light moved across them while the morning went on.

**"The rain has got into the hollow again,"** the man of about thirty-four said, to the middle of that floor.

**"Turn it over and it will dry,"** the woman of about fifty-two said, without looking up.

Nobody asked what the book was for. Nobody asked who had carried it down or who would carry it back. The page lay open between them all morning, and the talk went on round it about rates and weather and a trestle at the far end.

---

The light went off the bench wood before the tenth hour and left the page in shadow.
