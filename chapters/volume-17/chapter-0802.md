# Chapter 0802 — A Room Going On

The woman of about fifty-two came up that stair and stopped two steps below the top with her hands at the sides of her dress, and stood there long enough that the man of about thirty-nine came past her on his way to the near end of the bench and did not touch her.

It was the fourth hour on the four hundred and eighty-seventh day of the Bare Month, a Friday. The light came off the flags at the bottom of that stair and up the first four steps and no further. The bar lay on the top step where it had lain when the light found that room.

The morning filled in the ordinary way. The keeper turned a leaf. The man of about thirty-four took his hollow. The man of about twenty-seven set his bucket by the window and went down again with it.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of about fifty-two looked down the open length of that stair and the man of about thirty-nine looked at the near end of the bench, and neither of them said one word, and about four seconds went by before the talk started again about a trestle at the far end.

The man of about thirty-one shifted his satchel from one arm to the other under the wall along that side and stood at the far end with his back half turned to the top of the stair.

**"I could carry that down and set it,"** he said, to the middle of that floor. **"There is nothing in it to carry and it would not take a minute."**

The man of about thirty-nine did not turn round and did not answer him. The carrier waited, and then said nothing either, and took his hand off the strap and put it back on the strap.

---

The hollow of the man of about thirty-four stood on the board at the far end of that bench with its open side up, and it had not been swept out at any hour of that day, and there was chalk dust in the bottom of it.
