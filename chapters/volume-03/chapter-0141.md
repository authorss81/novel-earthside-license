# Chapter 0141 — The Thing That Is Not Counted

On the fifty-first day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and forty-first day of the flood and a Tuesday, about nine people sat in a room above a chandlery at about the eighth hour of the evening and failed for the seventh time in a hundred and forty-one days to find out how a page that has already been used once came to end a document, and the failure was entered with the date on it, and the count of rooms on the page is six and is not being made seven.

**The licence is ninety-one days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty-six days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eighty-second time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and forty days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

It is a different room and it is not a different room, and the difference is the whole of the night.

The sixth room was at the back of the Open Hand and it had no key and no owner, and about nine people were allowed four hours in it because nobody could be angry at them for being in it. This one is over a chandlery and it is got up for one night a week by a man who takes a shilling for it and who went down to the market at about the seventh hour and left the key on the table, and the room has a window and a stove and about nine chairs and a smell of tar.

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said in about four seconds, before anybody had sat down, that it is the same room, and the reason is that the key on the table is the same thing as no key, and the shilling is the same thing as no owner, and a man who takes a shilling is a man who is owed, and the person who is owed can turn them out in the morning.**

"It is a different room," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It is a different room with the same door on it,**" she said, "**and I have said that before in about nine rooms in a hundred and forty-one days and I am not going to get new words for it.**"

---

**And they tried a seventh thing, and it took about nine minutes to find, and the seventh thing is the only one of the seven that anybody in this city thought of, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out and it is hers.**

> **What did the absence cost. Not how the page worked. What it was worth not having.**

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**That is not about the page,**" said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It is the seventh thing about the page and it is the only one of the seven that is about a person instead of a thing,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and I want to say why I thought of it and it took me two days and it is this. Five rooms asked what the page says and what it was laid on and who was in the room and what the document was and which one holds and why, which is nothing. The sixth room asked who wanted it to work, and the answer was nobody, and that is the finding of the thirty-seventh day of the month after this one. Not one of the six ever asked what it was worth not having, and I have been in this city for a hundred and forty-one days and I have watched about nine people try to explain a thing by asking what it did, and that is what you do with a machine, and this is not a machine, it is a page that went in a room on the second day of the month after this one and ended an undertaking that about a hundred and forty people had signed.**"

---

**And then the room got about four minutes from something, and it is on the page, and what came out of it was a list of people and not a sentence, and a notary of fifty put her hand flat on the table.**

The document that had been ended was the undertaking of the Lantern Concord. It had made a body of about a hundred and forty people the permanent holder of the licence in exchange for taking the whole of a standing charge off a private debtor, at no cost to anybody.

"**There,**" said the girl of seventeen. "**There is the answer to the seventh question and it is about a hundred and forty people and eleven households on a nine-mile road and a woman of forty-one who was owed the work in her own words in a document that says the work is not written down anywhere, and it is a cost column, and a cost column is a place where a cost goes.**"

Nobody touched the table.

"**No,**" said a notary of fifty.

"**I have not written it down.**"

"**You were about to.**" She did not take her hand off. "**I have been in about four rooms in a hundred and forty-one days with about four people in them who put things together, and what comes out is always a cost, and a cost with about a hundred and forty people in it is not a description of a page, it is a description of about a hundred and forty people, and it goes in a book that travels, and there is a clerk nine hundred miles away with a column for where they are.**"

"Then what do we write," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Nothing,**" said the notary. "**I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since the second day of the month after this one and it is four words. There is no harm.**"

---

**And a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, said the rest of it, and it is on the page, and he did not try to say the sentence that came closest and everybody in that room noticed that he did not try.**

"**There is harm. I want to correct her in one line because she is wrong and she has been wrong since the second day of the month after this one and nobody has corrected her.**"

"Then say it," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**I am not going to.**" He put his hands flat on the table on either side of hers, which is a thing he has never done. "**Not because it would be a mechanism. Because the only evidence anybody in four hundred miles has of what that page did is what it cost, and about a hundred and forty people paid it, and the cost of the page is a set of people, and if we put them in a book tonight then we have described about a hundred and forty people in order to explain an object, and a description of people is the thing this city has spent a hundred and forty-one days refusing to do, and I would rather have no explanation at all than that one.**"

---

**And the sentence that came closest came about four minutes later, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard it, and it is not written down in any book, on any board, in any stone, in any column, in any state file, and a girl of seventeen had a slate under her hand and did not put it on the table, and it is entered that she did not.**

It is entered that she said afterwards, on the road, in about nineteen seconds, that she had had the words and that she had not written them because **a sentence about a page that works is a mechanism, and a mechanism is a diagram, and a diagram of the most dangerous object in this city is a worse thing to have in a hundred and forty-one days than nine minutes of nothing**, and that she had been told that on the thirty-seventh day of the month after this one and had written it in a book and had thought about it every day since.

"That is a rule about a document," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It is a rule about a page that works,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and a page that works is a document about the room it was in.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the stove ticked, and the man who takes the shilling came up at about the ninth hour and found nine people in his room and did not say anything about it and went back down.

---

**And the failure was entered with the date on it, and the count of rooms on the page is six, and a girl of seventeen wrote the six down and then wrote a line under it in her own hand, and the line is binding, and it is the reason the seventh is not a seven.**

> **Six rooms. Six attempts. The seventh thing tried is what the absence cost, and it was reached on the fifty-first day of the month after this one, in a room above a chandlery, and it got about four minutes. The count of rooms on the page is six and is not being made seven. A count that moves is a score and this is not a score, and a city that has failed at a thing five times and has written the five down has failed a sixth time in the same place, and one that has written the six down has failed a seventh time in a different room in the same place, and the second of those is not progress. The page is in a lockable room off the salt store with the door shut. Nobody produced a mechanism, nobody showed the page working, nobody reproduced it, nobody in this city can say why it did what it did, and the one sentence that came closest was not written down and will not be written down.**

It is entered that about four people in that room wanted the page to have not worked, and that **not one of them wanted it out loud, and that a thing a person wants in a room is not a thing anybody can hear, and that is why there is no seventh room and there is a sixth.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the fifty-first day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in thirteen days and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there twenty-five days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five. And a printed appointment of an interpreter with nine terms on it is on a table two floors down and a man of thirty-two has not answered it and has said he will answer it in a room on a day with a date on it. And a compact of eleven lines is drafted and is not read out and is not signed. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is twenty-nine days ago, and the water has not been received for seventy-two days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is fifty-six days, and one of the forty has not come for twenty-seven days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for fifty-six days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and forty-one days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And a Tuesday on which about nine people got four minutes from the answer and put down a hand on a table is the forty-seventh silence and the pattern is forty-seven for forty-seven.**

**And the last thing of the fifty-first day of the month after this one is a slate with a six on it and a line under the six, and a page in a lockable room off the salt store with the door shut, and a hearing in thirteen days.**
