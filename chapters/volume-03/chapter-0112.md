# Chapter 0112 — A Date on a Door Is Not a Page

On the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twelfth day of the flood and a Monday, about nine people walked four miles up the Reach road in the wrong weather to a village of nine households and stood in a gateway and looked at a date in chalk on a door, and the date was that day, and the document the date came with is void, and the date is on the door anyway, and nobody in this city is going to be told what happens on a door, and nobody washed it off.

**The licence is sixty-two days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-third time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and eleven days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And there is no ninth household in that village and there have not been nine for thirty years, and the waterboard still counts it as nine because nine is the number that village was assessed at when the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream at a public act with witnesses a hundred and forty years ago, and that is a Crown number and it is on a Crown page and the page is nine hundred miles away.**

The road is the upper road and eleven households keep nine miles of it and nobody has to and nobody has asked them. Two of the eleven had a gate open on the way out and one of those two put out a cup of water without being asked and without asking, and it is the third time in a month that has happened on that road and nobody has written any of it down.

At the fourth mile the road forks and the left fork goes to a village of nine households and the right fork goes nowhere anybody in about nine people wanted to go, and about four of them looked at the right fork anyway and nobody said why.

**A woman of seventy of Nine Elms was standing in the gateway of the second house with a stick in her hand and she had been standing there since before the light, and she is Ilda Marsh, and she said the thing she has said three times before before anybody in about nine people had got their coats off, and she said it to the date on the door and not to any of them.**

"Three is a thing a village can do," she said. "**Four is a thing a person does.**"

And it is entered that nobody answered her for about nineteen seconds, and that a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall had his hand out of his coat pocket and put it back, and that a woman of twenty-four said afterwards that she had wanted to ask a question all the way from the fourth mile and that she did not ask it and that the reason she did not was on the door in front of her.

---

**And the date in the chalk is the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and it is on the door of the second house, and there are eight more doors in that village and every one of them has the same date on it in the same hand, and the hand is not the hand of anybody in this village, and the chalk is two months old and has not been washed.**

The notice it came with was nailed to nine doors on the sixty-fourth day of the new month, which was the eighty-ninth day of the flood, and it carried one date and one line, and the line was that the fourth channel at the Nine Elms junction would be shut on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and the date is what everybody in nine households has been looking at for two months, and it is still on nine doors.

"The thing that sent it is void," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who had said almost nothing for four miles. "**The undertaking of the Lantern Concord went down a river on the sixty-fourth day of the new month and was void on the ninety-second day of this flood, in a room in this city, in about nineteen minutes, with nine witnesses, and none of them was in this village.**"

"Then the notice is void," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**The notice is not void. The notice is on a door. The undertaking is what would have paid for the water and the quarter, and it is void, and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force and was never given up and never needed it and never repealed it, and the fourth line is what shut the channel. So the date is in force and the money is gone and both of those are true at once and that is the ordinary shape of this city and not a fault in anybody.**"

The water has not been received for forty-three days, counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. The quarter is gone. A drain in the fourth channel is being cleared by one woman every morning. The whole of the standing charge is on a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything. **And nobody in this city has said what happens if that village refuses a fourth time, and the question went onto a page in a room on the ninth day of the month after this one, and it is unanswered, and it is entered that nobody answered it, and today is the day anybody in this city would have wanted the answer.**

---

**And the woman of seventy said the reason the date is still on her door, in about nine seconds, and it is a reason about a page and a door and it is the whole of what this city has learned in a hundred and twelve days, and a man of thirty-two asked for it to be entered and it was entered in about four seconds.**

"You are going to ask me what the four does," she said. "**I have had a cart coming at noon since before the light and I have not been asked one question about it in a hundred and twelve days, and I am seventy, and I am going to keep it that way.**"

"We are not going to ask you what the four does," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**Then I will tell you what the four does, because I have had two months with a door to look at and none of you have. The four does what I say it does. That is the whole of it. Three times this village said no and the no belonged to nine households and nobody could take it away from anybody. The fourth one is mine. A village cannot be a person. A person can be a person, and a person can be held to it, and a person can be asked whether she will have it, and nobody in nine hundred miles has any instrument that can ask me that. So the fourth refusal is mine and it is a person and not a village and there is nobody nine hundred miles away who can overrule it, and that is not a victory, and it is a cart at noon.**"

"Whose cart," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Mine. I am going to the market at Four Elms and I am going in a cart because I am seventy, and I have been a person about this for two months and I am going to be a person about it in a cart, and I would like all nine of you to write down that I said the four does what I say it does and that I said it on my own door.**"

It was written down.

---

**And a man of thirty-two then walked about four feet back from the door and read the date off it out loud, because that is the whole of what he can do in this world and he has never once pretended otherwise, and he read it and then he said the sentence the day turns on, and a woman of twenty-four said it was the same sentence and he said it was a different one and that she was right and that the two of them were the same sentence said by two people and neither had copied the other.**

"**The twenty-second day of the month after this one,**" he said. "**In chalk, on a door, in a village that was assessed at nine households a hundred and forty years ago and has not had nine since before I was born. And here is the whole of what is different about it. A page in this world can be taken down on a date by the household that keeps it and nobody is asked why twice. I have watched a date come and go in a house four miles up the other road six days ago and the door did not shut. That is the only reason anybody trusts a page in this city. A date on a door cannot be taken down by anybody, because the door is not a household and the chalk is not a page and the village is not nobody's servant. Nobody in four hundred miles can wipe that off this door and nobody in four hundred miles can explain it either, and a thing nobody can take down and nobody can explain is not a protection. It is a mark with no way of being answered.**"

"So what is it," said the woman of seventy.

"**It is a door. It is the only door in this city that nobody can shut, and it is shut anyway, and a man of thirty-two cannot find one instrument in four hundred miles to open it, and he has spent a hundred and twelve days learning that the reason is that the instrument would have to be a document and a document can be refused.**"

And it is entered that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said in about four seconds that he had it exactly backwards, and a man of thirty-two said in four seconds that she was right, and that the two of them had said the same sentence and neither had copied the other, and that a notary of fifty who was not there entered three days later that she had noticed.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing and is named on nothing said it in nine seconds, and it is the shortest thing anybody said on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out and did not improve on it, and nobody in about nine people argued with it, and a man of thirty-two said that she and he had said the same sentence on the same afternoon and that neither of them had heard the other do it.**

"**A page can be taken down. A door cannot. That is not a door that cannot be shut, that is a door that cannot be shut, and those are the same sentence with the words moved about, and one of them is a protection and the other one is a thing nobody can do anything with.**"

"And a chalk mark," said the woman of seventy, "is not a page and is not a door."

"**No. A chalk mark is a thing nine hundred miles away has to be able to explain, and there is no column in this world for a door nobody can shut, and that is why nobody in Aurel has ever heard of Nine Elms and that is also why nobody in Aurel is going to hear about it in a quarter.**"

"Nobody washed it off," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Nobody is going to wash it off,**" said the woman of seventy, "**and I will tell you why and then I am going to my cart. A mark that comes off is a mark somebody can take back. A page can be taken down on a date by the household that owns it. This door has no household that owns anything. So the mark stays, and I have had a person to be for two months and the mark is the only part of it that is out of my hands.**"

---

**And nothing else happened at Nine Elms on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and about nine people went back down four miles in the wrong weather, and they did not go to a fourth house, and they did not stand outside one, and nobody asked anybody anything about a woman of forty-eight, and it is entered that about four of them wanted to and that nobody said so, and the reason is on a page in a stone nine miles up the road with a date on it and says that this city chose not to ask.**

**And a hearing is in forty-two days. And there is one hearing and one clock. And nine households have refused three times and have refused a fourth time this morning and nobody in this city can say what the fourth refusal is, and the question about it is on a page unanswered. And the water has not been received for forty-three days and the quarter is gone and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force. And the licence is sixty-two days old and the fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-third time. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent, and the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch said nothing today. And the wall has said twenty-nine things in this city and one has been refused, and a day spent standing in a gateway looking at a date on a door is the twenty-first silence, and the pattern is twenty-one for twenty-one.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-second day of the month after this one is a date in chalk on a door in a village of nine households, and it is not a page, and it is not going to be answered, and it is the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and it came and it went.**
