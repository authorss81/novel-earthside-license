# Chapter 0143 — Three Is a Shrine, and There Is No Word for Four

On the fifty-third day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and forty-third day of the flood and a Thursday, a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and a girl of seventeen who is on no roll asked a household at a fork nine miles up a road whether it would keep a fourth page in a stone, and the household said yes, and put three conditions on it, and refused to let the second page be corrected, and the reason for that is on the page and it is the finding of the fifty-third day of the month after this one.

**The licence is ninety-three days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty-eight days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eighty-fourth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and forty-two days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The stone is on the north side of a fork and it is about the height of a man's chest and there is a cut in the flat of it and the cut is a line about a hundred and forty years old and the line is a decision, and the ground drops away on the north side and the water was on the other side of the cut before the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream at a public act with witnesses.

**It has three pages in it. The first has six things and a price and a right of refusal. The second is nineteen lines — twelve names, six lines of count, and a seventh line under them saying it is a count and not a debt — and a quarter of a pound of salt a month written under the list and not as another item on it. The third is a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, with a date and a price and a right of refusal and a line saying the keeping household may close it for its own reasons on a date written on the page and is not asked why twice.**

Nobody goes near the third one. It has a date on it and nobody has moved it and nobody has closed it and it has not been moved since the day that date is on and is not going to be.

**And the second page is wrong. It says a woman of forty-four of Corrow is owed a figure on the end of a cost column and a decision about ninety-one acres, and on the thirtieth day of the month after this one a rule drawn under the eleventh line gave her both without asking her, and a page cannot be told the difference between a person paying a debt and a rule settling it in, and the only correction available to anybody in four hundred miles is to ask this household to take the page down on a date.**

---

**And nobody in this city had ever asked this household anything, and a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow said the reason out loud at a fork on the thirty-second day of the month after this one, and a woman of seventy of a village of nine households said on the thirty-eighth day that somebody would have to ask it and that she would not ask it and neither would anybody else, and that whoever asked it would be a person and not a village and she would be glad of that and she would be watching.**

She was watching, in the sense that about four people in this city knew she had said it.

"**She is seventy and she is four miles the other way and she is not coming and she said so,**" said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, on the road, at about the seventh hour, with a barrow she was not going to let anybody else push. "**And the question is hers and I am carrying it, and if anybody in this party tries to make it mine I will put the barrow down.**"

Nobody tried.

---

**And the household that keeps the stone does not put its name on anything, and it is four people and a dog, and one of the four is a woman of about sixty-one with a barrow and she is the one who answered, and it is entered that she was not asked whether she would answer and that she answered anyway and that about four people noticed.**

"**Say the question in the words it is in,**" said Marda Rood. "**And I have had a barrow up here for four years and I am sixty-one and I have said three pages in a stone is a shrine in about nine rooms in this city and one page is a wall somebody kept and two is a mistake, and I am not going to unsay any of it.**"

> **Will the keeping household hold a fourth page in the stone. The page will say that a village of nine households refused a fourth time, as a person and not as a village; that the fourth refusal has no document under it; that the first three were not hers; and that the only record of it in four hundred miles is a date in chalk on nine doors. Nine is a count and not a list. The page will not name the village and the person who refuses it is not asked to.**

She read it twice. The second time was slower.

"**You have not named it,**" she said.

"**No.**"

"**Then the page is about a number of houses somewhere, and in about four years a stranger is going to stand here and read it and be told a story about a village that agreed to something, and there will be a page in the ground saying a village of nine households did not, and neither of those can be checked and the stranger will believe the one that is on paper.**"

"**Yes,**" said a girl of seventeen.

"**Then do not do it,**" said Marda Rood.

---

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had it read out to her twice as well, and did not pretend she had not.

"**I am not telling you not to ask,**" said Marda Rood. "**I am telling you what a fourth page is and I have been four years getting to it and I have not got there. Three is a shrine and I can say what three is. One is a wall somebody kept and I can say what one is. Two is a mistake. Four is a different thing and I do not have a word for four and I have been up this road two hours thinking about it and I have not got one, and I am sixty-one and I am going to say what I do have.**"

"**Then say it,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**I have a barrow.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and about four of them looked at the barrow and nobody looked at her.

"**The stone keeps three pages and the stone is nine inches of stone and the household is nobody's servant and a household may take a page down on a date and is not asked why twice. That is the whole of the protection and it is a protection that works because a person can change their mind and nothing in the stone stops them. Now put a fourth page in and the stone has a thing in it that nobody in that household cares about and that a stranger in about four years will need. And when the household stops caring about it, and I am telling you that I will stop caring about it, because it is a village four miles away and it is not mine, then somebody in that household is going to say the stone should come out of the field, and the whole stone goes, and I have argued for three pages for four years on the grounds that a stranger needs them. I am not going to be the woman who lost three pages to a fourth.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen said the thing that made the answer yes, and it took her about four seconds, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it, and nobody improved on it.**

"**Then it does not have to go in the stone.**"

"It is a page about this city's refusals," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall. "**Where else is it going to go.**"

"**On the other side of it,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and the keeping household is the only household in four hundred miles that is nobody's servant, and a thing on the back of a thing nobody in four hundred miles can take down is the safest thing anybody has ever heard of, and nobody has ever asked for one because everybody has assumed that a page is the front.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and Marda Rood put her hand flat on the stone, which is the third time in a hundred and forty-three days that a person in this city has put a hand flat on something to stop a conversation rather than to make a record, and a notary of fifty did it on the thirty-ninth day and a girl of seventeen's whole life is not that long.

"**That is not a shrine,**" she said.

"**No,**" said a girl of seventeen. "**That is a page a person can be angry at and cannot be taken down, and it is the only combination anybody in this city has built in a hundred and forty-three days and it took the two of us about nine minutes and a barrow.**"

---

**And the household said yes, and put three conditions on it, and the three conditions are on the page, and the third of them is the finding of the fifty-third day of the month after this one.**

> **One. The keeping household may take the fourth page down on a date of its own choosing and is not asked why twice, the same as the first and the third.**
> **Two. The person who asks is not thanked, now or in four years, and the household will say so if anybody in this city says otherwise.**
> **Three. The second page stays as it is, wrong, for as long as the stone stands.**

"**The second page is wrong,**" said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**It says a woman of forty-four is owed a thing and a rule settled it in without asking her. We have all known that for twenty-three days. Are you telling us you will keep a wrong page on purpose.**"

"**I am telling you I will not correct it,**" said Marda Rood. "**And there is a difference and it took me four years and a barrow to get to it. If I take that page down, then somebody in this household knew what was on it and took it down, and in about four years a stranger stands here and reads three pages and asks where the second went, and the answer is in a book, and the answer is that a page was corrected, and then this stone is a thing that can be corrected. And a thing that can be corrected is a thing that will be corrected, and it will be corrected by whoever comes next, and they will be doing it for a reason.**"

"And a wrong page is not a thing anybody can be angry at," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**A wrong page is a thing a person can be angry at,**" said Marda Rood, "**and the woman whose name is on it is forty-four and lives at Corrow and has not been asked about any of it, and if you correct that page you will have to go and ask her, and you will not, and if you do not correct it you will have to look at it every time you come up this road for the next forty years, and that is the point. I am going to have to look at it. That is what I am offering her. I am not going to take it down and I am not going to ask her and I am going to have to look at it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the road was empty in both directions and it stayed empty.

"**Say that again,**" said a woman of twenty-four.

"**No,**" said Marda Rood. "**I said it four years ago about the first page and I am not going to be a woman who says things twice on a road. It is on a page. Ask the girl.**"

---

**And the fourth page went into the stone on the north side of a fork nine miles up a road, and it is the fourteenth shape, and it is dated, and it is a page a person can be angry at and cannot be taken down by anybody in four hundred miles except a household that will not put its name on anything, and it does not go on a roll and it does not go into a case.**

> **THE FOURTEENTH SHAPE, and it is dated the fifty-third day of the month after this one. A page on the back of a stone on the north side of a fork, kept by a household that will not put its name on anything, entered on the asking of a person and not a village, to the effect that a village of nine households refused a fourth time as a person and not as a village, that the fourth refusal has no document under it, that the first three were not hers, and that the only record of it in four hundred miles is a date in chalk on nine doors, and that nine is a count and not a list. It is local. It is not the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood, and not the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month, and not the second answer of the twenty-second, and not the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first, and not the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month. It is not the undertaking of the Lantern Concord. It is not the design of the Quiet Choir. It is not the eighth shape, and not the ninth shape, and not the tenth shape, and not the eleventh shape, and not the twelfth shape, and not the thirteenth shape. It is the first page anybody in this city has built for the express purpose of not being able to be taken down, and that is a new kind of protection and there is no word for it yet and a woman of sixty-one has said so.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the fifty-third day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in eleven days and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there twenty-seven days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five. And a printed appointment of an interpreter with nine terms on it is on a table two floors down and a man of thirty-two has not answered it. And a compact of eleven lines is drafted and is not read out and is not signed. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is thirty-one days ago, and the water has not been received for seventy-four days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and the date in the chalk on nine doors is not washed and there is now a page on the back of a stone about it and the page does not name the village. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is fifty-eight days, and one of the forty has not come for twenty-nine days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for fifty-eight days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and forty-three days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And a Thursday on which a household agreed to hold a page it does not care about, on the back of a thing nine inches deep, and refused to correct a wrong page on purpose, is the forty-ninth silence and the pattern is forty-nine for forty-nine.**

**And the last thing of the fifty-third day of the month after this one is a stone with four pages in it, one of which is wrong on purpose, and a barrow, and a hearing in eleven days.**
