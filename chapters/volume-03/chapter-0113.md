# Chapter 0113 — A Ledger Nobody Asked For

On the twenty-third day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirteenth day of the flood and a Tuesday, about nine people went to a room where nine other people were trading on a board with rates on it and a refusal on it and no names on it, asked a cooperative of traders under local law to carry nine people nine hundred miles, were refused in about four seconds, and the refusal went on the board with no name on it, and a man of thirty-nine then asked to be added to the board by name and was also refused in about four seconds, and it is the third time in a hundred and thirteen days that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing has been asked to put her name on anything at all and said no.

**The licence is sixty-three days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-fourth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twelve days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the board is a board, and it is four feet by three, and it is sixteen days old, and it has a rate on it for nineteen things and a refusal on it and nothing else, and a girl of seventeen asked for it to be described in four lines in a room and nobody would describe it and she wrote it down herself.**

The rates are in the local hand and there are nineteen of them, and a man of about thirty-nine has traded on those same nineteen rates in four markets for four years without once writing them down, and has wanted his name against his own since the day somebody else wrote them up. There is no column for a name. **There is a line under the last rate that is not a rate and is not a name and says that a person who does not want to be found here has not been found here, and it is the oldest thing on the board, and it is not a rule anybody agreed to, it is a rule nobody broke, and the difference between those two things in four hundred miles is about the whole of this city.**

Nine people were trading on it at the ninth hour of the morning when about nine other people came in, and the two sets of nine were in one room for about an hour and forty minutes and it is entered that not one person in either set was asked to buy anything and that not one person in either set bought anything.

"You have come about a carrier," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"Yes."

"**Then say it out loud and do not stand near the board while you say it, because there are nine people trading on it and two of them owe money to nobody and one of those two has a bad leg.**"

---

**And the ask was made, and it was made badly, and it was made by a man of thirty-two who is on no roster of anything, and he made it badly on purpose and said so afterward, and the refusal took about four seconds and it is the shortest refusal anybody has entered in a hundred and thirteen days.**

"We need nine people in a room nine hundred miles away in forty-one days," he said. "**There is no road. There is no case going until a quarter, and a quarter is five weeks after the hearing. We have a cooperative of traders in this city with a right of refusal that cannot be bought out, and every one of you has been somewhere, and I am asking whether the Open Hand will carry nine people.**"

"No," said the woman of about thirty-four.

"And the reason is not a vote," she said, and nobody in the room had said it was a vote. "**You have asked a thing that has a right of refusal that cannot be bought out, and the right of refusal is mine and it is not a vote and nobody in this room is allowed to outvote it, including you, and you knew that when you asked because you have been in this city a hundred and thirteen days. The reason is not that we will not carry you. The reason is that carrying you means putting nine names in a book, because a carrier's book is a book of names, and a book of names is a roll, and a roll goes in a case, and a case goes to Aurel. You have asked me to put this board into the one instrument in this city that travels, and I have been on this board for a hundred and thirteen days because it is the one page in four hundred miles that does not.**"

"Entered," said a girl of seventeen.

"And put the date on it," said the woman of about thirty-four. "**And put nothing else on it. Not my name. Not the year. There is a refusal on that board now with a date on it and there is no name on the refusal, and that is the fourth thing on that board that is a rule and not a rate, and the reason none of the four of them has a name on it is that a rule with a name on it is a person, and a person can be asked whether they will have it, and I have been asked three times in a hundred and thirteen days whether I will have a thing and I have said no three times and the board is the reason the third one was easy.**"

It was entered. **And a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the rates said out loud that he had traded them for four years and had never once been asked to carry anything and had never once thought about it, and a woman of twenty-four said that this was the correct state of affairs and that a thing nobody has to think about is the only kind of institution in four hundred miles that has never yet been anybody's instrument.**

---

**And then the man of thirty-nine asked to be added to it by name, and it was not a surprise, and a girl of seventeen wrote the time on it, and the woman of about thirty-four said no in about four seconds and did not sit down first.**

"I want my name against my own rate," he said. "**I have traded on those rates for four years and I have never been late and I have never been wrong about one of them, and I have a right to be found.**"

"You have a right to be found," she said. "**A name on this board is a name in a book. A name in a book is a column.**"

And it is nineteen words, and a girl of seventeen counted them twice, and the room did not check the count, and it is entered that the room did not check the count.

"Nineteen," said the girl of seventeen.

"**And the rest of it is in a book nine hundred miles away and not on this board,**" said the woman of about thirty-four. "**In this city a person is somebody you can find. In a book nine hundred miles away a person is a line, and a line can be copied, and a copy of a line is a name in another column, and that column is a removal with a space in it for where the person is. You have asked me to be the first person to put one of those on a board like this in four hundred miles, and there is no room on it for a space for where somebody is, and there is never going to be, and that is the entire reason I stand in a market and sell things.**"

"I am not a removal," said the man of thirty-nine.

"**No. You are a man of thirty-nine with a bad leg who has never been late. And the moment your name is on that board you are a line in a book, and a line in a book does not know what it is for.**"

He said nothing for about nineteen seconds. **And it is entered that nobody persuaded him and that nobody told him he was wrong, and that he said the sentence the day turns on at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and that he is not a person in this city who is given to sentences, and that the girl of seventeen wrote it out and that a man of thirty-two said it was the finding of the day.**

"That is three things today," said the man of thirty-nine. "**You refused to carry nine people, you refused a rule with no name on it, and you refused me. And I have traded on those nineteen rates for four years and this morning I found out that I have been standing next to a document and not one of us has ever read it.**"

---

**And the fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-fourth time, and a carrier of the second table who has been carrying for eleven years stood in the doorway through the whole of it and said the thing nobody in that room had said in a hundred and thirteen days, and she said it in nine seconds, and about four people heard it and about four did not.**

"A case is a case a carrier is paid for," said Peth Lammay, twenty-four. "**And the emptiest thing in a case is the safest thing in a case, and I have been saying that since before any of you arrived and each time I have said it it has been about a different thing, and today it is about a name. A case with a name in it can be carried.**"

"Carried where," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Anywhere. That is the whole of what a name does in a case. Eight columns go down a river to an office and seven of them are about a district and one of them is a question, and the office can do nothing with the question, and the office can do something with a name, and that is the difference between this city and a place that has lost. This city has sent a question and a number. If it had sent eight names it would have got an answer, and the answer would have been wrong, and it would have been wrong in about four seconds and it would have come back as a number about a district with eight people not in it, and nobody in Aurel would have known that anything had happened at all.**"

"So the name is the thing that travels," said the man of thirty-two.

"**The name is the only thing in a case that anybody nine hundred miles away can act on, and that is the whole trouble with names, and it is why a board with no names on it is worth more than a book with nineteen of them in it, and it is why I have carried for eleven years and never once sent one.**"

And a woman of twenty-four said out loud that the whole of what a carrier is paid for is the right to open a thing, and that a board with no names on it is a thing nobody has ever been paid to open. **And a girl of seventeen asked the carrier the seventh time in a hundred and thirteen days which of the two books a thing goes in, and the carrier said the question had been asked seven times and had been unanswerable seven times, and that she had answered it wrongly twice and had not been told which twice were wrong, and that a man of thirty-two then said the sentence he said in a room over a market six days ago, in the local language, and that nobody counted it, and that a woman of twenty-four said that was the third time anybody had counted one of his sentences in his absence and that the three of them had all come out the same.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the twenty-third day of the month after this one, and about nine people went back up a hill in the wrong weather, and the board is still a board, and it has a rate on it for nineteen things and a refusal on it with a date on it and no name on it and four rules that are not rates and have no names on them, and it is not a roll and it is not going to Aurel, and about nine people trade on it in the morning.**

**And a hearing is in forty-one days. And there is one hearing and one clock. And nine people said out loud on the twenty-first day that they will stand in it, and the number went in a book with no heading and no date, and it is a number of people and not a list. And nine households at Nine Elms refused a fourth time yesterday and nobody in this city can say what the fourth refusal is. And a fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-fourth time and a carrier of the second table has said the only sentence anybody in this city has said about why the city has not sent the eight names, and the sentence is that a name in a case can be carried. And the licence is sixty-three days old and the seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken and the dressed edge is dressed. And the stone in the arch said nothing today, and there have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and a morning spent standing next to a board with no names on it is the twenty-second silence, and the pattern is twenty-two for twenty-two.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-third day of the month after this one is nineteen words said in about four seconds in a market by a woman who is on nothing and is named on nothing and has now said no three times in a hundred and thirteen days, and the nineteen words are on a board in a room and the board does not travel, and that is the whole of the protection and the whole of the trouble.**
