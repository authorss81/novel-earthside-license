# Chapter 0114 — A Column with No Room in It

On the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and fourteenth day of the flood and a Wednesday, forty men stood under the near gate at the second hour of the morning in the wrong weather and did the work, and have done every working day since the first of March, which was the sixtieth day of the new month and the eighty-fifth day of the flood, and it is twenty-nine days since any of them was paid anything, and there is no column in four hundred miles that can say they are owed it, and one man who has said the same four words under that gate every working day since the forty-sixth day of the new month did not come this morning, and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said thirteen words about him and nobody improved on them.

**The licence is sixty-four days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-fifth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirteen days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the cost column has eleven entries in one month, and there is no figure on the end of any of the eleven, and there is no twelfth, and the reason there is no twelfth is that the join stopped being worked on the first of March, and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason, and it is on the page in a notary of fifty's hand and nobody has ever found a way round it.**

The column is on a board that hangs on a post by the gate and has hung there for a hundred and fourteen days. There is a date against every line. There is a hand against every line. **There is a space at the bottom of the column for a twelfth line and there is a rule drawn under it, and the rule is ruled, and it is empty, and it is the only thing on that board anybody has looked at twice in a month.**

"It is not a matter of room," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**They would have squeezed it in. I have seen four hundred men squeeze a line in where there was no line.**"

"No," said a woman of twenty-four. "**It is a matter of what a column is. The eleventh entry was made on the fifty-eighth day of the new month and the twelfth would have to be made on the first of March, and the join stopped being worked on the first of March, and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped, and a cost that has stopped may not be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason, and that rule was written by four people who had been asked twice, and if we put a twelfth line in that column tonight then one of two things happens. Either we are saying the join was worked on the first of March, which is a lie, or we are saying the join was not worked and therefore there was no cost, and that is worse, because a lie about work is a lie about men.**"

"So they are not owed it," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Nobody can enter that they are owed anything. That is not the same as them not being owed it, and this city has spent a hundred and fourteen days being extremely careful about that difference on about nine separate occasions, and this is the first time in a hundred and fourteen days that the difference has had forty men standing under it.**"

---

**And the forty men came, and they came at the second hour, and there were thirty-nine of them, and they have not been paid since the first of March, and nobody at the gate said anything about it to any of them, because there is nothing at the gate to say.**

They came the second hour and they worked the wall side of the road and a carter came the other way with a barrow of reed and gave them the wall side without being asked, the way a carter has given that side of that road to about nine people for a month. **A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf was at the gate with a barrow of her own and gave out eleven cups of something hot to about eleven of them without asking one of them a question, and it is entered that no man of the forty said thank you, and that this was not resented by anybody, and that a girl of seventeen entered both of those things.**

"They are not going to say it," said a woman of twenty-four. "**If they say it they are asking, and if they are asking then somebody is going to have to write it down, and there is nowhere to write it down, and so the correct thing for forty men who have not been paid for twenty-nine days to do is turn up, and they are all going to turn up tomorrow, and the day after that, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can stop them and nobody in four hundred miles who can pay them, and both of those are true on the same morning.**"

"Then say it for them," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then I am a woman of twenty-four saying that forty men are owed a fortnight, and my name is on nothing and I am not on a roll, and a thing a person says on a gate post is a thing a person said on a gate post, and in about a quarter there is a case going down a river and a thing somebody said on a gate post is the easiest thing in the world to put in a column.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds. **And a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who has been in nine rooms in fourteen days and has not slept in any of them, said the thing that about four people in that gateway understood and about four did not, and it is in a column four lines high, and the column has no room in it either.**

"A man who is not paid and a man who will not come are two different men," he said. "**And the rule that the first one cannot be entered as owed anything is the rule that makes the second one look like the first one. In a hundred and fourteen days this city has built about nine pages, and every one of them is a place where a person can be found, and there is not one of them that is a place where a person can be missed. So when a man misses us we have nowhere to look, and where there is nowhere to look, whatever we find standing in the gap is what he is. That is not a rule. That is a hole with a man in it.**"

"Nobody put him in it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**No. Nobody put him in it and nothing put him in it and the only thing in this city that did anything to a man who did not come this morning is a column with a rule drawn under the eleventh line. And the rule is empty, and it has been empty for twenty-nine days, and I have been in this city for a hundred and fourteen days and I did not find that out until this morning, and I have read every one of the about nine pages.**"

---

**And then at about the sixth hour of the morning four people went down two hundred steps of a condemned rail with a gauge, because that is what they do twice a day, and the four disagreed for the sixth time since the first of March, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken, and one ruled line in a column is still ruled and still empty, and the sixth time was not about the number.**

It is still twenty-five and has been for nine days, said a woman of fifty-eight, who cannot read, and who reads the join by standing on it twice a day and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading.

"**Twenty-four, and one ruled, and the dressed edge is dressed, and it is the hundred and fourteenth day of this flood, and I am a sixteenth under five times out of six and this morning was not the sixth,**" said a man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew, who reads the number and has said the first half of that sentence on this step every working day since the first of March.

"I am at the same line as both of you," said a woman of seventy who keeps a floor and reads the line, who is seventy and has kept a floor for fifty-one years and was not asked to come and came. "**And I have said the number out loud twice today and I have not been asked to.**"

"**I am a sixteenth under the edge and the edge is the whole of it,**" said a woman of forty-four of Corrow, who reads the edge and who came four miles and was not asked about anything on the way. "**And the difference is not the number. The number is where it was. If you wrote the twenty-fifth down this afternoon you would be writing down that a dressed join has narrowed, and a dressed join does not narrow. It gets dressed. I have said that three days in a row and I am not going to say it again this week for anybody.**"

The sixth entry went in and the fifth entry from before it stayed where it was, and all six of them stand, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken, and the reason is the reason it has not been taken since the first of March. **A man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, was in the room afterwards and did not ask to look at the gauge and was not offered it, and it is entered that about four people noticed that he did not ask and that about four people did not notice anything at all.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening a woman of fifty-eight who cannot read stood at the top of the two hundred steps in the wrong weather and said thirteen words, and a girl of seventeen counted them, and the room did not check the count, and it is entered that the room did not check the count.**

"**A man who has stopped coming is not a cost that has stopped.**"

And then she said the rest of it, and the rest of it is not counted and a notary of fifty, who was not there, entered three days later that she had not counted it on purpose.

"**The foreman of the day crew has said four words under that gate every working day since the forty-sixth day of the new month, which was the seventy-first day of this flood, and the four words are *mind the low stone*, and he said them to forty men and to nobody else, and he said them this morning to nobody, and the reason he did not say them is that he is not here. And the entry that would be made about a man who does not turn up is that he has not been paid, and there is nowhere to make that entry, and so the correct reading of this morning is that a man who has not been paid is a man who has stopped coming, and that is the wrong reading, and it is wrong in the direction that is easiest for everybody in this city, and that is why thirteen words is all I have got.**"

"Who is going to tell him," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Nobody. That is the whole of it. In a hundred and fourteen days this city has worked out how to say a thing in a room where two hundred and forty people can hear it, and it has not worked out how to say a thing to one man who is not in the room, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles for that either, and a man of thirty-two has told us for six days that the only thing this city has is a room, and he is right, and there are thirty-nine men in that room this morning and one man who was not in it and who has not been told.**"

It is entered that a girl of seventeen wrote the four words on the back of a slate in her own hand because a docker of fifty-eight cannot write them, and that she did not put the foreman's name on it, and that nobody asked her to.

---

**And nothing else happened on the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one. The thirty-nine men did their day. A man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew, said in the yard that he is a sixteenth under five times out of six and is going to be a sixteenth under on the day somebody writes the twenty-fifth, and that he will not read it alone. And a hearing is in forty days, and there is one hearing and one clock, and nine people said out loud they will stand in it and the number is in a book with no heading and no date. And a field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid and has not been paid since the first of March, which is the eighty-fifth day of the flood, and twenty-nine days ago. And the fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-fifth time. And the licence is sixty-four days old and the seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody. And the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and thirteen days and was not opened today in front of forty men and about nine other people. And the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch said nothing today. And there have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and a day on which forty men turn up unpaid and one does not is the twenty-third silence, and the pattern is twenty-three for twenty-three.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one is a rule with no room left in it, and a ruled line at the bottom of a column on a board by a gate, and it is still ruled, and it is still empty, and thirty-nine men went home at the seventh hour and one man did not come at the second hour, and neither of those two facts is on the board, and the board is the only instrument in four hundred miles that this city built for this.**
