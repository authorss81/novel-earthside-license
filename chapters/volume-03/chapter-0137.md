# Chapter 0137 — A Room, on a Day He Did Not Choose

On the forty-seventh day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirty-seventh day of the flood and a Friday, about nine people sat in a room over a market in daylight with a date on it, every one of whom had been given the question four days beforehand in writing, and a Hearthguard of forty-four of the outer ward was asked about his own eight for the first time in a hundred and thirty-seven days, on a day he did not choose, and he said no, and the no was entered, and the asking was entered, and the asking is the whole of it.

**The licence is eighty-seven days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty-two days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventy-eighth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirty-six days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

He had asked for it, and that is the part that has been got wrong for a hundred and thirty-seven days.

**On the thirtieth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of this flood, in daylight, in about two hundred and forty people, because the fifth item of the form on the nail is a question and not a duty, a Hearthguard of forty-four said no to the form and yes to a yard, and the two were entered separately, and then he asked to be asked again on a different day, and said that he would say no again for about four years.**

The count is **twenty-seven days**, counted from the twenty-first day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and eleventh day of this flood, and it is the twenty-seventh day in a row on which nobody in this city has asked him anything about his own eight in a room on a different day, and nobody counted it out loud until a woman of twenty-four read it out of a book and then said the number twice to be sure of it.

---

**And the asking was done properly, and doing it properly took four days and about nine people's whole afternoons, and it is entered that it is the first instrument for asking anybody in this city has ever built, and that it was built in a hundred and thirty-seven days by a city that had spent a hundred and thirty-six days refusing to.**

The four days were these. A question, in the words of the person it is about, written out by a girl of seventeen who copies things and is on no roll, given to every person who would be in the room four days before the day, and **if anybody could not be given it then they were not going to be in the room.** A room over a market with a door and a window and about nine chairs, which is nine chairs, and the ninth chair is the only one that is spoken for and nobody put a name on it. A date on a slate. **And nobody asked him whether the day suited him, because that would have been an asking, and it would have been the first asking, and it would have been on a day he chose.**

"**Who chose the day,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, afterwards.

"**A wall does,**" said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, who had done it. "**There is a duty rota and a tide and a market on a Friday and I did not ask him anything about any of it. If you want a man asked properly then the day has to be a day somebody else needed him in anyway, and the whole of this city got that wrong for a hundred and thirty-seven days.**"

---

**And he came in his own time, off duty, nineteen minutes late, and nobody said anything about the nineteen minutes, and it is entered that about four people in that room wanted to and did not.**

He put the waxed slate on the table. It is the same slate. He has had it for nineteen years and it has a number on it that he calls every eleven minutes and it is not a page and it goes in his coat.

"**Say the question,**" he said. "**In the words on the slate. And then nobody improve on it, and I have read the words on the slate and they are the words and I have not changed them and I would like that said first.**"

The words on the slate were these, and they had been given to about nine people four days before, and the giving was the work:

> **Hettis Roke, keeper of the ground behind the ordinary wall, is asked: the eight names your grandfather entered in the roll of the dead of the outer gate, and the eight stones, and the four names cut on the stones of people buried with them — where are they, and what became of them, and who may say so.**

Four days is enough to be frightened of a question in. He read it out once. It is his own account of it, in his own words, and it is a page a person can be angry at.

---

**And he said no, and it took about four seconds, and the four seconds are the same four seconds as on the thirtieth day of the new month, and nobody in that room improved on them or was pleased with them or told him he was brave.**

"**No.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**Say the rest,**" said a man of about thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything.

"**I said I would say no for about four years and it is a hundred and thirty-seven days and I am not going to be interesting about it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I will say why, because you were all given it in writing and you have had four days and you are owed one thing, and it is the same reason and it is not a better one than the yard.**"

---

**And he said it, in about forty seconds, in the local language, in a room with the door open, in daylight, on a day he did not choose, and it is on the page and it is not on any other page, and about nine people heard it and it did not travel.**

"**The names were written down once. In his hand, in my great-grandfather's hand, in the year the ground was made, because a Hearthguard who held a gate gave a roll of the dead of that gate twice a year and it went down a river in a wooden case. Eight got a stone. Four got a name cut on the stone of a person they were buried with, because there was not enough money for eight. The book went to Aurel. It went four times a year for nineteen years and a clerk on a quay wrote the count in a book and the count was eight every year. Nobody came.**"

He put his hand flat on the table the way a notary does and not the way a Hearthguard does, and it is entered that nobody noticed that and everybody remembered it.

"**That is the yard, word for word, and I have given it to about two hundred and forty people once and to nine people in a room, and it is the same sentence and it is not better in here and I would like it entered that I know that, because the difference is not in the sentence. The difference is that there are nine people in this room who were each given the question four days ago and one of them can be asked whether the asking was proper, and nobody at the end of a yard can be.**"

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the thing that a hundred and thirty-seven days of this city have been walking towards without knowing that they were walking towards it, and there is a person standing in it, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it, and nobody improved on it, and it is the finding of the forty-seventh day of the month after this one.**

"**I have wanted for a hundred and thirty-seven days to say that the eight on a boundary page a hundred and forty years old and your grandfather's eight are the same eight, and I have had four chances this week to say it and I have not said it, and I am saying now that I am not going to, and the reason is the reason for everything I have said in a hundred and thirty-seven days.**"

"**They are the same eight,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**There are three eights in four hundred miles. Yours is eight stones in a ground. The one in the book that came into this city is eight names at the foot of a page with one place for the count and one place for the assent and they are the same place. And there is a third one, and it is a number that has been kept up for nineteen years by a clerk on a quay writing eight in a book. That is three eights and a hundred and thirty-seven days, and I have wanted them to be one for a hundred and thirty-seven days, and a thing a man wants is not a thing a man knows.**"

"Then what is it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**It is that a number can be maintained for a hundred and forty years and names cannot. That is all I am prepared to say and it is on the page and I would like the room to notice that it is the first thing I have said in a hundred and thirty-seven days that anybody has not already worked out.**"

---

**And the asking was entered with the date on it, and the name of the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf went at the top of it, because a page with a person's name at the top of it is the oldest protection there is and it is also the thing that can be used against the person, and she asked for it herself in about four seconds. And not one word of what he said was entered anywhere, in any book, on any board, in any stone, in any column, and that is the shape of the whole thing and it is the same shape as a slate on the back of a shut stall and a slate with a figure on the end of a line, and it is entered that about four people in this city worked that out separately in a hundred and thirty-seven days and nobody has improved on the other three.**

**And nobody thanked him. He said before he went that he would like it entered that he was not going to be thanked, and that if anybody thanked him he would have to think about it for a week, and a woman of twenty-four said the only thing anybody in that room was going to say, which was that the count of days was twenty-seven and it stood where it stood and it was not going to be counted again, because the debt was a debt about a person and not a page and it had been paid on the forty-seventh day of the month after this one in a room in daylight with a date on it.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the forty-seventh day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in seventeen days and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there twenty-one days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and it names nobody and it is still on the water. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is twenty-five days ago, and the water has not been received for sixty-eight days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is fifty-two days, and one of the forty has not come for twenty-three days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for fifty-two days, and six disagreements stand. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty-seven days. And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-four, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is eleven, and it has never been made smaller. And a Friday on which a man of forty-four was asked a question he wrote himself, in a room, on a day he did not choose, and said no, and nobody thanked him, is the forty-fourth silence and the pattern is forty-four for forty-four.**

**And the last thing of the forty-seventh day of the month after this one is a slate with one name at the top of it and no word of his in it, and twenty-seven days that are not going to be counted again, and a hearing in seventeen days.**
