# Chapter 0115 — A Number Is Not a Roll

On the twenty-fifth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and fifteenth day of the flood and a Thursday, about nine people spent four hours in a room over a market doing arithmetic about a hearing in thirty-nine days, got as far as a list inside about nine minutes, worked out in about nine minutes that a list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is, crossed the list out, entered a number instead, asked a woman of seventy to be that number, were refused by her in about four seconds, and entered the refusal and did not ask her again.

**The licence is sixty-five days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-sixth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and fourteen days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the thirty-nine days had thirty-nine things in them, because about nine people had spent a morning writing them down, and then they ran out of days before they ran out of things, and a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall said the sentence that made the rest of the day.**

"Thirty-nine days," he said. "**And we have thirty-nine things. That is a list. That is the only thing thirty-nine days and thirty-nine things makes. I have been in about nine rooms in fourteen days and every one of them ended with somebody writing down what we are going to do, and not one of those nine pages has ever been a list, and I would like one.**"

"Write it," said a woman of twenty-four.

He wrote it. **It took him about nine minutes and it went to twenty-two lines and there were nine names on it in a column on the left and thirty-nine days in a column on the right, and he put a date at the top of it because he is the sort of man who puts dates at the top of things, and a girl of seventeen read it for about nineteen seconds and then put her hand flat on it.**

"You have written a roll," she said.

"It is a list."

"**It is a list with a column on the left and the column is where nine people are, and in this world a column is a roll, and a roll goes to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year, and a quarter is about thirteen weeks, and the next case goes out about five weeks after the hearing that this list is for. So this piece of paper is the ninth thing this city has built that cannot leave the room and you have built it in nine minutes on a Thursday afternoon.**"

Nobody argued. **It is entered that about four people in that room said the sentence about a list about nine seconds apart and that a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall said both of them.**

"And it is not that we must not write it," said a girl of seventeen. "**A list of things to do is a list of things to do and there are about nine of those in this city. It is that the left-hand column is a place where nine people are, and if that goes on a page with a date on it then in a quarter it is a description with a column for where each of us is, and I have known that since the sixteenth day of the month after this one and I have not been able to say it out loud in a room without somebody else saying it first, and today I was the one who said it first.**"

"Can I keep it," said the man of the stall, "**with the names off it.**"

"You can keep it with the left-hand column off it," she said, "**and then it is thirty-nine things and no people, and I will copy the left-hand column into my book in a column with no heading and no date, which is where it already is from Sunday, and neither of those two pages is going anywhere.**"

He kept it. **It is entered that a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall has never in his life crossed a line out of a list and that he did it in about four seconds and that he said the reason was that he had put a name on it and that he did not want a name of his on it.**

---

**And the number was entered at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, with a date on it, and it is nine, and for the first time in a hundred and fifteen days there is a page in this city with a number at the top of it and no person at the top of that, and a woman of twenty-four said out loud that this is the opposite of the most dangerous page in this city and that the two of them are the same kind of page and the reason one of them worked and the other one cannot is that this one has nobody on it.**

"Nine," said a girl of seventeen, and wrote it, and put the date on it, and did not put a name on it.

"That is a page," said a woman of twenty-four.

"That is a number with a date on it."

"**It is a page with a heading and a date and a number and no person, and the reason a page that can end a document is the most dangerous object in this city is that a person was standing at the top of it and nobody asked him whether he would have it. This one has nobody at the top of it. That is the difference. A number cannot be asked whether it will have it. A page cannot. A person can, and we have had that argument in this room on the seventeenth day of the month after this one and it has not been improved on since, and it is the whole of what is left.**"

"And the number is not a roll," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**The number is not a roll because a roll has to be a place where a person is, and nine is not a place where anybody is. Nine is a quantity. When that hearing sits down, whoever is in the room will count the people in it, and if there are nine of us then there will be nine, and if there are eight then there will be eight, and the count will be true whichever it is, and that is the only kind of page in four hundred miles that cannot be wrong by being looked at.**"

"Entered," said a girl of seventeen. **And it is entered that about four people in that room said that this was the first entry anybody had made in a hundred and fifteen days that nobody could be angry at, and that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said that it was also the first entry that nobody could correct, and that the two of those were the same thing and that she had said on a road four days ago that they were.**

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the part of it that the room had been building towards for an hour, and it took him about nine minutes, and he was not the only person in the room who had worked it out, and he said so, and then he said it anyway, and about four people let him finish.**

"**We have spent a hundred and fifteen days learning that a person is the only shape that can be named, and so the only shape that can be found, and so the moment somebody agrees to carry a cost this city writes down where the cost is. That is the law and it is about nine days old and it is correct. And every one of us in this room has been applying it to a household all morning without noticing, and a household is a person.**"

"So a household can be asked," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**A household can be asked and a household can refuse, and that is the road. Eleven households keep nine miles of it and nobody asked them, and a village of nine households has been asked three times and refused three times, and a woman of seventy told us on Monday that the fourth is a thing a person does and not a thing a village does. And that is the whole of the two institutions in this city that are going to still be here in four months, and they are the same shape, and the shape is a household, and the reason neither of them can be carried is that a household is a person and a person has to stand there.**"

"Then we cannot ask the nine," said the man of the stall.

"**We cannot ask nine households to stand in a room nine hundred miles away, and we cannot ask nine people either, because every one of those nine people is somebody's household in the way that matters, and I have watched a village find that out in two months and I am not going to tell a man of thirty-nine at a gate to find it out in a building nine hundred miles away in forty days.**"

"Then what do we enter," said the man of the stall.

"**The number. Nine, with a date on it, and no names, and nobody at the top of it. And if the room in Aurel wants to know who, then in about eight weeks it will be told who by the nine people in it, in daylight, to their faces, in a room, where about two hundred and forty people of that district can hear them say no. And that is the only instrument in four hundred miles that is left and it is the reason a hearing is a room.**"

---

**And at about the sixth hour of the evening about nine people went and asked a woman of seventy to be the number, and it was a Thursday, and she was washing a floor, and she said no in about four seconds without stopping, and the reason was about herself and not about the number, and nobody in about nine people asked her a second question and nobody asked her again for the rest of that day.**

Nyla Ferrow has kept the floor of the lock chapel for fifty-one years. **She washes it on Thursdays. She has re-limed it twice since the first day of the month after this one. She is one of the four who read the join-line and reads the line, and she is on one page in a stone nine miles up a road exactly once, and she is the only one of the nine witnesses whose name may be taken back.**

"You want me to be the number," she said.

"**We want one person to say it out loud, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, so that in about eight weeks somebody cannot say nobody in this city ever claimed anything,**" said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"No."

And it is entered that she said it in about four seconds without stopping, and that she did not put down the cloth, and that the reason took about nineteen seconds and that nobody improved on it, and that a girl of seventeen wrote all of it out and did not put the number nine at the top of the page because it was not hers to put there.

"**I have kept a floor for fifty-one years and I have done it by being a person nobody can find, and that is not modesty, it is the job. There is a form on a nail in that chapel with five things on it and a price under them and the fifth thing is a question and not a duty, and I have not been asked anything in a hundred and fifteen days and I am seventy and I intend to go on not being asked for as long as there is anything left to not be asked about. And if I am your number then the number is a person, and a person can be asked whether the number is right, and in about eight weeks there will be somebody in a room nine hundred miles away asking me. I have read a form four times in nine years and I know exactly what happens the first time somebody is asked a second time.**"

"**You are not being asked to be a seat,**" said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who has refused a seat five times.

"**Then I would take it, and I am not going to, and you have not asked me anything, and I am not going to help you out.**"

Nobody asked her again that day. **It is entered that about four people in that room wanted to argue with her and did not, and that a woman of twenty-four said afterward that the correct thing to do about a refusal given by a person who has never been asked anything in a hundred and fifteen days is to let it stand and go away, and that a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said that a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused, and that both of those were the same sentence, and that nobody improved on either.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the twenty-fifth day of the month after this one, and the four went down two hundred steps at the seventh hour and did not take the twenty-fifth reading, which is the fifth day in a row that they have not taken it, and one ruled line is still ruled and still empty, and the sixth disagreement stands with the other five, and the dressed edge is dressed. And a hearing is in thirty-nine days, and there is one hearing and one clock, and the number nine is entered on a page with a date on it and no person at the top of it, and it is not a roll and it is not going to Aurel in a case. And a village of nine households refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day and nobody can say what that is. And a board by the near gate has a ruled line at the bottom of it that is still empty, and thirty-nine men turned up and one did not. And the fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-sixth time. And the licence is sixty-five days old and the seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody. And the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch said nothing today. And there have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and a Thursday on which a woman of seventy said no in four seconds is the twenty-fourth silence, and the pattern is twenty-four for twenty-four.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-fifth day of the month after this one is a page with a number on it and a date on it and nobody at the top of it, and it is the safest page in this city and the only one anybody here can be certain of, and about four feet away from it a woman of seventy was washing a floor and will not be asked again.**
