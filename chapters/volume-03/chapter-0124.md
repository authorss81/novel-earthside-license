# Chapter 0124 — Two Empty Places

On the thirty-fourth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twenty-fourth day of the flood and a Thursday, four people went down two hundred steps of a river wall in the wrong weather on a rail that is condemned and did not take the twenty-fifth reading, and a man of thirty-one with a right hand that will not close came down with them and held a gauge in the crook of his arm and said four words nobody asked him for, and a Hearthguard of forty-four stood on the landing and was not asked anything by anybody, and the finding of the thirty-fourth day of the month after this one is that an empty ruled line and a missing man are the same shape and that this city has spent thirty-nine days protecting one of them and has no rule at all for the other.

**The licence is seventy-four days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is thirty-nine days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixty-fifth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty-three days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the twenty-fifth reading was not taken on the thirty-fourth day of the month after this one, and one ruled line in a column is still ruled and still empty, and it has been empty for thirty-nine days, and a dressed join does not narrow and it gets dressed, and nobody in this city has taken the twenty-fifth reading and nobody is going to take it today.**

There are twenty-four readings in the column and the last two are both eight and seven-eighths, and there are two steps in it in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, which were the hundred and third and hundred and ninth day of this flood, and there is one ruled line left.

It is still eight and seven-eighths, said a woman of fifty-eight, who cannot read, and who reads the join by standing on it twice a day and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading.

"**Twenty-four, and one ruled, and the dressed edge is dressed, and it is the hundred and twenty-fourth day, and I am a sixteenth under five times out of six and this morning was not the sixth,**" said a man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew, who reads the number and has said the first half of that sentence on this step every working day since the first of March. "**And the sixth time was not about the number. I have said for ten days that it was not about the number and nobody has written down which of the six was mine.**"

"You have never claimed one," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**I have never claimed one and I am not going to, and a man of fifty who says the reading is a sixteenth under five times out of six is a man who is telling you the number and telling you nothing about the edge, and those are two different pieces of work and I do them both.**"

"I am at the same line as both of you," said a woman of seventy who keeps a floor and reads the line, and who is seventy and has kept a floor for fifty-one years and was not asked to come and came. "**And I have said the number out loud twice today and I have not been asked to.**"

"**I am a sixteenth under the edge and the edge is the whole of it,**" said a woman of forty-four of Corrow, who reads the edge and who came four miles and was not asked about anything on the way. "**And the difference is not the number. The number is where it was. If you wrote the twenty-fifth down this afternoon you would be writing down that a dressed join has narrowed. I have said that six times since the first of March and I am not going to say it a seventh time this month for anybody.**"

The seventh entry did not go in. The sixth stays where it was with the other five, and all six stand, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken, and it is the thirty-ninth day since the first of March that they have not taken it.

---

**And the man of thirty-one came down the two hundred steps at about the seventh hour of the morning with a gauge in the crook of his left arm, and he is Teodor Blaz, of the second list, and his right hand has not made a fist since the twenty-fifth day of this flood, and he was asked to carry it and he said yes, and nobody in about nine people said anything about it afterwards, and it is entered that four of them noticed and that four of them did not notice anything at all.**

He had not been up here before. **He said so himself, on the top step, in about nine seconds, and he said that a warehouse supervisor in a city nine hundred miles away counted things for a living and was good at it, and that he came up this morning because a man of fifty had said in a yard nine days ago that he would not read the twenty-fifth alone, and a man of fifty who says that is a man expecting to be asked for help by somebody he has never asked.**

"You were not asked," said a man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew.

"**Nobody,**" said Teodor Blaz, "**and that is the whole of it and it is why I am here, and I can hold a gauge in the crook of an arm and I cannot hold it in a hand, and the four of you have spent thirty-nine days not agreeing about an edge on purpose, and I have spent ninety-nine days not being able to close a hand, and neither of those has anybody standing next to it.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

"You cannot hold that," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I cannot hold that,**" he said, "**and I have said that in a room four times in a hundred and twenty-four days and I have said it in exactly the same words four times, and I would like somebody to notice that a man who cannot hold a thing is still the man holding it, and that the reason nobody in this city has ever asked me about that is that I am not a page and a page is the only shape anybody here knows how to be responsible for.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"You have been on nine of these walls," said a woman of twenty-four, who is on no roll of anything.

"**Nine. And I have read a number on some of them, and I have stood on this one and told you whether it has moved, and neither of those is reading, and both of those are the reason the four are four and I am not one of them. A person who can read is a person who can be asked whether the number is right. A person who cannot read is a person who is asked whether it has moved, and that question has an answer that is not a number.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board asked a question on that landing that nobody in this city has asked, and it took him about four seconds, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out and did not improve on it, and a man of thirty-one answered it out loud without being asked for it.**

"Can the four make a rule about a man."

"Nobody asked a question," said a man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew.

"**I asked one,**" said a man of about thirty-nine. "**You have a rule for the line. It is not filled unless all four of you agree where the edge is, and you wrote that rule yourselves, and nobody outside this city checks that you are keeping it, and you have kept it for thirty-nine days and it has cost you one reading. And I have been in nine rooms in ten days and every one of them had a rule in it and a page with the rule on it, and there is not one of them with a rule for a man who has not turned up. So I am asking it properly, in a room, on a day I did not choose. Can the four make a rule about a man.**"

"**I do not know,**" said Teodor Blaz.

And it is entered that he said it in about four seconds and that nobody had asked him, and that about four people in that place understood in about four seconds that the sentence had not been about the four, and that nobody said so, and that a girl of seventeen did not count the four words because nobody counts them any more.

"A rule about a thing is not a rule about the world," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who came down the two hundred steps at the third hour of the afternoon and was not offered the gauge and did not ask for it. "**A rule about a document is a rule about a document, and I said that in a room on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one and it is on a page, and it is about documents. A rule about a man is not a rule about a document and it is not a rule about a thing, because a man is not one person. A man is a person this morning, and a man who does not turn up is a different man, and a man who has not been paid for thirty-nine days is a third one, and the rule that the first one cannot be entered as owing anything is the rule that makes the second one look like the third one.**"

"That is what a docker said nine days ago," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**That is what a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said on the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one in thirteen words, and a girl of seventeen counted them and the room did not check her count, and I have known it for nine days and I have not been able to do anything with it, and this morning a man of thirty-nine has asked the question out loud and that is different from me knowing it.**"

---

**And a Hearthguard of forty-four of the outer ward, nineteen years in, stood on the landing at about the fifth hour of the afternoon in the wrong weather for about forty minutes and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is entered with the date on it, and it is the fourteenth day in a row, and nobody in that place said so to him.**

He said **I do not know** on the nineteenth day of the month after this one, in a room, and that is four words, and it is on the page, and nobody improved on it and nobody turned it into a yes. **Nobody may ask him about his own eight until he is asked properly, in a room, on a different day, and it was not asked today on a stair in the wrong weather in front of about nine people, and about four people in that place wanted to and did not.**

**And the four words a foreman of a day crew has said under the near gate every working day since the seventy-first day of this flood are *mind the low stone*, and they are on the back of a slate in a girl's book with no name on them, and the slate went up that stair this morning because the girl of seventeen is the only person in this city who copies things and who is on no roll, and nobody asked her to bring it and she said on the way up that she had brought it because she was going to be asked anyway.**

Nobody was asked anything about the slate. **And it is entered that a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read had the four words read out to her twice and said nothing about them, and that a man of about thirty-nine looked at the four words for about nine seconds and then asked a different question, and that about four people in that place thought he had looked at the four words to decide whether to put a name on them, and that he had not, and never said so.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the thirty-fourth day of the month after this one, and a hearing is in thirty days and there is one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after the hearing has finished. And thirty-nine of forty men turned up under the near gate at the second hour of the morning and have not been paid since the first of March, which is thirty-nine days, and the column is full and nobody can enter that they are owed anything, and the foreman has not come for ten days. And a box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one and it is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and it is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a nineteen-line page headed *the box as received* is in a girl's book and has a name at the top of it since yesterday. And a page in a stone nine miles up a road has a line in it that is now wrong and the household that keeps that stone has never been asked what it thinks. And a page that ends a document is spent and five rooms have failed to explain it in the same place. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and twenty-four days, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty-four days. And there have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one has been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-three, and a Thursday on which two empty places were looked at in the same weather is the thirty-second silence and the pattern is thirty-two for thirty-two.**

**And the last thing of the thirty-fourth day of the month after this one is a ruled line at the bottom of a column that has been empty for thirty-nine days because four people would not agree about an edge, and a gate on a road where a fifth place has been empty for nine days because nobody in this city can say what a man wants, and both of them are empty for entirely different reasons, and the difference is that one of them has a person standing next to it and the other one does not, and a hearing is in thirty days.**
