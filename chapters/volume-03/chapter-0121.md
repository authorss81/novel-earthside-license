# Chapter 0121 — Standing Near a Thing

On the thirty-first day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twenty-first day of the flood and a Wednesday, a wooden box with a strap and a flat wax and a second strap stood on the floor of a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market, and it had been standing there for five days, and about nine people had come up a stair at the back of that building in those five days and stood near it and not touched it, and the finding of the thirty-first day of the month after this one is that a thing nobody is waiting for is a thing everybody is standing near, and standing near a thing is a practice, and a practice is a toll.

**The licence is seventy-one days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is thirty-six days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixty-second time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the room was not built for a box, and the stair goes past a hall where nine people eat, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf worked that out on the second day and has not stopped.**

It is a stair at the back of a building over a market and it goes up past the kitchen and past the long table and past the door of the room at the front where two of the arrivals sleep in turns. **Whoever comes up it to look at a box walks through a hall with nine people in it, and about four people have worked out in five days that this is the reason there have been nine people on that stair and not three, and nobody has said it, and it has been said anyway by the amount of traffic.**

On the second day somebody put a chair on the landing outside that door. It came from the hall below, it is not on any page, and nobody asked for it. **It is the only object in four hundred miles that this city built for the purpose of standing near a thing, and it has no price on it and no date on it and no right of refusal, and a person can sit down in it and get up out of it and that is the whole of it.**

---

**And the toll is on a page, and the page is nineteen lines long, and the page is in a girl's book, and every person who stands near that box on the thirty-first day of the month after this one is adding to what the page is.**

A pale field came up in a room with a door on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood, and a notary of fifty accepted it in about four seconds without thinking about it, and what it said was that a thing that has come up a river may be described by any person who has seen it, and that the person who describes it is not described by it, and that the person is not on any roll, and that **the cost is that the description is the only account of the thing that will exist.** The cost was entered. There is no further cost.

**The cost arrived on the twenty-seventh day in about four hours, and it is a page with a heading on it, and the heading is *the box as received*, and it is nineteen lines, and three of the nineteen are about the box and sixteen are about nine people.**

"It is not a bad description," said a notary of fifty, on the twenty-seventh day, in about nineteen seconds. "**It is not a description. It is a page about the people who have been near it.**"

**And the finding of the thirty-first day of the month after this one is that a page about the people who have been near a thing gets a line for every person who comes near it, and that nobody in this city is going to stop coming near it, and that the chair on the landing is the reason, and that a chair with no page on it is the most successful instrument anybody in four hundred miles has built this month.**

Nineteen lines. Sixteen of them about nine people. **A twentieth line is going to be somebody standing on that landing today with a reason for being there, and the reason is the only part of the standing that anybody outside this city will ever be told about, and the box is not in the reason and the wax is not in the reason.**

"I am not going up there again," said a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, at the fourth hour of the afternoon, on the stair. "**I have been up that stair three times in five days and I have not touched it and I am not going to touch it and I am not going up again, because every time I go up it somebody writes down where I was standing, and I cannot read, and I do not know what it says about me, and a thing I cannot read about myself is a thing about somebody else.**"

Nobody argued. **It is entered that a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said on the thirty-first day of the month after this one that she is not going up a stair again, and that nobody asked her why, and that a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall wrote it in a column in his own book, and that the word *herself* is in it.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two came up that stair at about the fifth hour of the afternoon on the thirty-first day of the month after this one, and stood near the box for about nine minutes, and did not touch it, and did not read the writing round the edge of the wax.**

He did not read it. **He stood four feet back from the door with his hands in his pockets and looked at the flat of the wax from four feet away, which is the only thing he can do in this world, and he did not read the writing round the edge of it, and nobody in that stairwell asked him to, and nobody has read that wax in five days and one man in this city could have.**

"You have been up here three times," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, who was on the landing in the chair and who is not on a roll of anything.

"Three times," he said. "**And I have not read the wax and I am not going to read the wax, and that is the third time in six days I have said a version of it and I am not going to say it again this week.**"

"I am not asking you about the wax," she said.

"Then what is it."

"**It is that there is a chair on this landing and nobody put a date on it, and there is nobody going to take it away, and in about four months somebody is going to come up this stair and find a chair on a landing and a room with a door and a strap that is not tied, and there will be no page anywhere in four hundred miles that says why. A chair is a page nobody can be angry at. I have been standing in this city for a hundred and twenty-one days refusing to put my name on a board, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the thing this city has actually built in five days is a chair.**"

He said nothing for about nineteen seconds.

"**Then put a date on the chair,**" he said.

"No," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, in about four seconds. "**A date on the chair is a page, and a page can be taken down on a date, and then it is a chair again, and then in about four months the chair is a chair that nobody can ask anything about. I would rather it be a chair. A chair is the only thing in this city that is allowed to be nothing.**"

It is entered that a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who has no mark of his own and no workway and no title and no seat, was told no in about four seconds by a person who was not refusing him and did not say so, and that a girl of seventeen wrote the whole of it out, and that he did not add anything, and that it is the first time in a hundred and twenty-one days that anybody has done that to him.

---

**And a notary of fifty was asked for the fifth time in five days on the thirty-first day of the month after this one, and she was not asked, because the question was written out and not delivered, and that is the whole of what happened to it.**

She entered on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one that she would like to be asked before the box is opened, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person, and the reason is that she has been the notary of about nine hundred things and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence. **She has been asked about it four times in four days and has answered none of the four, and the not-knowing is entered with the date on it, and a notary entering that she does not know is the first time in twenty-six years that she has done it.**

On the thirty-first day of the month after this one, at about the sixth hour of the evening, a girl of seventeen read out to that room a question that had been written out on a slate and had not been delivered. **It is in the hand of a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, it is five lines, and it is the fifth asking, and the reason it was not delivered is that a woman of twenty-four said no to the delivering of it in about four seconds and that nobody argued with her, and the reason she gave is that the notary of fifty entered that she does not know, and a person who has entered that she does not know has a thing to protect, and every question put to her this week is a hand on it.**

"Read it out anyway," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I am not going to deliver it and I will read it out so that it exists, on a slate and in a book that cannot travel and not in a room,**" said a woman of twenty-four. "**Those are two different things and about four people in this city keep confusing them, and the difference is the whole of what a hundred and twenty-one days has been for.**"

**It is entered that the five lines were read out in the local language in a room over a market with about nine people in it, and that the notary of fifty was not in that room, and that nobody in it sent the five lines to her, and that about four of the nine wanted to, and that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said in about four seconds that wanting to is not a reason, and that she had said no three times in a hundred and twenty-one days.**

**And nobody in this city has asked her a sixth time and nobody is going to this week, and the not-knowing is the entry, and it has a date on it, and it is the only entry in a hundred and twenty-one days that anybody in this city has made about themselves and then let stand.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the thirty-first day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in thirty-three days and there is one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after that hearing has finished, and a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and no name in it, and it is still on the water and it may not be taken back, and what comes back from it will be a correct number about a district with eight people not in it. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one and it is nine days old, and nobody in this city can say what a person's fourth refusal is. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is thirty-six days, and the column is full and nobody can enter that they are owed anything, and one of the forty has not come for a week and there is no instrument in four hundred miles for telling a man who is not in the room. And a page in a stone nine miles up a road is nineteen lines long and it says the count of seven is a count and not a debt, and a household may take any of its three pages down on a date. And a page that ends a document is spent and is in a lockable room off the salt store with the door shut, and five rooms have failed to explain it and all five failed in the same place. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for thirty-six days, and the four have disagreed six times since the first of March and all six entries stand. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and twenty-one days, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty-one days. And there have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and the refusals in a clerk of the Registry's second book are twenty-three, and a Wednesday on which nine people stood near a box is the twenty-ninth silence and the pattern is twenty-nine for twenty-nine.**

**And the last thing of the thirty-first day of the month after this one is a chair on a landing outside a room with a door, with no date on it and no page under it and nobody able to say why it is there, and about four feet inside that door a strap is still untied on a box that came up this river five days ago for the first time in a hundred and forty years, and nobody has read the writing on the wax, and a hearing is in thirty-three days.**
