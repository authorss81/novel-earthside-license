# Chapter 0106 — The Reason Nobody Can Answer

On the sixteenth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixth day of the flood and a Tuesday, about nine people put two pages side by side in a room over a market — a copy of the second page in the shrine at Four Elms, and the eighth column of a quarterly description that went down a river eight days ago — and found out the reason nobody in this city can answer the question in that column, and it is that this city has spent a hundred and six days not writing names down, and the not-writing was the protection, and it is now the reason, and a man of forty-four who asked for the question instead of his own name said so first and was not absolved by anybody in the room.

**The licence is fifty-six days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the forty-seventh time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**A copy is a copy, and a girl of seventeen said so in the first thirty seconds, and the copy is in a book and was made on the ninetieth day of the flood by three people at a cost that is entered, and nobody in that room treated it as the stone.**

"I have got the second page in the stone in this book," she said, "**in a hand that is not the hand on the stone, made on the ninetieth day of this flood by me and a woman of twenty-four and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, and it says at the top in my hand that it is a copy and that the stone is nine miles up a road and does not travel. The stone has three pages in it. This book has one of them. If the household takes that page down on a date this book does not change and nobody has to tell us.**"

"Will they take it down," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**They may, on a date, and nobody is asked why twice. That is on the page and it is the same rule as the Wayhouse and it is the rule that makes a page in a stone work at all, and I would like the room to notice that we have just said the whole of the finding out loud and none of us noticed.**"

---

**The second page in the stone is a list of what is owed, owed to twelve named people and not to a body, dated the sixty-fifth day of the new month, which was the ninetieth day of the flood, with a quarter of a pound of salt a month written under the list and not as another item on it.**

Three debts are on it and none of them has been asked for. A woman of forty-eight in a fourth house at Nine Elms is owed one question and it has been entered as unasked on purpose with a date on it. A woman of forty-one is owed the work in her own words in the document that says the work is not written down anywhere. A woman of forty-four is owed a figure on the end of a cost column and a decision about ninety-one acres, and both of those are on one line because they are one woman. That is three lines and three people. And there are nine more lines and the nine are not debts at all; they are the four who read the join-line, a woman of seventy who keeps a floor, a woman of fifty-eight who sets bones, a man of forty-two who has said nine sentences in his life and been right in eight, a man of sixty-one who is owed a sentence from a man who read his book, and a man of thirty-two.

"That is twelve," said a man of about forty-four. "**And it is twelve and not eleven because one woman's two debts are on one line, and a list that enters a person twice is a list of a person and not of a debt, and the reason that is written on the page in a woman's own words is that she has said that a list of a person is not a list of a debt, and it is the only arithmetic anybody has done in a hundred and six days that made a thing smaller.**"

---

**And then the finding of the day was made by a girl of seventeen in about nine minutes out loud, and it is in a column four lines high in her own book, and it is about the shape of the page and not about anybody in it, and it is the thing the whole hundred and six days have been standing on.**

"**This page is a description with a date on it.** The date is the sixty-fifth day of the new month. Twelve people are on it by name. And every name on it is a place where a person is. That is all a description is. A description is a removal with a column for where the person is, and this page is a column for where twelve people are, and it is cut into a stone in a household at a fork, and the keeping household is nobody's servant and there is no key and it goes nowhere."

"So it is a roll," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**It is a roll, and it is the only roll in this city that does not go to Aurel in a wooden case four times a year, and the reason it does not go is that it is nailed inside a stone at a fork nine miles up a road that eleven households keep and that is on no page, and the reason it is on no page is that a page on a wall is a page people can be angry at and a page in a stone cannot, and a woman of sixty-one argued for it on that ground on the hundredth day of the flood and nobody improved on her.**"

"**And a roll that does not go is a roll that nobody can check,**" said a man of forty-four of the outer ward, "**and a document nobody may check is a document nobody may refuse, and we have got one of those already and it is the most dangerous page in this city.**"

"Yes," said the girl of seventeen. "**And that is why I wrote at the top of my copy that it is a copy and the stone does not travel. Because if somebody comes to this book and wants the eight names in the eighth column, and this book has twelve, then this book is a door into a list, and the twelve would be looked at, and the eight would be looked for. So the second page in the stone is the reason this city cannot answer the question, and it is also the only reason there is a stone.**"

---

**And it took about nineteen minutes to get from there to the thing, and the thing was said by the man it is about, and he said it before anybody put it to him, and it is in the book in his own words, and it is eighteen words, and the room did not check the count, which is entered.**

"**I asked for a question instead of a name, and I have got a question I cannot answer.**"

And then he said the rest of it, and the rest of it is not counted and nobody counted it, and a notary of fifty entered that she did not count it on purpose.

"**On the eighth day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-eighth day of the flood, I stood in a room and I asked for the eighth column to be answered with a question and not with my name, and I gave a reason in my own words and nobody improved on it. The question went down a river in a wooden case with a strap and a wax and a second strap. I have had a form four times in nine years and not one question, and I would rather be a question than a name, and I said that in a room and it is in the book.**"

"Yes," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**So here is what I have, and I am not going to be comfortable about it, and I am not going to ask anybody in this room to make me comfortable about it. That question asks which gate, in which year, on whose authority the count of eight was continued after that gate came off the roll, and what the eight names in it are. And the answer to it is in about nine rooms in this city, and the answer is that we have spent a hundred and six days not writing down the names of people.**"

"It was a protection," said a woman of twenty-four, who has not said a kind thing about this city in a hundred and six days.

"**It was a protection and it is the best thing anybody in this city has done since I came here, and I want that said in the same breath and in the same entry and neither of them above the other. The roll of strangers went down the river with a hole in it on purpose and the hole is a place where a number used to be. The second list did not go at all. The seventh column of that same document says *they were described in the quarter last past and nothing has been asked of them since*, and that is the sentence that has kept seventeen people out of a column for a hundred and six days. And on the ninth day of the month after this one a review put four words on a page with a heading and a date, and a dated refusal is an answer, and an answer is a description, and that is on the page and nobody undid it and nobody could.**

"**And about nine hundred miles away there is a clerk with a book, and the book wants eight names in it, and there is exactly one way for that book to be filled, and the way is that a place sends the names. And we have spent a hundred and six days being the only place in the world that does not.**"

---

**And nobody absolved him, and he did not ask them to, and it is entered that about four people in that room understood in about four seconds that a man who has been right for nine years has just found out what his rightness cost, and that the correct thing to do about that in a room in this city was to let it stand.**

"I asked for the question," he said. "**And I have got a question I cannot answer, and I am not going to be told it was the right thing to do, because if somebody tells me that then in four months I have to do it again.**"

"Nobody has said that."

"**I know nobody has said that. I have been in this city a hundred and six days and I have watched four hundred men be told a true thing in a yard and not one of them go home and do it again.**"

---

**And then about nine people spent about nineteen minutes trying to find a way to refuse the question, and they found two, and both of them are this city's own instruments, and neither of them can be used, and the reason is the reason the whole hundred and six days is about.**

The first way to refuse it is to write a refusal on a page, with a date on it, in a room, in daylight, where about two hundred and forty people can hear it. **A dated refusal is an answer, and an answer is a description, and a description is a removal with a column for where the person is.** The four words *nobody has asked them* were said out loud in a converted salt store on the fiftieth day of the new month, which was the seventy-fifth day of the flood, and for twenty-four days they were a refusal with nothing on them at all, and then a review put a heading and a date on them and nobody could undo it, and that is the whole of this city's protection and it is seven days old.

The second way to refuse it is to put the eight names on a page so that the office can match them and stop asking. **That is the one that would work, in about eleven minutes, and about nine people in that room understood that it would work while it was being said, and it is the one thing this city has not done in a hundred and six days, and it is the one thing that would make every other thing anybody has built here a description with a date on it.**

"So we do not do either," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing.

"**We do not do either,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and I would like it entered that we did not do either on purpose and not because we ran out of time, and I would like the date on it, and I would like somebody to write down that the reason we did not do the second one is not that the names are lost. They are not lost. Seven of them are nine miles up a road in a wall and one of them is on a stone in a hand about a hundred and forty years old and nobody has looked at the other seven in nine years.**"

---

**And then a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said the thing that the room had been building for nineteen minutes, and it is in the book in her own words, and nobody improved on it, and it is what the next four days of this city's story are made of.**

"**Then we go and look at the seven.**"

Nobody argued with her, and it is entered that nobody argued, and that a man of forty-four of the outer ward stood up before anybody had finished deciding, and that a boy of nine had already said on a wharf five days ago that he wanted to go, and that the date is the nineteenth day of the month after this one, and that it is on a slate, and that it is the only thing on the slate, and that the road is nine miles and eleven households keep it and nobody has ever asked them to.

**It is entered that this is not a plan. It is a date on a slate and about nine people and a road, and a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who put nothing in an eighth column of any document in this city, was asked whether it was his doing and said no, and a woman of twenty-four said that it was the first time in a hundred and six days that anybody in this city had said no to being the author of a thing and that she was going to write it down before he could improve on it.**

---

**And a hearing is in about seven weeks and there is one hearing and one clock. The quarterly description is in a wooden case on a river with a question in its eighth column and no name in it, and a clerk of that office may not ask a question and may not refuse one, and a person named in a question is not told the question exists, and no person is named in this one. The undertaking is void and is on a nail and is on a river and no office has been told. Nine Elms' water is not received, the quarter is gone, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and a village of nine households has refused three times and has not refused a fourth time and nobody in this city has said what happens if it does. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid. The cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth, because the join stopped being worked on the first of March and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason. The chair of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and six days and was not opened today, and the two people who went through it were not asked. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken and was not taken today. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. There have been twenty-eight things the wall has said in this city, one of them has been refused, and the wall said nothing on the sixteenth day of the month after this one and the count is sixteen for sixteen, counted silences and not days. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and performed no working today, and it is entered that he said nothing for about four hours in that room and that a woman of fifty entered that he said nothing and that she entered that she noticed.**
