# Chapter 0118 — Only Arriving

On the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and eighteenth day of the flood and a Sunday, an answer a boy of nine gave to a question on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of the flood, went onto a slate in his own hand and was read out by a girl of seventeen because he could not be sure of the local hand, and it is sixty-eight days late, and nobody improved on it, and nobody in that room thanked him for it, and he did not expect to be thanked, and it is not a lesson and it does not settle anything.

**The licence is sixty-eight days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-ninth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and seventeen days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the question was asked on a step and the answer has been unwritten for sixty-eight days, and on the hundred and eleventh day of this flood it was sixty-one days, and a girl of seventeen wrote that down on the fourteenth day of the month after this one and wrote it down again on the twentieth, and the number is on a page in a room over a market and is the only number anybody in this city is keeping for no reason at all.**

The question was whether the next ones are going to be players. He asked it on a step on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of this flood, and a man from another world did not have an answer that did not make the child smaller, and the man wrote one line in a private book saying that he had been asked a question by a boy of nine and had not had an answer and was going to go and find one, and he did not find it in ten days, and then in twenty, and then in forty-four, and then in sixty, and then in sixty-eight, and the line is still in the book and it is the only thing in it that is not a number off a wall.

"He has asked three questions in this city," said a woman of twenty-four. "**One on the thirty-fifth day of the new month, which was the sixtieth day of the flood, and it was three words and it was about a man he had never met, and the answer to that one is written. One on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day, and it is this one. And the third was on the sixth mile of a nine-mile road on the twentieth day of the month after this one and it was six words and it is on the back of another slate and nobody has answered it.**"

"How do you know it is the same slate," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"It is not," said a girl of seventeen. "**It is the other one. He has four slates. One of the four is nineteen shapes and it belongs to a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read and is not repaid and is on no roll. The other three are his.**"

---

**And he was asked on the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one in a room over a market whether he wanted to say it out loud or have it written, and he said write it, in about four seconds, and he has been carrying a piece of chalk in a fold of his coat since the second week of this flood because his mother owns the building the loft is the top of and sold him a key, and he wrote it on the flat of a slate that has nothing on the other side, and he wrote it in about nineteen seconds, and he is nine.**

**And it is on the slate in his own hand, and a girl of seventeen read it out in the local language because he could not be sure whether he had got the words in the right order, and he said she could have the slate and did not want it back, and nobody improved on it, and nobody asked him a second question about it, and nobody in that room said anything at all for about nineteen seconds afterwards.**

> **I asked my mother and she said she did not know, and I have thought about it every day since and I still do not know.**
>
> **And I have got an answer to a different question. Every one of you was somewhere before you were here. That is not coming. That is only arriving.**

**And the second of those is twenty-eight words, and a girl of seventeen counted it, and the room did not check her count, and it is entered that the room did not check her count, and it is entered that the first of the two is twenty-six words and that she did not count the first one and that the first one is the one that answers the question and that the second one is the one that everybody in that room is going to be thinking about on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one and on the day after that and in about eight weeks in a room nine hundred miles away.**

Nobody answered it. **It is entered that nobody answered it, and that nobody was asked to, and that a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who was asked this question on a step sixty-eight days ago and did not have an answer, did not say a single word about it for about four minutes, and that a woman of twenty-four wrote that down and then did not write it down, and that both of those things are on the page and that she entered that she noticed the second one and entered that she noticed.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing said the thing that the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one turns on, and it took her about nineteen seconds, and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read had said the other half of it in a room on the nineteenth day of the month after this one, and neither of them had heard the other do it, and a girl of seventeen entered both of those facts with the date on them.**

"He did not solve it," said the woman of about thirty-four. "**He has said the word arriving and he does not know what it means, he is nine, and he has thought about it for sixty-eight days and nobody helped him, and he has got a sentence that is either the best thing anybody in this city has said in a hundred and eighteen days or it is nothing, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can tell us which, and we are not going to get one, and that is correct.**"

"Do not tell him that," said a woman of twenty-nine, who was in the room and who has not been asked one question in fifty-eight days, counted from the sixtieth day of this flood.

"I am not going to tell him anything," said the woman of about thirty-four. "**I am going to say one thing to the room and not to him. In about eight weeks nine of us are going to be in a room, and somebody is going to get up and give a speech about what this city has built and what it has refused, and I have heard that speech four times already in nineteen days and it is going to be the fifth time, and every word of it is going to be about us.**"

"And that is wrong," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**That is not wrong and it is not right and that is why it will happen. We are the people in the room. The second sentence of it is that everything in this city was done by persons and that a person can be asked. And it is true. And there is a boy of nine in this room whose answer is the only sentence any of us has said in a hundred and eighteen days that was not about us, and I am not going to use it, and I am not going to let anybody else use it, and I would like that entered.**"

It was entered.

"And I would like one more thing entered," said a woman of twenty-four, "**because it is the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one and in about eight weeks I am going to be in a room, and if I am not careful about this I am going to use it. He is nine. He answered a question that a man could not answer, sixty-eight days late, in a room he did not choose, in front of about nine people, and none of those are reasons to use a sentence. They are reasons to be ashamed of the sentence. I am entering the shame now so that in eight weeks I have to look at it.**"

"Entered," said a girl of seventeen.

"**And put the date on it,**" said the woman of twenty-four.

"It is on it," said the girl of seventeen.

---

**And a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read said the fourth thing, and it is in her own words, and it is a finding about a failure and it is the only kind of finding this city has ever written down, and a man of sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years said that the reason he had said it in a room and not on a road four days ago was that a boy of nine was going to ask him about it in about nine minutes and that this was predictable and that he had known it was predictable since the sixth mile.**

"You did not answer it before he asked," said the woman of twenty-four.

"**No. I had a sentence ready on the sixth mile about nineteen yards in front of him and I have had it ready for two days, and it is about a road and a wall and a stone, and it is a good sentence, and it would have been about me.**"

"**And a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused,**" said a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, "**and a man who has been asked on a road and has not been asked in a room is not a person who has said no, and I have said that once in a hundred and eighteen days and I am saying it again because a man of sixty-one has just been asked in a room by a boy of nine in front of about nine people, and that is the first time anybody in this city has been asked properly in a room on a day that somebody did not choose.**"

Nobody said anything. **It is entered that about four people in that room understood in about four seconds that the sentence that was the finding of the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one had nothing to do with a man of sixty-one, and that they all worked it out at the same moment, and that nobody said so, and that the correct thing to do about that in this city was to let it stand.**

---

**And then somebody asked the boy whether he wanted to change any of it, and he said no in about four seconds, and after that he said nothing else, and it is entered that he said nothing about having been asked, and that nobody in that room said anything to him about having been asked, and that nobody mentioned the sixty-eight days, and that a woman of twenty-four wanted to say the number out loud and did not, and that the number is on a page over a market and does not need to be said to a boy of nine.**

"You did not have to write it down," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who said it in about four seconds and then said nothing else about it.

"I know," said Tam Crane, who is nine. "**I am not going to take it back, though. My mother says a thing you have said out loud is not a thing you can put in a drawer, and she is right, and she has said it nine times and she has never had to take one back.**"

"**That is her sentence and not yours, and I am not going to write it down,**" said a girl of seventeen, and did not write it down.

---

**And nothing else happened on the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one. The four went down two hundred steps at the seventh hour and did not take the twenty-fifth reading and one ruled line is still ruled and still empty, and the six disagreements all stand. A hearing is in thirty-six days, and there is one hearing and one clock, and the number nine is on a page with nobody at the top of it. A box is in a room with a door and is not opened. A village of nine households has refused a fourth time and nobody can say what that is. A board by the near gate has a ruled line at the bottom of it that is still empty. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-ninth time. The licence is sixty-eight days old and the seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody. The boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch said nothing today. There have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one has been refused, and a Sunday on which a boy of nine's answer was written down and not improved on is the twenty-sixth silence, and the pattern is twenty-six for twenty-six.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one is a slate with a child's handwriting on it in two blocks, one of which is eighteen words long, and about nine people in a room over a market have gone to sleep, and nobody has said a word to that slate about being asked, and the slate is not a page and is not going anywhere and will still be here in four months.**
