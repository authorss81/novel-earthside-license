# Chapter 0145 — A Delegation Is a Number With Legs

On the fifty-fifth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and forty-fifth day of the flood and a Saturday, about nine people in a room over a market agreed that if the thing on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one goes the way anybody in this city hopes it goes, it will be a recognition and it will be provisional, and a provisional recognition has a price on it, and the price is four people and a question going to the ruins of the First Landing, and nobody asked a person whether they would go, because a person who is asked whether they would go is a person on a list.

**The licence is ninety-five days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is sixty days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eighty-sixth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and forty-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

"**Say the name of the place,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, "**because we have said *the First Landing* in about four rooms in three days without anybody putting it on anything, and a place that is only ever said out loud is a place that has no record, and this city has spent a hundred and forty-five days not liking that.**"

"It is in the register," said a woman of twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and had been in the room for about nine minutes and had not sat down. "**First Landing. It is in the public register of business of a town four hundred miles inland, in a hand about three centuries old, in a line about a market, and it is the only line in nine hundred years of that register with that name on it, and it is a name for a piece of ground and not for a building.**"

"**And the ruins.**"

"**The ruins are not in the register.**"

---

**And then she said the whole of what she had, and it took about four minutes, and it is on the page, and nobody improved on it, and it is the only description of the First Landing that exists anywhere in four hundred miles.**

"**Three things, and I am going to keep them apart, which is a thing I do and which about four people in this city have learned to expect.**"

> **One. In the public register of a town four hundred miles inland there is one line, in a hand about three centuries old, about a market, with the name First Landing on it, and the line says a thing about salt, and it is the only line in nine hundred years of that register with that name on it.**
>
> **Two. The Bright Pilgrims. About three centuries ago a fragment near the Saltwake lock opened a narrow crossing and a small number of people crossed without knowing they were entering a real world. Their machines spoiled, their language changed, and their stories became a thing people in this city tell about somebody arriving from somewhere. There is a version of it in about nine rooms. In four of those rooms they came down out of the sky. In three they came out of a hill. In two they came out of the water. I have never worked out why and I have never asked, because a story that a place tells about where people came from is a thing a place can change, and I have been in this city a hundred and forty-five days.**
>
> **Three. There is ground four hundred miles inland that people call the First Landing, and there is stone on it, and nobody in this city has been there and nothing about it is on a page in a hundred and forty-five days. It is not on the eight. It is not on the nine. It is not on the ten, and it is not on the table I have been carrying in a coat for seven years, and I have checked all four and I checked them on the forty-first day of the month after this one and I checked them again this morning because somebody had to.**

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**So what is the question,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**That is the question. It is the only thing I have and it is a hole and I have been standing next to it for four days.**"

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the part of the fifty-fifth day of the month after this one that is the finding, and it took about four minutes, and nobody improved on it, and it is entered as what a person said and not as a finding.**

"**It is a list.**"

"**A delegation is not a list,**" said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board. "**A delegation is four people going to look at something.**"

"**Four people is a list of four and you know it is a list of four and you have known it since you were about eleven.**" He did not raise his voice and he did not move from four feet back. "**A list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is, and you got that out in about nine minutes on the fifteenth day of the month after this one and you have been right ever since. A list of four people going four hundred miles is the same page with a longer column and a worse place to be found in.**"

"Then what is it," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**A number with legs,**" said a man of thirty-two. "**You can send a number. You cannot send a page. Four goes. Nobody in nine hundred miles can ask four anything, and when four comes back and says there is stone on a piece of ground four hundred miles inland, then that is what the ground is, and there is no person in it to be cross with, and it will be the truest account anybody has of a place and it will be about nine words long and there will not be one person in it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and a girl of seventeen wrote that down and then asked, in about four seconds, whether she had better not.

"**Write it down,**" said a man of thirty-two, "**and do not put a name on it, and that is a different thing and I have had a hundred and forty-five days to work out that it is a different thing.**"

---

**And then a man of thirty-nine said the thing that everybody in that room had been waiting about four days for, and it took him about nine seconds, and it is the first thing he has said in this city in a hundred and forty-five days that nobody had to ask him for.**

"**I will go.**"

"**You will not be named,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, in about four seconds.

"**I have been refused by name four times in four years by that woman,**" said the man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board, "**on that board, with nineteen rates on it and no column for a name, and I have asked once in every one of those four years and been refused in four seconds every time, and I said in this room on the thirty-third day of the month after this one that it was a different thing and it was not. It is not a different thing. I am the man who has been left off a page for four years because I trade on it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**So no,**" he said. "**I said I will go and I am not going to be on it and both of those are true and I would like the second one written down and not the first.**"

It was written down, without the first.

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the thing about himself, and it is on the page, and it is four sentences and nobody in that room argued with any of it, and nobody said it was a pity, and it is entered that about four people in that room wanted to say something else and did not.**

"**I cannot go.**"

"You could go," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I cannot go, and the reason is on the page from the second day of the month after this one and I have said it in about four rooms and I am not going to improve on it in the sixth. I hold no mark of my own. I have no workway and no title and no seat and no roster and no land and there is no office in Saltwake for what I am. To open a crossing takes a named local holder with a return mark, a receiving community that has agreed, a fresh inspection, two witnesses who are not of the crew, and a route that has not been opened twice in a row by the same person. I have none of that and there is nobody in this world who can carry me there and bring me back, and the reason there is nobody is that the crossing does not work from this side, and I have known that since the second day of the month after this one and I am not going to be the man who goes and looks at a seam in the ground.**"

"Then what do you add to it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**Nothing,**" he said. "**And I have thought about that for four days and the nothing is correct, and the four people going are local people who can say no to what they find, and if I were going I would be the only man in four hundred miles who could say what a thing in the ground is, and I would be the account of it before anybody else had stood on it. A delegation with me in it is a description. A delegation of four people who have never been anywhere is four people with legs.**"

---

**And a woman of twenty-nine who keeps a public register then said what the delegation is for, and it is on the page, and it is four lines, and it does not resolve anything and nobody improved on it.**

> **To the ground four hundred miles inland that people call the First Landing: to say what is standing on it; to say whether there is a seam in the ground; if there is, to say how long it is and what is in it; to open nothing; to come back; and to say it in a room, in daylight, in the local language, with a date on it.**

"Not to open it," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**Not to open it,**" said the woman of twenty-nine. "**And that clause is mine and I have put it in myself and I would like it kept as mine, because in about four months somebody is going to say that a delegation was sent to open something and I did not write that.**"

"It is yours," said a woman of twenty-four.

---

**And nothing else happened on the fifty-fifth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in nine days and there is one hearing and one clock, and none of the above is decided. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there twenty-nine days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five. And a printed appointment of an interpreter with nine terms on it is on a table two floors down and a man of thirty-two has not answered it. And a compact of eleven lines is drafted and is not read out and is not signed. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is thirty-three days ago, and the water has not been received for seventy-six days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is sixty days, and one of the forty has not come for thirty-one days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for sixty days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and forty-five days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And a Saturday on which four people and a question were agreed to a piece of ground four hundred miles inland and nobody was asked is the fifty-first silence and the pattern is fifty-one for fifty-one.**

**And the last thing of the fifty-fifth day of the month after this one is four lines in a woman's hand with a date on them and a clause in it that says not to open it, and a number with legs, and a hearing in nine days.**
