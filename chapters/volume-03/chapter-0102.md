# Chapter 0102 — Nine Minutes, and the Fifth Room

On the twelfth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and second day of the flood and a Friday, about nine people stood in a converted salt store in a yard under a lock chapel and tried for about nine minutes to find out how a page had ended a document, and they did not find out, and the failure was written down with the date on it, and the page was not opened and is not going to be opened, and the number of rooms in this city that have now tried is five.

**The licence is fifty-two days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the forty-third time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and one days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**Five things were tried in nine minutes, and every one of them was a fair thing to try, and every one of them ran out in about a minute and a half, and the room got no further with the fifth than it did with the first, and nobody in that room pretended afterwards that the fifth had been closer.**

The first thing they tried was what the page says, and the outside of the page is in the book, and it is a heading and seven lines, and it was read back in the local language and then in English, and nobody changed a word of it, and there is nobody in four hundred miles who is qualified to change a word of it.

> **The first day of the month after this one.**
>
> **A page that ends one document, once.**
>
> **The document is one that claims to be the work of a person.**
>
> **The document's own condition is the ground. No other ground is used.**
>
> **Whether the condition was met is not argued. It is set down.**
>
> **This is a record and not a finding. It does not say why.**
>
> **Nine witnesses. One of them is named by herself, and her name may be taken back, and nothing whatever happens to the page.**
>
> **The page is not a Crown instrument, is not this city's, and is on no roll of anything.**

"Sixth line," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**It says it does not say why. That is the only paper I have ever read that tells you in writing that it is not going to tell you.**"

"It is not a paper," said Sera Vail. "**It is a page, and it is spent, and it is in a lockable room off this room with the door shut, and the sixth line is the reason I sealed it with no second copy, and I said so out loud on the day and it is in the book in my own words.**"

---

**The second thing they tried was what it was laid on, and the answer was a barrel, and a salt store floor, and a yard under a chapel, and a woman of seventy who has washed that floor every Thursday morning for fifty-one years and re-limed it twice since the first day of the month after this one.**

"Thursday," said Nyla Ferrow, seventy, who keeps the floor. "**I have washed that floor on Thursdays since before your mother was born and I washed it on the Thursday in between, and the floor is not evidence of anything and neither is the barrel, and if any one of you had come and asked me for a scrape of it on the second day of the month after this one I would have given it to you, and I did not know to.**"

"Would it have mattered," said Adrian Vale.

"**No,**" she said. "**A thing a page was laid on is a thing about a page, and we are trying to find out about a document. I would rather it had been asked for and come back with nothing than that nine of us have spent a morning on a floor.**"

---

**The third thing they tried was who was in the room, and that one turned out to be the only part of the whole of it that anybody can check, and it turned out to be true and completely empty, and a man of thirty-one with a hand that will not close got further with it in one sentence than the rest of the room got in eight minutes.**

Nine people. All named in a book. All still living in this city or within four miles of it. A keeper of seventy. A mason of fifty who is not of the crew. A woman of forty-four who came four miles and would not enter the four miles anywhere. A Hearthguard of forty-four. A notary of fifty. A woman of twenty-four. A woman of seventy of Nine Elms who said yes against her own village's interest. A man of sixty-one who said yes before he was asked. And a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, who named herself, and whose name may be taken back, and which nobody has taken back.

"That is the whole of what is checkable," said the man with the stall. "**Nine names, a date, a barrel. Everything else about that page is nine people seeing something happen and being unable to repeat it, and two of the nine are in this room and one of the two has not been asked anything in a hundred and two days.**"

"**Then a page works on nine people seeing it,**" said the man of thirty-one. "**That is the only sentence anybody has said this morning that is about the page and not about the document, and I have been in nine rooms in a month and I have never once heard a room get that far, and I am going to say the second half so that it is on the page: I do not know what that means, I have no way of finding out, and I am the only person in this room who will say that out loud without being asked.**"

Nobody improved on it. It was not written down, and it is entered that it was not written down, and the reason it was not written down is that a woman of twenty-four said that a sentence about a page that works is a mechanism, and a mechanism is a diagram, and a diagram of the most dangerous object in this city is a worse thing to have in a hundred and two days than nine minutes of nothing.

---

**The fourth thing they tried was the document, and that one worked, and it took about four minutes, and it is the only thing anybody in this city can hold in their hands about the twelfth day of the month after this one, and holding it is what made the fifth thing impossible.**

A thing that argues for itself is a thing you can check. A thing that is silent can only be believed. The undertaking said out loud in its own first sentence that it was a body and not a person, and it was silent for nineteen years about who would stand at the top of it, and an undertaking is not in force until a person stands at the top of it and answers for it alone. The condition was in the first sentence. The condition was not met. The whole of it took about nineteen minutes in a room with about two hundred and forty people in it and nobody was convicted of anything, and the paper is on a nail in an arch, dry, unmarked, with the string untouched, and the only place in four hundred miles where the voidness exists is a book in this city in a notary's hand.

"That is a ground a room can hold," said a man of forty-two who had come nine miles to watch a document be ended and had not been asked anything and had said one sentence.

"That is a ground," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing at the ninth hour of the morning. "**And I have been standing here for nine minutes waiting for somebody to say the other half, so I am going to say it. The page has a ground. Nine people have a page that ends a document on a condition the document states about itself, and that is checkable, and a man of forty-four is right that it is the only part of it a room can hold.**"

"And the other half."

"**That the page is the part that worked, and the page is a heading and seven lines, and it does not say why, and in a minute the room is going to try the fifth thing and the fifth thing is why.**"

---

**And they tried the fifth thing. It is the thing that has been tried in five rooms in a hundred and two days, and the morning's contribution to it was that the room noticed it, and the morning's other contribution was a man of sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years and who said, in sixteen words, the whole of what is left.**

"Why," said the man of thirty-two.

And then nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and a man of forty-one in a very good coat said this, and he asked for it to be entered before he said it, and it was entered:

"**I am not going to guess, and I want that on the page before anything else, because I have been in nine districts in nineteen years and in every one of them somebody guessed. A guess about a thing like this is not a mistake. A guess about a thing like this is a road, and a road with a guess at the bottom of it is a road that somebody comes back down in four months holding a document.**"

"Then what is it," said the man with the stall.

"**It is a page that says what it is and does not say what it does.** That is all I have. I have kept the records of an office for nineteen years. I have never in my life seen a thing end a document. I have read about four hundred attempts at an explanation, and every single one of them was a man saying why something he did not understand had worked, and not one of the four hundred has ever been true, and one of them was mine."

"And I have been reading pages for nineteen years and I have never asked one anything," said Garret Slee, who is sixty-one, and it is sixteen words, and nobody improved on it, and the room did not check the count, which is entered.

---

**And the count of rooms was entered, because a girl of seventeen entered everything, and it is four, and this morning is the fifth, and the four are named because a city that has failed at a thing four times and has never written the four down is a city that will fail at it a fifth time in the same place.**

One. **The salt store on the first day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-first day of the flood.** Nine witnesses, a page drawn in about eleven minutes, and nobody asked what it was, including the man who drew it, including the man who named the thing it was aimed at, and including a man of forty-one who said two words in that room four days early and asked for all three of those things to be written down.

Two. **The salt store on the second day of the month after this one.** A document ended in about nineteen minutes. The first person to ask why could not answer it, and the reason is that the person who carried the page into the room did not read it out and said so in fifteen words, because the inside of it is not his.

Three. **A room on the second day of the month after this one**, where a man of sixty-one was asked, for the first time in nineteen years, whether he understood what he was copying, and answered, and about nine people in that room noticed inside four minutes that the question about the page and the question about the man were the same shape, and did not find the difference, and it is entered that they looked.

Four. **The room where the licence was reviewed on the ninth day of the month after this one**, where four words that had been a refusal for twenty-four days went onto a page with a heading and a date on it, and about four people noticed the shape of that, and nobody said so out loud, and a notary of fifty noticed that nobody had said so out loud and entered that she noticed.

"And this one," said the girl of seventeen, "**is the fifth, and we did not get one step further than the fourth, and the reason we did not is not that we are stupid. It is that there is nothing further to get into.**"

---

**And then a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read asked the question that a hundred and two days of this city had been standing behind without knowing it, and she asked it in about four seconds, and it is hers, and nobody improved on it.**

"A form can be refused," she said. "**A slate can be read. A page in a stone can be taken down on a date. A room can be asked a question. And that page is a thing nobody in this city knows how to ask, and it has been sitting in a locked room nine feet from where we are standing for ten days, and the reason nobody has asked it anything is not fear. It is that every one of us has been waiting to be in the room when somebody else does it first. And I have watched four hundred men do that and I have never once watched a woman do it.**"

"You have not asked it anything either," said the man of thirty-two.

"**I have not asked it anything either,**" she said, "**and I am not going to, and I would like it written down that a man of thirty-two said that to a woman of fifty-eight in a room in front of nine people and that she agreed with him in about a second, and that nobody in the room wrote any of it down for about nine minutes, and that I have been in this city since the second day of this flood and I have watched about nine people build four things they cannot name and get to the fifth one and stop.**"

Nobody asked her anything else. **And the page stayed shut, and the salt store was locked at about the eleventh hour, and the fifth room was entered in a column four lines high in a girl of seventeen's book with the date on it, and the entry says what was tried and what was not found, and that the not-finding is the entry and not the absence of one.**

---

**Nothing was gained.** Five rooms have now tried to find out how a page ends a document, and five of them have failed in the same place, and the failure is entered, and the page is not opened and will not be opened by anybody in this city. The undertaking is void and is on a nail and is on a river and no office has been told. The chair of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and two days and was not opened today. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room.

Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and performed no working today. **There have been twenty-seven things the wall has said in this city, one of them has been refused, and a room in which nine people failed at a page for nine minutes and entered the failure is a thirteenth silence, and the pattern is thirteen for thirteen.** The black line in his right palm is one hundred and one days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.

**And a room is a thing that can be asked and a door is a thing that can be asked, and about nine people went down two hundred steps of a river wall on the morning of the thirteenth day of the month after this one to find out whether a gate is the same kind of thing as a page. It is. Nobody opened it.**
