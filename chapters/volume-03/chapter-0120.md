# Chapter 0120 — A Slate on a Bench

On the thirtieth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twentieth day of the flood and a Tuesday, about nine people sat in a room over a market and entered that a field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty has been settled in a second time, that the first was on the first of March at a gateway in daylight by the person the field is on, that this one was in a room, off a board, by a rule, by nobody, that nobody was asked either time, and what the second one cost, and that a hearing is in thirty-four days and there is one hearing and one clock, and that about nine people are going to be asked to be in a room nine hundred miles away and there is no road in this world that reaches it.

**The licence is seventy days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is thirty-five days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixty-first time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and nineteen days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the season was settled in at about the fourth hour of the afternoon for the second time in thirty-five days, by a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, who read the bottom of a board out loud, stopped, and then said the sentence that is now the entry, and the person the field is on was standing in the room the whole time and nobody asked her anything and she did not say anything. It was settled in the first time at a gateway four miles down the Reach road on the first of March at about the eighth hour of the morning, in daylight, in the local language, by the woman it is on, and a number was said once and then not said again, and about four people in that room had been in one or the other of the two and nobody said which.**

The board is the one by the near gate. There are eleven entries in the column and a date and a hand against every one of them, and there is no figure on the end of any of the eleven, and below the eleventh there is a ruled line and the rule is ruled and it has been empty since the first of March.

"**The season of ninety-one acres is settled in,**" said the man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, reading it out, and then he stopped, and it is entered that he stopped for about nineteen seconds and that nobody helped him, and that a woman of twenty-four said afterward that he had not been reading it out, he had been handing it over, and that this was the first time in a hundred and twenty days she had seen anybody hand a thing over in this city instead of making a person take it.

"Settled in by whom," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**By the rule under the eleventh line. There is no room in that column and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason, and the join stopped being worked on the first of March, and nobody can enter the cost of working a season, and so nobody can work a season, and that is not a decision anybody took. And the rule was ruled and empty on the first of March as well, and it has been empty for thirty-five days.**"

"Entered that," said a girl of seventeen.

"Entered all of it," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**Entered who settled it in. Entered that it was a rule drawn under the eleventh line of a cost column and that the rule is empty. Entered what it cost. Entered that nobody was asked.**"

"**And entered that it has been settled in before,**" said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, "**and that on the first of March she settled it in herself at a gateway in daylight and a number was said once and then not said again, and it was a person and could be asked, and this afternoon it is a rule under a line and it cannot be asked anything, and she is standing in this room and has not been asked by anybody including me, and that is the whole of what is different.**"

"What did it cost," said a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read.

---

**And a woman of forty-four of Corrow, who reads the edge of a join and who came four miles on the fifteenth day of the month after this one and asked to be left alone and was left alone for four hours, said the number, and it is five words, and a girl of seventeen counted it, and the room did not check her count.**

"**A figure and a decision.**"

And it is entered that nobody in that room said anything for about nineteen seconds, and that the woman of forty-four of Corrow said nothing else at all for the rest of the day, and that she was in the room from the second hour of the afternoon to the seventh hour and was asked nothing in either, and that a woman of twenty-four entered that she had noticed that, and entered that she noticed, and that she has entered the noticing four times in a hundred and twenty days.

"That is the second time in a hundred and twenty days that this city has settled something about a person standing in the room," said a woman of twenty-four. "**And the first time was on the hundredth day of the flood, and that one was a question owed to a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house, and this city chose not to ask her and wrote the choice on a date, and I am not comparing the two. And I am not counting the first of March, because on that day she settled it in herself and nobody settled anything about her. I am saying that a rule under the eleventh line of a column has just done in about nine seconds what four people did in a room in about four minutes, and that the rule did it in a building and the four people did it in daylight.**"

"And nobody said no," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Nobody said no because a rule cannot be refused. A rule about a document is a rule about a document, and that is on a page from the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one, and it is about documents, and this is not a document, it is a column with a rule under it, and I have been in this city a hundred and twenty days and I have never once seen a rule about anything be refused by anybody, and I do not know whether that is because the rules are right or because the rules are not people.**"

"It is because the rules are not people," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything. "**A number cannot be asked whether it will have it. A page cannot. A person can. We wrote that down on the seventeenth day of the month after this one in about nine seconds and nobody has improved on it since, and this afternoon is the first time one of those rules has gone at a person instead of at a page, and it is the same woman and the same ninety-one acres, and she is standing in the room.**"

And it is entered that the woman of forty-four of Corrow heard that, and that she did not turn round, and that it is entered that she heard it, and that a girl of seventeen entered both of those things with the date on them, and that nobody improved on any of it.

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening a notary of fifty said the thing that has been standing in a book since the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, and it is in her own words and nobody improved on it, and she has been asked about it four times in four days and has answered none of the four.**

"I entered on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one that I would like to be asked before it is opened," she said, "**in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person, and the reason is that I have been the notary of about nine hundred things and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence. I have been asked about it four times in four days by four different people and a girl of seventeen wrote the four times down and I have not answered any of them.**"

"You are not going to answer us," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**I am not going to answer you because I do not know what my answer is. I have not known for four days. I have entered the not-knowing with the date on it, which is a thing a person can do and a thing nobody in this city has ever done, and I would like the room to notice that a notary of fifty has just entered that she does not know and that four people have let her, and that this is the whole of the law about a person carrying a cost and I did not write the law and I am not the notary of the Open Hand and I am not going to be asked to be.**"

Nobody asked her to be. **And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing said in about four seconds that the reason nobody asked was that the right of refusal that cannot be bought out is a rule nobody is going to check, and that this city got that from a keeper of about seventy and has had it for a hundred and twenty days, and that it is the only rule anybody here has brought that is worth having, and that she is not going to be gladder about a number.**

---

**And about nine people sat in that room on the thirtieth day of the month after this one and went through what there was, out loud, because that is what they do, and it is on the page, and none of it goes anywhere.**

The hearing is in thirty-four days. There is one hearing and one clock, and it was given by that office as about eleven weeks on the fifty-second day of the new month, which was the seventy-seventh day of the flood, and eleven weeks is seventy-seven days, and the two figures agree exactly. **About nine people have said out loud that they will stand in it. The number nine is entered on a page with a date on it and no person at the top of it, and it is a count and not a list, and there are no names on it anywhere in this city, and about four people have asked for names and have not been given them and a girl of seventeen said no to the first of the four in under a second and to the other three more slowly.**

A case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-eighth day of the flood, in a wooden case with a strap and a wax and a second strap, and it is still on the water, and it carries a question in its eighth column and it names nobody, and a clerk of that office may not ask a question and may not refuse one, and a person named in a question is not told the question exists. **It may not be intercepted, and it may not be taken back, and it may not be translated into the local language in a room where about two hundred and forty people can hear it, and it has not been. What will come back from it is a correct number about a district, and eight people will not be in it, and nothing anybody in this city does in the next thirty-four days changes that, and about four people in that room said that sentence out loud on four different days in the last ten days and nobody improved on it.**

A box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood, and it is the first thing in a hundred and forty years that has come up, and it is in a room with a door on the second floor and it is not opened, and the strap on it is still untied, and a nineteen-line page headed *the box as received* is in a girl's book and is not a roll.

A village of nine households refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one and the refusal is a person's and not a village's, and nobody in this city can say what the fourth refusal is, and the water has not been received for fifty-one days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and the quarter is gone, and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force.

A page in a stone at a fork at Four Elms carries three pages and the second of the three is nineteen lines long and is twelve names and six lines of count and a seventh line under them that says it is a count and not a debt, and the household that keeps that stone may take any of the three down on a date and is not asked why twice, and has not taken any of them down.

Nine miles of road are kept by eleven households who are not a council and pay no levy and answer to no clerk, and that is on no page.

Two Wayhouses and one Open Hand exchange stand under local law and a board with nineteen rates on it and four rules that are not rates and no names on it has been trading all morning, and the third time in a hundred and twenty days that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf has been asked to put a name on anything she has said no.

A page that ends a document is spent and is in a lockable room off the salt store with the door shut, and five rooms have failed to find out how it did it and all five failed in the same place, and a sixth was wanted on the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one and was not held.

The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and one ruled line left, and the four who read the join-line have disagreed six times since the first of March and all six entries stand, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken on any of the ten days from the twenty-first to the thirtieth day of the month after this one and was not taken on the four days before that either.

The main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word. **The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and nineteen days and was not opened on any of these ten days, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty days and has never said a word, and it is not repaired.** The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt written under them, and a fifth item which is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. The seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times.

**There have been thirty things the wall has said in this city across thirteen batches and one of them has been refused, and the refusals in a clerk of the Registry's second book, which is kept at home and has never been shown to anybody, are twenty-three and did not move in these ten days, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is ten, and it was nine before the first registered refusal, and a clerk entered on the seventeenth day of the month after this one that he could close his half of that difference alone tonight and did not.** And the thirtieth day of the month after this one is the twenty-eighth silence and the pattern is twenty-eight for twenty-eight, counted silences and not days, and the wall spoke once in these ten days and a person answered it in about four seconds and it was not refused.

**And a man of forty-one in a very good coat is in a room in about eleven weeks, and he has not been convicted of anything, and he is not going to be, and he has said on the page twice that he is not going to explain either of the two names anybody in this city has, and he has not mentioned a letter.**

---

**And about nine people went to sleep in four buildings in this city on the thirtieth day of the month after this one, and the first of the nine to go was a boy of nine who left a slate face down on a bench in a room over a market, and there is nothing on the other side of that one and there has not been since the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one, and the six words are on the back of another of his three and are not the twenty-eight, and nobody turned either of them over, and the case is upstairs in a room with a door, and the strap on it is still untied, and there is a hearing in thirty-four days, and there is one hearing and one clock, and the number nine is on a page with nobody at the top of it.**
