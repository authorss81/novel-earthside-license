# Chapter 0122 — Two Things Nobody Did

On the thirty-second day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twenty-second day of the flood and a Thursday, thirty-nine of forty men stood under the near gate at the second hour of the morning in the wrong weather and did the work, and it is eight days since the foreman of the day crew did not come, and nobody has told him anything, and about nine people walked nine miles up a road in the afternoon to stand in front of a stone and look at a page in it, and the finding of the thirty-second day of the month after this one is that both of those are the same thing, and that the same thing is a decision not to do something, and that a decision not to do something is a page, and that this city has got very good at writing pages in a hundred and twenty-two days and has not once written one about itself.

**The licence is seventy-two days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is thirty-seven days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixty-third time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty-one days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the foreman of the day crew has said four words under that gate every working day since the forty-sixth day of the new month, which was the seventy-first day of this flood, and the four words are *mind the low stone*, and he said them to forty men and to nobody else, and he has not said them for eight days, and nobody has said them for him, and a girl of seventeen wrote them on a slate in her own hand on the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one and did not put his name on it and nobody asked her to.**

The slate is in a book in this city. It is not a roll and it is not going to Aurel. **Four words and no name, and about four people have looked at it in eight days, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said on the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one that a name on it would be the end of it, and nobody argued.**

They came at the second hour, thirty-nine of them out of forty, and nobody at the gate said anything to any of them, because there is nothing at the gate to say. **A carter came the other way with a barrow of reed and gave them the wall side without being asked, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf gave out eleven cups of something hot to about eleven of them without asking one of them a question.**

"It is eight days," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then somebody should carry a word to him,**" said the same man, four seconds later. "**There is a road. There are eleven households on it and one of them put out a cup of water without being asked. There is a person who could walk up that road in a day and come back in a day and say four words at a gate.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**A word is a thing that travels along a road,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"It is," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**And a thing that travels along a road arrives. And what it says when it gets there is that we know he has not come, and that is a sentence about a man, and a sentence about a man is a description, and a description is a removal with a column for where the person is. In about five weeks the next case goes out of this city and there is a column on a wall in a room nine hundred miles away with a heading on it, and a thing four people said on a gate post is the easiest thing in the world to put in a column, and I have been refusing to put my name on a board for a hundred and twenty-two days and I am not going to be the reason a foreman of a day crew is a line.**"

"You are saying we should not tell him," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I am saying that this city has spent a hundred and twenty-two days working out that a person is the only shape that can be found, and the only shape that can be found is the only shape that can be turned into a column, and that this morning the best thing anybody here can do for a man who is owed thirty-seven days is to say nothing at all, and nobody nine hundred miles away is going to be able to check that we said nothing, and that is either a protection or a hole and I do not know which and I am not going to decide it in about nine seconds.**"

---

**And then a woman of twenty-four wrote the decision down on a slate in a book in this city, and it is not a roll, and it has a date on it, and it is the first page anybody in this city has written about itself.**

> **On the thirty-second day of the month after this one it was decided, in about nine minutes, in daylight, in about nine people, that no word would be carried to the foreman of the day crew of the near gate.**
>
> **He has not come for eight days. He has not been paid since the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is thirty-seven days. Nobody knows where he is and nobody has asked.**
>
> **A word can be carried. It was not carried. The reason is entered and it is the reason of about four people and not the reason of the room.**

And a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read, who said thirteen words at the top of two hundred steps on the twenty-fourth day of the month after this one, stood at the gate and had it read out to her, and said eleven words, and a girl of seventeen counted them and the room did not check her count.

"**You have just written down that we are not telling him.**"

It is entered that nobody improved on that, and that about four people have been keeping a count of the sentences she has said in a room in a hundred and twenty-two days, and that the count is nine, and that nobody checked it, and that she has not once said one about herself.

---

**And about nine people went up nine miles in the wrong weather after the third hour of the afternoon, and eleven households keep that road and are not a council and pay no levy and answer to no clerk, and one of them put out a cup of water without being asked and without asking, and nobody wrote any of it down, and at the fork there is a stone with a cut in the flat of it and the cut says where the water was before the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream at a public act with witnesses a hundred and forty years ago.**

The stone is not paper. **Nothing may be worked at it. There is no pattern in it and nobody has ever worked at one. It is not an instrument and it is not going to be one.** It holds three pages and the household that keeps it is a household and not a person and will not put its name on anything, and it has never been asked whether it will keep them.

The first page has six things and a price and a right of refusal. The third page is the second Wayhouse, with a date, a price, a right of refusal, and a line saying the keeping household may close it for its own reasons on a date written on the page and is not asked why twice. **The second page is nineteen lines: twelve names, six lines of count, and a seventh line under them saying it is a count and not a debt.**

**And one of the twelve names is a woman of forty-four of Corrow, and the line beside her name says she is owed a figure on the end of a cost column and a decision about ninety-one acres, and both of those are on one line because they are one woman, and the thirty-second day of the month after this one is the second day after a rule drawn under the eleventh line of that column gave her a figure and a decision without asking her.**

"So the page is wrong," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**The page is not wrong. The page is correct as of the sixtieth day of the new month, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and it says that this woman is owed a figure and a decision, and on the thirtieth day of the month after this one she was given a figure and a decision by a rule under a line and was asked nothing, and a person cannot be given a thing that was owed to her without it being owed any more, and a page cannot be told the difference between a person paying a debt and a rule settling it in.**"

"Then somebody should scratch the line out."

"**No,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. "**Nobody is going to scratch that line out, and here is the whole of it and it is the reason this city has three pages in a stone and not a shelf. A page in this world can be taken down on a date by the household that keeps it and nobody is asked why twice. That is the only reason anybody trusts a page in this city. And it means that the only correction anybody in four hundred miles can make to a wrong page in a stone is to ask the household to take it down, and the household is not ours, and nobody in this city has ever asked the household whether it will, or whether it knows what is on the second page, or whether it thinks the line about the woman of forty-four is a good line.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**Nine inches of stone is not a safe place for anything,**" she said, "**and I have said a great many things in a hundred and twenty-two days and that is the first one I have said that somebody is going to write down. I would like it written down exactly.**"

It is entered. **And a girl of seventeen wrote it down with the date on it, and did not improve on it, and it is the second page anybody in this city has written about itself and both of them were written in one day by two people who are on nothing.**

---

**And the household that keeps that stone was not there, and had not been asked to be, and a woman of about sixty-one of Four Elms, who argued for the third page on the hundredth day of this flood on behalf of a stranger who will have to keep it in about four years, was standing at the fork with a barrow.**

"**Three pages in a stone is a shrine. One page is a wall somebody kept. Two is a mistake. That is my sentence and I am not going to improve on it today.**" She put the barrow down. "**And nobody has asked that household anything, because asking is how you find out what a household thinks, and this city does not want to know what a household thinks, because a household that thinks is a household that can say no, and if it says no to one of these three pages then the page is gone and we cannot get it back, and a page we cannot get back is a page we cannot check, and we would rather have a page we can never check.**"

"That is what we want," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**That is what you want and I am sixty-one and I have a barrow and I am going to the market.**"

---

**And nothing else happened on the thirty-second day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in thirty-two days and there is one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after the hearing has finished. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and no name in it and it is still on the water. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is ten days ago, and the date is in chalk on nine doors in one hand and is not washed, and the water has not been received for fifty-three days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and the quarter is gone and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force. And a box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one and it is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and it is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked about it five times in five days and has answered none of the five. And a page that ends a document is spent and five rooms have failed to explain it in the same place. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for thirty-seven days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and twenty-two days and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty-two days. And there have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-three, and a Thursday on which nobody told a man anything and nobody corrected a page is the thirtieth silence and the pattern is thirty for thirty.**

**And the last thing of the thirty-second day of the month after this one is a line on the second page of a stone at a fork nine miles up a road, and the line says a woman of forty-four of Corrow is owed a figure and a decision, and the figure and the decision have been given to her by a rule and not asked for, and the line is not scratched out, and the household that could take the page down has not been asked whether it will, and a foreman of a day crew has said four words every working day under a gate since the seventy-first day of this flood and nobody has carried them to him.**
