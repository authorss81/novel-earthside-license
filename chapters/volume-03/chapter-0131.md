# Chapter 0131 — A Compact Is a Document

On the forty-first day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirty-first day of the flood and a Saturday, about nine people stood in a room over a market and worked out that the thing they had been calling a compact for four days was a piece of paper, and that a piece of paper has to go somewhere, and that there is nowhere in four hundred miles it can go to in twenty-three days, and a chair on a landing outside a door has no date on it and is not going to get one.

**The licence is eighty-one days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is forty-six days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventy-second time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirty days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood, and it has been in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market for fifteen days, and the strap on it is still untied, and nobody has read the writing round the edge of the wax, and one man in this city could have read it and did not on four days and has said he will not say it again.

**And a notary of fifty entered on that day that she would like to be asked before it is opened, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person. She has been asked five times in five days and has answered none of the five. The fifth asking was written out on a slate on the thirty-first day of the month after this one and not delivered. It is ten days since the slate was written and it has not been delivered and nobody has asked her a sixth time and nobody is going to this week, and the not-knowing is the entry and it has a date on it and a date on it is the whole of what it has got.**

The chair is on the landing outside that door. It came from the hall below on the second day of the month after this one and it has no price, no date, no right of refusal and no page under it, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf refused in about four seconds to let a man of thirty-two put a date on it, and the reason is on a page in a book in this city: a page with a date on it can be taken down on a date, and then the chair is a chair that nobody can ask anything about, and a chair is the only thing in this city that is allowed to be nothing.

Nobody sat in it. It is not a seat. It is a chair.

---

**And then a woman of twenty-four said the thing that made the room stop, and she said it about nine seconds after somebody had used the word compact for the fifth time in four days, and what she said was that nobody in that room knew what the word meant, and that a thing that means whatever the room says it means on the morning is not a compact.**

"**It is a document,**" she said. "**That is all it is. It is the first document anybody in this city has wanted to travel, and every other thing we have built in a hundred and thirty-one days we built on purpose so that it could not. And the finding is not that we are frightened. The finding is that we have been working for a hundred and thirty-one days without noticing that the only instrument we have ever had is a room, and a room does not go anywhere, and in twenty-three days a room is going to be asked to be a compact.**"

**And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall read the arithmetic out loud, and a girl of seventeen wrote it down in a column four lines high in his words and not in hers, and it is four lines and not a paragraph and it is on the page.**

> **A case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one and a case came up on the twenty-sixth. Eighteen days down. Eighteen days up. The office is nine hundred miles away and there is no road in this world that reaches the building. A document written today arrives on the thirty-ninth day, which is three days after the hearing, if it is read on the day it arrives, which is a thing no office has ever done for anybody.**

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

---

**And then the second half of it, which is worse and which a man of forty-one in a very good coat said in about nine seconds and which is on the page, and he has not been asked anything in a hundred and thirty-one days and has said nothing wrong in every one of them.**

"**None of you has asked who is going to hold it.**" He had not sat down. "**My office has no name for a document that is in a room and not in a case, and I said that in this city on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one and I have not found a name for it in nine days, and I am telling you that the holding is the wrong problem. You are not writing it for me. There is no version of this that is a page I can hold.**"

"Then what is it for," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**For whoever has to be able to say they did not agree to it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

**And it is entered that about four people in that room understood the same sentence and that about four of them had understood it in four different ways, and that the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said out loud that she had understood it twice in her life and that both times had been in a kitchen.**

---

**And then a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who was standing four feet back from the trestle, said the part of it that is the whole of what he said on the forty-first day of the month after this one, and it took about four minutes, and nobody improved on it, and it is entered as what a person said and not as a finding.**

"**A compact is a document, and a document is a description, and a description is a removal with a column for where the person is. So the question is not how to get it there. The question is what happens to a person who is named in it. Every name in it is a line, and every line is a place where somebody can be found, and I have been in this city a hundred and thirty-one days and the only thing I have ever been able to do for anybody is read a thing, and on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one a page turned out to be a description of a district and the column was me.**"

He put his right hand flat on the trestle, palm down, and did not look at it.

"**So there are two documents and only one of them is a person. The one that travels is a description. The one that is read out in a room is a person. If those two ever say different things, this city will believe the one that travels, and it has believed that one every time for a hundred and thirty-one days.**"

"And that is the argument against it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**That is the argument for writing it twice and putting it in two places, and it is not an argument for me and I am not going to be in either place.**"

---

**And then the room found out what it had actually been arguing about for four days, and the finding is that a document is a thing that can be un-refused by a person who reads it, and the fourth refusal of a village of nine households is a refusal with nothing under it and is held up by luck, and a woman of seventy said on the thirty-eighth day of the month after this one that a refusal with nothing under it can be un-refused by anybody who never heard it, and that in about two months there is going to be a piece of paper in a building nine hundred miles away that says a village agreed to something and nothing anywhere that says she said no.**

"**So it cannot live in the room either,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. "**And it cannot go in a case, and it is not a Crown instrument, and I will not have my name in it, and I have said no three times in a hundred and thirty-one days and I am not going to find a fourth thing to say no to in the last week before a hearing.**"

Somebody said the word *slate* and somebody else said the word *stone*, and a girl of seventeen said both out loud, in that order, in about four seconds, and then said the reason.

"**A slate goes in a book that travels. A stone nine miles up a road cannot be read and a household that keeps it may take it down on a date and is not asked why twice. So one of the two is a place a person can be angry at and the other is a place a person can be angry at, and there is no such thing as a document that is only safe.**"

**And a notary of fifty, who has not been in this room for two days and came in for nine minutes at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, said the thing that the room had been standing in the middle of for four days without seeing, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it.**

"**Then write it twice, and put the second one where nobody can get at it, and be very clear with yourselves about which of the two is the one you are writing for. A document that lives in two places is not safer than a document that lives in one. It is twice as many things for a person to be cross with, and that is not nothing, and I would like it entered that I noticed and that it is not modesty.**"

It was entered.

---

**And the compact was named on the forty-first day of the month after this one, in a room in daylight with about nine people in it, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out in a column four lines high and read it back before anybody had asked her to, and it is a new shape and it is dated and it is the eleventh.**

> **THE ELEVENTH SHAPE, and it is dated the forty-first day of the month after this one. A compact of guests: a proposal that persons arriving from another world are guests in this district, that the guesthood may be ended, and that the ending may be asked for by a person. It is local. It is not the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood. It is not the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month and it is not the second answer of the twenty-second and it is not the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first and it is not the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month. It is not the undertaking of the Lantern Concord, which is ours and void. It is not the design of the Quiet Choir, which is not the Crown's. It is not the eighth shape, which came up a river sealed on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one. It is not the ninth shape, a boundary page of the village of Nine Elms. It is not the tenth shape, a table in a coat. It is not the eleventh thing that office put in a case, which is the fourth shape with one more column and has not happened. It is not written down yet, it is not signed, it is not read out, and it does not travel before the hearing.**

**And the eleventh shape has a problem on it that none of the other ten have, and the problem is that it is the first shape anybody in this city has wanted to travel, and a shape that travels stops being a description of this city and becomes a description of a district, and the words *guest* and *revocable* are both a cost and both have a person in them, and a guest is a person you can turn out and a revocable thing is a thing you can take back.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the forty-first day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in twenty-three days and there is one hearing and one clock and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after the hearing has finished. And a box is upstairs with its strap untied and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and not a sixth. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and it names nobody and it may not be answered here and it may not be intercepted and it may not be taken back. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is nineteen days ago, and the water has not been received for sixty-two days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is forty-six days, and one of the forty has not come for seventeen days and nobody has been told. And a Hearthguard of forty-four has gone twenty-one days without anybody in this city asking him about his own eight, in a room, on a different day, and he is the only unpaid debt in this city that is about a person and not about a page. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for forty-six days, and the four who read the join-line have disagreed six times since the first of March and all six entries stand. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty-one days and is not repaired. And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-four, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is eleven, and it has never been made smaller. And a Saturday on which a room found out that its own word was a piece of paper is the thirty-eighth silence and the pattern is thirty-eight for thirty-eight.**

**And the last thing of the forty-first day of the month after this one is a chair on a landing with no date on it, and a shape that is the eleventh and is not written down, and the four lines a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall read out loud are in a column in a girl's book in a market, and a hearing is in twenty-three days.**
