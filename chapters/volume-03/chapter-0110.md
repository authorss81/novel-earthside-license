# Chapter 0110 — The Count of the Seven

On the twentieth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and tenth day of the flood and a Saturday, about nine people came down a nine-mile road in the wrong weather with a count of seven cuts in three hands and no meaning at all in them, and put the count on the second page inside a stone at a fork, because it is the only page in this city that can hold a list and be taken down on a date by a household that is nobody's servant, and the household may take it down and is not asked why twice, and nobody asked, and four days from now the first Wayhouse on that road closes by the right of its own page and nobody is going to be asked why twice about that either.

**The licence is sixty days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the fifty-first time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**A count of the seven is not a finding and was entered as a count, on the twentieth day of the month after this one, in six lines, and nobody improved on it, and the finding of the road was that nobody in this city can say what the seven mean, and that this is now on a page with a date on it instead of only being true.**

> **Seven cuts, on seven stones, further down the road than the eleventh.**
>
> **Four of the seven are one hand. Three of the seven are three hands, and none of the three is the hand of the eleven.**
>
> **One has an older cut under it and the older cut is in the hand of the eleven.**
>
> **One is not letters. It is a figure about two inches high with a bar across the top of it. It may be a number.**
>
> **Two of the seven are a third lower than the other five and the ground is higher there.**
>
> **The seven are in a different order from the eleven. Nobody can say what the seven mean.**

**And a mason of thirty-four years said, in the road, in about nine seconds, and it is on the page, that the last of those six lines is a finding and the five above it are not, and that she entered the finding because a thing this city cannot answer has to be on a page or it does not exist, and that the finding is that nobody can answer it, and that a finding about a failure is the only kind of finding this city has ever written down, and she entered that she knows the difference between that and the other kind.**

---

**And the shrine at a fork at Four Elms was passed on the way down at about the eleventh hour of the morning, and it is a stone with a cut in the flat of it, and the cut is where the water was before the boundary was moved a hundred yards downstream at a public act with witnesses a hundred and forty years ago, and nothing may be worked at it and there is no pattern in it and nobody has ever worked at one, and a woman of about sixty-one keeps it and will not have her name on anything.**

It carries three pages. The first has six things and a price and a right of refusal. The second is a list of what is owed, owed to twelve named people and not to a body, with a quarter of a pound of salt a month written under it and not as another item. The third is a Wayhouse, with a date, a price and a right of refusal. The keeping household may take any of the three down on a date and is not asked why twice.

**And the count of seven went on the second one, because the second one is the only page in four hundred miles that can hold a list of people and cannot be carried to a building nine hundred miles away, and about nine people in a road at a fork worked that out in about nine minutes, and the woman of sixty-one agreed to it in about four seconds and asked for three things.**

"Put it under the twelve," she said. "**And put a line under it that says it is a count and not a debt, and put the date on that line, and put the word *nobody* on the line above it. Because the first line of a page in a stone is a thing everybody can find, and the last line is a thing nobody can find, and I have been keeping this stone for two pages and a fortnight, and I am sixty-one, and in about four years the person holding it is going to be a stranger, and a stranger cannot be angry at a page, and that is exactly why it works.**"

"And nobody is going to ask you why."

"**Nobody is going to ask me why, and that is the fourth thing on a nail in an arch, and it is a question and not a duty, and I have not been asked anything in a hundred and ten days and I intend to go on not being asked for as long as there is anything left to not be asked about.**"

So the count went on the second page inside the stone, under the twelve names, under the price of salt, in a hand that is not the hand of the twelve, in six lines, with the date on it. The keeping household did not read it before it went in and was not asked to and is not asked why twice. And nobody in about nine people at that fork said out loud that a number in a stone is the safest number in the world and also the only one that a clerk nine hundred miles away can do nothing with, and a notary of fifty, who was not there, entered on the twentieth day of the month after this one that she had noticed, and entered that she noticed, and that was the last thing she entered for four days.

---

**And on the way down, on the sixth mile, a boy of nine asked the question that the whole ten days had been walking towards, and he asked it because nobody else had thought of it, and it is six words, and a girl of seventeen read it off the back of a slate where he had written it, and it is in the book in his own hand, and nobody improved on it.**

"**Who is going to be told?**"

Nobody answered for about nineteen seconds.

"That is the hearing," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who said it in about four seconds, and then said, in the local language, four more words, and nobody counted them and it is entered that nobody counted them.

"**We will stand there.** Forty-four days from today, counted from the hundred and twentieth day of this flood. That office gave it as about eleven weeks on the fifty-second day of the new month, which was the seventy-seventh day of this flood, and there is one hearing and one clock, and nobody in this city has moved it, and a hundred and forty years of somebody else's decision is what it is."

"So we go and stand in it," said a docker of fifty-eight.

"**We go and stand in it with about nine people who can say what this city will not write down, and that is not a plan, it is a number of people.**"

**And a woman of twenty-four said that the whole of what he had said was that a hearing is a room, and that a room is the only instrument this city has that an office nine hundred miles away cannot rewrite, and that a room is what a hundred and ten days of this city has been for, and that she would like it entered that a man from another world said the plainest thing anybody has said on this road in about four seconds and then said nothing else for two miles.**

---

**And at about the second hour of the afternoon they came down through the eleven households, and the eleven households keep nine miles of road and nobody has to keep it, and a carter with a barrow of reed came the other way and stopped and looked at nine people coming down a hill in the wrong weather and said nothing and gave them the wall side of the road, and a woman at a gate with a shutter open put out a cup of water to a docker of fifty-eight without being asked and without asking, and a boy of nine counted eleven households and got nine again and told everybody about it twice.**

"That is the whole of the road," said a man of sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years. "**Eleven households, one of whom is a shutter and one of whom is a wall, and a carter who did not ask us a question, and a cup of water from a gate. And none of it is on a page and none of it is in a case and none of it is going to Aurel, and if all eleven of them stopped tomorrow this road would still be here in four months, and if all eleven of them had been asked to keep it in a room in daylight with a date on it, in my experience, we would have about two.**"

"Nobody asked them," said a girl of seventeen.

"**Nobody asked them, and that is the whole of it, and I have been in nine rooms in four days and I am going to say the other half and I am not going to be thanked for it. The two institutions in this city that will still be here in four months are the one where nobody asked and the one nobody can carry. Everything anybody in this city built in a hundred and ten days is on paper, and paper goes in a case, and a case goes to Aurel, and a document that a person can be angry at is also a document an office can act on, and a cup of water at a gate is neither and it is going to outlast all of it.**"

---

**And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon a woman of twenty-four said the thing about the sixteenth, and she said it because a boy of nine had asked her on the second mile whether there was a bed at the top of the road, and there is, and it closes in four days.**

"The page nailed inside the first house has a date on it," she said. "**The sixteenth day of the month after this one. Four days. The keeping household may close it for its own reasons, on that date, and is not asked why, and is not asked a second time. A bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, a quarter of a pound of salt and an hour of washing, open to a list of nobody. And on the sixteenth day of the month after this one the household closes it, and nobody in this city is going to be told why, and nobody is going to ask, and that is not a fault in anybody and it is not a form and it is not going to be in a case.**"

"Can anybody stop it," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, who had carried two benches into a yard in the wrong weather twice in a hundred and ten days and lost a seat twice.

"**No. That is the whole point of it. There is no instrument in four hundred miles that can stop a household closing its own door on a date written on its own page, and the reason there is not is that it was built that way on purpose by four people who had been asked twice and did not want to be asked three times.**"

"Then what do we do about it."

"**We do nothing about it,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing, "**and that is going to be the hardest thing any of us does in the next four days, and I am going to say it now while we are all tired so that nobody in about nine people can be caught out by it on Wednesday: there is a date on a page in a house four miles up this road, and every single thing any of us wants to do about it makes it a case, and a case goes to Aurel, and an office that has a household's date in it has a household's door in it.**"

It is entered that nobody argued, and that about four people in that room at the fourth hour of the afternoon understood that a door in a house on a road is the only kind of protection this city has left that does not turn into a description, and that about four of them wanted to write a notice about it anyway, and did not.

---

**And then, at about the seventh hour of the evening, in this city, in the wrong weather, four people went down two hundred steps of a river wall on a rail that is condemned, because that is what they do twice a day in whatever weather there is, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken, and the reason is the reason it has been since the first of March, and one ruled line in a column is still ruled and still empty, and the dressed edge is dressed and does not narrow and has never said a word.**

"Twenty-five," said a woman of fifty, who is a docker and cannot read and reads the join by standing on it and knowing whether it has moved, which she has entered is not reading.

"**Twenty-four, and one ruled, and the dressed edge is dressed,**" said a man of fifty, a mason, not of the crew, who reads the number. "**And it is the hundred and tenth day and I have been a sixteenth under six times and I am going to be a sixteenth under on the day somebody finally writes the twenty-fifth, and I would like the room to know that I have not got any better and I am not going to pretend to.**"

"Entered," said a girl of seventeen. **And the reason the twenty-fifth reading was not taken is the reason it has not been taken since the first of March, which is the sixty-first day of this flood, and it is in a mason's words, and there is one ruled line in a column and it is still ruled and it is still empty, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken on any day of this chapter and is not going to be taken this week.**

**And a hearing is in forty-four days. And there is one hearing and one clock. And the fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-first time. And the undertaking is void. And the chair of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch. **There have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city, one of them has been refused, and this is the nineteenth silence, and the pattern is nineteen for nineteen, counted silences and not days.**

---

**And the last thing of the twentieth day of the month after this one is a number, and it is not a wall and it is not a door, and it is seven cuts in three hands on a page in a stone nine miles up a road that eleven households keep, and the page is one of three in that stone, and the household that keeps the stone may take that page down on a date, and is not asked why twice, and nobody has asked.**

**And a hearing is in forty-four days. And there is one hearing and one clock. And the meaning of nineteen shapes is unwritten, and the meaning of a room at the far end of a seam is unwritten, and the reason a page ended a document is unwritten, and nobody in this city wrote any of them down, and about nine people went up a road and came back with a number instead, which is not a finding, and is on a page, and is the only thing they have.**
