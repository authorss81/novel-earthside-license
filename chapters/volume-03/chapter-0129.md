# Chapter 0129 — A Woman Who Keeps a Register

On the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twenty-ninth day of the flood and a Thursday, Tamsin Quill stood in a converted salt store in front of about forty people where the Concord's roll of public business is nailed under oiled cloth on the north wall, and she had not stood in a room in this city in seventy-nine days, and she brought a single sheet folded into eight that she has carried in a coat for seven years, and she did not read it out, and she said one thing about the top of it, and a man of forty-one in a very good coat said the one thing in that room that nobody else could say, and a count was written in a book and not read out, and one line went onto a wall, and the sheet went back into a coat.

**The licence is seventy-nine days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is forty-four days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventieth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty-eight days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And a person writing in a room and a person standing in a room are not the same thing, and she has been insisting on that distinction for a hundred and twenty-nine days without once having had to stand in one.**

She writes the roll, and she has written in this room every week since the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of this flood, and that is seventy-nine days, and she came today because a girl of seventeen told a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, in about nine seconds on the thirty-third day of the month after this one, that the heading of a boundary page a hundred and forty years old is a day of a public act with witnesses, and the woman told a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, and the man told her.

Nobody sent for her. **And it is entered that she came on a Thursday in daylight, which is a day nobody in this room chose, and that a woman of twenty-four said afterward that this was the first time in a hundred and twenty-nine days that anybody had arrived here who had not been asked to.**

"You have not been in a room in seventy-nine days," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I have been in about nine rooms in this city in a hundred and twenty-nine days and I have stood in two of them, a gate and a floor, and I have written in about nine hundred, and that is not the same, and I would like this room to know that it is not the same before anything else in this room gets said.**"

She put the sheet on the trestle and did not open it.

---

**And the sheet is a single sheet folded into eight and it is soft along the folds from seven years of being carried in a coat, and it is a table of every attempt this city has made to move the old river boundary, and it has eleven lines, and it is a new shape and it is dated, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out in a book in a column four lines high and read it back before anybody had asked her to.**

> **THE TENTH SHAPE, and it is dated. A table in a single sheet folded into eight, carried privately in a coat for seven years, of every attempt this city has made to move the old river boundary. Eleven lines. The oldest line is a public act with witnesses a hundred and forty years ago. It is local. It is not a Crown instrument. It is not the notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood and it is not the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month and it is not the second answer of the twenty-second and it is not the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first and it is not the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month. It is not the undertaking of the Lantern Concord, which is ours and void. It is not the design of the Quiet Choir, which is not the Crown's. It is not the eighth shape, which came up a river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one. It is not the ninth shape, which is a boundary page of the village of Nine Elms. It has not been in a room in seven years and it is not going into a case.**

"I am not going to read it out," she said.

"**That is not a reason. I have carried nine rates in my head for four years and I have been asked to be on a board and refused.**"

"**Here is the reason and it is a real one. Line one is that act. The one a woman of seventy carried into a room on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one in a fold of oilcloth, and gave to a girl of seventeen and not to anybody else. The heading of that page is the day of that act, and the act is the only attempt ever carried out in living memory, by three jurisdictions together, at a public act, with witnesses, and its cost column says the bottom field failed in that year and four hundred people were rehoused and the boundary was not moved again. Line seven is a cost column about the Reed margin and the villages that take water from it.**"

"And the other nine," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who was standing four feet back from the trestle.

"**Are they counts,**" he said, "**or are they places where a number went in.**"

"**Line one is a place where a number went in, and so are four others, and that is why it is in a coat and not in a room, and you are the only person in four hundred miles who could have asked me that, and you asked it in about four seconds and in the wrong order, after the reason and not before it.**"

"I asked it in the order it was in my head."

"**Then that is a finding about you and not about me, and there is a girl of seventeen writing it down, and I would like her to keep the two halves apart.**"

It was kept apart.

"**The other nine are not yours and I am not going to read them. I have been asked four times in this city for a translation and I have refused four times, and my reason is a thing a person said to me on a stair a long time ago, and it is not the reason another person in this room gives on a wharf, and neither of us has heard the other one do it. A person who reads a table out in a room in the local language has made the table the account of the thing, and the account travels, and a case goes down this river in about five weeks.**"

"Then what did you come for."

---

**And Tamsin Quill said the thing she has been not saying for eight days, and it took her about four minutes, and it was not about a man of thirty-two, and about four people in that room understood that and nobody said so, and a girl of seventeen wrote all of it out in her own hand and did not improve on any of it.**

"**I have had a table about that act in a coat for seven years and I have not put it in a room. On the twenty-ninth day a woman of seventy carried a page about the same act into a room and gave it to a girl of seventeen and not to anybody else, and I was not offered it, and I was not in the room, and I have been not saying something about that for eight days and I am going to say it now and then I am going to stop.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**I keep the public register of this city and I am on a roll, and my name is on the front of it in the second line and has been for about twenty years. And the page that came into that room on the twenty-ninth day was a Crown district form about a public act, and it went to a person on no roll, and a man on no roster read it out loud in the local language and found out it was a description of a district. Both of those were correct, and the finding is not that somebody was rude to me.**"

"Then what is it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**It is that I have been holding a document about that act out of a book for seven years on purpose, and keeping it out of a book is the single most consequential thing I have done in seven years, and it is why a woman of seventy had to carry a page down a road in a cart herself. There was no book. There was me, and a coat, and a decision I made when I was about twenty-two that a table about a boundary is a record and a record is a door, and I was right. And I have been right for seven years and about four people in this city have worked out the cost of it, and one of them is Adrian Vale, who told us in a room on the twenty-ninth day that a translation is a description, and he is not the one who should have had to say it.**"

A man of thirty-two said nothing for about nineteen seconds.

"I am not asking you for anything," she said. "**I have not come here to hand you a document and I have not come here to be forgiven a thing I was not asked for and I am not going to apologise in a room to a man who did not ask, because that is a speech about somebody and I do not do them. I have come to say the top of line one out loud, once, in daylight, in a room, and to have a date on it, and then to go and write a roll.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

---

**And a man of forty-one in a very good coat, who has been in this city a hundred and twenty-nine days and has read every book that office keeps, said the one thing in that room that nobody else could say, and he said it in about nine seconds, and it is entered that he said nothing wrong in a hundred and twenty-nine days and is not convicted of anything and is not going to be.**

"**My office has no name for what you are doing. I have looked for one for eleven days. A document that is in a room and not in a case is not a document, is not a refusal, and is not a compliance, and it is not on any form that office keeps, and there is a column for a document and there is a column for a refusal and there is nothing at all for a thing that was written down in front of nine people and stayed in the room, and about four hundred years of that office have run on the two columns and one was added in about a hundred and twenty years.**"

"**It exists and it is the only thing in this city that office cannot act on, and I have been trying since about the fourteenth week of this flood to find a way to act on it, and I have not found one, and I would like the room to know that I looked, and I would like the room to notice that I am the man whose whole life has been finding ways to act on things and I have failed at it, and that is not modesty either.**"

It is entered. **And nobody asked him anything, and he was not asked to explain either name, and he said neither of them, and in a book on the trestle a number was written in his own hand and dated, and it was not read out, and it was eighty-nine, and it is counted from the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the thing about the ninety, and it is the fifth clause of the notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood, and it is a document this city cannot answer and did not write, and about four people in that room started to say something about it and did not, and nobody in that room answered it, and it was not answered.**

"There is a clause," she said, "**and a notary read it out on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one and I have been carrying it about ever since. A person who could not afford to answer an instrument has not answered it. Forty-one of ninety. And a line in a table in a coat about a boundary says four hundred people were rehoused in a year, and that is a cost column, and a cost column is a place where a cost goes.**"

"Don't," said a notary of fifty.

"I have not said anything."

"**You were about to put them together, and I have been in a room with about four people in it for a hundred and twenty-nine days who put things together, and there is a line in there with twelve names on it and a line under them saying it is a count and not a debt, and it is a count. It is a count of the seven stones nobody had looked at. It is not a count of forty-one people who could not pay, and I am not going to be in a room where it is, and I am fifty and I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I am putting my hand flat on the table.**"

Nobody touched the table.

"It is a count," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, after about four seconds. "**You are right and I was about to do it and I would like it entered that I was about to do it, because a woman of twenty-four is going to write down that I did not do it and the reason I did not do it is that a notary of fifty put her hand on a table.**"

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the evening she wrote one line on the roll of public business in her own hand, and read it out in the local language, and put the date on it, and a girl of seventeen copied it, and the sheet with eleven lines on it went back into a coat.**

> **On the twenty-second day of the month after this one a village of nine households refused a fourth time, as a person and not as a village. The fourth refusal has no document under it. The only record of it in four hundred miles is a date in chalk on nine doors. Nine is a count and not a list.**

"It is on a wall in a room," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and a wall in a room is read by about forty people and is not a column, and a column goes in a case, and it took me a hundred and twenty-nine days to be able to say that sentence.**"

**And Tamsin Quill said she had been able to say it in about four seconds on the second day of this flood, to a man who did not know what it meant, and had not said it since because nobody had asked, and that is the whole of the seventy-nine days.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one, and a hearing is in twenty-five days and there is one hearing and one clock, and about nine people have said out loud that they will stand in it and nobody has been asked whether they will, and nothing has been written down about any of the nine. And a box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one and it is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and it is not opened and the strap on it is still untied. And the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month, its office's answer of the twenty-second, and the instrument of the twenty-second are three documents, and this room called them three documents, and in about five weeks one of them is going to arrive described as the other two. And a page that ends a document is spent and six rooms have failed to explain it in the same place, and the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for forty-four days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and twenty-nine days and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty-nine days. And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused, and a Thursday on which a woman who keeps a register said one line out loud and put the sheet back in a coat is the thirty-sixth silence and the pattern is thirty-six for thirty-six.**

**And the last thing of the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one is a roll of public business under oiled cloth on the north wall of a converted salt store with one line on it that was written this evening and has no name on it, and a single sheet folded into eight soft along the folds from seven years of being carried in a coat, and a hearing is in twenty-five days.**
