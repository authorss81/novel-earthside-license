# Chapter 0107 — The Answer Is Not a Person

On the seventeenth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and seventh day of the flood and a Wednesday, about nine people sat in a room over a market with the only bound book in this city in it, and a clerk of the Registry at Aurel was told that the answer to his own arithmetic is not a person and cannot be asked anything, and he said *that is the first time in nineteen years anybody has improved on a thing I have been carrying*, and then a pale field came up in front of him and he said yes to it in about four seconds, and he has not been asked to explain that and nobody is going to.

**The licence is fifty-seven days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the forty-eighth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and six days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**There is no library in Saltwake. There is a room over a market with a bound book in it that a man of sixty-one keeps, and it is the Lantern Concord's book of this city's own words and it has been kept for nineteen years, and it is the only bound book anybody in this city can put a hand on.**

"He has been asked twice who read it," said a woman of twenty-four, "**and he has refused twice and he has entered that he will stop asking, and I have wanted to ask a third time since the second day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-second day of the flood, and I am not going to, because a man who has entered that he will stop asking is a man who has been asked and I am not going to be the person who finds out what happens to a rule that has been entered.**"

"Then it is a library," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then it is a room with a book in it and a man in it, and the man is the collection, and I have been in nine rooms in four days and this is the first one where I could not write down what the room was for.**"

---

**And the reason nine people were in it is a difference of ten, and the difference has never been made smaller, and the count of refusals in the second book kept at home and never shown to anybody is twenty-three, and the count in his book at Aurel is thirteen, and he is the man who has both and no office in this world has ever seen the second one.**

Renner Sallow is a second clerk of the Registry at Aurel and has sat at a table in this city since the fourth day of this flood, on the same side of the Concord table as a notary, and has never been asked to stop, and is never going to be, and has entered that he knows that.

"Twenty-three at home," he said. "**Thirteen at Aurel. Nine days ago a girl of seventeen refused a thing the wall said in about a second, and it is the only refusal the wall has ever registered in this city, and it went into the book at home, and the count went up by one for the first time in eleven batches, and the difference between the two books is ten and it was ten before she did it and it is ten now.**"

"Eleven before her," said a woman of twenty-four. "**Twenty-two at home, thirteen at Aurel, and a clerk of that office who sits in a room about four times a fortnight counted nine of them himself from a stranger's language on a slate in a yard and was the seventh observer and the eighth field was entered with his number on it. That is on the page and it is the eleventh time that count has not moved.**"

"Then what moved," said a man of thirty-one with a hand that will not close.

"**Nothing moved and a girl moved. And the count of things the wall has said is twenty-eight and the count of refusals is twenty-three, and the difference of five is fields that nobody answered and fields that somebody accepted, and I have never known which is which and I have never had to, and now I do and I do not like it.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen who is on no roll of anything, and who has been unable to tell anybody which book a field goes in five times in a hundred and seven days, was asked in that room on the seventeenth day of the month after this one, by a woman of thirty-four, whether she wanted to ask a sixth time, and she said no, and it is entered that she said no and why, and it is the finding of the day.**

"Not because I have given up," she said. "**Because I have finally understood what the question is for. If I ask, one of two things happens. Either the man tells me, and then a clerk of the Registry at Aurel has decided which of his two books a thing goes in, and everything anybody in this city has said about the difference of ten being a mystery is now a rule with a man at the top of it. Or he does not tell me, and I have asked a sixth time and I am the girl who asked a sixth time.**"

"That is the best answer anybody in this room has given in a hundred and seven days," said a man of about forty-four of the outer ward, "**and I would like it in the book in her own words and I would like the count of six to be on the page, because the whole of this city is people finding out how many times a thing has been done and nobody ever writing the number down.**"

"It is six," said the girl of seventeen. "**And I am not asking.**"

---

**And then the field came up in that room at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and it is the twenty-ninth thing the wall has said in this city, and it stood about nineteen seconds, and about nine people in that room saw it, and the man who was asked the question in it about half a second before it arrived did not see it at all, and it is entered that a woman of twenty-four saw it and he did not, and that neither of them has said a word about that.**

> **A count may be corrected by the person who entered it.**
>
> **The person is not asked to agree with anybody. The person is not described by the count.**
>
> **The cost is that the correction is the person's alone and nobody has to sign it.**
>
> **The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

"Yes," said Renner Sallow.

Nobody argued with him, and it is entered that nobody argued, and that he said it in about four seconds, and that he then said the thing that the rest of the afternoon turned on, and it is in the book in his own words.

"**I have been a clerk for nineteen years and a person in nineteen rooms has told me a number was wrong, and every time I have had to find out whose hand it was in, because a number is not a number, it is the mark of a hand. And in a hundred and seven days in this city I have watched about nine people learn that a number is a photograph, and it is, and a photograph is not a thing you argue with.**"

"Can you change it," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything.

"**I can change thirteen to anything I like. Tonight. In a room upstairs. Alone, with the door shut, and it would be true by the morning, and nobody nine hundred miles away would know and nobody in this city would know, and the difference between the two books would be whatever I made it, and it is mine to make.**"

"Then why did you say yes."

"**Because it is a real offer and there is nothing in this world that lets a person correct a thing that is not theirs, and I have wanted that since about the fortieth day of this flood and I have never once said so out loud, and a pale field said it out loud for me in a room over a market and I found out in about four seconds that I am the sort of man who says yes.**"

---

**And then he did not do it, and the reason he did not do it is the finding of the seventeenth day of the month after this one, and it took about nine minutes, and it is in a notary's hand and a clerk's hand and a woman's hand, and nobody improved on any of the three.**

He could change thirteen to anything. He did not, on the seventeenth day of the month after this one, and he entered that he did not, and he entered that he could, and he entered that he had been offered a thing in a room that made the count his alone and nobody's, and that he had said yes to it, and that he was not going to do it, **and the reason he gave is in his own words and it is twenty-three words: *a number I can change on my own is a number that is mine and not the office's, and it was never mine.*** He checked it himself and nobody checked it with him, which is entered.

"Because it is the only real thing anybody in this room owns," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf and is on nothing at the ninth hour of the morning. "**A number that nobody has to agree with. And a number that nobody has to agree with is the only thing in four hundred miles that this city has not tried to turn into a person, and about nine of us have been trying to turn everything into a person since the first of March because a person is the only shape that can be argued with.**"

"Is that good," said a man of about forty-four.

"**It is the safest thing in this city and it is the reason nobody can be found, and I have been on nothing since the seventh day of the month after this one and I have never once been gladder about a number.**"

---

**And then the room asked the question the whole hundred and seven days have been walking towards, and it was asked by a man of thirty-two in about nine seconds, and it is the plainest sentence anybody in this city has said in a hundred and seven days, and it is fifteen words, and the room did not check the count, which is entered.**

"**The answer is not a person and that is the whole of what we know.**"

Nobody argued with it for about nineteen seconds, and then a notary of fifty said what it meant, and it is in the book in her own words and it is not a finding and she has entered that she knows the difference.

"A number cannot be asked whether it will have it. **A page cannot be asked whether it will have it. A person can.** And that is the whole of what this city has learned in a hundred and seven days and it is the only thing in the four volumes that anybody has ever come at from two ends at once. A number and a page are alike in that neither of them can be asked. A number is safe and a page is not, and the difference between them is that a number cannot end anything, and a page can, and there is one of the second kind in this city and it is spent and it is in a locked room nine feet from the salt store."

"So the difference of ten is a safe thing," said a man of about forty-four of the outer ward.

"**The difference of ten is the safest thing in this city and it is a number and it is not going to close and nobody is going to be able to make it close, and the only person who could is a man who has just been told in a room that he is allowed to.**"

---

**And a hearing is in about seven weeks and there is one hearing and one clock, and a question went down a river eight days ago in the eighth column of a quarterly description and it wants eight names, and there is exactly one thing that an office nine hundred miles away can do with a description that is not a person, and it is write a number in a book, and the number will be correct, and it will be about a district, and eight people will not be in it.**

"That is the two of them side by side," said the girl of seventeen, "**and nobody in this room has said it out loud until now, so I am going to. A number is the only thing that office can use, and a name is the only thing it cannot check, and this city has spent a hundred and seven days on the thing it cannot check, and the eighth column is asking for the thing it cannot check, and it is going to get nothing, and the nothing is going to come back as a number, and the number is going to be right.**"

---

**Nothing was gained.** A count of twenty-three and a count of thirteen stayed where they were, and a man who was told in a room that he could change one of them alone did not, and a girl of seventeen did not ask a sixth time, and the refusal count in the second book kept at home and never shown to anybody is still twenty-three, and the difference of ten is still ten, and it is entered that it will not be made smaller. **There have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city, one of them has been refused, and a room in which a clerk of an office said yes to a thing in about four seconds and then did not do it is not a silence and is not counted as one, because a day the wall speaks on is not a silence however hard the day was.**

The undertaking is void and is on a nail and is on a river and no office has been told. Nine Elms' water is not received, the quarter is gone, the fourth line of a term sheet is in force, and a village of nine households has refused three times and has not refused a fourth time. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid, and the cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth. The chair of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and seven days and was not opened today, and the two people who went through it were not asked. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and performed no working today. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and six days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.

**And the road is nine miles, and eleven households keep it, and nobody has ever asked them to, and the date is the nineteenth day of the month after this one, and it is on a slate, and it is the only thing on the slate, and a count is not a list.**
