# Chapter 0135 — A Figure on the End of an Entry

On the forty-fifth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirty-fifth day of the flood and a Wednesday, thirty-nine of forty men turned up under the near gate in the wrong weather on a rail that has been condemned for a month, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall paid one of them a day, and the finding of the forty-fifth day of the month after this one is that the person who pays is on the page and the person who is paid is not, and that this is the first page in a hundred and thirty-five days that has been the right way round.

**The licence is eighty-five days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventy-sixth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirty-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

They go down at about the seventh hour and they come up at about the eleventh, and the rail is condemned, which means a man has a hand on it the whole way, and everybody who has used it in the last month has said nothing about that and has been right not to.

**Thirty-nine of forty turned up. They have not been paid since the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty days, and there is no twelfth entry in the cost column and there is not going to be one today, and the column has eleven entries in one month and no figure on the end of any of them, and a cost that stops is a cost that has stopped and is never to be entered as paid by anybody on any day for any reason.**

Nobody at the gate said anything to any of them, because there is nothing at the gate to say, and a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall has said that in three different rooms in ten days and it is on the page.

---

**And then a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put a slate on the flat of the top step and asked one man whether he would take a day, and he asked him and not the other thirty-eight, and the asking is the whole of it, and it took about four seconds, and the man said yes in about four seconds and neither of them made a speech.**

"**One day,**" said the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall. "**Not the fifty. I want you to hear the shape of it before you say anything. It is one day and it is out of a stall and it is not the fifty days and it does not take the fifty days off anybody, and I am going to write a figure on the end of a line and my hand and today's date, and I am going to leave your name off it, and if you want your name on it you will not get it, and that is not me being hard on you, it is the only way I have found to do this without making a page that is a roll.**"

"**Then whose name is on it.**"

"**Mine. A figure, my hand, and the forty-fifth day of the month after this one. And that is the whole of it and it is the first time in fifty days that a figure has gone on the end of a line in this city.**"

---

**And a man of fifty-eight, who does not write and has not written in the whole of a hundred and thirty-five days of this flood, was asked whether the man had said it right, and said nine words, and a girl of seventeen counted them and nobody checked her count.**

"**You paid him and you signed it. Good. That is the two.**"

**And a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall asked the second half, which is what everybody in that place wanted to ask and which is the reason the two matters.**

"**Why is that the two.**"

"**Because a person who is owed is a person somebody can point at. I do not want to be able to point at him. If he is owed on a page he is owed to me, and a man who is owed to a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall is a man with somebody, and in about four months that somebody is going to be asked a question by somebody nine hundred miles away.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**And that is the whole of fifty days,**" he said, "**and I have been owed it too, and I am not going to say the rest of it on a stair.**"

---

**And the man who was paid asked for nothing and was thanked by four people, and refused the thanking in about nine seconds, and the refusing is on the page and it is his.**

"**Do not,**" he said. "**If I am thanked then I have had something. I have had a day and a figure and a hand on a slate and my name is not on it, and if you make a thing of it in front of thirty-eight men then in about a month every one of them knows I got something, and then in about two months one of them asks why, and there is no answer to that on a stair.**"

"And who is owed the fifty days," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**The same forty men. That is what a favour is. It is a thing that stops, and it stopped the moment it was given to one of us and not to the other thirty-eight, and I knew that when I said yes and I am not going to stand here and have it explained to me.**"

He went back down the steps and held the rail the whole way, because the rail is condemned.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the sentence that is the finding of the forty-fifth day of the month after this one, and it took about nine seconds and it is on a page in a book in this city with a date on it.**

"**A person who carries a cost is a person who can be asked.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**That is the whole of it and about four people in this city have wanted to do it for a hundred and thirty-five days, and the reason nobody has is that this city has spent a hundred and thirty-five days working out that a name is a description, and a description is a removal, and so we worked out that asking a person is dangerous, and we have been using that to not ask people for a hundred and thirty-five days.**"

She was standing at the top of the steps and she was not looking at anybody.

"**It is dangerous and it is the only instrument anybody has. A household is a person and can be asked and can refuse. A person is a person and can be asked. And the reason we do not do it is not that we worked it out. It is that we are frightened, and we found a rule that says not to, and we have been hiding behind the rule since about the fortieth day.**"

"You have said no four times in a hundred and thirty-five days," said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board.

"**And the fourth of them is yours and not mine, and you were not in the room when I said it, and I have not improved on it in your mouth and I am not going to.**"

---

**And then she went down about nine steps, which she had not done in a hundred and thirty-five days, and stood on a step that is condemned, and asked the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall what asking a person who carries a cost would look like if anybody in this city ever did it, and it took him about nine seconds to answer and it was not an answer anybody wanted.**

"**It looks like a room,**" he said. "**And a date on it. And a person asking and not a page. And it looks like the twenty-second day of the month after this one in a gateway at a village of nine households, when a woman of seventy said no as a person and not as a village, and the no went nowhere and there is a mark in chalk on nine doors and no paper at all.**"

"**That was a village,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**That is what I am saying and I have been saying it since the thirty-eighth day of the month after this one and I would like it entered that a woman said it to me on a stair this afternoon and that she had not noticed it for five days, and that noticing it and saying it are two different things and this city has got very good at one of them.**"

She came back up the nine steps and did not say anything else about it, and a girl of seventeen wrote one line and did not read it out, and the line is that a room with a date on it is the instrument and there has not been one made yet.

---

**And a girl of seventeen wrote the whole thing down in a book that cannot travel, and read it back before anybody had asked her to, and then read the last four lines of it again out loud because she wanted them in the local language in a room, and the four lines are the finding, and nobody improved on them.**

> **On the forty-fifth day of the month after this one a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall paid one man of about thirty-nine, of about forty who turn up under the near gate, the wage of one day, out of a stall, and it was entered with a figure on the end of a line and his hand and the date, and the man who was paid is not named. The fifty days are not reduced by it. The thirty-eight are not paid by it. It is a favour and a favour is a thing that stops. The person who pays is on the page. The person who is paid is not. This is the first page in a hundred and thirty-five days that is the right way round.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the forty-fifth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in nineteen days and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there nineteen days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five and was asked for a sixth time on Sunday and said no in about four seconds, and she is not going to be asked again this week. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and it names nobody and it is still on the water. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is twenty-three days ago, and the water has not been received for sixty-six days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and the fourth line of a term sheet is in force and the undertaking that would have paid for it is void and no office has been told. And one of the forty has not come for twenty-one days and nobody has been told, on purpose, and the decision is on a slate with a date on it and no name on it. And a Hearthguard of forty-four has gone twenty-five days without anybody in this city asking him about his own eight, in a room, on a different day. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for fifty days, and six disagreements stand. And a page in a stone nine miles up a road says a woman of forty-four of Corrow is owed a figure and a decision, and on the thirtieth day of the month after this one a rule under the eleventh line gave her both without asking her, and the page is now wrong and cannot be corrected. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty-five days. And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-four, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is eleven, and it has never been made smaller. And a Wednesday on which one day was paid to one man out of a stall with the payer's name on the slate and the paid man's name off it is the forty-second silence and the pattern is forty-two for forty-two.**

**And the last thing of the forty-fifth day of the month after this one is a slate with a figure on the end of a line and a hand and a date on it, and a cost column with eleven entries in it and no figure on the end of any of them and no twelfth, and a hearing in nineteen days.**
