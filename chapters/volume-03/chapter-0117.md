# Chapter 0117 — A Description of a Box

On the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and seventeenth day of the flood and a Saturday, about nine people spent about four hours in a room with a door trying to describe a wooden box, attempted it six times, got six descriptions, and found out that every line of every one of the six was about a person and not about the box, and a notary of fifty asked the one question nobody in that room could answer and nobody answered it, and a man of sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years asked to see the wax and was refused and said the reason in his own words.

**The licence is sixty-seven days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the fifty-eighth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and sixteen days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the reason they were in a room with a door at the seventh hour of the morning on the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one is a field that came up in the air in a room over a market at about the fourth hour and nine minutes of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth and was accepted in about four seconds by a notary of fifty, and the field said the description is the only account of the thing that will exist, and the cost was entered, and there is no further cost.**

"We are writing it down," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, "**because it has to be written down and not because anybody wants to. Yesterday the wall offered us the only account and a person took it in four seconds, and a person did not think about it, and she entered that she did not think about it. I am the stall and I am not the person, and I have been the person three times in a hundred and seventeen days and I have got nothing.**"

"It does not have to be a good description," said a man of about sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years. "**It has to be the first one. I have copied about nine thousand sentences in nineteen years and I can tell you exactly what happens when two people copy the same one: the second one is a photograph of the first one, and it is true, and it is not a description, and nobody can be angry at it.**"

Nobody improved on that. **It is entered that nobody improved on it and that it is the first time in a hundred and seventeen days that a man of sixty-one has said anything anybody wrote down without arguing with it first.**

---

**And the four who wrote were a man of sixty-eight who has carried nine cases in his life, a woman of twenty-four, a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, and a girl of seventeen who cannot read the local hand and wrote down what the other three said, and the count of attempts is six, and it is entered with the date on it.**

**Attempt one, in about nine minutes, was a list of materials.** It is a foot and a half long. It is wood. It has a strap. It has a wax. It has a second strap and the second strap is not tied. The seal is flat and has writing round the edge of it. Four of the four lines under the seal say that the district shall name one person to be heard. That is a description of a thing and it took nine minutes and it is correct and it is useless, and the reason it is useless is that any nine hundred miles of river can be described in nine minutes by anybody who has a stick.

**Attempt two, in about nineteen minutes, was a list of the way the box got here.** It came up. It is the first one in a hundred and forty years. It went up a river in the middle of the water. It was four hundred yards abreast of a case going down. It came in on the tide at the fifth hour. A man of sixty-eight was on the quay and can say so. That is a description of a thing and it is about four men and a tide.

**Attempt three was a list of the numbers.** It is one box. It is the eighth shape out of that office. It went on the nineteenth day of the new month, which was the forty-fourth day of this flood. It arrived on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood. It is seventy-two days on the water and nobody can check the seventy-two. That is a description of a thing and it is four numbers and a column.

**Attempt four, at about the eleventh hour of the morning, was the first one anybody noticed, and it took about nine minutes to notice and nobody noticed it while it was happening.**

"There is a line about the strap," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"Everybody has written a line about the strap."

"**The line about the strap is the only line in four attempts that is about the box. Everything else is about the water, or about four men, or about a column. I have read four attempts of a description of a box and the only thing in them that is the box is a strap, and a strap is a thing a person does when they tie a thing, and a strap is not tied, and somebody undid it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"That is the first true thing anybody has said about this object in a hundred and seventeen days," said a man of sixty-one.

**Attempt five was the good one, and the good one is the reason this city cannot be trusted with a box, and it took about nineteen minutes and about four people argued about it for half of those nineteen minutes and it is nineteen lines long and it is a page with a heading and a date on it, and the heading is the kind of heading this city has invented nine times in a hundred and seventeen days, and a notary of fifty, who was not in the room when the fifth attempt was made, has since read it and entered that the heading is *the box as received*.**

**And attempt six was abandoned at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and the count of six is entered with the date on it, and the reason it was abandoned is in the next part of this, and nobody argued about the six.**

---

**And then a notary of fifty read the whole of the fifth attempt aloud in about nineteen seconds and then read the first attempt again, and then asked the question nobody in that room could answer, and it is four words, and nobody answered it, and it is entered that nobody answered it, and that a girl of seventeen counted the four and that the room did not check her count.**

"It is not that it is a bad description," she said. "**It is that it is not a description. It is a page about the people who have been near it. Nineteen lines, and sixteen of them are about where a person was standing when this box was looked at, and which of them had touched it, and which of them had not, and which of them read the seal and which of them did not. Three of the nineteen are about the box. They are the third line and the fifth line and the eleventh, and the eleventh is a strap that is not tied.**"

**And it is entered that at this point a girl of seventeen said, out loud, that this city had already done this, and that it had done it in four lines, and that the four lines had been about a room at the far end of a seam and had been entered on the fourteenth day of the month after this one, and that a mason of thirty-four years had said the sentence about it in seventeen words and then seven, and that nobody had written anything on the wall, and that a notary of fifty entered on the seventeenth day of the month after this one that she had noticed and entered that she noticed.**

"Say the four words," said the man of sixty-one.

And Sera Vail, fifty, a notary of twenty-six years, in the flat voice:

"**What is this about?**"

Nobody answered for about nineteen seconds.

"Nobody is going to answer that," said a woman of twenty-four. "**And nobody is going to answer it tomorrow either, and the reason is that we know the answer, and the answer is nine people, and a description of nine people is a column, and a column is a roll, and this is the ninth roll this city has built in a hundred and seventeen days and the eighth one goes to Aurel in a quarter.**"

"Then burn it," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**No. We are not burning it and here is why, and it is the same reason as everything else in this city. In about eight weeks a man or a woman in a room nine hundred miles away is going to be asked what is in a box, and if there is no description then the answer is a description, and the answer is somebody's impression, and an impression is a person. And the finding of the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one is that a description of a wooden box is a description of the nine people who have handled it, and it is entered with the date on it, and a thing this city cannot answer has to be on a page or it does not exist.**"

**And a notary of fifty said in about four seconds that the cost she had accepted in four seconds on the previous day had arrived, and that nobody had to do anything to make it arrive, and that it had arrived in about four hours, and that she had entered on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one that she had not thought about the offer and that the reason she had not thought about it is now on a page nineteen lines long with a heading and a date on it, and that this is the third time in a hundred and seventeen days that a thing she accepted without thinking had cost somebody something, and that she is not going to be asked about it twice, and that nobody in that room asked her about it twice.**

---

**And then a man of sixty-one asked to see the wax, and it was the last thing that happened in that room on the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one, and he asked it standing up, and he is a man who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years and has been in about nine rooms in seventeen days and has not slept in any of them, and the asking took him about nine seconds and the refusal took about four.**

"I would like to see the writing round the edge of the seal," he said. "**I am not going to tell anybody what it says. I have copied other men's sentences for nineteen years and I have never once asked to see the original and there is a reason and I would like to state the reason before I am refused.**"

"**You have been refused,**" said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, "**and the reason is mine and it is the reason I gave yesterday, and I am not going to give it twice, and it is four seconds long and it is on a page.**"

"Then I will give mine," said the man of sixty-one, "**because mine is not yours. I have copied about nine thousand sentences in nineteen years and every one of them I copied before I knew what it said, and not one of them in nineteen years have I been able to put down again afterwards. A mark somebody pressed is a sentence somebody said. If I look at that wax I will have read it, and then I will be a man who has read it, and there is no putting that down."**

And a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said in about four seconds that he had it, and that a woman of about thirty-four had said the same thing in a different way on a wharf on the eleventh day of the month after this one, and that she was not going to claim his, and that the two of them had said one sentence on two days and neither had heard the other do it, and that this city had now said about four sentences twice without knowing it and that this was not a coincidence and that nobody was going to write it down.

Nobody wrote it down.

---

**And nothing else happened on the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one, and the box is in a room with a door and it is not opened, and a nineteen-line page headed *the box as received* is in a girl's book with a date on it and is not a roll and is not going to Aurel, and sixteen of the nineteen lines are about nine people. And a hearing is in thirty-seven days, and there is one hearing and one clock, and the number nine is entered on a page with nobody at the top of it. And the fee of eleven ounces of silver is owing with no date for the fifty-eighth time. And the licence is sixty-seven days old and the seat of holder of the pull is vacant. And the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch said nothing today. And the wall said nothing today, and there have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one of them has been refused, and a Saturday spent failing to describe a box is the twenty-fifth silence, and the pattern is twenty-five for twenty-five.**

**And the last thing of the twenty-seventh day of the month after this one is four words asked by a notary of fifty in a room with a door, and nobody answered, and the four words are the only ones in a hundred and seventeen days that nobody in this city has been able to improve on, and the box is still on the floor and the strap is still untied and nobody has tied it.**
