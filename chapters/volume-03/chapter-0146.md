# Chapter 0146 — Nobody Is Asked a Second Time

On the fifty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and forty-sixth day of the flood and a Sunday, about nine people sat in a kitchen over a wharf and decided, on purpose, not to ask nine people anything, and the decision is on a slate with a date on it and no name on it and no list on it, and it is the fourth page anybody in this city has written about itself.

**The licence is ninety-six days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is sixty-one days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eighty-seventh time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and forty-five days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The kitchen is two rooms over a wharf and it is the best room in this city and it is not a room, and about nine people have eaten in it in a hundred and forty-six days and about nine people have never been in it.

**And the man who proposed it was a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and he proposed it well, and that is on the page, because the fact that a good argument lost is the whole of the finding.**

"**A man of thirty-two said on the forty-ninth day of the month after this one that he will answer the post in a room, in daylight, in the local language, on a day with a date on it, and that everybody who has to live with it will hear him say it, and he named about nine things and four of them were people and one of them was eight houses.**"

"He did," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**Then the nine are in the room, are they not. Not the nine who will stand. The nine he named. You, a girl of seventeen, a notary of fifty, a woman of twenty-nine, about nine men at a gate, a man of forty-four with a floor, and eight houses. And a household is a person. And we have eight days and a hearing, and if we ask those nine properly, in a room, on a day they did not choose, then we know who is going to be standing there when he says it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"That is a good argument," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything, and who had not been asked to be in this kitchen and had come.

"**It is the best argument anybody has made in this kitchen and I am the one who made it and I am going to be the one who takes it back, and I would like it entered that I knew it was the best one.**"

---

**And a girl of seventeen said the reason, and it took her about four minutes, and it is on the page, and she has said this before and has said it better, and nobody improved on her.**

"**We went round nine kitchens.**"

"You did not ask them anything," said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**We went round nine kitchens and asked them nothing and got nine remarks, and a remark is not an answer, and an answer is not a refusal, and a refusal that was never asked for is not a refusal, and I have said all three of those in about four rooms in eleven days and I am saying them in a kitchen now because they are the whole of it.**"

She put both hands on the table.

"**If we ask them now, then we have asked them. And if we have asked them, then whatever they say is an answer, and nine answers is a list, and a list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is, and I have been the person who puts things in columns since the second day of the month after this one and I am not going to do it eleven days before a hearing because a man of about thirty-four made a good argument in a kitchen.**"

---

**And a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall said the other half of it, and it is the half that costs, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it.**

"**She is right and she has been right since the forty-sixth day of the month after this one and I want to say the thing that is on the other side of it and then I am going to shut up about it for a week.**"

"Say it."

"**If the number is eight on the day, nobody will ever be able to say who was not in the room.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the fire went over and somebody opened the damper and nobody said who it was.

"**Nine is on a page with a date on it and nobody at the top of it and about four people have asked for names on it for a hundred and forty-six days and have not been given them, and it is the safest page this city has built, and the reason it is safe is that it cannot be corrected. And if it is eight on the sixty-fourth day, then there is a person in this city who was not in that room, and there is no page anywhere that says who, and there never will be, and we will have chosen that. Not by accident. On purpose, in a kitchen, with nine people in it.**"

"That is true," said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**I know it is true. I have been carrying it since the fortieth day of the month after this one. And I have also noticed that the alternative is nine answers in a book, and I do not know which of the two I would rather be able to look at, and I have noticed that not knowing is a preference and that is the first time in a hundred and forty-six days I have caught myself having one.**"

---

**And then a girl of seventeen said that she would stand up, and nobody wrote it down, and that is on the page.**

"I will stand up."

"**You will not be written,**" said a woman of twenty-four, who has the slate.

"**No. That is why I said it.**"

"It will be a hearing and about two hundred and forty people will be able to hear it and a man is going to refuse a Crown post in it and then somebody is going to read eleven lines out loud and if you are standing up when that happens then you are part of it and there is no page that says you were not asked, because you were not asked, you volunteered, and a thing a person volunteers for is a thing a person can be asked to do again."

"**Then I will stand up and I will say I was not asked,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**and if that is a description of me then it is one I chose in a kitchen and I am seventeen and I have put my name at the top of a page in a hundred and forty-six days and I know exactly what a name at the top of a page is, and it is the oldest protection there is, and it is also the thing that can be used against you, and I have not been able to decide for eleven days which of those it is going to be, and I have decided to find out in a room rather than in a book.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the girl of seventeen did not look up when it was said, and it is entered that she heard it.

"**That is the best sentence anybody has said in this kitchen,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**I have had it for four days and I have not said it and I am not going to be thanked for saying it,**" said the girl of seventeen, "**because I would rather nobody said anything at all.**"

---

**And the decision was put on a slate in a woman's hand, with a date on it, and no name on it, and no list on it, and it is the fourth page anybody in this city has written about itself, and a girl of seventeen read it back twice and did not improve on it, and it is on the page.**

> **On the fifty-sixth day of the month after this one, in a kitchen over a wharf, in daylight, with about nine people in it, it was decided not to ask nine people anything about whether they will be in a hearing. The reason is that asking them would make nine remarks into nine answers, and nine answers is a list, and a list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is. It is entered that the best argument for asking them was made by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and that he took it back himself. It is entered that if the number is eight on the day, nobody will ever be able to say who was not in the room, and that this city has chosen that on purpose, in a room, with a date on it. Nothing will be written down about any answer, by anybody, in any book, on any board, in any stone, or in any column. The number nine stands, with nobody at the top of it.**

---

**And then nothing else happened on the fifty-sixth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in eight days and there is one hearing and one clock, and it is the last day of the twenty-four. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there thirty days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five, and nobody has asked her a sixth time in twenty-five days. And a printed appointment of an interpreter with nine terms on it is on a table two floors down and a man of thirty-two has not answered it and has said he will answer it in a room on a day with a date on it. And a compact of eleven lines is drafted and is not read out and is not signed. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store and cannot be stopped by anybody in this city. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is thirty-four days ago, and the water has not been received for seventy-seven days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and there is a page on the back of a stone nine miles up a road about it that does not name the village. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is sixty-one days, and one of the forty has not come for thirty-two days and nobody has been told, on purpose. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for sixty-one days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and forty-six days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And a Sunday on which about nine people decided on purpose not to ask nine people anything is the fifty-second silence and the pattern is fifty-two for fifty-two.**

**And the last thing of the fifty-sixth day of the month after this one is a slate in a kitchen with a decision on it and no list, and a girl of seventeen who is going to stand up and is not going to be written, and a hearing in eight days.**
