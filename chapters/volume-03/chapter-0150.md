# Chapter 0150 — A Room, in Daylight, with a Date On It

On the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and fifty-fourth day of the flood and a Monday, about nine people stood in a room over a market in daylight with a date on the door, a man of thirty-two refused a Crown appointment in the local language in front of about two hundred and forty people who could hear him, a woman of twenty-nine read eleven lines out loud and became the account of them, a notary of fifty was asked for the first time in a hundred and thirty-eight days and said no, and the arrivals were recorded as named guests under a compact that can be ended, and none of that is a victory, and three things were left unpaid and one of them is a person. The day before it, which was the sixty-third day of the month after this one, a girl of seventeen stood alone in that room for about four minutes and said out loud that there was nothing in it to look at.

**The licence is one hundred and three days old and then one hundred and four. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is sixty-eight days ago and then sixty-nine. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the ninety-first time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and fifty-two days old and then one hundred and fifty-three, and it has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**A hearing is in one day, and there is one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after the hearing has finished, and that is not a sad thing and it is the mechanism of everything that has happened for a hundred and fifty-four days.**

The room has a date in chalk on the door and nothing else on it, one shutter open and one shutter off, about nine places to stand, and a step at the back about nine inches high with no name on it. It has no table and no bench and no seat and nothing on the walls, and the floor below is a hall with a door on the street, and a landing, and a market under that, and about two hundred and forty people can hear out of all three. **And she had expected there to be something in it, and a room is the only shape in four hundred miles that is not a page, and she had never once had that proved to her by a room being empty.**

---

# The sixty-fourth day of the month after this one

**A clerk of the fourth desk came up the river on the sixty-first day and was asked, on the sixty-second, in a room and not in a hall, whether she would be in this one. She said yes, and said it would not be thanked and would not be improved on, and was thanked, and asked for it not to be entered, and it was entered that she asked for it not to be entered. A man of forty-one in a very good coat stood by the shutter with his coat on, because it is not his room.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two, who is Stage 2 and Passage holder and in the doing and not in a ceremony, and who is unbound and barred from every roster at his own request in his own hand with the date on it, and who holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and for whom there is no office in Saltwake, was asked the question in the local language and answered it in the local language in about nine minutes, and the ninth minute is the whole of it, and nobody improved on the ninth minute and nobody praised him for it.**

"Is there anything you want to say first," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, and he had said the same words in about nine rooms in a hundred and thirty-nine days.

"No."

> **A man of thirty-two from another world has declined the appointment of interpreter for this district. The appointment is of the fortieth day of the month after this one and it offered a reading, and no other reading, and it could not be revoked by this district, and it asked him nine terms of which the fifth was the only one anybody in this room wrote. The declination is entered by the person appointed and by nobody else, and a declining is not a refusal until it is read by the person appointed, and it has been read by the person appointed, and it is a refusal. There is nothing further in it, because the second eighth clause says that nothing in the preceding is a consent, and that clause was in the same hand as the other eight.**

He read that out himself, in the local language, because there is no one else in the room who can.

**And then he said the reason, once, and did not say it again, and a girl of seventeen wrote it out in his words and not in hers.**

"**I said on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one in this room that a translation is a description, and a description is a removal with a column for where the person is, and the column was me. I said on the forty-ninth day of the month after this one that the post is not about the reading, it is about the places, and that a post changes who fills in the places, and that a man who is filling nine places is a column. That is the reason and I am saying it once and I am not going to improve on it, and there is a person at the back who is going to want to say something to that and I am asking her not to.**"

A notary of fifty said nothing at all.

---

**And then he said what it costs, in about four minutes, with the hall and the landing and the market full behind an open door, and about two hundred and forty people heard every word of it in the local language.**

"**I have given up the only thing I am good at as a way of being needed. I have no office, no standing, no roster, no mark, no seat, and there is no office in Saltwake for what I am. I was told in this room five days ago that a man with no mark and no roster is the most findable person in this world, and that this is not a safety, and I have known it since, and I said it out loud today in front of about two hundred and forty people who do not know me and will not know me tomorrow.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**The knowing does not come back. I lost it in about four minutes in this room on the second day of the month after this one and it has not come back in a hundred and thirty-two days and the way home goes through a room with no door. Neither of those was coming back whether I said yes or no, and I want that said, because about four people in this city have thought that saying no brings it back, and it does not.**"

"**And when the answer to the eighth column comes back in about five weeks it is going to be a number, and there is going to be a clerk nine hundred miles away who may read it, and there is going to be a man in this city who can read what is in front of a clerk, and it is not going to be me, because I chose that about four minutes ago in front of everybody who would have to live with it. Nobody is going to thank me for it and I would like it entered that I said that before it happened.**"

---

**And then a man of forty-one in a very good coat put the last thing on the table, and asked for it to be entered that he put it after the man said no and not before.**

"One reading. One document. One day. In a room, in daylight, with a date on it, for that one case in the eighth column, when it comes back. No standing. No roster. No seat. No wage. No mark and no title and no page. You read it in the local language in a room and you stop, and the room is the end of you, and there is a person from that office standing in the next one who can read it."

"**It took me four hours to build out of everything that office does not do and it is the best instrument anybody in four hundred miles has offered me, and I could not make the places be anything else.**"

**And the man of thirty-two said no in about four seconds, and the reason was the reason, and it was not improved on.**

"**No. The reason is the one I gave in four minutes and you are not going to get a second version of it today. The reason is that the shape is the shape. A man who cannot be found in a page is still a man standing in a room, and a reading that is the price of a place is a reading that is the price of a place, and this one is a place. I have watched about nine people in this city build things nobody can be angry at and I have watched what happens to them, and a chair with no date on it is the only thing here allowed to be nothing. That is what I am refusing. I am refusing a thing that cannot be argued with because it is a day.**"

"**It is a place with one day in it,**" he said, "**and you are not a villain for offering it and I am not a hero for refusing it, and about four people in this hall are going to go home and say one of those two things and I am asking them not to.**"

---

**And then a woman of twenty-nine who keeps a public register stood up, and nobody had asked her, and there was no line on the compact that let anybody choose, and it took her about four seconds to be on her feet, and what she said is in a book in this city with a date on it and it is hers alone.**

"**I have been in a room in this city three times in a hundred and thirty-two days and I have written a roll in it every week for seventy-nine of those. I have a sheet in a coat about every attempt this city has made to move its own river boundary and I have carried it for seven years and kept it out of a book on purpose, and on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one I said out loud that keeping it out of a book has been the single most consequential thing I have done in seven years. On the forty-ninth day I said no in four seconds to being made the account of this city, and the cost was that I know what a page that ends a document is, and a person who knows that is a column.**"

She did not stop and nobody stopped her.

"**I am standing up for the opposite reason. The eighth line says that the person who reads this out in the local language in a room is the account of it and is not on any roll. That is not a clause against my doing it. That is a clause that says what it costs.**"

""Then why," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Because a compact nobody reads is a piece of paper, and I know what a piece of paper is, because I have kept one out of a book for seven years. It is a door. And if I do not read it out in a room in daylight with a date on it, then the only account of it for four hundred years is a correct number about a district on a wall in a converted salt store, and that account has one place for a count and one place for the assent and they are the same place.**"

"**My name is on the front of the public register of this city in the second line and has been for about twenty years, and it is the only name in this city on a public page in ordinary course, and I have never once been asked to read anything out loud on purpose, and about two hundred and forty people in this hall are about to hear my voice say eleven lines, and in about four weeks a clerk on a quay nine hundred miles away is going to write down who read it.**"

---

**And she read it out. In the local language. In daylight, with the light off the west window and up the wall, in a room over a market with about nine people in it and a hall and a landing and a market full behind an open door. The eleven lines were the eleven lines drafted on the fiftieth day of the month after this one. Nine of them went without a pause and two of those were heard by about nine people to be the same as they had been drawn, which is a thing a person can say and is not an improvement. The two that did not were the one about the second place and the third place and the one about a guest asking, and both were read twice, once in the order they are in and once in the order this room has been talking about them for ten days, and both times they came out the same. And a girl of seventeen read the whole of it back in the hall, twice, for the people at the back, from a slate and not from hers, because hers has her own name at the top of it. And the man from that office was not read back at, and nobody asked, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf had offered in advance that nobody would be read back at unless they agreed.**

**And it was a refusal of the post, and it did not produce the compact, and a man of thirty-two said the other half of that in about nine seconds and nobody improved on it.**

"**I want it written down that the compact was not the result of my saying no,**" he said. "**The compact was on a slate on the fiftieth. The post was on a table on the forty-ninth. If I had said yes this morning the compact would be exactly the eleven lines it is, with my voice in it instead of hers. I have given something up, and it is a real thing, and it cost, and it is not the reason those eleven lines exist, and nobody is entitled to say it is.**"

It was entered.

---

**And then the arrivals were recorded as named guests, and this is what that meant, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it, and nobody in four hundred miles has ever done it and it was not a form. There is a page in this city with a count of nine on it and a date on it and nobody at the top of it, and it is the safest page this city has built because it cannot be corrected. And about nine people said their own names, out loud, once each, in the local language, in that room, in daylight, with a date on the door behind them, and not one of them was asked to say it, and not one of the nine names was written down by anybody anywhere, and the two pages — the one with the count, and the one with no names on it — are both in this city now, and they disagree, and both of them are true.**

"That is the whole of *named*," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, afterwards, in the hall. "**A count is not a person, and a name on a page is a place a person can be found in, and a name said out loud once in a room is a person. About two hundred and forty people in this building have just heard about nine of them and there is nowhere on this side of the water to write any of it down. That is not a trick. That is the only instrument in four hundred miles that had not been built, and it is a room, and it is gone in a day.**"

---

**And then a notary of fifty was asked, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person, whether she wished to be asked before the box was opened, and it was the sixth asking in a hundred and thirty-eight days, and she answered it, and she said no, and it is on the page.**

"**I have been asked five times in five days and have answered none of the five, and the fifth asking was written out on a slate on the thirty-first day of the month after this one and not delivered, and it is twenty-eight days since, and nobody has asked me a sixth time and nobody was going to, and the not-knowing was the entry, and it had a date on it, and I am not going to say that it was not modesty again.**"

"Sera Vail," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, in the local language, and it is the first time anybody in this city has said her name in a room in a hundred and thirty-eight days, and he said it because she had asked to be asked by a person.

She was quiet for about nineteen seconds.

"**No,**" she said. "**Not no to being asked. No to the opening. Today, in this room.**"

"The hearing," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**A thing that is opened in a hearing is a thing the hearing is about.**" She put both hands flat on the step at the back, which is not a table. "**And after this morning the only instrument this city has with that office is this room, and it is gone in a day, and if I open a box in it this afternoon then the room is about a box for four hundred years. About nine people in this city have spent a hundred and thirty-eight days building an instrument that cannot be taken away, and I am not going to be the woman who turns it into a box.**"

""**It is. The cost of it is that I am fifty and I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I am the notary of about nine hundred things and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence, and I have never in twenty-six years been the person who said no to an opening in a room. I wanted to be asked. The thing I wanted and the thing I said are not the same thing and I did not know that this morning.**"

Both were entered. **And the box is in a room with a door and has been there thirty-eight days and is not opened, and the strap on it is still untied, and nobody has read the writing round the edge of the wax, and one man in this city could have read it and did not on four days and said nothing on a fifth.**

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the thing that a hundred and fifty-four days have been walking towards, and it is not an answer to anything anybody asked, and it cost him, and nobody is going to act on it, and it is unpaid.**

"**A woman of seventy of a village of nine households has refused four times, and the fourth is a refusal of a person, and the only record of it in four hundred miles is a date in chalk on nine doors. There are eight names at the foot of a boundary page a hundred and forty years old and I have not read them and I am not going to. There is a man of about sixty-eight in this city with eight graves and no name stone. There is a clerk nine hundred miles away who has written the number eight in a book every quarter for nineteen years. And there is a correct number about a district on a wall in the salt store that came up a cart in the ordinary course and is correct.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**So what happened to them is not that somebody took them off a list. It is that a form has one place for a count and one place for an assent, and it has had that for a hundred and forty years, and a man who was taken goes in the count and does not go in the assent, and there is no column anywhere in nine hundred miles for a man who was taken. That is not a crime and it is not a conspiracy and it is not a person. It is a shape. And a clerk filling it in on the forty-fourth day of the month after this one was doing the ordinary work of a Tuesday.**"

**And he stopped there, and it is entered that he had about nine more sentences and that he did not say them and that nobody asked him for them.**

---

**And the hearing did what a hearing does, and it is four lines on a page that is not a roll, and a man of forty-one in a very good coat read it out in the local language, and said before he read it that he was reading it as himself, that nobody in his office had told him to read it, and that it was in no book and that he had looked.**

> **The arrivals are recorded as named guests in this district, under a compact read out in a room in daylight on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, which may be ended by the asking of a person. The licensed crossing is recognised provisionally, and provisionally means that it is recognised for the purposes of this district and for no other purpose, and that the recognition may be ended by a person, and that ending it is a form.**
>
> **Four people and a question are to go to the ground four hundred miles inland that people call the First Landing, to say what is standing on it, to say whether there is a seam in the ground, and if there is, how long it is and what is in it, to open nothing, to come back, and to say it in a room in daylight in the local language with a date on it.**

"**Provisional,**" said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Provisional is revocable,**" said the man of forty-one in a very good coat. "**It is in no book and it is correct, and a district recognised provisionally is a district that can be described as not recognised, and there is a form for that too, and I have not seen it in a hundred and fifty-four days and I would rather say so now.**"

---

**And then the room was over. It was over in about four hours, and about nine people went out of it into a Monday, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put the table back at about the seventh hour, because a room with nothing in it that nine people have been in for four hours needs a table in it for the people who come in after, and nobody agreed to that and he did it anyway.**

---

**And three things were left unpaid, and they are on the page, and nobody in this city is going to pay any of them.**

**One: the eight names at the foot of a boundary page a hundred and forty years old are in a fold of oilcloth in a book that cannot travel, and nobody in this city has read them and nobody is going to, and the case with a question about them in its eighth column is on a desk nine hundred miles away and may not be answered here, intercepted, or taken back.**

**Two: a man of thirty-two is outside every office in this city and holds no mark and is on no roster and has no standing and no seat and no wage, and the answer to the eighth column, when it comes in about five weeks, will be read by somebody else or by nobody, and he chose that in a room in daylight in the local language and nobody praised him for it, and a man with no mark, on no roster, outside every office, is the most findable person in this world, and that is not a safety and he knows it.**

**Three: a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house eleven miles away is not asked, not approached, not named in any room where it could reach her, and nobody stands outside the fourth house, and a page in a stone nine miles up a road says a woman of forty-four of Corrow is owed a figure and a decision, and the page is wrong, and the keeping household is not going to correct it on purpose. And a girl of seventeen has put her name at the top of a page in this city and read it out badly and stood up in a room today, and a woman of seventy of a village of nine households is a different person and the two were never put in one sentence.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is over and there was one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that held it is about five weeks from now. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is sixty-nine days, and one of the forty has not come for forty days and nobody has been told, on purpose, and one of the other thirty-nine was paid one day out of a stall on the forty-fifth day of the month after this one with the payer's name on the slate and the paid man's name off it. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for sixty-nine days, and six disagreements stand and none of the six is the number. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and fifty-four days and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and fifty-four days and is not repaired. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And the sixty-third and sixty-fourth days of the month after this one are the fifty-ninth and sixtieth silences, and the wall said nothing in either of them, and the pattern is sixty for sixty.**

**And the last thing of the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one is a room over a market with a table back in it and a shutter open and a step at the back with no name on it and a date in chalk on a door that will still be there in about four months, and a woman of twenty-nine walking down a hill with a page on her, and a girl of seventeen carrying a slate, and eight names at the foot of a page in a fold of oilcloth that nobody read, and sixty silences.**
