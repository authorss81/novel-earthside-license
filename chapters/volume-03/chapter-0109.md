# Chapter 0109 — Seven Nobody Has Looked At

On the nineteenth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and ninth day of the flood and a Friday, about nine people stood in the wrong weather nine miles up a road at eight that are different from the eleven and looked at seven of them for the first time in nine years, and could say what every one of them is and not one of them what it means, and nobody asked a Hearthguard of forty-four a single question, and he said one thing about why.

**The licence is fifty-nine days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid on the first of March. The fee is owing with no date for the fiftieth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and eight days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**Nineteen stones on the upper road carry writing. Eleven of the shapes are the same. Eight are not, and all eight are further down the road than the eleventh, and they are in a different order. One of the eight is a name cut in a hand about a hundred and forty years old and it has been looked at since the thirty-fifth day of the new month, which was the sixtieth day of the flood. Seven of the eight have never been looked at, in nine years, by anybody, and this is the first time anybody has walked down that road to them.**

They went down at about the sixth hour of the morning because the light is not good on that wall before about the seventh and because a mason of thirty-four years said that a cut cannot be read in flat light and that she was not spending a nine-mile walk on flat light.

**And the first of the eight is the name, and the name is what the whole road is for, and it took about four minutes, and it is in the book in a Hearthguard's own words, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him anything.**

"It is my grandfather's hand," said Hettis Roke, forty-four, of the outer ward, nineteen years in. "**About a hundred and forty years old. He taught me to read it when I was nine and I have read it about nine times in thirty-five years and I have not read it aloud. He never told me whose hand it was, and the book in this city says a Hearthguard's hand of that century, and nobody has ever asked which Hearthguard, including me.**"

"Read it," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**No.**"

And nobody argued, and it is entered that nobody argued, and that about four people in that group understood in about four seconds that a man of forty-four had been asked to do the one thing he has not done in nine years and had said no to a person and not to a document, and that a document would have got a yes out of him, and that this was the first time anybody had found that out.

---

**And then they went to the seven, and the seven were looked at for about four hours in the wrong weather, and a mason of thirty-four years and a woman of about sixty-one who cannot read and a man of sixty-one who has read nothing of his own in nineteen years and a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read and a girl of seventeen with a slate went along the wall together, and this is what is on the page, and none of it is what they mean.**

There are seven cuts on seven stones and all seven are below the name. **Four of the seven are cut in one hand**, and that hand is not the hand of the name and not the hand of the eleven. **Three of the seven are cut in three different hands**, and each of the three is different from the other two and from the four, and none of the three is the hand of the eleven. **One of the seven has an older cut under it**, and the older cut is in the hand of the eleven, and it is not the eleventh stone's cut, and the mason spent about nine minutes on that one and would not say what she thought was under it. **One of the seven is not letters.** It is a figure about two inches high with a bar across the top of it, and nobody has looked at it in nine years, and it may be a number, and a mason said it may be a number and would not say that it was. **Two of the seven are a third lower than the other five** and were cut closer to the ground, and the ground there is higher, and the difference between the two and the five is not a third.

**And that is the whole of it. It is in two slates, and a third slate, and the two slates and the third slate are in a book in a village at a fork, and every word of the above is in the book in the words of the people who did the looking, and not one word of it is a meaning.**

"Four hands in seven cuts," said a man of about forty-four of the outer ward, when they had been at it four hours. "**In about nine miles of wall. And eleven cuts in one hand above us. And a mason of thirty-four years who has not laid hands on a join in these nineteen stones in thirty-four years, who does not intend to start today, and who says the four hands are four hands.**"

"That is a count," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything.

"That is a count."

---

**And about nine people then spent about an hour and a half trying to say what the seven mean, and four of them got close, and none of them got there, and the finding of the nineteenth day of the month after this one is that the reason is not that nobody is clever enough.**

The mason: "**A cut is a mark somebody made with a tool on a thing that was already there. I can tell you the order the seven were made in if the stone will let me and I cannot, and I can tell you that the four are one hand and the three are three hands, and I cannot tell you what a hand was doing nine miles from a gate. I have been thirty-four years in this city and I have never read a join in these nineteen stones, and I am not going to start, and if anybody in this group thinks I have, they have misunderstood me.**"

The woman of sixty-one: "**I have written these shapes nineteen times and I do not know what they say. That is the true sentence and it is the first true sentence anybody has said about them in nine years and it took four hours to get it out of me on a road.**"

The man of sixty-one: "**I have kept this city's words for nineteen years and I have never had words of my own that anybody wanted, and I have been on a wall for four hours reading cuts and I can say that a person cut them and I cannot say who, and I have spent nineteen years being able to say the second thing about anybody and not the first, and this morning the first thing is the only one available and it is not enough.**"

The docker of fifty-eight: "**I cannot read. I have a slate and I have written down nineteen shapes from a woman's mouth and I have stood on a join-line twice a day for a hundred and nine days and I know whether it has moved. That is all I know. I have known that since the second day of this flood and it has been enough for a hundred and nine days and it is not enough this morning, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to a man of sixty-one that I read.**"

The girl of seventeen: "**Eleven of the nineteen are the same and eight are not and the eight are in a different order. That sentence has been on a wall and in a book and in about four rooms for nine years and nobody has ever asked what the order of eight means when the order of eleven does not. And I asked it, on the nineteenth day of the month after this one, on a road, in the wrong weather, out loud, and nobody answered me, and it is entered that I asked and nobody answered.**"

---

**And then a woman of twenty-four said the thing out loud that nobody had said, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is the reason a Hearthguard of forty-four said one sentence on the nineteenth day of the month after this one and did not say it to anybody.**

"Nobody has asked him," she said. "**About nine of us have been standing on a road for four hours with a man whose grandfather's hand is at the top of it, and not one of us has asked him a single question, and I would like that entered, and I would like it entered with the date on it, because in about four months somebody is going to say that this city had its chance on a road in the wrong weather and did not take it, and it is going to be said by a person who was not here.**"

"It is not a chance," said Hettis Roke.

That is five words, and nobody checked the count, which is entered.

"**It is a man standing next to his grandfather's hand.** And I have not been asked in nine years and I have not refused, and a person who has never been asked is not a person who has refused, and I said that to a room in this city on the twenty-fifth day of the new month, which was the fiftieth day of this flood, and I have not said one word about my own eight until this morning and I am not going to say it on a road in the wrong weather to nine people who have not asked me."

"Will you say it in a room," said a man of thirty-two.

"**I do not know,**" said Hettis Roke, "**and I have not been asked, and that is the first honest sentence about myself I have said in nine years and it is four words and I am not going to improve on it.**"

---

**And then a mason of thirty-four years said the thing that the volume's question is made of, and it took her about nine seconds, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is not a finding and she entered that she knows the difference.**

"Somebody did this," she said. "**Eight cuts, nine miles, a knife and a reason, and about a hundred and forty years ago or a bit more, and the reason is not on any page in this city and there is nobody left who did it, and there is a person standing on this road whose grandfather's hand is at the top of it, and he has not been asked and he is not going to be asked by me, and I want that written down as a fact about the work and not as a fact about a man.**"

"And you are not going to name the stone," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**I am not going to name the stone. I have said once in a yard in this city that a quarter of a thing is not a thing, and I have been right about it, and seven of eight is not the eight, and one of eight is a name and a name is a person, and I have a piece of stone with eight cuts on it and no name at the top of it and I am not going to put one there, because the day somebody puts a name at the top of that wall the wall becomes a list, and a list goes four times a year.**"

"Nobody is going to write anything on the wall," said a girl of seventeen.

"**Nobody is going to write anything on the wall,**" said the mason, "**and I would like the wall entered as a wall, and the seven entered as seven, and the four hands entered as four hands, and the figure that may be a number entered as a figure that may be a number, and none of it entered as what it means, and I would like all six of those entered with the date on it and I would like them entered before we go down the hill.**"

---

**And then the woman of about sixty-one who cannot read was asked what she thought one of the seven meant, by a woman of twenty-four, in plain words, on a road, and she said that she did not know, and then she said the rest of it, and it is in the book in her own words.**

"Nobody has asked me that in nine years," said Sabra Vint, who is about sixty-one and whose house has a burnt lintel four miles back down this road. "**Not that. I have been asked whether I have the paper, and whether I have looked again, and whether I think the rain is getting into the wall, which it is. Nobody has asked me what I think it says. And the answer is that I do not know, and it is the first time in nine years I have been asked, and it is the first time in nine years that the honest answer and the answer I was going to give anybody anyway are the same thing, and I have written down what I think the shapes mean about four hundred times in my head and I have never once written it, and you have just made me say I do not know, and I am sixty-one and I am not going to be ashamed of it in front of nine people, and I would like it entered that I was not ashamed.**"

**And nothing was gained on the nineteenth day of the month after this one except a count of four hands and three hands and one figure that may be a number, and seven stones nobody has looked at. The undertaking is void and is on a nail and is on a river and no office has been told. Nine Elms' water is not received and has not been received for forty days, counted from the forty-fourth day of the new month, which was the sixty-ninth day of the flood. A field of ninety-one acres out of a hundred and forty is not paid, and the cost column has eleven entries in one month, no figure on the end of any of them, and no twelfth. The chair of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times. The Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and nine days and was not opened today, and the two people who went through it were not asked. The measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading was not taken today. The chapel stone is silent and is not repaired, the ground under the chapel is a scar, and the boards have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt under them, and a fifth that is a question, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing, and holds no mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege and no seat, and performed no working today, and asked a man of forty-four one question on a road and was refused in one word.** **There have been twenty-nine things the wall has said in this city, one of them has been refused, and a day the wall says nothing on is a silence, and this is the eighteenth, and the pattern is eighteen for eighteen.** The black line in his right palm is one hundred and eight days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.

---

It was entered. **And at about the second hour of the afternoon they came back up to the first of the eight, and the name was where it was, and nobody asked, and the weather had not got any better, and about nine people went down a nine-mile road in the wrong weather on the nineteenth day of the month after this one because anybody may pick the work up, and the work is not a post, and the meaning of nineteen shapes is still unwritten, and nobody has written anything on the wall.**
