# Chapter 0125 — An Asking Is a Kitchen

On the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and twenty-fifth day of the flood and a Sunday, about nine people spent four hours in a room over a market trying to build an instrument for asking nine people whether they will stand in a room nine hundred miles away, got as far as a list of who must not be asked to do the asking inside about nine minutes, found that the only instrument this city has is a room, and that a room is not an instrument for asking one person a question without nine other people hearing it, and then said the thing that is the finding of the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one, which is that a hearing is a room and an asking is a kitchen, and that this city has worked out the first of those in a hundred and twenty-five days and has not worked out the second one at all.

**The licence is seventy-five days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is forty days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the sixty-sixth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty-four days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the number nine is on a page with a date on it and no person at the top of it, and it is a count and not a list, and it was said out loud by four separate people on the twenty-first day of the month after this one and entered properly on the twenty-fifth, and about nine people have said out loud that they will stand in the hearing, and nobody has asked any of the nine whether they will.**

"Nobody has asked," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**And I have realised in about nine seconds that I have known that for four days and that I have been in three rooms in four days and that not one of those three rooms had anybody in it who had been asked.**"

"So ask them."

"**Then it is nine answers,**" said a woman of twenty-four, "**and nine answers is a list. And a list of who will stand in a room is a removal with a column for where the person is, and that is on a page from the twenty-fifth day of the month after this one and nobody has improved on it since. We crossed one list out in about four seconds on that Thursday. I am not writing another one on a Sunday.**"

"Even if the answers are no?"

"**Especially if the answers are no. A column of nine people who will not come is a column of nine people who will not come, and it is the same column with the other word in it, and in about five weeks the next case goes out of this city and there is a wall in a room nine hundred miles away with a heading on it, and I have spent a hundred and twenty-five days learning that the heading is the dangerous part.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and the girl of seventeen turned her slate over and left it face down, and it stayed face down.

---

**And then about four people in that room worked out inside about nine minutes that there are exactly two people in this city who could carry an answer out of a kitchen and put it anywhere, and that both of them are exactly the two people who may not do it, and neither of them worked that out for themselves, and a man of thirty-two worked it out in about nine seconds and said it out loud before anybody had asked him, which is what he has been doing for a hundred and twenty-five days.**

"I cannot ask," he said.

"You have asked a man of thirty-nine a question in a room this morning—"

"No. **I have never asked anybody anything. I have read things to rooms and said numbers out loud off walls and been handed a page and read it, and about four people in this city have decided in the last ten days that reading is a form of asking, and it is not. I have not asked one person one question in a hundred and twenty-five days and I want that entered with the date on it, and I want the reason entered too.**"

"Which is," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**Two reasons and both of them are about the same thing. I am on no roster of anything, and a person on no roster who goes to nine kitchens and asks nine households whether they will stand in a room nine hundred miles away is a person who is asking, and asking is what a chain is made of, and I have been in this city a hundred and twenty-five days and I have watched what happens to the people at the bottom of one. And I am the only person in this city who can read two systems of writing.**"

"That is not a reason," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall. "**That is a gift. We have wanted somebody who can read a form in a room nine hundred miles away for a hundred and twenty-five days.**"

"**It is the reason and it is the other half of the reason, and they are one reason, and here it is. If I ask a person a question in their kitchen, they answer me in the language they speak. I hold the other one. So whatever they say arrives in two systems at once and I am the person standing at the junction, and about four minutes later I am the person who says what they meant, and a man who says what nine people meant is a description of nine people. On the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one I read a form out loud in a room in the local language and it turned out to be a description of a district and the column was me, and I said in four minutes that I am not going to be able to stop, and nobody in that room found me one instrument that would let me stop and nobody looked, and I have had six days to think about it and this is what I have.**"

It is entered. **And it is entered that about four people in that room had worked out one of those two reasons in the last four days and that the second one was new, and that a girl of seventeen wrote both of them out and put the date on them and did not put a name on the page, and that a man of thirty-two asked her to put a name on the page and she said no in under a second and that is the fifth time in a hundred and twenty-five days and it is entered six times that a girl of seventeen has said no in under a second and that nobody has improved on any of the six.**

---

**And a girl of seventeen was asked at about the third hour of the afternoon whether she would go and ask them, and she said no in under a second, and then said the rest of it in about nineteen seconds, and it is the first time in a hundred and twenty-five days that she has refused a thing she wanted to do.**

"**I am on no roll. That is not the reason. I have been on no roll since the twenty-fifth day of this flood and I have never once been asked a question about it and I am not going to start by being the one who goes to nine doors.**"

"The doors are not yours," said a man of about thirty-two.

"**The doors are not mine and the asking is mine. I am the only person in this city who copies things, and every page that has ever gone anywhere out of this city has gone because I copied it, and if I go and ask nine people a question and they say yes then I am a woman with nine answers and no roll, and a woman with nine answers and no roll is a carrier, and I have been reading about carriers since the twenty-third day of the month after this one and a case with a name in it can be carried.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds, and a woman of twenty-four took her hand off the edge of the table and put it in her lap, and did not ask for it to be said again.

"**And I am seventeen, and I have been the person who asks about the fourth refusal, and about the foreman, and about the woman of forty-four of Corrow, and about the woman of forty-eight in a fourth house that nobody is going to ask, and every one of those was a question I asked because nobody else in that room could, and I have never once been asked whether I wanted to be the one who asks.**"

"Then who goes," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**People go to their own kitchens. That is all of it. I am not going to build an instrument for it. I am going to go to my mother's kitchen and I am going to ask her, and she is going to say no or she is going to say yes, and I am not going to write either one down, and neither is anybody else, and if I write it down it is a column and if you write it down it is a column and if a man of thirty-two writes it down it is a description.**"

---

**And then about nine people sat in that room for about an hour and did the thing this city does, which is to write down what will not be done, and this one is the third in a hundred and twenty-five days, and it is on a slate in a book in this city with a date on it, and there is no name on it and there is no list on it.**

> **On the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one, in daylight, in about nine people, it was decided that nobody will be asked to ask.**
>
> **Nine people have said out loud that they will stand in a hearing twenty-nine days away. Nine people have not been asked whether they will. Nothing will be written down about any answer, by anybody, in any book, on any board, in any stone, or in any column.**
>
> **A hearing is a room. An asking is a kitchen. This city has worked out the first of those in a hundred and twenty-five days and has not worked out the second one at all.**

"And if one of them says no on the day," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**Then the number is eight,**" said a woman of twenty-four, "**and it is on a page with a date on it and no person at the top of it, and it cannot be corrected, and there is nobody who can be angry at it, and about four of us have said in the last ten days that a number with nobody at the top of it is the safest page in this city, and that is still true, and it is the reason I am frightened.**"

"You have been frightened for ten days about a number."

"**I have been frightened for ten days about a number with nobody at the top of it, and the reason is that a number cannot be asked whether it will have it. On the seventeenth day of the month after this one four people wrote that down in about nine seconds in this room, and nobody has improved on it since, and it applies to me. If the number is eight on that day then I will have written a thing about myself that I did not know I was writing, and I cannot take it back, because a number nobody can check is a page nobody can be angry at, and those two are the same protection, and the same protection is what I have been buying with a hundred and twenty-five days.**"

"Then do not write the number either," said a man of thirty-two.

"**No,**" said a woman of twenty-four. "**The number is the only thing that goes in the room. I am not giving up the number. I am giving up the list, and the list was never mine, and the number has nobody at the top of it and I have checked that twice.**"

---

**And a keeper of about seventy who has kept the floor of a lock chapel for fifty-one years was not asked anything at about the seventh hour of the evening, and nobody went near the chapel, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said in about four seconds why, and nobody improved on her.**

"**Any instrument for asking has to work on everybody. That is what an instrument is. And she said no to being a number in four seconds on the twenty-fifth day of the month after this one, and the reason was that if the number is a person then the number can be asked whether the number is right, and in twenty-nine days there is going to be a room nine hundred miles away with people in it who will be asking somebody. So there is one house in this city that this asking may not go to, and an instrument that has one house it may not go to is a favour, and a favour is a thing that stops.**"

"Nobody was going to ask her."

"**I want that written down as *because she asked to be left alone and not because we thought of it*, and I am on nothing and I am named on nothing and I would like one page in this city to have the correct reason on it.**"

It is entered. **And the finding of the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one is that a hearing is a room and an asking is a kitchen, and that the only instrument this city has is a room, and that a room is the wrong shape for an asking because a room has about nine people in it and nine people hearing an answer is eight other people hearing an answer.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one, and a hearing is in twenty-nine days and there is one hearing and one clock, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after the hearing has finished. And a box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one and it is upstairs in a room with a door and it is not opened and the strap on it is still untied. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and no name in it, and it is still on the water. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one and the water has not been received for fifty-six days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood and the quarter is gone. And a page in a stone nine miles up a road has a line in it that is now wrong. And a page that ends a document is spent and five rooms have failed to explain it in the same place. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for forty days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and twenty-five days and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and twenty-five days. And there have been thirty things the wall has said in this city and one has been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-three, and a Sunday on which about nine people agreed not to write anything down is the thirty-third silence and the pattern is thirty-three for thirty-three.**

**And the last thing of the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one is a slate with three lines on it and no name and no list, lying face up on a bench in a room over a market, and a girl of seventeen has gone out into the weather to ask her mother in her own kitchen whether she will stand in a room nine hundred miles away, and nobody is going to write the answer down, and there are eight more kitchens, and a hearing is in twenty-nine days.**
