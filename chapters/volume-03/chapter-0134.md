# Chapter 0134 — One Person, or a Number

On the forty-fourth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirty-fourth day of the flood and a Tuesday, a man of sixty-one who has copied other men's sentences for nineteen years read four lines out loud in a room over a market for the first time in his life, and a notary of fifty refused an answer that four people had already agreed she would give, and a man of thirty-two said nothing at all about a wax, and the not-saying is the entry.

**The licence is eighty-four days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is forty-nine days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventy-fifth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirty-three days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

The four lines are under a seal in a box that came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood, and the box has been in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market for eighteen days, and the strap on it is still untied, and the box is not opened, and there is no procedure in four hundred miles for opening a thing an office has sent, and a case that is opened early is a case that has been read.

**The four lines under the seal have been read out in the local language twice in eighteen days and both times by a notary of fifty, and she is not going to read them again, and so on the forty-fourth day of the month after this one a man of sixty-one read them out, and everybody in that room understood that they had all been waiting nineteen years for that and not one of them had noticed.**

"You have never read one out," said a woman of twenty-four.

"**No,**" said Garret Slee.

"**Nineteen years.**"

"**Nineteen years of other men's sentences, in my own hand, and I have never once been asked to read one out, and I have had a sentence of my own ready for about two days of my life in a road in this city and I never said it and nobody ever found out that I had it, and I would like it entered that both of those are true and that they are the same shape.**"

It was entered. **And it is entered that he did not explain the sentence and that nobody asked him about it, and that a woman of twenty-four wanted to ask him about it and did not, and that the reason she gave afterwards was that a sentence a man has had ready for two days is the only thing he owns that nobody in this city can take off him, and that asking about it would have been a description.**

---

> **THE EIGHTH SHAPE, dated the nineteenth day of the new month, which was the forty-fourth day of this flood, seventy-two days on the water, and it came up on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one. Four lines under the seal. The district shall name one person to be heard at the hearing. Where no person is named, the district shall be described by a number.**

He read it twice, and the second time was slower, and nobody improved on either.

"**And that is a document in a room and not in a case,**" said a man of forty-one in a very good coat, who had not come and had sent nothing and was in the room because a room over a market is a room anybody may be in. "**It is not the notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood and it is not the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month and it is not any of the other five, and it came up on its own account seventy-two days after it went down, and I have now had it in front of me for nine days and I have not found a way to act on it and I looked.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything and who can read two systems of writing and is the only person in this city who can, was asked by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall whether he was going to say the thing he says about the wax, and he said nothing, and it took about nineteen seconds, and a girl of seventeen wrote on a slate that he was asked and did not answer, and nobody read the slate out.**

"**You were going to say a form is not a face,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**I was going to say it,**" said the man of thirty-two. "**I said it on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one and on the twenty-seventh and on the thirty-first and on the thirty-second. That is four times in seven days. I said on the thirty-first that I would not say it again that week and I have not said it since and it is now the forty-fourth day and I am not going to say it at all in this room, and I would like that written down as a refusal of an act and not as a finding, and it is not a refusal of anybody.**"

Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds.

"**You could have said it,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, "**and about four of us would have nodded, and we would all have felt better, and there would be one fewer person in this city who could read the writing round the edge of that wax.**"

"**Yes,**" said the man of thirty-two.

---

**And then a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said the thing that four people in that room had been carrying since the quay on the forty-second day of the month after this one, and said it badly, and he was not a keeper of a register and he knew it, and it went on the page anyway.**

"**So we have made a thing in three days that nobody asked for. That office asked for one person. We have got a compact that asks for nine. Those are not the same instrument and they are not two versions of the same instrument and I do not think anybody in this room can put them in the same order, and I have been trying since Tuesday.**"

"You cannot," said a woman of twenty-four. "**And the reason you cannot is that one of them came up a river and one of them was invented on the back of a shut stall, and the one that came up a river is the only one that office will have seen.**"

"Then the compact is not the answer to anything," said a man of about thirty-nine who trades on the Open Hand board.

"**The compact is the answer to something,**" said the man of thirty-two. "**It is just not the answer to that.**"

---

**And then four people in that room said out loud, in about nine seconds between them, what a notary of fifty would say when she was asked about the box, and she was in the room, and she had been in the room for all of it, and she said no in about four seconds and the four seconds are on the page.**

She had come up at about the ninth hour of the morning on purpose and had sat down at the end of the table, and nobody had asked her anything, and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall said that everybody in the room knew she would want to be asked after the hearing and before anything was opened, and that it was the sensible order and that somebody ought to put it to her before the day got late.

"**No,**" said a notary of fifty.

Nobody said anything.

"**You are all being kind,**" she said, "**and I have been the notary of about nine hundred things and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence, and what you have just done is supply an answer of mine before I have said it, and it is a good answer and it is very likely the right one, and it is not mine.**"

"It is what you would say," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**It is what anybody would say about me. That is the difference and it is the whole of twenty-six years.**" She put her hands flat on the table, one on each side of a slate that had nothing on it. "**I entered on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one that I would like to be asked before it is opened, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person. That is on a page in a book in this city. I have been asked five times in five days and I have answered none of the five, and I have entered that I do not know, and a notary entering that she does not know is the first time in twenty-six years and it is on the page. If one of you supplies that for me this afternoon then the not-knowing is on the page and the not-knowing is not mine, and in four months there is a person who can say that on the forty-fourth day of the month after this one I knew, and I will not have that. A supplied answer is a description of a person and I have been the description of about nine hundred people.**"

"**You are not angry,**" said a woman of twenty-four.

"**I am fifty and I have been a notary for twenty-six years and I am not going to be angry in a room on a Sunday about four lines and a chair, and I am also not going to be relieved, and I would like both of those entered, and the second one especially, because relief is what a supplied answer feels like from the inside and I am naming it before somebody else does.**"

Both were entered.

---

**And the finding of the forty-fourth day of the month after this one is that this city has two documents that both want to be in a room on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, and one of them has been on the water for thirty-eight days and one of them has existed for three, and neither of them has been read out yet, and a hearing is going to be asked to hold both, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles for holding two things that are not the same thing, and the only thing anybody in that room said about it was that the hearing is a room and a room is the wrong shape for two of anything.**

Ledger for the day, in a girl's hand, with the date on it and no name on it: **the box is eighteen days in a room with a door. The four lines are nineteen days old and were read out twice by the notary and once by a man of sixty-one. The not-knowing is nineteen days old. The fifth asking is a slate that was written on the thirty-first day of the month after this one and not delivered, and it is thirteen days since it was written. Nobody has asked her a sixth time and nobody is going to this week. The wax has not been read and one man could and did not, four times, and said nothing this morning when he was asked.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the forty-fourth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in twenty days and there is one hearing and one clock and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold the hearing is about five weeks after the hearing has finished. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and it names nobody and it is still on the water and it may not be answered here and it may not be intercepted and it may not be taken back. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is twenty-two days ago, and the water has not been received for sixty-five days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is forty-nine days, and one of the forty has not come for twenty days and nobody has been told. And a Hearthguard of forty-four has gone twenty-four days without anybody in this city asking him about his own eight, in a room, on a different day. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for forty-nine days, and six disagreements stand and none of them is the number. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty-four days and is not repaired. And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city and two of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-four, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is eleven, and it has never been made smaller. And a Tuesday on which a notary of fifty refused an answer that four people had agreed she would give is the forty-first silence and the pattern is forty-one for forty-one.**

**And the last thing of the forty-fourth day of the month after this one is four lines under a seal in a box with its strap untied, and a slate with a notary's five askings written on it and not delivered, and the count of days since the thirty-first is thirteen, and a hearing is in twenty days.**
