# Chapter 0139 — The Thing That Is Asked, and It Is a Post

On the forty-ninth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirty-ninth day of the flood and a Sunday, a printed appointment came out of a cart that had been standing in a lane since Saturday morning, and a man of forty-one in a very good coat put it on a table in a room over a market and said that he was required to put it and that he was putting it, and it is a Crown-appointed interpreter with unlimited authority, offered to a man of thirty-two in daylight and in the local language with about nine people in the room, and he did not answer it, and nobody in that room did.

**The licence is eighty-nine days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is fifty-four days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eightieth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and thirty-eight days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

It had come up the Reach road in the same cart as the return of the forty-fourth day of the month after this one and it had been in the lane since Saturday morning, folded in with a bill of lading, and nobody had opened it, because the cart had been opened for the return and the return was the thing everybody in this city has been waiting for, and **a thing that is addressed to a person and not to a district does not get looked at.**

It is a single sheet. It is printed. It has a seal on it that is the seal of that office and not the seal of a carrier.

> **THE THIRTEENTH SHAPE, and it is dated the fortieth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirtieth day of this flood, and it came up the Reach road in a cart on the forty-eighth day and was not opened until the forty-ninth. An appointment of an interpreter for a district, with authority without limit. It is Crown. It is not the Registry's notice of the twenty-eighth day of this flood and it is not the third instrument of the seventeenth day of the new month and it is not the second answer of the twenty-second and it is not the schedule of crossings of the thirty-first and it is not the fifth thing that office put in a case on the fifty-second day of the new month. It is not the undertaking of the Lantern Concord, which is ours and void. It is not the design of the Quiet Choir, which is not the Crown's. It is not the eighth shape, which came up a river sealed. It is not the ninth shape, a boundary page of the village of Nine Elms. It is not the tenth shape, a table in a coat. It is not the eleventh shape, a compact, which is not written down. It is not the twelfth shape, a return of a district made by a waterboard in the ordinary course. It is a new shape and it is dated and it is the only instrument anybody in four hundred miles has ever printed for a person by name.**

---

**And a man of forty-one in a very good coat read it out in the local language in about four minutes and then said the thing nobody in that room had expected him to say, and it is in a book in this city with a date on it.**

"**I am required to put it. I am putting it. I said in this city on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one that my office has no name for a document that is in a room and not in a case, and that I have been trying since about the fourteenth week of this flood to find a way to act on one and have not found it. This is a way to act on a room. I have had it since Friday morning and I do not like it and I am putting it anyway, and I would like the room to notice that the man who has spent nineteen days failing to act on something has just been handed the means, and that the means is a person.**"

"Read the terms," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything.

"**The terms are these, and there are nine of them, and eight of them are ordinary.**"

---

> **One. A person is appointed interpreter for this district, and the appointment names him.**
> **Two. The reading of that person, of any document produced before that office in this district, is the reading.**
> **Three. No other reading of any such document may be entered anywhere.**
> **Four. Any person in this district may bring a document to that person and be answered.**
> **Five. The appointment is not a roster and confers no land, no title, no workway and no seat.**
> **Six. The appointment is not revocable by this district.**
> **Seven. The appointment ends when the district ends.**
> **Eight. The person appointed is not to be asked whether he accepts it.**
> **Nine. The person appointed shall not be described by any page produced by this district.**
> **Nothing in the preceding is a refusal and nothing in the preceding is a consent.**

"I am not on a roster and I am not going to be," said the man of thirty-two. "**Number five is the only one of the nine that anybody in this room wrote.**"

"**The eighth clause appears twice,**" said the man of forty-one in a very good coat. "**In the original, between the eighth and the ninth, there is a second eighth, and it says this: *the person appointed shall not be entered as declining, and a declining is not a refusal until it is read by the person appointed.* I read it out once and I have not read it twice because I have read it nineteen times and I have been trying for four days to work out what it is for.**"

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said the thing that four people in that room had been carrying for a hundred and thirty-nine days and had not said, and it is on the page, and it is the finding of the forty-ninth day of the month after this one.**

"**That is not the first shape anybody in this world has ever built that puts a whole cost on one named person and calls it fair. It is not even the second. And I am not going to say what it is called in this room, because the name has to be said in a room in daylight with a date on it and in the local language, and that is not this room, and about four people in this city know what that shape is called and two of them are sitting at this table.**"

"I will not confirm it and I will not explain it," said the man of forty-one in a very good coat. "**I said those two words out loud in a room in this city on a named day and I put them in my own hand in a book and I have been asked to explain them twice since and I have not, and I am not going to, and I would like that entered, and I would like the room to notice that I have just declined to be useful in the one room in four hundred miles where I am the only person who could have been.**"

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said one of the two words out loud, in the local language, in a room, with a date on it, and did not explain it, and nobody asked him to, and it is the first time either of them has been said out loud in a hundred and thirty-nine days.**

"**Single witness,**" he said. "**One person, whose word is the reading, and nothing may say a different reading. That is what is on the table. I am not going to explain it because I have watched two people in this city refuse to explain it and I am not going to be the third.**"

"**Then say what it costs,**" said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall.

"**It costs what it has always cost. It puts a person in a place where a person can be found, and a person in a place where a person can be found is a person who can be stopped, and the clause that stops this district describing me is eleven lines from the clause that stops this district asking me, and both of them are fair, and both of them are about me, and nobody in nine hundred miles is going to be hurt by either of them.**"

---

**And at about the fifth hour of the afternoon a pale field came up in the air in front of about nine people in that room and stood there for about nineteen seconds, and it is the thirty-second thing the wall has said in this city, and a notary of fifty read it out in the local language in about nine seconds, and it was answered in about four seconds, out loud, in the local language, by a woman of twenty-nine who keeps a public register, and it was not accepted.**

> **One person may be made the reading of this district.**
>
> **The person who reads it is not described by it. The person is not on any roll.**
>
> **The cost is that after this, a document that this person has read cannot be un-read by anybody, including the person, and every later account of it will be checked against this person, and this person will not be asked whether they would have it.**
>
> **The cost is entered. There is no further cost.**

"No."

It is entered that she said it in about four seconds, that nobody had asked her anything, and that **the count of refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home and has never shown to anybody is twenty-five, and it was twenty-four yesterday, and this is the third time it has moved in a hundred and thirty-nine days. The count at Aurel is thirteen. The difference is twelve, and it was eleven, and it was ten for about a hundred days, and it was nine before the first registered refusal, and it has never been made smaller.**

"And what it cost," said a woman of twenty-four, who is on no roll of anything.

"**That I have said it out loud, in the local language, in front of about nine people, one of whom is a man from that office and has written it down already, and that a thing said out loud in a room an office knows about is a thing that can go into a return.**" She did not stop. "**And that my name is on the front of the public register of this city in the second line and has been for about twenty years, and that I have stood in a room in this city twice in a hundred and thirty-nine days and I have never once been asked to read anything out loud on purpose, and I have just said no to being the one, in front of the only man in four hundred miles who could put it in a case.**"

"And you knew that," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**I knew it before I said it, and I said it anyway, and I would like it entered that I said it before he asked me to, because he was going to.**"

It was entered.

---

**And then a man of thirty-two said the part of the forty-ninth day of the month after this one that is the finding, and it took about four minutes, and nobody improved on it, and it is the shape of what the next sixteen days are for.**

"**Then here is what the post does and does not do, and I have worked it out twice since Friday and I got it wrong the first time.**"

"**The wrong way round,**" said a woman of twenty-four.

"**The wrong way round. I thought on Friday that if I said no then nothing in this city could be read to that office, and that was vanity, and it took me two days to see it.**" He put his hand flat on the table and did not look at it. "**A compact is a form. A form exists whether or not anybody will stand in it. On the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one a form is going to be read out in the local language in daylight with a date on it, and it is going to happen with me in it or without me, and if I take the post it happens exactly the same way with one extra person in it who cannot be asked whether he wants to be there. So it is not about the reading. The reading is the whole of what I can do in this world and I said that out loud on the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one and I am not going to unsay it. It is about the places. A post does not change the form. A post changes who fills in the places. And there is a place in that form for a person and a place for a count and the two are not the same place, and if I hold the post then every place in it that wants a person gets me, and a person cannot be in nine places, and I have known for a hundred and thirty-nine days that a man standing at the junction of two systems of writing is the man who will say what nine people meant.**"

---

**And he said the last thing about it himself, and nobody asked him and nobody in that room was going to get anything else out of him, and it is on the page, and he stood up when he said it, which he does not do.**

"**I am not going to say no today. I have been asked once in a hundred and thirty-nine days about a thing I can do and I said no in about four seconds, and I am not going to do that in a room where nobody would have to live with it afterwards, and there are people in this city who would have to live with it for the rest of their lives.**"

"**Which is who,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf.

"**You, and a girl of seventeen, and a notary of fifty, and a woman of twenty-nine at that table, and about nine men who turn up under a gate in the wrong weather, and a man of forty-four who has a floor, and eight houses. Every one of those is somebody's household and a household is a person.**"

"**I will answer it in a room, in daylight, in the local language, on a day with a date on it, and everybody who has to live with it will hear me say it. That is the only promise I am going to make about it and I am not going to improve on it in fifteen days.**"

---

**And nothing else happened on the forty-ninth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in fifteen days and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there twenty-three days and is not opened and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five, and on the forty-fourth day of the month after this one she refused an answer that four people had agreed she would give. And a case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one with a question in its eighth column and it names nobody and it is still on the water, and on the forty-eighth day a receipt came up the Reach road saying received, carried, not closed, and no answer due before the quarter. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store and cannot be stopped by anybody in this city. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is twenty-seven days ago, and the water has not been received for seventy days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is fifty-four days, and one of the forty has not come for twenty-five days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for fifty-four days. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty-nine days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And a Sunday on which the wall spoke and a person refused it in about four seconds is the forty-fifth silence and the pattern is forty-five for forty-five, and the count stands where it stood.**

**And the last thing of the forty-ninth day of the month after this one is a printed sheet with nine terms on it and a seal on it and a man of thirty-two who has not answered it and has said he will answer it in a room, in daylight, in the local language, on a day with a date on it, and a hearing in fifteen days.**
