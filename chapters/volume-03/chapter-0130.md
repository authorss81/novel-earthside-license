# Chapter 0130 — Twenty-Four Days

On the fortieth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and thirtieth day of the flood and a Wednesday, about nine people sat in a room over a market at about the ninth hour of the evening and went through what there was, out loud, because that is what they do, and it is on the page, and none of it goes anywhere, and a boy of nine left a slate on a bench with his own handwriting on the back of it and nothing at all on the front, and nobody turned it over, and the case is upstairs, and the wall has said nothing.

**The licence is eighty days old. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is forty-five days ago. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the seventy-first time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and twenty-nine days old and has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

**And the hearing is in twenty-four days and there is one hearing and one clock, and the clock runs the wrong way round, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of the office that will hold it is about five weeks after that hearing has finished and the room has gone home, and there is no road in this world that reaches the building, and nothing can be sent and nothing can be prepared.**

About nine people have said out loud that they will stand in it. **The number nine is on a page with a date on it and no person at the top of it, and it is a count and not a list, and about four people have asked for names on it and have not been given them. Nine people have not been asked whether they will. A girl of seventeen has been to nine kitchens in ten days and has written nothing down about any of them, and a man of thirty-two has not asked anybody anything, and a keeper of about seventy who has kept a floor for fifty-one years was not asked anything on the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one and nobody went near the chapel, and the reason for that is on a slate in a book in this city and the reason is *because she asked to be left alone and not because we thought of it*.**

A case went down this river on the eighth day of the month after this one, which was the ninety-eighth day of the flood, in a wooden case with a strap and a wax and a second strap, and it is still on the water, and it carries a question in its eighth column and it names nobody, and a clerk of that office may not ask a question and may not refuse one, and a person named in a question is not told the question exists. **It may not be intercepted, and it may not be taken back, and it may not be put into the local language in a room where about two hundred and forty people can hear it, and it has not been. What will come back from it is a correct number about a district, and eight people will not be in it, and nothing anybody in this city does in the next twenty-four days changes that.**

A box came up this river on the twenty-sixth day of the month after this one, which was the hundred and sixteenth day of the flood, and it is the first thing in a hundred and forty years that has come up, and it is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and it is not opened, and the strap on it is still untied, and nobody has read the writing round the edge of the wax and one man in this city could have and did not. **A notary of fifty entered on the twenty-sixth day that she would like to be asked before it is opened, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person, and she has been asked about it five times in five days and has answered none of the five, and the not-knowing is entered with the date on it, and nobody has asked her a sixth time and nobody is going to this week, and there is a chair on the landing outside that door with no date on it.**

---

**And a woman of twenty-four read out the ten days, because that is what she does, and nobody improved on any of it, and it is on the page, and it is the first time anybody in this city has read a list of its own last ten days out loud, and the list has no names on it.**

A page in a stone nine miles up a road has a line in it that is now wrong and cannot be corrected, and the household that keeps that stone has never been asked what it thinks. **A decision not to carry a word to a foreman of a day crew who has not come for nine days, on a slate, with a date on it, and no name on it.** **A decision that nobody will be asked to ask, on a slate, with a date on it, and no name on it, and no list on it.** **And a sentence said out loud in front of a gate about nine inches of stone not being a safe place for anything, and it is the first thing anybody in this city has written a page about, and it is about this city, and it is on a page in a book in this city with a date on it.**

A name went onto a page on the thirty-third day of the month after this one. **It is the first time in a hundred and thirty days that a person in this city has put her own name at the top of a page in a room in daylight with about nine people in it, and the page is a description of a box, and the description is the only account of the thing that will exist, and the cost of it is that a girl of seventeen is now a person who knows what a page she wrote is about, and she read her own page out badly in the local language and nobody corrected her and nobody counted what she said.** A man of thirty-two was asked to read it and said no in about four seconds, which is the first time in a hundred and thirty days he has refused a thing he is the only person in the city able to do.

A page that ends a document is spent, and it is in a lockable room off the salt store with the door shut, and **six rooms in this city have now failed to explain it and all six failed in the same place, and the sixth was held on the thirty-seventh day of the month after this one in a room at the back of the Open Hand that has no key and no owner, and the sixth thing they tried was who wanted it to work, and the answer is that nobody in this city ever did.**

A woman of seventy of a village of nine households said on the thirty-eighth day of the month after this one, in a kitchen, in daylight, that **a person's fourth refusal is a refusal with nothing under it, and that it is the strongest refusal this city has, and that it is held up by luck**, and that the first three were not hers and that she has never in her life been asked what they were. It is on the public roll in one line with no name on it, and the only other record of it in four hundred miles is a date in chalk on nine doors that nobody can wash off and nobody can explain.

**And a woman who keeps a register stood in a room on the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one for the first time in seventy-nine days, and said the top of one line of a table she has carried in a coat for seven years out loud once, and put the sheet back in the coat, and it is a new shape and it is the tenth, and it is local, and it is not any of the other nine.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the fortieth day of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is eighteen days ago, and the water has not been received for sixty-one days counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, and the date in the chalk on nine doors is not washed and nobody in four hundred miles can wipe it off. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is forty-five days, and the column has eleven entries and no figure on the end of any of them and there is no twelfth, and one of the forty has not come for nine days and nobody has been told. And a Hearthguard of forty-four has now gone twenty days without anybody in this city asking him anything about his own eight, in a room, on a different day, and nobody may ask him until he is asked properly, and that is the correct number of times and it is the only unpaid debt in this book that is about a person and not about a page. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings, both of the last two eight and seven-eighths, two steps in an unnamed mason's hand on the eighteenth and the twenty-fourth day of the new month, and one ruled line left, and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for forty-five days, and the four who read the join-line have disagreed six times since the first of March and all six entries stand. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice in a hundred and thirty days and was not opened on any day of the ten days since the twenty-first day of the month after this one, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty days and is not repaired. The form on the nail has four items, the price of the salt written under them, and a fifth item which is a question and not a duty, and none of the five can close anything or say where a residue goes or say who is in a room. The seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times, and a room is due in about ten weeks to a man of forty-one in a very good coat, and he has not been convicted of anything and is not going to be, and he has not been asked to explain a name and did not.**

**And there have been thirty-one things the wall has said in this city across fourteen batches and one of them has been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home, which is kept at home and has never been shown to anybody, are twenty-four, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is eleven, and it was ten for about a hundred days, and it was nine before the first registered refusal, and it has never been made smaller, and it moved by one on the thirty-seventh day of the month after this one when a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf said no out loud in a room with no key in it to being made the account of a page, and she has entered that she knew the cost before she said it. And the fortieth day of the month after this one is the thirty-eighth silence and the pattern is thirty-eight for thirty-eight, counted silences and not days, and the wall spoke once in these ten days and a person refused it in about four seconds and the refusal cost her the only thing she had.**

**And a woman of twenty-four then said the other half of the list, out loud, in the same voice, and it is the part that is not on any page, and it is nine things, and a girl of seventeen counted them and nobody checked her count.**

The twenty-eight words of a boy of nine are not in the roll and are not in any book. The two words in his second block are in about nine rooms in this city and in no room together. **The answer to his six words is on a slate in a boy's coat and in no book, and a man of thirty-two said it once on a sixth mile in about four seconds and is not going to say it again.** The eight names at the foot of a boundary page a hundred and forty years old are not on a page in this city. Five lines of a question written on a slate on the thirty-first day of the month after this one were read out in a room and were not delivered. A figure and a decision were said in five words by a woman of forty-four of Corrow and the room did not check the count. **A sentence a man of sixty-one had ready for two days on a road was never said. A count of seven cuts in a stone says it is a count and not a debt and nobody knows what it is a count of. And the thing a woman of about thirty-four has been carrying since the twenty-ninth day of the month after this one and did not say out loud is on no page in this city and is not going to Aurel.**

"Nine things," said a girl of seventeen. "**And every one of them is a person or a person's sentence, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that any of them can be put into, and that is not because the instrument has not been built yet.**"

"No," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. "**It is because the instrument would be a document, and a document is a description, and a description is a removal with a column for where the person is. In about eight weeks, in a room nine hundred miles away, nine of us are going to say those things out loud, and that is the only account of any of them that will ever exist, and it will be about four hundred miles long and it will not travel either.**"

---

**And about nine people went to sleep in four buildings in this city on the fortieth day of the month after this one, and the first of them to go was a boy of nine, who came into a room over a market at about the ninth hour of the evening with a slate and put it down on a bench and left it, and the back of it was up and the front of it was blank, and the six words are on the back of it in his own hand and there is nothing on the front of that one at all, and the twenty-eight words are on the flat of a different one of his three which is face down and has been since the twenty-eighth day of the month after this one, and the third of his three is in the pocket of a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read and is not repaid and is on no roll and has nineteen shapes on it, and nobody in that room turned any of the three over, and a girl of seventeen said afterwards that she had wanted to turn the blank one over and had not, and that the wanting was hers, and a man of thirty-two said that there is a third thing on that bench that nobody in this city can read, and that a docker of fifty-eight who cannot read has said all nineteen of them out loud to four different people over about five weeks, and that nobody has checked the count and that nobody is going to.**

**And a case is upstairs in a room with a door, and the strap on it is still untied, and a Hearthguard of forty-four is in a yard on no list, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and thirty days and has never said a word, and there are about nine rooms in this city with two words in them and none of those nine rooms has met any of the other eight, and a hearing is in twenty-four days, and it is not in this room, and it is in the next twenty-four days, and about nine people are going to be asked in a room, in daylight, whether they will stand in a room, and that is the only instrument anybody has.**
