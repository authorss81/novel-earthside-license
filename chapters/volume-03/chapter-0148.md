# Chapter 0148 — The Room Is Built

On the fifty-ninth and sixtieth days of the month after this one, which were the hundred and forty-ninth and hundred and fiftieth days of the flood and a Wednesday and a Thursday, about nine people built a room in a day, in daylight, in a building over a market, and it is the first thing anybody in this city has built that is meant to be sat in by somebody who is not from this city, and a building is the only shape in four hundred miles that is not a page, and it took eleven hours and about nine people and a borrowed bench and a great deal of argument about a table.

**The licence is ninety-nine days old and then one hundred. The undertaking is void and no office has been told. The field was not paid on the first of March, which was the eighty-fifth day of this flood, and that is sixty-four days ago and then sixty-five. The fee of eleven ounces of silver is entered as owing with no date for the eighty-ninth time. The black line in his right palm is one hundred and forty-eight days old and then one hundred and forty-nine, and it has not moved one sixteenth of an inch.**

---

They started at about the seventh hour on the Wednesday and the first thing they did was take a table out of the room, and it took about four minutes, and the argument about it had started four days earlier and had gone on for two of those days.

**It is the table every room in this city has had in it for a hundred and forty-nine days, and about nine people have sat round it and written things down and read things out and put hands flat on it, and a notary of fifty has used it as a place to stop a conversation, and the table is why a room is an office, and an office is a document.**

"A table is a page you can sit at," said a man of thirty-two, who is on no roster of anything. "**Nobody in this city has written that sentence down in a hundred and forty-nine days and I have been waiting since the thirty-ninth day of the month after this one for somebody else to say it. A table is a page you can sit at. A page is a thing a person is described on. A table is a page a person is described at.**"

"It is a table," said a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall, who had carried most of it down the stair.

"**It is a table in a room that is going to be asked to decide something about nine people, and if there is a table in it then about nine people will be on one side of it and about two hundred and forty people will be on the other, and that is not a room. That is a form with people in it.**"

---

**So the table went down the stair and stood in the hall below for two days, and it is entered that a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall looked at it in the hall on the Wednesday and said that it had been a table for about forty years and had been a form for about a hundred and forty-nine days, and that nobody had noticed, and that this is the only thing anybody has built in this city that has been got rid of in a hundred and forty-nine days without a fight.**

The floor underneath is boards and there is a mark on it. Nine people have stood round a mark on a floor in this city a very large number of times and the mark is still there and it is not going anywhere and there is no instrument in four hundred miles for taking a mark off a floor, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall found it with his thumb at about the ninth hour and did not say anything about it, and about four other people found it with their eyes in the next nine minutes and none of them said anything.

**And a keeper of a floor for fifty-one years, who is about seventy, who was not asked anything on the thirty-fifth day of the month after this one and has not been asked anything since, was sent for on the Thursday morning and did not come, and nobody sent a second message, and the reason is on a slate in a book in this city and the reason is *because she asked to be left alone and not because we thought of it*.**

---

The window is the other thing, and it took most of the morning.

There were two shutters and one of them had been nailed shut since before any of them were born, and there is a mark on the frame where a third shutter used to be and nobody in this city knows when it was taken off, and a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall got the second one off at about the eleventh hour with a bar and a boy of nine holding the other end of the bar, and the boy of nine wanted to be the one holding the bar and was not the one holding the bar.

**The light came in from the west, over the market, and at about the second hour of the afternoon on the Wednesday it came across the floor of a room over a market and hit the wall where a man of about forty-four who keeps a stall had chalked nothing, and a woman of twenty-four said that a hearing is to be in daylight and a room with the shutters nailed shut is a room somebody can say anything in, and a girl of seventeen said that a room with the shutters nailed shut is a room where about nine people can hear each other and about two hundred and forty cannot, and that is a different room and it is a form with people in it.**

---

**And the two hundred and forty was the hard part and it took about four hours and it is the finding of the sixtieth day of the month after this one, and it is that a room in this city is a room and the floor below it is a hall with a door on the street, and the stairs are narrow, and about two hundred and forty people standing on a landing and in a hall is not a crowd and is not a mob and is a thing that happens at a market, and the finding is that this city already knows how to have two hundred and forty people hear a thing in a building and has known it for a hundred and forty-nine days, at a market, in daylight, on a Saturday, and has been using it four times.**

"It is the roll," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. "**It is the roll of public business in the converted salt store, and about forty people read it, and the reason forty is the right number and not two hundred and forty is that a wall in a room is read by forty people. So we put a thing in a room that about two hundred and forty people can hear out of the market on a Saturday, and then a girl of seventeen reads it back four times in the hall for the people at the back, and that is the whole of the instrument and it is two rooms and not one.**"

"A man from that office will not agree to be read back at," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall.

"Then he will not be read back at," said the woman of about thirty-four, "**and that will be entered, and it will be the first time in a hundred and forty-nine days that anybody has declined to be described, and I would like that noted while I am saying it.**"

---

**And the bench, which is where the two days went, and it is a borrowed bench and it came from the hall below and it is the same bench a boy of nine put a slate on on the fortieth day of the month after this one, and it is the only bench in the room.**

"There is a seat for somebody," said a man of thirty-two. "**Put it against the wall. Somebody who cannot stand.**"

"It is not against the wall," said a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. "**It is in the middle and it is facing the door.**"

"It is a bench," said a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall, who was not arguing, who was simply stating the fact of a bench.

"**It is a bench facing the door,**" said the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, "**and a bench facing the door is a seat, and a seat is a place to be angry at, and Nell Bray said that in the second week of this flood and it has been on a wall in this city ever since, and I have refused four things in a hundred and forty-nine days and I am not going to let a bench be the fifth, because in about four months there will be a story about a bench and a person in it and the story will be that somebody was made to sit on it.**"

**And a man of forty-four who keeps a floor said the thing that settled it, and it is nine words, and a girl of seventeen did not count them, because nobody counts them any more.**

"**Then it is not a bench. It is a step.**"

They took it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the Wednesday and it is now a step at the back of the room, about nine inches high, and it is where anybody who cannot stand for two hours sits, and it is against the wall, and it has no name on it, and it is not a seat and nobody is put on it, and a man of about forty-four who keeps a floor said that a step is a thing a floor has and not a thing a room offers.

---

**And the room was finished at about the seventh hour on the Thursday with nothing on the walls, and that is the last of the decisions and it took four minutes and a girl of seventeen said the reason out loud before anybody had argued for it.**

"No notices," she said. "**Nothing on the wall. Not the date, not the nine, not the eleven lines, not the compact, not the return, not the appointment, not the receipt. A wall in a room that is going to be read by about forty people is the cheapest thing in this city and there is already one. And if there is a thing on this wall then in about four months somebody will say that a thing on a wall in a room is a page, and it is a page, and it will be the first page anybody in this city has put in a room on purpose.**"

Somebody said the date.

"**The date goes on the door,**" said the girl of seventeen. "**A door is not a page. A door is a thing a person can be asked at, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall put a date on a door in a chalk hand in this city about a hundred and thirty days ago and it is still there and nobody can wash it, and a woman of seventy said that is not a protection and it is not. But it is not a page either.**"

---

**And the room was built in about eleven hours across two days by about nine people, and it is not a document, and it is not a roll, and it is not a form, and it is not a stone and it is not a slate and it is not a book, and it is the first thing anybody in this city has built that is meant to be sat in by somebody who is not from this city, and a building is a shape and the only shape in four hundred miles that is not a page, and a girl of seventeen wrote that down in a column four lines high and read it back before anybody had asked her to.**

> **On the fifty-ninth and sixtieth days of the month after this one a room was built in a building over a market for a hearing on the sixty-fourth day. It has a door with a date on it in chalk and no other mark on it. It has one shutter open and one shutter off. It has about nine places to stand and a step at the back about nine inches high with no name on it. It has no table and no bench and no seat. It has nothing on the walls. The floor below it is a hall with a door on the street, and the hall and the landing and the market below it are part of the instrument, and about two hundred and forty people can hear out of them. It is a room and it is gone in a day. Nothing in it travels. A page travels. This does not.**

---

**And nothing else happened on the fifty-ninth and sixtieth days of the month after this one that anybody has written down, and a hearing is in five days and then in four, and there is one hearing and one clock. And a box is in a room with a door on the second floor of a building over a market and has been there thirty-three days and then thirty-four, and is not opened, and the strap on it is still untied, and a notary of fifty has been asked five times and has answered none of the five. And a printed appointment of an interpreter with nine terms on it is on a table in the hall below. And a compact of eleven lines is drafted and is not read out and is not signed. And a correct number about a district is on a wall in a converted salt store. And nine households of a village refused a fourth time on the twenty-second day of the month after this one, which is thirty-seven days ago and then thirty-eight, and the water has not been received for eighty days and then eighty-one counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood. And thirty-nine of forty men turn up under the near gate and have not been paid since the first of March, which is sixty-four days and then sixty-five, and one of the forty has not come for thirty-five days and nobody has been told. And the measurement column has twenty-four readings and one ruled line left and the twenty-fifth reading has not been taken for sixty-four days and then sixty-five. And the main breach is closed, dressed, measured and silent and has never said a word, and the Narrow Mark has been opened twice and not a third time, and the boards under the chapel have not been lifted since the fortieth day of this flood, and the ground is a scar, and the stone in the arch has said nothing in a hundred and fifty days. And there have been thirty-two things the wall has said in this city and three of them have been refused, and the refusals in the second book a clerk of the Registry keeps at home are twenty-five, and the count at Aurel is thirteen, and the difference is twelve, and it has never been made smaller. And the fifty-ninth and sixtieth days of the month after this one are the fifty-fifth and fifty-sixth silences and the pattern is fifty-six for fifty-six.**

**And the last thing of the sixtieth day of the month after this one is a room with a table in the hall below it and a bench turned into a step and a shutter off and nothing on the walls, and a hearing in four days.**
