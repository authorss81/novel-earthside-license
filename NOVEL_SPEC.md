# Novel Specification

Title: The Earthside License
Repository slug: novel-earthside-license
Genre: isekai / game-lit / political fantasy
Male lead: A game designer who accidentally becomes the first licensed player
Length target: 900 chapters
Relationship policy: One slow-burn relationship or no romance, decided in the bible phase.
Power rule: Growth must be earned through practice, cost, failure, relationships, and changed decisions. The lead must not become instantly overpowered unless the approved genre premise requires it.

## Premise
Limited groups of Earth players enter a medieval fantasy world believing they have joined a game, while the local world experiences them as divine refugees and invaders.

**As commissioned. As written, from Volume 05 on, this premise is played inside its own frame: the
"game" is real, the license is real, and the two worlds are real, and the book's later volumes are
about what real people are owed when someone has built a system that tells them what counts. Adrian
Vale is still a game designer and still holds the first Earthside License; he is not a man inside a
simulation, and no chapter says he is. See "What this manuscript actually became" below and
`outline/series.md` DECISIONS ONE THROUGH FOUR.**

## Ending
The lie becomes a stable bridge between worlds or is exposed before the two civilizations destroy each other.

## What this manuscript actually became, and why this file's own premise is half-stale
The premise above is the pitch the book was commissioned on. The book that has been written is the
second volume of that pitch, and the change was deliberate, numbered, and recorded.

**`outline/series.md` DECISION TWO, clause 3, is the governing rule and it is not up for re-litigation
by a review pass:**

> There is no System, no quest, no class, no reward and no obedience, and there will not be. Where a
> Volume 06 or later chapter needs the instrument this manuscript calls the wall, it is a person who
> has said a plain rule out loud, and it is set plain, in its own block, with no emphasis marks, at
> most one in a whole volume, and it answers nothing.

**Consequence for reviewers: a low or falling count of System panels in a late volume is the plan
being executed, not a defect, and must not be "fixed" by re-introducing game vocabulary.** Panel
blocks per volume run 40, 46, 26, 22, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1. The collapse happens between
Volumes 04 and 05 and is where the series outline records that the earlier book ended and this one
began. The one panel in Volume 13, in Chapter 0640, is the instrument working as written.

Two further recorded rules that read as missing features and are not:

- **The word *player* is not a word in this world and may not become one by narration.** It cannot
  enter a page as narration. It enters a page when a person says it out loud in a room, in daylight,
  with a date on it, or not at all. Its absence from a page is therefore correct by default.
- **The narrator has no frame.** No *this chapter*, *this volume*, *in this batch*, or *the reader*.
  The one deliberate exception is Volume 02's *this volume*, named in the series outline as a closed
  volume and standing on purpose.

`outline/ending.md` keeps the System in the far future as "a visible fragment of the Veyran First
Grammar, not a simulation or a remotely created game." That is the same rule seen from the end of the
book, and the ending has not been contradicted by any volume written since the decision.
