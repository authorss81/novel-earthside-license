# Volume 01 Outline: The First Door

## Volume identity

- **Chapter range:** 1–50
- **Central pressure:** A damaged Anchorstone is pulling a **breach** open at the Lock of Salt in Veyra; the lock itself stays undamaged until Chapter 1. Adrian must survive the first crossing, stop the damage from spreading past the breach, protect the people the door has already taken, and decide whether any controlled passage is safer than an uncontrolled one. His private objective remains a way home; the volume forces him to accept that a return route is also a public decision with a named cost.
- **Starting state:** Adrian is alone on Earth, working on a canceled game project and trying to finish one last onboarding system. He has no Veyran relationship, no combat ability, and no knowledge that the interface in front of him is attached to a real place. The old maintenance oath at the lock has not yet failed, and no Earthside person has been displaced.
- **Starting relationships:** Adrian has a distant but real relationship with his sister Nora, strained by missed calls and his habit of treating personal conversations like maintenance tasks. He has no Veyran relationship of any kind. Tamsin Quill, Mina Okoye, Dante Kuo, Priya Shah, Jonah Pike, Noor Haddad, Owen Park, and Ivenn Marrow are all strangers to him. Mina, Dante, Priya, Jonah, Noor, and Owen live in five other cities, know neither Adrian nor one another, and will be pulled in as strangers when the outer tear opens in Chapter 5.
- **Starting power level:** **Stage 0, Unlicensed body.** Adrian can see an occasional pale prompt and perceive harsh light in a damaged join, but he has no workway, return mark, controlled passage, translation, or combat ability. He holds no rank, duty, or debt. In Chapter 1 he becomes a **fragment-bearer**, not a recognized License holder.
- **Ending power level:** Adrian has earned **Stage 1, Witness**. He can perceive and mark the nearest join-line and understand one direct Veyran promise at a time. He cannot create a working, open a threshold alone, or protect a place by strength. He remains accountable for the public duty he accepted. He holds **no workway**: he declines the Wayfinder offer in Chapter 7 and stays Unbound through the volume, so every route-reading he has is borrowed from Tamsin or from a local practice. A thin black line on his palm is an overload warning, not a return mark. It never becomes a black scar in a person in this volume; that is reserved for Owen’s return mark in Chapter 38, and the restriction is a deliberate authorial choice rather than a protection the System grants him. He never reaches Stage 2 during this volume.

## Major locations

- **The Morrowglass workroom, Earth:** Adrian’s ordinary life, deadline pressure, and the Chapter 1 acceptance.
- **The Lock of Salt and its maintenance tunnels:** The public tide gate and working site where the main breach opens.
- **The near gate:** the inner working gate inside the lock tunnels, held by dock workers during repairs. It is a piece of local infrastructure, not the arriving door, and the confusion between the two causes the Chapter 4 failure.
- **The lock chapel:** Local practice, disputed history, the Chapter 7 black line, and the chapel stone. The hairline seam that opens in its floor in Chapter 3 is the same old damage that the Chapter 24 dig exposes in full beneath it.
- **The outer channel and its wall:** the site below the lock where the outer tear opens in Chapter 5, is closed by quorum in Chapter 9, and leaves the maintenance seam that becomes the Chapter 10 route.
- **The dry-goods shelter above the river market:** The first temporary home for the Earthside group.
- **Reed Reach:** the wetland network whose shared water gates take the same river pressure as Saltwake’s lower fields, which is why the Reed Commons are the receiving community for the Chapter 10 route and why one Reed village refuses by name: the route’s posted cost lands on water they also use. No Reed character is on stage before Chapter 10.
- **Aurel (by letter only in this volume):** The Glass Crown’s capital, courts, clerks, and the political pressure it brings to the Saltwake trial.
- **The Quiet Provinces (named, not visited):** Ivenn names this as the region where villages were removed from official records. The promise stays a promise in this volume and does not require a journey the first fifty chapters do not need.

## Major factions

- **The Lantern Concord:** Saltwake’s civic council of dock families, guilds, healers, and notaries. It holds the lock where the breach opened, maintains the public ledger, and treats every crossing as a civic question. Its notary writes and reads the Chapter 10 ledger entry and presses the fragment-mark into the seam; the working itself is run by the anchorwright and the dock crew, because a notary records a working rather than performing it.
- **The Hearthguard:** Village and road companies who will protect people, flood work, and lost travelers only on terms they accept.
- **The Reed Commons:** The wetland assemblies downstream. They are a receiving community rather than a rescue party, and their refusal in Chapter 10 is recorded rather than overruled.
- **The Glass Crown:** A distant but watchful authority that wants the bridge registered, taxed, and eventually controlled.
- **The Quiet Choir:** A hidden order of custodians and scholars. Ivenn Marrow is its first visible face; the Choir as an institution remains mostly offstage.
- **The Brass Measure:** Material suppliers and repair contractors who see profit and leverage in a city that needs stone, iron, and labor.
- **The first cohort:** seven people — Adrian plus the six who came through the outer tear, Mina Okoye, Dante Kuo, Priya Shah, Jonah Pike, Noor Haddad, and Owen Park. They have bodies, injuries, money, paper, phones without signal, and Earthside family obligations. “The six” always means the arrivals alone. Eli Mercer arrives later in a smaller crossing, and is not one of the seven.

## Site map

The names below are the volume’s stable working vocabulary. The prose should not rename the same place twice, and it should not give one event two sites.

- **The main breach:** the damaged opening in the Lock of Salt gate itself. It is the Chapter 1 arrival site, it stays open and under watch for the rest of the volume, and it is the thing the closing distributed working closes in **Chapters 48–50**. Nothing in Chapters 4–5 happens here; Adrian’s repair sequence pushes the failure somewhere else.
- **The outer tear:** the smaller join in the wall of the outer channel, below the lock. It **opens in Chapter 5** at the one joint the Chapter 4 repair leaves unwatched, is closed by a quorum working in **Chapter 9**, and leaves a **maintenance seam**. In Chapter 10 the Chapter 1 fragment-mark is pressed into that seam and the site is recorded as a narrow **quorum route**: one named living traveler per opening, no crowd movement, a fresh inspection before reuse, and a receiving community that may suspend it. Because one Reed village refuses, the recognition is incomplete and the route is pending rather than licensed; it stays that way until the closing public working of Chapters 48–50. The seam is reached by the two-hundred-step **walk-round** along the outer channel, with a low parapet and a half-rotten rail, and it sits under the cap of an old landing. Established on the page in Chapters 1–10: the seam is eighteen inches long and three fingers wide, and its shape is the exact negative of the Chapter 1 fragment-mark, which is why the two fit. The **displaced Anchorstone** that Ivenn's Chapter 7 fragment points to is a different place entirely — under the lock chapel's own yard-side landing, four steps left of the yard, dry in its footing. Do not let the two be confused; nothing in Chapters 1–10 has moved the Anchorstone or touched the old river boundary.
- **The near gate:** the inner working gate in the lock tunnels where dock workers hold a join of their own during repairs in Chapters 4 and 9. It is infrastructure that can fail through neglect, not a crossing.
- **The chapel-floor hairline seam:** the hairline crack in the lock chapel floor that opens in Chapter 3 and carries water uphill. It is the visible edge of the older damage beneath the chapel, and it is the same damage the Chapter 24 dig exposes fully as the outer threshold. The **chapel stone** takes the Chapter 7 black line and is a different surface from the floor seam. Established on the page in Chapters 3, 7 and 8: the chapel stone is a single pale slab laid *across* the flagstones at the foot of the arch, raised four inches, worn into a shallow bowl by four hundred years of boots, which every person entering the chapel steps over. The floor seam is in the long flagstones four feet beyond it. Keep them apart in every later chapter, including the dig: the Chapter 24 excavation is in the floor, not in the slab.
- **The scarred outer threshold:** a separate, older damaged Anchorstone beneath the chapel floor, on the bank **above** the outer channel and therefore out of reach of Adrian’s Chapter 4 repair sequence. Its first visible sign is the Chapter 3 hairline; the dig **uncovers and identifies it in Chapter 24**; it does not tear until Chapter 25, when it becomes the first **black scar in a place**. It carries voices and cannot be controlled. Owen’s fatal crossing in Chapters 31–40 is tied to this site and not to the Chapter 10 route.

## Volume promise

The volume must answer a real question: **can the first crossing be made survivable without pretending that the people inside it are disposable game characters?** It must close the first-door arc, establish the no-respawn rule, give Adrian the limited Witness permission, and introduce Ivenn’s method of editing the System.

**Not discharged in Batch 0001:** the first ten chapters build a route, not a permanent answer. The main breach stays open, the Chapter 10 route is a narrow and empty **pending** instrument whose terms are entered in the ledger but whose recognition is incomplete, and the first return mark has not been created for anyone. The volume’s gate is whether the main breach can be closed without putting one hand in charge.

**Premise status:** the “Earthside arrivals believe they have joined a game” half of the premise is **deliberately not established in Chapters 1–10**. The first seven are too busy surviving to theorize, and the batch argues the opposite of the genre assumption: they are not a selected party, not a class roster, and nobody respawns. The mistaken-belief premise is seeded instead by Earthside game vocabulary in Adrian’s own mouth, and it becomes explicit later, when larger groups of players cross expecting a game. Which volume carries that shift is not yet fixed and must not be assumed by a Chapters 1–10 writer. A writer of Chapters 1–10 must therefore not have the cohort state the belief that this is a game.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 1–3: The wrong opening

1. Adrian accepts an impossible permission fragment during a late-night onboarding test at Morrowglass.
2. He wakes at the Lock of Salt with no combat ability, no local language, and a breach already beginning to damage the lock.
3. Tamsin Quill and Hearthguard workers contain him, but locals call him a skyborn omen and a possible spy. His first useful acts are physical and apologetic, not magical.

### Chapters 4–5: The door takes six strangers

4. Adrian recognizes the lock’s failure as a systems problem and proposes a repair sequence built from water, pressure, and a join he can read while other workers hold a working of their own at the near gate; the sequence drives the failure out to an unwatched joint beside the outer channel.
5. The unwatched joint opens as the **outer tear** and pulls six more Earthside people through. Nobody consented to that door and nobody was holding it. Mina Okoye, Dante Kuo, Priya Shah, Jonah Pike, Noor Haddad, and Owen Park arrive as strangers with no shared history and no way back.

### Chapters 6–12: Seven people, one city

The cohort and the Saltwake community struggle with shelter, food, language, and the first appearance of a class-like template. Adrian learns that the System will show a possible action but will not necessarily tell the truth about its social cost. One early repair works and leaves a small residue that damages a market. The arrival is not a free party spawn: the newcomers have bodies, injuries, money, paper, phones with no signal, and Earthside family obligations.

Tamsin insists that the newcomers keep her three conditions: do not touch a working, do not use a local name, ask before taking a thing. Adrian argues that an emergency requires faster action. Their disagreement is practical, personal, and about who has the right to say no. Adrian adds a fourth rule of his own in Chapter 6 — tell Mina about every injury — which is the first time he legislates for people instead of asking them.

### Chapters 13–20: The formal audience

Ivenn Marrow, already met in the Chapter 7 chapel audience, presents himself publicly as a Crown Law scholar. He demonstrates that he knows the System’s vocabulary and claims the present breach was a failed public experiment. He offers Adrian a “Wayfinder” workway and a complete quest to recover a displaced Anchorstone before the next tide. The offer is useful, partly true, and omits the cost of closing the river’s old boundary. **Adrian refuses it again here, and stays Unbound for the whole volume.** Ivenn does not withdraw the offer; it stays open, cheap, and pointed at Aurel, which is how a man with no official power keeps hold of the first player.

The first cohort begins to acquire local roles: Mina works with local healers, Dante organizes supplies, Priya records the crossing, Noor audits food, and Jonah negotiates with the Brass Measure. They keep making game-shaped assumptions while Adrian decides whether to use the workway for personal escape or for the group’s survival — a decision he answers by not taking it and learning route-reading from Tamsin instead. The Glass Crown’s first letters arrive asking who is answering for the bridge.

### Chapters 21–30: The black scar

The quest leads to a chamber beneath the lock chapel. Ivenn’s instructions cause a join-line to overload, and the dig under the chapel floor **uncovers and identifies the separate outer threshold in Chapter 24** — the same older damage the Chapter 3 hairline has been showing — but it does not yet tear. The voice channel the chapel stone has carried since Chapter 7 becomes a two-way, uncontrolled route: people on both sides can hear one another, but the crossing cannot be controlled. The workers already know what a voice can do, so the discovery is more frightening, not less.

Local water pressure drops, a child is missing, and Tamsin, Mina, and Adrian argue over whether to spend the first opening of the narrow route on the rescue — which means establishing a return mark and breaking the standing terms in public — or hold the route for the first possible return. Ivenn uses the confusion to promote the idea that only one “correct” version can keep the city safe. Adrian learns that a local oath can stabilize a damaged join only if the people affected understand the cost.

**The Chapter 25 volume midpoint reversal:** a deliberately controlled, locally witnessed attempt to stabilize the main lock succeeds at the work it was meant to do and still goes wrong. The older damaged Anchorstone beneath the chapel floor — the outer threshold, uncovered in Chapter 24 and out of reach of the working — tears open as a breach without consent or witnesses and pulls in a second, unrequested group of arrivals, including Eli Mercer, while the agreed working holds the main gate. The grab leaves the outer threshold as the first **black scar in a place**. This is not the Chapter 5 mechanism: the quorum route is doing exactly what its witnesses and cost allow, one named person per opening, and the failure is at the site it could not reach. Owen is at the exposed site as a bystander when it tears, is injured reaching for the child, and spends the rest of the volume carrying a damaged shoulder that no healer can fully set right; he is a victim of the reversal, not its author, and it does not kill him. Adrian learns that controlling one seam was never the same as controlling the site. His immediate goal changes from “find a way home” to keeping the people who came through with him alive without turning them into a sacrifice for his return.

### Chapters 31–40: The false rescue

Jonah proposes a settlement deal with the Brass Measure: give the players a secure warehouse, shared equipment, and work rights in exchange for exclusive access to the lock. It is attractive to the frightened cohort and unacceptable to dock workers who will lose their bargaining position. Adrian’s delay allows Jonah to present himself as the only practical leader.

A temporary return mark is established for Owen as part of a volunteer rescue. He tries to cross **out** through the **scarred outer threshold** — not the Chapter 10 route, which the Concord is withholding while it rules on whether a first license may exist at all — but the crossing never completes. The route tears and drops him back into Saltwake badly hurt, on top of the Chapter 25 shoulder the local healers never fully set. Mina and local healers treat him, and he dies in Chapter 38 anyway. The mark breaks into the first **black scar in a person**, which is the first scar that ends a life and the first one another traveler might have tried to inherit. It does not resurrect him or transfer to another player. Adrian recognizes that his earlier decision to keep the door open contributed to the danger, without erasing Owen’s own choice to force it. The second group from Chapter 25 is named in the public record and housed separately from the first cohort; Eli Mercer is injured and refuses to join the six or speak for them.

### Chapters 41–47: Choice at the Lock

The Hearthguard and Lantern Concord give Adrian one night to choose among:

- close the main breach and strand any person still near the scar,
- keep the unstable opening and risk a citywide reverberation,
- accept a single-author Crown containment that would make the **main breach’s** containment answerable to one named holder — a Concord clerk answerable only to Aurel — instead of to a local quorum. This is a different instrument from the Chapter 10 route, which is not what the Crown is offering to absorb, and Adrian must say so out loud or the choice reads as a technicality.

Ivenn privately offers the third option in a more attractive form. Adrian recognizes the Single Witness logic. Tamsin and the dock workers reveal that the old lock can be held by a distributed public working, but only if the standing route is one that **no single person, including Adrian, can open or suspend alone**, and he accepts that he cannot guarantee the result.

### Chapters 48–50: The trial license

Adrian, Tamsin, Mina, Dante, Noor, Priya, and the Lantern Concord perform a narrow public working. The main breach closes, the black scar remains as a visible scar rather than a hidden secret, and the Chapter 10 quorum route is re-inspected and narrowed to a **two-witness route whose standing order requires a fresh inspection before every opening**, with a flood-season review. The two witnesses are the count required *per opening*; the four people who signed the Chapter 10 inspection are the quorum that authorized the route in the first place, and the reduction from four to two is the volume's argument that a working can be made cheaper without being made private. The route is not an invitation for unlimited players; it is a trial arrangement, and even after the working it carries one named person per opening, only after a return mark is separately established and only after the witnesses sign a fresh inspection.

Owen is buried with his real name and his family’s message, and the record notes the Chapter 25 shoulder injury as the injury that was never set, so the cause of his death is not simplified into the crossing alone. The surviving first cohort and the separately recorded Chapter 25 arrivals receive shelter, food, and medical care, but no automatic land rights, no class privileges, no workway of any kind — Adrian is still Unbound at the end of the volume — and no promise that they will all be allowed home. Ivenn remains publicly useful and privately dangerous.

## Midpoint reversal

Two failures are deliberately staged in this volume, and they are not the same event.

- **Chapters 4–5, the forced expansion:** Adrian’s repair sequence drives the failure out to an unwatched joint beside the outer channel, and that joint opens as the outer tear and drags six strangers through. The door takes people because nobody consented to that door and nobody was holding it. This is the **Batch 0001 midpoint** and the moment the genre promise changes from one man discovering a world to a group managing an emergency.
- **Chapter 25, the volume midpoint reversal:** a *deliberate, controlled, locally witnessed* attempt to stabilize the lock succeeds physically and still fails at the site, losing a second, unrequested group of arrivals. The first cohort has already proven that the License is not a private escape hatch; the Chapter 25 surge proves that even a good working can fail at a place it could not reach.

Chapter 25 must not repeat Chapter 5’s mechanism. In Chapter 5 there is no consent to that door and nobody holding it, and the failure happens at the outer tear because Adrian’s own sequence pushed the water there. In Chapter 25 there is consent, a quorum, and a cost for the working, and the failure happens at the older damaged Anchorstone beneath the chapel floor that the working could not reach. The quorum route still carries one named person per opening; it is the outer threshold that grabs, not the route.

## Volume climax

Across Chapters 41–47, Adrian rejects Ivenn’s single-author stabilization and commits to a distributed working that will close the main breach without giving him sole control. Tamsin has authority over the local record, Mina can prove the medical cost, Dante can move civilians, Noor can account for supplies, Priya can publicly name the people at risk, and the Lantern Concord’s workers can maintain the lock. Adrian’s Witness standing lets him identify and mark the join clearly enough that the witnesses can work it safely; it cannot perform the closure alone. The working itself is performed in **Chapters 48–50** by those named people, not by Adrian.

The climax is built from the volume’s own work: the flood, the lock, the seam, the scars, the records, the food, the injuries, and the people who signed or refused. No one wins because Adrian became stronger.

## Concrete resolution

- The Lock of Salt is repaired enough to prevent a citywide collapse, but the black scar remains visible and monitored.
- Saltwake, the Hearthguard, and the surviving Earthside arrivals sign a **trial license** with a fixed quorum, a public cost record, and a right to review. The trial can create individual return marks, but it does not create them automatically for every survivor.
- Adrian has the Witness stage and can mark a join, but he cannot open a portal alone and holds no workway. The route is operated by the local quorum.
- The first cohort’s names, injuries, and one death are recorded rather than hidden behind game language. The Chapter 25 arrivals are named separately, including Eli Mercer’s injury and refusal to speak for the first cohort.
- Ivenn has established himself as a translator and adviser. He has not yet been exposed as the editor of the prompts.
- The larger world beyond Saltwake knows that something arrived, but not that Earth is connected by a recoverable route.
- The immediate promise is answered: the first crossing is survivable, bounded, and accounted for. It is not yet safe, fair, or permanent.

## Next-volume question

If the first bridge can be held by shared witnesses, who will control the next wave of people, resources, and “workways” that will use it? The Saltwake trial is a beginning, not an institution.

## Final chapter image

The repaired lock opens only for a single witnessed return mark. Tamsin writes the next inspection date in a public ledger while the ledger entry naming Adrian as the route’s first witness rests on a piece of ordinary river stone. A child asks whether the next person will be a player; Adrian looks toward the dark water and has not yet found an answer that does not make the child smaller.
