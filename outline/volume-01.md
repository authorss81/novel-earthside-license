# Volume 01 Outline: The First Door

- **Chapter range:** 1–50
- **Central pressure:** A damaged Earthside threshold is opening at the Lock of Salt in Veyra. Adrian must survive the first crossing, prevent the breach from widening, and decide whether any controlled passage is safer than a door no one can close.
- **Starting state:** Adrian is alone on Earth, working on a canceled game project and trying to finish one last onboarding system. He has no relationship, no combat ability, and no knowledge that the interface on his screen is connected to a real world.
- **Starting relationships:** Adrian has a distant but real relationship with his sister Nora, strained by missed calls and his habit of treating personal conversations like maintenance tasks. He has no Veyran relationships. Mina, Dante, Priya, Jonah, Noor, and Owen are strangers who become the six-person first cohort through the damaged crossing. Eli Mercer joins a later, smaller crossing in this volume.
- **Starting power level:** Stage 0, Unlicensed body. Adrian can see an occasional pale prompt and sense a harsh light in a damaged join, but he has no stable workway, return mark, or translation. He is physically ordinary and fully vulnerable.
- **Major locations:** The Earth workroom; the Lock of Salt and its under-stone maintenance tunnels; Saltwake’s river market and Lantern Concord hall; the old lock chapel; the first temporary shelter above a dry goods store; the black-scarred outer threshold.
- **Major factions:** The Lantern Concord, Hearthguard volunteers, the Quiet Choir’s first visible agent Ivenn Marrow, local dock workers, and the arriving Earthside cohort. The Brass Measure appears as a material supplier and political pressure, not yet a primary antagonist.

## Volume promise

The volume must answer a real question: **can the first crossing be made survivable without pretending that the people inside it are disposable game characters?** It must close the first-door arc, establish the no-respawn rule, give Adrian a limited Witness permission, and introduce Ivenn’s method of editing the System. It may open the larger mystery of the First Grammar but must not require the reader to understand it yet.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 1–5: The wrong opening

1. Adrian accepts an impossible License prompt during a late-night onboarding test at Morrowglass.
2. He wakes at the Lock of Salt with no combat ability, no local language, and a portal already beginning to damage the lock.
3. Tamsin Quill and Hearthguard workers contain him, but locals call him a skyborn omen and a possible spy.
4. Adrian recognizes the lock’s failure as a systems problem and helps with a practical repair, but his first intervention pulls additional people through.
5. Mina, Dante, Priya, Jonah, Noor, and Owen arrive disoriented in a surge of light. The locals’ fear becomes immediate and physical.

### Chapters 6–12: Seven people, one city

“Seven” includes Adrian and the six-person first cohort. The cohort and the Saltwake community struggle with shelter, food, language, and the first appearance of a class-like template. Adrian learns that the System will show a possible action but not necessarily tell the truth about its social cost. One early repair works; another leaves a small local residue and damages a market. The cohort’s arrival is not a free party spawn: they have bodies, injuries, money, paper, phones with no signal, and Earthside family obligations.

Tamsin insists that the newcomers ask permission before using local names or tools. Adrian argues that an emergency requires faster action. Their disagreement is practical and personal.

### Chapters 13–20: The formal audience

Ivenn Marrow, first seen at the preliminary chapel audience, now presents himself publicly as a Crown Law scholar. He demonstrates that he knows the System’s vocabulary and claims the present breach was a failed public experiment. He offers Adrian a “Wayfinder” workway and a complete quest to recover a displaced Anchorstone before the next tide. The offer is useful, partly true, and omits the cost of closing the river’s old boundary.

The first cohort begins to acquire local roles: Mina works with local healers, Dante organizes supplies, Priya records the crossing, Noor audits food, and Jonah negotiates with the Brass Measure. They keep making game-shaped assumptions, while Adrian must decide whether to use the workway for personal escape or for the group’s survival.

### Chapters 21–30: The black scar

The quest leads to a chamber beneath the lock chapel. Ivenn’s instructions cause a join-line to overload, and the outer threshold becomes the first black scar **in a place**. People on both sides can hear one another through it, but the crossing can no longer be controlled. Local water pressure drops; a child is missing; Owen insists on forcing the door open and is injured when the route tears. This is the volume’s scar in the world, not in a person: no one has died yet, and no one has a scar of their own.

Adrian learns that a local oath can stabilize a damaged join only if the people affected understand the cost. Tamsin, Mina, and Adrian argue over whether to sacrifice a private return route to close the scar. Ivenn uses the confusion to promote the idea that only one “correct” version can keep the city safe.

### Chapters 31–40: The false rescue

Jonah proposes a settlement deal with the Brass Measure: give the players a secure warehouse, shared equipment, and work rights in exchange for exclusive access to the lock. It is attractive to the frightened cohort and unacceptable to dock workers who will lose their bargaining position. Adrian’s delay allows Jonah to present himself as the only practical leader.

A temporary return mark is established for Owen as part of the volunteer rescue. He returns through the black scar badly hurt, and his condition proves that the crossing can carry disease and injury as well as people. Mina and local healers treat him, but he dies in Chapter 38. The mark breaks into the first black scar **in a person** — not the first scar in the story, since the outer threshold is already scarred and the chapel stone has carried a black line since Chapter 7. It is the first scar that ends a life, and the first one another traveler could have tried to inherit. It does not resurrect him or transfer to another player. Adrian recognizes that his earlier decision to keep the door open contributed to the danger, without erasing Owen’s own choice to force it.

### Chapters 41–47: Choice at the Lock

The Hearthguard and Lantern Concord give Adrian one night to choose among:

- close the main threshold and strand any person still near the scar,
- keep the unstable opening and risk a citywide reverberation,
- accept a single-author Crown containment that will let one authority control all future crossings.

Ivenn privately offers the third option in a more attractive form. Adrian recognizes the Single Witness logic. Tamsin and the dock workers reveal that the old lock can be held by a distributed public working, but only if Adrian gives up his exclusive hold on the License and accepts that he cannot guarantee the result.

### Chapters 48–50: The trial license

Adrian, Tamsin, Mina, Dante, Noor, Priya, and the Lantern Concord perform a narrow public working. The main breach closes, the black scar remains as a visible scar rather than a hidden secret, and the Chapter 10 quorum route is re-inspected, narrowed to a two-witness standing route, and given a flood-season review. The route is not an invitation for unlimited players; it is a trial arrangement, and even after the working it carries one named person per opening, only after a return mark is separately established and only after the witnesses sign a fresh inspection.

Owen is buried with his real name and family’s message. The surviving cohort receives shelter, food, and medical care, but no automatic land rights, no class privileges, and no promise that they will all be allowed home. Ivenn remains publicly useful and privately dangerous.

## Midpoint reversal

Two failures are deliberately staged in this volume, and they are not the same event.

- **Chapters 4–5, the forced expansion:** Adrian’s *unplanned* repair of the lock widens the breach and drags six strangers through. The door takes people because nobody consented and nobody was holding it. This is the **batch-1 midpoint** and the moment the genre promise changes from one man discovering a world to a group managing an emergency.
- **Chapter 25, the volume midpoint reversal:** a *deliberate, controlled, locally witnessed* attempt to stabilize the lock succeeds physically and still pulls in a second, unrequested group of arrivals. The six-person first cohort has already proven that the License is not a private escape hatch; the Chapter 25 surge proves that a properly agreed opening can fail too, which is worse news and cannot be fixed by better procedure. Adrian’s immediate goal changes from “find a way home” to "keep the people who came through with me alive without turning them into a sacrifice for my return.”

Chapter 25 must not repeat Chapter 5’s mechanism. In Chapter 5 there are no witnesses and no agreement; in Chapter 25 there is agreement, a quorum, a cost, and a failure anyway.

## Volume climax

At Chapters 44–47, Adrian rejects Ivenn’s single-author stabilization and joins a distributed working that closes the main breach without giving him sole control. Tamsin has authority over the local record, Mina can prove the medical cost, Dante can move civilians, Noor can account for supplies, Priya can publicly name the people at risk, and the Lantern Concord’s workers can maintain the lock. Adrian’s Witness mark lets him identify and hold the join long enough for the working; it cannot perform the closure alone.

## Concrete resolution

- The Lock of Salt is repaired enough to prevent a citywide collapse, but the black scar remains visible and monitored.
- Saltwake, the Hearthguard, and the surviving Earthside cohort sign a trial license with a fixed quorum, public cost record, and right to review. The trial can create individual return marks, but it does not create them automatically for every survivor.
- Adrian has the Witness stage and can mark a join, but cannot open a portal alone. The route is operated by the local quorum.
- The first cohort’s names, injuries, and one death are recorded rather than hidden behind game language.
- Ivenn has established himself as a translator and adviser. He has not yet been exposed as the editor of the prompts.
- The larger world beyond Saltwake knows that something arrived, but not that Earth is connected by a recoverable route.

## Next-volume question

If the first bridge can be held by shared witnesses, who will control the next wave of people, resources, and “workways” that will use it? The Saltwake trial is a beginning, not an institution.

## Final chapter image

The repaired lock opens only for a single witnessed return mark. Tamsin writes the next inspection date in a public ledger while Adrian’s first limited Witness mark rests on a piece of ordinary river stone. A child asks whether the next person will be a player; Adrian looks toward the dark water and has not yet found an answer that does not make the child smaller.
