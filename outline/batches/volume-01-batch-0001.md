# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline: The First Mark

- **Chapter range:** 1–10
- **Batch goal:** Establish the real crossing, give Adrian a bounded first working, form the first mixed local/Earthside group, and contain the uncontrolled breach without pretending that anyone is a disposable game character.
- **Starting condition:** Adrian is alone on Earth, finishing an onboarding build for a game project that may be canceled. He has no Veyran relationship, no stable magical ability, and no reason to believe the hidden prompt on his screen is real.
- **Starting relationships:** Adrian and his sister Nora are close but strained by missed calls and his habit of treating personal obligations as maintenance tasks. He has never met Tamsin Quill or any Veyran. Mina, Dante, Priya, Jonah, Noor, and Owen are strangers connected to the same test environment but not yet a party.
- **Starting power level:** Stage 0, Unlicensed body. A pale prompt and a physical mark appear, but Adrian has no stable workway, return mark, translation, or combat ability.
- **Batch midpoint:** Chapters 4–5. Adrian’s attempt to repair the Lock of Salt makes the breach wider and pulls six additional Earthside people through. The genre promise changes from one man discovering a fantasy world to a group managing a real emergency.
- **Batch climax:** Chapters 9–10. Adrian joins a collective working that closes the smaller outer tear, accepts a public debt, and helps establish a narrow quorum route. He refuses a reward-like prompt that would reopen the tear without consent. He remains a Witness, not a Passage holder.
- **Aftermath:** The group has shelter, a public ledger, and one limited route. It does not have land rights, class privileges, a guaranteed way home, or a resolved political relationship with Saltwake.
- **Required character changes:** Adrian moves from amused observer to responsible witness; Tamsin moves from wary captor to reluctant professional ally; Mina becomes the group’s medical conscience; Dante becomes the physical coordinator; Priya begins a public record; Ivenn establishes himself as a knowledgeable but dangerous adviser.
- **Required power changes:** Adrian learns the distinction between a working and a reward, earns the first Witness mark, and discovers that a collective quorum route can be maintained without granting him personal Passage. He does not receive a class template or unrestricted translation.
- **Required world reveals:** Veyra is real; the lock is a resonant site; the System uses a permission grammar that can be incomplete; the first crossing is physical and non-respawning; earlier Bright Pilgrims are part of local history; Ivenn knows more about the System than he explains.
- **Open questions after the batch:** Who inserted the License prompt? Why does the System know Adrian’s game vocabulary? What did the earlier crossing change? Why does the old lock answer with a voice from the wrong side? What will the Lantern Concord demand in the first formal hearing?

## Batch pacing

- **Chapters 1–3:** Immediate action, displacement, and relationship friction
- **Chapters 4–6:** First working, first cohort, practical survival
- **Chapters 7–8:** Mystery, manipulation, and the first system consequence
- **Chapter 9:** Irreversible decision and cost
- **Chapter 10:** Public aftermath, limited victory, and the next political pressure

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — The License on the Deadline

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Morrowglass Interactive, Earth; a little after 2 a.m. near the end of a deadline for *A Crown of Ash* onboarding
- **Immediate goal:** Finish the onboarding build and get through the morning meeting without being blamed for the project’s cancellation.
- **Resistance:** Adrian is exhausted, underpaid, and cynical about the company’s latest schedule change. The interface shows a hidden permission screen with no publisher header, and it appears to know the name he uses in the game’s test profile.
- **Information revealed:** A pale, unverified permission fragment offers a “limited Earthside test” and asks for a real name, a witness, and an acceptance of bodily risk. The fragment is not a valid threshold permission because no Veyran witness or receiving community exists yet. Adrian treats it as a prank or leaked prototype until the wording repeats a private joke from his own design notes.
- **Action or decision:** He enters his legal name and accepts the fragment instead of deleting the file. The screen does not show a success message; it shows a simple rule about a door that needs two willing names. The rule may be genuine, but Adrian’s acceptance is not enough to make the crossing legal.
- **Emotional change:** Professional defensiveness becomes alarm when the room lights fail and the sound of running water reaches him from nowhere.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian remains Stage 0, but a small mark appears on his palm and his phone loses signal. No abilities, items, or rewards are granted.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** *A Crown of Ash* is a fictional Earth game; Nora Vale is Adrian’s emergency contact; Adrian has no prior fantasy experience.
- **Ending type:** Discovery / earned hook
- **Next-chapter pull:** Adrian wakes beside a public lock he has never seen, and the mark on his palm is still warm.

### Chapter 0002 — The Wrong Kind of Loading

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake, Veyra; dawn during a rising flood tide
- **Immediate goal:** Find a dry route back to the place he came from and stop the water from pulling him under.
- **Resistance:** Adrian is bruised, cold, and cut off from the opening. Local dock workers approach with tools and weapons because they believe he is a skyborn spy. He cannot explain the crossing, and he cannot understand the local warning calls.
- **Information revealed:** Veyra is a living world with a functioning river economy. Locals see the arrival as a divine omen and an incursion. The opening is a **breach**, not a controlled threshold: the old maintenance oath supplies raw resonance, but no living Veyran has knowingly witnessed or accepted Adrian’s crossing. The System gives Adrian one short prompt: he may ask for a witness, but a witness is not a servant.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian uses a dropped iron tool and a length of line to reach a maintenance ledge, then stops a young dock worker from falling into the unstable join. He chooses immediate physical help over a desperate attempt to reopen his arrival point.
- **Emotional change:** Panic narrows into observation and action. He is still terrified, but he begins to notice that people respond to choices rather than to the status display.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian sees a faint join-line around the lock for the first time. He cannot use it, and a headache remains after the vision.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The arrival is bodily. Adrian has no health bar, regeneration, or automatic return. The initial opening is unstable rather than a reliable door.
- **Ending type:** Survival discovery
- **Next-chapter pull:** Tamsin Quill steps between Adrian and the armed workers and asks him to explain what the mark on his palm is bound to.

### Chapter 0003 — Names Are Prices

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt maintenance yard and the old lock chapel; midmorning
- **Immediate goal:** Pass an interrogation without giving the locals a reason to destroy him or close the only route he can see.
- **Resistance:** Tamsin distrusts him; the Hearthguard wants a confession; Adrian instinctively calls the local rite a “tutorial” and the local witnesses react as if he has insulted a living person.
- **Information revealed:** Veyran practice calls the mark a bond. A working requires a pattern, a witness, and a cost. Tamsin’s people are not servants; they are responsible for what the lock does to the river and to the families downstream.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian stops trying to optimize the conversation. He returns a copper token he took, asks Tamsin to correct his pronunciation, and offers to work under a public obligation rather than demand a reward.
- **Emotional change:** He feels genuine shame after seeing the dock workers’ fear. His first useful act is an apology with a practical next step, not a clever explanation.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian receives a dry coat and access to a maintenance area through Tamsin’s personal risk. He receives no class or permission.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Tamsin’s skepticism is rational. She knows the local consequences of an open threshold and has no obligation to trust Adrian because he appears helpless.
- **Ending type:** Relationship friction / earned hook
- **Next-chapter pull:** The lock chapel floor opens a hairline seam, and the water begins flowing uphill toward it.

### Chapter 0004 — A Door in the Lock

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt maintenance tunnels; late morning as the flood rises
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the lock from tearing open and give the dock workers a workable repair sequence.
- **Resistance:** Adrian has no local tools or knowledge of water craft. The Hearthguard will not follow an outsider’s directions, and the seam responds to spoken promises as well as physical force.
- **Information revealed:** The first clear License rule appears: a working needs a willing witness and a cost. The lock is a resonant site because several communities repeat the same public maintenance oath there.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian identifies three join-points from the pattern of water and pressure, then asks Tamsin to recruit the dock workers who actually maintain the lock. He accepts that the work may leave a visible residue.
- **Emotional change:** He stops treating himself as an outside observer and accepts responsibility for the next instruction, even when the first instruction is likely to fail.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian earns the first **Witness** mark. He can see one join-line and understand a direct promise, but cannot create a working by himself. Headache and nosebleed show the immediate cost.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The Witness mark is a responsibility to a place, not a passive stat. It can be refused by a local witness and can leave a scar if the working fails.
- **Ending type:** First power discovery
- **Next-chapter pull:** The repair sequence opens a second slit in the lock, and six people from Earth tumble through before Adrian can close it.

### Chapter 0005 — Seven Names, One Door

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake; midday during the failed expansion
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the newly arrived people from being crushed by the crowd and find out whether any of them can return to Earth.
- **Resistance:** Mina is injured, Dante is trying to restrain frightened strangers, locals believe the opening is a divine judgment, and each person has a different assumption about what happened.
- **Information revealed:** The first cohort is physically present: Mina Okoye, Dante Kuo, Priya Shah, Jonah Pike, Noor Haddad, and Owen Park. The System does not offer a party screen, a save point, or a way to undo the crossing.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian uses Tamsin’s limited translation and Mina’s triage system to separate the injured from the curious. He tells the newcomers the truth he has so far and asks them to make decisions without promising that he can save them.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian’s abstract responsibility becomes six specific people with names, injuries, money, and families. He feels the first fear that his earlier choice has trapped them because of him.
- **Power or resource change:** No new power. The group gains temporary shelter and a public inventory, but no stable return route; the present breach remains unstable, and Adrian’s existing Witness mark worsens the headache when he tries to use it alone.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The first cohort is not a selected army and has no automatic classes, equipment, or common language. Their Earth skills matter in different ways.
- **Ending type:** Escalation / responsibility turn
- **Next-chapter pull:** Owen reaches toward a relic-shaped light that only he can see, and the lock chapel’s old inscription begins to glow.

### Chapter 0006 — No Respawn

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Dry-goods shelter above the river market; afternoon
- **Immediate goal:** Create a workable living arrangement before the next tide and prevent the newcomers from spending money or taking local property without permission.
- **Resistance:** Jonah wants to buy exclusive shelter and information. One of the newcomers treats the event as a game challenge. A local worker is injured by an improvised tool, and the cohort has no common language or legal standing.
- **Information revealed:** Earthside objects are useful but not magic; Veyran work is bound to local relationships. A person can be lost, injured, or unable to return. The System shows no health bar and no automatic rescue.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian establishes a public inventory and a three-rule agreement: do not touch a local working, ask before taking a name, and tell Mina about every injury. He surrenders his remaining cash and phone access to the shelter ledger.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian enjoys being needed for a moment, then recognizes the pleasure of control and deliberately gives part of the authority to Mina, Tamsin, and the cohort.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian can read the direction of a nearby route for a short time, but cannot open it. The cohort gains shelter and supplies; no one gains a class.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The first local casualties and injuries are caused by misunderstanding and improvised work, not by a monster proving the world is a game.
- **Ending type:** Social pressure / small victory
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Lantern Concord announces that the next tide will widen the breach unless Adrian can prove the lock can be worked without a single person controlling it.

### Chapter 0007 — The First Quest Is a Trap

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Old lock chapel; evening during a preliminary chapel audience before the formal Concord hearing
- **Immediate goal:** Find a repair route that will keep Saltwake from flooding without accepting a workway from a man who has not explained its cost.
- **Resistance:** Ivenn Marrow offers a readable fragment of a System permission and hints at a Wayfinder workway. The fragment points toward a displaced Anchorstone but omits what happens to the old river boundary. Tamsin sees the omission immediately.
- **Information revealed:** The System can offer a workway as a class-like template. Ivenn knows the interface’s vocabulary and claims to have studied the earlier Bright Pilgrims, but he presents only a partial prompt, not yet a complete quest.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian asks Ivenn to state the cost in front of local witnesses. Ivenn refuses to turn the fragment into a full offer. Adrian declines the template, accepts only a short route-reading test, and lets Tamsin choose whether the local team participates.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian’s professional curiosity becomes distrust. He is embarrassed that he wanted the upgrade even after seeing the old boundary’s damage.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian confirms that a template is not a complete skill. He can now see a route if a Veyran witness accepts the question, but the work leaves a temporary **black line** on the chapel stone rather than a full black scar.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Ivenn is knowledgeable, helpful, and not openly hostile. His danger comes from selective truth and from having authority over hidden permissions.
- **Ending type:** Revelation / ethical turn
- **Next-chapter pull:** A voice answers through the black line from the wrong side of the lock, the lantern at the chapel window goes out, and the Lantern Concord summons Adrian to a formal hearing.

### Chapter 0008 — The Price of Translation

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock chapel and river tunnels; night after the preliminary audience
- **Immediate goal:** Explain the black line to the local workers and keep Ivenn’s incomplete prompt from becoming a command before the formal hearing.
- **Resistance:** The black line is too thin to carry a body and may be an echo, a trapped message, or a new tear. Tamsin wants the chapel sealed; Owen wants to force it open; Mina needs a safe way to examine the people who handled the lock; the dock workers will not abandon a public route without a public reason.
- **Information revealed:** A threshold can carry a voice farther than it can safely carry a body. The System can show a path but not a person’s intent. Earlier Bright Pilgrims appear in the chapel inscription as a disputed historical fact, not a confirmed divine mission. The line is a precursor to a full scar, not yet a usable return route.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian compares the prompt’s route with the physical pressure in the lock, asks a local mason to name the missing witness, and uses his Witness mark only to identify the safest line. He refuses to answer the voice with a private promise even when it seems to know Adrian’s name.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian accepts that understanding a person is not the same as controlling the route to them. He feels the loss of a private answer but chooses the local workers’ safety and public record.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian gains a short-lived route sense and a physical black mark on his palm. The line cannot be used twice without a new local agreement, and no return mark has been created for the cohort.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Local “echoes” are residues of choices, not automatically ghosts with complete memories. The story must preserve ambiguity until evidence supports a conclusion.
- **Ending type:** Mystery / moral cost
- **Next-chapter pull:** The voice repeats a phrase from an older maintenance oath, proving that someone is still using the lock as a witness, and the lower field begins to lose pressure.

### Chapter 0009 — Seal or Spend

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt outer channel; pre-dawn during the second tide surge
- **Immediate goal:** Stop the smaller outer tear from widening while deciding whether the damaged lock should be sealed or held as a possible route.
- **Resistance:** Tamsin and the Hearthguard want the tear sealed; the cohort wants an immediate way home; Ivenn argues that keeping the line open is the only humane choice; the lower fields are already losing water pressure.
- **Information revealed:** A local quorum can close the smaller tear or preserve it as a narrow route, but each choice will cost the lower fields a season of stability. The choice is between real harms, not between a safe option and an exciting one.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian identifies the failing join and holds it visible while Tamsin, dock workers, and Hearthguard witnesses perform the collective closure. He accepts a public debt for the damaged fields and leaves the **main breach** for later work rather than pretending the crisis is solved.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian feels relief and grief at the same time. He has made a real decision instead of designing a hypothetical one, and he cannot pretend it was free.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian remains a **Witness**. The smaller tear is closed by a quorum, not by his personal power. The damaged lower field is a lasting local cost, and the narrow route can carry one named living person at a time only after a return mark is separately established.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The System records a choice, not a reward. Adrian’s public debt is enforceable by the Lantern Concord, and Tamsin can challenge how he spends the Witness mark.
- **Ending type:** Costly decision
- **Next-chapter pull:** The collective working leaves a thin maintenance seam, and the formal Concord hearing is set for the next morning with Ivenn’s altered permission still active.

### Chapter 0010 — The Narrow Mark

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake; morning after the second tide
- **Immediate goal:** Make the temporary arrangement legible to everyone affected before the formal hearing, without pretending the main breach is permanently solved.
- **Resistance:** The cohort is frightened and divided; Saltwake needs the lock repaired; Tamsin distrusts Adrian’s temptation to become the permanent gatekeeper; Ivenn offers a reward-like prompt that would reopen the black line without showing its cost; Adrian is exhausted and exposed.
- **Information revealed:** A quorum route is not an Adrian-only passage. It can carry one named person at a time after a return mark is established, requires a new inspection before another use, and can be suspended by the receiving community. No one receives a class until they understand the work and accept the obligation. The first cohort is a set of people, not a party roster.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian refuses the unearned reward, helps Tamsin create a public ledger, and records the damaged field as a cost rather than hiding it. He accepts a fixed review date and loses the hope of controlling who comes next.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian becomes a witness in the literal sense: he accepts that the people affected by the working must be able to judge and revoke it. He does not earn trust; he earns a hearing.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian remains a Witness with no class template and no personal Passage permission. The group gains shelter, medical care, a public inventory, and access to a narrow quorum route only. The lower fields are damaged and the main breach remains under watch.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Owen is still alive at the end of this batch; his later death in the volume must be permanent and must not be retconned as a respawn. Tamsin and Adrian are cautious allies, not lovers yet.
- **Ending type:** Aftermath / earned forward hook
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Lantern Concord schedules a formal hearing on whether the first license can continue, while Ivenn privately tells Adrian that the narrow mark is only the first one to remember his name.

## Batch continuity handoff

The next batch should begin with the Lantern Concord hearing, not with a larger portal opening. The immediate questions are political and practical: compensation for the lower fields, legal status for the six-person first cohort, limits on the narrow quorum route, and whether Adrian can be trusted to translate a Veyran law. Ivenn remains publicly helpful. Mina, Dante, Priya, Noor, Jonah, and Owen are still alive. Tamsin’s affection is not established; her professional respect is the only relationship milestone reached so far.
