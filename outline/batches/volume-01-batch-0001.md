# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline: The First Mark

## Batch identity

- **Chapter range:** 1–10
- **Batch goal:** Establish the real crossing, give Adrian a bounded first working, form the first mixed local/Earthside group, stabilize the smaller outer tear, and convert its seam into a narrow, empty quorum route while the main breach is held under watch for the Lantern Concord’s hearing. The batch must not pretend that anyone is a disposable game character.
- **Starting condition:** Adrian is alone on Earth, finishing an onboarding build for a game project that may be canceled. He has no Veyran relationship, no stable magical ability, and no reason to believe the hidden prompt on his screen is real. The old maintenance oath at the Lock of Salt is still being repeated by several jurisdictions, and nothing has failed there yet.
- **Starting relationships:** Adrian and his sister Nora are close but strained by missed calls and his habit of treating personal obligations as maintenance tasks. He has never met Tamsin Quill or any Veyran. Mina, Dante, Priya, Jonah, Noor, and Owen live in five other cities, know neither Adrian nor one another, and are strangers connected to the breach only because it grabs whoever is nearest the Earth-side opening.
- **Starting power level:** **Stage 0, Unlicensed body.** Adrian can see an occasional pale prompt and perceive harsh light in a join, but he has no workway, return mark, controlled passage, translation, or combat ability. He becomes a **fragment-bearer** the moment he accepts the Chapter 1 permission and remains one until a local quorum gives him standing in Chapter 10. No one on the Veyran side has recognized him at the start.
- **Batch midpoint:** **Chapters 4–5.** Adrian’s attempt to repair the Lock of Salt makes the breach wider at an unwatched joint and pulls six additional Earthside people through. This is the **batch** midpoint: the door grabs people without anyone’s permission. The genre promise changes from one man discovering a fantasy world to a group managing a real emergency. It is not the volume midpoint; see `outline/volume-01.md` for the Chapter 25 reversal, which fails at a different and worse place.
- **Batch climax:** **Chapters 9–10.** Adrian joins a collective working that closes the smaller outer tear, accepts a public debt, fails in front of witnesses to finish it alone, and only then earns the formal Witness mark. He then helps bind the leftover maintenance seam into a narrow quorum route whose holder, witnesses, receiving community, and cost are all named on the page. He refuses a reward-like prompt that would reopen the tear without consent. He remains a Witness, not a Passage holder.
- **Aftermath:** The group has shelter, a public ledger, and one limited route that has no return mark in it yet. It does not have land rights, class privileges, a guaranteed way home, a solution to the main breach, or a resolved political relationship with Saltwake. The Lantern Concord’s decision is still ahead, so the Chapter 10 route is provisional in scope rather than a settlement.

## Required changes

- **Required character changes:** Adrian moves from amused observer to responsible witness. Tamsin moves from wary captor to reluctant professional ally and, by Chapter 10, from information-holder to a voter who can refuse. Mina becomes the group’s medical conscience and delivers the batch’s first consent warning: a route shown is not permission to use it. Dante becomes the physical coordinator. Priya begins a public record of the crossing. Noor notices who has been left out of every distribution and starts the ledger habit that the trial will inherit. Jonah argues for exclusive shelter and information because he believes a settlement needs a gatekeeper, which plants the colonial mirror early. Owen starts treating locals as obstacles and clings to a private light he believes is a relic. Ivenn establishes himself as a knowledgeable but dangerous adviser whose partial prompts are already editing the interface.
- **Required power changes:** Adrian learns the distinction between a working and a reward; perceives harsh light in a damaged join at Stage 0 in Chapter 2; gains a provisional join-reading in Chapter 4 that no local witness granted him; earns the formal Witness mark in Chapter 9 through a public duty plus a public failure; and discovers in Chapter 10 that a collective quorum route can be maintained without granting him personal Passage. He does not receive a class template or unrestricted translation.
- **Required world reveals:** Veyra is real; the lock is a resonant site; the System uses a permission grammar that can be incomplete and edited; the first crossing is physical and non-respawning; earlier Bright Pilgrims are part of local history; Ivenn knows more about the System than he explains; and a damaged join is infrastructure, not a monster.
- **Open questions after the batch:** Who inserted the License prompt? Why does the System know Adrian’s game vocabulary? What did the earlier crossing change? Why does the old lock answer with a voice from the wrong side? What will the Lantern Concord demand in the first formal hearing? Why does Owen alone see the relic-shaped light, and is it a relic at all?

## Batch pacing

- **Chapters 1–3:** Immediate action, displacement, and relationship friction
- **Chapters 4–6:** First working, first cohort, practical survival
- **Chapters 7–8:** Mystery, manipulation, and the first system consequence
- **Chapter 9:** Irreversible decision and cost
- **Chapter 10:** Public aftermath, limited victory, and the next political pressure

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — The First Rule

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Morrowglass Interactive, Earth; a little after 2 a.m., near the end of a deadline for *A Crown of Ash* onboarding
- **Immediate goal:** Finish the onboarding build and get through the morning meeting without being blamed for the project’s cancellation.
- **Resistance:** Adrian is exhausted, underpaid, and cynical about the company’s latest schedule change. A hidden permission screen appears inside a build that has no publisher header, no ticket, and no file on the machine. It asks him to put a real name into something he cannot audit.
- **Information revealed:** A pale, unverified permission **fragment** offers a “limited Earthside test.” It explains only three things: name yourself, the body follows, and there is no guarantee of a way back. It has no publisher, no reward, and no client. Adrian treats it as a prank or a leaked prototype until the wording repeats a private joke from his own design notes, which no one else has read.
- **Action or decision:** He enters his legal name and accepts the fragment instead of deleting the file. The screen does not show a success message. The room’s lights fail, and the sound of running water reaches him from nowhere. His acceptance is a knowing act of responsibility, but it cannot make the crossing legal; he is now a **fragment-bearer**, not a holder.
- **Emotional change:** Professional defensiveness becomes alarm. The ordinary safety of an auditable system fails him on the one night he was alone with it.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian remains Stage 0, but a small pale mark appears on his palm and his phone loses signal. No abilities, items, or rewards are granted.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** *A Crown of Ash* is a fictional Earth game; Nora Vale is Adrian’s emergency contact; Adrian has no prior fantasy experience. The Chapter 1 panel is the only System message in the chapter, and the fragment is unanchored with no resonant site, no Veyran witness, and no receiving community.
- **Ending type:** Discovery / earned hook
- **Next-chapter pull:** Adrian wakes beside a public lock he has never seen, and the mark on his palm is still warm.

### Chapter 0002 — The Wrong Kind of Loading

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake, Veyra; dawn during a rising flood tide
- **Immediate goal:** Find a dry route back to the place he came from and stop the water from pulling him under.
- **Resistance:** Adrian is bruised, cold, and cut off from the opening. Local dock workers approach with tools and weapons because they believe he is a skyborn spy. He cannot explain the crossing, and he cannot understand the local warning calls.
- **Information revealed:** Veyra is a living world with a functioning river economy. Locals see the arrival as a divine omen and an incursion. The opening is a **breach**, not a controlled threshold: the old maintenance oath supplies raw resonance, but no living Veyran has knowingly witnessed or accepted Adrian’s crossing. The System gives Adrian one short prompt: he may ask for a witness, but a witness is not a servant.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian uses a dropped iron tool and a length of line to reach a maintenance ledge, then stops a young dock worker from falling into the unstable join. He chooses immediate physical help over a desperate attempt to reopen his arrival point.
- **Emotional change:** Panic narrows into observation and action. He is still terrified, but he begins to notice that people respond to choices rather than to the status display.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian perceives harsh light along a join near the lock, but it gives him no direction, no language, and no usable action. He still cannot follow or work the join. A headache and a nosebleed follow, which is all Stage 0 permits.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The arrival is bodily. Adrian has no health bar, regeneration, or automatic return. The initial opening is unstable rather than a reliable door. The dock workers are present because they are repairing the lock during the flood; nobody is holding the arriving door.
- **Ending type:** Discovery
- **Next-chapter pull:** Tamsin Quill steps between Adrian and the armed workers and asks him to explain what the mark on his palm is bound to.

### Chapter 0003 — Names Are Prices

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt maintenance yard and the old lock chapel; midmorning
- **Immediate goal:** Pass an interrogation without giving the locals a reason to destroy him or close the only route he can see.
- **Resistance:** Tamsin distrusts him; the Hearthguard wants a confession; Adrian instinctively calls the local rite a “tutorial” and the local witnesses react as if he has insulted a living person.
- **Information revealed:** Veyran practice calls the mark a **bond**. A working requires a pattern, a witness, and a cost, and the residue belongs to the place. Tamsin’s people are not servants; they are responsible for what the lock does to the river and to the families downstream.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian stops trying to optimize the conversation. He returns a copper token he took, asks Tamsin to correct his pronunciation, and offers to work under a public obligation rather than demand a reward. Tamsin sets the first three conditions: he will not touch a working, he will not use a name, and he will ask before he takes a thing.
- **Emotional change:** He feels genuine shame after seeing the dock workers’ fear. His first useful act is an apology with a practical next step, not a clever explanation.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian receives a dry coat and access to a maintenance area through Tamsin’s personal risk. He receives no class or permission.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Tamsin’s skepticism is rational. She knows the local consequences of an open threshold and has no obligation to trust Adrian because he appears helpless. The “tutorial” line should embarrass him, not charm anyone.
- **Ending type:** Cost
- **Next-chapter pull:** The lock chapel floor opens a hairline seam, and the water begins flowing uphill toward it.

### Chapter 0004 — A Door in the Lock

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt maintenance tunnels; late morning as the flood rises
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the lock from tearing open and give the dock workers a workable repair sequence.
- **Resistance:** Adrian has no local tools or knowledge of water craft. The Hearthguard will not follow an outsider’s directions, and the seam responds to spoken promises as well as physical force. Some dock workers are holding a join at the near gate, which is not the same as holding the arriving door.
- **Information revealed:** The first clear License rule appears: a working needs a willing witness and a cost, and a promise that nobody accepts is not a working. The lock is a resonant site because several communities repeat the same public maintenance oath there.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian identifies three join-points from the pattern of water and pressure, then asks Tamsin to recruit the rest of the dock workers who actually maintain the lock. He accepts that the work may leave a visible residue. He sequences the repair so the workers can hold the near gate, and he does not notice the unwatched joint upstream until it is too late.
- **Emotional change:** He stops treating himself as an outside observer and accepts responsibility for the next instruction, even when the first instruction is likely to fail.
- **Power or resource change:** While some dock workers hold their working, Adrian gains a **provisional join-reading**: he can follow one join-line and say what he sees. It is not a rank, carries no duty, and was not granted by any local witness. The cost is a nosebleed and a headache that does not clear. The formal Witness mark is not earned here and must not appear before Chapter 9. The local repair leaves a residue the dock workers will have to repair again.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The Witness mark is a responsibility to a place, not a passive stat. It can be refused by a local witness and can leave a scar if the working fails. Its prerequisites are a public duty plus a public failure to solve the problem alone, and Chapter 9 is where both exist. A working in progress is not a controlled threshold.
- **Ending type:** Discovery
- **Next-chapter pull:** The repair sequence opens a second slit at the unwatched joint, and six people from Earth tumble through before Adrian can close it.

### Chapter 0005 — Seven Names, One Door

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake; midday during the failed expansion
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the newly arrived people from being crushed by the crowd and find out whether any of them can return to Earth.
- **Resistance:** Mina is injured, Dante is trying to restrain frightened strangers, locals believe the opening is a divine judgment, and each person has a different assumption about what happened.
- **Information revealed:** The first cohort is physically present: Mina Okoye, Dante Kuo, Priya Shah, Jonah Pike, Noor Haddad, and Owen Park. The System does not offer a party screen, a save point, or a way to undo the crossing. Priya starts the group’s first public record in a note file, then checks a livestream app with no signal and keeps narrating to the empty room anyway.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian uses Tamsin’s limited translation and Mina’s triage system to separate the injured from the curious. He tells the newcomers the truth he has so far and asks them to make decisions without promising that he can save them. He also asks each person for a name and one thing they are afraid of, and writes both down, because a name is the only thing he has that can be recorded locally.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian’s abstract responsibility becomes six specific people with names, injuries, money, and families. He feels the first fear that his earlier choice has trapped them because of him.
- **Power or resource change:** No new power. The group gains temporary shelter and a written list of names and fears; the public inventory arrives in Chapter 6. There is still no stable return route. Adrian’s provisional join-reading worsens the headache when he tries to use it without somebody else holding a working.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The first cohort is not a selected army and has no automatic classes, equipment, or common language. Their Earth skills matter in different ways. Owen is a security contractor who treats the crossing as a problem to be secured; his private relic-shaped light is the only thing he sees that the others do not.
- **Ending type:** Reversal
- **Next-chapter pull:** Owen reaches toward the relic-shaped light that only he can see, and the lock chapel’s old inscription begins to glow.

### Chapter 0006 — No Respawn

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Dry-goods shelter above the river market; afternoon
- **Immediate goal:** Create a workable living arrangement before the next tide and prevent the newcomers from spending money or taking local property without permission.
- **Resistance:** Jonah wants to buy exclusive shelter and information. Owen insists the relic-shaped light is real and that he can retrieve it before it disappears, and the group treats him as a crank until he nearly leaves the shelter alone. A local worker is injured by an improvised tool, and the cohort has no common language or legal standing.
- **Information revealed:** Earthside objects are useful but not magic; Veyran work is bound to local relationships. A person can be lost, injured, or unable to return. The System shows no health bar and no automatic rescue.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian establishes a public inventory and a three-rule agreement: do not touch a local working, ask before taking a name, and tell Mina about every injury. Noor builds the first public ledger from Adrian’s list, and he surrenders his remaining cash and phone access to it. Adrian gives Mina authority over the medical list and Tamsin authority over the inventory. He makes the relic retrieval a question the group can refuse, which gives Owen a reason to stay that is not an order Adrian can enforce.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian enjoys being needed for a moment, then recognizes the pleasure of control and deliberately gives part of the authority to Mina, Tamsin, and the cohort.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian can follow the direction of a nearby route only for as long as Tamsin’s crew is holding the lock’s working, and he cannot open it. The cohort gains shelter and supplies; no one gains a class.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The first local casualties and injuries are caused by misunderstanding and improvised work, not by a monster proving the world is a game. Jonah’s instinct toward a locked door and a ledger is the seed of the settlement dispute in Volume 01.
- **Ending type:** Decision
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Lantern Concord announces that the next tide will widen the breach unless Adrian can prove the lock can be worked without a single person controlling it.

### Chapter 0007 — The First Quest Is a Trap

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Old lock chapel; evening during a preliminary chapel audience before the formal Concord hearing
- **Immediate goal:** Find a repair route that will keep Saltwake from flooding without accepting a workway from a man who has not explained its cost.
- **Resistance:** Ivenn Marrow uses the interface’s exact vocabulary, including a phrase Adrian recognizes from his own unpublished design notes, then hints at a Wayfinder workway. The fragment points toward a displaced Anchorstone but omits what happens to the old river boundary. Mina is the first person to say that a route shown is not permission to use it. Tamsin sees the omitted local cost immediately and names it aloud for the record.
- **Information revealed:** The System can offer a workway as a class-like template. Ivenn knows the interface’s vocabulary and claims to have studied the earlier Bright Pilgrims, but he presents only a partial prompt, not yet a complete quest. The earlier Bright Pilgrims were a real but incomplete crossing that later generations made into myth.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian asks Ivenn to state the cost in front of local witnesses. Ivenn refuses to turn the fragment into a full offer. Adrian declines the template, accepts only a short route-reading test, and lets Tamsin choose whether the local team participates. He accepts the black line left by the test as part of the record instead of hiding it.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian’s professional curiosity becomes distrust. He is embarrassed that he wanted the upgrade even after seeing the old boundary’s damage.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian confirms that a template is not a complete skill. For as long as a Veyran witness accepts the question, he can follow one route while the local team holds its working, and the work leaves a temporary **black line** on the chapel stone rather than a full black scar. The black line is the first of its kind in the story and is a place, not a person.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Ivenn is knowledgeable, helpful, and not openly hostile. His danger comes from selective truth and from having authority over hidden permissions. The chapel stone’s black line must remain visible and unhealed for the rest of the volume.
- **Ending type:** Discovery
- **Next-chapter pull:** A voice answers through the black line from the wrong side of the lock, the lantern at the chapel window goes out, and the Lantern Concord summons Adrian to a formal hearing.

### Chapter 0008 — The Price of Translation

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock chapel and river tunnels; night after the preliminary audience
- **Immediate goal:** Explain the black line to the local workers and keep Ivenn’s incomplete prompt from becoming a command before the formal hearing.
- **Resistance:** The black line is too thin to carry a body and may be an echo, a trapped message, or a new tear. Tamsin wants the chapel sealed; Owen wants to force it open because the light behind it matches the relic-shaped light he saw at the arrival; Mina needs a safe way to examine the people who handled the lock; the dock workers will not abandon a public route without a public reason.
- **Information revealed:** The **breach** carries a voice farther than it can safely carry a body. The System can show a path but not a person’s intent. Earlier Bright Pilgrims appear in the chapel inscription as a disputed historical fact, not a confirmed divine mission. The line is a precursor to a full scar, not yet a usable return route, and the bright edge of the overloaded join is a physical feature of damaged infrastructure, not a relic.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian compares the prompt’s route with the physical pressure in the lock, asks a local mason to name the missing witness, and uses only his **provisional** join-reading, while the mason’s crew holds the working, to identify the safest line. He refuses to answer the voice with a private promise even when it seems to know Adrian’s name, and he tells Owen plainly that the line is not a door and that a relic cannot be retrieved by reaching for it.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian accepts that understanding a person is not the same as controlling the route to them. He feels the loss of a private answer but chooses the local workers’ safety and public record.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian gains a short-lived route sense and a thin **black line** on his palm along the Chapter 1 mark, with a headache that does not clear. The line is a precursor to a black scar, not a black scar in a person; it never deepens during this batch. The chapel line cannot be used twice without a new local agreement, and no return mark has been created for the cohort.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Local “echoes” are residues of choices, not automatically ghosts with complete memories. The story must preserve ambiguity until evidence supports a conclusion. The line on Adrian’s palm is not a return mark and must not be described as one.
- **Ending type:** Cost
- **Next-chapter pull:** The voice repeats a phrase from an older maintenance oath, proving that someone is still using the lock as a witness, and the lower field begins to lose pressure.

### Chapter 0009 — Seal or Spend

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt outer channel; pre-dawn during the second tide surge
- **Immediate goal:** Stop the smaller outer tear from widening while deciding whether the damaged lock should be sealed or held as a possible route.
- **Resistance:** Tamsin and the Hearthguard want the tear sealed; the cohort wants an immediate way home; Ivenn argues that keeping the line open is the only humane choice; the lower fields are already losing water pressure.
- **Information revealed:** A local quorum can close the smaller tear, or leave a seam that might be made into a narrow route after a separate public agreement. Either choice will cost the lower fields a season of stability. The choice is between real harms, not between a safe option and an exciting one.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian identifies the failing join and holds it visible while Tamsin, dock workers, and Hearthguard witnesses perform the collective closure. He accepts a public debt for the damaged fields, and in the failure that follows he tries to finish the working alone and cannot. He leaves the **main breach** for later work rather than pretending the crisis is solved. The closure leaves a thin **maintenance seam** in the stone, shaped around the fragment he has carried since Chapter 1, which is a physical anchor and not yet a route.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian feels relief and grief at the same time. He has made a real decision instead of designing a hypothetical one, and he cannot pretend it was free.
- **Power or resource change:** This is where the formal **Witness** mark is earned, because both of its prerequisites land here: Adrian accepts a public duty he cannot clear by himself, and the working fails in front of witnesses as a thing he cannot finish alone. He can now see a join without somebody else holding the working, and he still cannot create one. The smaller tear is closed by a quorum, not by his personal power. The seam has no legal standing, and no return mark exists for anyone, so nobody can use it yet; the next chapter is what makes it a route.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The System records a choice, not a reward. Adrian’s public debt is enforceable by the Lantern Concord, and Tamsin can challenge how he witnesses. The damaged lower field is a lasting local cost.
- **Ending type:** Decision
- **Next-chapter pull:** The collective working leaves a thin maintenance seam, and the formal Concord hearing is set for the next morning with Ivenn’s altered permission still active.

### Chapter 0010 — The Narrow Mark

- **POV:** Adrian Vale
- **Location/time:** Lock of Salt, Saltwake; morning after the second tide, as the Lantern Concord convenes the hearing
- **Immediate goal:** Make the temporary arrangement legible to everyone affected before the hearing can decide it, without pretending the main breach is permanently solved.
- **Resistance:** The cohort is frightened and divided; Saltwake needs the lock repaired; Tamsin distrusts Adrian’s temptation to become the permanent gatekeeper; Ivenn offers a reward-like prompt that would reopen the chapel’s black line without showing its cost; Adrian is exhausted and exposed.
- **Information revealed:** A quorum route is not an Adrian-only passage. The second public working sets out all five parts of a controlled threshold on the page: the resonant site is the Chapter 9 maintenance seam, with the Chapter 1 fragment pressed into it and now installed; the named holder is Adrian, in the same way the named holder of a bell rope is the person who agreed to pull it; the willing witnesses are Tamsin, two dock workers, and a Hearthguard member who sign the inspection themselves; the receiving community is the flood-side villages, and one village refuses through its witness at the hearing, so the route is approved for a reduced receiving area with the refusal recorded by name; and the cost is a posted figure — opened salt, the damaged field’s season, and a shared labor shift. The route carries one named person per opening after that person has a return mark, requires a new inspection before another use, and can be suspended by the receiving community. No one receives a class until they understand the work and accept the obligation. The first cohort is a set of people, not a party roster.
- **Action or decision:** Adrian refuses the unearned reward, helps Tamsin and Noor open the public ledger, and records the damaged field as a cost rather than hiding it. He reads the reduced receiving area into the record, including the village that refused, and accepts the role of named holder as a duty rather than a privilege. He accepts a fixed review date and loses the hope of controlling who comes next. The working installs the route as a recognized License whose receiving area and review date are provisional; the route exists and is empty, because no return mark has been created for him or for anyone in the cohort, so the first thing the route will ever do is wait for the Concord’s decision about who is allowed to try it.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian becomes a witness in the literal sense: he accepts that the people affected by the working must be able to judge and revoke it. He does not earn trust; he earns a hearing.
- **Power or resource change:** Adrian remains a Witness with no class template and no personal Passage permission. The group gains shelter, medical care, a public inventory, and a narrow quorum route that can carry one named person per opening once a return mark is separately established. The lower fields are damaged and the main breach remains under watch.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Owen is still alive at the end of this batch; his later death in the volume must be permanent and must not be retconned as a respawn. Tamsin and Adrian are cautious allies, not lovers yet. The route is provisional, not a signed trial license; the trial license is the volume’s closing act in Chapters 48–50.
- **Ending type:** Earned hook
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Lantern Concord has opened the record on whether the first license can continue, while Ivenn privately tells Adrian that the narrow mark is only the first one to remember his name.

## Chapter distribution check

The ten cards are deliberately not the same shape. Chapters 1–4 are action, displacement, and a first working; Chapter 5 is discovery and reversal; Chapter 6 is practical social work; Chapters 7–8 are investigation and a consent cost; Chapter 9 is an irreversible decision with a residue; Chapter 10 is a political and relational aftermath. No chapter carries more than one System message. Chapters 1, 2, 7, and 8 each use a single prompt, and none of them offers a reward.

## Batch continuity handoff

The next batch should continue inside the Lantern Concord hearing already opened in Chapter 10, not with a larger portal opening. The immediate questions are political and practical: compensation for the lower fields, legal status for the six-person first cohort, limits on the narrow quorum route, who is allowed to be the first person to receive a return mark in it, and whether Adrian can be trusted to translate a Veyran law. The route exists and is empty at the end of this batch, so the first use of it is a hearing decision rather than a story beat Adrian gets to trigger. Ivenn remains publicly helpful. Mina, Dante, Priya, Noor, Jonah, and Owen are still alive. Owen has no scar of his own yet; the chapel stone’s black line is already there from Chapter 7, and the scarred outer threshold comes later. Tamsin’s affection is not established; her professional respect is the only relationship milestone reached so far. The main breach is closed only at the end of the volume, not in this batch.
