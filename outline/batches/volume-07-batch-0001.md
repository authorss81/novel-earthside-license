# Volume 07, Batch 0001 — Chapters 0301–0310, days 301 to 310

**Ten chapters, and here is why ten and not twenty.** A first batch of this volume has to do four things that cannot be split and cannot be deferred: it has to put the volume's central pressure on a page as **a boundary that has to be settled before the season turns and that nobody in this city can say where it is**, it has to **find on a page that the Short Month ends on day 315 and that no form in four hundred miles covers a day after it**, it has to get **a copy of an entry a hundred and forty years old onto a page in a clerk's hand, attached to a deed, with a town four hundred miles inland at the end of it**, and it has to **put the volume's word on a line in the ground in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person** — because a word put in Chapter 0342 and taken back has to have been put somewhere, in a room, by a mouth, on a page this batch owns, and no batch may improvise it. **Ten chapters, one chapter to one day, days 301 to 310, ending on a Wednesday**, and the hinge falls on the ninth and tenth of them, which is why this batch is these ten chapters and not any other ten.

**The mapping is one chapter to one day for all ten. There are no double days in this batch and there are no double days in this volume, and `outline/volume-07.md` §14.2 says in writing why a boundary volume does not get them here.** The volume's four movements are 12, 12, 12 and 14 and this batch is the first ten days of the first movement.

The hinge of the volume falls in the ninth of the ten and a first-tag count hides it, which is the reason this file prints the table above and not only the count.** Two of ten is procedural or political, and this file says so rather than pretend otherwise: a boundary, a deed, a copied entry and a slate are a procedural pressure, and in this volume that pressure is a room and a page and not a person, and calling it something else would be the worse failure. **What is different from the last volume is not the ratio. It is that three of these ten chapters have a person's hands on the work in the first paragraph, two of the ten carry a notice going out in a hand, and the tenth chapter is the volume's word.**

**And every one of these ten cards names a third party, a task, or a thing that has to be carried somewhere, and says which. That is the answer to a finding left open by the close of the last volume, and it is in the cards and not in the prose.** Across the fifty chapters of Volume 06 the two-hander broke in six of them and all six were in batches whose own records certified the ceiling as held, **so the certification is not the instrument and the card is.** A chapter that hands four consecutive exchanges to the same two people is the fifty-day two-hander again. **The counting device, the *about N seconds* refrain, the *a person who does not want to be asked* refrain and the *in about nine seconds* refrain are all declared motifs of this manuscript, none of them may stand twice in one chapter, and none of them may stand in a chapter whose finding is a refusal.**

## Chapter 0310 — A Word, In A Room, In Daylight, With A Date On The Door

- **Chapter:** 0310. **Day 310, Wednesday, the fifty-fifth day of the Short Month.**
- **POV:** the man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall and wrote the ninth line, **and he is on a page twenty-nine times across eight chapters of Volume 06 and is the fifth person in this manuscript's descriptor map and is not any of the three women of about thirty-four, and the card names the collision so that a writer does not walk into it.**

- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** A man of about thirty-four has been asked a question and the question is about a mark of a certain width and a certain depth, and the room wants the word for it, and **the word for it is on a piece of chalk that has been lying on a table since the forty-third day of the Short Month and that he put there himself and has not touched.**
- **Immediate goal:** **to answer the question, which he can do in one word, and to stop there.** The chapter is built on the distance between those two acts.
- **Resistance:** the room. **About nine people in it and six of them want the rest of the sentence, and the man of about thirty-four has refused five times to say whether the ninth line works and this is not one of those five and it may not be counted as a sixth, and the chapter does not have him refuse anybody.** And the resistance in him: he has a piece of chalk and a page and a word, and about four people in four hundred miles have worked out that a man who puts a word on a wall and then cannot say whether the wall holds is not a man who will say what a word is for.
- **Information revealed:** **THE WORD.** Said out loud, in the local language, in daylight, with a date in chalk on the door, by a person, in one word, and the chapter does not define it and does not improve on it and does not say what it means. **And what he says instead, in about four sentences, is that it is not a path and not a watercourse and not a furrow and not a boundary, and that he is not going to say what it is, and that the reason he is not going to is that he has a word on a page now and he has found out in the last fifty days what a word on a page is for and he is not going to find out twice.** **And the one clause that may not be crossed and that the chapter puts on the page exactly once and does not repeat: that the mark is not a pattern.**
- **Action or decision:** the register-keeper writes the word on her second slate in chalk, and **she asks him to say it again so that she can get it right and he does not, and she gets it right, and the chapter says she got it right and does not say how.** The word goes on the slate. **It is one line, and the date goes beside it, and no name goes on the line, because the man of about thirty-four said so and the reason he gave is that a line with a name at the top of it is a line somebody can come back to, and he is right and nobody improves on it.**
