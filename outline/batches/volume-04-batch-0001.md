# Volume 04, Batch 0001 — Chapters 0151–0170, days 155 to 174

**Twenty chapters, and here is why twenty.** A first batch of this volume has to put a settlement on the page as a fact, put a rival in a room, put a month on the page so that any later page can carry a date, name a place the manuscript has never named, name a water gate the manuscript has never named, show a workway offered with its cost read out, and end on a date in the future that a family has not been asked about. **Twenty chapters, one day each, days 155 to 174, ending on a Sunday**, does all seven and leaves the volume's midpoint — a household saying yes in a room on day 179 — for the batch after. Ten chapters would have had to choose between the calendar and the rival, and the calendar is the one that cannot be repaired later without rewriting every date in the volume.

**The mapping is one chapter to one day for all twenty.** There are no double days in this batch. The volume's four double days are 194, 196, 198 and 200 and all four are in the fourth movement.

**Rotation, honestly counted: five chapters of physical work or bodily work (0155, 0159, 0162, 0165, 0170), one of recovery (0161), three of relationship that are not about a form (0157, 0160, 0168), four of discovery (0153, 0154, 0156, 0158), and eight of political or procedural pressure.** The eight is more than a third and the file should say so rather than pretend otherwise: a rival who arrives with a wage list *is* a political pressure, and disguising it as anything else would be a worse failure than the count. What has changed from Volume 03 is not the ratio, it is the **texture** — five of the eight political chapters turn on a person saying a number out loud in a market or at a gate rather than on a form being read in a room, and the two chapters that are pure procedure (0152 and 0167) are the two where procedure is the *subject*.

## Batch goal

**Put a working settlement on the page as a fact nobody in this city can argue with, name the place and the gate it is going to take, and make the cost of Adrian's silence visible and dated before the thing it costs has happened.**

## Starting condition

Day 155, the hundred and fifty-fifth day of the flood, a Tuesday, the sixty-fifth and last day of the month after this one. A hearing was held yesterday. A man of thirty-two is outside every office in the world, holds nothing, and is the most findable person in this world and said so himself in front of about two hundred and forty people. There is one clock and it runs out on day 189, a Monday, thirty-five days from the hearing, and it is the only clock in the book running towards something. Thirty-nine men under the near gate have not been paid since the first of March. A village of nine households has refused a fourth time and its gate is shut. A man of forty-two has not been on a page since day 97.

## Batch climax

**Chapter 0170, day 174, a Sunday — the meeting-ground sets a date.**

The settlement has asked the Reed Commons for the days on the Redroot gate. The meeting-ground at Verrow has heard the ask on day 171 and has not answered it, and the reason it has not answered it is a hole in the Reach's own rule, and the hole is not filled. On day 174 the answer comes back and it is neither a refusal nor a grant. It is that **the household must be asked, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and the date is the twenty-fourth day of the Long Month. Twenty-four is day 179, which is a Friday, and it is five days away.** A family is going to be asked on a known day by people who have nothing to hide and no reason to hurry, and the batch ends on the date and not on the asking.

**And the meeting-ground does not choose the day, and this file does not put the twenty-fourth in its mouth.** It says the day must come back to it. **The day came back chosen by somebody who was not asked either: the keeper of the Redroot gate, and she says so out loud at a door on the Sunday morning in Chapter 0170, and it is the one thing in the batch that is not the meeting-ground's and the one that costs her.** Card 0167 is where the meeting-ground declines to set it and card 0170 is where the keeper sets it, and the two clauses have to be carried together; a chapter that has the meeting-ground name a day has broken both cards and improved on nothing.

## Required character changes

# The cards

## Chapter 0151 — The Nine Miles

- **Chapter:** 0151. **Day 155, Tuesday, the sixty-fifth and last day of the month after this one.**
- **POV:** Adrian Vale, thirty-two, in the room, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf has more of it than he does.
- **Location/time:** the quay and the step at the back of the room over a market, from about the second hour of the afternoon to about the ninth hour of the evening.
- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** A man of forty-two has come down nine miles in the rain and is standing on a step with a printed sheet in his hand, and he hands the sheet over before he says anything, which is the first thing anybody in this city has been handed by him.
- **Immediate goal:** to get what the settlement actually is onto a page in this city today, without an office and without a form and without Adrian reading a single word of it out loud.
- **Resistance:** There is nobody here with standing to receive terms. A man of thirty-two who holds nothing is the only person in the room who cannot be asked to sign, cannot be put on a roll, and cannot be refused. And Jonah will not give a description — he will give **a figure and a date**, which is the most dangerous shape there is, and a man who has spent a hundred and fifty-fifth days learning that a description is a removal with a column for where the person is has to decide in public whether a figure is better.
- **Information revealed:** The White Ledger has been on the upper road since the seventh week of the flood and is **about a hundred and forty people**, and it is a store, a queue, a wage list, a rota and a drying line, and it has **taken the ground at Redroot and has not asked.** The wage is a share and a ration and a dry bed and a place at the front of a queue that works, **and the settlement says out loud, unprompted, that the paper money is worth nothing here and always was, and that everybody in the store knows it.** That sentence, and not the wage, is what the room cannot get past.
- **Action or decision:** The figures go on a trestle in the hall for one day, entered by a girl of seventeen in a narrow column, **with the payer's name on them and no names of the paid on them**, which is the shape of a page this city has already used once and which the settlement's own people did not think of. Jonah notices and says so.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian is not relieved and does not know what he is. He has spent a hundred and fifty-fifth days being the only man in the room who can read two systems of writing, and a man of forty-two walks nine miles in the rain with a wage list and no interpreter at all.
- **Power or resource change:** None. Nothing is gained, nothing is signed, no seat exists, and the standing charge does not move.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **This is the first time Jonah Pike is on a page since Chapter 0097, which is day 97 — fifty-eight days.** He has not been on a page and he is not a discovery; he is a man who was always there. He is right about nine parts in ten and the tenth part is always a local woman's word for a social fact.
- **Ending type:** earned hook. **At the ninth hour, in front of about nine people, Jonah Pike answers the ninth sentence.** The ninth sentence was Adrian's, from day 59, and it was this: that Adrian has made a page of things the seventeen may not have, and that there is not one thing in it they may do, and that in March one of them is going to be a person with no work in a city with no money. **The answer is nine names of people in a store who have work and are paid this week.** Adrian does not answer it. He stands at the back of a room and does not answer it, and it is the first time in three volumes he has done nothing at all in a room.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The woman of twenty-four has to write a date tomorrow and there is no month to write it in, and the form ran out at midnight.

## Chapter 0152 — The Form Ran Out

- **Chapter:** 0152. **Day 156, Wednesday, the first day of the Long Month.**
- **POV:** Sera Vail, notary of fifty, twenty-six years a notary and the notary of about nine hundred things, and about nine hundred of them were somebody else's sentence.
- **Location/time:** a room over a market, then a room with a door, in daylight, from about the ninth hour of the morning to about the sixth hour of the evening.
- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** A woman of twenty-four puts a date at the top of a page and stops, and the date will not come, because the sixty-fifth day of the month after this one was yesterday and there is no sixty-sixth and nobody in this city has ever had to write one.
- **Immediate goal:** to write a date. That is all. A notary whose job is that a thing can be certified cannot certify a thing she cannot date.
- **Resistance:** A month is not a thing a person gets to name, and the person who would most obviously be asked — a woman of seventy of a village of nine households that has refused four times — refuses, and her refusal is correct, and it is entered, and it does not stop it.
- **Information revealed:** The form ran out. **The two month forms are both sixty-five days and the flood has already turned the calendar over twice, and nobody in four hundred miles has had to name a month since the first of it.** The name is **the Long Month** and it is named in this chapter on the page, in this room, in daylight, with a date on it, and it begins today, and the sixty-fifth day of it will be day 220.
- **Action or decision:** Before she says the name she says the cost, in about nine sentences, and it is hers: **that a name invented by a notary is a name an office can use, and that in about four months a clerk nine hundred miles away is going to write *the Long Month* at the top of a page and nobody will be able to tell whether it means the same month, and that she has been the only person in this city keeping a count of her own counting from the fortieth day of this flood and from nothing else, which she said out loud in a room on the eighty-seventh day of this flood, and is tired of it.** She names it anyway, because the alternative is a compact with no date on it, **and a compact with no date on it is a chair with no date on it, which is the only thing here allowed to be nothing.** The woman of seventy's refusal and the reason for it are both entered and both are hers.
- **Emotional change:** The notary is frightened and does not perform fear. She is fifty and she has never in twenty-six years had to name anything, and she has now put her own name on a month, which is the first time she has been the person at the top of a page in this city.
- **Power or resource change:** **A date form exists that did not exist yesterday.** That is the whole of it and it is not a power. It is also the precondition of the volume's resolution: **a compact between two named communities cannot be dated in a month that has no name, and that is the whole reason this volume ends in a room with a document in it.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The word *knock* does not appear. The box is not in this chapter. **The correct number of times a chapter may use a form no page has established is zero**, and this chapter is the one that establishes the fourth.
- **Ending type:** decision. **A girl of seventeen writes the date at the top of a page in the new form for the first time, and reads it back, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a stall says the sentence the volume is going to need in about nine words: that a thing with a month on it can be found again, and a thing without one cannot, and that this city has eleven pages with no month on them and has had them for a hundred and fifty-sixth days.**
- **Next-chapter pull:** A woman of about fifty who counts water has come up the Reach road and will not come to the room, and she has said that if anybody wants to know what is at the bottom of the flats they can come and stand at a gate.

## Chapter 0153 — Somebody Is Told

- **Chapter:** 0153. **Day 157, Thursday, the second day of the Long Month.**
- **POV:** Hessa Ruun, about fifty, the water-count of the Reed Reach, who is the closest thing the wetland has to a notary and nothing like one, and who counts water on the fingers of her left hand and has said a thing about a fourth line since day 80.
- **Location/time:** a gate at the bottom of a channel, in daylight, about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in about four people, and then the same gate at about the seventh hour with about nine.
- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** A man of thirty-two is standing at a gate in the wrong weather with a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, and the woman says the words *Redroot* out loud once, at the gate, before anybody has asked her anything, and then says she is not going to say it again today and does not know why she said it.
- **Immediate goal:** the water-count's goal, stated by her and not inferred: to say a name out loud, once, in daylight, to the safest person in four hundred miles to say it to, and to have it be a name and not a description.
- **Resistance:** Adrian asks for more than four people in the room. She says no, and the no is about nine seconds long, and it is not about him: **naming a place that is not on a list is how a place becomes findable, and a man with no mark and on no roster is the most findable person in this world, and that is not a safety and he has said so himself in front of two hundred and forty people, and she has read about it and does not believe him and is using it.** And the second resistance is in the finding: **Redroot is not on the Chapter 0010 list of the hamlets on the shared gates, and nobody in this city can say why, and the answer takes about four minutes and is that a list is a list, and that Redroot's gate is a shared gate of the Reach and has been for longer than the licence.**
- **Information revealed:** Redroot is a low place at the bottom end of a long channel where the red root grows in the cut bank, and it carries the Reach's salt racks, and it has households on it. It is a place the Reach knows. It is not on the list. It is not on any roll of this city. **Nobody has ever been told its name in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and that is the first time in this volume that a place has been said out loud by somebody who did not have to.**
- **Action or decision:** She says the name a second time, to four people, in daylight, with a date on it, and the date is written on a gate board in chalk and not on a page, **because a gate board is not a document and a document is a place people can go to.** Adrian is asked nothing and asks nothing and is told, and it is entered that he was not asked and that he noticed.
- **Emotional change:** Adrian is told something he cannot act on, by a person who is not doing him a kindness, and it is the first time in a hundred and fifty-seventh days that information has arrived in this city that he could not have got by walking nine miles.
- **Power or resource change:** None. He holds one more fact and no instrument. **The knowledge is not a page and goes nowhere and cannot be carried to an office, and that is entered by the person who told it.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **The Reed Commons is not introduced, founded, discovered or re-constituted.** Hessa Ruun speaks as a water-count and not as the Commons, and the Commons' refusal of Adrian's page of day 80 stands and is not re-opened.
- **Ending type:** discovery. **The name is on a gate board in chalk and a girl of seventeen did not write it and a man of thirty-two did not ask for it, and the last thing on the page is that a water-count who records who may use a water gate has just written a place on a board instead, on purpose, and has said why, and the reason is that a board is a thing and a page is a place a person can be found in.**
- **Next-chapter pull:** A water-count records who may use a water gate. The junction at Nine Elms has four channels and one of them has been shut since the twenty-second day of the month after this one, and nobody in this city has ever called it anything.

## Chapter 0154 — The Fourth Channel Gets a Name

- **Chapter:** 0154. **Day 158, Friday, the third day of the Long Month.**
- **POV:** Hessa Ruun again, and then the keeper of the Nine Elms gate, who is a person the chapter names on the page and whose second handle is that she answers with her hands before she answers with her mouth.
- **Location/time:** the junction at Nine Elms, at about the second hour of the afternoon and again at about the fifth hour, in daylight, out of the weather.
- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** A man of thirty-two and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf walk eleven miles on a Friday to stand in front of a channel that has been shut for forty-six days and look at it, and the woman of about thirty-four says, before anybody has said anything, that a shut channel is not a gate, and the water-count says it is, and does not say why, and walks out onto it.
- **Immediate goal:** to give the fourth channel a name, a keeper and a day, in daylight, at the thing, and to do it in the form the Reach keeps such things in — **which is a board and a chalk and a speaker's memory and not a page.**
- **Resistance:** Two, and the second is the chapter. **First: the Reach's own practice requires that a thing be recorded, and what a water-count records is who may use a water gate, and that record is a list of persons.** So the chapter has to hold a commons keeping a list of people for good reasons, on the page, without flinching and without resolving it. **Second: the keeper of the Nine Elms gate is not on that list, and the reason she is not on it is in the chapter and is not flattering to anybody, and it is that she was never asked, because being asked would have meant the gate was hers.**
- **Information revealed:** The fourth channel at the Nine Elms junction **is** one of the shared water gates of the Reed Reach, and it is the Nine Elms gate, and it has a name chosen on the page, and it has a keeper chosen on the page, and it was shut on day 112 by the fourth line of a term sheet that is in force, and the quarter is gone, and the undertaking that would have paid for it is void and no office has been told. **And what a household has of a gate is not the gate: it is the days on it, and a board, and a keeping of it, and a name in a speaker's record. The gate itself stays where it is.**
- **Action or decision:** The name and the day go on a board in chalk at the junction, and the keeper's name is **not** put on the list, at the keeper's own request and in her own words, and the refusal is entered. **Adrian does not ask the woman of forty-eight in a fourth house anything, does not go near the fourth house, and nobody stands outside it, and it is entered that about four of the nine wanted to.**
- **Emotional change:** Adrian came eleven miles to do something and does nothing, and the chapter is the second time in two days he has come a long way and left with a fact and an instrument that does not exist.
- **Power or resource change:** None. **The gate does not open and is not going to open in this volume and no chapter of Volume 04 opens the fourth channel at Nine Elms.** What changes is that a thing in four hundred miles has a name and a keeper and a day, and a keeper is not on a list.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The water not received is counted from the sixty-ninth day of this flood, which was the sixty-ninth, and a chapter that needs the figure takes it from that day and not from a neighbouring chapter. **The last page in Volume 03 that carried the count was Chapter 0149, at eighty-two and then eighty-three; the figure at day 158 is whatever the rule gives and is not a page figure until a page carries it.**
- **Ending type:** discovery, and a cost. **A shut channel has a name and a keeper and a day and nobody outside this junction can find any of them, and a woman who has been keeping a gate for nine years is not on the Reach's own list of gate-keepers because the Reach never asked her, and the woman who wrote that sentence is standing in front of it.**
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Redroot gate is a shared gate too, and the settlement has the ground and not the days, and there is one person in this city with no standing who can stand in a queue for two hours and come back and say what it is like.

## Chapter 0155 — Two Hours in a Queue

- **Chapter:** 0155. **Day 159, Saturday, the fourth day of the Long Month.**
- **POV:** the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf. **Physical work. Wet, cold, carrying, two hours on her feet and about nine sacks.**
- **Location/time:** the upper road, outside a store, from about the seventh hour of the morning to about the eleventh hour, in weather that is not fit for it, and then a market at Saltwake at about the second hour of the afternoon.
- **Beginning — the pressure, not the finding.** She goes up the nine miles with a barrow she does not need and comes down it with wet feet and a thing in her chest she cannot get out by arguing, and the chapter opens on the queue moving and does not say what she thought of it.
- **Immediate goal:** to stand in the settlement's queue for two hours as a person with nothing to lose and no standing, carrying other people's sacks, and to find out whether it works, **and her own goal, which she says out loud in about nine words at the top of the chapter: to be able to say one true thing about it in a market without it being a description of nine people.**
- **Resistance:** She is not a customer and not a worker and she will not be either, so she carries for a wage of one day and takes the wage, which is the first wage anybody has been paid out of that store, **and it is the first wage paid in this city in this whole run of days that has come out of anybody's own stock and not out of a stall.** And the resistance that matters is that the queue **is good.** It moves.
