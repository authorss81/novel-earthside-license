# Volume 02 Outline: Salt and Silver

## Volume identity

- **Chapter range:** 51–100
- **Central pressure:** The flood is over and the winter has started, and the trial licence is four days old and has been used twice. The seventeen have no work, no land rights, no class, and no route home, and the door that exists in this city opens into **a room with no window and no door anybody has found**. Winter makes every number in the book physical: the salt for the scar form, the iron for the gauge, the timber for the walk-round's half-rotten rail, the silver the Crown charges to look at a page, and the bottom field four miles away that a woman has to decide about in March. **Three parties say the word *welcome* and mean three different things**, and the volume's argument is that a welcome is a document with a price on it or it is a mood, and a mood cannot be checked in four months.
- **Starting state:** Everything `state/volume-01-close.md` records as true is true. The main breach is closed and dressed. The Narrow Mark is licensed and has carried two people in fifty days. The seat of holder of the pull is vacant. Adrian is Stage 1, Witness, Unbound, and is party to the licence and given nothing by it.
- **Starting relationships:** Adrian and Tamsin are working partners with a mutual, public, documented check. Adrian and Wenna Cray are four statements of error apart and counting. Adrian and Marrow are neither refused nor accepted, and Adrian cannot tell which. Jonah Pike has said *say that again in a month* and Adrian has said *I will not.* Dante Kuo has not had a scene in ten chapters and the rescue argument is owed.
- **Starting power level:** **Stage 1, Witness.** Perceives the nearest join-line and understands one direct Veyran promise at a time. No workway, no return mark of his own, no title, no seat, no land right, no class privilege, and barred from every roster of the instrument he committed to.
- **Ending power level:** **Stage 2, Passage holder.** Earned in the closing chapters by voiding Ivenn's false oath by taking a local debt onto himself, and paid for with the loss of an easy route home. He can open a single-holder threshold — one named living person per opening, one return mark carried, willing local witnesses required, and **the same route may not be opened twice in a row by him.** He remains barred from the trial licence's own rosters, holds no land rights and no title, and is still the man who reads the number off the wall four feet back. **He never reaches Stage 3 in this volume.**

## What "salty" is the actual name of

**Salt and silver are the two things this city cannot do without and does not own in the same currency.** Salt is Saltwake's trade and the price of it went up a third when the flood season turned. Silver is the Crown's metal and the fee for the Registry's schedule, and this city owes it and cannot pay it. Every scene in this volume is one of the two, and the exchange rate between them is a person.

## Major locations

- **The Lock of Salt, the lower gallery, and the wall of the second bay.** The number is eight and seven-eighths, the pale edge is dressed, one ruled line is left in the column, and the main breach has never said a word.
- **The walk-round:** two hundred steps down a river wall, a low parapet, and **a rail that is half-rotten** and is discovered to be half-rotten in Chapter 52. In winter it is iced. Every fresh inspection the licence requires is a walk down it.
- **The yard under the lock chapel:** the five-item form on a slate on a nail, a salt line, four chalk depths, four people twice a day, a readable slate, and the price of the salt.
- **The converted salt store:** where every instrument in this volume is signed, refused, and dated.
- **The loft above the market:** the group rota with a filled fourth column, and the work in it — the nineteen stones of the upper road.
- **The upper road above the salt works:** Sabra Vint's house with the burnt lintel, and the nineteen stones. **The name-work is now on a wall and anybody may pick it up.**
- **Corrow, four miles down the Reach road:** the bottom field, the year of certainty gone out of a hundred and forty acres, and the four villages whose gates cannot be promised.
- **The rope store above the chandlery, upper road:** where the second group of eleven are housed, dry, with a list of what is owed for it on the wall and a key and nothing else.
- **Aurel (by document only this volume):** the Registrar of Crossings and Tides, the quarterly sitting, the wooden case, and a clerk who has been counting fields in a book since the eighth day of the flood.

## The five new institutions this volume creates

These are Volume 02's gains. Every one of them is a page, and none of them is a power, and all of them are heavier.

1. **The receiving page.** A form that answers the question *who is on the other side, and who has agreed to have them.* It is the first instrument in the series that names the absent party. It is created because the licence cannot go home and the reason has to be written down somewhere that a person can be angry at.
2. **The honest useless form.** A schedule of crossings filled in truthfully and in the truth's own shape, so that the Crown's form is complete and carries nothing. It is a compliance instrument, and its first cost is that compliance writes down what eleven people want.
3. **The Wayhouse.** A Veyran roadside house kept under local law: a bed and a bowl for anybody travelling, paid for by a levy on the road's trade, kept by a household, **open to a list of nobody** and closable by its own household for its own reasons with a date on the page. Two of them are established by the volume's end and the first is nearly lost to a misunderstanding.
4. **The Open Hand.** A cooperative of Earthside and Veyran traders under local law: no share, no interest, and **a right of refusal that cannot be bought out.** It is established at the volume's resolution and it is the first institution in the series that Adrian does not control and is not needed by.
5. **The Void.** Not an institution and not a licence: a single sealed page that voids one instrument on the ground that the instrument's own condition was not met, witnessed by nine people of whom one is named by herself, and **it can be used once and only on a thing that claims to be the work of a person.** It is Adrian's Stage 2 and it is the most dangerous page in the book.

## Major factions in this volume

- **The Lantern Concord:** still the notary, the roll, the quarterly description in a wooden case, and the assessor of strangers. **It is the party that will be made the permanent owner of the Licence, and it does not want to be, and that is the volume's last and best fact.**
- **The Reed Commons / Nine Elms:** water not received, nine households refusing for the third time, and **a refusal a person cannot afford is not a refusal** — which is the Crown's fifth clause and this volume's sharpest weapon.
- **The Brass Measure and Ottavia Reek:** winter credit, materials, and the fourth term in a new coat. Not a villain. A factor with a quarter and a bad winter.
- **The Glass Crown:** four documents by the end of the volume, none of them the same as any other, and one Registry schedule with a fee in silver.
- **The Quiet Choir:** **the name Single Witness is used in prose for the first time in Chapter 91, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and not before.** Before that it is a shape, as in Volume 01.
- **Earthside, the first and second groups:** six and eleven, named, with no work, in a city that describes them four times a year.
- **Jonah Pike and the beginning of the White Ledger:** the volume's *volume* antagonist is not Ivenn. It is a man who is right in eight sentences out of nine, whose ninth sentence is always a local woman's word for a social fact, and whose error is that he thinks a structure is a kindness.

## Volume promise

The volume must answer a real question: **what is the door for, if it is not a way home, and what does a city owe the people who are standing in front of it?** It must establish winter, the Open Hand and the Wayhouse under local law, the honest useless form, and the Void; it must lose an easy route home; and it must end with the question the series has been carrying since Chapter 25 answered in a form nobody wanted: **why do Crown records describe earlier Bright Pilgrims when no living local can say where they went, and who has been carrying the names?**

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 51–60: What the winter is for (Batch 0001)

The first ten days after the licence. The door is found not to be a road home and the reason is written down. Salt, iron, timber, silver. Ovin Sarr's number on the wall of the second bay and the half-rotten rail. The eighteenth field, offered to a man whose hand will not close. Adrian says out loud in a room that he wrote the word *player* on a market board. Hettis Roke is asked about his eight graves for the first time in nine years and says that the names went to Aurel in a wooden case, which is the same machine as the roll of strangers, which puts the seventeen in danger. The Registry's schedule arrives with a fee in silver. **Perry Lask, 41, of the second list, accepts a workway**, and this city has no procedure for one, and writes a bad one in a day. The batch ends with four knocks in a dressed join and a page that says what it cannot do.

### Chapters 61–70: The silver and the beds (Batch 0002)

The fee in silver is owed and cannot be paid. The Brass Measure's credit is the only winter money in Saltwake and its fourth term is a list of men. The **first Wayhouse** is offered and nearly lost to a misunderstanding about a list. The **first mixed trade** happens on a floor, badly, in the wrong weather, and one of the seventeen is paid and one is not. AdriAN is asked, for the second time in the book, what he is for, and gives a procedure. The batch's turn: the Crown's fee is paid **in salt**, which is a currency the Crown does not accept, and Renner Sallow says the sentence that makes the volume's politics legible: *a fee is a thing that tells you what an office thinks you are.*

### Chapters 71–80: The shrine (Batch 0003 — the volume midpoint)

A workway holder does a work there is no office for, on a resonant place, with a boundary cut into it, in the wrong weather, on a day when the licence's own fresh inspection was signed four people who were not there. **The midpoint of the volume, and the failure is Adrian's: he wrote a bar and it was broken by something that was not the instrument, and his own page cannot reach it, because the bar bars the instrument and not the wall.** A boundary shrine at Four Elms is taken. The Reed Commons' position hardens. Nine Elms refuses for the third time, and this time the refusal is affordable, and the reason it is affordable is that a Crown clerk has quietly made it affordable. **The Crown's fifth clause is used against a village by a Crown officer**, and the village is right and cannot afford to be right.

### Chapters 81–90: The false oath (Batch 0004)

Ivenn's design becomes a document. **The false oath** is a thing a city can sign without knowing what it is: it is an instrument of immense convenience, it is offered in the Lantern Concord's own words, and it makes the **Concord** the permanent holder of the Licence — a single civic body as a single author, which is the same instrument as the old form of the oath with a guild in it instead of a man. Nobody convicted. Marrow at his most useful. Adrian sees the shape and cannot get round it, because a guild that is a hundred and forty people and holds the lock, the roll, the notary and the gauge is *actually* the most durable answer anyone has offered. **The name Single Witness is spoken in this volume, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, for the first time, in Chapter 91.**

### Chapters 91–95: The Void (Batch 0005, days 91–95 of 91–100)

Adrian takes the debt onto himself. The Void is sealed: the false oath is void on the ground that its own condition — that the holder be a person and answerable alone — was not met, and the finding is entered as a **record and not a finding**, and nobody may write why it worked. **The cost is an easy route home, spent, and not offered again in this series.** Nine witnesses, one named by herself. He becomes Stage 2 in the doing and not in a ceremony. **He has no return mark and the route to Earth goes through a room with no door.** *(Batch 0005 runs Chapters 91–100 in one prompt, as `AGENTS.md` requires a ten-to-twenty-chapter handoff; this section is the first half of it and the next section is the second half. The plot is unchanged — days 91 to 100 either way.)*

### Chapters 96–100: Two houses and a road (Batch 0005, days 96–100 — the close)

The first mixed Wayhouse and Open Hand exchange under local law, with a refusal exercised in public and honoured, and a price on a page. The Concord's quarterly description goes out in a wooden case with the honest useless form inside it and Roke's eight entered as **a question and not a name**, because he asked for that. And the final image: **a shrine on a boundary that has been given back and a road that is open because eleven households keep it, and Adrian reading a number off a wall four feet back, and the number is a number and not a sentence about anybody.**

## Midpoint reversal

**The shrine, in the middle third of the volume, and it is a failure of Adrian's own instrument.** He wrote a bar that said no workway of any kind may be offered to any of the seventeen by this instrument or by anybody using it. The bar is perfect and it is **scoped to the wrong thing**: it bars the instrument and not the wall. A workway offered by a pale field in a room is not the instrument. The volume's argument is that a rule about a thing is not a rule about the world, and Adrian learned the mirror image of that in Chapter 0048 about a form and not a promise, and this time it is his own sentence turned over and used on him.

## Volume climax

**Ivenn's false oath is voided by a local debt and by nine witnesses and by a page that cannot say why.** Nobody is convicted of anything; Marrow is not accused and remains the most useful man in every room; the Concord does not lose the lock, the roll, the notary or the gauge, and gains a term. **Adrian's Stage 2 is paid for with the loss of an easy route home and with the knowledge that a void is a thing that can only be used once.**

## Concrete resolution

- **The Lock of Salt:** the licence survives its flood-season review in a form weaker than the one it went in with, and the review is a date on a page before the day.
- **The seventeen:** still no land rights, no class and no workway, **except one, and the one is a procedure invented badly in a hurry and entered with its weaknesses.**
- **Two Wayhouses and one Open Hand exchange** are established under local law, and neither is Adrian's, and both can be refused by the people who keep them.
- **Adrian is Stage 2 and has lost an easy route home.** He is still Unbound on every roster of the licence and party to it and given nothing by it.
- **Ivenn Marrow** is publicly useful, privately dangerous, unaccused, and has said the name *Single Witness* once, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it.
- **The Bright Pilgrims question is open and sharper:** the names went to Aurel in a wooden case; one of Roke's eight is on a stone nine miles up the upper road in a hand his grandfather taught him to read; and nobody living can say where the rest went.

## Next-volume question

**What happened to the people whose names were removed from the first records?** — the Volume 03 question, unchanged, and Volume 02's Close Record is where the trail is laid.

## What must not be re-litigated

- **Owen Park is dead and buried and his four pages stand.** Nothing in this volume may soften, revisit, explain away or generalise his death, and nothing may turn it into a lesson or an instruction, and **nobody may write *he died because*.**
- **The four of the forty are hurt before March, one at a time, on an ordinary morning, in no working.** Renna Ord's prediction stands with her name on it and is entered as a prediction and not as a fact. Volume 02 pays the first of them and does not soften the other three.
- **The main breach is closed and dressed and does not narrow; it gets dressed. It has never spoken.**
- **The ground under the chapel floor is a scar, is not repaired, and the boards have not been lifted.** A form with five items watches it twice a day and **cannot close anything and cannot say where a residue will go.**
- **No crowd ever crosses.** One named person per opening, a fresh inspection before every one of them, two witnesses who are not of the crew, and a receiving community. **The licence is still the only standing route and it is on trial.**
- **Adrian never performs a working in Volume 02 until Stage 2, and when he does he is the one holding the door and somebody else is holding the cost.**
- **Two date forms only** for the flood and the new month, and **March is a month in this world's calendar and is written here as March**, which is the local word for it, and is not the Earth month.
- **Ivenn Marrow is not convicted and is not made useless in any scene.**

## Final chapter image

A boundary shrine stands where it stood before, with the cut in it that was in it before, and a page nailed to the inside of it with a date and a price and a right of refusal, and about nine people are in the room who kept it. At the top of the upper road, a woman of sixty-one who cannot read is reading nineteen shapes aloud to a docker who has a slate, because anybody may pick the work up. And on a river wall, a man from another world reads a number off a wall four feet back from it and says it out loud, and the number is a number, and the joined edge is dressed, and there is no field in the air, and the day book says what it cannot do.
