## 1. Central pressure

**A thing happened at a gate and this world has no instrument for a thing that happened.**

On day 900 about nine ordinary people went through a gate at the far end of a road that has been shut since before
this flood, in the rain, with a child carrying a bundle of seed husks, and a woman on the other side with a book
under her arm, and it rained on afterwards and the gate was left standing open. **None of it was written down.**
Not by anybody, and not because anybody decided not to. Because this manuscript's cheapest instrument for a person
who arrives is somebody standing up, and its second cheapest is a name said out loud once in a room in daylight
with a date on the door, **and neither of those two things survives past a morning, and on the morning after day 900
there are people standing at the bottom of a stair asking whether the gate is to stand open tomorrow.**

**The volume's subject is the difference between a person and a line somebody writes about a person.** Volume 16
spent a number said aloud in a room. Volume 17 spent a bar in two sockets on a top step. Volume 18 spent a person,
and found that the cheapest instrument this world has for a person is somebody standing up. **This volume spends a
line** — a stroke in a column against a day, in a hand — and a line is the only one of the four that can be put
back, and that is why it is the one everybody wants and the one this volume is about.

**KEPT IN THE VOLUME-LIST LINE'S OWN TERMS, as every reconciled line since Volume 10 has kept it: a thing made by
somebody else is going to be used about the people it is about, and the people it is about are not in the room.
Here about a line in a book, and not about a lock, and not about a version, and the people it is about are the nine
who went through a gate and the four hundred who were never going to hear about any of it.**

**AND THE PRESSURE IS NOT THE BOOK. THE BOOK IS THE FIFTH MORNING OF IT. The pressure is that a room which has gone
a hundred days without writing a person down cannot begin on a morning of its own, and that the first person to
write anything down in that room for a hundred days is going to be a person who is not the keeper, and that a
person who writes their own name into a public record is doing something they cannot undo.**
