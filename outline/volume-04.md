# Volume 04 Outline — *The White Ledger*, Chapters 151–200

**This is a plan and it is not canon.** Nothing in this file is true until a chapter says it on a page. Every figure below is a proposed figure, every date is a proposed date, and every person named is a person a chapter has to bring onto a stage in daylight with a date on it. Where this file settles a question the manuscript left open, the settlement is recorded here as a decision of the outline and **the chapter that carries it must establish it in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and may not settle it by narration.**

The plan this volume writes is five lines in `outline/series.md` and it is not a version of a plan:

- **Central pressure:** Jonah Pike establishes a successful, well-run player settlement that takes land, water and template access without local consent.
- **Midpoint:** Adrian's delayed response causes a local family to lose a water gate. He cannot solve the problem simply by declaring the settlement illegal.
- **Climax:** Adrian closes a profitable resource crossing and helps the Reed Commons recover the water gate, splitting the player coalition.
- **Resolution:** Redroot Commons and the Reed Commons establish a land-and-labor compact; Jonah becomes a serious rival.
- **Question:** How many people on Earth are disappearing, and who is exploiting the fear?

## Volume number and shape

**Volume 04, *The White Ledger*, Chapters 151–200, days 155 to 200 of the flood, in four movements of 12, 13, 13 and 12 chapters.**

The title is not invented. The White Ledger is a settlement on the upper road, named in Chapter 0087 and carried in Chapters 0092, 0097 and 0098, and a man of forty-two has been building it since the seventh week of the flood. **The volume is named after the thing that is winning.**

---

## 1. Central pressure

**A settlement that works, on ground that nobody consented to give it, taking the one thing a wetland village cannot replace.**

The White Ledger pays on time. It has clean water. Its queue works and it works on a Tuesday. Thirty-nine men under the near gate of the Lock of Salt have not been paid since the first of March, which is seventy days and more by the end of this volume, and the White Ledger pays a wage out of a store on the upper road and enters it on a slate with a figure on the end of a line and a hand and a date. **The settlement is not a villain with a schedule and this volume must not make it one.** Its competence is the argument.

Its method is not force. It is three things, in this order:

1. **It pays.** A wage, a share, a dry bed, a place at the front of a queue that works, and Earth money printed on paper that is worth nothing here, which the settlement says out loud on the first day it is on a page.
2. **It offers workways**, and the License's own rule requires the cost to be told before the accepting, and the cost is told. It is told in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person who says the reason for the tenth part as well.
3. **It asks the gate.** It does not seize the gate. It asks a household for the days on it, in a room, with the cost read out, and the household says yes.

**Every one of those three is correct and the sum of them is the volume.** Adrian cannot answer it by declaring the settlement illegal, for four separate reasons, and all four are on the page before the midpoint:

- **The settlement is not obviously illegal.** It holds no Crown instrument, claims no office, and stands in no roll. It is a store, a queue, a wage list and a set of people who are good at building.
- **One thing it does is arguably a breach.** The attached page of the trial licence, entered as a term with a date, says **no workway of any kind, offered to anybody, to anybody else, now or at any time, by that instrument or by anybody using it.** A workway is offered in the settlement's own rooms by people who are parties to that licence. **Whether the offer is a breach is a question about a term, and the only office that can answer it is nine hundred miles away, and the next time this city can put anything at all in front of that office is about five weeks from the hearing on day 154.** So the question is real, correct, unanswered, and beside the point, and the volume's whole argument about forms is that a real correct unanswered question is worth nothing to a family.
- **The four Elms matter more to a family than the law's opinion of a boundary.** A household on the Reach that loses its gate has lost the thing its days are organised around, and no finding about a term gives it back.
- **Adrian has no standing of any kind to object.** No office, no roster, no mark, no seat, no wage, no title, no land right, no workway. He said so himself in front of about two hundred and forty people on the sixty-fourth day of the month after this one, and he said in the same four minutes that a man with no mark and on no roster is the most findable person in this world and that this is not a safety.

## 4. Starting power level

**Adrian Vale is Stage 2, Passage holder, in the doing.** Unbound, barred from every roster at his own request in his own hand with the date on it. No mark of his own, no workway, no title, no land right, no class privilege, no seat, no wage, no standing. He performed one working in his life, on the second day of the month after this one, and nobody can say why it worked.

**He does not change stage in this volume and no chapter may move him.** The reason is on the record: the gains of Volume 04 are a share, a name he did not choose, a month, a date and a room, and **none of them is a power.** He performs **no working** in Volume 04, no threshold is opened, the Narrow Mark is not opened a third time, and the other world is not named.

**What does change is the kind of thing he is good at.** He spent three volumes being the man who gives the only thing he has away. This volume is the volume in which he learns that **the only instrument a man with no mark and no roster and no seat has is a question**, and that he has not asked one person one question in a hundred and twenty-nine days as of day 128, and that the instrument for asking a person properly already exists on the page and took four days to build and was used once, on a Hearthguard of forty-four, on a day he did not choose. **Adrian turns that instrument on himself.** The question is given in writing to everybody who will be in the room four days beforehand, and he is the person it is given to, and nobody asks him whether the day suits him, and he answers, and the answer is refused, and the refusal is correct.

That is the interior arc and it is the whole of it. It is a decision, not a power, and it is paid for in a person.

## 6. The three decisions this outline had to make, and how each is reachable in prose

### 6.1 Redroot is a place on the Reed Reach, and Redroot Commons is a body that forms inside this volume

**Redroot has never been named in prose. Zero occurrences in `chapters/`.** The name is in `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md` and nowhere else, and the resolution of this volume is a compact between Redroot Commons and the Reed Commons, so it cannot be invented in a card and forgotten in a body.

**The decision.** Redroot is a low place at the bottom end of a long channel on the Reach where the red root grows in the cut bank, and it carries the Reach's salt racks, and it has households on it. It is a place the Reach knows. **It is not on the Chapter 0010 list.** That list reads: *the shared gates of the Reach and the hamlets on them — Four Elms, Long Water, Silt Row, Corrow, Black Sluice, Nine Elms and the two gatehouses.* Redroot is not in it, and nobody in this city has ever asked why, and the finding of Chapter 0153 is that **a list is a list**, and the omission is a form's fault and not a village's decision, and Redroot's own gate is a shared gate of the Reach and has been for longer than the licence.

**The body.** Redroot Commons is not founded in this volume and it is not a rival to the settlement. It is the settlement's own practice, taken up locally. The White Ledger runs a queue, a wage, a rota and a rota of who does what; the people who work in it begin running it themselves; and at the point where a rota, a queue and a shared labour shift are doing the work of a commons, somebody asks whether it has a name. **The practice comes first and the name comes second, and the name is given in Chapter 0166 in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person who does not want to be asked — and Adrian is not in that room and is told afterwards.**

**How the name first reaches a page: Chapter 0153, and it is a chapter about somebody being told.** Hessa Ruun, the water-count of the Reed Reach, who is about fifty and who is the closest thing the wetland has to a notary and nothing like one, tells a man of thirty-two the name Redroot, at a gate, in daylight, with a date on it, in about four people, and she does not want to be asked and says why before she says it: **that naming a place which is not on a list is how a place becomes findable, and that a man with no mark and on no roster is the most findable person in this world, and that therefore he is the safest person in four hundred miles to tell, and that this is not a safety and she has heard him say so.** He asks for more than four people in the room. She says no. The chapter is that refusal and the telling.

### 6.2 The fourth channel at Nine Elms is one of the Reach's shared water gates, and it is named in a room, in daylight, with a date on it

**There is no water gate on a page and a water gate is not an invented noun.** The phrase appears once in the whole manuscript, in a hypothetical in a speech at `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0029.md:65`, and it is a category in an argument and not the name of an asset. The category has been in the bible since Volume 01: `bible/world.md` has the Reed Reach villages *maintain shared water gates* and *Reed Common speakers record who may use a water gate*; `bible/themes.md` lists the Reed water gates as a recurring setting; `outline/volume-01.md` says the shared water gates *take the same river pressure as Saltwake's lower fields*, which is why the Commons are the receiving community and why one Reed village refuses by name.

**The decision: the fourth channel of the junction at Nine Elms is a water gate of the Reach, and it is the Nine Elms gate, and Volume 04 gives it a name, a keeper and a day.** It is named in **Chapter 0154**, at a junction, in daylight, with a date on it, by the water-count, who is the person whose office it is to record who may use a water gate. The name is chosen on the page and is not set here. The keeper is named on the page and is not set here.

**The chapter also has to hold something uncomfortable, and it is the point of the chapter.** The Reach *records who may use a water gate.* That record is a list of persons. **A commons that keeps a list of persons is doing the thing this book has spent a hundred and fifty-four days being angry about, and it is doing it for good reasons, and the chapter says so and does not resolve it.** And the keeper of the Nine Elms gate is a person who is not on that list, and the reason she is not on it is in the chapter and is not flattering to anybody.

**The fourth channel stays shut for the whole of Volume 04.** The quarter is gone, the fourth line of the term sheet is in force, the undertaking that would have paid is void, and no chapter of this volume opens that channel, and a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house is not asked, and nobody stands outside the fourth house. **The volume is about a different gate.**

### 6.3 A water gate is a quantity of water on given days, and that is the only way a shared gate can be lost

**The decision.** A water gate of the Reach is not owned and cannot be bought. What a household has is **the days on it** — which days of which week the household's share may be let through, and a board, and a keeping of it, and a name in a speaker's record. The gate itself stays where it is and stays open in the season. **So the only way a household loses a gate is to lose the days, and the only way to lose the days is to agree to them, and the agreement is the whole of the loss.**

This is the volume's central precision and it is why the midpoint is a consent and not a seizure. It also means the gate is recoverable: **give the days back and the gate has never gone anywhere.** That is what the climax does.

### 6.4 The profitable resource crossing is salt, and the salt goes out through one gate

**The decision.** The White Ledger's money is salt. Salt is the one product in four hundred miles that a person can sell to somebody nine hundred miles away, and the settlement's causeway down off the upper road and its racks on the Reach flats are how it gets out. **The settlement is not a trading post; it is an export.**

So the crossing the climax closes is **the salt road at the Redroot gate**: a resource crossing, profitable, and running on a gate a community grants rather than owns. And the reason the settlement cannot simply buy its way round it is not a prohibition and not a wall: **there is one set of racks that dries, and it is at the bottom of a channel, and a channel takes its water from a gate.** Any other route is four miles of causeway and forty days.

**What makes the closing possible in canon and not in invention.** The instrument of Chapter 0010 says, of the route: *the seat may be suspended by the receiving community and may be filled by no one but the man whose mark is in the stone.* **The receiving community is the Reed Commons.** The seat of holder of the pull is vacant and the instrument says nobody and has said nobody five times and is not a fault. So:

**The climax is Adrian asking a meeting-ground to withdraw the days on the Redroot gate from the settlement, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, and then standing at the back of the room and not answering, which is the first thing he has been able to do since he gave the reading away.** The Commons can do it because a gate is theirs. Adrian cannot do it and does not do it. **And the salt road stops inside a quarter, and the loss falls on the settlement and not on the Reach, and that is the finding and it is the reason the room agrees.**

**The cost.** Thirty-nine men under the near gate lose the one thing the settlement was offering them, and about nine of the seventeen go to the Reach side and about nine go with Jonah, and Adrian has not defeated anybody and has not won a wage. A favour is a thing that stops; this was not a favour, it was a wage, and taking it away is a different act from a favour stopping and he has to say so out loud.

### 6.5 Jonah Pike gives Adrian Vale his ninth name

**The reader is owed a name for Adrian in this volume and the outline assigns the act: Jonah Pike gives it, in Chapter 0169, on day 173, a Saturday, in a room, in daylight, with a date on it, in nine sentences, and it is *the witness*.**

It is right. It is a role, and Adrian has refused every role in three volumes, and a role given by a private man in a room is not a post and is not on a roster and costs nothing and cannot be refused, because refusing it would be a speech about a person, and the woman of twenty-nine has already said in her own words that a speech about somebody is a thing she does not do. **And it is the most useful thing anybody has ever offered Adrian, because the White Ledger's central need is a man with no mark and no roster and no standing and no wage who can be sent into any room and be believed, and that is the most findable person in this world.** By the end of the day the name is in four mouths and it is doing work, and that is the problem.

**The prose may not claim a total count of names that no page supports.** The manuscript's established count of named guests is nine and the handoff's phrase *his ninth name* is read here as *the naming of Adrian in this volume*, not as an arithmetic claim.

## 8. Midpoint reversal

**Chapter 0175, day 179, a Friday, in a room at Redroot, in daylight, with a date on it.**

The household on the Redroot gate is asked for the days on it by the settlement. The ask is made properly: the cost is read out, in the local language, in front of the household and about nine other people, and the person who is to take the workway is told what the workway costs before she takes it, and the person taking it says yes with the cost in front of her. **The household says yes.** The days change hands. **The first full load of salt goes out of the Reach on the settlement's causeway on day 182, three days later, and it is the only product in four hundred miles that anybody can sell.**

**It is Adrian's delayed response that causes it, and the delay is the quarter.** Everything that would have stopped it needed something to be put in front of an office, and the office is nine hundred miles away, and the next time anything can be put in front of it is day 189, and the asking is on day 179, and **Adrian said the interval out loud on day 164, in a gateway, in about four minutes, attached to a decision, and entered on day 165 in his own hand with the date on it that he was going to do nothing about it for another twenty-five days on purpose. That entry is the first decision he has made in three volumes that he cannot improve on, because it is not a shape. It is a date.**

He cannot solve it by declaring the settlement illegal, for the four reasons in §1, and the chapter says each of them out loud in about four minutes and improves on none of them.

**And it is not a defeat, because the person who lost the gate is not a victim in a story — she is a woman with a second handle, a wage that arrives, nine days of racks saved, a workway she wanted, and a child, and she says in her own words that she would say yes again and that she is going to be angry about it for a year.** The volume does not hand her a rescue and does not hand her a lesson. The cost is Adrian's and it is the specific cost of a silence he chose.

## 9. Escalation sequence

**Movement I — *The Queue* (Chapters 0151–0162, days 155–166, one chapter to a day).**
Jonah walks in and the settlement is on the page as a fact and not a rumour. The month runs out and a notary names the next one. Redroot is told to Adrian by the water-count. The fourth channel at Nine Elms is named as a water gate with a keeper and a day, and the Reach's own list of gate-keepers is a list, and nobody fixes it. Somebody stands in the settlement's queue for two hours in bad weather and comes back with the truth, which is that it works. Salt is found to be the profit, and it goes out through one gate. The keeper of the Redroot gate is met in a kitchen and gives Adrian nothing. A workway is offered with its cost told out loud and a person takes it. A Warden's duty is to a claimed place, and the claimed place is the settlement's. The days are asked for. **The quarter is named out loud.** Adrian enters, with the date, that he is the reason.

**Movement II — *The Gate* (Chapters 0163–0175, days 167–179, one chapter to a day).**
The delegation to the First Landing becomes a live problem inside the volume instead of furniture, and the *to open nothing* clause is said by the woman it belongs to. Jonah offers Adrian a share, which is a post with the post taken out, and it takes four minutes instead of four seconds, and Adrian concedes the ninth sentence out loud, and that is the volume's first change in him. Redroot Commons has a practice before it has a name; the name is refused once and given in Chapter 0166, in a room with a door, in daylight, with a date on it, by a person who does not want to be asked, and Adrian is not in that room. The meeting-ground at Verrow hears the ask and does not answer it, and the finding is that the Reach's own rule — every household affected must be heard — has a hole in it shaped exactly like a woman of forty-eight in a fourth house, and nobody fills the hole and nobody goes near the house. **Movement III — *The Quarter* (Chapters 0176–0188, days 180–192, one chapter to a day).**
The day after the loss, and the wage is real, which is worse. A recovery chapter in a kitchen with the keeper of the Redroot gate and no form in it. The first full load of salt goes out of the Reach on the settlement's causeway, and nine households watch it, and the finding is that it is the only product in four hundred miles anybody can sell. Adrian takes the legal road and discovers that the question about a term of the trial licence is real, correct, and cannot be asked for five weeks, and that a correct unanswered question is worth nothing to a family. The settlement offers to pay the licence's cost, which is the most dangerous offer in the book. The seventeen split. A Warden is the price of the ground and the settlement survives a crisis Adrian did not predict. One relationship chapter about a person, and it is about Tamsin. **Day 189, a Monday: the quarter runs out and for the first time in a hundred and fifty-four days this city can put something in front of that office, and it puts there a number.** The fifteenth shape, dated day 190, correct, and agreeing with the wall in the salt store. ## 10. Volume climax

## 14. The calendar, and the decision the calendar forced

## 17. The five prose instructions Volume 04 is written under, from a review of all 150 chapters

1. **A chapter may not open by summarising itself, and least of all its ending.** Chapter 0127's first sentence gives the reader the failure, the sixth attempt, the offer, the refusal and both counts. **The opening sentence of a Volume 04 chapter states a pressure or an arrival. It does not state the finding, and the finding is the thing the chapter is for.** With the ledger gone and the coda cut, the opening sentence is also the only thing standing between the reader and the day's shape, and it has to be a thing happening.
2. **The bolded ledger paragraph may not open a chapter.** *The licence is N days old. The undertaking is void. The field was not paid. The fee is entered as owing. The black line in his palm is N days old and has not moved.* It is in 46 of Volume 03's 50 chapters and it recites rather than dramatises. **Where a figure is genuinely load-bearing — a licence age, a day count, a palm line, the age of a gate, the number of days a wage is owed — it is paid for once in a chapter, in a mouth, attached to a decision, and it is paid for again only when it changes.** The palm line, the licence age and the fee are not load-bearing in this volume and appear at most in the volume's own accounting, not in its chapters.
3. **The closing coda is cut by at least half and may not enumerate the standing threads.** It is a 200-to-400-word recap of the whole unresolved world in 34 of Volume 03's 50 chapters and it is the single largest drag on the prose in the manuscript. **One thread, one image, one thing that changed and one thing that did not. A chapter that ends on the sound of a book being recited has not ended.** A Volume 04 chapter may end on a figure, a hand, a door, a queue, or a sentence somebody said and will not say again, and on nothing else.
4. **The *about N seconds* refrain is used deliberately and sparingly.** It is a motif, declared as one, and it is at roughly 760 instances across 150 chapters. **A motif repeated inside one chapter is two beats where there is room for one, and `Nobody said anything for about nineteen seconds` is the sentence to cut first.** A pause that matters is a thing a person does with their hands. **Target for the volume: fewer than a third of the density of Volume 03, and never twice in one chapter.**
5. **The people who carry the volume get a second handle beside the age and the trade and never instead of it** — a name, a habit, a piece of clothing, a thing they do with a pen. Nine near-identical descriptors is a cast a reader cannot hold and the device has already produced a phantom, a ninth person who does not exist, out of one wrong word in Chapter 0149. **The people who carry Volume 04 and who carry a second handle: Adrian Vale, whose is a name given to him; Hessa Ruun, who counts water on her fingers and has said a thing about a fourth line since day 80; Sera Vail, who has been counting days on her own initiative since day 47 and is tired of being the only person who keeps one; Jonah Pike, whose is a printed sheet with a wage on it that he hands over before he speaks; the keeper of the Redroot gate; the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a stall by the wharf, whose is a slate she has never written on. The age-and-trade form is not abolished and is not replaced.**

**And one more, which is not a style instruction and is the reason the five exist.** Nobody in Volume 03 got stronger. A woman of seventy said a plain sentence about luck. A girl of seventeen read her own page out badly and nobody corrected her and the cost of it was hers. **A man from another world gave up the only thing he is good at as a way of being needed, and nobody thanked him, and the gains of the whole volume were a name, a page on the back of a stone, a form, a room, a wage and a mechanism.** Volume 04's gains are a share, a name he did not choose, a month, a date, a gate that came back, and a woman who went on a page on purpose. **None of them is a power. One of them is the price of a power. All of them are heavier than a power.**
